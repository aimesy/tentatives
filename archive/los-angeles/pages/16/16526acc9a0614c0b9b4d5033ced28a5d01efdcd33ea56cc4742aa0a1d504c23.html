Case Number: 23PSCV01105    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR TERMINATING SANCTIONS AGAINST DEFENDANTS ZENGLE WANG AND XIUWEI LI (STRIKING ANSWER AND ENTERING DEFAULT) is TBD at the hearing/likely granted.

 

Background

 

This case arises from an unpaid loan (excluding interest) of $300,000.

 

On April 14, 2023, Plaintiffs SHILAN ZHAO and YIIXIN QIAO filed suit against Defendants ZENGLE WANG and XIUWEI LI.

 

On October 31, 2023, Plaintiffs filed a first amended complaint (FAC) for:

 

1. BREACH OF CONTRACT;

2. FRAUD;

3. CIVIL CONSPIRACY;

4. CONVERSION;

5. PROMISSORY ESTOPPEL;

6. UNJUST ENRICHMENT;

7. INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS.

 

 On November 30, 2023, Defendants filed their answer.

 

On December 4, 2023, there was a CMC; there was defense counsel present.

 

On May 2, 2024, the parties attended another CMC.

 

On November 7, 2024, the parties attended an MSC.

 

On September 15, 2025, a CMC was held. The minute order (MO) Indicated that discovery is still ongoing.

 

On February 26, 2026, the defendants filed substitutions of attorney indicating that they would represent themselves.

 

On March 11, 2026, a CMC was held; the defendants attended. “The Defendants make an oral request for a continuance in order to retain an attorney.”


On June 11, 2026, there was a status conference; the defendants did not appear. The court set an Order to Show Cause Re: Why the Court Should Not Issue Monetary Sanctions Against the Defendants for Their Failure to Appear at the Status Conference on 06/11/2026 is scheduled for 09/21/2026 at 09:00 AM in Department O at Pomona Courthouse South. The minute order also “direct[ed] Counsel for Plaintiff to file a formally noticed motion prior to the next hearing if they would like to seek further court action aside from monetary sanctions.”

 

On June 22, 2026, the instant motion was filed, along with a motion for leave to file an amended complaint.

 

To date, as of 9/4/26, no opposition has been filed (due 9 court days before the hearing).

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiffs seek that the court strike Defendants’ answer and enter default “on the grounds that Defendants have engaged in a willful and continuing pattern of failing to participate in this litigation and to obey the orders of this Court including, without limitation, Defendants’ failure to appear at the Status Conference Re: Defendants’ Representation on June 11, 2026, and their failure to file the declarations the Court directed them to file such that no sanction short of a terminating sanction will cure the prejudice to Plaintiffs or secure Defendants’ compliance.”

As framed by the Slesinger court, “The essential requirement is to calibrate the sanction to the wrong.” (Id. at p. 755.) Here, the wrong has been an effective abandonment of the case, and that is further evidenced by the failure of Defendants to file an opposition to this motion. With that, the court is inclined to grant the motion.

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion is TBD at the conclusion of the hearing/likely granted .


Case Number: 24PSCV01631    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: O

DEFENDANT WEST COVINA UNIFIED SCHOOL DISTRICT’S DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF’S SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT is TBD.

 

Background

 

This is a discrimination case. Plaintiff DENNIS ROWLAND alleges the following against Defendant WEST COVINA UNIFIED SCHOOL DISTRICT ("Defendant" or the “District”): Plaintiff, who is over 40 years old, from around October 2022 to the present time, was subject to a racially hostile working environment. Specifically, Plaintiff focuses on a racial slur used on the campus. On one occasion, he was called the derogatory racial slur by an employee; in another the word was scrolled on the wall of a room he was cleaning; and in another the word has been scribbled on the school’s walls. Defendant has not eliminated the use of the word.

 

On May 20, 2024, Plaintiff filed suit asserting the following causes of action (COAs):

 

1.     Discrimination Based On Age In Violation Of Feha

2.     Discrimination Based On Disability In Violation Of Feha

3.     Failure To Accommodate In Violation Of Feha

4.     Failure To Engage In A Good Faith Interactive Process In Violation Of Feha ( Against All Defendants)

5.     Retaliation In Violation Of Feha

6.     Failure To Take All Necessary Steps To Prevent Discrimination, Harassment, And Retaliation In Violation Of Feha

7.     Discrimination Based On Race (Against All Defendants) In Violation Of Feha

8.     Discrimination Based On Color In Violation Of Feha

9.     Slander And Defamation

10.  False Light

11.  Negligent Hiring, Supervising Or Retention

12.  Wrongful Demotion/Termination Of Public Policy

13.  Violation Of Labor Code

14.  INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS

 

On April 8, 2025, Defendant filed a demurrer with a motion to strike, which on 5/21/25 was sustained with leave to amend. (The court did so largely as the conclusion was devoid of the necessary factual allegations which the opposition tried to cure.) 

 

On June 9, 2025, a first amended complaint (FAC) was filed.

 

On May 18, 2026, a second amended complaint (SAC) was filed for:

 

1. Discrimination Based on Age (Gov. Code § 12940(a))

2. Discrimination Based on Disability (Gov. Code § 12940(a))

3. Failure to Accommodate (Gov. Code § 12940(m))

4. Failure to Engage in a Good Faith Interactive Process (Gov. Code § 12940(n))

5. Retaliation (Gov. Code § 12940(h))

6. Failure to Prevent Discrimination and Harassment (Gov. Code § 12940(k))

7. Discrimination Based on Race (Gov. Code § 12940(a))

8. Discrimination Based on Color (Gov. Code § 12940(a))

9. Slander and Defamation

10. Wrongful Demotion in Violation of Public Policy

11. Violation of Labor Code § 1102.5

12. Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress

 

On June 17, 2026, a demurrer was filed.

 

On July 27, 2026, an opposition was filed.[1]

 

On July 28, 2026, a reply was filed.

 

Discussion

 

The District demurs to the entirety of the complaint on a variety of grounds largely based upon the COAs failing to state sufficient facts (e.g., Plaintiff fails to allege facts to show he suffered an adverse employment action or any other circumstance that would suggest any sort of discriminatory motive on the District’s part or that Plaintiff fails to identify the District’s actions or inactions that would give rise to District liability). For purposes of this demurrer, there is one dispositive issue: whether Plaintiff filed a claim under the Government Claims Act.

 

As argued in the demurrer, prior to filing suit for damages against a public entity, a party must present a claim to the public entity alleged to have caused the damages. (Gov. Code §§ 910 et seq; § 945.4.) The claim must include, inter alia, the circumstance of the transaction and a general description of the injury. (Gov. Code § 910, subds. (c), (d).) Courts have been clear: “If a plaintiff relies on more than one theory of recovery against the governmental agency, each cause of action must have been reflected in a timely claim.” (Fall River Joint Unified School District v. Superior Court (1998) 206 Cal.App.3d 431, 434 [theory omitted from Government Code claim was barred.].) The claim must give the government “an opportunity to investigate and evaluate its potential liability.” (Id. at 435–436.)

 

Here, the only allegation regarding such claim is as follows: “A timely tort and FEHA claim was filed and rejected by the District. Plaintiff has exhausted all required administrative remedies before filing this Complaint.” (SAC ¶ 3.) In opposition, for the first time, Plaintiff argues that documents submitted “with Plaintiff’s concurrently filed Request for Judicial Notice and the Declaration of Mansfield Collins, the Civil Rights Department issued Plaintiff’s right-to-sue notice on May 20, 2024 (CRD Matter No. 202405-24791921), and Plaintiff filed this action that same day, comfortably within the one-year limitations period.” However, no request for judicial notice is attached to the opposition/separately filed; the District also did not receive such documents. (The docket also not provide any supplemental RJN.)

 

To the extent that Plaintiff cites to State of California v. Superior Court (2004) 32 Cal.4th 1234, 1243 to support their position that a “plaintiff need only allege facts demonstrating or excusing compliance with the claim-presentation requirement,” that is what the case held, but it is not what Plaintiff has done. In fact, in that case, the plaintiff pled slightly more facts and the state’s high court deemed the allegations insufficient. There the plaintiff alleged “that plaintiff had submitted a claim to the Office of the Attorney General, which “represented that [it was] authorized to accept service for the State Board of Control for the State of California” and “led [plaintiff] to believe that [he was] serving the State Board of Control.” The complaint further alleged that plaintiff “received telephonic notice that there were small errors contained in the original claim and was requested to file an Amended Claim.” According to the complaint, “an Agent of the Attorney General represented to [plaintiff] that she would accept service of said amended claim, and that the requested changes would correct any errors concerning said claim.” As requested, plaintiff filed an amended claim. The complaint then alleged that plaintiff only learned that the Office of the Attorney General—rather than the State Board of Control—had been mistakenly served over one year after he discovered he had lung cancer.” (Id. at p. 1238.)  Going through an overwhelming amount of statutory interpretation and case law, the court said more was needed and that “requiring plaintiffs to allege facts sufficient to demonstrate or excuse compliance does not deprive them of their due process rights or unfairly bar just claims.” (Id. at p. 1245.) Accordingly, what this court is asking is consistent with the overwhelming amount of case law: Plaintiff need not allege strict compliance with the statutory claim presentation requirement just substantial compliance, the latter which has not been demonstrated. (Ibid.)

 

If this was in advertent error and the District is amenable, the court is inclined to continue the matter for said document(s) to be filed.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the hearing is TBD.

 

 

 


[1] The opposition was untimely (hence why the court continued the hearing), and Plaintiff admits that much. The issue then is whether the court should exercise its discretion to overlook the untimeliness due to an “Through an inadvertent calendaring and filing error.” The court will consider the opposition because sustaining the demurrer without addressing the opposition would likely be an abuse of discretion and require more motion(s). Thus, to hear the case on its merits, the court will address the opposition.

 


Case Number: 26PSCV00584    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

Defendants’ Motion to Strike is GRANTED with 30 days leave to amend; the court requests Defendants file a proposed order.

 

Background

 

This is a dog bite case that happened in March 2024 when the plaintiff was in a public place.

 

On February 18, 2026, Plaintiff Ismael Gomez filed suit against Defendants Ernesto Jimenez and Maria Jimenez for:

 

1.     Strict Liability

2.     Negligence

On May 6, 2026, the instant motion to strike (MTS) was filed.

 

On July 17, 2026, an opposition was filed.

 

To date, as of Thursday, September at 3 PM, no reply has been filed (due 5 court days before the hearing (Tuesday, 9/1).

 

Discussion

 

Defendants seek to strike references to punitive damages because the complaint lacks in specificity. The court agrees.

 

The complaint alleges, in part, the following:

 

The subject dog suddenly and violently lunged at Plaintiff, jumping onto him and attempting to bite his neck and throat. In a desperate effort to protect himself from being mauled, Plaintiff raised his arm to shield his neck, at which point the dog clamped down and bit Plaintiff’s left arm. As Plaintiff attempted to defend himself and create distance, he struck the dog in its midsection, causing him to momentarily fall. The dog immediately resumed its attack and bit Plaintiff on his left leg. Throughout the attack, Plaintiff reasonably believed he was about to be killed or suffer fatal injuries and experienced intense fear for his life. Additionally, upon information and belief, Defendant’s dog had known dangerous propensities, including a history of aggressive behavior, prior attacks, or a tendency to bite without provocation. Defendant was aware or should have been aware of these dangerous tendencies yet failed to take adequate precautions to control or restrain the dog, further contributing to the preventability of this incident. (Complaint pp. 3-4.)

 

As noted by Defendants, punitive damage allegations require factual specificity. To survive a motion to strike, a plaintiff must plead the specific facts showing entitlement to such relief. (Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67

Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255; Grieves v. Superior Court (1984) 157 Cal.App.3d 159, 166; Blegen v. Superior Court (1981) 125 Cal.App.3d 959, 962-63.) The specific facts here would be delineating the history of aggressive behavior, prior attacks, or a tendency to bite without provocation (e.g., the number of times this dog under these owners had previously attacked people without provocation). Instead, Plaintiff alleges so “under information and belief.” That is insufficient. (See e.g., Carney v. Simmonds (1957) 49 Cal.2d 84, 100; Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550 (“[A] pleading made on information and belief is insufficient if it ‘fails to allege specific facts upon which the belief is founded.’”)].)[1]

 

To the extent an opposition has been filed, it merely reiterates the allegations above. To the extent that Plaintiff relies upon the allegation that “Defendants knowingly and with conscious disregard of the consequences, unleashed the large dog in a public place,” that insufficient for malice. After all, malice refers to an evil motive. (See e.g.., Ebaugh v. Rabkin (1972) 22 Cal.App.3d 891, 895 [“Under general definition, malice in fact denotes ill will on the part of the defendant, or his desire to do harm for the mere satisfaction of doing it. In ultimate analysis, malice in fact is malice of evil motive.”].) Accordingly, on this fact alone, there is no inference that people who have their dogs off leash carry an evil motive or conscious disregard of the probability that an injury will result. (See e.g., Karlsson v. Ford Motor Co. (2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 1202, 1230.) At most, on these facts, that may be negligence.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion is granted WITH 30 DAYS leave to amend.

 

 

 

 

 

 


[1] And though the federal rules of civil procedure and CCP have different pleading standards, the guidance on such pleading is instructive here. (See e.g., Kowal v. MCL Communications Corp. (1994) 16 F.3d 1271, 1279 [“In general, pleadings on information and belief are permitted when ‘the necessary information lies within defendants' control.’ [] Nonetheless, standards for pleadings on information and belief must be construed consistent with the purposes of Rule 9(b), which attempts in part to “ ‘prevent[ ] the filing of a complaint as a pretext for the discovery of unknown wrongs.’ [] We therefore affirm the district court's determination that pleadings on information and belief require an allegation that the necessary information lies within the defendant's control, and that such allegations must also be accompanied by a statement of the facts upon which the allegations are based.” (Id. at p. 1279, emphasis added, internal citations omitted.)

 


Case Number: 26PSCV01342    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

DEFENDANTS CONCORD SERVICING, LLC AND CONCORD SERVICING INTERNATIONAL, LLC’S DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT is SUSTAINED with 30 days leave to amend.

 

Background

 

This case arises from the installation of solar panels. Plaintiffs RAED FAWAZ and HELEN FAWAZ allege the following against Defendants ALL PREMIUM CONTRACTORS, INC., a California Corporation; SOLAR MOSAIC LLC; a Delaware Limited Liability Company; CONCORD SERVICING LLC, a Delaware Limited Liability Company, CONCORD SERVICING INTERNATIONAL LLC, an Arizona Limited Liability Company: In or around February 2023, Defendants, through their sales side, initiated contact with Plaintiffs by cold call in an effort to sell a residential solar system for the Subject Property; The solar sales company was All Premium Contractors, and the finance company or lender was Mosaic. The transaction was presented and consummated as a loan. The documents were signed on or about February 24, 2023, at Plaintiffs’ home; Plaintiffs are informed and believe, and based thereon allege, that after the initial $25,000 approval, Defendants and/or their agents used Helen’s personal information to

open a second loan for approximately $75,000 without Plaintiffs’ knowledge, consent, signature, or authorization (Plaintiffs never signed, approved, or were informed that a second loan was required); as for the system, although the system is presently capable of some operation, it has not functioned as represented. Plaintiffs have experienced system shutoffs, inconsistent system performance, failure to meet performance projections, and battery-related problems; after Mosaic originated and/or initially held the subject loan obligations, Mosaic assigned, transferred, sold, delegated, conveyed, and/or otherwise caused its rights to be acquired in whole or in part by Defendants Concord Servicing LLC and Concord Servicing International LLC.

 

 

On April 13, 2025, Plaintiffs filed suit asserting the following causes of action (COAs):

 

1. Fraud / Intentional Misrepresentation;

2. Negligent Misrepresentation;

3. Negligent Hiring, Retention, and Supervision;

4. Breach of Contract and

5. Unfair Business Practices in Violation of Business & Professions Code §

17200 et seq.

 

On June 16, 2026, Solar Mosaic, LLC filed a motion to compel arbitration.

 

On August 12, 2026, DEFENDANTS CONCORD SERVICING, LLC AND CONCORD SERVICING INTERNATIONAL, LLC filed the instant demurrer.

 

On August 17, 2026, DEFENDANT SOLAR SERVICING LLC’S filed a motion to compel arbitration.

 

On August 26, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition to the demurrer.

 

On September 1, 2026, a reply was filed.

 

Discussion

 

The crux of the demurrer is that sufficient facts have not been asserted against Concord Defendants because the Complaint does not allege that either “Concord entity participated in the February 2023 sales process, made any representations to Plaintiffs regarding the solar system or its benefits, employed or supervised the sales agents or installers, originated or funded any loan, entered into any contract with Plaintiffs, or had any contact with Plaintiffs prior to acquiring loan servicing rights from Mosaic.” (Demurrer p. 5.)

 

The court will not reach the merits on this demurrer because the opposition, as even noted in reply, extraneous matter (i.e., matter outside the four corners of the complaint). This includes Plaintiffs relying upon June 2026 collection letters and a Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Theory. With that, as the court is bound by the complaint and nothing more, the court finds it prudent to sustain the demurrer with 30 days leave to amend to ensure such matters are pled. (And the opposition on pages 12 and 13 delineates additional facts which may be pled.)

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer is sustained.
