Case Number: 25STCP03226    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner University of Southern California’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is granted in the amount of $20,906.09. The proposed judgment filed on 09/19/2025 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On August 19, 2025, Petitioner University of Southern California (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award (“the Petition”) against Respondent Gabriela Moreno (“Respondent”). The Petition is supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and declaration, filed on the same day. Petitioner sought multiple continuances of the time to serve Respondent and to allow for the hearing on its Motion for Approval to Electronically Serve the Petition. At the initial hearing on March 24, 2026, the Court continued the matter to August 12, 2026. (Minute Order, 03/24/26.) On June 10, 2026, the Court denied Petitioner’s Motion for Approval to Electronically Serve the Petition. (Minute Order, 06/10/26.)

 

On July 15, 2026, Petitioner filed Proof of Service by Mail. To date, no response to the Petition has been filed.

 

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party to an arbitration in which an award has been made may petition the court to confirm, correct or vacate the award.  The petition shall name as respondent all parties to the arbitration and may name as respondents any other persons bound by the arbitration award.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.) “If a petition or response under this chapter is duly served and filed, the court shall confirm the award as made, whether rendered in this state or another state, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.)

 

A response to a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award that seeks to vacate or correct the award must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288.2.)

 

Discussion

 

Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing

 

Code of Civil Procedure, section 1290.4 requires that the petition and notice of hearing be served on Respondent “in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice” or “[i]f the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made . . . [s]ervice within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4, subds. (a), (b).)

 

Petitioner does not attach a copy of the arbitration agreement. Instead, it includes a copy of the demand for arbitration of an employment dispute submitted by Respondent to the American Arbitration Association. (Pet., Attachment 4(b); Aguado Decl., ¶3 and Exh. A.) Therefore, the Court finds the Petition to Confirm and Notice of Hearing must be served upon Respondent in the manner of service for a summons. On July 15, 2026, Petitioner filed Proof of Service by Mail, demonstrating service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing upon Respondent in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 415.30, and Respondent’s Acknowledgment of Receipt of the papers. The Notice of Ruling with the continued hearing date was then served upon Respondent by electronic and first-class mail on July 15, 2026. Accordingly, the Court finds that the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4 are satisfied.

 

Service of the Arbitration Award (CCP § 1283.6)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 requires that “[t]he neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1283.6.) This requirement may be satisfied by service by the arbitrator, or upon proper service of the award with the petition. (See Murray v. Civil Service Employees Ins. Co. (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 796, 799-800.) Petitioner moves to confirm the costs award issued by the arbitrator in connection with the parties’ arbitration proceeding. A copy of the costs award was served on the parties on June 23, 2025. (Pet., Aguado Decl., ¶14 and Exh. C.) It was also served on Respondent as a part of the instant Petition. (Ibid.) Petitioner, therefore, has satisfied the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6.

 

Confirmation of the Arbitration Award

 

An arbitration award is not directly enforceable until it is confirmed by a court and judgment is entered.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1287.6; Jones v. Kvistad (1971) 19 Cal.App.3d 836, 840.) The court must confirm the award as made, unless it corrects or vacates the award, or dismisses the proceeding.  (CCP § 1286; Valsan Partners Limited Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility, Inc. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.) Code of Civil Procedure, section 1285.4 states a petition under this chapter shall:

 

a)     Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

b)     Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

c)     Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4.) The Petition complies with these requirements. It sets forth the nature of the arbitration agreement, the name of the arbitrator, and attaches a copy of the award of costs. (Pet., Aguado Decl., ¶¶3-4, 14 and Exhs. A-C.) The arbitrator awarded Petitioner $20,906.09 in costs. (Id. at Exh. C.) Therefore, the award of arbitration costs is confirmed in favor of Petitioner in that amount.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner University of Southern California’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is granted in the amount of $20,906.09. The proposed judgment filed on 09/19/2025 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Moving party to give notice.

 


Case Number: 25STLC00960    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Robertson’s Ready Mix, Ltd.’s Motion to Strike Defendant Sahara Construction Co., Inc.’s Answer is denied.

 

On its own motion, the Court sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Status of the Legal Representation of Defendant Sahara Construction Co., Inc. for 09/15/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 in the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Robertson’s Ready Mix, Ltd. (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Sahara Construction Co., Inc. (“Defendant SCC”) and others on February 6, 2025. Defendant SCC filed its Answer to the Complaint on June 17, 2025. On February 11, 2026, the Court granted the motion of Defendant SCC’s counsel to be relieved. (Minute Order, 02/11/26.) Defendant SCC’s counsel filed proof of service of the order being relieved on April 30, 2026, making it effective on that date.

 

On July 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed and served the instant Motion to Strike Answer. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion to Strike Answer is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 436 on the grounds that Defendant SCC, despite being a corporation, is no longer represented by an attorney, making its previously filed Answer improper. (Motion, p. 2:1-14.)

 

Motions to strike in the Limited Civil Court may only challenge pleadings on the basis that “the damages or relief sought are not supported by the allegations of the complaint.” (Code of Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (d).) The instant Motion to Strike does not pertain to whether the damages or relief sought are supported by the allegations, and therefore, cannot be raised in this Court. Furthermore, the Court finds that the Answer itself is not improper as it was filed by an attorney. The Motion provides no authority for a retroactive finding that a pleading is improper after a motion to be relieved as counsel is granted with respect to a corporate defendant. (Motion, p. 4:22-23.)

 

That said, it is black letter law that a corporation cannot represent itself in court. (Clean Air Transport Systems v. San Mateo County Transit Dist. (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 576, 578 (citing Merco Constr. Engineers, Inc. v. Municipal Court (1978) 21 Cal.3d 724, 729-730).) This rule applies to all entities regarded as separate from their owners, including partnerships and unincorporated associations. (See Clean Air Transport Systems, supra, 19 Cal.App.3d at 578.) Therefore, the Court finds Defendant SCC cannot appear going forward without an attorney. Lack of counsel is a curable defect.  (CLD Constr., Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal. App. 4th 1141, 114.)  However, if not cured after notice, the corporate entity’s pleading may be stricken.  (See id. at 1150 (“the court retains authority to dismiss an action if an unrepresented corporation does not obtain counsel within reasonable time.”)  The Court sets a show-cause hearing on the date below. If Defendant SCC does not appear through counsel on or before that date, the Court will strike Defendant’s Answer.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Robertson’s Ready Mix, Ltd.’s Motion to Strike Defendant Sahara Construction Co., Inc.’s Answer is denied.

 

On its own motion, the Court sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Status of the Legal Representation of Defendant Sahara Construction Co., Inc. for 09/15/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 in the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 25STLC07596    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendants Kings Collision Center, LLC’s and Azeez Adams’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is sustained without leave to amend.

 

Defendants are to file and serve a proposed judgment of dismissal within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On October 2, 2025, Plaintiff Impound Compliance, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendants Kings Collision Center, LLC (“Defendant Kings Collision”) and Azeez Adams (“Defendant Adams”). On March 10, 2026, the Court sustained Defendants’ Demurrer to the Complaint with leave to amend. (Minute Order, 03/10/26.)

 

Defendant filed a Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint on April 20, 2026, which came for hearing on May 14, 2026. The Court continued the hearing on account of the First Amended Complaint not yet being filed, and ordered Plaintiff to file the amended pleading by May 26, 2026. (Minute Order, 05/14/26.) Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint on May 22, 2026. At the second hearing on June 11, 2026, the Court took the Demurrer filed on April 20, 2026 off calendar and gave Defendants leave to file a Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint filed on May 22, 2026 within 30 days.

 

Defendants filed and served the instant Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint on July 2, 2026. Defendants served Plaintiff with the Demurrer through Attorney Jonathan Easton by first-class and electronic mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.  

 

Discussion

 

Allegations in the First Amended Complaint

 

The First Amended Complaint alleges causes of action for (1) Contravention of Cal. Veh. Code § 22524 and Cal. Veh. Code § 22658 – Excessive and Presumptively Unreasonable Charges; (2) Contravention of Cal. Veh. Code § 22658 – Refusal to Accept Payment by Bank Debit or Credit Card; (3) Contravention of Cal. Civ. Code § 3070; (4) Conversion; (5) Intentional Misrepresentation; (6) Negligent Misrepresentation; and (8) Unfair Competition in violation of BPC § 17200, based on the following facts.

 

Plaintiff is a limited liability company and a division of Odorzx Inc. (“Odorzx”) (FAC, ¶1.) Plaintiff is wholly owned by Odorzx Inc. (Id. at ¶6.) The vehicle that is the subject of this action is a 2024 Nissan Sentra with VIN: 3N1AB8BV7RY287753 and CA registration plate: 9LCN156 (“the subject vehicle”). (Id. at ¶8.) Flex Drive Services, LLC (“Flex Drive”) is the owner of the subject vehicle. (Id. at ¶9.) Flex Drive is a client of Odorzx; as a wholly owned entity of Odorzx, Plaintiff operates as an agent for Flex Drive. (Id. at ¶9.) Flex Drive authorized Plaintiff to recover the vehicle. (Id. at ¶11 and Exh. 7.) “[E]ach of the Defendants was the agent, servant, partner, joint venturer, representative, servant, employee, managing agent, managing supervisor, and/or coconspirator of the remaining Defendants.” (Id. at ¶17.)

 

 On or about June 25, 2025, Defendants towed the Subject Vehicle from 10960 Hoover St, Los Angeles, CA 90044 to Defendant Kings Collision’s operating location at 7523 S Western Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90047. (Id. ¶19.) A purported tow authorization document is attached, but Plaintiff cannot trace the towing company, TowCo, that produced it. (Id. at ¶20 and Exh. 10.) On information and belief, TowCo is an illegal and unregistered towing enterprise. (Id. at ¶20.) Defendants and TowCo conspired in a criminal enterprise to take custody, fraudulently obtain possession, and convert the Subject Vehicle. (Id. at ¶22.) Defendants misrepresented their bona fides to the driver of the Subject Vehicle to fraudulently obtain possession of it. (Id. at ¶23.)

 

Defendants demanded payment for purported “impounds/storage fees,” “tow/hook fees,” “forklift fees,” “in house hazmat clean up fees,” and “lien sale fees.” (Id. at ¶13 and ¶9.) Plaintiff tendered the fee in the amount of $2,700.00. (Id. at ¶24.) The registered owner of the Subject Vehicle did not authorize these fees. (Id. at ¶25.)

 

Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint

 

The Demurrer is not accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. The Declaration of Demurring Party Regarding Meet and Confer filed by Defendants on July 2, 2026 details meet and confer efforts with respect to a First Amended Complaint served on Defendants on March 23, 2026. (Declaration, filed 07/02/26, Ekekeulu Decl., ¶¶2-7.) The declaration does not mention any meet and confer by the parties following service upon Defendants of the First Amended Complaint on May 22, 2026, which was different from the one served, but not filed, on March 23, 2026. (Ibid.) Although failing to meet and confer as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 is not grounds to overrule the Demurrer, it may be grounds to continue the hearing, place the matter off calendar, or strike the Demurrer for non-compliance with the statutory requirements. The parties are admonished that future non-compliance with the meet and confer mandate will result in the demurrer or other motion being stricken and the matter being taken off calendar.

 

Additionally, the memorandum of points and authorities in support of the Demurrer exceeds the number of pages allowed by law.  Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1113(d) limits the moving memorandum to 15 pages unless authorized by the Court, but Defendants have submitted 21 pages (pages 7 to 28). The Court, therefore, will limit its consideration of the Demurrer to the first 15 pages (pages 7 to 22).

 

The Demurrer is brought on the grounds of Plaintiff’s lack of standing / capacity to sue and failure to allege facts sufficient to state a cause of action. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subds. (a), (e).)

 

            Lack of Standing to Sue

 

Plaintiff’s purported lack of standing is grounds for a general demurrer. A person who has no interest in the subject matter of an action, and therefore no right to relief, has no standing and cannot state a cause of action, so a general demurrer will be sustained. (City of Industry v. City of Fillmore (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 191, 208 [citing Parker v. Bowron (1953) 40 Cal.2d 344, 351].)

 

The First Amended Complaint alleges that Flex Drive is the owner of the Subject Vehicle, and that Plaintiff is the authorized agent of Flex Drive. (FAC, ¶¶9-11 and Exh. 7.) Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s agency was limited to paying retrieval fees and for retrieval of the vehicle. The language of the “hold harmless” letter sent from Flex Drive to Defendant Kings Collision which the FAC attaches as proof of Plaintiff’s authorization to act supports this limitation on Plaintiff’s agency, as follows: “It is our intention to retrieve the vehicle from your tow yard using our Authorized Agent Erik Zambrano to pay any related fees in order to recover and return the vehicle back into our possession.” (FAC, Exh. 7, ¶2, emphasis added.) The scope of the agency conferred does not include the right to pursue legal claims. (See Cohen v. TNP 2008 Participating Notes Program, LLC (2019) 31 Cal. App. 5th 840, 856 (“agents ordinarily do not have ‘a cause of action based upon some third person’s violation of its principal’s rights’”) (citation omitted).) Even if it did, the letter does not make “Impound Compliance LLC” the authorized agent; it names an individual.  Further, the reference to “our possession” demonstrates that Flex Drive asserted its right to possession of the Subject Vehicle, and was not assigning a right of possession to the authorized agent.  

 

Therefore, the First Amended Complaint does not include allegations or incorporated exhibits that demonstrate Plaintiff’s interest in the Subject Vehicle supports its right to bring the causes of action asserted therein. The Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint for Plaintiff’s lack of standing is sustained.

 

            1st Cause of Action for Contravention of Cal. Veh. Code § 22524 and Cal. Veh. Code § 22658 – Excessive and Presumptively Unreasonable Charges

 

Defendants contend that Plaintiff has not alleged facts showing that Defendants charged excessive towing or storage fees, which is the basis for liability under Vehicle Code section 22658. At paragraph 35, the First Amended Complaint points to section 22658, subdivision (i)(1)(A), which provides:

 

A charge for towing or storage, or both, of a vehicle under this section is excessive if the charge exceeds the greater of the following:

 

(i) That which would have been charged for that towing or storage, or both, made at the request of a law enforcement agency under an agreement between a towing company and the law enforcement agency that exercises primary jurisdiction in the city…

 

(ii) That which would have been charged for that towing or storage, or both, under the rate approved for that towing operator by the Department of the California Highway Patrol for the jurisdiction in which the private property is located and from which the vehicle was, or was attempted to be, removed.

 

(Veh. Code, § 22658, subd. (i)(1)(A).) Plaintiff goes on to allege that in Los Angeles County, the rate set by law enforcement is $195.00 for towing and $60.00 per day. (Id. at ¶36.) However, it does not allege which law enforcement agency charges this rate, and between the law enforcement agency that exercises primary jurisdiction in the city and the rate approved for that towing operator by the Department of the California Highway Patrol, which rate is greater. Whether the rate charged by Defendants exceeds the greater rate, therefore, is not alleged, which is the basis for the statutory violation.

 

Additionally, Defendants argue that section 22658 is inapplicable to this action because it applies only when the owner or person in lawful possession of private property causes a vehicle to be removed. (Citing Cal. Veh. Code, § 22658, subd. (a).) The First Amended Complaint does not allege that the owner or person in lawful possession of private property caused the Subject Vehicle to be towed.

 

Plaintiff also alleges that Vehicle Code section 22524.5 identifies as presumptively unreasonable charges any administrative or filing fees, except those incurred related to documentation from the Department of Motor Vehicles and those related to the lien sale of a vehicle, security fees, dolly fees, load and unload fees, pull-out fees, and gate fees. (FAC, ¶39, citing Veh. Code, § 22542.5, subd. (c)(3).) However, Plaintiff does not allege which of the charges by Defendants identified in paragraph 24 and Exhibit 12 of the First Amended Complaint it contends fall within these presumptively unreasonable charges. (Id. at ¶¶40-41.) The Demurrer for failure to allege liability under either of these statutes is sustained, as well as for lack of standing.

 

            2nd Cause of Action for Contravention of Cal. Veh. Code § 22658 – Refusal to Accept Payment by Bank Debit or Credit Card

 

Defendants argue that the second cause of action fails to state a claim because it alleges that Defendants violated Veh. Code § 22658(k) by refusing to accept a credit card or debit card payment, whereas the statute requires that a person operating or in charge of a storage facility accept “a valid bank credit card or cash” for payment. (Citing Veh. Code, § 22658, subd. (k)(1)(emphasis added).)  Although subdivision (k)(1) uses the disjunctive “or,” subdivision (k)(3) makes it a misdemeanor to refuse to accept a valid credit card or post the required notice that credit cards are acceptable means of payment.  Reading these subdivisions together means that a storage facility must accept a valid credit card, otherwise the language of subdivision (k)(3) would not make sense.  Subdivision (o) affirms this construction, stating, “It is the intent of the Legislature in the adoption of subdivision (k) to assist vehicle owners or their agents by, among other things, allowing payment by credit cards for towing and storage services.”  (Cal. Veh. Code § 22658(o)(1).) ) Plaintiff sufficiently alleges that payment by bank debit and credit card was refused by Defendants. (FAC, ¶46.)

 

However, it remains that Plaintiff has not alleged standing to assert this cause of action, as discussed above.  The Demurrer is sustained for lack of standing.

 

             3rd Cause of Action for Contravention of Cal. Civ. Code § 3070

 

Civil Code section 3070 states in relevant part:

 

Any person who improperly causes a vehicle to be towed or removed in order to create or acquire a lienhold interest enforceable under this chapter, or who violates subdivision (c), shall forfeit all claims for towing, removal, or storage, and shall be liable to the owner or lessee of the vehicle for the cost of removal, transportation, and storage, damages resulting from the towing, removal, transportation, or storage of the vehicle, attorneys' fees, and court costs.

 

(Civ. Code, § 3070, subd. (d)(1), emphasis added.) Based on this statutory language, Defendants’ Demurrer for lack of standing is again sustained.

 

            4th Cause of Action for Conversion

 

A cause of action for conversion must allege (1) plaintiff’s ownership or right to possession of the property; (2) defendant’s wrongful act or disposition of property rights; and (3) damages. (Lee v. Hanley (2015) 61 Cal.4th 1225, 1240.) Defendants’ Demurrer to the conversion cause of action reiterates the standing argument. As discussed above, the Court has determined that Plaintiff fails to sufficiently allege its standing with respect to the Subject Vehicle.

 

The Demurrer to the fourth cause of action is sustained.

 

            5th Cause of Action for Intentional Misrepresentation

 

Defendants demur to this cause of action for lack of particularity. This requires “pleading facts which ‘show how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered.’” (Lazar v. Superior Court, 12 Cal. 4th 631, 645.) The First Amended Complaint attaches the specific communication from Defendant Kings Collision Center that is the alleged misrepresentation. On July 1, 2025, Ruki Adams, Manager of Defendant Kings Collision Center emailed Plaintiff the requested invoice and “signed tow authorization.” (FAC, ¶64 and Exh. 13.) However, the email does not state that “the towing, storage, and other fees charged were legitimate and that said charges were legally permissible” as Plaintiff alleges. (Ibid.) The First Amended Complaint, therefore, does not allege a misrepresentation regarding the legality of the charges in support of this cause of action.

 

The Demurrer to the fifth cause of action is sustained on this ground and as to standing.

 

            6th Cause of Action for Negligent Misrepresentation; 7th Cause of Action for Unfair Competition in Violation of B&P Code 17200

 

Defendants’ arguments with respect to these causes of action and the liability of Defendant Adams individually are outside the page limit, as discussed above. The Court, therefore, will not address them.  The Demurrer is sustained as to these counts based on standing for the reasons discussed above.

 

Leave to Amend

 

Plaintiff bears the burden of demonstrating what facts can be added to the pleading to correct the above defects. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) Plaintiff, however, filed no opposition to the instant Demurrer and the Court accordingly denies leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants Kings Collision Center, LLC’s and Azeez Adams’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is sustained without leave to amend.

 

Defendants are to file and serve a proposed judgment of dismissal within 20 days.

Case Number: 25STLC09269    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Shiswa Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Admissions, Set One; and Request for Sanctions, are denied without prejudice.

 

Defendant Shiswa Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; and Request for Sanctions, set for hearing on 08/13/2026, are advanced to today and denied without prejudice on the same grounds.

 

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

On December 12, 2025, Plaintiff Marcia Jacoba Colocho Garcia (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Shiswa Inc. (“Defendant Shiswa, Inc.”) and Danmar Motor Inn. Defendants filed their Answer to the Complaint on January 8, 2026. Defendant Shiswa then filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Admissions, Set One; and Request for Sanctions, on July 17, 2026. The Motions were served on Plaintiff by certified mail with return receipt requested on the same date. No opposition has been filed to date. Defendant Shiswa filed Notices of Non-Opposition on August 6, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant Shiswa Inc.’s discovery motions are procedurally and substantively defective such that the Court cannot grant them. First, the Motions were not served with the minimum statutory notice, as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 1005. Service by mail must be made at least 16 court days and five regular days before the hearing. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).) The instant Motions were served on July 17, 2026, which was only 16 court days and four regular days before the hearing.

 

Second, the discovery requests served on Plaintiff do not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 94, which allows only 35 written discovery requests in the Limited Civil Court unless otherwise authorized by court order or by stipulation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 94, subd. (a); § 95.) Defendant Shiswa Inc. served Plaintiff with more than 35 form interrogatories alone. (Motions, Hoover Decl., Exh. A.) The Court additionally notes that the form interrogatories served on Plaintiff include subparts, which is also a violation of subdivision (a)(1) of Code of Civil Procedure section 94.  The Court directs the parties’ attention to DISC-0004, which is the official court form for form interrogatories in limited civil cases.

 

Finally, Defendant Shiswa Inc.’s Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Admissions, Set One; and Request for Sanctions is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.260 and 2030.290, which pertain to interrogatories, not requests for admission.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds no basis to grant the instant discovery Motions.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Shiswa Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Admissions, Set One; and Request for Sanctions, are denied without prejudice.

 

Defendant Shiswa Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; and Request for Sanctions, set for hearing on 08/13/2026, are advanced to today and denied without prejudice on the same grounds.

Case Number: 26STLC01920    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendants Essex Portfolio, L.P. and Essex Property Trust,
Inc.’s Motion to Quash Alleged Service of Summons is
granted.  The Proofs of Service filed by
Plaintiff with respect to the Summons and Complaint on March 20, 2026, March
26, 2026, and April 1, 2026 are hereby ordered stricken.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to Serve Defendants with
Summons and Complaint is set for 09/02/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the
Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Analysis:

 

On March 20, 2026, Plaintiff Chris
Paul (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the Complaint
in this action against Defendants Essex Portfolio, L.P. and Essex
Property Trust, Inc. (erroneously sued herein as Essex Property Trust)
(“Defendants”). The Complaint alleges cause of action
for (1) Wrongful
Eviction (Illegal Lockout); (2) Violation of Civil Code 789.3; (3) Conversion;
and (4) Intention Infliction of Emotional Distress.

 

On
the same day, Plaintiff filed an Ex Parte Application to Restore Possession of
Premises. The Court denied the application without prejudice for lack of proper
notice upon Defendants. (Mintue Order, 03/23/26.) On March 27, 2026, the Court
granted (1) Ex Parte Application For Order Restoring Possession of Premesis
filed by Chris Paul on 03/24/2026; and (2) Ex Parte Application For Order
Restoring Possession of Premesis filed by Chris Paul on 03/26/2026. (Minute
Order, 03/27/26.) The Court issued a temporary restraining order requiring
Defendants to stop denying Plaintiff access to the premises and set a hearing
on preliminary injunction for April 17, 2026. (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff filed Proof of Service
by Substituted Service with respect to the Summons and Complaint upon
Defendants on April 1, 2026. On April 10, 2026, Plaintiff’s Ex Parte
Application for Order to Compel came for hearing and both parties appeared.
(Minute Order, 04/10/26.) The Court denied the Ex Parte Application for Order
to Compel, as follows: “The Court will not make a finding of contempt or issue
any further order to compel compliance at this time. The Court’s prior order
for a temporary restraining order is valid and enforceable. Defendants are
required by court order to allow Plaintiff back into the premises, as stated in
the March 27, 2026 Order, which was signed by this Court. . . . Self-help, by
locking Plaintiff out of the premises, is not a permissible means to contest
the TRO.” (Minute Order, 04/10/26, pp. 1-2.)

 

On April 17, 2026, the Court held
the Order to Show Cause Re: Why a Preliminary Injunction Should Not Issue to
Restore Plaintiff’s Possession of the Premises Located at 401 S. Detroit
Street, Apartment 114, Los Angeles, CA 90036, and to Order Defendants to Cease
Interfering with Plaintiff’s Access to the Premises. (Minute Order, 04/17/26.)
Only Plaintiff appeared. (Ibid.) The Court granted the preliminary
injunction and ordered that it would remain in effect until February 10, 2027.
(Ibid.) The Court also set an Order to Show Cause Re: Why Defendants
Should Not Pay Reasonable Expenses for Failure to Comply with the 03/27/2026
Minute Order for June 18, 2026. (Ibid.)

 

On May 5, 2026, Plaintiff’s Application
for an Order to Show Cause Re: Contempt came for hearing and the Court set an Order
to Show Cause Re: Monetary Sanctions Under Code of Civil Procedure § 177.5 as
to Each Defendant for Violation of (i) The TRO and (ii) The Preliminary
Injunction, for Total Potential Sanctions of $3,000 as to Each Defendant for
May 12, 2026. (Minute Order, 05/05/26.) On May 12, 2026, again, Plaintiff was
the only party to appear. (Minute Order, 05/12/26.) The Court ordered each
Defendant to pay sanctions of $1,500.00 for violation of the TRO and $1,500.00
for violation of the preliminary injunction, by May 26, 2026. (Minute Order,
05/12/26.)

 

Defendants, specially appearing, filed
an Ex Parte Application to Reconsider and Dissolve the Preliminary Injunction
and Vacate the Order Imposing Monetary Sanctions Against Defendants or, in the
Alternative, to Shorten Time or Specially Set a Hearing on the Motions, on May
21, 2026. The Court denied the Application to shorten time, but set the matter
for hearing on June 11, 2026 upon Defendants’ subsequent showing of proper
notice upon Plaintiff. (Minute Order, 05/26/26.) On June 11, 2026, the Court
determined that Defendants gave proper notice of the hearing to Plaintiff and continued
the matter as an evidentiary hearing set for June 30, 2026. (Minute Order,
06/11/26.) Also on that date, the parties agreed to accept electronic service.
(Ibid.)

 

Following the evidentiary hearing,
the Court dissolved the preliminary injunction, set aside and vacated all
sanctions against Defendants, and discharged the Order to Show Cause Re: Why
Defendants Should Not Pay Reasonable Expenses for Failure to Comply with the
03/27/2026 Minute Order. (Minute Order, 06/30/26.) Defendants filed and
electronically served the instant Motion to Quash Alleged Service of Summons on
July 15, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Defendants bring the Motion to Quash pursuant to
Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, subdivision (a)(1), which states in
relevant part: “A
defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any
further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a
notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: To quash service of
summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1), emphasis added.)
A defendant has 30 days after the service of the summons to file a responsive
pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 412.20,
subd. (a)(3).)  

 

An
appearance at a hearing at which ex parte relief is sought is not a general
appearance and does not constitute a waiver of the right to make a motion under
Section 418.10.  (Code Civ. Proc., §
418.11.) Defendants’ appearance pursuant to the hearing set on their Ex Parte
Application to Dissolve the TRO and Vacate the Order Imposing Monetary
Sanctions, therefore does not waive the ability to file this motion.

 

The
Court finds good cause for the timing of the Motion, in light of the procedural
history recounted above. Defendants filed the instant Motion within two weeks
of the evidentiary hearing set to resolve Defendant’s Ex Parte Application to
Dissolve the TRO and Vacate the Order Imposing Monetary Sanctions.

 

Substantively,
“[w]hen a defendant challenges the court’s personal jurisdiction on the ground of improper
service of process ‘the burden is on the plaintiff to prove the existence of
jurisdiction by proving, inter alia, the facts requisite to an effective
service.’” (Summers v. McClanahan (2006) 140 Cal.App.4th
403, 413.) A proof of service containing a declaration from a registered
process server invokes a rebuttable presumption affecting the burden of
producing evidence, of the facts stated in the return. (Cal. Evid. Code, § 647;
see American
Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383,
390.)

 

Defendants
challenge the three proofs of service filed by Plaintiff with respect to the
Summons and Complaint on March 20, 2026, March 26, 2026, and April 1, 2026.  All of the challenged proofs of service are
deficient.

 

The
March 20, 2026 proof of service purports to demonstrate service upon both
Defendants by mail at 915 Wilshire Blvd., Los Angeles, California. (Proof of
Service, filed 03/20/26, ¶¶4-6.) The proof of service is defective in a number
of respects. First, it attempts to improperly demonstrate service upon two
different Defendants in a single affidavit. The statute for demonstrating proof
of service of the summons refers to persons and parties in the singular,
thereby requiring a separate proof of service for each Defendant. (See Code
Civ. Proc., § 417.10.) Second, service by mail alone is not an authorized
method of service of a summons. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 415.30) Third, it does
not indicate upon whom service was made on behalf of the corporate Defendants,
as required to demonstrate compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section
416.10. In fact, the proof of service used by Plaintiff specifically states
that it must not be used to show service of a summons and complaint. (Proof of
Service, filed 03/20/26, p. 1.) The correct (and mandatory) proof of service
form includes a section to list the name of the person to whom the papers were
delivered, as well as the party upon whom service was made. (Judicial Council
Form, POS-010, ¶3.) The March 20, 2026 proof of service, therefore, does not
demonstrate service of the Summons and Complaint upon Defendants in accordance
with the statutory requirements.

 

Plaintiff
then filed a proof of service on March 26, 2026 using the correct form, but indicating
substitute service upon “Anne Morrison Agent for Service of Process” as the
party. (Proof of Service, filed 03/26/26, ¶3a.) Therefore, the proof of service
does not demonstrate service upon either Defendant Essex Portfolio, L.P.,
nor Essex Property Trust, Inc. In addition to failing to state that Defendants
were the parties served with the papers, the proof of service does not list in
the proper location the name of the person who was served on behalf of the
corporate Defendants, nor show that the documents were thereafter mailed to the
service address. (Id. at ¶¶3b, 5b(4).) Substitute service is only
complete on the tenth day after the papers are mailed. (Code Civ. Proc., §
415.20.) Therefore, the March 26, 2026 proof of service also fails to
demonstrate service of the Summons and Complaint upon either Defendant as
required by statute.

 

Finally, another proof of
substitute service was filed on April 1, 2026, which again improperly refers to
service on both Defendants. (Proof of Service, filed 04/01/26, ¶3a.) This proof
of service states that the papers were served on Defendants by leaving them
with Anne Morrison, Agent of Service, at 1100 Park Place, #200, San Mateo,
California, on March 30, 2026. (Id. at ¶¶4-5.) It also states that the
papers were mailed to the service address on the same day. (Id. at ¶5c.)
Defendants dispute service in this manner was ever accomplished. Their Vice-President
and Associate General Counsel, Michael Beler, declares that no papers were ever
served on a person employed by Defendants at 1100 Park Place, including Anne
Morrison. (Motion, Beler Decl., ¶4.) Nor were the papers mailed to that
address. (Ibid.) The April 1, 2026 proof of service is not attested to
by a registered process server, placing the burden of proving effective service
upon Plaintiff. (Proof of Service, filed 04/01/26, ¶7.) Plaintiff, however, has
not filed an opposition to the instant Motion to demonstrate that service was
accomplished upon either Defendant as stated in the proof of service.  

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds Plaintiff has failed
to demonstrate service of the Summons and Complaint upon Defendants in
accordance with the statutory requirements. The Court quashes service of the
Summons upon Defendants, without which it has no personal jurisdiction over them.
(Dill v. Berquist Const. Co., Inc. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1433.)

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants Essex Portfolio, L.P. and Essex Property Trust,
Inc.’s Motion to Quash Alleged Service of Summons is
granted.  The Proofs of Service filed by
Plaintiff with respect to the Summons and Complaint on March 20, 2026, March
26, 2026, and April 1, 2026 are hereby ordered stricken.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to Serve Defendants with
Summons and Complaint is set for 09/02/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the
Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 26STLC02476    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff CF Ascent II Trust’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is granted.

 

The proposed judgment filed on 07/06/2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On April 13, 2026, Plaintiff CF Ascent II Trust (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Jose Villalobos (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint, in propria persona, on May 22, 2026.

 

On July 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings. The Motion was served on Defendant by first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for judgment on the pleadings, the standard for which is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer. That is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.  (Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.) Matters which are subject to mandatory judicial notice may be treated as part of the complaint and may be considered without notice to the parties. Matters which are subject to permissive judicial notice must be specified in the notice of motion, the supporting points and authorities, or as the court otherwise permits. (Id.)  The motion may not be supported by extrinsic evidence. (Barker v. Hull (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 221, 236.)

 

A statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, et seq. must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 439 [moving party must file declaration demonstrating an attempt to meet and confer in person or by telephone, at least five days before the date a motion for judgment on the pleadings is filed].)

The Motion is supported by a meet and confer declaration. (Motion, D’Anna Decl., Exh. 1.)

 

Substantively, the Motion is brought on the grounds that the Answer fails to allege sufficient facts to raise any defense to the Complaint for breach of contract. The elements of a cause of action for breach of contract are (1) the existence of contract; (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance; (3) defendant’s breach (or anticipatory breach); and (4) resulting damage. (Wall Street Network, Ltd. v. N. Y. Times Co. (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1171, 1178.)

The Complaint alleges that Defendant breached a loan agreement entered into with Plaintiff’s assignor, thereby causing Plaintiff to incur damages of $26,502.72, plus costs to which it has a contractual right under paragraph 5c of the loan agreement. (Compl., ¶¶ 1, 6-10, 15-26 and Exhs. A-C.) The Answer admits the material allegations of the Complaint and only alleges facts regarding Defendant’s financial hardship. (Answer, ¶¶3b, 6c.) The Answer does not deny the material allegations in the Complaint that support the cause of action for breach of contract. Based on Defendant’s admission of fault in the Answer and the lack of any opposition to the instant Motion, the Court finds that Plaintiff is entitled to judgment on the pleadings.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff CF Ascent II Trust’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is granted.

 

The proposed judgment filed on 07/06/2026 is signed and entered this date.
