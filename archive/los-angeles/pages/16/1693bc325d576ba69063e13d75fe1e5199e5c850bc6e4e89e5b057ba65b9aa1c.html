SAADIA SQUARE LLC v. ALL-WAYS PACIFIC, LLC

CASE NO.:  21NWCV00133

HEARING: Tuesday, July 28, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Cross-Complainant’s Motion to for Assignment and a Restraining Order is GRANTED.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Procedural History

 

This action concerning a right of first offer to purchase a property (“ROFO”) was filed by Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant SAADIA SQUARE LLC (“Saadia”) on March 5, 2021.

 

On March 3, 2025, a jury trial commenced.

 

On March 6, 2025, outside the presence of the jury, counsel indicated that Saadia and Cross-Complainants Rialto Merrill Holdings, LLC; Rialto Distribution LLC; RD Real Estate Holdings LLC; Rialto Pacific LLC had executed a confidential settlement with the inclusion of CCP §664.6 for the Court’s reservation of jurisdiction over the terms of the settlement.

 

Trial thereafter proceeded between Saadia Square and Defendant SM Logistics Member, LLC (“Managing Member”).

 

The jury rendered their special verdict on March 18, 2025. On May 15, 2025, Judgment was entered after trial on the jury’s special verdicts in this action.

 

On July 25, 2025, this Court issued rulings on the parties’ various Post-Judgment Motions. (See M.O., 07/25/25).

 

On July 28, 2025, an Amended Final Judgment was entered.

 

On August 8, 2025, Saadia filed a Notice of Appeal of the Judgments or Orders entered on: September 26, 2023; January 11, 2025; January 13, 2025; May 15, 2025; July 25, 2025; and July 26, 2025.

 

On August 19, 2025, Managing Member filed a Notice of Cross-Appeal of the Judgments or Orders entered on: May 15, 2025; July 25, 2025; July 28, 2025 (Amended Judgment); and July 28, 2025 (Order on Ex Parte Application).

 

Now, Rialto asks that all rights of Plaintiff Saadia Square LLC, entered in favor of Saadia and against SM Logistics Members, LLC be assigned to Rialto until Rialto’s judgment is fully satisfied.

 

Assignment

 

Pursuant to CCP § 708.510, a judgment creditor may apply to the court for an order requiring the judgment debtor to assign to the creditor or a receiver appointed under CCP § 708.610, et seq., all or part of the debtor's right to a payment due or to become due, whether or not the right is conditioned on future developments. (CCP § 708.510(a); See, e.g., Philippine Export and Foreign Loan Guarantee Corp. v. Chuidian (1990) 218 Cal.App.3d 1058, 1098-1099.)

 

Under CCP § 708.510, the creditor must apply to the court on noticed motion and serve notice on the judgment debtor, either personally or by mail. (CCP § 708.510(a),(b).) CCP § 708.510(a) provides that the assignment order procedure is applicable to, but not limited to, the following types of payments: (1) Federal wages not subject to withholding under an earnings withholding order; (2) Rents, commissions, and royalties; (3) Payments due from a patent or copyright; (4) The loan value of an insurance policy. (Id.)

 

Subject to limitations on recovery under an assignment order, the court may consider all relevant factors in determining whether an assignment is appropriate, which include: (1) the reasonable requirements of a debtor who is a natural person and of persons supported by the debtor; (2) payments the debtor is required to make or that are deducted to satisfy other judgments and wage assignments, including earnings assignment orders for support; (3) the remaining amount due on the money judgment; (4) the amount received to satisfy the right to payment that may be assigned. (CCP § 708.510(c).)

 

When an application for an assignment order is pending, the creditor may apply for an order restraining the debtor from assigning or otherwise disposing of the right to payment that is sought to be assigned. (CCP § 708.520(a).) The application may be made ex parte, unless the court or a court rule requires a noticed motion. (Id.) The court may issue the order on a showing of need, may also require the creditor to provide an undertaking, and may modify or vacate the order at any time with or without a hearing. (CCP § 708.520(b),(c).) The order must be personally served on the debtor and must contain a notice that failure to comply may subject the debtor to being held in contempt. (CCP § 708.520(d).)

 

In response, SBM TRADING, LLC (SBM) argues that the pending appeal and CCP §§ 708.410-4.80 sections preclude assignment. However, CCP § 708.510 contains no limitation or exception for judgments on appeal. Indeed, CCP § 708.510 authorizes assignment, “whether or not the right is conditioned on future developments.” (Id.)

 

Further, SBM’s reliance on Pangborn Plumbing Corp. is inapposite, that case concerned attorney’s contingent, contractual lien over the settlement proceeds for fees not yet earned and not assignment. (Pangborn Plumbing Corp. v. Carruthers & Skiffington (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 1039, 1055.)

 

The Court also rejects SBM’s reliance on Buckeye, where the Court found the creditor could not split his demand, and by assignment of a portion thereof impose the debtor the legal obligation of paying the assignee. (Buckeye Refining Co. v. Kelly (1912) 163 Cal.8, 12-13.) CCP § 708.510(d) also supports partial assignment as it states, “A right to payment may be assigned pursuant to this article only to the extent necessary to satisfy the money judgment. (Id.)

 

The Court finds that assignment is proper. Indeed, Saadia procured judgment in the amount of $40,491.173.30 against SM Logistics Member, LLC. Rialto obtained judgment in the amount of $9,216.656.07 against Saadia. Saadia has not satisfied any part of the judgment.

 

Rialto provided sufficient evidence that it has a right to payment, that Saadia has not made payments, and that the amount does not exceed the amount necessary to satisfy the money judgment.

 

Rialto’s Motion for Assignment is GRANTED.

 

Restraining Order

 

Rialto further asks the Court to restrain Saadia from assigning or otherwise disposing of the right to payment.

 

CCP § 708.520 authorizes a court to restrain a judgment debtor from assigning or otherwise disposing of the right to any payment sought to be assigned upon a showing of need. 

 

Here, Rialto argues that Saadia’s former counsel, filed a notice of pre-judgement right to attach and writ of attachment as to all assets subject to levy on Saadia in the amount of $591,585.28, for legal fees Saadia failed to pay its attorneys.

 

The Court agrees with Rialto that a restraining order is necessary to ensure Saadia complies with its judgment against Rialto.

 

Accordingly, Rialto’s Motion for a Restraining Order is GRANTED.

CISNEROS, ET AL. v. WHITTIER CITY SCHOOL DISTRICT, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 24NWCV01043

HEARING:  Tuesday, July 28, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

I.             Plaintiff AGUSTINA CISNEROS Motion to Quash Subpoenas for Production of Business Records Served on Plaintiff’s Medical Providers is GRANTED.

II.            Plaintiff AGUSTINA CISNEROS Motion to Quash Subpoenas for Production of Business Records Served on Plaintiff’s Medical Providers is GRANTED.

III.          Plaintiff AGUSTINA CISNEROS Motion to Quash Subpoenas for Production of Business Records Served on Plaintiff’s Medical Providers is GRANTED.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Background

On April 4, 2024, Plaintiffs AGUSTINA CISNEROS and MICHAEL CHAVARRIA-CISNEROS filed this Motor Vehicle action against Defendants WHITTIER CITY SCHOOL DISTRICT (“Defendant”); ROBERTO PETER PINEDA; and DOES 1 to 20, inclusive.

 

Plaintiffs allege, “On or about April 12, 2024, Plaintiffs AGUSTINA CISNEROS and MICHAEL CHAVARRIA-CISNEROS were lawfully stopped while operating their motor vehicle, southbound on Workman Mill Rd. in lane #1, at or near the intersection of Workman Mill Rd. and North Drive in or near the City of Whittier, in the County of Los Angeles, in the State of California, when Defendant ROBERTO PETER MEDINA, while in the course and scope of his employment with Defendant WHITTIER CITY SCHOOL DISTRICT, and DOES 1 to 20, inclusive, negligently and carelessly failed to keep a proper lookout and negligently and carelessly operated their vehicle at excessive speeds so as to cause this collision between Defendant's vehicle and Plaintiffs' vehicle. Defendants ROBERTO PETER MEDINA, and DOES 1 to 20, inclusive, and each of them, caused their vehicle forcefully and violently collided with the rear of Plaintiffs AGUSTINA CISNEROS and MICHAEL CHAVARRIA-CISNEROS' vehicle, resulting in injuries and damages to Plaintiffs.” (Complaint at p. 4.)

 

Plaintiff now moves to quash deposition subpoenas served on Plaintiff AGUSTINA CISNEROS’ (“Plaintiff”) medical providers.

 

Motion to Quash

 

“If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b),… may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person. (CCP §1987.1(a).)

 

Plaintiff filed three different motions to quash deposition subpoenas. For the ease of Court and Counsel, the Court will reference each motion by Reservation ID.

 

Reservation ID: 699289592255 and 754820954446[1]

 

Plaintiff contends these deposition subpoenas are overbroad and infringe on Plaintiff’s right to privacy.

 

Privacy

 

In ruling on a privacy objection, the party asserting a privacy right must establish a legally protected privacy interest, an objectively reasonable expectation of privacy in the given circumstances, and a threatened intrusion that is serious. (Williams v. Sup. Ct. (2017) 3 Cal. 5th 531, 552.) The party asserting a privacy right must also establish an objectively reasonably expectation of privacy in the given circumstances. (Id.) “The party seeking information may raise in response whatever legitimate and important countervailing interests disclosure serves, while the party seeking protection may identify feasible alternatives that serve the same interests or protective measures that would diminish the loss of privacy. A court must then balance these competing considerations.” (Id.)

 

The subpoena at issue generally seek Plaintiff’s medical records. For instance, Subpoena to the Custodian of Records for Emanate Health Queen of the Valley Hospital seeks the following: “Any and all medical documents, paper and digital records pertaining to the care, treatment and examination of Agustina Cisneros born on 3/23/1981, with SS# Unk.; including, but not limited to, all office, emergency room, inpatient and outpatient charts and records either in hard copy or electronic and/or faced from any and all dates. Including all electronic communications from and to the patient.  (Plaintiff’s Separate Statement at p. 2.)

 

“It is settled that a person’s medical history, including psychological records, falls within the zone of informational privacy protected under Section 1.” (People v. Martinez (2001) 88 Cal.App.4th 465, 474-475.)  Thus, before the discovery will be allowed, the opposing parties must identify what legitimate and important interests would be served by the disclosure.

 

Here, the Court agrees with Plaintiff that broad reaching subpoenas of Plaintiff’s medical records violate her right to privacy. Indeed, the Court finds Defendant can limit its request to body parts Plaintiff alleges to have sustained injury to.

 

Furthermore, Defendant’s request for medical records from the past 10 years is overbroad and unnecessary. The Court finds it more appropriate for Defendant to request medical records from the past 5 years.

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash is GRANTED.

 

Reservation ID: 715177170748

 

Non-Party Medical and Billing Records

 

Any party may obtain any discovery of information, documents, land, property or electronically stored information so long as the discoverable matter is not privileged, is relevant to the subject matter and can lead one to admissible evidence. (CCP § 2017.010.) 

 

Defendant contends the documents sought are necessary to assess the “reasonableness of Plaintiff’s medical expenses.” The Court disagrees.

 

The Court does not find that non-party records are relevant to any claim or defense in this action. Moreover, Defendant does not need non-party’s records to determine if Plaintiff’s medical expenses were reasonable. Instead, Defendant can rely on evidence of referral agreements or ask an expert to evaluate Plaintiff’s claimed expenses.

 

Referral Evidence

 

Defendant’s request for patient referrals by BD&J is relevant. Indeed, referral evidence is relevant because it may show bias or financial incentives on the part of lien-physicians. (Qaadir v. Figueroa (2021) 67 Cal.App.5th 790, 808.) The Court further finds that any privacy issues are mitigated by deleting any identifying information within these referral requests.

 

Attorney Client Privilege

 

The attorney-client privilege authorizes a client to refuse to disclose, and to prevent others from disclosing, confidential communications between attorney and client. (Mitchell v. Superior Court (1984) 37 Cal. 3d 591, 599.) The evidentiary privilege, while not constitutionally based, is the oldest recognized confidential communication privilege. (Sullivan v. Superior Court (1972) 29 Cal.App.3d 64, 71.) The privilege applies not only to communications made in anticipation of litigation, but also to legal advice when no litigation is threatened. (Roberts v. City of Palmdale (1993) 5 Cal.4th 363, 371.) The term “confidential communication” is broadly construed, and defined as either information transmitted between a client and his lawyer or advice given by the lawyer. The privilege does not protect independent facts related to a communication, that a communication took place, and the time, date and participants in the communication. Further, the privilege does not protect disclosure of underlying facts which may be referenced within a qualifying communication, and it does not extend to independent witnesses. (State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 54 Cal.App.4th 625, 639-640.)

 

Here, none of the requests seek communications between Plaintiff and Counsel and thus attorney-client privilege is inapplicable.

 

Work-Product

 

Writings containing attorney impressions, conclusions, and opinions are absolutely protected.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2018.030(a).)  Other word product is subject to qualified protection—that is, the information is discoverable if “denial of discovery will unfairly prejudice the party seeking discovery in preparing that party’s claim or defense or will result in an injustice.”  (CCP. § 2018.030(b).)  

 

The Court does not find the work-product doctrine is implicated as the subpoena seeks documents belonging to and prepared by medical entities and not attorneys.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s Motions to Quash are GRANTED.

 

Defendant may modify its subpoena and seek documents related to referrals/referral agreements by BD&J and communications related to those referrals. Defendant may not subpoena medical or billing records of other patients.

 

Defendant may also limit its medical subpoena to injuries Plaintiff has placed at issue and limit the request to records from 2021 onward.

SAENZ v. GENERAL MOTORS LLC

HEARING: Tuesday, July 28, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Defendant GENERAL MOTORS LLC’s Motion for Compliance is DENIED as MOOT. Sanctions are awarded.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Background

 

On May 29, 2025, Plaintiff VANESSA SAENZ (“Plaintiff”) filed this “lemon law” action against Defendant GENERAL MOTORS LLC (“Defendant” or “GM”) and DOES 1 through 10.

 

Plaintiff’s Complaint alleges the following causes of action:

(1) Violation of Civil Code § 1793.2(d)

(2) Violation of Civil Code § 1793.2(b)

(3) Violation of Civil Code § 1793.2(A)(3)

(4) Breah of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability

(5) Fraudulent Inducement – Concealment

 

On February 10, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for Compliance.

 

Deposition Requirements

 

Within 120 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties have the right to conduct initial depositions, each not to exceed two hours, of the following deponents:

(1) The plaintiff.

(2) The defendant, and if the defendant is not a natural person, the person who is most qualified to testify on the defendant's behalf. 

(CCP § 871.26(c)(1),(2).)

 

Defendant filed its Answer on September 23, 2025. Defendants had a right to depose Plaintiff by January 21, 2026.

 

After Defendant filed the instant motion, the parties agreed to depose Plaintiff on July 20, 2026. Thus, Defendant’s Motion for Compliance is MOOT.

 

 

However, sanctions are mandatory as Plaintiff failed to demonstrate good cause in failing to appear for deposition within 120 days of Defendant’s answer. (CCP 871.26(j)(1).) The Court is required to impose sanctions of $1,500. (CCP 871.26(j)(1).) Defendant’s request for Sanction is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff’s attorney is ORDERED to pay Moving Party and their counsel of record sanctions in the total amount of $1,500, payable with 15 business days of the Court’s issuance of this order.

LEIVA, ET AL. v. LNW APARTMENTS, LLC., ET AL.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV01642

HEARING: Tuesday, July 28, 2026, at 9:30 AM  

 

Defendant LNW APARTMENTS, LLC’s Motion to Compel Defendant Compel Plaintiffs Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One, Request No. 22 is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On May 6, 2025, Plaintiffs AILYN LEIVA, an individual and Guadian ad Litem for MARIELA SANCHEZ LEIVA; ADRIANNA SANCHEZ; ANTHONY SANCHEZ; MARIELA SANCHEZ LEIVA, a minor child by and through her Guardian ad Litem, AILYN LEIVA; CRISTIAN IBARRA; ACE IBARRA; ZOLIA IBARRA; KERI BLANCO; DAMIAN BLANCO; and NADIA MORALES (“Plaintiffs”) filed this real property action against Defendants LNW APARTMENTS, LLC (“Defendant”); YUNGLIN JOHN WEN; CHUNG-HSIUNG WEN; and DOES 1 through 25.

 

On February 19, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”).

 

Plaintiffs’ FAC alleges the following causes of action:

(1) Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability;

(2) Tortious Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability;

(3) Negligence;

(4) Private Nuisance; and

(5) Violation of Civ. Code §1942.4

 

On June 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to Compel Plaintiffs’ Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One, No. 22.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS

 

With respect to requests for production of documents, there are only three appropriate ways to respond: (1) a statement that the party will comply with the request, (2) a statement of inability to comply, or (3) an objection. (CCP  §2031.210.)

 

Request 22 seeks:

 

“For the period from January 1, 2021, to the present, produce documents sufficient to identify each healthcare provider, date of service, general category of treatment, amount billed, amount paid, and patient-responsibility amount for any medical or mental-health treatment that plaintiffs contend was caused by, contributed to, or aggravated by the alleged mold exposure, water intrusion, unsanitary conditions, or other alleged habitability defects at the subject property.” (Leong Decl., Ex. I.)[1]

 

Defendant further specified: “this request does not include plaintiffs’ health insurance policies, premium details, enrollment records, benefit election forms, applications, dependent or family coverage data, Social Security numbers, tax information, unrelated financial records, or unrelated medical and mental health histories. If plaintiffs believe it necessary, they may redact any unrelated diagnoses, treatments, providers, dependent or family details, full policy numbers, birth dates, addresses, and other personal identifiers that are not essential for identifying the provider, date of service, general treatment category, or claimed medical expenses.” (Id.)

 

Plaintiffs asserted the following response to each request: “Plaintiffs object in that this request is overboard, overly-intrusive, and burdensome. The request is not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence and calls for information which violates Responding Partys’ right to privacy. Further, this request seeks information in violation of the collateral source rule.”

 

Plaintiffs’ boilerplate objections are unfounded. Indeed, the request is limited in time and in scope. Defendant only seeks documents from the past five years and limits the request to medical or mental-health treatment Plaintiffs contend was necessary due to Defendant’s alleged negligence.

 

Privacy

 

The right of privacy protects a person’s work history and personnel records. (Alch v. Superior Court (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 1412, 1427.) However, third party’s right of privacy is not absolute and may be abridged when there is a compelling and opposing state interest. (Board of Trustees, Etc. v. Superior Court (1981) 119 Cal.App.3d 516, 525; see also Binder v. Superior Court (1987) 196 Cal.App.3d 893, 900.) One such competitive state interest is facilitating in the ascertainment of truth pertaining to legal proceedings. (Binder, supra, 196 Cal.App.3d at 900.) 

 

By filing a personal injury action, plaintiffs place in issue their past and present physical and/or mental conditions related to the injury sued upon.¿¿All medical and/or psychological records relating to the claimed injuries are thus discoverable.¿¿Evidence Code §§ 996, 1016; Britt v. Superior Court (1978) 20 Cal.3d 844, 862–864.¿¿Normally, information about medical conditions entirely different from the injury sued upon is beyond the scope of discovery.¿¿However, medical records¿pertaining to¿an unrelated condition are discoverable on a showing of “good cause” if the condition is relevant to the issue of proximate causation.¿¿(Evidence Code §999; Slagle v. Superior Court (1989) 211 Cal.App.3d 1309, 1314–1315 [good cause shown by info that plaintiff was blind 6 months before accident].)¿ 

 

Here, the request is limited to medical or mental-health treatment Plaintiffs contend was necessary due to Defendant’s negligence. In other words, it relates to Plaintiffs’ claimed injuries and thus is discoverable.

 

Collateral Source Rule

 

Finally, Plaintiffs contend the collateral source rule bars production. Under the traditional collateral source rule, a jury, in calculating a plaintiff’s damages in a tort action, does not take into consideration benefits – such as medical insurance or disability payments – which the plaintiff has received from sources other than the defendant – i.e., “collateral sources” – to cover losses resulting from the injury. (Fein v. Permanente Medical Group (1985) 38 Cal.3d 137, 164.)

 

However, admissibility is not the test for discovery. (See CCP §2017.010) (“Unless otherwise limited by order of the court in accordance with this title, any party may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, that is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action or to the determination of any motion made in that action, if the matter either is itself admissible in evidence or appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence…”). Plaintiffs confuse admissibility with relevance.

 

The information sought could lead to the discovery of other injuries or allow Defendant to verify Plaintiffs’ medical payments.

 

Conclusion

 

Finding Plaintiffs’ response insufficient, Defendant’s Motion is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiffs, each of them, shall serve verified, code-compliant responses to Defendant’s Request for Production of Documents, Set One, No. 22, without objection within 30 days of the Court’s issuance of this order.

 

To the extent any document is protected by privilege, Plaintiffs may produce a privilege log.

ESCARTIN v. ALDI, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV02647

HEARING:   Tuesday, July 28, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

     I.        Defendant AI CALIFORNIA LLC’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff FILIBERTA ESCARTIN’s Response to Form Interrogatories, Set One is GRANTED.

    II.        Defendant AI CALIFORNIA LLC’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff FILIBERTA ESCARTIN’s Response to Special Interrogatories, Set One is GRANTED.

  III.        Defendant AI CALIFORNIA LLC’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff FILIBERTA ESCARTIN’s Response to Request for Production of Documents, Set One is GRANTED.

 

 

Moving Party to give Notice.

 

Background

 

Defendant AI CALIFORNIA LLC (“Defendant”) moves to: Compel Plaintiff FILIBERTA ESCARTIN’s (“Plaintiff”) Response to (1) Request for Production of Documents, Set One; (2) Special Interrogatories, Set One and (3) Form Interrogatories, Set One.

 

Legal Standard

 

“If a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to serve a timely response to it, the following rules shall apply: (a) The party to whom the demand… is directed waives any objection to the demand, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product…. The court, on motion, may relieve that party from this waiver on its determination that both of the following conditions are satisfied: (1) The party has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance…. (2) The party’s failure to serve a timely response was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.” (CCP §2031.300.)  

 

“If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the following rules apply: (a) The party to whom the interrogatories are directed waives any right to exercise the option to produce writings under Section 2030.230, as well as any objection to the interrogatories, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product…. The court, on motion, may relieve that party from this waiver on its determination that both of the following conditions are satisfied: (1) The party has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance…. (2) The party’s failure to serve a timely response was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.” (CCP §2030.290.)  

 

If a party to whom interrogatories and document demands are directed fails to respond at all, the propounding party’s remedy is to seek a court order compelling answers thereto. (CCP §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) All that needs to be shown is that the discovery was properly served on the opposing party, that the time to respond has expired, and that no response of any kind has been served. The moving party is not required to show a reasonable and good faith attempt to resolve the matter informally before filing this motion. A motion to compel initial discovery responses need not show good cause, meeting and conferring, or timely filing, and need not be accompanied by a separate statement. (See Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pac. Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 404.)  

 

On April 10, 2026, Defendant propounded onto Plaintiff the following discovery: Form Interrogatories, Set One; Special Interrogatories, Set One; and Request for Production of Documents, Set One.

 

Plaintiff’s responses were due on May 12, 2026. Plaintiff failed to provide a response.  

 

Therefore, Defendant’s Motions are GRANTED, and Plaintiff FILIBERTA ESCARTIN is ORDERED to provide verified responses and documents, without objection by no later than 30 days from date of the Court’s issuance of this Order.

 

This date may be extended by stipulation of the parties. If any objections are asserted, it will be tantamount to no response at all and will be deemed a violation of this Court’s order.

 

Reasonable sanctions are GRANTED in the total amount of $400.00. Plaintiff FILIBERTA ESCARTIN is ORDERED to pay Moving Party and their counsel of record sanctions in the total amount of $400.00, payable within 60 days from the date of the Court’s issuance of this Order.

SOLARES, ET AL.  v. CITY OF BELL

CASE NO.:  25NWCV04639

HEARING:  Tuesday, July 28, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Defendant CITY OF BELL’s Demurrer to Plaintiffs’ Complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Background

 

On December 30, 2025, Plaintiffs MARIA ANTONIA SOLARES, an individual, as surviving spouse and heir of JOSE LUIS ARIAS BARRIENTOS; JOSE LUIS ARIAS JR.; ADRIANA ELIZABETH ARIAS; MARLENIE JUDITH ARIAS; LESLY MARIBEL CASTREJON; LILIAN PETRONA PEREZ; AND BYRON STACEY SOLARES (“Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendants CITY OF BELL and DOES through 50, inclusive.

 

Plaintiffs’ alleges the following causes of action:

(1) Dangerous Condition of Public Property;

(2) Survival Action;

(3) Negligence (Wrongful Death)

 

On February 13, 2026, Defendant CITY OF BELL (“Defendant”) filed the instant demurrer.

 

Survival Action

 

Defendant demurs to Plaintiffs’ Survival Action claim arguing that Plaintiffs failed to present their Survival Action claim in accordance with the Government Claims Act.

 

 A public entity is not liable for injury, whether such injury arises out of an act or omission of the public entity or a public employee or any other person except as provided by statute. (Gov. Code §815(a).) Because all public entity liability is limited and statutory, a heightened pleading standard applies: every fact material to the existence of statutory liability must be pleaded with particularity. (City of Los Angeles v. Superior Court (2021) 62 Cal.App.5th 129, 138.) Thus, a Plaintiff must pled a statute that authorizes liability to state a claim against a public entity. (Ibid.) 

 

“Except as provided in Sections 946.4 and 946.6, no suit for money or damages may be brought against a public entity on a cause of action for which a claim is required to be presented in accordance with Chapter 1 (commencing with Section 900) and Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 910) of Part 3 of this division until a written claim therefor has been presented to the public entity and has been acted upon by the board, or has been deemed to have been rejected by the board, in accordance with Chapters 1 and 2 of Part 3 of this division.” (Gov Code § 945.4.)

 

“Where there has been an attempt to comply [with the claims statute] but the compliance is defective, the test of substantial compliance controls. Under this test, the court must ask whether sufficient information is disclosed on the face of the filed claim ‘to reasonably enable the public entity to make an adequate investigation of the merits of the claim and settle it without the expense of a lawsuit.’” Wood v. Riverside Gen. Hosp. (1994) 25 Cal. App. 4th 1113, 1118.) 

 

Plaintiffs represented that they would assert a wrongful death claim in their Government Claim Forms. (RJN Ex. A.) Plaintiffs did not represent decedent’s estate would file a survival action.

 

In Nelson v. County of Los Angeles, the Court held:

 

Since Dwyane’s estate did not file a claim, and since there is nothing in Mrs. Nelson’s claim to suggest it was filed in anything other than her individual capacity, and since the damages described in the claim were for “loss of a son” (with no mention of any damage incurred by Dwayne before his death), the trial court properly resolved survivorship claims against Mrs. Nelson.

 

(Nelson v. County of Los Angeles (2003) 113 Cal.App.4th 783, 797.)

 

Here, Plaintiffs’ claims do not suggest they will file a claim on decedent JOSE LUIS ARIAS BARRIENTOS’ behalf. Indeed, each Plaintiff asserts a wrongful death claim and not a survival action.

 

Plaintiffs contends the phrase “both economic and non-economic losses sustained as a result of the decedent’s death” is enough to give Defendant notice of a survival action. Not so. This phrase does not give notice of decedent’s pre-death damages. Moreover, like Nelson, there is nothing on Plaintiffs’ claims to suggest they were filed in anything other than their individual capacities. Simply put, decedent’s estate failed to file a claim.

 

Plaintiffs further argue that Plaintiff Maria Solares provided a statutory declaration, in compliance with CCP § 377.11, which allows a successor-in-interest to file a claim on decedent’s behalf. This argument fails for the same reasons above. Plaintiffs did not indicate Ms. Solares would be filing a survival action on decedent’s behalf on the Government Claim Form.

 

The Court also rejects Plaintiffs’ argument that Defendant was required to inform Plaintiffs that their claim for was insufficient. A public entity’s duty to notify a claimant that the claim form is defective presumes the defect is disclosed on the face of the form. (Nelson, supra, 113 Cal.App.4th at p. 798; Gov. Code § 910.8.) Here, the issue is that Plaintiffs failed to file a claim on behalf of decedent’s estate, not that Plaintiffs’ claim is defective. Thus, Defendant’s failure to object is inapposite. (See Ibid.)

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant’s Demurrer to Plaintiffs Second Cause of Action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend. The Court does not find amendment can cure Plaintiffs’ defect in their failure to present a survival action on their Government Claims Forms.

 

Defendant’s Request for Judicial Notice is GRANTED. (Evid. Code. § 452(c).)
