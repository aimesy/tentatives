MARIA SOLORIO, ET AL. V. SKY ZONE, LLC, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 24NWCV01047

HEARING: Tuesday, September 22, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Strike Defendant Epicent, LLC dba Sky Zone Cerritos’s Cross-Complaint is GRANTED, and the Cross-Complaint filed May 27, 2026 is STRICKEN for failure to obtain leave of court as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50, subdivision (c).

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Background

 

On April 2, 2024, Plaintiff Maria Solorio (“Solorio”) and Ashley Eileen Morales, a minor (“Morales”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed the instant action against Sky Zone, LLC (“Sky Zone”), Circustrix Holdings, LLC (“Circustrix”), and Does 1 through 25 (collectively, “Defendants”) for general negligence and premises liability.  On April 5, 2024, Solorio was appointed guardian ad litem for Morales.

 

On May 21, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”).  The FAC alleges that Defendants operated a trampoline park with closely positioned trampolines but failed to properly manage and supervise patron use.  As a result, an older, larger patron jumped onto the trampoline being used by the minor Plaintiff, causing severe injury to her leg.

 

On January 14, 2025, Plaintiffs amended the FAC to add Epicent, LLC dba Sky Zone Cerritos (“Epicent”) as Doe 1.

 

Epicent filed an Answer on October 24, 2025, and a verified Answer on February 10, 2026.

 

On May 27, 2026, Epicent filed a Cross-Complaint against Yovani Gonzalez (“Gonzalez”) and Roes 1 through 20 for express indemnity, breach of contract, and declaratory relief.

 

On August 19, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion to Strike Cross-Complaint (“Motion”).  Epicent filed an Opposition on September 8, 2026, and Plaintiffs filed a Reply on September 14, 2026.

 

 

 

 

Legal Standard

 

Motion to Strike

 

According to Code of Civil Procedure section 436:

 

The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:

 

(a) Strikeout any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.

 

(b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.

 

Cross-Complaint

 

According to Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50:

 

(a) A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint.

 

(b) Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.

 

(c)  A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint except one filed within the time specified in subdivision (a) or (b).  Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.

 

Motion to Strike Cross-Complaint

 

Plaintiffs move to strike the Cross-Complaint filed by Defendant Epicent on May 27, 2026, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50, arguing that Defendant filed the Cross-Complaint nearly 19 months after its Answer and several months after a trial date was set, without leave of court.  Plaintiffs contend the late filing causes prejudice and violates section 428.50’s mandatory timing requirements.

 

In its Opposition, Defendant acknowledges that it did not obtain leave, but argues that Plaintiffs failed to meet and confer pursuant to section 435.5.  Defendant contends that the Court should allow the Cross-Complaint “in the interests of justice,” particularly because the pleading is directed at a nonparty—Yovani Gonzalez—based on a contractual waiver and indemnity agreement.  Defendant has filed a concurrent motion for leave to file cross-complaint.

 

In its Reply, Plaintiffs argue that the meet and confer requirement is not applicable in this instance because it pertains to motions to strike brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 435 that challenge the contents of a pleading.  However, the instant Motion is brough pursuant to section 428.50.

 

Having considered the moving papers, Opposition, and Reply, the Court makes the following findings.

 

Defendant Epicent filed an Answer on October 24, 2025, and a verified Answer on February 10, 2026.  A trial date was set on October 9, 2025, for May 24, 2027.  On May 27, 2026, Defendant filed a Cross-Complaint against Yovani Gonzalez without prior leave of court.  Defendant acknowledges that leave should have been obtained under section 428.50.

 

Balancing the statutory requirement with the remedial authority described in the papers, the Court determines that the Cross-Complaint was procedurally improper when filed and is subject to being stricken for lack of prior leave.  Nevertheless, the statute relied upon by Defendant allows the Court to consider granting leave “in the interest of justice.”  On this record, the most efficient course is to strike the improperly filed Cross-Complaint without prejudice to Defendant’s concurrently filed motion for leave, so that the procedural defect is cured, if at all, through the mechanism section 428.50, subdivision (c) contemplates.  The meet-and-confer requirement dispute does not alter the dispositive fact that the Cross-Complaint was filed without the required leave after a trial date was set.

 

As to prejudice, Plaintiffs assert the late addition of a new party risks delaying trial and prejudicing Plaintiffs, while Defendant asserts that the Cross-Complaint was filed roughly a year before trial and has identified no concrete prejudice.  These arguments will be addressed and evaluated in connection with the motion for leave to file cross-complaint.

 

Accordingly, the Court will strike the Cross-Complaint for failure to obtain leave before filing, without prejudice to Defendant’s pending motion for leave and without adjudicating the merits of the parties’ claims.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Strike Defendant Epicent, LLC dba Sky Zone Cerritos’s Cross-Complaint is GRANTED, and the Cross-Complaint filed May 27, 2026 is STRICKEN for failure to obtain leave of court as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50, subdivision (c).

 

Case Number: 26NWCV00825    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: R

#18

DIGITAL VERNON 5, L.P. V. VUTHY DUKE AUEYONG, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 26NWCV00825

HEARING: Tuesday, September 22, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Plaintiff Digital Vernon 5, L.P.’s Motion to Amend Court’s Judgment for Possession is GRANTED.  The judgment entered on March 27, 2026, is amended to include a monetary award of $60,370.06, entered jointly and severally against both Defendants.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Background

 

On March 5, 2026, Plaintiff Digital Vernon 5, L.P. (“Plaintiff” or “Digital”) filed the instant commercial unlawful detainer action against Defendants Vuthy Duke Aueyong (“Aueyong”), Bulksavers Inc. (“Bulksavers”) (collectively, “Defendants”) and Does 1 through 10.

 

On March 27, 2026, default and default judgment were entered against Defendants.

 

On August 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Amend Court’s Judgment for Possession (“Motion”).  No opposition has been filed.

 

Legal Standard

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1169:

 

If, at the time appointed, any defendant served with a summons does not appear and defend, the clerk, upon written application of the plaintiff and proof of the service of summons and complaint, shall enter the default of any defendant so served, and, if requested by the plaintiff, immediately shall enter judgment for restitution of the premises and shall issue a writ of execution thereon.  The application for default judgment and the default judgment shall include a place to indicate that the judgment includes tenants, subtenants, if any, named claimants, if any, and any other occupants of the premises.  Thereafter, the plaintiff may apply to the court for any other relief demanded in the complaint, including the costs, against the defendant, or defendants, or against one or more of the defendants.

 

Section 1174, subdivision (b) requires assessment of damages occasioned by the unlawful detainer and determination of rent due where the action is based on nonpayment of rent.

 

Motion to Amend Judgment

 

Plaintiff moves to amend the judgment granting Plaintiff possession against Defendants Aueyong and Bulksavers on March 27, 2026, to add a monetary award of $60,370.06.

 

Plaintiff invokes Code of Civil Procedure sections 1169, 1174, 1032, 1033.5, and 1034.5, Rules of Court 3.1112 and 3.1113, applicable local rules, the parties’ written lease agreement, and the Court’s inherent and statutory authority to award relief demanded in the complaint following a default judgment for possession in an unlawful detainer action.  Plaintiff seeks to add to the judgment: (1) past due rent and late charges totaling $38,971.80; (2) holdover damages of $14,930.54; (3) prejudgment interest of $2,855.12 through the hearing date; and (4) recoverable costs and attorneys’ fees totaling $7,952.60; less a $4,340 security deposit; for a net $60,370.06, to be entered against both Defendants, jointly and severally.

 

The motion asserts that Defendants are not entitled to notice after default; default was entered on March 27, 2026 . The record supports proceeding without additional notice.

 

1.    Past Due Rent and Late Charges

 

The Rent Collection Report shows missed base rent of $35,428.96 for August 2025 through February 2026 at $5,061.28 per month.  Lease §13.4 imposes a one-time 10% late charge if rent is not received within five days; § 4.1 defines “Rent” to include all monetary obligations other than the security deposit; late charges total $3,542.84.  The record supports $38,971.80.

 

2.    Holdover Damages

 

Defendants surrendered possession on April 27, 2026, after the notice to pay rent or quit expired on February 26, 2026, resulting in 59 days of holdover from February 27 to April 27, 2026.  Lease § 26 sets holdover rent at 150% of the prior base rent; with base rent of $5,061.28, the daily holdover rate is $253.06, yielding $14,930.54 for 59 days.  This amount is substantiated.

 

 

 

3.    Prejudgment Interest

 

Lease §13.5 provides 10% per annum interest on monetary payments (other than late charges) beginning the 31st day after due; Plaintiff’s spreadsheet calculates $2,855.12 through the September 22, 2026, hearing date.  The showing is adequate as to method and amount through the stated date.

 

4.    Costs and Attorneys’ Fees

 

Plaintiff seeks $7,952.60 consisting of $278.10 in costs (filing and reservation fees) and $7,674.60 in attorneys’ fees, supported by invoices and a declaration addressing work performed, rates, and reasonableness.  Lease §31 contains a prevailing-party fee clause providing for reasonable fees fully reimbursing fees reasonably incurred.  The documentation sufficiently supports the requested amounts.

 

5.    Security Deposit Offset

 

The tenant estoppel identifies a $4,340 security deposit, which Plaintiff credits against the total recovery.

 

6.    Total Amount

 

The requested amended judgment components net to $60,370.06 and Plaintiff asks that it be entered jointly and severally against both defendants.  The amounts are supported by sworn declarations and exhibits.

 

Plaintiff’s Motion is GRANTED.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Digital Vernon 5, L.P.’s Motion to Amend Court’s Judgment for Possession is GRANTED.  The judgment entered on March 27, 2026, is amended to include a monetary award of $60,370.06, entered jointly and severally against both Defendants.
