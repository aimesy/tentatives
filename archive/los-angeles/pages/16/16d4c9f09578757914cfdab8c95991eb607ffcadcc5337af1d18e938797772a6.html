MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff AEG Presents Productions, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  Defendant Danny Wimmer Presents, LLC

Motion for Issue and Evidentiary Sanctions

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff AEG Presents Productions, LLC (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order awarding issue and evidentiary sanctions for Plaintiff and against defendant Danny Wimmer Presents, LLC (“Defendant”) for failure to comply with the court’s December 18, 2025 order to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s Requests for Production Nos. 252 and 253.

The court denies the motion because Plaintiff has not demonstrated that Defendant has engaged in conduct that is a misuse of the discovery process by disobeying a court order to provide discovery. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.010 subd. (g).)    

On December 18, 2025, the court issued an order on Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production which, as relevant here, granted the motion as to requests for production numbers 252 and 253. (Order filed December 18, 2025.) That ruling was subject to three conditions: “(1) Defendant’s identifying deposition transcripts or portions thereof, and responses to interrogatories or requests for admission or portions thereof, that are subject to a protective order entered in the Davis Shapiro action, (2) Plaintiff’s obtaining an order from the court in that action allowing for the production of those documents pursuant to this order, and (3) Defendant’s right to assert the attorney-client privilege as to those materials and providing a privilege log concerning any such materials as to which Defendant asserts the attorney-client privilege pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.240. (Ibid.)

Plaintiff contends that Defendant violated this order by not identifying materials subject to a protective order and instead asserting blanket privilege objections. Defendant maintains that all of the materials sought are subject to the attorney-client privilege. Whether or not Defendant’s objection has merit, the court expressly permitted Defendant to assert the attorney-client privilege contingent on the provision of a privilege log. Defendant provided that privilege log on May 20, 2026. (Jorrie Decl. ¶ 19, Exs. 13-14.) Although Plaintiff plainly disputes the merits of Defendant’s privilege claim and the sufficiency of the privilege log, that dispute does not transform Defendant’s conduct into disobedience of the court’s order. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.010 subd. (g).) Other remedies are available if Plaintiff seeks to challenge Defendant’s assertion of attorney-client privilege.

 

 

ORDER

          The court DENIES plaintiff AEG Presents Productions, LLC’s motion for sanctions.

The court orders defendant Danny Wimmer Presents, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[Tentative] Order RE:

 

 

(1)  Motion to Substitute Annette Rodridguez as Successor-in-Interest to Decedent Alex Martinez

(2)  Motion to Compel Arbitration

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            (1) Plaintiff Alex Martinez (deceased) and proposed successor-in-interest Annette Rodriguez; (2) Defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   (1) No opposition; (2) Plaintiff Annette Rodriguez, successor-in-interest to Alex Martinez

 

(1) Motion to Substitute Annette Rodriguez as Successor-in-Interest to Decedent Alex Martinez

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.

(2) Motion to Compel Arbitration

The court considered the moving and reply papers filed in connection with this motion. Annette Rodriguez filed and served a timely opposition to this motion while the motion to substitute was pending. As the court has granted the motion to substitute, the court also exercises its discretion to consider the opposition to the motion to compel arbitration.

DISCUSSION

Motion to Substitute Annette Rodriguez as Successor-in-Interest

          Annette Rodriguez moves the court for an order substituting her as successor-in-interest to plaintiff Alex Martinez.

          The court finds that the proposed successor-in-interest has submitted an affidavit which satisfies the statutory requirements. (Rodriguez Decl. ¶¶ 2-6, Ex. 1; Code Civ. Proc., § 377.32.) Therefore, the court grants the motion to substitute Annette Rodriguez as successor in interest to the deceased plaintiff Alex Martinez. (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.31.)

 

Motion to Compel Arbitration

          Defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order compelling binding arbitration of this dispute.

1.     Applicability of Federal Arbitration Act

“The party asserting the FAA applies to an agreement has ‘the burden to demonstrate FAA coverage by declarations and other evidence’” (Evenskaas v. California Transit, Inc. (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 285, 292 [internal citation omitted].) “[W]hen an agreement provides that its ‘enforcement’ shall be governed by the FAA, the FAA governs a party’s motion to compel arbitration[.]” (Victrola 89, LLC v. Jaman Properties 8 LLC (2020) 46 Cal.App.5th 337, 346.)

The court finds that Defendant has met its burden to show that the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. § 1 et seq.) (the “FAA”) governs this motion because the arbitration agreement that is the subject of this motion states that it “shall be construed and enforced in accordance with and governed by the Federal Arbitration Act and the procedures set forth in the Federal Arbitration Act shall govern any petition to compel arbitration.” (Guzman Decl. Ex. A, § 1.8.)

2.     Existence of Agreement to Arbitrate

“‘ “The party seeking to compel arbitration bears the burden of proving the existence of an arbitration agreement, while the party opposing the petition bears the burden of establishing a defense to the agreement’s enforcement.” ’”¿ (Beco v. Fast Auto Loans (2022) 86 Cal.App.5th 292, 302.)¿ To determine the existence of an arbitration agreement, the court uses “a three-step burden-shifting process.”¿ (Iyere v. Wise Auto Group (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 747, 755.)¿ “The arbitration proponent must first recite verbatim, or provide a copy of, the alleged agreement.¿ [Citations.]¿ A movant can bear this initial burden ‘by attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement purportedly bearing the opposing party’s signature.’”¿ (Ibid. [internal citations omitted].)¿ “If the movant bears its initial burden, the burden shifts to the party opposing arbitration to identify a factual dispute as to the agreement’s existence . . . .”¿ (Ibid.)¿ If the opposing party meets its burden to “submit sufficient evidence to create a factual dispute” as to the existence of the agreement, the burden shifts back to the arbitration proponent, who retains the ultimate burden of proving its existence by a preponderance of the evidence.¿ (Ibid.; Gamboa v. Northeast Community Clinic (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 158, 165-166.)¿¿¿ 

First, the court finds that Defendants have met their burden to provide a copy of an alleged agreement to arbitrate this controversy.

Defendants have submitted a copy of a signed “Arbitration Agreement” which states that Alex Martinez (“Decedent”) and Defendant agree to arbitrate any dispute as to medical malpractice, or any claims, disputes, controversies, or demands relating to or arising out of the provision of services or healthcare or any failure to provide services or healthcare by Defendant. (Guzman Decl. Ex. A, §§ 1.1, 1.2.) The Arbitration Agreement also states that Decedent and Defendant agree to arbitrate any disputes relating to the admission agreement, the Arbitration Agreement, or the validity, interpretation, construction, performance, and enforcement thereof. (Guzman Decl. Ex. A, § 1.2.) The Arbitration Agreement bears a handwritten signature dated December 14, 2022. (Guzman Decl. Ex. A, p. 5.)

Second, the Court finds that plaintiff Annette Rodriguez (“Plaintiff”), as successor-in-interest to the Decedent, has not met her burden to demonstrate a factual dispute as to the existence of the Arbitration Agreement.

Plaintiff contends that the Decedent did not have the capacity to enter into an arbitration agreement at the time because he suffered from a variety of physiological and psychological ailments which cast doubt on his capacity to understand the consequences of the agreement. Although Plaintiff presents medical records concerning the Decedent at the time, Plaintiff does not present competent evidence that establishes the Decedent did not have capacity to contract at the time he signed the Arbitration Agreement. (Peck Decl. Ex. B [Decedent’s medical records].) A declaration by Plaintiff’s counsel is not competent evidence of the Decedent’s capacity to contract because that is a matter which requires specialized medical knowledge. Plaintiff’s counsel has not demonstrated that knowledge. (Peck Decl. ¶¶ 1-6.)

The court further finds that Defendant has shown that the Arbitration Agreement encompasses Plaintiff’s claims because it requires arbitration of any claim arising from the provision of services or care to the Decedent or any failure to do so. (Guzman Decl. Ex. A, § 1.2.) The court also notes that the Arbitration Agreement delegates to the Arbitrator “the exclusive authority to resolve any Dispute relating to the interpretation, applicability, enforceability, or formation of this Agreement.” (Guzman Decl. Ex. A, § 1.6.) This is, on its face, a delegation clause regarding the arbitrability of claims. (Rodriguez v. American Technologies, Inc. (2006) 135 Cal.App.4th 1110, 1123.)

For the reasons stated above, the court finds that Defendant has established that Decedent entered into a binding agreement to arbitrate his claims against Defendant. The court therefore grants the motion to compel arbitration.

ORDER

The court GRANTS Annette Rodriguez’s motion to substitute successor-in-interest.

The court orders that Annette Rodriguez be substituted as plaintiff and successor-in-interest to the deceased plaintiff Alex Martinez.

The court GRANTS defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP’s motion to compel arbitration.

The court orders (1) plaintiff Annette Rodriguez and defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP to submit the claims alleged in the Complaint against defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP to arbitration and (2) this entire action is stayed pending completion of the arbitration between plaintiff Annette Rodriguez and defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP.

The court sets (1) an Order to Show Cause Re: Completion of Arbitration (between plaintiff Annette Rodriguez and defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP), and (2) a Case Management Conference for hearing on Monday, April 5, 2027, at 8:30 A.M. in Department 513.

The court orders defendant York Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[Tentative] Order RE:

 

 

Motion to Compel Arbitration

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendants Aaron Edward Haith, II, Ethel Arias, and 4928 La Calandria, LLC; joined by A&H Realty Group, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Brooklyn Gibson Greene

 

Motion to Compel Arbitration

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

 

Motion to Compel Arbitration

          Defendants Aaron Edward Haith, II, Ethel Arias, and 4928 La Calandria, LLC, joined by A&H Realty Group, LLC (“Defendants”) move the court for an order (1) compelling plaintiff Brooklyn Gibson Greene (“Plaintiff”) to submit her claims to binding arbitration and (2) staying this action pending resolution of arbitration.

 

1.     Existence of Agreement to Arbitrate

“‘ “The party seeking to compel arbitration bears the burden of proving the existence of an arbitration agreement, while the party opposing the petition bears the burden of establishing a defense to the agreement’s enforcement.” ’”¿ (Beco v. Fast Auto Loans (2022) 86 Cal.App.5th 292, 302.)¿ To determine the existence of an arbitration agreement, the court uses “a three-step burden-shifting process.”¿ (Iyere v. Wise Auto Group (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 747, 755.)¿ “The arbitration proponent must first recite verbatim, or provide a copy of, the alleged agreement.¿ [Citations.]¿ A movant can bear this initial burden ‘by attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement purportedly bearing the opposing party’s signature.’”¿ (Ibid. [internal citations omitted].)¿ “If the movant bears its initial burden, the burden shifts to the party opposing arbitration to identify a factual dispute as to the agreement’s existence . . . .”¿ (Ibid.)¿ If the opposing party meets its burden to “submit sufficient evidence to create a factual dispute” as to the existence of the agreement, the burden shifts back to the arbitration proponent, who retains the ultimate burden of proving its existence by a preponderance of the evidence.¿ (Ibid.; Gamboa v. Northeast Community Clinic (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 158, 165-166.)¿¿¿ 

The court finds that Defendants have met their burden to provide a copy of an alleged agreement to arbitrate this controversy.

Defendants seek to arbitrate this action based on an arbitration provision in the Partnership Agreement that is the subject of the action. The Agreement in question is attached to the First Amended Complaint as Exhibit 1. Section 69 of that Agreement is titled “Mediation and Arbitration,” and states that “[i]f any dispute relating to this Agreement between Partners, or between one or more Partners and the Partnership, is not resolved through formal discussion within 14 days from the date a dispute arises, the Partners agree to submit the issue first before a non-binding mediator and to an arbitrator in the event mediation fails. The decision of the arbitrator will be binding on the Partners. Any mediator or arbitrator must be a neutral party acceptable to all Partners. The cost of any mediations or arbitrations will be shared equally by the Partners.” (FAC Ex. 1, ¶ 69.) The agreement bears Plaintiff’s handwritten signature and is dated July 18, 2024. (FAC Ex. 1, p. 16.)

2.     Recission of Partnership Agreement

Plaintiff argues that the Partnership Agreement should be rescinded because it was presumptively procured through a breach of fiduciary duty and without sufficient consideration.

The court may refuse to compel arbitration if it determines that grounds exist for recission of the agreement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.2.) A real estate agent bears “the same obligation of undivided service and loyalty that [the law] imposes on a trustee in favor of his beneficiary.” (Batson v. Strehlow (1968) 68 Cal.2d 662, 674-75.) A transaction between a trustee and a beneficiary “which occurs during the existence of the trust or while the trustee’s influence with the beneficiary remains and by which the trustee obtains an advantage from the beneficiary is presumed to be a violation of the trustee’s fiduciary duties.” (Probate Code, § 16004, subd. (c).) Such agreements are also presumed to lack sufficient consideration. (See Smith v. Zak (1971) 20 Cal.App.3d 785, 793 [applying Civil Code section 2322, since repealed and replaced with Probate Code section 16004].)

The court finds that Plaintiff has not demonstrated that the Partnership Agreement should be rescinded. Plaintiff has not demonstrated that her cited authorities for recission, set forth above, are applicable, because Plaintiff has neither alleged nor shown that Defendants were acting as Plaintiff’s real estate agent. Plaintiff alleges that defendant Haith acted as Plaintiff’s loan agent and contractor. (FAC ¶¶ 10, 11.) The allegation that Haith is a licensed real estate broker does not establish that Haith was Plaintiff’s real estate broker. (FAC ¶ 3.)

The court therefore cannot refuse to compel arbitration based on a breach of fiduciary duty.

3.     Unconscionability

Plaintiff also contends that arbitration should not be compelled because the Partnership Agreement is unconscionable.

“‘[A]greements to arbitrate [may] be invalidated by “generally applicable contract defenses, such as fraud, duress, or unconscionability.” ’”¿ (Beco v. Fast Auto Loans (2022) 86 Cal.App.5th 292, 302.)¿ “The burden of proving unconscionability rests upon the party asserting it.”¿ (OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho (2019) 8 Cal.5th 111, 126.)¿ “Unconscionability entails an absence of meaningful choice on the part of one of the parties together with contract terms which are unreasonably favorable to the other party.”¿ (Iyere, supra, 87 Cal.App.5th at p. 759 [internal quotations omitted].)¿ “Both procedural and substantive unconscionability must be shown for the defense to be established, but ‘they need not be present in the same degree.’”  (Kho, supra, 8 Cal.5th at p. 125.)

The court finds that Plaintiff has not established that the agreement is unconscionable because she has not demonstrated procedural unconscionability.

“Procedural unconscionability pertains to the making of the agreement . . . .”¿ (Ajamian v. CantorCO2e, L.P. (2012) 203 Cal.App.4th 771, 795.)¿ It “‘“focuses on two factors: ‘oppression’ and ‘surprise.’¿ [Citations.]¿ ‘Oppression’ arises from an inequality of bargaining power which results in no real negotiation and ‘an absence of meaningful choice.’ [Citations.]¿ ‘Surprise’ involves the extent to which the supposedly agreed-upon terms of the bargain are hidden in the prolix printed form drafted by the party seeking to enforce the disputed terms.”’”¿ (Zullo v. Superior Court (2011) 197 Cal.App.4th 477, 484 [citations omitted].)¿

Plaintiff has not presented any evidence of oppression or surprise. Plaintiff asserts that the Partnership Agreement is unconscionable because she relied upon Haith to draft the contract based on their long friendship. This is not evidence of superior bargaining power, nor is it evidence of hidden terms. Indeed, Plaintiff herself admits that Defendant Haith solicited Plaintiff’s input as to any specific terms in the Agreement. (Greene Decl. ¶ 8.) The terms that Plaintiff specified—prohibiting the sale of the subject property for twenty years and requiring her approval for single expenditures over $1,000—are both present in the Agreement. (Greene Decl. ¶ 8; FAC Ex. 1 ¶¶ 60, 77.) The court cannot find based on this showing that the agreement is procedurally unconscionable.

Because Plaintiff has not shown any procedural unconscionability, the court does not reach the issue of substantive unconscionability.

For the reasons stated above, the court finds that Defendants have established that Plaintiff entered into a binding agreement to arbitrate her claims against Defendants. The court therefore grants the motion to compel arbitration.

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendants Aaron Edward Haith, II, Ethel Arias, 4928 La Calandria, LLC, and A&H Realty Group, LLC’s motion to compel arbitration.

The court orders (1) plaintiff Brooklyn Gibson Greene and defendants Aaron Edward Haith, II, Ethel Arias, 4928 La Calandria, LLC, and A&H Realty Group, LLC to submit the claims alleged in the First Amended Complaint to arbitration and (2) this action is stayed pending completion of the arbitration.

The court sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Completion of Arbitration for hearing on Monday, April 5, 2027, at 8:30 A.M. in Department 513.

The court orders defendants Aaron Edward Haith, II, Ethel Arias, 4928 La Calandria, LLC, and A&H Realty Group, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion to Strike Portions of Complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant General Motors, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Stefanie Cuesta

Motion to Strike Portions of Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order striking portions of the Complaint filed by plaintiff Stefanie Cuesta (“Plaintiff”).

The court finds that Plaintiff has not alleged facts entitling her to request civil penalties under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.24 because she has not alleged compliance with the vehicle retention requirement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.24, subd. (c).)  

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.24, subdivision (a) states that a party seeking to commence an action for civil penalties under the Song-Beverly Act must, at least 30 days before commencing the action,  “(1) [n]otify the manufacturer of the consumer’s name, the accurate Vehicle Identification Number (‘VIN’) of the motor vehicle, and a brief summary of the repair history and problems with the motor vehicle [¶]” and “(2) [d]emand that the manufacturer repurchase or replace the motor vehicle.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.24, subd. (a).) The consumer must also have possession of the vehicle at the time the notice is sent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.21, subd. (c).) 

The court finds that Plaintiff has not alleged that she has retained possession of the subject vehicle. Although Plaintiff asserts that possession can be inferred from the allegation that she made a buyback request, that argument would subsume the possession requirement of subdivision (c) within the general notice requirement of subdivision (a). Tellingly, subdivision (h), which authorizes claims for relief without civil penalties when there is no valid buyback request, also requires the consumer to have possession of the vehicle at the time of filing of the complaint. Therefore, a complaint under the Song-Beverly Act which, as here, does not allege continued possession of the vehicle at all does not demonstrate conformity with the laws of this state, whether it seeks civil penalties or not.

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  Plaintiff has not shown how she could amend her pleading to correct the deficiency that requires striking Plaintiff’s requests. The court therefore orders the requests for civil penalties stricken without leave to amend.

For the reasons stated above, the court grants Defendant’s motion to strike Plaintiff’s requests for civil penalties because they are not in conformity with the laws of this state, without leave to amend. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 436, 871.24 subd. (c).)

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendant General Motors, LLC’s motion to strike without leave to amend. The court orders that paragraphs 29-31, 36, 39, and the prayer for civil penalties set forth on page 12, section f, line 5 of the Complaint filed by plaintiff Stefanie Cuesta are stricken.

The court orders defendant General Motors, LLC to file an answer to plaintiff Stefanie Cuesta’s Complaint within 10 days of the date of this order.

The court orders defendant General Motors, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
