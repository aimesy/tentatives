DEPARTMENT 534 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCP04639    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 534

Background

 

On December 1, 2025, California Resources Corporation, California Resources Production Corporation, and California Resources Petroleum Corporation filed this action for recovery of charges pursuant to Public Resources Code § 3430. The First Amended Complaint, filed April 10, 2026, names Fiona Ma, in her official capacity as State Treasurer; California Department of Conservation, Geologic Energy Management Division (“CalGEM”), and Douglas Ito, in his official capacity as State Oil & Gas Supervisor, as Respondents and Defendants.

 

The FAC asserts causes of action for: (1) recovery of charges [Pub. Res. Code § 3430] asserted against all defendants and (2) violation of due process (U.S. Const. amend. XIV § 1; Cal. Const. Art. I § 7) asserted against CalGEM and Ito only. Plaintiffs allege CalGEM failed to allow Plaintiffs the opportunity to plug and abandon idle oil and gas wells, performed the work itself at exorbitant cost, and assessed charges to Plaintiffs for the P&A work CalGEM performed. Plaintiffs contend this violates the applicable statutes and failed to provide Plaintiffs with due process.

 

Demurrer

 

On May 26, 2026, Defendants filed the instant demurrer to the FAC. Defendants demur to the first cause of action based upon Plaintiffs’ failure to include an accompanying petition for writ of mandate. Defendants demur to the second cause of action arguing a writ proceeding provides the requisite due process.

 

Opposition

 

In opposition, Plaintiffs argue there is no basis to require or construe this action as a writ proceeding and doing so would violate due process.

 

Reply

 

In reply, Defendants reiterate their contention that a writ proceeding is required.

 

Discussion

 

Standard

 

When ruling on a demurrer, the Court accepts the truth of all properly pleaded material facts of the subject pleading, (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-967), and draws reasonable “inferences favorable to the plaintiff, not the defendant.” (Perez v. Golden Empire Transit Dist. (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1239.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732.) Courts may also consider matters properly subject to judicial notice, (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318), but need not accept “contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1967) 67 Cal. 2d 695, 71.)

 

If the demurrer is sustained, plaintiff must prove the possibility of cure by amendment. (Czajkowski v. Haskell & White, LLP (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 166, 173 (citing Grinzi v. San Diego Hospice Corp. (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 72, 78-79).) 

 

The State Treasurer is the Only Proper Party to the First Cause of Action

 

“Any person claiming and protesting that the assessment made or charges assessed against him are void, in whole or in part, may bring an action against the State Treasurer for the recovery of the whole or any part of the charges, penalties, or costs paid on such assessment, upon the grounds stated in his protest.” (Pub. Res. Code § 3430.)

 

The first cause of action pursuant to Public Resources Code section 3430 is brought against all named defendants. (FAC at 14:26-27.)

 

In their demurrer, Defendants contend “the crux of the First Cause of Action is a challenge to CalGEM’s underlying administrative action in issuing the NOA. The appropriate vehicle for review of CalGEM’s agency action is a section 1085 petition for writ of mandate.” (Dem. at 12:5-8.) Defendants further argue “section 3430 authorizes a recovery action to be brought only against the Treasurer, not CalGEM, and a claim for declaratory relief cannot be used to review CalGEM’s quasi-judicial agency action.” (Id. at 12:12-14.) In opposition, Plaintiffs disclaim any request for declaratory relief. (Opp. at 8:21-9:13.) The parties also disagree as to whether CalGEM and Ito are proper parties to the first cause of action.

 

As argued by Defendants, Plaintiffs “cannot seek [] relief against CalGEM under Section 3430 because the statute does not contemplate any relief other than a refund from the State Treasurer.” (Reply at 3:7-8.) Public Code section 3430, by its express terms, provides for a refund action only against the State Treasurer.

 

Plaintiffs’ summary and unsupported contention that “nothing in that section prohibits CRC from also naming CalGEM and CalGEM would have undoubtedly intervened if CRC had only named the State Treasurer,” (Opp. at 9:11-13), lacks merit and is contrary to the express and limited authority provided by Section 3430.

 

While there is no case authority specifically addressing Section 3430, as noted by Plaintiffs, the statute operates as a “pay-first-litigate-later statute.” (Opp. at 8:5-13.) Pay-under-protest statutory provisions are generally considered the exclusive means of relief and lie only against the public entities identified in the statute. (See generally County of Sacramento v. Assessment Appeals Bd. No. 2 (1973) 32 Cal.App.3d 654, 671 (“The exclusive means of review of tax proceedings in California has been the remedy of suit to recover alleged overpayments, and the power of the state to provide such suit as the exclusive remedy is unquestioned.”); William Jefferson & Co., Inc. v. Orange County Assessment Appeals Bd. No. 2 (2014) 228 Cal.App.4th 1, 12 (holding tax refund action may only be brought against county or city that collected the tax, as provided by statute, not the local assessment appeals board).)

 

Plaintiffs’ fail to state facts sufficient to assert the first cause of action against CalGEM and Ito. The demurrer to the first cause of action under Section 3430 is SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to Defendants CalGEM and Ito.

 

An Accompanying Petition for Writ of Mandate is not Required

 

Defendants’ claim that Plaintiffs were required to seek a writ related to CalGEM’s actions is not supported by Public Resources Code section 3430, nor any parallel pay-under-protest statutory provisions. Public Resources Code section 3431 refers to the filing of a complaint, not a writ, and Public Resources Code section 3432(b) provides that “[t]he provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure relating to pleadings, proofs, trials, and appeals are applicable to these proceedings.” The statute incorporates the Code of Civil Procedure, does not require writ relief, and does not explicitly impose any procedural limitations on the only two proper parties to the action: the payor plaintiff and the State Treasurer.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiffs that Defendants’ reliance on general case authority surrounding writ relief does not “address any issue remotely relevant to what Section 3430 provides.” (Opp. at 7:21.)

 

The statutory language mirrors other tax and fee refund provisions and “[t]he ‘pay first’ rule also appears in numerous statutes.” (Mojave Pistachios, LLC v. Superior Court (2024) 99 Cal.App.5th 605, 626. Compare Pub. Res. Code § 3430 (“Any person claiming and protesting that the assessment made or charges assessed against him are void, in whole or in part, may bring an action against the State Treasurer . . .”) with Cal. Const., art. XIII, § 32 (“After payment of a tax claimed to be illegal, an action may be maintained to recover the tax paid, with interest, in such manner as may be provided by the Legislature.”); Rev. & Tax. Code, § 5140 (“The person who paid the tax, his or her guardian or conservator, the executor of his or her will, or the administrator of his or her estate may bring an action only in the superior court, but not in the small claims division of the superior court, against a county or a city to recover a tax . . .”); Wat. Code § 10726.6 (“Any person may pay a fee imposed pursuant to Section 10730, 10730.2, or 10730.4 under protest and bring an action against the governing body in the superior court to recover any money that the governing body refuses to refund.”).)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1086 provides that a writ may issue “in all cases where there is not a plain, speedy, and adequate remedy, in the ordinary course of law.”

 

Where, as here, there is statutory authority allowing an action for a refund, writ relief is unavailable. (See e.g. William Jefferson, supra, 228 Cal.App.4th at 11 (“Because a tax refund action provides property owners with an adequate remedy at law, equitable actions for mandamus, injunctive, and declaratory relief generally are unavailable to obtain judicial review of a local assessment appeals board decision.”); Little v. Los Angeles County Assessment Appeals Bds. (2007) 155 Cal.App.4th 915, 923 (“a writ of mandamus only issues when there otherwise is no speedy and adequate remedy of at law. A suit for a refund of taxes paid is an adequate remedy.”). (citations and quotations omitted); County of Sacramento v. Assessment Appeals Bd. No. 2 (1973) 32 Cal.App.3d 654, 672 (“A writ of mandamus or a writ of prohibition only issues when there otherwise is no speedy and adequate remedy at law. . . . allowing the taxpayer to pay the disputed tax under protest and to sue for refund, is such an adequate remedy and mandate is denied. . . . Where the procedure for testing the validity of the assessment and the tax levied pursuant thereto is applicable, through a payment under protest and suit for refund, there is no right to employ administrative mandamus, under Code of Civil Procedure section 1094.5.”).)

 

The demurrer to the first cause of action is OVERRULED as to the State Treasurer, who is the only proper party to the refund cause of action under Public Resources Code section 3430.

 

This Action Provides Plaintiffs the Requisite Due Process

 

The second cause of action is alleged in the alternative and appears to relate solely to the contention that Defendants’ purported writ requirement would deny Plaintiffs’ due process: “[i]f the Court agrees with Defendants’ interpretation of Section 3237, including that any challenge should be treated similarly to a writ action in which Plaintiffs are not entitled to discovery, then Plaintiffs will be left with no remedy at law by which to challenge the Charges, thereby violating Plaintiffs’ substantive and procedural due process rights.” (FAC ¶ 71. See also FAC ¶ 81 (“If the Court concludes that Defendants’ interpretation of Section 3237 is correct, Plaintiffs alternatively plead that CalGEM’s application of Section 3237 as to Plaintiffs violates their substantive and procedural due process rights under the United States Constitution and California Constitution.”); Opp. at 11:16-17 (“In seeking to avoid their discovery obligations and hide behind a writ action, Defendants are running squarely into a due process violation and inviting further error.”).)

 

Because Public Resources Code section 3430 permits an action for postpayment refund, there are no due process concerns. (See e.g. Mojave Pistachios, LLC v. Superior Court (2024) 99 Cal.App.5th 605, 631 (“There is no due process concern from imposing the ‘pay first’ rule because water extractors have an adequate remedy at law in the form of a postpayment refund procedure.”); Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Assn. v. Coachella Valley Water Dist. (2025) 108 Cal.App.5th 485, 518 (“if a state both prohibits predeprivation tax relief and denies a meaningful opportunity to secure postpayment relief, then the state violates the due process clause of the United States Constitution.”).) “The provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure relating to pleadings, proofs, trials, and appeals are applicable to these proceedings.” (Pub. Res. Code § 3432(b).)

 

The demurrer to the second cause of action, plead in the alternative, is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

 

Plaintiffs’ action shall proceed as a refund action pursuant to Public Resources Code section 3430 against the State Treasurer, absent a showing from Plaintiffs that they seek some other relief that is procedurally proper against CalGEM or Ito.

 

Conclusion

 

Respondents and Defendants’ Demurrer to Petitioners and Plaintiffs’ First Amended Verified Complaint is SUSTAINED in part and OVERRULED in part.

 

The demurrer is OVERRULED as to the first cause of action against Fiona Ma, in her official capacity as State Treasurer.

 

The demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to the first cause of action against CalGEM and Douglas Ito, in his official capacity as State Oil & Gas Supervisor.

 

The demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to the second cause of action.

 

Counsel for Defendants to give notice.

 


Case Number: 26STCV21357    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 534

Joshua Kim, the child of Christina Kim and Peter Kim, brings a renewed ex parte application seeking an order relating one (or two, it is not clear) civil cases with a family law case filed in 2021 and set for trial in a few weeks. In the alternative Joshua seeks an order shortening time to hear a motion to relate the cases. The court will refer to the parties by their first names for clarity; no disrespect is intended.

 

The current ex parte application has a notice of related case identifying two cases: civil case 26STCV121357 and family law case 21STFL04935. His earlier NORC also identified a second civil case, 24STCV22915. The current ex parte application lists all three cases on the same caption.

 

Having reviewed the underlying cases, the court denies the ex parte application to relate the cases without prejudice. The court also denies the application to shorten time for a hearing.

 

It appears that Christina has pending in the family law matter a motion to join Joshua for all the same reasons Joshua now raises: he is a claimant as to a key asset in the marital dissolution matter. If the family court grants the joinder motion, then Joshua has the relief he seeks. And that relief is more complete than an order relating the civil and family law cases because an order relating cases is not the same as an order consolidating cases. If the joinder complaint is allowed, Joshua’s interest in the property will be adjudicated in the family law proceeding.

 

Additionally, the court is confused as to whether Joshua is or is not seeking to relate the civil case filed in 2024. He recently had asked be also be related.

 

Last, a technical point. When cases are filed in the civil division and the family law division, and a party seeks to relate the cases, the request is done via notice of related case filed in the underlying cases, and a copy of the notice of related case delivered to Department 534. This procedure is set out in our local rules. Joshua is not entitled to a motion hearing in the first instance on a notice of related case. The court acknowledges that it gave Joshua different information at the first ex parte hearing because the court did not fully appreciate the procedural stance of the case at that time.  


Case Number: 26STUD04115    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 534

Background

 

On September 9, 2024, Jesus Rodriguez filed Superior Court of Los Angeles County case 24STCV23097 against Guadalupe Diaz arising out of disputed ownership of real property located at 1451 E. Vernon Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90011. The First Amended Complaint, filed March 12, 2025, asserts causes of action for: (1) quiet title; (2) cancellation of instrument; (3) accounting; (4) unfair business practices; (5) injunctive relief; and (6) declaratory relief and added Diaz in her capacity as Trustee of the Diaz Family Revocable Living Trust. The FAC alleges Defendant sold and transferred the property to Plaintiff and a third-party on February 2, 2007. Plaintiff allegedly obtained sole ownership of the property on June 20, 2008 and Defendant forged a deed dated March 24, 2023 returning the property to Defendant.

 

On April 24, 2026, Diaz filed the First Amended Cross-Complaint against Jesus Rodriguez, Rene Gonzalez, and Julia Molina asserting causes of action for: (1) quiet title; (2) slander of title; (3) fraud; (4) removal of lien; (5) breach of contract; (6) tortious interference with contractual relationship; (7) violation of covenant of good faith and fair dealing; (8) intentional infliction of emotional distress; and (9) negligent infliction of emotional distress. The FACC alleges the alleged transfer to Jesus Rodriguez was procured by fraud, seeks damages for failure to pay rent from Rene Gonzalez and Julia Molina, and alleges Rodriguez improperly entered into a different rental agreement with Gonzalez and Molina.

 

On April 22, 2026, Maria Guadalupe Diaz sued Rene Gonzalez for unlawful detainer of real property located at 1451 E. Vernon Ave., Unit #3, Los Angeles, CA 90011. Defendant Gonzalez contends Diaz is not the owner of the property and lacks standing.

 

On June 4, 2026, Plaintiff Jesus Rodriguez filed a Notice of Related Case involving 24STCV23097 and 26STUD04115 indicating the cases were related based upon every ground enumerated in California Rules of Court, rule 3.300.

 

On June 30, 2026, retired Judge Rolf M. Treu issued an order finding 24STCV23097 and 26STUD04115, were not related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a).

 

Motion

 

On July 24, 2026, Rene Gonzalez filed the instant motion to relate the two cases.

 

The motion is unopposed. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1005.)

 

Motion to Relate Cases

 

Standard

 

Pursuant to Superior Court of Los Angeles County Local Rules, rule 3.3(f)(3), “[i]n the event that the judge designated under California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(h)(1)(A)(B)(C) to make the decision, does not order related any of the cases set forth in the Notice of Related Cases, any party may file a motion to have the cases related. Department 1 [renumbered as of May 4, 2026, Department 534]  shall hear the motion, if the cases are all pending in the Central District or are pending in two or more different districts.”

 

The Court Shall Relate the Cases at Issue

 

On June 30, 2026, Judge Rolf M. Treu issued an order finding 24STCV23097 and 26STUD04115, were not related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a). The motion is therefore properly before this Court. (SCLAC Local Rules, rule 3.3(f)(3); Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(h)(1)(D).)

 

Cases are related when they (1) involve the same parties and are based on the same or similar claims, (2) arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact, (3) involve claims against, title to, possession of, or damages to the same property, or (4) are likely for other reasons to require substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a).) “When for tactical reasons one side files an action related to an existing case, both sides must file this routine notice. The notice goes to the trial judge in the low-numbered case, who then can decide how to handle the parallel litigation. The notice allows the trial court to promote efficiency, to avoid duplicative effort, to combat judge-shopping, and to minimize the prospect of conflicting results. The trial court system relies on this mechanism to detect and to cure the problem of parallel litigation within its jurisdiction.” (Harris v. Rojas (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 817, 820.)

 

The cases do not involve the same parties as only Plaintiff and Defendant Gonzalez are common to both cases. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(1).) The cases also are not based on the same or similar claims. (Ibid.) The cases also do not arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(2).) Unlawful detainer proceedings only concern straightforward issues regarding the right to possession of disputed premises and the award of damages for any unlawful detention; therefore, issues regarding the property’s title cannot be adjudicated therein because unlawful detainer proceedings are designed to be summary proceedings. (Martin-Bragg v. Moore (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 367, 385.)

 

However, the cases involve title to, and possession of, the same property, (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(3)), and will require a substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(4).) The property at issue in the unlawful detainer action is subject to competing quiet title claims in the civil case and the unlawful detainer defendant has challenged plaintiff’s ownership. (Martin-Bragg v. Moore (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 367, 385 (“a successful claim of title . . . would defeat the [plaintiff’s] right to possession.”).) Additionally, the cases involve competing lease agreements for the property. The Court finds the cases are related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300.

 

Where, as here, “an unlawful detainer proceeding and an . . . action concerning title to the property are simultaneously pending, the trial court in which the . . . action is pending may stay the unlawful detainer action until the issue of title is resolved in the unlimited action, or it may consolidate the actions.” (Ibid.) In the Superior Court of Los Angeles County, a party cannot request consolidation unless the cases are pending in the same department, (SCLAC Local Rules, rule 3.3(g)(1), which is achieved via an order relating the cases. A single judicial officer should determine which of these remedies, if any, are appropriate under the circumstances and resolve all disputes between the parties related to the property.

 

The motion is GRANTED.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Rene Gonzalez’s Motion to Relate Cases is GRANTED.

 

The Court relates 24STCV23097 and 26STUD04115 and orders 26STUD04115 reassigned to Department 731 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse for all purposes.

 

All hearings currently set in 26STUD04115 are hereby advanced and vacated.

 

Counsel for Moving Party is ordered to give notice.
