(1) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Admissions

(2) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories

(3) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories

(4) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with these motions. No oppositions were filed.

DISCUSSION

Defendant MIOD and Company, LLP (“Defendant”) moves the court for orders compelling Plaintiff Amitiss Nasiri (“Plaintiff”) to provide responses to (1) requests for admissions; (2) form interrogatories; (3) special interrogatories, and (4) requests for production, and for sanctions to Defendant and against Plaintiff.

First, the Code of Civil Procedure does not authorize a motion to compel responses to requests for admissions. Instead, the proper motion is a motion deeming the truth of the matters stated in the requests for admissions as admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.)

Second, all four of Defendant’s motions are untimely. The last date the motions could be heard was July 14, 2026, 15 days before the previous July 29, 2026 trial date. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2024.020 subd. (a); Stipulation entered February 24, 2026 [setting trial and continuing cutoff dates].) The court’s continuance of trial at the July 16, 2026 Final Status Conference did not reopen discovery. (Minute Order entered July 16, 2026.) Defendant’s motions are beyond the discovery motion cutoff and must be denied.

 

ORDER

The court DENIES defendant MIOD and Company, LLP’s motions to compel responses.

The court orders plaintiff Amitiss Nasiri to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Martin Ramirez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Defendants California Landmark Group, Inc., R3 Lofts, LLC, and CLG Management, LLC

 

Motion to Strike Pleadings and Vacate May 27, 2025 Order

The court considered the moving and opposition papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Martin Ramirez (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order striking pleadings filed on behalf of defendants California Landmark Group, R3 Lofts, LLC, and CLG Management, LLC (“Defendants”) and vacating the court’s May 27, 2025 order setting aside the Defendants’ default.

 

 

The court denies Plaintiff’s motion for the following reasons.

First, the court entered judgment in favor of Defendants and against Plaintiff in this action on April 13, 2026.  The court therefore does not have jurisdiction to grant the relief requested by Plaintiff’s motion.

Second, even if the court were to have jurisdiction, Plaintiff’s motion is a motion for reconsideration of an issue already decided by the court, but Plaintiff has not shown by affidavit new or different facts, circumstances, or law that would support the relief requested. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008; May 27, 2025 Minute Order; December 16, 2025 Minute Order.)

 

ORDER

The court denies plaintiff Martin Ramirez’s motion to strike pleadings and vacate May 27, 2025 order.

The court orders defendants California Landmark Group, Inc., R3 Lofts, LLC, and CLG Management, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

Demurrer to Complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Michelle Tejeda, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Aracely Polanco Salguero

 

Demurrer to Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this demurrer.

 

DISCUSSION

Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District (“Defendant”) demurs to the complaint filed by plaintiff Michelle Tejeda, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Aracely Polanco Salguero (“Plaintiff”) in its entirety.

The court sustains the demurrer to the Complaint in its entirety as time-barred by the two-year statute of limitations. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).)

Claims for personal injury and property damage against a public entity must ordinarily be presented to that entity within six months of accrual of the claim. (Gov. Code, § 905.) All other claims must be presented within a year. (Id.) When a claim that must be presented is rejected, by action or inaction, by the public entity and the entity fails to give written notice, the claim is subject to a two-year statute of limitations. (Gov. Code, § 945.6, subd. (a)(2).)

Plaintiff alleges claims for negligence, failure to discharge a mandatory duty, and violation of the Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act stemming from injuries sustained in a fall on May 4, 2022. (Complaint ¶ 8.) Plaintiff presented her claim to Defendant on May 25, 2022. (Complaint ¶ 12.) Plaintiff received no formal rejection of the Complaint. (Complaint ¶ 13.) By operation of law, Plaintiff’s complaint is subject to a two-year statute of limitations, which placed the deadline to file this action on May 4, 2024. The Complaint was not filed until May 28, 2024, twenty-four days past the deadline.

The court does not find that the equitable tolling doctrine applies because nothing within the four corners of the Complaint nor judicially noticeable material establishes reasonable and good faith conduct on Plaintiff’s part. (Addison v. State (1978) 21 Cal.3d 313, 319 [elements for application of equitable tolling doctrine].) Plaintiff’s assertion that the late filing of the Complaint was the result of “an administrative error” is extrinsic to the pleadings and insufficient to demonstrate reasonable and good-faith conduct. (Opposition p. 8:23.)

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  The court finds that Plaintiff has not articulated the facts that could be alleged to demonstrate that the Complaint falls within the statute of limitations or that the statute of limitations should be tolled and therefore sustains the demurrer without leave to amend.

ORDER

The court SUSTAINS defendant Los Angeles Unified School District’s demurrer to the complaint filed by plaintiff Michelle Tejeda, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Aracely Polanco Salguero without leave to amend.

The court orders that defendant Los Angeles Unified School District is dismissed from this action.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 581, subd. (m).) 

The court orders defendant Los Angeles Unified School District to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendants Descartes Financial Corporation and Descartes Financial, LTD

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response

Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendants Descartes Financial Corporation and Descartes Financial, LTD (“Defendants”) move the court for an order determining that the settlements between Defendants and plaintiffs Daminuque Ledane Wilson, Janae Gomez, Ja’Saih Gomez, Khylee Wilson, and Ayanna Wilson (“Plaintiffs”) are made in good faith pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 877 and 877.6.

Any party to an action “in which it is alleged that two or more parties are joint tortfeasors or co obligors on a contract debt” is entitled to a hearing “on the issue of the good faith of a settlement entered into by the plaintiff or other claimant and one or more alleged tortfeasors or co-obligors, upon giving notice in the manner provided in subdivision (b) of Section 1005.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 877.6(a)(1).) There is no precise yardstick for measuring the “good faith” of a settlement with one of several tortfeasors, but it must harmonize the public policy favoring settlements with the competing public policy favoring equitable sharing of costs among tortfeasors. (Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499 [setting forth factors for consideration in determining good faith of settlement].) The party contesting the settlement bears the burden of proving that it is in bad faith. (Code Civ. Proc. §877.6(d).)

The court finds, in consideration of the Tech-Bilt factors and the absence of any opposition to this motion, that the settlements between Defendants and Plaintiffs were made in good faith.

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendants Descartes Financial Corporation and Descartes Financial, LTD’s motion for determination of good faith settlement.

The court finds that the settlements between (1) plaintiffs Daminuque Ledane Wilson, Janae Gomez, Ja’Saih Gomez, Khylee Wilson, and Ayanna Wilson, and (2) defendants Descartes Financial Corporation and Descartes Financial, LTD were made in good faith pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6, subdivision (c), the court’s order shall bar any other joint tortfeasor or co-obligor from any further claims against the settling tortfeasor or co-obligor for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault. 

 

 

 

 

The court orders defendants Descartes Financial Corporation and Descartes Financial, LTD to give notice of this ruling. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority

(1) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production

(2) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with these motions.

DISCUSSION

Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production

Plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order (1) compelling defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (“Defendant”) to serve further responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production, Set Two, No. 54, and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve a further response to Request No. 54 because the objections in the response are without merit.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a)(3).)

The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).)  The court finds that $1,500 (3 hours x $500 per hour = $1,500 attorney’s fees) is a reasonable amount of sanctions to impose against Defendant in connection with this motion. 

Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories

Plaintiff moves the court for an order (1) compelling Defendant to serve responses to Plaintiff’s Special Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 1-13 and 15-40, and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve further responses to Special Interrogatory Nos. 1-13, 15-20, 22-23, 25-26, 30, and 33-40 because the objections to the interrogatory are without merit.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a)(3).)

The court denies Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve a further response to Special Interrogatory No. 21 because the interrogatory is overbroad since it includes information not relevant to the subject matter involved in this action and not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.010.)

The court denies Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve a further response to Special Interrogatory No. 24 because the interrogatory is unintelligible.

The court denies Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve further responses to Special Interrogatory Nos. 27-29, and 31-32 because the interrogatories contain compound and conjunctive questions. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.060, subd. (f).)   

The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (c).)  The court finds that $3,750 is a reasonable amount of sanctions to impose against Defendant in connection with this motion. 

ORDER

The court GRANTS plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production.

           Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.310, the court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (1) to serve on plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez further written responses, without objections and which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2031.210-2031.250, to plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez’s Request for Production of Documents, Set Two, No. 54 and (2) to produce to plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez all documents and things in defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority’s possession, custody, or control which are responsive to that request, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.

           The court GRANTS plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez’s motion to compel further responses to special interrogatories in part as follows. 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.300, the court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (1) to serve on plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez further, full and complete answers which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2031.210-2031.250, to plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez’s Special Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 1-13, 15-20, 22-23, 25-26, 30, and 33-40, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.

           The court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez on these two motions in the total amount of $5,250 within 30 days of the date of service of this order.

 

 

 

The court orders plaintiff Jennifer Vasquez to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
