Case Number: 25SMCV00988    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE ORDER

Plaintiff Faramarz Shamoeil’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees, Costs, and Expenses is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $10,305 as to attorney fees. Costs shall be awarded per statute.

Plaintiff Faramarz Shamoeil to give notice.

Defendant does not dispute that Plaintiff is entitled to recover fees and costs here. Rather, Defendant argues that there is no basis to award $37,080.90  to Plaintiff, which consists of $30,332.50 in attorney fees, a multiplier of 1.2 for a total of $6,066.50, and costs and expenses of $681.90. The Court has reviewed the motion and the billing sheets provided and finds that the requested fees are excessive and must be reduced. First, there is no basis to employ a multiplier here. While Plaintiff’s counsel took on the case on a contingency basis, this alone does not warrant a multiplier, and the Court cannot conclude that there are other factors warranting a multiplier. Plaintiff’s counsel is in the business of litigating Song-Beverly actions, meaning that many documents consist of work already done and merely edited to account for a different plaintiff, defendant, vehicle, and defect. There is no particular novelty in the work performed, as the Court itself sees that many Song-Beverly actions are substantially similar to each other, and there are no facts showing that this case was unique. Put simply, the skill employed by Plaintiff’s counsel in litigating this action led to the settlement itself, and there is no basis to employ a multiplier in this action.

The Court also reduces the hourly rate from $675 per hour to $450 per hour, as neither the case itself nor any of the tasks in this action were particularly complex. The Court also reduces the hours to reduce time spent collecting and reviewing repair records by 5.0 hours; reduce time spent preparing to file and filing the complaint by 2.0 hours; reduce time spent relating to the Case Management Statements and Conference by 1.5 hours; omit time spent preparing an opposition to Defendant’s motion to compel arbitration by 3.4 hours, given that the motion was not opposed on the papers or at the hearing; and reduce the time spent on this fee motion by 4.7 hours; and reduce the time spent reviewing the opposition to this motion, preparing a reply, and attending the motion hearing by 5.6 hours. Thus, the Court reduces the total amount of attorney hours to 22.9 hours at an hourly rate of $450 per hour, and the Court does not employ a multiplier, for total attorney fees award of $10,305. Accordingly, Plaintiff Faramarz Shamoeil’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees, Costs, and Expenses is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $10,305 as to attorney fees. Costs shall be awarded per statute.

Case Number: 26SMCV01450    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE ORDER

Defendant Michael Payne’s Petition to Compel Arbitration and Stay Proceedings is GRANTED. The proceedings are hereby STAYED pending the outcome of arbitration.

Defendant Michael Payne’s Request for Attorney’s Fees and Costs is DENIED.


“The right to arbitration depends upon contract; a petition to compel arbitration is simply a suit in equity seeking specific performance of that contract.” (Eng’rs & Architects Ass’n v. Cmty. Dev. Dep’t (1994) 30 Cal.App.4th 644, 653.) General principles of contract law determine whether the parties have entered a binding agreement to arbitrate. (Chan v. Drexel Burnham Lambert, Inc. (1986) 178 Cal.App.3d 632, 640-641 [“The existence of a valid agreement to arbitrate involves general contract principles”].)

Alternatively, under Code of Civil Procedure section 638, “[a] referee may be appointed upon the agreement of the parties filed with the clerk, or judge, or entered in the minutes, or upon the motion of a party to a written contract or lease that provides that any controversy arising therefrom shall be heard by a referee if the court finds a reference agreement exists between the parties: [¶] (a) To hear and determine any or all of the issues in an action or proceeding, whether of fact or of law, and to report a statement of decision. [¶] (b) To ascertain a fact necessary to enable the court to determine an action or proceeding.”

Judicial reference is described as follows:

In a judicial reference, a pending court action is sent to a referee for hearing, determination and a report back to the court. A general reference directs the referee to try all issues in the action. The hearing is conducted under the rules of evidence applicable to judicial proceedings. In a general reference, the referee prepares a statement of decision that stands as the decision of the court and is reviewable as if the court had rendered it. The primary effect of such a reference is to require trial by a referee and not by a court or jury.

(Treo @ Kettner Homeowners Association v. Superior Court (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 1055, 1061.)

“A general reference may be had only with the consent of the parties.” (Ellsworth v. Ellsworth (1954) 42 Cal.2d 719, 722.) “[T]he court has no power to make an unconsented-to general reference, which conclusively decides all or part of a matter, because not only is such a general reference not authorized except by explicit agreement of the parties, but also, the California Constitution prevents delegation of judicial power except for the performance of ‘subordinate judicial duties,’” which does not include “[d]eciding a major legal issue in a case, which probably will determine liability.” (Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Superior Court (1986) 182 Cal.App.3d 431, 435-436.)

Because “[a] judicially ordered reference to alternative dispute resolution pursuant to [Code of Civil Procedure] section 638 is a matter of contract between the parties” (Carr Business Enterprises, Inc. v. City of Chowchilla (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 25, 28), the existence of a reference agreement is subject to the normal canons of contract interpretation. (See Badie v. Bank of America (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 779, 800-801.) Appointment of a referee is discretionary, not mandatory. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 638 [“referee may be appointed”].) The Superior Court of Los Angeles County, Local Rules, rule 2.24(m), provides that “[a] written agreement for an order directing a reference is subject to the court’s approval, and the court may, in its discretion, refuse to approve the reference.”

On February 6, 2024, Plaintiff entered into a Home Improvement Contract between him and Defendant, stating, in part, as follows:

ARBITRATION OF DISPUTES

NOTICE: BY INITIALIZING IN THE SPACE BELOW YOU ARE AGREEING TO HAVE ANY DISPUTE ARISING OUT OF THE MATTERS INCLUDED IN THE ‘ARBITRATION OF DISPUTES’ PROVISION DECIDED BY NEUTRAL ARBITRATION AS PROVIDED BY CALIFORNIA LAW AND YOU ARE GIVING UP ANY RIGHTS YOU MIGHT POSSESS TO HAVE THE DISPUTE LITIGATED IN A COURT OR JURY TRIAL. BY INITIALIZING IN THE SPACE BELOW YOU ARE GIVING UP YOUR JUDICIAL RIGHTS TO DISCOVERY AND APPEAL, UNLESS THOSE RIGHTS ARE SPECIFICALLY INCLUDED IN THE ‘ARBITRATION OF DISPUTES’ PROVISION. IF YOU REFUSE TO SUBMIT TO ARBITRATION AFTER AGREEING TO THIS PROVISION, YOU MAY BE COMPELLED TO ARBITRATE UNDER THE AUTHORITY OF THE BUSINESS AND PROFESSIONS CODE OR OTHER APPLICABLE LAWS. YOUR AGREEMENT TO THIS ARBITRATION PROVISION IS VOLUNTARY.

(Mot., Payne Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. A, Ex. A, at p. 3, bolding omitted.) The agreement is signed by Defendant. (Ibid.) The agreement is bolded and appears in capital letters to set it apart. Similarly, page 4 of the agreement states, at paragraph 9:

ARBITRATION OF DISPUTES: All disputes and controversies arising under or in connection with this Agreement where the amount in controversy exceeds Five thousand dollars ($5,000) shall be determined by arbitration through the Judicial Arbitration & Mediation Service pursuant to the provisions of California Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) §§638-645.1[.] The referee shall decide all issues of fact and law, report his decision thereon, and issue legal and equitable relief appropriate under the circumstances. To the extent the referee is unable to issue and/or enforce any legal or equitable relief, either party may petition the court to issue and/or enforce such relief on the basis of the referee’s decision. The California Evidence Code rules of evidence and procedure relating to the conduct of the hearing, examination of witnesses and presentation of evidence shall apply. Judgment may be entered on the decision of the referee in accordance with CCP §644, and the decision may be excepted to, challenged and appealed according to law.

(Mot., Payne Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. A, Ex. A, at p. 4.)

Plaintiff relies on the second provision’s reference to a “referee” as requiring that this dispute be resolved before a judicial referee. The Court cannot conclude that the parties intended for this to be the case. The reference to a referee lies within a self-described “Arbitration of Disputes” provision, and while the provision cites certain statutes relating to referees rather than arbitrators, it is not clear that Defendant would have understood this provision as requiring resolution before a judicial referee where the provision is described as relating to arbitration. Given that the law states that appointment of a referee is discretionary even where a judicial reference provision exists, the Court cannot conclude that resolution before a referee is warranted where the language is ambiguous and does not clearly place Defendant on notice that it agreed to resolution before a judicial referee. Given that Plaintiff provides no argument that the arbitration provision is not valid, and Defendant has provided evidence of a valid arbitration agreement, Defendant Michael Payne’s Petition to Compel Arbitration and Stay Proceedings is GRANTED. The proceedings are hereby STAYED pending the outcome of arbitration.

Defendant asks the Court to award him attorney’s fees and costs pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1293.2, which states that “[t]he court shall award costs upon any judicial proceeding under this title as provided in Chapter 6 (commencing with Section 1021) of Title 14 of Part 2 of this code.” This is a motion to compel arbitration within another action, not a petition to compel arbitration filed as its own judicial proceeding, such that the Court cannot conclude that attorney fees and costs are proper at this juncture. Accordingly, Defendant Michael Payne’s Request for Attorney’s Fees and Costs is DENIED.

Case Number: 26SMCV02078    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants Matthew Mayne and Sofia Grant’s Special Motion to Strike Complaint Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 (Anti-SLAPP) is GRANTED.

Defendants Matthew Mayne and Sofia Grant shall prepare, serve, and submit a proposed judgment as per statute.

Defendants Matthew Mayne and Sofia Grant to give notice. 

First Prong: Claim Arising from Protected Activity
To invoke Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16, a defendant need only demonstrate that a suit arises from the defendant’s exercise of free speech or petition rights. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (b); City of Cotati v. Cashman (2002) 29 Cal.4th 69, 78.) This is determined by “the gravamen or principal thrust of the action.” (See In re Episcopal Church Cases (2009) 45 Cal.4th 467, 477.) “In the anti-SLAPP context, the critical point is whether the plaintiff’s cause of action itself was based on an act in furtherance of the defendant’s right of petition or free speech.” (City of Cotati v. Cashman, supra, 29 Cal.4th at p. 78.) “In making its determination, the court shall consider the pleadings, and supporting and opposing affidavits stating the facts upon which the liability or defense is based.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (b)(2).)

Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16, subdivision (e)(1), states that “any written or oral statement or writing made before a legislative, executive, or judicial proceeding, or any other official proceeding authorized by law” or “any written or oral statement or writing made in connection with an issue under consideration or review by a legislative, executive, or judicial body, or any other official proceeding authorized by law” is considered protected activity under the anti-SLAPP statute.

In the complaint, Plaintiff takes issue with Defendant Mayne’s submission of a false declaration to the court in support of a conservatorship petition (Compl. ¶ 76), and it is clear that submission of a declaration to the court falls under the description of a writing “made before a . . . judicial proceeding” or “made in connection with an issue under consideration or review by a . . . judicial body.” Plaintiff also brings this action based on Mayne’s endorsement of allegations and lending credibility to a petition for conservatorship and assistance with a conservatorship petition (ibid.), which also constitutes protected activity. There are no specific allegations against Defendant Grant (see Compl. ¶ 9), such that the Court must conclude that this claim, too, arises from protected activity. In opposition, Plaintiff contends that the gravamen of the claim is the unlawful deprivation of an elder’s property, i.e., a financial taking, but there is no allegation of a taking in the complaint. It is clear from the complaint that Plaintiff’s claim for elder abuse, the one actually alleged in the complaint, would not arise without the alleged submission of a declaration connected to the conservatorship petition. Put simply, the allegations are not background, as Plaintiff argues; they are the basis of the alleged claim. Therefore, the Court finds that Defendants have satisfied the first prong of the anti-SLAPP statute as to Plaintiff’s claims, as Plaintiff’s claim arises from protected activity.

Second Prong: Plaintiff’s Likelihood of Prevailing on the Merits 
If the defendant makes a threshold showing that the challenged cause of action is one arising from protected activity, the burden shifts to the plaintiff to establish a likelihood of prevailing on the complaint. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (b)(1).) “In order to establish a probability of prevailing on the claim, a plaintiff responding to an anti-SLAPP motion must state and substantiate a legally sufficient claim.” (Wilson v. Parker, Covert & Chidester (2002) 28 Cal.4th 811, 821, superseded by statute on other grounds as noted in Hutton v. Hafif (2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 527, 547, citation, quotation marks, and brackets omitted.) To do so, “the plaintiff must demonstrate that the complaint is both legally sufficient and supported by a sufficient prima facie showing of facts to sustain a favorable judgment if the evidence submitted by the plaintiff is credited.” (Ibid., quotation marks omitted.) In considering whether a plaintiff’s claim has merit, “the trial court considers the pleadings and evidentiary submissions of both the plaintiff and the defendant,” and while “the court does not weigh the credibility or comparative probative strength of competing evidence, it should grant the motion if, as a matter of law, the defendant’s evidence supporting the motion defeats the plaintiff’s attempt to establish evidentiary support for the claim.” (Ibid., emphasis in original.) 

Civil Code section 47, subdivision (b), makes privileged any statements made during judicial proceedings, which “applies to any communication (1) made in judicial or quasi-judicial proceedings; (2) by litigants or other participants authorized by law; (3) to achieve the objects of the litigation; and (4) that have some connection or logical relation to the action.” (Silberg v. Anderson (1990) 50 Cal.3d 205, 212.) Such communications are protected by the litigation privilege for “all torts except malicious prosecution.” (Ibid.) As stated above, the complaint makes clear that Plaintiff takes issue with conduct relating to a conservatorship petition, and the conduct at issue occurred as part of the litigation process in regards to that petition. It follows that the claims are barred by the litigation privilege, and the Court need not determine whether Plaintiff may otherwise be able to prove his claims. Accordingly, Defendants Matthew Mayne and Sofia Grant’s Special Motion to Strike Complaint Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 (Anti-SLAPP) is GRANTED. Defendants Matthew Mayne and Sofia Grant shall prepare, serve, and submit a proposed judgment as per statute.

Attorney Fees 
Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16, subdivision (c), provides that “a prevailing defendant on a special motion to strike shall be entitled to recover his or her attorney’s fees and costs.” Under section 425.16, an award of fees and costs is mandatory. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1131.) In that Defendants are the prevailing parties, they are entitled to their reasonable attorney fees and costs, the amount of which shall be determined upon Defendants filing a noticed motion for attorney fees and memorandum of costs.
