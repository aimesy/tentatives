(2) PETITIONS TO APPROVE COMPROMISE OF PENDING ACTION ON BEHALF OF MINORS

 

Petitions filed on June 17, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Petitioner Jeffery Huffman, as guardian ad litem for minor claimants Kinsley Huffman and Kyler Huffman

RESPONDING PARTY: None

NOTICE: Defective.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Approval of minors’ compromise petitions as to Kinsley Huffman and Kyler Huffman.

RULING: Denied without prejudice.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On August 1, 2023, Plaintiffs filed the Complaint against Defendant Ric Garrison (Defendant), asserting claims arising from a motor vehicle collision. The Court granted applications appointing Jeffrey Huffman as guardian ad litem for the minor plaintiffs, including Kinsley Huffman and Kyler Huffman.

 

On January 8, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case.

 

On June 17, 2026, Petitioner filed a Petition for Approval of Compromise of Pending Action on behalf of Kinsley Huffman, together with proposed forms MC-351 and MC-355. The petition reflects a gross settlement of $25,001.00, with $4,010.00 in medical expenses, $6,250.25 in attorney’s fees, and $2,982.91 in costs, leaving $11,757.84 for deposit into a blocked account.

 

On the same date, Petitioner filed a Petition for Approval of Compromise of Pending Action on behalf of Kyler Huffman, together with proposed forms MC-351 and MC-355. The petition reflects a gross settlement of $17,501.00, with $2,700.00 in medical expenses, $4,375.25 in attorney’s fees, and $3,013.97 in costs, leaving $7,411.78 for deposit into a blocked account.

 

No proofs of service of the petitions were filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Notice

 

Neither petition is accompanied by a proof of service. The Court therefore cannot make the finding required by form MC-351 that notice was given as required by law.

 

Required Medical Reports

 

Item 8 of form MC-350 requires attachment of a doctor’s report containing a diagnosis or prognosis and a report of the claimant’s current condition. Both petitions state that the minors have recovered completely, but neither petition includes the required Attachment 8 report.

 

Attorney’s Fees and Agreements

 

Each petition states that Petitioner and counsel have a written agreement for legal services. Item 17a requires that a copy of the agreement be attached as Attachment 17a. Neither petition includes the written fee agreement.

 

Medical Expenses

 

Although the aggregate medical-expense calculations reconcile, the disclosures in item 12b require clarification. Kinsley’s petition identifies $110.00 as paid by a non-ERISA self-funded plan, while the stated $254.00 reduction and $740.00 reimbursement correspond to a separate $994.00 MCA/High Desert charge shown in the attached schedule. Kyler’s petition similarly identifies $1,113.45 as paid by the plan, while the stated $203.40 reduction and $550.00 reimbursement correspond to a separate $753.40 MCA/Sierra charge. The petitions should clarify MCA’s role and correctly identify the amounts paid by, and reimbursable to, any plan or lienholder, if necessary.

 

Item 14

 

Neither petition checks item 14a or item 14b concerning reimbursement of expenses paid or incurred by Petitioner.

 

Item 17f

 

Both petitions state in item 17f that counsel expects to receive additional attorney’s fees from the other plaintiffs “Upon Settlement.” The corresponding attachments state that those plaintiffs “has paid” the fees. The renewed petitions must clarify whether those amounts have already been received or remain payable upon settlement.

 

Proposed Orders

 

The proposed forms MC-351 correctly calculate the deductions and net recoveries, and the intended distributions are apparent. However, item 8b is completed inconsistently. Item 8b concerns only the balance remaining for the minor after the fees and expenses authorized in item 8a, but the proposed orders use item 8b(2) to describe both the item 8a payments and the minor’s net recovery. The proposed orders should be corrected so that item 8b addresses only each minor’s net recovery and the blocked-account disposition is made consistently under item 8b(1), as referenced in item 9.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The petitions to approve compromise of pending action on behalf of Kinsley Huffman and Kyler Huffman are denied without prejudice.

 

The OSC re Dismissal (Settlement) is continued to September 21, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department F-46 to permit the filing and hearing of renewed petitions.

DEFENDANT AMERICAN CREDIT ACCEPTANCE, LLC’S MOTION TO COMPEL ARBITRATION AND STAY ACTION

 

Motion filed on May 26, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant American Credit Acceptance, LLC (Defendant)

RESPONDING PARTY: None.

NOTICE: Defective. The moving papers were served on self-represented Plaintiff Deandra M. Marshall (Plaintiff) by email only, and the record does not establish Plaintiff’s express consent to electronic service.

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Plaintiff to arbitrate her claims and staying this action pending arbitration.

RULING: Continued to September 16, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department F-46.

 

BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION

 

On October 23, 2025, Deandra M. Marshall (Plaintiff), in propria persona, filed the Complaint against American Credit Acceptance, LLC (Defendant). Defendant filed its first motion to compel arbitration on March 13, 2026. On May 18, 2026, the Court denied the motion without prejudice because the contract containing the arbitration provision was not attached and the supporting declaration did not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 2015.5. Defendant filed the renewed motion on May 26, 2026, supported by the declaration of Courtney Griffin and the Retail Installment Sale Contract. No timely opposition was filed.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6, subdivision (c)(3), provides that an unrepresented party may expressly consent to electronic service by serving and filing a notice or by affirmatively consenting through the court or its electronic filing service provider while providing an electronic service address. “The act of electronic filing shall not be construed as express consent.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (c)(3).) California Rules of Court, rule 2.251(b)(1), sets forth the procedures for providing that consent.

 

Here, Defendant served the motion and supporting declaration on self-represented Plaintiff by email only. The record does not show that Plaintiff served and filed a notice accepting electronic service, filed form EFS-005-CV, or affirmatively consented through the court or its electronic filing service provider. Plaintiff’s prior electronic filing and use of email to serve Defendant do not establish express consent. Although the Court is inclined to grant the motion, the Court will continue the hearing to permit proper service on Plaintiff.


CONCLUSION

 

The hearing is continued to September 16, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department F-46. Defendant shall properly serve the motion and supporting papers no later than August 7, 2026, and shall file proof of service.

DEFENDANT VICTOR TANKAZYAN’S DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT AND DEFENDANT DUDA ADAMS’S JOINDER

 

Motion filed on April 29, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Victor Tankazyan, joined by Defendant Duda Adams

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Suraiya Zoobi Ahmed

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order sustaining demurrers to the first through fourth causes of action for uncertainty and failure to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

RULING: Overruled.

 

BACKGROUND

 

Suraiya Zoobi Ahmed (Plaintiff) filed the Complaint on February 19, 2026, asserting causes of action for attempted illegal eviction and tenant harassment, interference with quiet enjoyment, trespass and unauthorized entry, and injunctive and declaratory relief. Victor Tankazyan (Tankazyan) filed the demurrer on April 29, 2026, supported by the declaration of Gary Kurtz. Duda Adams (Adams) filed a notice joining the demurrer on the same date. No timely opposition was filed.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

DISCUSSION

 

Scope of Demurrer

 

The notice identifies Tankazyan as the defendant seeking relief, and Adams separately joined the demurrer. Although V&A Construction, Inc. appears in counsel’s signature block, it did not file a notice of demurrer or a joinder. The Court therefore considers the demurrer only as to Tankazyan and Adams.

 

Uncertainty

 

California Rules of Court, rule 2.112(4) requires each cause of action to specifically state “[t]he party or parties to whom it is directed.” Failure to comply with rule 2.112 may render a complaint subject to a special demurrer for uncertainty. (Grappo v. McMills (2017) 11 Cal.App.5th 996, 1014.) A demurrer for uncertainty should nevertheless be overruled where the substantive allegations sufficiently apprise the defendant of the issues it is being asked to meet. (Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, fn. 2.)

 

Here, none of the cause-of-action headings identifies the defendant or defendants against whom the claim is directed. The allegations also shift between “Defendants” collectively and the singular possessive “Defendant’s.” (Compl. ¶¶ 22-29, 31-36, 38-49.)

 

Read as a whole, however, the Complaint sufficiently indicates that each cause of action is asserted against all Defendants. Plaintiff alleges that Adams claimed ownership or control of the property and authorized or ratified the challenged conduct, Tankazyan exercised control over V&A and authorized or ratified the conduct of its workers, and V&A acted through its workers and agents. Plaintiff also alleges that each Defendant acted as the agent, employee, co-conspirator, or representative of the others. (Compl. ¶¶ 5-9.) The causes of action repeatedly attribute the conduct to “Defendants,” and the prayer seeks relief against “Defendants, and each of them.” (Compl. ¶¶ 22-26, 32, 38, 41, 46-49, Prayer.)

 

The singular references are imprecise, but in context they do not create a reasonable ambiguity concerning which defendants are being sued. Unlike the complaint addressed in Grappo, this Complaint identifies the defendants, alleges their respective roles, and describes their asserted relationships to one another. The failure to comply with rule 2.112 does not substantially impair Tankazyan’s or Adams’s ability to understand the claims or determine what must be admitted or denied. The special demurrers for uncertainty are overruled.

 

First Through Third Causes of Action

 

Moving Parties do not identify any element of the first, second, or third cause of action that is insufficiently alleged. They argue only that Plaintiff fails to specify “who allegedly did what and when” and to identify the individual workers, their authority, and whether they acted within the scope of that authority. (Dem. at 3, 8.) That argument substantially repeats the lack-of-specificity challenge raised in support of uncertainty and does not establish that the first three causes of action fail to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

 

The general demurrers for the first through third causes of action are overruled.

 

Fourth Cause of Action

 

An injunction is a remedy, not an independent cause of action. (Church Mut. Ins. Co., S.I. v. GuideOne Specialty Mut. Ins. Co. (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 1042, 1051, fn. 1.) Declaratory relief requires “(1) a proper subject of declaratory relief, and (2) an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to the party’s rights or obligations.” (Jolley v. Chase Home Finance, LLC (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 872, 909.)

 

Here, the fourth cause of action incorporates paragraphs 1 through 43. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants treated her as an unauthorized occupant, attempted to exclude her from the property without lawful process, demanded that she vacate, and may continue attempting to change the locks, obstruct access, or otherwise interfere with her possession. Plaintiff seeks a declaration that Defendants may not remove her or interfere with her possession without complying with lawful eviction procedures. (Compl. ¶¶ 17-20, 44-49.)

 

Moving Parties do not explain why a declaration would be unnecessary or improper under Code of Civil Procedure section 1061 as a matter of law. Although injunctive relief is not an independent cause of action, the fourth cause of action adequately alleges declaratory relief. A general demurrer does not lie to only part of a cause of action. (PH II, Inc. v. Superior Court (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1680, 1682-1683.) The general demurrer to the fourth cause of action is overruled.

 

The general demurrer to the fourth cause of action is overruled.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The special demurrers for uncertainty and the general demurrers for failure to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action are overruled.

 

Tankazyan and Adams shall file and serve their answers within 10 days after service of notice of ruling.

 

The Court does not rule on behalf of V&A Construction, Inc.
