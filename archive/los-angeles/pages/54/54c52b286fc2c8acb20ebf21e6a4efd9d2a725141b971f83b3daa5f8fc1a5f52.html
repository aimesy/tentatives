Case Number: 24STLC09285    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Mihee Kim’s Motion to Facts Admitted and Request for Sanctions is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Mihee Kim (“Plaintiff”) served Requests for Admissions, Set One, on Defendant Jason Lee (“Defendant Lee”) on March 13, 2026. (Motion, Kim Decl., ¶2 and Exhs. A-B.) As of the filing of the instant Motion to Deem Requests of Admission Admitted, on May 13, 2026, Defendant Lee had not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶5.) Plaintiff now moves for an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280 and for an award of sanctions. Defendant Lee filed an opposition on June 17, 2026 and Plaintiff replied on June 23, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to deem requests for admission can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to serve responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) The Court is required to deem the requests admitted unless substantially compliant responses are served prior to the hearing date. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Defendant Lee served responses to the Requests for Admissions, Set One, on June 4, 2026. (Opp., Exh. 1.) Plaintiff acknowledges receipt of the responses and, with the exception of Nos. 21 and 28, does not argue that they are not in substantial compliance with the Discovery Code. (Reply, Kim Decl., ¶5.) The request to deem the admissions admitted, therefore, is denied.

 

The Court is required to award sanctions under the moving statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) However, sanctions may be denied if the Court finds the lack of a timely response was substantially justified or imposition of the sanctions would be unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030.) On April 8, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Ex-Parte Application For Order: (1) Declaring Defendant’s Counsel of Record Disqualified Due To Disbarment; (2) Ordering Defendant to Retain New Counsel or Appear in Propria Persona; and (3) Directing Direct Service of Future Filings and Service Directly to Defendant. (Minute Order, 04/08/26.)

 

The Court does not find that Plaintiff propounded the discovery in bad faith. At the time of service, although Defendant’s attorney had been disbarred, the Court and parties had not resolved how to handle service in this pending action. Defendant’s former attorney, Kevin Jang, was no longer practicing at the time the discovery was served upon him, and he promptly forwarded the discovery to Defendant upon receipt. (Motion, Kim Decl., Exh. A, pp. 2-3.) However, the Court finds that an award of sanctions would be unjust given service of the discovery during a time that Defendant Lee’s attorney had been disbarred, and Defendant Lee was in the process of learning of this disbarment and finding new counsel.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Mihee Kim’s Motion to Facts Admitted and Request for Sanctions is denied.

Case Number: 25STCP02905    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Rombro & Manley LLP’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is Granted. Judgment is to be entered in the amount of $23,991.42 principal, plus interest accruing at the daily rate of $5.55 from May 21, 2025, until paid. Costs are awarded according to proof. Petitioner is to file a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

On August 1, 2025, Petitioner Rombro & Manley LLP (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award against Respondent Sherita Marie Bernardez (“Respondent”). The Court continued the hearing date from December 5, 2025 to December 22, 2025 and gave notice to both parties. (Notice Re: Continuance, 11/19/25.) The December 22, 2025 hearing date was then continued at Respondent’s request to February 10, 2026. (Minute Order, 12/18/25.) Notice of the continuance was served on Respondent by first-class mail on December 19, 2025. (Notice of Continuance, filed 12/19/25.)

 

On February 10, 2026, the parties appeared for the hearing and represented that they were trying to reach a conditional settlement. (Minute Order, 02/10/26.) The Court continued the hearing upon the parties’ stipulation to July 1, 2026. Petitioner gave notice of this continuance, and it remains that no response to the Petition has been filed to date.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party to an arbitration in which an award has been made may petition the court to confirm, correct or vacate the award.  The petition shall name as respondent all parties to the arbitration and may name as respondents any other persons bound by the arbitration award.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.) “If a petition or response under this chapter is duly served and filed, the court shall confirm the award as made, whether rendered in this state or another state, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.)

 

A response to a petition to confirm an arbitration award, which seeks to vacate or correct the award, must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288.2.)

 

Discussion

 

Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing

 

Code of Civil Procedure, section 1290.4 requires that the petition and notice of hearing be served on Respondent “in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice” or “[i]f the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made . . . [s]ervice within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4, subds. (a), (b).)

 

The arbitration agreement is attached to the Petition, but does not provide for the manner of service for a petition to confirm arbitration award. (Pet., Attachment 4b, ¶¶17-18.) Therefore, the Court finds that the Petition and Notice of Hearing must be served in the same manner as a summons. On August 12, 2025, Petitioner filed proof of personal service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing with respect to Respondent, showing that service was effectuated on August 9, 2025. (Proof of Personal Service, filed 08/12/25, ¶3.) This satisfies the service requirement of Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4.

 

Service of the Arbitration Award (CCP § 1283.6)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 requires that “[t]he neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1283.6.) This requirement may be satisfied by service by the arbitrator, or upon proper service of the award with the petition. (See Murray v. Civil Service Employees Ins. Co. (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 796, 799-800.) The arbitration award was served on the parties on June 2, 2025. (Pet., Attachment 8(c), pp. 13-14.)

 

Confirmation of the Arbitration Award

 

An arbitration award is not directly enforceable until it is confirmed by a court and judgment is entered.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1287.6; Jones v. Kvistad (1971) 19 Cal.App.3d 836, 840.) The court must confirm the award as made, unless it corrects or vacates the award, or dismisses the proceeding.  (CCP § 1286; Valsan Partners Limited Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility, Inc. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.) Code of Civil Procedure, section 1285.4 states that a petition under this chapter shall:

 

a)     Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

b)     Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

c)     Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4.) The Petition complies with these requirements. It sets forth the nature of the arbitration agreement, the name of the arbitrator, and attaches a copy of the arbitration award. (Pet., ¶¶6-8 and Attachment 8(c).) The award provides that the arbitrator awarded Petitioners $23,991.42, plus interest accruing at the daily rate of $5.55 from May 21, 2025, until paid. (Id. at Attachment 8(c), p. 13.) Therefore, the arbitration award is confirmed in favor of Petitioner in that amount.

 

Costs

 

The Petition also requests costs according to proof, which are permitted under Code of Civil Procedure section 1293.2. Petitioner may file a memorandum of costs pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner Rombro & Manley LLP’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is Granted. Judgment is to be entered in the amount of $23,991.42, plus interest accruing at the daily rate of $5.55 from May 21, 2025, until paid. Costs are awarded according to proof. Petitioner is to file a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

Case Number: 25STLC02798    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant A&B Maintenance and General Contractors dba A & B General Contractors Co.’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons and Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is denied.

 

 

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On April 8, 2025, Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant collections action against Defendant A&B Maintenance and General Contractors dba A & B General Contractors Co. (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered its default on December 23, 2025, and default judgment on January 28, 2026.

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Quash Service of Summons and Set Aside Default and Default Judgment on April 14, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 17, 2026 and Defendant replied on June 24, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Quash Service and Deem Entry of Default and Default Judgment Void Due to Improper Service

 

Defendant first moves to quash service of the Summons and Complaint pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, and for relief from the default and default judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d). Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10 provides: “A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1), emphasis added.) A defendant has 30 days after the service of the summons to file a responsive pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 412.20, subd. (a)(3).)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) provides that “[t]he court may, .... on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) In California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn (2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, the California Supreme Court held that there is no two-year limitation to challenge a void default judgment, regardless of whether it is valid on its face. (California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn (2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, 225-226.) A motion for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), therefore, can be brought at any time.

 

On November 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed Proof of Substitute Service, which states that the papers were served on Defendant through Afshin Salahshour, Registered Agent, by leaving them with “M. Rochter Doe,” refused to give first name, clerk at 6442 Platt Avenue #140 West Hills, CA. (Proof of Substitute Service, 11/25/25, ¶¶3-5.) M. Rochter Doe is described as “a person at least 18 years of age apparently in charge at the office or usual place of business of the person to be served.” (Id. at ¶5.) The papers were then mailed to the same address on November 5, 2025. (Ibid.)

 

Defendant’s Motion challenges the proof of service as failing to demonstrate compliance with the requirements for service at a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (“CMRA”), as set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 415.20, subdivision (c), which allows service on a corporation at a private mailbox obtained through a CMRA in accordance with Business & Professions Code section 17538.5. Under the Business & Professions Code, the owner or operator of the CMRA shall place a copy of the documents or notice of their receipt in the customer’s mailbox within 48 hours, and send the documents to the customer’s last known address within five days of receipt. (Bus. & Profs. Code, § 17538.5, subd. (d).) Defendant argues that there is no evidence Plaintiff complied with these requirements.  In opposition, Plaintiff presents evidence from the manager of the UPS store at 6442 Platt Avenue that notice of the papers delivered by the process server were left in Box No. 104 and then mailed to the last known address for Mr. Salahshour, the Registered Agent. (Opp., Star Decl., ¶¶8-9 and Exh. 1.) Plaintiff, therefore, has demonstrated that service upon Defendant to the mailbox located at 6442 Platt Avenue complied with the statutory requirements for service.

 

Defendant also argues that 6442 Platt Avenue address is not Defendant’s corporate office or place of business. This is not relevant. Section 415.20 allows for service at a private mailbox where that is the only address reasonably known.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20(c).) Defendant does not argue or demonstrate that Plaintiff could have served it at its corporate office or place of business.  6442 Platt Avenue, as the address of Defendant’s agent for service of process, was an appropriate address for service under Code of Civil Procedure section 415.20, subdivision (c).

 

In its reply, Defendant argues that the proof of service filed by Plaintiff attests to service under 415.20, subdivision (b), and that the requirements for service were not met because the documents were left with a UPS employee.  This is a technical defect in the proof of service but does not establish that service was not proper.  (Crenshaw v. Smith (1946) 74 Cal. App. 2d 255, 263 (“[I]t is the fact of service and not the proof of service which determines the validity or invalidity of a judgment.”).  The proof of service accurately states where and with whom the documents were left. The Starr Declaration establishes that the notice and mailing requirements of Business & Professions Code section 17538.5 were met.  The use of the term “substitute service” in the proof of service does render the judgment void.

 

Therefore, the request to quash service of the Summons and deem the entry of default and default judgment void, are denied.

 

Relieve Defendant from Entry of Default and Default Judgment on account of Mistake, Surprise, Inadvertence, or Excusable Neglect

 

Defendant alternatively moves for relief from the entry of default and default judgment on account of its mistake, surprise, inadvertence, or excusable neglect. Relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) must be sought within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

 

First, the Motion does not show that it was brought within a reasonable time. It only argues that the Motion is timely because it was filed within the six-month outer deadline, but does not address the reasonableness requirement. Where the statute requires that “ ‘ “the application must be made within a ‘reasonable time’ … what is a reasonable time in any case depends upon the circumstances of that particular case.” While in “the determination of that question, a large discretion is necessarily confided to [the trial] court” ... there must be some showing—some evidence—as the basis for the exercise of such discretion.’ ” (Caldwell v. Methodist Hospital (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1521, 1524 [citing Carrasco v. Craft (1985) 164 Cal.App.3d 796, 805].) Defendant retained counsel in early February 2026, at which time defense counsel reached out to Plaintiff’s counsel. (Motion, Sadeghi Decl., ¶¶12-15 and Exhs. H-J.) The attorneys’ discussion concluded by February 12, 2026, but Defendant did not file the instant Motion for another two months. No explanation is provided for this delay.

 

Nor does Defendant provide a reasonable excuse for failing to respond to the action. Defendant’s CEO, also Afshin Salahshour, declares that he believed only personal service of a summons and complaint triggers the need to respond to an action. (Motion, Salahshour Decl., ¶13.) No other explanation is provided regarding the source of this erroneous belief, nor did Salahshour apparently seek to confirm this belief with anyone, or through any resource. This does not describe excusable conduct, but rather inexcusable neglect.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant A&B Maintenance and General Contractors dba A & B General Contractors Co.’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons and Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC02833    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Esurance Insurance Company’s Demurrer to Complaint is sustained with 20 days’ leave to amend.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Irina Margaryan (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for breach of contract against Defendant Esurance Insurance Company (“Defendant”) on April 9, 2025. It is not known when Defendant was served with the Summons and Complaint in this action. Defendant filed the instant Demurrer to the Complaint on June 5, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 11, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

A meet and confer declaration is required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. The Demurrer is accompanied by a minimal meet and confer declaration that simply states Plaintiff failed to respond to Defendant’s request to meet and confer, or otherwise failed to meet and confer in good faith. (Declaration of Demurring Party, filed 06/05/26, ¶2b.) After the Demurrer was filed, Plaintiff’s counsel sent a response stating that a First Amended Complaint has been prepared to address the issues raised in the Demurrer. (Opp., Torosian Decl., Exh. B.) Plaintiff’s supporting exhibits, therefore, are in agreement with the Demurrer the Complaint is defective and in need of amendment.

 

Indeed, a review of the Complaint demonstrates that it lacks the basic elements of a cause of action for breach of contract. The elements of a cause of action for breach of contract are (1) the existence of a valid contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance of his obligations, or excuse from performance, (3) defendant’s breach of the contract, and (4) resulting damages to plaintiff. (CACI No. 303; Richman v. Hartley (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1182, 1186.) The Complaint makes no allegation of the breach of the purported contract by Defendant. (Compl., ¶BC-2.)

 

In light of the foregoing, the Demurrer to the Complaint is sustained with leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Esurance Insurance Company’s Demurrer to Complaint is sustained with 20 days’ leave to amend.

Case Number: 25STLC02921    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Motion of Plaintiff Scott Jacoby, Trustee of the Lare Ocean Blvd. Trust for Order Re-Assigning this Action to Department 12, the Honorable Barbara A. Meiers, is placed off calendar.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service of the Summons and Complaint is scheduled for 07/15/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Reassignment of Action to Unlimited Civil Court is scheduled for 07/15/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Scott Jacoby, Trustee of the Lare Ocean Blvd. Trust (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for “ejectment” against Defendant Anthony Felix, Jr. (“Defendant”) on April 8, 2026. Proof of Service of the Summons and Complaint, which appears to have been improperly attempted by first-class mail, was filed on March 19, 2026.

 

On April 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Order Re-Assigning this Action to Department 12, the Honorable Barbara A. Meiers.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an order relating this action to unlawful detainer and quiet title actions, Case Nos. 22STCV07491, BC722193 and 22LBUD0045, and re-assigning this action for all purposes to Department 12, the Honorable Barbara A. Meiers, the judicial officer who presided over both the unlawful detainer and the quiet title actions. The Motion is brought pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.300 and Los Angeles Superior Court, rule 3.3(f).

 

Neither of these rules supports the relief requested. Instead, they articulate a particular procedural mechanism to relate cases, with which Plaintiff has not complied.

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.300 defines a “related case” and mandates that a party that knows of related proceedings must serve and file a Notice of Related Case in all pending cases listed in the notice, within 15 days of learning of the facts concerning the related cases. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.300(b)-(e).) Once the Notice of Related Case is filed, if “the cases listed in the notice include both unlimited and limited civil cases, the judge who has the earliest filed unlimited civil case must determine whether the cases should be ordered related and assigned to his or her department.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule (h)(1)(B).) Plaintiff has not filed a Notice of Related Case in the instant action, despite knowing of the other cases at the time this action was filed in April 2025.

 

Los Angeles Superior Court, rule 3.3(f) provides that if, after filing the Notice of Related Case, the judge designated in California Rules of Court, rule 3.300 does not order the cases related, the party may file a motion to have the cases related in Department 1. (LASC Rules, rule 3.3(f)((3).) Given that no Notice of Related Case has been filed in this action and the ones Plaintiff contends are related, the triggering event for the filing of a motion to have the cases related has not yet occurred. Furthermore, such a motion would have to be heard in Department 1.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court takes the instant Motion for Order Re-Assigning this Action to Department 12, the Honorable Barbara A. Meiers off calendar as premature and as scheduled in the wrong department.

 

Additionally, the Court sets an Order to Show Cause regarding whether an action for ejectment is within the jurisdiction of the Limited Civil Court, under Code of Civil Procedure sections 85, 86, and 580(b).  Specifically, an action for “[a] determination of title to real property” may not be heard in a limited civil court.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 580(b)(3).)

 

The Court also sets an Order to Show Cause re: Proof of Service.  The Proof of Mailing filed March 19, 2026 is on a form that states it is NOT to be used to establish proof of service of the summons and complaint and does not establish the requirements for substitute service.

 

Conclusion

 

Motion of Plaintiff Scott Jacoby, Trustee of the Lare Ocean Blvd. Trust for Order Re-Assigning Action for Department 12, the Honorable Barbara A. Meiers, is placed off calendar.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service of the Summons and Complaint is scheduled for 07/15/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Reassignment of Action to Unlimited Civil Court is scheduled for 07/15/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 25STLC09554    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

In light of the stay of this action, the following hearings are placed off calendar and may be rescheduled upon the lifting of the stay:

 

-Plaintiff Elena Berman’s Motion to Compel Further Responses; and Motion for Order Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted, set for hearing on 07/01/2026.

 

-Plaintiff Elena Berman’s Motion for Order Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted Pursuant to CCP § 2033.280, set for hearing on 07/16/2026.

 

-Defendants Joel Bauer, Cheryl Bauer, and Joel Bauer and Associates Inc.’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, set for hearing on 07/28/2026.

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Elena Berman (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the instant action against Defendants Joel Bauer (“Defendant Joel), Cheryl Bauer (“Defendant Cheryl”), and Joel Bauer and Associates Inc. (“Defendant JB”) on December 29, 2025. Defendants filed their Answer on March 3, 2026.

 

On May 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses; and Motion for Order Deeming Requests For Admission Admitted. On June 12, 2026, Defendants filed a Motion to Declare Vexatious Litigant; in relation to that motion, Defendants filed a Notice of Automatic Stay on June 24, 2026.

 

Plaintiff filed a reply in support of the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses; and Motion for Order Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted on June 25, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Court finds that the instant Motion is stayed in light of Defendants’ Motion to Declare Vexatious Litigant, which is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 391.1. Section 391.6 expressly provides that “when a motion pursuant to Section 391.1 is filed prior to trial the litigation is stayed … until 10 days after the motion shall have been denied, or if granted, until 10 days after the required security has been furnished and the moving defendant given written notice thereof.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.6.) The hearing on the Motion to Declare Vexatious Litigant is set for July 28, 2026. Therefore, the stay is in effect until at least August 7, 2026.

 

Additionally, the Court notes that Plaintiff improperly filed a combined motion to compel further responses with respect to six separate sets of discovery: (1) Special Interrogatories as to Defendant Joel; (2) Special Interrogatories as to Defendant Cheryl; (3) Special Interrogatories as to Defendant JB; (4) Requests for Production as to Defendant Joel; (5) Requests for Production as to Defendant Cheryl; and (6) Requests for Production as to Defendant JB; and a combined motion to deem requests for admission with respect to three separate sets of discovery: (1) Requests for Admission as to Defendant Joel; (2) Requests for Admission as to Defendant Cheryl; and (3) Requests for Admission as to Defendant JB.

 

Filing a single motion to compel further responses or to deem admissions admitted with respect to multiple sets of discovery creates the following problems. First, it negatively impacts the Court’s calendar by placing more motions on the calendar than slots have been provided by the online reservation system. Second, it results in confusion in the motion papers regarding the arguments and evidence with respect to each set for discovery. Finally, combining multiple discovery sets into a single motion allows the moving party to avoid paying the requisite filing fees. Statutorily required filing fees are jurisdictional and “it is mandatory for the court clerks to demand and receive statutorily required filing fees.” (See Duran v. St. Luke’s Hospital (2003) 114 Cal.App.4th 457, 460.) Although Plaintiff has been granted a fee waiver in this action, they must still file separate motions addressing each set of discovery as to each separate Defendant, the separate underlying legal basis, and the separate evidence in support thereof. Here, Plaintiff effectively combines nine discovery motions into a single motion.  

 

Conclusion

 

In light of the stay of this action, the following hearings are placed off calendar and may be rescheduled upon the lifting of the stay:

 

-Plaintiff Elena Berman’s Motion to Compel Further Responses; and Motion for Order Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted, set for hearing on 07/01/2026.

 

-Plaintiff Elena Berman’s Motion for Order Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted Pursuant to CCP § 2033.280, set for hearing on 07/16/2026.

 

-Defendants Joel Bauer, Cheryl Bauer, and Joel Bauer and Associates Inc.’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, set for hearing on 07/28/2026.

Case Number: 26STLC00963    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant The Pep Boys Manny Moe & Jack of California LLC’s Motion to Vacate Default is granted. The default entered on March 20, 2026 and default judgment entered on April 13, 2026 are hereby vacated.

 

Defendant The Pep Boys Manny Moe & Jack of California LLC is to file and serve its Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this order.

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On February 11, 2026, Plaintiff Ashley Citron (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant collections action against Defendant The Pep Boys Manny Moe & Jack of California LLC (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered its default on March 20, 2026, and default judgment on April 13, 2026.

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Vacate Default on May 1, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 5, 2026 and Defendant replied on June 17, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves for relief from the default pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). The Court initially addresses the fact that the Motion only seeks relief from the entry of default, despite default judgment having been entered prior to the filing of the Motion. In its reply, Defendant also asks for relief from the judgment. Accordingly, the Court will consider this a Motion for relief from entry of default and default judgment.

 

It appears from the moving papers and reply that Defendant is moving for relief under both the mandatory and discretionary provisions of this statute. Mandatory relief applies when the default is shown to be the fault of Defendant’s attorney and the motion is brought within six months of the default judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) Relief under the discretionary provision must be sought within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

 

The Motion is supported by a declaration that refers to conduct by both Defendant and defense counsel. Due to a calendaring error, Defendant’s claims adjuster did not send the case to defense counsel until March 16, 2026, which was two days before the deadline to respond to the Complaint. (Motion, Sutton Decl., ¶2.) Then, due to defense counsel’s conflict check procedures, defense counsel was not retained until March 26, 2026.  (Motion, Sutton Decl., ¶2; An Decl., ¶¶6-7.) This combined delay, which is mostly attributable to Defendant and not counsel, demonstrates excusable neglect in Defendant’s failure to respond prior to the entry of default. The Motion was also timely brought less than two months after the entry of default, and attaches a copy of Defendant’s proposed answer. (Motion, An Decl., Exh. A.)  The Court grants relief under the discretionary provision of § 473(b), and not based on attorney fault.

 

Plaintiff’s opposition requests an award of attorney’s fees under the language of the statute that provides “[t]he court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or the party’s legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against the party through the party's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b), emphasis added.) It also cites subdivision (c)(1)(A), which allows the Court to “[i]mpose a penalty of no greater than one thousand dollars ($1,000) upon an offending attorney or party.” Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they billed $2,800.00 to obtain default and default judgment, and to oppose the instant Motion. (Opp., Kirschner Decl., ¶8.) The Court is not inclined to award attorney’s fees under the latter provision. A penalty is not warranted for Defendant’s minimal delay in responding to the action.  The Court also does not find an award attorney fees warranted.   Costs may be determined at the conclusion of the litigation based on the prevailing party statutes. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, 1033.5.)   

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Defendant The Pep Boys Manny Moe & Jack of California LLC’s Motion to Vacate Default is granted. The default entered on March 23, 2026 and default judgment entered on April 13, 2026 are hereby vacated.

 

Defendant The Pep Boys Manny Moe & Jack of California LLC is to file and serve its Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this order.
