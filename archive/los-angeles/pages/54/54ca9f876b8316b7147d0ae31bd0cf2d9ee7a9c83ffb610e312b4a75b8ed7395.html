Case Number: 24CHCV02023    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 9/15/26

Case Name: Edward Hurtado; Sreela Ajithkumar; Sidra Baig vs. Edwin Cardova; Water Damage Rescue, Inc.; and Does 1-50

Case No. 24CHCV02023

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

SEPTEMBER 15, 2026

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 24CHCV02023

 

Motion filed: 4/8/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Edwin Cardova and Water Damage Rescue, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Sreela Ajithkumar

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court compelling Plaintiff Sreela Ajithkumar to serve further verified responses to Defendants’ Form Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 10.1 and 12.1; and issuing evidentiary sanctions precluding Plaintiff from introducing evidence concerning the subject matters addressed in the interrogatories.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The motion is GRANTED IN PART. Defendants’ request for sanctions is DENIED. Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from injuries allegedly sustained by Plaintiffs Edward Hurtado (“Hurtado”), Sreela Ajithkumar (“Ajithkumar”), and Sidra Baig (“Baig”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) in a motor vehicle collision that occurred on May 31, 2022. On May 31, 2024, Plaintiffs filed the Complaint against Defendants Edwin Cardova, Water Damage Rescue, Inc. (collectively, “Defendants”), and Does 1 through 50, alleging motor vehicle negligence.

 

On July 30, 2024, Defendants filed their joint Answer to the Complaint.

 

On April 8, 2026, Defendants filed the instant Motion to compel Ajithkumar to further respond to their Form Interrogatories, Set One (the “Motion”).

 

On August 31, 2026, Ajithkumar filed an Opposition.

 

On September 8, 2026, Defendants filed a Reply.

 

ANALYSIS

 

            “On receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete. (2) An exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate. (3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a).)

 

A.    Procedural Requirements

 

(1)   Timeliness of Motion

 

A party must bring a motion to compel further responses to interrogatories within 45 days after the responding party serves verified responses or any supplemental verified responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (c).) Parties may also agree in writing to a specific later date by which to file a motion to compel further responses. (Ibid.)

 

Although not “‘jurisdictional’ in the fundamental sense, [the time limit] is … ‘jurisdictional’ in the sense that it renders the court without authority to rule on motions to compel other than to deny them.” (Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1410.)

 

Defendants’ counsel attests that Ajithkumar served verified responses to Defendants’ Form Interrogatories, Set One on February 20, 2026 via email. (Carroll Decl., ¶¶ 5, 16.) The deadline to file a motion to compel further responses to the interrogatories was April 8, 2026. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1010.6, 2030.300.) The Motion, filed on April 8, 2026, was therefore timely.

 

(2)   Meet and Confer

 

A motion to compel further responses to interrogatories must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1).) A meet and confer declaration must “state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a).)

 

Defendants’ counsel attests to sending meet and confer correspondence to Plaintiff’s counsel and following up via telephone and further written communication. (Carroll Decl., ¶¶ 7-11.) Plaintiff’s counsel did not respond to the written communication, and during the phone call indicated that she had not reviewed the earlier meet and confer letter. (Ibid.) The parties thus never engaged on the merits to resolve the issues informally.

 

Nevertheless, given that Plaintiff’s counsel would not grant an extension of time to bring the Motion and Defendants’ counsel did make several attempts to reach out concerning resolution (Carroll Decl., ¶¶ 9, 13), the Court finds that Defendants’ counsel’s effort fulfills the purpose of the requirement.

 

(3)   Separate Statement

 

“Any motion involving the content of a discovery request or the responses to such a request must be accompanied by a separate statement. The motions that require a separate statement include a motion … (2) To compel further responses to interrogatories.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345, subd. (a).) The separate statement must “provide[] all information necessary to understand … all the responses … that are at issue” and “be full and complete so that no person is required to review any other document in order to determine the full request and the full response.” (Id., Rule 3.1345, subd. (c).)

 

Defendants have complied with the separate statement requirement.

 

B.     Motion to Compel Further Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One

 

A party to whom interrogatories are propounded must respond “in writing under oath separately to each interrogatory by” answering the substance of the interrogatory, exercising an option to produce writings, or objecting. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.210, subd. (a).) “Each answer in a response to interrogatories shall be as complete and straightforward as the information reasonably available to the responding party permits.” (§ 2030.220, subd. (a).) “If an interrogatory cannot be answered completely, it shall be answered to the extent possible.” (§ 2030.220, subd. (b).) “If the responding party does not have personal knowledge sufficient to respond fully to an interrogatory, that party shall so state, but shall make a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain the information by inquiry to other natural persons or organizations, except where the information is equally available to the propounding party.” (§ 2030.220, subd. (c).)

 

“As a general matter, the statutory scheme imposes no obligation on a party propounding interrogatories to establish good cause or prove up the merits of any underlying claims.” (Williams v. Super. Ct. (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 550.) A litigant “is entitled to demand answers to its interrogatories, as a matter of right, and without a prior showing, unless the party on whom those interrogatories are served objects and shows cause why the questions are not within the purview of the code section.” (Id., at p. 541, citing West Pico Furniture Co. v. Super. Ct. (1961) 56 Cal.2d 407, 422.)

 

Defendants seek further responses to their Form Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 10.1 and 12.1. Defendants argue that Ajithkumar’s responses are evasive and incomplete because they do not respond to the interrogatories in a clear and straightforward manner. (Mot., at pp. 7-9.)

 

Plaintiff contends that the responses are complete and fully compliant with the Discovery Act, suggesting that Defendants filed the Motion for an improper purpose. (Opp’n, at p. 2.)

 

(1)   The Responses at Issue

 

(a)   Form Interrogatory No. 10.1

 

Form Interrogatory No. 10.1 asks: “At any time before the INCIDENT did you have complaints or injuries that involved some part of your body claimed to have been injured in the INCIDENT? If so, for each state: (1) a description of the complaint or injury; (b) the dates it began and ended; and (c) the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number of each HEALTH CARE PROVIDER whom you consulted or who examined or treated you.” (Separate Statement (“SS”), at p. 2.)

 

Ajithkumar’s response to Form Interrogatory No. 10.1 is “No.” (SS, at p. 2.)

 

Ajithkumar contends that this is a complete and compliant response to the interrogatory, leaving nothing to explain or supplement. (Opp’n, at p. 4.) While the Court would generally agree that the answer constitutes an unequivocal denial that Ajithkumar had any prior complaints concerning the parts of her body she claims were injured in the accident, Defendants present evidence in Reply that Ajithkumar suffers from a preexisting dental condition that she would have known about when responding to the interrogatory. Specifically, an oral evaluation of Ajithkumar performed on July 28, 2022, shortly after the accident, concluded that she had “pre-existing severe periodontal disease with bone loss and loose/weakened teeth exacerbated due to current trauma.” (Fersch Decl., Exh. B.) This would constitute an injury preexisting the accident and renders a negative response to the interrogatory incomplete and inaccurate.

 

The Court accordingly GRANTS the Motion as to Form Interrogatory No. 10.1

 

(b)   Form Interrogatory No. 12.1

 

Form Interrogatory No. 12.1 asks: “State the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number of each individual: (a) who witnessed the INCIDENT or the events occurring immediately before or after the INCIDENT; (b) who made any statement at the scene of the INCIDENT; (c) who heard any statements made about the INCIDENT by any individual at the scene; and (d) who YOU OR ANYONE ACTING ON YOUR BEHALF claim has knowledge of the INCIDENT (except for expert witnesses covered by Code of Civil Procedure section 2034.)” (SS, at p. 3.)

 

Ajithkumar’s response states: “(a-d) Plaintiff Edward Hurtado, Plaintiff Sreela Ajithkumar, and Plaintiff Sidra Baig, who should be contacted through their Counsel of record; and Defendant who can be contacted through his counsel of record. Discovery and investigation are ongoing and Plaintiff reserves the right to amend and/or supplement this response should additional information become available.” (SS, at p. 3.)

 

Ajithkumar contends that this is a complete response which provides the names of all witnesses presently known to have witnessed the accident, made or heard statements concerning the accident, or otherwise possess knowledge of the accident, and provides the appropriate means of contacting them, which is through counsel. (Opp’n, at p. 5.)

 

In Reply, Defendants assert that the response is incomplete because it provides no addresses or telephone numbers and Ajithkumar’s deposition testimony revealed other percipient witnesses—the drivers of three other vehicles involved in the accident—who were not disclosed. (Mot., at p. 8; Reply, at p.4.)

 

Upon review of the deposition testimony provided, Ajithkumar testifies to a lack of knowledge concerning the other vehicles. She testifies that she did not speak with anyone other than Baig at the scene of the accident, does not know the names of anyone else involved in the accident or who witnessed the accident, and was driven home straight from the scene of the accident. (Fersch Decl., Exh. A.)

 

As Defendants note, the Discovery Act only requires a responding party to answer an interrogatory to the extent possible based on reasonably available information. Given that Ajithkumar spoke only to Baig at the scene of the incident, it is entirely reasonable that she does not know the names and contact information of any percipient witnesses beyond the parties listed in her response, and Defendants present no evidence showing that Ajithkumar did not respond to the extent that she reasonably could. 

 

As to the addresses of the parties, the Court notes that the Rules of Professional Conduct prohibit a lawyer from directly contacting a person known to be represented by counsel about the matter that is the subject of the representation. (Prof. Conduct Rule 4.2(a).) Plaintiffs are all represented by counsel. The purpose of providing contact information in response to Form Interrogatory No. 12.1 is therefore satisfied by directing Defendants to contact them through their counsel of record.

 

The Motion is DENIED as to Form Interrogatory No. 12.1.

 

C.    Sanctions

 

“The court may impose a monetary sanction and order that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process … pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.030, subd. (a).) Misuse of the discovery process includes making an evasive response to discovery. (Id., § 2023.010, subd. (f).)

 

The court may also impose evidentiary sanctions against a party engaging in misuse of the discovery process by prohibiting that party from introducing matters into evidence. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (c).)

 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction … against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a further response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (d).)

 

Defendants request evidence sanctions against Ajithkumar precluding her from introducing “evidence concerning the subject matters addressed in Form Interrogatory Nos. 10.1 and 12.1 unless and until Plaintiff provides code-compliant responses.” The Court finds evidentiary sanctions to be unwarranted under the circumstances.

 

Ajithkumar requests monetary sanctions against Defendants and their counsel of record in the amount of $1,500.00. Given that the Court has partially granted the Motion, the Court finds that Ajithkumar is not entitled to sanctions under section 2030.300 and that Defendants have not misused the discovery process.

 

Both requests for sanctions are therefore DENIED.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Defendants’ motion to compel Plaintiff Sreela Ajithkumar to further respond to their Form Interrogatories, Set One is GRANTED IN PART. Ajithkumar is ordered to serve further verified response to Defendants’ Form Interrogatories, Set One, No. 10.1 within 20 days.  

 

Defendants’ request for evidence sanctions is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25CHCV00107    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 9/15/26

Case Name: Chad Walters vs. ABC Plastics, Inc.; ABC Plastic Fabrication, Inc.; Joseph Walters; and Does 1-50

Case No. 25CHCV00107

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

                                                                                       

SEPTEMBER 15, 2026

 

MOTION TO STAY PROCEEDINGS

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25CHCV01007

 

Motion filed: 4/8/25

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants ABC Plastics, Inc. and ABC Plastic Fabrication, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Chad Walters

NOTICE: Proper

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court: (1) staying litigation; (2) appointing appraisers to determine the value of Plaintiff Chad Walters’ shares in Defendant corporations ABC Plastics, Inc. and ABC Plastic Fabrication, Inc.; (3) providing a procedure for the appraiser’s collection of evidence and a timeline for the appraisal; and (4) providing that the Court will further enter an order permitting Defendants a period to tender payment to Plaintiff in lieu of dissolution of the corporations.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The motion is GRANTED.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises out of management disagreements between the two equal shareholders of close corporations ABC Plastics, Inc. (“ABCPI”) and ABC Plastics Fabrication, Inc. (“Fabrication”) (collectively, the “Corporations”). On January 13, 2025, Plaintiff Chad Walters (“Plaintiff”) filed a Complaint against the Corporations and Defendant Joseph Walters (“Joseph”) (collectively, “Defendants”), and Does 1 through 50, alleging the following four causes of action: (1) Involuntary Dissolution of ABCPI, (2) Involuntary Dissolution of Fabrication, (3) Specific Performance, and (4) Breach of Fiduciary Duty.

 

On February 13, 2025, Joseph filed an Answer. Subsequently, on March 14, 2025, the Corporations jointly filed their Answer to the Complaint.

 

On July 17, 2025, the Corporations filed the instant Motion to Stay Proceedings (the “Motion”).

 

On December 16, 2025, Plaintiff’s counsel filed and served a Declaration Regarding Plaintiff’s Non-Opposition to the Motion.

 

No opposition or reply papers have been filed.

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.    Applicable Law

 

The Corporations bring this Motion under California Corporations Code section 2000 to elect buyout of Plaintiff’s shares in each of the Corporations. (Mot., at p. 1.) Corporations Code section 2000 governs the buyout of ABCPI, a California corporation. (Compl., ¶ 2.) However, Fabrication is an entity incorporated in the state of Florida, (Compl., ¶ 3), and neither the Corporations nor Plaintiff have offered authority for the Court’s application of California law to claims against Fabrication.

 

Courts have held that the internal affairs doctrine requires California courts “to apply to a dissolution claim the law of the state under which the entity was organized.” (Boschetti v. Pacific Bay Investments Inc. (2019) 32 Cal.App.5th 1059, 1066.) The doctrine applies to any matters relevant to internal corporate governance. (Lidow v. Superior Court (2012) 206 Cal.App.4th 351, 362.) Because compulsory buyout is a right dependent on an action for judicial dissolution, buyout necessarily involves issues of internal governance. (Boschetti, at p. 1066.) As Fabrication is a Florida corporation, Florida law thus applies to the Corporations’ buyout election of Plaintiff’s shares.

 

B.     ABCPI

 

When a 50 percent shareholder in a corporation files suit for involuntary dissolution, the corporation may avoid dissolution by “purchasing for cash the shares owned by the plaintiff[].” (Corp. Code, § 2000, subd. (a).) The Court must stay the winding up and dissolution proceeding and “proceed to ascertain and fix the fair value of the shares owned” by the party initiating the action if certain conditions are met. (Id., § 2000, subd. (b).) Those conditions are: (1) the corporation elects to purchase the shares owned by party seeking dissolution; (2) the parties are unable to agree upon the fair value of the shares owned by the party seeking dissolution; and (3) the corporation gives bond sufficient to pay the estimated reasonable expenses of the party seeking dissolution if the corporation does not timely make payment for the shares. (Id., § 2000, subds. (b), (c).)

 

“The court shall appoint three disinterested appraisers to appraise the fair value of the shares … , and shall make an order referring the matter to the appraisers so appointed for the purpose of ascertaining the value.” (Corp. Code, § 2000, subd. (c).) “The order shall prescribe the time and manner of producing evidence, if evidence is required.” An award determined by a majority of the appraisers is final and conclusive upon all parties. (Ibid.) The Court must enter a decree providing for the winding up and dissolution of the corporation unless the purchasing parties pay for the shares within the time prescribed by the decree. (Ibid.)

 

(1)   Stay of Proceedings Pending Appraisal

 

A stay of proceedings is required when the corporation moves to stay litigation for the purpose of appraising the shares of a 50 percent shareholder who has filed for involuntary dissolution. (Corp. Code, § 2000, subds. (a), (b).)

 

The Corporations have met the requirements of Corporations Code section 2000. As the Corporations note in their Motion, Plaintiff alleges a 50 percent interest in ABCPI, with Joseph owning the remaining interest. (Compl., ¶ 2; Mot., at p. 5.) The Complaint seeks judicial dissolution of ABCPI due to internal dissention and alleged mismanagement. (Compl., ¶ 14.) Further, the Corporations and Plaintiff have been unable to determine the fair value of Plaintiff’s shares and agree that appraisal is appropriate. (Nowland Decl., ¶¶ 4-6; Lerner Decl., ¶¶ 9, 11.)

 

The Corporations also indicate that they are prepared to post bond pending the outcome of the appraisal. (Mot., at p. 3.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that staying the dissolution proceeding of ABCPI is mandatory.

 

(2)   Bond Amount and Appraisers

 

The Corporations propose a bond amount of $15,000. (Mot., at p. 3.) Although this amount is the estimated cost of the appraisals and not of Plaintiff’s estimated reasonable expenses, including attorneys’ fees, as required by the Corporations Code, Plaintiff’s counsel does not object to this amount in his declaration and does not offer either a different sum that would be more appropriate or any facts upon which the Court may make an estimate of reasonable expenses. (Lerner Decl., ¶ 13) The Court thus accepts $15,000 as a reasonable bond amount.

 

The Corporations also propose appointment of the following individuals as appraisers: Business Broker Steve Barnett (“Barnett”), Certified Valuation Analyst David Hahn (“Hahn”), and Certified Valuation Analyst Brandon Hall (“Hall”). (Stone Decl., ¶ 4.) Plaintiff’s counsel indicates that Plaintiff is amenable to the appointment of Barnett and Hahn but is silent as to Hall. (Lerner Decl., ¶ 12.) Given that the parties agree that Barnett and Hahn serve as appraisers, the Court finds appointment of Barnett and Hahn appropriate. The third appraiser shall be selected jointly by Barnett and Hahn.

 

C.    Fabrication

 

Under Florida law, in a voluntary dissolution proceeding, the corporation may elect to purchase all shares owned by the shareholder who initiates the proceeding. (Fla. Stats., § 607.1436, subd. (1).) An election to purchase may be filed with the Court at any time within 90 days after the plaintiff has filed for dissolution or at a later time allowed by the Court in its discretion. (Id., subd. (2).)

 

If the parties cannot agree to the fair value of shares and terms for purchase within 60 days after the election is filed, the Court may stay the dissolution proceeding to determine the fair value of the shares owned by the plaintiff. (Id., subd. (4).) After determining the fair value of shares, the Court must enter “an order directing the purchase upon such terms and conditions as the court deems appropriate.” (Id., subd. (5).) In determining fair value, the Court has discretion to select the valuation method so long as it relies on competent evidence. (G&G Fashion Design, Inc. v. Garcia (Fla.Dist.Ct.App. 2004) 870 So.2d 870, 872-873.)

 

(1)   Motion to Stay to Exercise Buyout Right

 

The Court finds that, under Florida law, Fabrication has made an election pursuant to Florida Statutes section 607.1436. The Motion, which asserts the statutory buyout right of Fabrication, was made on July 17, 2025. Although this was more than 90 days after Plaintiff filed for dissolution, the Court may exercise discretion to allow the election. The parties have indicated that they have been unable to agree to the fair value of Plaintiff’s shares, and more than 60 days have elapsed since the Motion was filed. (Nowland Decl., ¶ 6.) Further, the parties agree that appraisal is appropriate to facilitate the purchase of Plaintiff’s shares. (Lerner Decl., ¶¶ 9, 11.)

 

The Court finds, under these circumstances, that it may stay the dissolution proceeding as to Fabrication and that appointment of appraisers to determine the value of the shares of both Corporations is proper. The appraisers appointed to determine the value of Plaintiff’s shares in ABCPI are also directed to determine the fair value of Plaintiff’s shares in Fabrication.

 

D.    Derivative Claims

 

Appraisal of Plaintiff’s shares necessarily involves appraisal of derivative claims, which are a property right belonging to the corporation (Cotton v. Expo Power Systems, Inc. (2009) 170 Cal.App.4th 1371, 1380; accord Nelson v. Miller (Fla.Dist.Ct.App. 1968) 212 So.2d 66, 67.) Plaintiff has asserted claims for inspection rights as to both Corporations and breach of fiduciary duty against Joseph in his capacity as managing shareholder of each of the Corporations. (Compl., ¶¶ 34-51.) The Corporations request stay of these claims as well as the dissolution claims. Plaintiff does not oppose this request.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the Corporations’ Motion to stay all proceedings pending appraisal of Plaintiff’s interest in the Corporations.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court GRANTS the Motion to Stay. All proceedings are stayed pending appraisal of Plaintiff Chad Walters’ shares in Defendants ABC Plastics, Inc. and ABC Plastic Fabrication, Inc.

 

The Court orders Defendants ABC Plastics, Inc. and ABC Plastic Fabrication, Inc. to deposit a bond in the amount of $15,000 with the Clerk of the Court within 10 days of this order.

 

The Court appoints the following appraisers: Business Broker Steve Barnett and Certified Valuation Analyst David Hahn. Barnett and Hahn are to select the third appraiser.

 

The Court sets a hearing for ____________ in Department F49 to determine the fair value of Plaintiff Chad Walters’ shares. The Court orders the appraisers to file a report determining the fair value of Plaintiff’s shares in each of the Defendant corporations no later than nine days before the date of the hearing.

 

Moving party to give notice.
