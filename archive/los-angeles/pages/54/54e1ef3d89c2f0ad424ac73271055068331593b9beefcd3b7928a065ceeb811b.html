MOTION TO VACATE DISMISSAL

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(d))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court DENIES Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club’s motion to vacate the dismissal entered against Sarah Menendez.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club filed this subrogation action against Sarah Menendez and Julie Aldana.  It obtained a default against Aldana only, which it then agreed to set aside before dismissing the “[e]ntire action of all parties and all causes of action.”  Citing Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), Interinsurance now moves to vacate the dismissal as to Menendez only.

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) allows the Court to correct its own clerical mistakes.  The collision that was the genesis of this case happened almost four years ago.  Menendez was served over two years ago.  Dismissing the wrong party nearly a year ago was Interinsurance’s mistake, not the Court’s.  (Romadka v. Hoge (1991) 232 Cal.App.3d 1231, 1235, fn. 2.)  The Court will not revive this action against Menendez now. 

 

            The Court DENIES Interinsurance’s motion.

TENTATIVE RULING:


The Court GRANTS Aspire General Insurance Company’s motions.  Richard Espinoza is ordered to serve verified responses without objections to the form interrogatories within 30 days of this order.  The contents of Aspire’s first set of requests for admission are deemed admitted.

ANALYSIS:

            Aspire General Insurance Company filed this subrogation action against Amanda Vera and Richard Espinoza.  Espinoza answered and Vera defaulted.  Aspire then propounded 20 form interrogatories and 14 requests for admission on Espinoza.  Espinoza failed to respond to the discovery requests, so Aspire moves to compel responses to the interrogatories and have the requests for admission deemed admitted. 

 

A party must respond to interrogatories within 30 days after service of such interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).)  If the party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the interrogatories. (Id., § 2030.290, subds. (a), (b).)

 

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)  If a party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted.  (Id., § 2033.280, subd. (a), (b).) 

 

Aspire served Espinoza with the form interrogatories and requests for admission in April 2026.  To date, no responses have been served.  Espinoza has not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Aspire’s motions.  Espinoza is ordered to serve verified responses with objections to the form interrogatories within 30 days of this order.  The contents of Aspire’s first set of requests for admission are deemed admitted.

 

Espinoza must also pay sanctions for causing Aspire to move to compel responses and deem requests admitted.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); id., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)  Aspire requests a total of $2,330.00 for the two motions, reflecting six hours for traveling to and from and attending the hearings at the rate of $225.00 per hour, four hours for preparing the motions at the same rate, and $80.00 in filing fees.  Given the similarity of the motions, the shared hearing date, and the routine nature of the motions, the Court finds the requested sanctions excessive.  The Court thus awards Aspire sanctions in the reduced amount of $980.00, reflecting four hours of work incurred at $225.00, plus $80.00 in filing fees.  Espinoza must pay Aspire $980.00 within 30 days of this order.

 

Aspire is to give notice.
