DEPARTMENT A15 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

SUBMITTING ON A TENTATIVE RULING

A tentative ruling is not the final ruling of the Court. The Court may adopt, modify, or withdraw the tentative ruling at the scheduled hearing. Tentative rulings are not invitations or opportunities to file further documents related to the hearing. No such document will be considered by the Court.

If all parties wish to submit the matter without oral argument:

1. The parties must confer and confirm that every party agrees to submit.

2. One party—not each party separately—must send a single joint email to ATPdeptA15@lacourt.ca.gov no later than 8:00 a.m. on the date scheduled for the hearing.

3. The email must copy all other counsel and self-represented parties and use this subject line:

SUBMISSION — [CASE NUMBER] — [CASE NAME] — [HEARING DATE]

4. The email must state: “All parties have conferred and agree to submit the matter on the Court’s tentative ruling without oral argument. No party intends to appear. This email copies all counsel and self-represented parties.”

The email must identify the sender’s name, the party represented, and must include a telephone number for the sender.

Do not telephone the courtroom to submit on a tentative ruling. Do not send separate submission emails. Court staff will not acknowledge or confirm receipt.

If every party does not agree to submit, do not send a submission email. If all parties have not agreed to submit, any party wishing to be heard may appear at the scheduled hearing without giving advance notice to the Court.

A submission does not signify agreement with the tentative ruling. It means only that the parties do not request oral argument.

No party may submit another party’s position without that party’s express agreement.

If all parties submit and the tentative does not expressly require an appearance, the parties need not appear. The Court’s final ruling will be reflected in the minute order.

Emails are not part of the official court record. Do not use the Court's email address for any purpose other than submitting on a tentative ruling or as ordered by the Court. Do not copy the Court on emails among the attorneys, parties, or others. Do not use the Court's email address to ask questions regarding a case. 

The Court will not respond to emails submitted through the department's email address.

Inappropriate use or misuse of the Court's email address or violation of these or other rules may result in sanctions, including blocking receipt of emails by the violating sender, after the first misuse or violation.

SMITH v. AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC. 

Motion to Quash Notice to Appear and Produce at Trial

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s motion to quash plaintiffs’ notice to appear and produce at trial is set for hearing on September 24, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department A15. The Court has considered the motion, the declaration of Reshma A. Bajaj and attached notice, plaintiffs Almira Smith and Stephen Smith’s opposition, the declaration of David Lunn and its exhibits, and the file in this matter. The Court issues the following tentative ruling.

DISPOSITION

1. The Court exercises its discretion to consider plaintiffs’ late opposition.

2. The motion is GRANTED. Plaintiffs’ August 28, 2026 notice to appear and produce at trial is quashed, without prejudice to service of a new, statutorily compliant notice or subpoena for the continued trial.

3. Plaintiffs’ requests to compel production through the present notice, to modify that notice, and to require defendant to disclose within five days the former titles and departure dates of Jeff Chang, Steven Bailey, and Michael V. Tyrrell are DENIED.

4. Defendant’s request for $1,500 in monetary sanctions against Strategic Legal Practices, APC, and plaintiffs’ request for $2,537.50 in monetary sanctions against defendant and its counsel are DENIED.

REASONS

A. Procedural history and factual background

This warranty action concerns plaintiffs’ 2019 Honda CR-V. Plaintiffs allege that defendant failed to repurchase or replace the vehicle after unsuccessful repair attempts. (Lunn Decl., ¶¶ 16-17.) On August 28, 2026, plaintiffs served a notice directing Michelle Jongkind, Jeff Chang, Steven Bailey, and Michael V. Tyrrell to appear at trial and each to produce specified records. The demands include the vehicle’s electronic eVRM record, divided into 14 identified sections, and five types of documents for each applicable safety or noncompliance recall. (Bajaj Decl., ¶ 3 & Ex. A, pp. 2-4.)

The notice offers each witness an alternative: provide the requested records and a declaration stating, among other things, that the witness is defendant’s authorized custodian of records. That alternative must be completed at least five court days before trial. (Bajaj Decl., Ex. A, pp. 4-5.) On September 9, 2026, the Court continued trial from September 18, 2026, to January 29, 2027. (Minute Order, Sept. 9, 2026, p. 1.)

Plaintiffs acknowledge that their September 21 opposition is late and attribute the delay to counsel’s calendaring error. (Lunn Decl., ¶¶ 5-10.) The Court considers the opposition under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d).

B. Governing standards

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987, subdivision (b), permits service of a written attendance notice on counsel in place of a subpoena for a party, a person for whose immediate benefit the action is prosecuted or defended, or an officer, director, or managing agent of such a party or person. The notice must state the time and place of attendance. A production notice must also identify the exact materials sought and state that the party or person has them in that party’s or person’s possession or control. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987, subds. (b), (c).)

A party may move to quash, and the Court may quash the process entirely, modify it, or direct compliance on appropriate terms. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subds. (a), (b)(1).) Defendant must establish a basis for the relief it requests. Here, the notice itself establishes the defects material to the disposition.

C. The present notice does not satisfy the statutory requirements

The notice requires the four individuals to appear “at the time of trial” but supplies no calendar date or time. (Bajaj Decl., Ex. A, p. 2.) Plaintiffs emphasize that defendant knew the former trial date and recited it in the motion. (Opposition, p. 12.) That knowledge does not supply the omitted attendance information in the process directed to these witnesses. The September 9 continuance is not itself a ground to invalidate an otherwise proper notice. The defect is the notice’s failure to specify when attendance is required.

The production requirement presents an additional defect. The notice directs each named individual to produce the entire set of records, but it does not state that any of those individuals has the records in his or her possession or control. (Bajaj Decl., Ex. A, pp. 2-5.) Describing records as stored in defendant’s electronic system identifies their asserted corporate source. It does not state that each individual commanded to produce them possesses or controls them. Paragraph 3(d), describing what a future custodian declaration must say, does not supply that missing assertion in the notice itself.

The optional declaration procedure does not cure these defects. It requires a witness to certify that the witness is defendant’s duly authorized custodian and to authenticate records. (Bajaj Decl., Ex. A, pp. 4-5.) An option to provide such a declaration does not establish that a named person has that authority or the requested records. Nor does an offer to excuse attendance after production establish an otherwise missing statutory basis to compel attendance. The cited business records provisions do not dispense with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.

D. Modification and the remaining requests

Plaintiffs correctly identify modification as an available alternative. Correcting the date alone, however, would leave the production demands directed to four individuals without the required statement concerning their possession or control. Plaintiffs’ proposed corporate production order would materially recast those individual demands. Trial is now more than four months away, and plaintiffs state that they intend to serve a renewed notice for January 29, 2027, in any event. (Opposition, p. 13.) Under these circumstances, quashing the present notice without prejudice permits plaintiffs to identify the proper recipient and serve clear, compliant process without foreclosing access to trial evidence.

The Court does not resolve the disputed employment and managing agent issues. Bajaj states that three witnesses no longer work for defendant and that Jongkind is not an officer, director, or managing agent. Plaintiffs challenge the foundation and submit a March 2026 publication identifying Bailey as a vice president and a May 2026 agency letter addressed to Jongkind. (Bajaj Decl., ¶¶ 1, 4-5; Lunn Decl., ¶¶ 25-26 & Exs. A-B.) Those disputes need not be resolved to decide whether the present notice contains the information the statute requires. The Court makes no categorical finding that any witness is immune from proper trial process.

Nor does the Court accept defendant’s suggestion that every trial production demand served after the discovery cutoff is improper. A trial subpoena may obtain known documents for use as evidence without constituting further discovery. (Boal v. Price Waterhouse & Co. (1985) 165 Cal.App.3d 806, 810.) Here, the eVRM demand identifies a single vehicle and specified record sections. The Court does not conclude that the entire demand is inherently overbroad. Because the notice is quashed on other grounds, the Court does not reach the remaining category-specific objections or decide the scope of any renewed demand.

Plaintiffs also seek an order requiring defendant to disclose the titles and departure dates of three witnesses within five days. (Opposition, p. 14.) That information is not among the materials demanded by the challenged notice. Plaintiffs have not established a basis on this motion to compel those additional responses as a condition of relief. Their requests for that disclosure and for immediate production under the present notice are therefore denied.

E. Monetary sanctions

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.2, subdivision (a), permits a discretionary award of reasonable expenses incurred where the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification, or a requirement of the process was oppressive. Success on the motion alone does not establish one of those grounds.

Neither side has made the required showing. Defendant identified actual defects in the notice. Plaintiffs nevertheless had a substantially justified interest in obtaining vehicle-specific trial records and a reasonable basis to request modification rather than complete quashing. The defects do not establish that the notice was oppressive, and the record does not demonstrate bad faith by either side. Defendant’s characterization of the requests as an expeditionary search does not establish oppression where the notice identifies one vehicle and specified record types, and the motion supplies no concrete showing of the burden of obtaining those records. (Motion, pp. 6-7; Bajaj Decl., ¶¶ 6-7.)

Defendant’s $1,500 request also includes three to four anticipated hours for an ex parte application to advance the hearing before the former trial date. (Bajaj Decl., ¶ 7.) That estimate does not establish that the expenses were incurred or remain necessary after the continuance. Because neither side establishes entitlement to sanctions, the Court need not determine the reasonable amount of either request.

SHARMA v. GUY F. ATKINSON CONSTRUCTION, LLC et al. 

Cross-Complainant Alfredo Colon’s Motion to Compel Discovery Responses and for Monetary Sanctions

Cross-complainant Alfredo Colon’s motion to compel initial discovery responses from cross-defendant HG Trucklines, Inc. (HG), and for monetary sanctions is set for hearing on September 24, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department A15. The Court has reviewed the motion, limited opposition, reply, supporting declarations and exhibits, Southlake Specialty Insurance Co.’s September 10, 2026 intervention filing insofar as it bears on the requested continuance, and the file in this matter. The Court tentatively rules as follows.

DISPOSITION

1. The request for a further continuance of the discovery motion pending determination of Southlake Specialty Insurance Co.’s motion for leave to intervene is DENIED.

2. The requests to compel initial responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, served on HG on December 23, 2025, are GRANTED. Within 30 days after service of notice of the order, HG shall serve separate, verified, code-compliant responses, without objections, to the form interrogatories actually propounded, the 64 special interrogatories, and the 88 document demands. Compliance must occur through a legally authorized means. This order does not restore HG’s suspended corporate powers or authorize counsel to litigate in HG’s name while those powers remain suspended.

3. The request for an unconditional order requiring production of all responsive documents within 10 days is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE. The relief granted on the document demands is an order for initial written responses, including the required statement of compliance or verified representation of inability to comply for each demand.

4. The request for monetary sanctions against HG and its counsel is DENIED. The Court also declines to impose the additional sanction under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.050 for the reasons stated below.

5. The request to declare that a future intervening insurer automatically inherits HG’s discovery defaults and obligations is DENIED. This ruling imposes no response obligation on Southlake and does not decide its motion for leave to intervene, presently set for December 29, 2026.

6. Colon shall give notice of the order.

REASONS

A. Procedural history and factual background

The discovery concerns HG’s asserted relationship with Varun Sharma and information concerning a March 14, 2024 vehicle collision, including operation, maintenance, insurance, and liability contentions. (Mot., Ex. B, pp. 2-7; Ex. C, pp. 3-9.) Halpern’s reply declaration states that all three sets were served on HG on December 23, 2025, that extensions were granted through March 2026, and that no responses had been served when the motion was filed. (Halpern Reply Decl., ¶¶ 2-5.) Counsel’s limited opposition does not dispute service or identify any response subsequently served. It seeks additional time because HG is suspended and its insurer wishes to intervene. (Opp’n, pp. 2-5.)

On August 25, the Court continued the motion to September 24 and directed payment of two additional motion fees. Those fees have been paid. (Minute Order, Aug. 25, 2026, p. 1, ¶¶ 1-2; Halpern Reply Decl., ¶ 10.) The Court also required any further request for time based on insurer intervention to show that Southlake filed and served an intervention application with its proposed pleading by September 10. The order expressly stated that timely filing would not itself establish good cause for another continuance. (Minute Order, Aug. 25, 2026, pp. 1-2, ¶¶ 4-5.)

Southlake filed and served its motion with a proposed answer on September 10. It noticed the motion for December 29, 2026. (Southlake Mot. for Leave to Intervene, pp. 1-2, Latman Decl., ¶ 7 & Ex. A, and proof of service.) The filing satisfies the prior filing condition, but leave to intervene has not been granted.

B. Governing standards

A party propounding interrogatories or document demands may move to compel initial responses when the responding party fails to serve timely responses. Failure to respond also waives objections, subject to the statutory procedure for relief from waiver. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subds. (a)-(b), 2031.300, subds. (a)-(b).) Colon bears the burden of establishing service, expiration of the response period, and the failure to respond. His reply declaration establishes those facts, and the limited opposition does not contest them.

A corporation suspended under Revenue and Taxation Code section 23301 cannot prosecute or defend litigation in its own name. An insurer seeking to defend its interests must intervene rather than litigate in the suspended corporation’s name. (Kaufman & Broad Communities, Inc. v. Performance Plastering, Inc. (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 212, 217-218.) The disability extends to participation in discovery. (Palm Valley Homeowners Ass’n, Inc. v. Design MTC (2000) 85 Cal.App.4th 553, 563-564.)

C. Initial responses and the requested continuance

Colon has established entitlement to initial responses to each of the three discovery sets. No response, request for relief from waiver, or order staying discovery is identified in the papers. The Court therefore grants the requests for verified responses without objections. The ruling rests on nonresponse, not on Colon’s separate contention that unspecified objections were asserted while HG lacked capacity.

The suspension presents a genuine restriction on how HG may comply, but it does not require the Court to defer adjudication until Southlake becomes a party. Code of Civil Procedure section 387 provides the procedure for intervention. Its subdivision (f) governs responses to a pleading in intervention and supplies no automatic stay of an existing party’s discovery obligations. Southlake’s pending application does not restore HG’s corporate powers or make Southlake the responding party to discovery addressed to HG. A further continuance to December 29 would therefore postpone this motion without a showing that it would enable HG to supply lawful responses.

The Court allows 30 days for lawful compliance rather than the requested 10 days in light of the capacity issue. The order runs against HG. It does not permit counsel to disregard the suspension, direct Southlake to verify responses for HG, or predetermine any later request concerning noncompliance.

As to the document demands, Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300, subdivision (b), authorizes an order compelling a response. The response must address each demand through a statement of compliance or a representation of inability to comply that satisfies the statutory requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.210, subd. (a), 2031.230.) The present record does not establish that every requested document exists or is within HG’s possession, custody, or control. The Court therefore declines the separate request for unconditional production of all responsive documents before HG supplies the required responses.

D. Monetary sanctions

The applicable statutes require monetary sanctions for an unsuccessful motion or opposition unless substantial justification or other circumstances make sanctions unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (c), 2031.300, subd. (c).) Colon initially sought $1,500 and now requests $2,370 in attorney fees and filing costs. (Mot., pp. 7-8; Halpern Decl. in Supp. Mot., ¶ 7; Halpern Reply Decl., ¶ 11.)

The Court finds that imposing monetary sanctions against HG or counsel would be unjust on this record. HG was suspended before the discovery was served. The declarations report that its business has closed and that it lacks the resources needed for revivor. Latman states that, after taking over handling of the matter in May 2026, he discovered the suspension, repeatedly attempted to contact HG’s principals, and learned in August that revivor was not financially feasible. (Halpern Reply Decl., ¶ 9 & Exs. E-F; Latman Decl. in Opp’n, ¶¶ 2-5.) Counsel disclosed the disability and pursued insurer intervention, and Southlake filed within the deadline set by the Court. These circumstances present an actual legal impediment to verified litigation responses and a documented effort to use the lawful procedure for continuing the defense. They warrant relief from monetary sanctions, although they do not warrant leaving this discovery motion unresolved for another three months.

The Court has separately considered Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.050, which requires an additional $1,000 sanction upon the specified findings concerning document discovery, subject to its notice requirements and express exceptions. Even assuming a predicate finding under subdivision (a), the Court makes the foregoing written findings as to HG and counsel under subdivision (c) and concludes that the additional sanction would also be unjust in these circumstances.

E. Southlake’s independent rights

Colon also asks the Court to declare that any future insurer intervener must inherit HG’s discovery defaults. That request is too broad. An insurer permitted to intervene to protect its own interests is not automatically bound by the aligned insured’s procedural defaults. (Western Heritage Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 1196, 1206-1207.) The Court therefore confines the present response order and waiver determination to HG. Any discovery properly directed to Southlake, and any question concerning its participation after intervention, must be addressed in the appropriate procedural setting.

LOHMAN v. ESCROW MART, INC. et al. 

Escrow Mart, Inc.’s Demurrer and Motion to Strike the First Amended Complaint

Defendant Escrow Mart, Inc.’s demurrer and motion to strike portions of the First Amended Complaint are set for hearing on September 24, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department A15. The Court has considered the First Amended Complaint and its exhibits; the moving papers, supporting declarations of Elizabeth V. Duran, and request for judicial notice; plaintiff’s oppositions; defendant’s replies and accompanying judicial notice materials; and the prior ruling and procedural record identified below. The Court issues the following tentative ruling.

DISPOSITION

1. The request for judicial notice is granted as to the existence and filing of the Construction Services Escrow Agreement attached as exhibit 1 to the declaration of Josephine B. Langston filed December 11, 2025. The request is denied insofar as it seeks acceptance of disputed factual statements or a determination that the attached agreement exclusively defines the parties’ relationship.

2. Escrow Mart, Inc.’s demurrer to the ninth cause of action for false advertising is sustained with leave to amend. The demurrer to the first through eighth and tenth through thirteenth causes of action is overruled.

3. The motion to strike paragraph 1 of the prayer for compensatory damages is denied. The motion to strike paragraph 6 of the prayer for punitive damages is granted as to Escrow Mart, Inc., with leave to amend.

4. If this tentative ruling is adopted, plaintiff may file and serve a Second Amended Complaint curing the false advertising and punitive damages deficiencies within 20 days after service of notice of the ruling. Defendant shall respond within the time provided by law. Plaintiff shall give notice of the ruling.

REASONS

A. Introduction and Procedural History

Escrow Mart challenges all 13 causes of action in plaintiff Slade A. Lohman’s First Amended Complaint (FAC), filed April 7, 2026, and seeks to strike the demands for $5 million in compensatory damages and punitive damages. The March 13, 2026 ruling sustained a demurrer to the original claims for conversion and recovery of personal property with leave to amend. The deficiencies included the absence of sufficient allegations establishing plaintiff’s right to immediate possession of the entire fund and Escrow Mart’s authorization to pay plaintiff directly. (Duran Decl. in Supp. Demurrer, Ex. A, Memorandum Decision, pp. 2-4.) The Court subsequently permitted an expanded amended complaint.

Plaintiff objects that the opening demurrer memorandum exceeds the page limit by one page. The Court exercises its discretion to consider the memorandum. The excess is slight, and plaintiff submitted a substantive opposition addressing the challenged claims. (Cal. Rules of Court, rules 3.1113(d), (g), 3.1300(d); Opp’n to Demurrer, p. i; Reply to Opp’n to Demurrer, p. 2, fn. 1.)

B. Factual Background

The FAC alleges that Lohman and other lenders financed Kozun’s residential construction project through All American Financial. Escrow Mart held and administered the construction funds. Plaintiff alleges a longstanding oral agreement with Escrow Mart, continued for this project, requiring inspections at important construction stages, verification that disbursements paid for completed work, and return of unspent funds upon the borrower’s default. The lenders allegedly deposited $675,000 for the original loan and another $45,500 upon refinancing, paid Escrow Mart’s fees, and held security interests in the deposited funds. (FAC, ¶¶ 5-7, 20-26, 42-44.)

According to the FAC, Escrow Mart disbursed funds without the promised inspections and for work that had not been performed, failed to disclose those practices, and left unpaid claims against the project. Shortly before March 20, 2025, plaintiff allegedly learned that construction had ceased and was behind schedule. The loan matured without payment on April 7, 2025. Escrow Mart allegedly refused demands to return the remaining construction funds unless the lenders released claims arising from its prior disbursements. Plaintiff alleges that he acquired all lender interests, foreclosed on the property, and sustained loss from the incomplete project, impaired collateral, delay, and retained funds. He claims a remaining fund of $337,410.07. (FAC, ¶¶ 28-38.)

C. Legal Standard

A general demurrer tests whether the pleaded facts state a cause of action. The Court accepts properly pleaded material facts, reads the complaint reasonably and as a whole, and considers properly noticed matters. It does not accept bare legal conclusions or resolve disputed evidence. Leave to amend is appropriate when there is a reasonable possibility that amendment can cure the defect; plaintiff bears the burden of showing that possibility. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) A motion to strike may reach irrelevant or improper matter, including a demand for relief unsupported by the allegations. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 431.10, subd. (b)(3), 436.)

D. Discussion and Analysis

1. Judicial notice and the written escrow agreement

The request identifies Langston’s previously filed declaration and its agreement, although the opening request omitted the attachment. The reply supplies the identified materials. Plaintiff disputes both the omission and the use of the declaration to establish the truth of Escrow Mart’s account. The Court may notice the existence and filing of a court record, but ordinarily may not accept disputed factual assertions within that record as true. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d); Lockley v. Law Office of Cantrell, Green, Pekich, Cruz & McCort (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 875, 882.) This limitation applies to factual statements in filed declarations. (Oh v. Teachers Ins. & Annuity Assn. of America (2020) 53 Cal.App.5th 71, 81.)

The written agreement restricts duties to its terms, addresses inspection and disbursement discretion, permits withholding or interpleader in specified circumstances, and regulates assignments and conflicting prior agreements. (Langston Decl., Ex. 1, §§ 3.07, 3.11, 4.02-4.03, 5.04, attached to Reply in Supp. Request for Judicial Notice, Ex. A.) Those provisions may be important on the merits. They do not establish as an indisputable fact that Escrow Mart never separately accepted the lenders’ instructions or that those lenders agreed to supersede their alleged undertaking. A disputed interpretation or application of a noticed contract cannot be used to resolve such factual questions on demurrer. (Fremont Indemnity Co. v. Fremont General Corp. (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 97, 113-114.) The prior grant of judicial notice did not adjudicate those questions against allegations not then adequately pleaded.

2. Breach of contract and promissory estoppel: first and second causes of action

The FAC now identifies the asserted oral undertaking, its participants, consideration, performance, and breach. Paragraph 20 describes promises made through Escrow Mart’s president, Robert Langston, concerning inspections, controlled disbursements, and return of funds upon default. Paragraphs 21-22 connect that undertaking to this project. Paragraph 42 alleges a project agreement made on or about May 19, 2023, through All American on behalf of the lenders, their deposits and fees, and their performance. Paragraphs 28-31 and 43-44 allege breaches causing loss. These allegations supply substantially more than a vague appeal to industry custom or a lender’s unilateral expectation.

An escrow holder’s acceptance of separate lender instructions may create enforceable contractual obligations. (Money Store Investment Corp. v. Southern California Bank (2002) 98 Cal.App.4th 722, 728-729.) Accepting the FAC’s allegations for this purpose, Escrow Mart’s reliance on the borrower’s written agreement does not defeat the alleged direct agreement. Because this theory is sufficient, the Court need not resolve plaintiff’s alternative third party beneficiary theory.

The estoppel claim alleges definite promises, foreseeable and actual reliance in selecting Escrow Mart and depositing the funds, and resulting injury. (FAC, ¶¶ 47-51.) Escrow Mart argues that the alleged bargained-for performance establishes consideration and therefore forecloses promissory estoppel. An enforceable contract, if established, may make that alternative unnecessary. At the pleading stage, however, plaintiff may plead contract and promissory estoppel inconsistently where the contractual premise remains disputed. (Newport Harbor Ventures, LLC v. Morris Cerullo World Evangelism (2016) 6 Cal.App.5th 1207, 1224-1225, affd. on other grounds (2018) 4 Cal.5th 637 (Newport Harbor Ventures).) The first and second causes of action are sufficient.

3. Money had and received and restitution: third and fifth causes of action

Plaintiff alleges that Escrow Mart received the funds for the lenders’ benefit, misused disbursements intended to improve their collateral, and wrongfully retains the remaining balance despite the alleged right to its return. (FAC, ¶¶ 20-26, 33-37, 53, 62.) Funds may benefit a borrower through construction while also serving the lenders’ alleged security and controlled disbursement purposes. The allegations therefore are not defeated merely because the original deposits represented loan proceeds.

Restitution is available for unjust retention of a benefit at another’s expense. (Professional Tax Appeal v. Kennedy-Wilson Holdings, Inc. (2018) 29 Cal.App.5th 230, 238.) The fifth claim alleges that substance, including retention to obtain a release of Escrow Mart’s potential liability, rather than merely attaching a remedy label to no underlying facts. Although a valid express contract governing the same subject ordinarily precludes quasi-contract recovery, alternative pleading remains permissible while the asserted contract is disputed. (Newport Harbor Ventures, supra, 6 Cal.App.5th at pp. 1222-1223.) The demurrer to these claims is overruled.

4. Fiduciary duty and negligence: fourth and eleventh causes of action

Escrow Mart contends that plaintiff is a nonparty to its written agreement and therefore is owed no duty. An escrow holder is a limited agent and fiduciary whose obligations ordinarily are defined by the escrow instructions. (Summit Financial Holdings, Ltd. v. Continental Lawyers Title Co. (2002) 27 Cal.4th 705, 711 (Summit Financial).) The FAC alleges more than plaintiff’s economic interest in someone else’s escrow: it alleges Escrow Mart’s direct acceptance of lender funds and instructions for the lenders’ protection. (FAC, ¶¶ 20-26, 42, 44, 55-60.) Those allegations adequately plead the relationship and its asserted scope.

The alleged failures to perform the accepted inspection and disbursement functions, disclose their nonperformance, and return funds as agreed support the pleaded breaches and resulting loss. The negligence claim also alleges a failure to exercise reasonable care in carrying out that fiduciary undertaking. (FAC, ¶¶ 56-60, 78-81.) The Court does not read into the law a general duty on every escrow holder to inspect construction or protect every lender. It concludes only that the specific duties alleged here cannot be eliminated on demurrer by treating the disputed written agreement as the only undertaking. Both claims survive the grounds presented.

5. Intentional interference and declaratory relief: sixth and seventh causes of action

The interference claim identifies the loan and security contracts, Escrow Mart’s knowledge of them, intentional disbursements allegedly designed to disrupt them, resulting default, and damage. (FAC, ¶¶ 28-32, 64-66.) Escrow Mart’s reply does not press its argument that involvement in the overall transaction alone makes it immune from interference liability. (Reply to Opp’n to Demurrer, p. 8.) Its remaining contention that the FAC alleges no connection between the disbursements and default overlooks paragraph 65, read with the allegations of payment for unfinished work and abandonment of construction. Whether plaintiff can prove intentional interference and causation remains for later determination.

The declaratory relief claim incorporates the allegations describing a present dispute about obligations concerning funds Escrow Mart allegedly continues to hold. (FAC, ¶¶ 33-37, 67-68.) The controversy concerns present contractual and property rights, not only damages for completed misconduct. The incorporation identifies a concrete dispute within Code of Civil Procedure section 1060. The demurrer to the sixth and seventh causes of action is overruled.

6. Unfair competition and false advertising: eighth and ninth causes of action

The unfair competition law reaches unlawful, unfair, and fraudulent business practices in the alternative. (Cel-Tech Communications, Inc. v. Los Angeles Cellular Telephone Co. (1999) 20 Cal.4th 163, 180.) Plaintiff relies on the alleged use of entrusted funds to force a release of claims arising from Escrow Mart’s own disbursement practices. (FAC, ¶¶ 33, 55-60, 62, 69-71; Opp’n to Demurrer, p. 14.) In the context of the pleaded fiduciary relationship, those allegations identify an asserted unfair practice beyond a failure to pay an ordinary contract debt. A single act is not categorically outside the statute. (Klein v. Earth Elements, Inc. (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 965, 968, fn. 3.)

Plaintiff alleges economic injury and seeks return of the specific retained funds. (FAC, ¶¶ 33-38, 70-71; prayer, ¶¶ 2, 4.) Although compensatory damages are unavailable under this statute, the allegations supporting restitution of the retained funds prevent that limitation from defeating the entire claim. (Bus. & Prof. Code, §§ 17203, 17204.) The eighth cause of action does not depend on the sufficiency of the separate false advertising claim.

The ninth claim has a different defect. The FAC identifies website advertising in paragraph 6 and exhibit 1, including statements about inspection and payment practices. It does not allege that plaintiff encountered or relied on that advertising in selecting Escrow Mart or depositing the funds. Instead, the selection and reliance allegations concern the oral promises and prior performance. (FAC, ¶¶ 20, 48, 72-73.) Private standing for an advertising based economic injury requires the causal connection supplied by actual reliance. (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17535; Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310, 326-327.) Incorporating the advertisement does not itself supply that connection. The demurrer to the ninth cause of action is sustained with leave to allege reliance and resulting economic injury if plaintiff can truthfully do so.

7. Fraudulent concealment: tenth cause of action

The tenth claim alleges concealment of the failure to inspect, verify completed work, and properly control disbursements. It alleges knowledge, an intent to prevent the lenders from removing the remaining funds or replacing Escrow Mart, continued reliance, and loss. (FAC, ¶¶ 25-31, 75-76.) The alleged fiduciary undertaking supplies the asserted disclosure duty.

Escrow Mart applies the particularity requirements for affirmative misrepresentations without adequately accounting for the pleaded nondisclosure. Specificity must be adapted to concealment, especially where the relevant internal knowledge lies with the defendant. (Alfaro v. Community Housing Improvement System & Planning Assn., Inc. (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 1356, 1384.) Here the FAC identifies the transaction, concealed practices, period of disbursements, reason disclosure was required, intended effect, and resulting loss. It is not necessary to plead the speaker and communication method of a statement alleged never to have been made. The tenth cause of action is sufficiently particular.

8. Conversion and recovery of personal property: twelfth and thirteenth causes of action

A bare contractual right to payment does not necessarily support conversion, but an interest created by a security agreement may support the requisite possessory right. (Farmers Ins. Exchange v. Zerin (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 445, 452.) The FAC identifies a specific fund and alleges security and assignment rights, Escrow Mart’s notice and acceptance, a direct undertaking to return funds upon default, demand, and wrongful retention. (FAC, ¶¶ 20-26, 32-37, 83-87.) Those additional allegations address the central deficiencies identified in the prior ruling.

Escrow Mart’s reliance on Summit Financial does not establish that assignment rights are irrelevant. That decision distinguishes an assignment by an escrow principal from an assignment between persons outside the escrow. (Summit Financial, supra, 27 Cal.4th at pp. 713-714.) The earlier assignment decision it distinguishes also rejects the proposition that retaining assigned funds, rather than paying them to another person, necessarily avoids liability. (Builders’ Control Service of Northern Cal., Inc. v. North Am. Title Guaranty Co. (1962) 205 Cal.App.2d 68, 74.) Here the pleaded security, assignment, and direct acceptance allegations cannot be rejected by accepting Escrow Mart’s competing account as established fact.

The thirteenth claim alleges the ultimate right to possession and wrongful withholding, supported by the incorporated agreements and assignments. (FAC, ¶¶ 89-90.) A claim for recovery of personal property may plead that ultimate possessory right without setting out all evidentiary facts establishing its source. (Commercial & Savings Bank of Stockton v. Foster (1930) 210 Cal. 76, 79-80.) The Court need not determine that Commercial Code section 9604, standing alone, compels disbursement, because the claim also rests on the alleged agreements and accepted assignment.

9. Compensatory and punitive damages

Escrow Mart asks to strike the $5 million compensatory damages prayer because selected loss allegations total approximately $2.1 million. A prayer unsupported by the allegations may be stricken under Code of Civil Procedure section 431.10, subdivision (b)(3). But the FAC does not plead the selected figures as an exclusive maximum. It alleges completion costs of no less than $1,016,000, impaired collateral, delay, and unpaid interest, in addition to the retained fund. (FAC, ¶¶ 33-38, 43, 60.) Neither the recoverability nor the precise measure of those losses is established by adding selected figures on a pleading motion. Because viable claims for compensatory relief remain and the asserted ceiling has not been established, the request to strike paragraph 1 is denied.

The punitive damages request presents a narrower defect. Fraudulent concealment can provide a substantive predicate, but corporate punitive liability requires the participation, authorization, or ratification specified in Civil Code section 3294, subdivision (b), by an officer, director, or managing agent. Managing agent status depends on substantial discretionary authority over corporate policy, not a managerial title alone. (White v. Ultramar, Inc. (1999) 21 Cal.4th 563, 573.) Ratification requires actual knowledge of the conduct and its outrageous nature, together with an intent to adopt or approve it. (College Hospital Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 726.)

Paragraph 20 identifies Robert Langston as president in connection with the original promises and prior proper performance, not the later concealment. Paragraph 33 calls Mike Strong the manager and connects him to the refusal to release funds, but does not allege his corporate policymaking authority or an officer’s knowing authorization or ratification of actionable fraud, oppression, or malice. Demand letters addressed to Josephine Langston do not themselves establish the required culpable corporate involvement. (FAC, Exs. 8-9.) The corporate attribution allegations therefore are insufficient. The motion is granted as to paragraph 6 of the prayer against Escrow Mart, with leave to amend supporting ultimate facts.

The March 13 ruling addressed the original possessory claims and did not adjudicate the false advertising or corporate punitive liability deficiencies presented here. The identified advertising, relationship, and corporate actors provide a reasonable possibility that additional facts could cure the specific defects. Leave to amend is therefore appropriate.
