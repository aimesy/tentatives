Case Number: 24NWCV03518    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: P

SILBESTRE N. TAIZAN v. URBANO VASQUEZ, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  24NWCV03518 

HEARING:  09/11/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#8

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Defendant Urbano Armando Vasquez’s motion to compel responses to special interrogatories, set one is GRANTED.

 

II.            Defendant Urbano Armando Vasquez’s motion to compel responses to demand for inspection and production of documents, set one is GRANTED.

 

III.          Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $675.51 against Plaintiff and his counsel of record.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant The Estate of Urbano Armando Vasquez (Defendant) moves to compel Plaintiff Silbestre Navarette Taizan (Plaintiff) to provide responses to (1) specially prepared interrogatories (SROGs), set one and (2) demand for inspection and production of documents (RPDs), set one.

 

Background

 

On October 7, 2024, Plaintiff filed a form complaint against Defendant Urbano Armando Vasquez and Does 1 to 50, inclusive. This action arises out of a motor vehicle collision on November 2, 2023 at or near Washington Blvd and Paramount Blvd in Pico Rivera, CA 90660 resulting in injuries to Plaintiff. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

 

On September 5, 2025, Defendant’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories, set one; special interrogatories, set one; and demand for inspection and production of documents, set one were called for hearing. At the hearing, counsel for Defendant took the motions off calendar. (Order, 9/5/25.)

 

On October 24, 2025, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s responses to special interrogatories, set two.

 

On April 13, 2026, the Court dismissed Defendant Urbano Armando Vasquez and ordered that the case will proceed against The Estate of Urbano Armando Vasquez pursuant to a stipulation filed by the parties on January 29, 2026.

 

Legal Standard

 

Interrogatories 

 

A party to whom interrogatories are propounded must respond within 30 days of service of the interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260.) If a party fails to serve a timely response, the party propounding interrogatories may move for an order compelling responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)¿Additionally, “the party to whom the interrogatories are directed waives any right to exercise the option to produce writings under¿Section 2030.230, as well as any objection to the interrogatories, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)¿ 

 

Requests for Production

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.260 subdivision (a), a party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days of service. Failure to do so waives all objections including privilege or on the protection of work product. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) 

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to compel Plaintiff to provide responses SROGs, set one and RPDs, set one. These motions are unopposed.

 

On June 17, 2026, Defendant propounded SROGs, set one and RPDs, set one on Plaintiff. (Babadjanian Decls., ¶ 2.) After not receiving any responses, Defendant granted Plaintiff an extension to July 31, 2026. (Babadjanian Decls., ¶ 3.) As of the date of these motions, Defendant has not received Plaintiff’s responses to the subject discovery requests. (Babadjanian Decls., ¶ 4.)

 

The Court notes that Defendant has already moved to compel responses to sets one of its RPDs and SROGs. Upon review of the SROGs, the current set one of SROGs asks different requests than the prior SROGs motion, which the Court heard and granted on October 24, 2025. As to the RPDs, the motion was taken off calendar and was not heard.

 

Defendant therefore establishes that he properly served Plaintiff with the discovery requests and Plaintiff failed to serve timely responses.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff Silbestre Navarette Taizan to provide responses SROGs, set one, and RPDs, set one is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Silbestre Navarette Taizan is ORDERED to provide verified responses without objections to special interrogatories, set one and requests for production, set one within 20 days of this Order. This deadline may be changed pursuant to agreement by the parties.

 

Sanctions

 

Defendant moves for monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and his counsel of record for failure to provide discovery responses pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, subdivision (c) and 2031.300, subdivision (c).

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, section 2030.290 subdivision (c) and 2031.300, subdivision (c), “[t]he court shall impose a monetary sanction…against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories [or a demand for inspection], unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”

 

The Court finds sanctions warranted against Plaintiff and his counsel of record for failing to provide responses without substantial justification. Defendant requests $455.34 for each motion. This amount reflects “2 hour(s) of attorneys’ fees to meet and confer, review the motion and declaration, and appear at the subject motion hearing, at $160.17 per hour; plus 1 hour(s) of legal assistant time to reserve the hearing and draft the motion, at $75 per hour; plus one $60 motion filing fee.” (Babadjanian Decls., ¶ 5.) The Court finds this amount excessive given that 1 hour to reserve a hearing is unreasonable and 2 total hours for appearance at the hearings is excessive as the hearings will occur concurrently.

 

Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $675.51 against Plaintiff and his counsel of record. Plaintiff Silbestre Navarette Taizan and his counsel of record are ORDERED to pay this amount within 30 days of this Order.

 


Case Number: 25NWCV04347    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: P

MIGUEL GONZALEZ v. AREA 51 KUSTOMS, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.: 25NWCV04347

HEARING:  09/11/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#9

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Plaintiff Miguel Gonzalez’s motion to compel Defendant Andre Terrazas’s responses to request for production of documents, set one is MOOT.

 

II.            Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Miguel Gonzalez (Plaintiff) moves to compel Defendant Andre Terrazas (Defendant) to produce responses to requests for production of documents (RPDs), set one.

 

Background

 

On December 8, 2025, Plaintiff filed this breach of contract action against Defendants Area 51 Kustoms, Inc., Andrea Terrazas, and Does 1 through 10. The form complaint alleges that Defendant breached a contract with Plaintiff to restore Plaintiff’s vehicle and wrongfully detained Plaintiff’s vehicle at Area 51 Kustoms, Inc. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) breach of contract and (2) fraud.

 

On April 9, 2026, default was entered as to Area 51 Kustoms, Inc.

 

Legal Standard

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.260 subdivision (a), a party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days of service. Failure to do so waives all objections including privilege or on the protection of work product. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) 

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant to provide responses to RPDs, set one.

 

Plaintiff served Defendant with RPDs, set one, on April 23, 2026, making responses due by May 23, 2026. (Gomez Decl., ¶ 3.) Defendant did not respond to Plaintiff’s meet and confer letter requesting responses by July 1, 2026, and had not provided responses as of the date Plaintiff filed this motion. (Gomez Decl., ¶¶ 6-8.)

 

On August 27, 2026, Defendant, in pro per, did not file an opposition, but rather filed a declaration and his responses to requests for production of documents. Defendant’s declaration appears to be related to the facts of this case rather than to the discovery requests. Defendant additionally provides his verified responses to RPDs, set one.

 

In reply, Plaintiff’s counsel argues confirms receipt of the responses and makes substantive arguments arising out of them. Given that Plaintiff is in receipt of Defendant’s verified responses, the proper motion to raise arguments about the substance of these responses is a motion to compel further responses.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant’s responses to requests for production, set one is MOOT.

 

Sanctions

 

Plaintiff moves for monetary sanctions against Defendant for the failure to timely respond to RPDs, set one.

 

“[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction…against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)

 

Based on Defendant’s untimely responses and that Defendant did not unsuccessfully oppose this motion, the Court does not find monetary sanctions warranted.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s request for attorney’s fees is DENIED.


Case Number: 25NWCV04450    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: P

BRENDA CANSLER v. ARTESIA COMMUNITY HEALTHCARE, LLC, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 25NWCV04450 

HEARING:  09/11/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#10

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Petitioner’s Corey Cansler’s petition for approval of compromise of claim is CONTINUED to Friday, October 9, 2026 at 9:30 AM in Dept. P. Petitioner is instructed to correct the deficiencies by Friday, October 2, 2026.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Petitioner Corey Cansler (Petitioner), Guardian ad Litem for Claimant Brenda Cansler (Claimant) moves for approval of compromise of claim.

 

Background

 

On December 16, 2025, Plaintiff Brenda Cansler, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Corey Cansler filed this action against Defendants Artesia Community Healthcare, LLC dba Artesia Palms Care Center (Artesia Palms); PACS Group, Inc.; and Does 1-25 (collectively, Defendants). The complaint alleges that Plaintiff, aged 74, was admitted to Artesia Palms on March 25, 2025 for skilled nursing care and management of her psychiatric symptoms. (Complaint, ¶¶ 19, 24.) Despite Defendants’ awareness of Plaintiff’s risks of elopement and wandering, on May 18, 2025, Plaintiff and another resident left the facility and remained missing for a 27-hour period. (Complaint, ¶¶ 29-31.) The complaint alleges the CDPH conducted an investigation into the elopement episode and determined it was an “an Immediate Jeopardy (IJ) situation,” a situation in which a facility’s non-compliance with a regulation caused serious harm or impairment, to a resident. (Complaint, ¶ 38.) The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) negligence and (2) violation of patient’s rights.

 

On May 8, 2026, Petitioner filed a Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim (Petition).

 

On June 12, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s ex parte application to advance this Petition.

 

On June 30, 2026, Petitioner filed an Amendment to Plaintiff’s Petition to Approve Compromise Requesting Permission to Place Funds into Special Needs Pooled Trust (Amendment).

 

On July 16, 2026, this action first came on for hearing. The Court continued the hearing and requested supplemental information. The Court again continued this motion on August 7, 2026.

 

Legal Standard

 

A Petition for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment for minor or person with a disability must conform with Code of Civil Procedure section 372 and Probate Code sections 2504 and 3600 et seq. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Such a petition “must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Moving papers should include the Petition of Guardian Ad Litem for Compromise of Disputed Claim of Minor or Person with Disability (Petition) on Form MC-350 and an Order Approving Compromise of Minor’s Claim (Order) Form MC-351.

 

Discussion

 

Petitioner, as son and Guardian ad Litem for Claimant, a seventy-six-year-old with a disability, moves for approval of compromise of claim and to place funds into a special needs pooled trust on behalf of Claimant. This Petition is unopposed.

 

Petition to Approve Minor’s Compromise

 

Allocation

 

Defendants will pay $75,000.00 to Claimant. (Pet., ¶ 10.) Claimant’s net recovery will be $49,276.24. (Pet., ¶ 15.)

 

Medical Expenses

 

The Petition does not claim any medical expenses. (Pet., ¶ 12a.) Petitioner represents that there are no statutory or contractual liens for payment of Claimant’s medical expenses. (Pet., ¶ 12b(5).) 

 

Attorney’s Fees & Costs

 

The Petition seeks approval of $25,000.00 in attorney’s fees. (Pet., ¶ 13a; Attach., 13a.) This represents one third or 33.33% of the settlement amount. (Hutchins Decl., ¶ 6.) This is consistent with the attorney-client agreement. (Attach., 17a.) The Petition seeks costs in the amount of $723.76. (Pet., ¶ 13b.)

 

Based on the declaration of counsel and information provided in this Petition, the Court finds this amount reasonable. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.955.)

 

Disposition

 

The balance of the proceeds of the proposed settlement remaining for Claimant after payment of all requested fees and expenses is $49,276.24.

 

Petitioner proposes that the $49,276.24 “be deposited in insured accounts in one or more financial institutions in this state, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the court.” (Pet., ¶ 18b(2).) Petitioner filed an “Amendment to Plaintiff’s Petition to Approve Compromise” (Amendment) on June 30, 2026

indicating that “Plaintiff has determined that placing the funds in a pooled trust will better serve Ms. Cansler's needs by allowing her to retain her eligibility for certain public benefits.” (Amendment.)

 

The Court finds the proposed settlement proceeds to Claimant to be reasonable under the circumstances and now turns to Petitioner’s proposed special needs trust.

 

Special Needs Trust

 

Notice

                  

When seeking approval of a Special Needs Trust, service must be made upon three state agencies including the Department of Mental Health, Department of Developmental Services, and Department of Health Care Services. (Prob. Code, § 3611, subd. (c).)

 

There is no proof of service that reflects compliant service.

 

Proposed Special Needs Trust Instrument

 

Petitioner provides the proposed trust instrument as Exhibit 1 to the Amendment.

 

While the Master Trust Agreement was provided, the Amendment does not include the necessary Joinder Agreement, which must also be reviewed. The Joinder Agreement is where specific terms of the Master Trust Agreement can be modified to fit the specific circumstances of the beneficiary. Without the Joinder Agreement, the documents are incomplete and cannot be fully reviewed. Petitioner should provide a complete, but unsigned, copy of the Joinder Agreement for further review.

 

The proposed trust instrument, as it stands, does not make itself subject to the continuing jurisdiction and supervision of the Court. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.903(b).)

 

The proposed trust instrument does not meet the requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 7.903(c) and Los Angeles Superior Court (LASC) rule 4.116(b).

 

Findings Under Probate Code Section 3604

 

Probate Code Section 3604, subdivision (b), states the following: “A special needs trust may be established and continued under this section only if the court determines all of the following: [¶] (1) That the minor or person with a disability has a disability that substantially impairs the individual’s ability to provide for the individual’s own care or custody and constitutes a substantial handicap. [¶] (2) That the minor or person with a disability is likely to have special needs that will not be met without the trust. [¶] That money to be paid to the trust does not exceed the amount that appears reasonably necessary to meet he special needs of the minor or person with a disability.”

 

The Court finds that the Petitioner has offered sufficient evidence to support the finding that Claimant is a person with a disability within the meaning of Probate Code section 3604, subdivision (b), as Claimant “suffered from cognitive impairment due to her diagnoses of dementia and paranoid schizophrenia, and consequently was unable to make decisions on her own behalf.” (Pet., ¶ 5.) Additionally, the Court finds that Petitioner shows that Claimant is likely to have special needs that will not be met without the trust and the money to be paid to the trust does not exceed the amount that appears reasonably necessary to meet the Claimant’s special needs. The SNT funds will supplement her needs by allowing her to relocate or pay for adequate housing.

 

Accordingly, the findings under Probate Code section 3604, subdivision (b) are satisfied.

 

Trustee and Bond

 

The proposed initial trustee is CPT Institute (according to the Declaration of Laura Davis, the actual name is “Institute for Health Care Advocacy, Inc., a Florida non-profit corporation, doing business as CPT Institute.”)

 

Normally, bond must be required of a trustee unless the trustee is a corporate fiduciary/trust company. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.903(c)(5); Prob. Code, §§ 2320, 15602.) The proposed trustee does not meet that requirement and bond should be required.  While the proposed trustee is a non-profit tax-exempt charitable organization, no briefing was provided that it is a corporate fiduciary/trust company.

 

Petitioner does not request or calculate bond, and the proposed order checks “bond not required.” A proper bond calculation based upon the assets to be funded into the trust, plus anticipated annual income from investments and annuity if relevant, plus an additional amount required for the costs of any recovery on the bond, is $55,000.

 

Proposed Order

 

There are general trust orders missing from the Proposed Order:

 

Trust terms are not included in the order as required (LASC rule 4.115(c)(2).

Required Probate Code 3604, subdivision (b) findings are not made in the order.

Order does not provide for continuing court jurisdiction and supervision over the trust.

Order does not address bond.

Conclusion

Accordingly, while the Court finds the terms of the settlement reasonable, the aforementioned deficiencies preclude approval of the proposed trust. There are also deficiencies in the proposed order. The Court finds a continuance warranted to permit Petitioner to correct the deficiencies in the Petition and Proposed Order.

Accordingly, Petitioner’s petition for approval of compromise of claim is CONTINUED to Friday, October 9, 2026 at 9:30 AM in Dept. P. Petitioner is instructed to correct the deficiencies by Friday, October 2, 2026.

 

 


Case Number: 25NWCV04564    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: P

LUIS ELIZARDO v. ADAM WEST, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 25NWCV04564

HEARING:  09/11/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Defendant Adam West’s motion for leave to file a cross-complaint is GRANTED.

 

II.            Defendant is ORDERED to file and serve the proposed cross-complaint attached to the moving papers within 5 days of this Order.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Adam West (Defendant) moves for leave to file a cross-complaint against Plaintiff Luis Elizardo (Plaintiff).

 

Background

 

On December 23, 2025, Plaintiff filed this personal injury action against Defendants Adam West, Brenda Maldonado, and Does 1 through 25. The form complaint alleges that on January 8, 2025, at or about Whittier Blvd. and Philadelphia St., Whittier, CA 90606, Plaintiff sustained injuries after a vehicle driven by West, and negligently entrusted to West by Maldonado, collided into Plaintiff’s vehicle. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

 

Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 428.10 provides: “A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint or cross-complaint may file a cross-complaint setting forth either or both of the following:”

 

(a) Any cause of action he has against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him.

(b) Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.

 

“A party who fails to plead a cause of action subject to the requirements of this article, whether through oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause, may apply to the court for leave to amend his pleading, or to file a cross-complaint, to assert such cause at any time during the course of the action. The court, after notice to the adverse party, shall grant, upon such terms as may be just to the parties, leave to amend the pleading, or to file the cross-complaint, to assert such cause if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith. This subdivision shall be liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of causes of action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.)

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves for leave to file a cross-complaint. This motion is unopposed.

 

Defendant argues that the proposed cross-complaint arises directly out of the same transaction and occurrence as alleged in Plaintiff’s complaint. Defendant argues that the proposed cross-complaint seeks to recover for property damage due to Plaintiff’s alleged negligence arising out of the same motor vehicle collision.

 

Defendant contends that its failure to include this proposed property damage claim is not the result of bad faith or tactical delay, but rather due to inadvertence and the timing of confirming the full extent of damages. (Yun Decl., ¶ 6.) Defendant provides a copy of the proposed cross-complaint. (Yun Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. C.)

 

Upon review of Defendant’s moving papers and proposed cross-complaint, the Court finds that the allegations in the proposed cross-complaint arise out of the same transaction and occurrence as Plaintiff’s complaint. Moreover, the Court notes that this motion is unopposed and Defendant shows that no party will suffer substantial prejudice and the interests of justice support granting leave.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s motion for leave to file a cross-complaint is GRANTED.

 

Defendant is ORDERED to file and serve the proposed cross-complaint attached to the moving papers within 5 days of this Order.


Case Number: 26NWCV00193    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: P

MARIA C. HERNANDEZ v. EAST LA REMARKABLE CITIZEN’S ASSOCIATION, INC.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV00193

HEARING:  09/11/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#12

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Defendant East LA Remarkable Citizen’s Association, Inc.’s motion to deem requests for admissions, set one, admitted as to Plaintiff Maria C. Hernandez is GRANTED.

 

II.            Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel of record is GRANTED in the amount of $760.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant East LA Remarkable Citizen’s Association, Inc. (Defendant) moves for an order deeming the matters specified in its Requests for Admissions (RFAs), set one, admitted as to Plaintiff Maria C. Hernandez (Plaintiff).

 

Background

 

On January 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed this personal injury action against Defendant and Does 1 through 50. The form complaint alleges that on June 21, 2024, at or around I-710 S/B Freeway, in the vicinity of Florence Ave, City of Bell, Plaintiff sustained injuries after a vehicle owned by Defendant collided into her vehicle. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

 

Legal Standard

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.250, subdivision (a), a party must respond to requests for admission within 30 days of service. Failure to do so waives all objections including privilege or on the protection of work product. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (a).) “The requesting party may move for an order that the¿genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) The court “shall” grant a motion to deem admitted requests for admissions, “unless it finds that the party to whom the requests for admission have been directed has served, before the hearing on the motion, a proposed response to the requests for admission that is in substantial compliance with Section 2033.220.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) 

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to deem the truth of the matters specified in its RFAs, set one, admitted as to Plaintiff. This motion is unopposed.

 

Defendant states that it served Plaintiff with RFAs, set one, on July 2, 2026, making responses due by August 4, 2026. (Mulgrew Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) As of the date of this motion, Plaintiff has not provided any responses. (Mulgrew Decl., ¶ 4.)

 

Based on Plaintiff’s failure to serve timely responses to Defendant’s RFAs, set one, Defendant’s motion to deem the truth of the matters in RFAs, set one, admitted is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Maria C. Hernandez is deemed to have admitted the truth of all matters specified in Requests for Admissions, set one, as of this date.

 

Sanctions

 

Defendant moves for monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel of record pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure Section 2033.290.

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280, subdivision (c), “[i]t is mandatory that the court impose a monetary sanction…on the party or attorney, or both, whose failure to serve a timely response to requests for admission necessitated this motion.”

 

Here, sanctions are mandatory due to the failure to submit timely responses. Defendant requests $1,110 in sanctions for bringing this motion. This amount reflects defense counsel’s billing rate of $350, 2 hours to prepare this motion, 1 hour to prepare an opposition and reply, and $60 in filing fees. (Mulgrew Decl., ¶ 5.) The Court finds this unreasonably excessive given that this motion is unopposed. The Court reduces 1 hour.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $760 against Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel of record.

 

Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel of record are ORDERED to pay this amount within 60 days of this Order. This date may be extended pursuant to agreement between the parties.
