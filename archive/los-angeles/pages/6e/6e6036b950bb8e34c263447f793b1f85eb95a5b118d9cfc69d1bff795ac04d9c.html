PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND THE SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT TO STATE ADDITIONAL CLAIMS FOR PUNITIVE DAMAGES

 

Motion filed on August 21, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Kimberly Gallegos, Alex Gallegos, and Angel Sanchez (collectively, Plaintiffs)

RESPONDING PARTY: Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC (CDS) and Logan Jose Lizares (Lizares)

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Leave to file a Third Amended Complaint in the Gallegos action and a Second Amended Complaint in the consolidated Sanchez action adding allegations and a prayer for punitive damages against CDS.

RULING: Denied.

 

BACKGROUND

 

These consolidated actions arise from a November 20, 2019 collision involving a commercial truck owned or operated by Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC (“CDS”) and driven by Logan Jose Lizares (“Lizares”) (collectively, “Defendants”). Kimberly Gallegos and Alex Gallegos (“the Gallegos plaintiffs”) and Angel Sanchez (“Sanchez”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) allege that the Gallegos plaintiffs’ decedent, Sebastien Gallegos, was killed and that Sanchez was injured in the collision. The Gallegos action was filed on November 8, 2021, and was later consolidated with the Sanchez action.

 

On August 22, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the operative Second Amended Complaint in Kimberly Gallegos et al. v. Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC et al., Case No. 21STCV41026 (Gallegos), and the operative First Amended Complaint in the consolidated action Angel Sanchez v. Consolidated Disposal Service et al., Case No. 21STCV42588 (Sanchez).

 

On August 21, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the present motion, supported by the declaration of Alan Van Gelder and Exhibits 1 through 6. Exhibits 1 and 2 are the proposed Third Amended Complaint in Gallegos and proposed Second Amended Complaint in Sanchez. (Van Gelder Decl. ¶¶ 1-2.)

 

On August 24, 2026, at Plaintiffs’ request, the Court advanced the hearing from December 23, 2026 and continued it to August 28, 2026. (8/24/26 Minute Order at p. 7.) Trial is set for September 8, 2026. (Motion at p. 1.)

 

On August 26, 2026, Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC (CDS) and Logan Jose Lizares (Lizares) filed an opposition.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a)(1) provides, in relevant part:  “The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by adding or striking out the name of any party, or by correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect; and may, upon like terms, enlarge the time for answer or demurrer.  The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).) This discretion should be exercised liberally in favor of amendments, for judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters in the same lawsuit.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047.)

 

Under Rule 3.1324, subdivision (a) of the California Rules of Court, a motion to amend a pleading shall (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) state what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, subd. (a).)

 

Under Rule 3.1324, subdivision (b) of the California Rules of Court, a separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, subd. (b).)

 

The liberal policy favoring amendment does not require leave where the moving party has unreasonably delayed and the amendment would prejudice the opposing party. (Hughes v. Farmers Ins. Exchange (2024) 107 Cal.App.5th 73, 87.) Prejudice may be shown where an amendment on the eve of trial raises issues for which the opposing party has had no opportunity to prepare. (Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 487-488.) In Magpali, denial was proper where avoiding that prejudice would require continuing trial to permit further discovery. (Id. at p. 488.) Similarly, denial was affirmed where amendment was sought after the trial readiness conference, would require additional discovery or pretrial motion practice, and the delay was unexplained. (P & D Consultants, Inc. v. City of Carlsbad (2010) 190 Cal.App.4th 1332, 1345.) Leave may also be denied where the proposed amendment would be futile. (Nelson v. Tucker Ellis, LLP (2020) 48 Cal.App.5th 827, 848-849.)

 

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs seek leave to amend both operative complaints to add punitive damages against Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC (CDS).

 

In Gallegos, Case No. 21STCV41026, Plaintiffs seek leave to file a Third Amended Complaint adding paragraph 31(A) through (K) to the negligence cause of action. The proposed allegations assert, among other things, that CDS knew the truck was required to have a substantially constructed rear underride guard, that the guard installed on the truck was allegedly effective only at low speeds, that MFG claims CDS intentionally replaced the original guard MFG supplied, that CDS employees, supervisors, and managing agents knew of the hazards associated with tampering with or removing the guard, and that the allegedly noncompliant configuration was connected to CDS’s desire to expand the truck’s use and “maximize its profits.” Plaintiffs further allege that CDS acted with malice, oppression, and fraud and seek punitive damages on that basis. (Proposed TAC ¶ 31(D)-(K).) Paragraphs 41, 54, and 62 incorporate paragraph 31 into the negligent hiring, retention, supervision, training, and entrustment, strict liability, and negligent products liability causes of action, respectively, and the prayer adds punitive and exemplary damages against CDS. (Motion at pp. 8-10.)

 

In Sanchez, Case No. 21STCV42588, Plaintiff proposes the same substantive punitive damages allegations in paragraph 29(A) through (K). Plaintiffs state those allegations are “identical” to those proposed in Gallegos. (Motion at p. 10.) Paragraphs 38, 49, and 55 incorporate paragraph 29 into the negligent hiring, retention, supervision, training, and entrustment, strict liability, and negligent products liability causes of action, respectively, and the prayer adds punitive and exemplary damages against CDS. (Motion at pp. 10-11.)

 

Plaintiffs argue there is good cause to amend both complaints to add punitive damages against CDS because, in June 2026, CDS’s retained engineering expert, Jason Arst, allegedly revealed that the underride guard was “so mild, so flimsy, that it could not even handle a 5 mile per hour collision.” Plaintiffs contend the guard was a “dangerously weak fig leaf” intended to project an appearance of compliance while allowing CDS to expand the truck’s use and increase its profitability, which they characterize as a “shocking and despicable act of conscious disregard for public safety.” (Motion at p. 2.) Plaintiffs further describe Arst’s June 5, 2026 testimony concerning the guard as a “shocking development” and state that “[s]uch a development cries out for punitive damages which is what spawned Plaintiffs’ motion for leave to amend.” (Motion at p. 4.) They also contend there is no prejudice because the amendment “does not dramatically alter the facts, evidence, or claims being made in this case,” is based on evidence “Defendants themselves have offered,” and “will not change CDS’s defense.” (Motion at p. 7.)

 

First, Plaintiffs’ supporting declaration does not address each matter required by California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, subdivision (b). The Court can discern from Plaintiffs’ memorandum the effect of the proposed amendments and their asserted reason the amendments are necessary and proper. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, subd. (b)(1), (2).) The remaining requirements concern when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered and the reasons the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Id., subd. (b)(3), (4).)

 

As to when the supporting facts were discovered, CDS contends that the basis for the proposed punitive damages allegations was known well before June 2026. (Opposition at pp. 2, 8.) The record supports that contention. The proposed allegations rest in part on the alleged inadequacy and noncompliance of the underride guard. Plaintiffs themselves state that “[p]rior to June of 2026” they were already asserting that the guard was “in the up position and inadequate.” (Motion at p. 3.) Their expert Perry Ponder testified on January 30, 2026 that the guard was “not substantially constructed” and “not strong enough.” (Ponder Depo. at 59:21-60:16.) Thus, those aspects of the proposed punitive damages allegations were known before the June 5, 2026 deposition of Jason Arst. The proposed allegations also rely on MFG’s position that CDS intentionally replaced the original bar and on invoices produced by CDS in discovery. (Proposed TAC ¶ 31(G).) Plaintiffs do not state when those invoices were produced or when they learned the facts supporting the alleged replacement. The motion therefore does not identify when all of the facts giving rise to the proposed allegations were discovered. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b)(3).)

 

Plaintiffs also characterize Arst’s testimony as something CDS “revealed” and allege that CDS “admits” the guard would fail beyond a low-speed collision. (Motion at p. 2; Proposed TAC ¶ 31(E).) Arst, however, is CDS’s retained expert witness. A party’s retention of an expert does not automatically establish the necessary agency relationship for purposes of an authorized admission. (O’Neill v. Novartis Consumer Health, Inc. (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 1388, 1404.) Nothing in Plaintiffs’ showing establishes that Arst was authorized to make admissions on CDS’s behalf. (Opposition at pp. 7-8.)

 

As to why amendment was not sought earlier, Plaintiffs identify Arst’s June 5, 2026 testimony as a “shocking development” that “spawned” the motion for leave to amend. (Motion at p. 4.) Even assuming the June 2026 testimony supplied the operative factual basis for the proposed punitive damages allegations, Plaintiffs do not explain why leave was not sought promptly thereafter. The motion was filed more than two months later, on August 21, 2026, eighteen days before trial. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b)(4).)

 

Further, the Court finds that granting leave now would prejudice the defense. The Court established the September 8, 2026 trial date and corresponding pretrial schedule on July 1, 2026. On August 17, 2026, the Court conducted a Trial Readiness Conference addressing witness and expert availability, pretrial motions, and the anticipated trial schedule, and reserved the week of August 24 for pretrial proceedings. (7/1/26 Minute Order; 8/17/26 Minute Order.) CDS states that it “has stipulated to vicarious liability to streamline the issues. (Opposition at p. 6.) The record reflects no earlier indication that Plaintiffs intended to seek punitive damages. Plaintiffs filed this motion on August 21, 2026, after the Trial Readiness Conference and eighteen days before trial.

 

Granting leave when this motion is heard on August 28 would leave CDS eleven days before trial to respond to amended pleadings seeking punitive damages based on malice, oppression, and fraud. A punitive claim against CDS would require proof of authorization, ratification, or conduct by an officer, director, or managing agent (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (b)), placing at issue questions of corporate knowledge and ratification that CDS contends, having prepared to defend a negligence action, has had no occasion to develop. CDS states that it would move for summary adjudication if leave were granted. (Opposition at p. 9.) The Court finds that responding to the amended pleadings, preparing evidence and witnesses on these new issues, and hearing any resulting pretrial motions cannot be accomplished in the eleven days before trial. Affording CDS a fair opportunity to do so would require a continuance of a trial the parties have prepared for and the Court has firmly set. (See Magpali, supra, 48 Cal.App.4th at p. 488; P & D Consultants, supra, 190 Cal.App.4th at p. 1345.)

 

Separately, the proposed amendment in Gallegos appears futile insofar as Kimberly Gallegos and Alex Gallegos seek punitive damages on their wrongful death claims. Although the caption identifies them both individually and as successors-in-interest to Sebastien Gallegos, the proposed Third Amended Complaint alleges that they “bring this action, individually” under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60, subdivision (a), and seeks damages for their own loss of Sebastien. (Proposed TAC ¶¶ 1, 40, 53, 68.) Punitive damages are not recoverable by heirs on their own wrongful death cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.61; Rufo v. Simpson (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 573, 616, fn. 14; Ford Motor Co. v. Superior Court (1981) 120 Cal.App.3d 748, 751-752.

 

In sum, on this record, the liberal policy favoring amendment does not outweigh the delay in seeking leave and the resulting prejudice to CDS. Plaintiffs have not sufficiently explained when the facts giving rise to the proposed allegations were discovered, particularly given their own showing that those facts appear to have surfaced in earlier discovery, or why leave was not sought before trial was imminent. Adding a punitive damages claim now would require additional discovery or additional motion practice CDS could not reasonably complete before trial without a continuance. Amendment in any event appears futile insofar as the Gallegos plaintiffs seek punitive damages on their wrongful death claims, which cannot support an award of punitive damages. The motion is therefore denied.

 

CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs’ Motion for Leave to Amend is DENIED.





TENTATIVE
RULING RE: ALLOCATION OF PEREMPTORY CHALLENGES



 



I. Introduction and Ruling



The Court, having reviewed
the briefs of Plaintiffs, Defendant MFG Associates, Inc. (MFG), Defendants
Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC and Logan Jose Lizares (CDS/Lizares), and
Defendant/Cross-Defendant Estate of Eugene Oh (the Estate), and the Estate’s
Supplemental Trial Position Statement, and having met and conferred as
directed, tentatively finds four sides for purposes of allocating
peremptory challenges under Code of Civil Procedure section 231(c):



1.         
Plaintiffs (Kimberly and Alex Gallegos
and Angel Sanchez);



2.         
CDS/Lizares (Consolidated Disposal
Service, LLC and Logan Jose Lizares);



3.         
The Estate of Eugene Oh; and



4.         
MFG Associates, Inc.



Each side is allotted
eight (8) peremptory challenges, to be exercised separately. The Court
declines to grant any side additional challenges in the interests of justice,
and declines the competing proposals to treat the matter as two sides or to
award nine challenges to any side. The bases for this ruling follow.



 





II. The Parties’ Positions



•            
MFG contends there are four sides, each
entitled to eight challenges (32 total), because the three defendant groups
hold divergent, mutually adverse defense postures.



•            
CDS/Lizares contend there are four sides
exercised separately, or alternatively three sides with the Estate placed on
Plaintiffs’ side; and request no fewer than six challenges if grouped.



•            
The Estate contends there are two sides
(Plaintiffs; and all defendants together), but proposes nine challenges for
Plaintiffs and three for each defendant.



•            
Plaintiffs contend there are two sides
(Plaintiffs v. Defendants), and propose nine challenges per side, with the
defense nine divided 3/3/3 and unused defense challenges shared.



The parties met and conferred
and reached no agreement on the number of sides, the composition of each side,
or the numerical allocation.



 





III. Legal Standard



Code of Civil Procedure section
231(c) provides: In civil cases, each party shall be entitled to six peremptory
challenges. If there are more than two parties, the court shall, for the
purpose of allotting peremptory challenges, divide the parties into two or more
sides according to their respective interests in the issues. Each side shall be
entitled to eight peremptory challenges. If there are several parties on a
side, the court shall divide the challenges among them as nearly equally as
possible. If there are more than two sides, the court shall grant such
additional peremptory challenges to a side as the interests of justice may
require, provided that the peremptory challenges of one side shall not exceed
the aggregate number of peremptory challenges of all other sides. If any party
on a side does not use his or her full share of peremptory challenges, the
unused challenges may be used by the other party or parties on the same side.



No published California
decision construes section 231(c)’s sides clause for juror challenges. The
Court is guided by the analogous substantially adverse interests doctrine
developed under Code of Civil Procedure section 170.6. Ordinarily a case has
two sides, but there may be more if codefendants have substantially adverse
interests. A party joined with others may be considered to be on a different
side… when the joined parties have interests that are ’substantially adverse —
the paradigm being two drivers whose collision injures the plaintiff, jointly
named as defendants. Whether joined parties share a side is a question of fact,
and the proponent of separate-side status must provide evidence of a conflict;
substantially adverse interests are not presumed, and mere likelihood of, or
potential for, a conflict will not suffice. Conversely, the fact that
codefendants may be jointly liable for a judgment against them is insufficient,
standing alone, to show substantially adverse interests.



Because a single jury will be
selected to try both the bifurcated liability phase and the ensuing
damages/apportionment phase, the allocation is a single determination made
once, at jury selection, by reference to all issues this jury will decide. Once
the jury is passed for cause and sworn, the time for voir dire and peremptory
challenges is over, and a party may not reserve or exercise challenges at the
close of one phase before the next. The Court therefore evaluates the parties’
respective interests in the issues across the entire trial, not merely the
liability phase.



 





IV. Analysis



A. The three defendant groups are
substantially adverse to one another.



The
record establishes actual, evidenced conflict among the defendants — not the
mere potential for conflict that would be insufficient under. CDS/Lizares and
MFG have filed a live cross-complaint and actively contest which of them is
responsible for the selection, installation, condition, and operation of the
rear underride guard. The Estate disputes CDS’s and MFG’s contentions regarding
Mr. Oh’s speed and driving, while CDS and MFG contend Mr. Oh’s
conduct caused the collision. The jury will apportion comparative fault among
the Estate, CDS, and MFG, and each will argue for a different allocation. A
defense strategy of avoiding liability by shifting responsibility onto a
coparty is a classic example of the substantially adverse interest that places
parties on different sides. The Court finds each of the three defendant groups
substantially adverse to the others.





B. The Estate is its own side; it
is neither grouped with Plaintiffs nor with the other defendants.



The
Estate occupies a genuinely distinct position. It has asserted no cross-claims.
It aligns with Plaintiffs on the liability of CDS and MFG, but it aligns with
CDS and MFG on the Gallegos Plaintiffs’ damages. Critically, the Estate remains
a defendant whose degree of liability and apportionment of damages the jury
will determine in the Gallegos matter. Because each defendant is severally
liable for non-economic damages only in proportion to its own share of fault
(Civ. Code, § 1431.2), the Estate has an independent interest in minimizing its
apportioned percentage — an interest adverse to Plaintiffs and to its
co-defendants alike on an issue this jury will decide.



For
these reasons, the Court rejects the proposal to place the Estate on Plaintiffs’
side. Grouping the Estate with Plaintiffs would allow parties who remain
adverse on apportionment to pool their strikes, and would understate the Estate’s
distinct interests across the phases this single jury will decide. The Estate
is properly treated as its own, fourth side.





C. The Estate’s good-faith
settlement does not alter this conclusion.



The
Estate’s good-faith settlement determination was granted, unopposed, only in
the Sanchez matter, as to the plaintiff who accepted policy limits; the
Gallegos Plaintiffs declined and retain live claims against the Estate. A
good-faith settlement bars other tortfeasors’ claims against the settlor for
equitable contribution or indemnity (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6(c); ), but it
does not dismiss the Estate from the Gallegos action, and the settling
tortfeasor’s comparative fault is still determined by the jury for
apportionment purposes. The Estate itself acknowledges it remains a present
defendant subject to the jury’s liability and apportionment findings. The
settlement therefore does not remove the Estate as a party or merge its
interests with any other side.





D. The two-side proposals are
rejected.



Plaintiffs
and the Estate urge two sides, relying principally on People v. Ainsworth, 45
Cal. 3d 984, 755 P.2d 1017 (1988) and, in Plaintiffs’ case, California Rules of
Court, rule 3.516. Neither authority governs. Ainsworth construed former
Penal Code section 1070.5, the criminal statute that required jointly
tried defendants to exercise joint peremptory challenges, and held only that
compelling such joinder does not violate due process or equal protection. Its
observation that codefendants share a common interest at jury selection in
avoiding a pro-prosecution jury is not a holding, and it does not address civil
allocation under section 231(c). The civil statute, unlike former section
1070.5, affirmatively directs the Court to divide parties into two or more
sides according to their respective interests in the issues, expressly
contemplating that substantially adverse codefendants may constitute separate
sides. Rule 3.516, in turn, applies by its terms only to peremptory challenges
of the assigned judge under section 170.6 in coordinated proceedings; it does
not govern juror challenges under section 231(c). That the defendants share a
general interest in non-plaintiff-oriented jurors does not erase the
substantial adversity the record establishes on the issues to be tried.





E. The nine-challenge and 3/3/3
proposals are rejected.



The
Estate and Plaintiffs each propose nine challenges for the plaintiff side and a
3/3/3 split among the defendants. These proposals do not conform to the
statute. Section 231(c) fixes each side’s allotment at eight; a division of one
side’s eight challenges among three parties would be made as nearly equally as
possible (i.e., 3/3/2), and provides no basis to enlarge any side to nine. The
Court addresses the number of challenges through the statutory per-side figure,
not by adding a discretionary ninth.





F. The Court declines to award
additional challenges in the interests of justice.



Because
the Court finds more than two sides, it has discretion to grant additional
challenges as the interests of justice may require, subject to the cap that the
peremptory challenges of one side shall not exceed the aggregate number of
peremptory challenges of all other sides. Plaintiffs contend that an allocation
giving the defense collectively more challenges than Plaintiffs is imbalanced
and unwieldy. The Court has considered that concern and declines to adjust the
allocation. An equal allotment of eight challenges to every side treats each
party’s distinct interests evenhandedly; the statutory cap is satisfied,
because no single side’s eight challenges exceed the aggregate (24) held by the
other three sides; and Plaintiffs, who proceed on aligned theories through
common counsel, are not disadvantaged by receiving the same eight challenges
the statute affords every side. The Court finds the equal per-side allocation
just and declines to grant any side additional challenges.



 





V. Order



1.         
For purposes of allocating peremptory challenges
under Code of Civil Procedure section 231(c), the Court finds four sides:
(1) Plaintiffs; (2) CDS/Lizares; (3) the Estate of Eugene Oh; and (4) MFG.



2.         
Each side shall have eight (8) peremptory
challenges, exercised separately.



3.         
On the Plaintiffs’ side, the Gallegos Plaintiffs
and Angel Sanchez shall divide their eight challenges as nearly equally as
possible; on the CDS/Lizares side, Consolidated Disposal Service, LLC and Logan
Jose Lizares shall divide their eight challenges as nearly equally as possible.
If any party on a side does not use its full share, the unused challenges may
be used by the other party or parties on the same side.



4.         
Peremptory challenges shall be taken or passed
by the four sides alternately pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section
231(d), in the following order: (1) Plaintiffs; (2) CDS/Lizares; (3) Estate of
Eugene Oh; and (4) MFG. Each side, when called upon in that rotation, may
exercise one peremptory challenge or pass. After each exercised challenge, the
panel shall be restored to full before the next side is called upon to exercise
or pass a challenge. The rotation shall then repeat in the same order until the
jury is selected. A side's remaining peremptory challenges shall not be
diminished by a pass. When each of the four sides passes consecutively, the
jury shall be sworn unless the Court, for good cause, otherwise orders.  



5.         
No side is granted additional peremptory
challenges. This allocation is a single determination for the entire trial,
including both the liability and damages phases to be tried to the same jury,
and challenges may not be reserved for or reopened at the commencement of the
damages phase.

Case Number: 22CHCV01497

 

(1) CROSS-DEFENDANT CARY SPYROS MALLAS’S MOTION FOR DETERMINATION OF GOOD FAITH SETTLEMENT AND FOR DISMISSAL OF CROSS-COMPLAINT

(2) DEFENDANT/CROSS-COMPLAINANT CITY OF LOS ANGELES’S MOTION TO CONTEST CROSS-DEFENDANT CARY SPYROS MALLAS’S APPLICATION FOR DETERMINATION OF GOOD FAITH SETTLEMENT

 

Motions filed on April 9, 2026 and June 24, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Cary Spyros Mallas (Mallas) and City of Los Angeles (City)

RESPONDING PARTY: City and Mallas, respectively

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Mallas seeks a determination that his September 2022 settlement with Plaintiff Kimberly Yvonne Scott (Plaintiff) was made in good faith under Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6 and dismissal of the City’s cross-complaint. The City contests the determination and alternatively requests a continuance for additional discovery.

RULING: Mallas’s motion is denied for lack of jurisdiction under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008. The City’s motion to contest and alternative request for a continuance are denied as moot.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On December 27, 2022, Plaintiff filed the Complaint against the City.

 

On February 25, 2025, the City filed a cross-complaint against Mallas asserting indemnification, contribution, apportionment of fault, and declaratory relief.

 

On May 7, 2025, Mallas filed an application for determination of good faith settlement concerning his September 2022 settlement with Plaintiff. On June 2, 2025, the City filed a motion to contest the application. On October 6, 2025, the Court granted the City’s motion to contest and denied Mallas’s application. (10/6/25 Minute Order at pp. 1-2, 9-10.)

 

On January 20, 2026, the case was reassigned to Department F46. (1/20/26 Minute Order.)

 

On April 9, 2026, Mallas filed the present Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement and for Dismissal of Cross-Complaint, supported by declarations and exhibits concerning the incident, settlement, insurance, and his financial condition.

 

On June 24, 2026, the City filed its Motion to Contest and the declaration of Robert K. Dixon with exhibits.

 

On August 20, 2026, Mallas filed a reply in support of his motion and opposition to the City’s motion, together with a supplemental declaration and additional evidence.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1008 governs situations like the present where a motion has been denied and the same relief is thereafter sought again. It provides two options. Subdivision (a) governs motions for reconsideration. A motion for reconsideration must be made within 10 days after service of written notice of entry of the order, to the same judge or court that made the order, based on new or different facts, circumstances, or law, and supported by an affidavit containing the information specified in the statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (a).)

 

Subdivision (b) governs renewed motions. It provides that a party who originally made an application that was denied in whole or in part may make a subsequent application for the same order “upon new or different facts, circumstances, or law.” The application must be accompanied by an affidavit stating what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order or decision was made, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (b).)

 

Here, Mallas previously sought a determination that the same September 2022 settlement was made in good faith. On October 6, 2025, the Court denied that application because Mallas had not presented substantial evidence concerning his proportionate liability or financial condition. (10/6/25 Minute Order at pp. 9-10.) The present motion again seeks a determination that the same settlement was made in good faith and expressly characterizes itself as a “renewed motion” intended to cure the evidentiary deficiencies identified in the prior ruling. (Motion at pp. 2-3.)

 

To the extent the present motion could be treated as a motion for reconsideration under subdivision (a), it does not establish compliance with that subdivision. It was filed approximately six months after the prior ruling, is not presented as a motion for reconsideration, does not establish compliance with subdivision (a)’s 10-day requirement, and was not made before the judge who entered the prior ruling.

 

The motion does not comply with subdivision (b) either. Although Mallas now presents additional evidence concerning the accident and his financial condition, the supporting declarations do not state what application was previously made, when and to what judge it was made, what order or decision was entered, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown. (Atanous Decl. ¶¶ 1-9; Mallas Incident Decl. ¶¶ 2-10; Mallas Financial Decl. ¶¶ 2-11.)

 

Mallas also does not satisfactorily explain why evidence concerning matters already within his knowledge, including his own observations of the accident and his then-existing financial circumstances, was not presented with the prior application. A renewed motion requires diligence and a satisfactory explanation for failing to present the new or different information earlier. (Even Zohar Construction & Remodeling, Inc. v. Bellaire Townhouses, LLC (2015) 61 Cal.4th 830, 833.) The information must be such that the moving party could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered or produced it earlier. (New York Times Co. v. Superior Court (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 206, 212-213.)

 

Mallas characterizes the October 6, 2025 denial as without prejudice. The October 6, 2025 minute order does not contain that qualification, nor does the record before the Court contain any order authorizing Mallas to renew the application upon submission of additional evidence. The present motion again seeks the same order concerning the same settlement and is therefore a renewed application subject to section 1008, subdivision (b).

 

Section 1008, subdivision (e), explicitly provides that the section “specifies the court’s jurisdiction with regard to applications for reconsideration of its orders and renewals of previous motions” and that no application for renewal of a previous motion may be considered unless made according to section 1008. The court acts in excess of its jurisdiction when it grants reconsideration that does not comply with section 1008. (Gilberd v. AC Transit (1995) 32 Cal.App.4th 1494, 1499.)

 

Mallas has not complied with section 1008. The motion is denied on the basis that the Court lacks jurisdiction to consider the renewed motion. Because the Court lacks jurisdiction to consider the renewed motion, the Court does not determine whether the expanded evidentiary record would otherwise satisfy section 877.6 and the Tech-Bilt factors.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Mallas’s Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement and for Dismissal of Cross-Complaint is denied for lack of jurisdiction. The City’s Motion to Contest and alternative request for a continuance are denied as moot.

DEFENDANT FANG LI’S MOTION TO QUASH SERVICE OF SUMMONS

 

Motion filed on June 2, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Fang Li

RESPONDING PARTY: Zhuojun Yu

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order quashing service of the Summons and Complaint on Defendant Fang Li.

RULING: Granted. Defendant Fang Li’s motion to quash service of summons is granted as to the May 4, 2026 attempted service. The Court makes no determination concerning the validity of the purported July 15, 2026 service. Plaintiff is ordered to file, within 5 court days, any proof of service relating to the purported July 15, 2026 substituted service of Defendant Fang Li.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On August 2, 2024, Plaintiff Zhuojun Yu (Plaintiff) filed the Complaint against Defendants Jiao Du, Fang Li (Defendant), Mei Bai, and Does 1 through 25.

 

On June 2, 2026, Defendant filed the present motion to quash service of summons pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, supported by her declaration and Exhibit 1. Plaintiff filed an opposition on August 12, 2026. No reply was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, subdivision (a)(1) permits a defendant to move to quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction over the defendant.

 

When a defendant challenges the validity of service by moving to quash, “the burden is on the plaintiff to prove the existence of jurisdiction by proving, inter alia, the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Floveyor International, Ltd. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 789, 793.) The plaintiff must establish the jurisdictional criteria by a preponderance of the evidence. (Ziller Electronics Lab GmbH v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 1222, 1232.) Jurisdictional facts must be proved by admissible evidence, which generally requires documentary evidence and declarations by competent witnesses. (Rivelli v. Hemm (2021) 67 Cal.App.5th 380, 402.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 415.20, subdivision (b) provides that if the summons and complaint cannot with reasonable diligence be personally delivered, substituted service may be made by leaving the summons and complaint at the person’s dwelling house, usual place of abode, usual place of business, or usual mailing address in the presence of a competent member of the household or a person apparently in charge, at least 18 years of age, who is informed of the contents, and thereafter mailing a copy to the person at the place where the documents were left.

 

Here, Defendant’s motion challenges an attempted service on May 4, 2026 at the offices of JC Pacific Corp. Defendant declares that she was not personally served, that JC Pacific Corp. is not her residence, usual place of abode, or usual place of business, and that she was not present there on May 4, 2026. (Li Decl., ¶¶ 2-4.) Plaintiff’s opposition, however, identifies July 15, 2026 as the date of the substituted service on which Plaintiff relies and expressly states that the May 4 event occurred on “a different date from the actual service on July 15, 2026.” (Opp. at pp. 2-3, 7-9.) Thus, Defendant’s motion challenges the May 4 service attempt, while Plaintiff relies on a separate purported service occurring on July 15, 2026.

 

Plaintiff contends that its proof of service establishes substituted service on July 15, 2026 by leaving the summons and complaint at JC Pacific Corp. and thereafter mailing copies to Defendant at that address. (Opp. at p. 8.) No such proof of service is attached to the opposition or otherwise appears in the record provided to the Court. Plaintiff also submits no declaration from the process server establishing the circumstances of the purported July 15 service. The Court therefore does not determine the validity of the purported July 15, 2026 service on the present record.

 

The Court does not reach the parties’ remaining arguments concerning whether JC Pacific Corp. constitutes Defendant’s usual place of business or whether refusal by office personnel would defeat otherwise proper substituted service. Those issues do not affect the disposition on the present record.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Defendant’s motion to quash the purported May 4, 2026 service is granted. The Court makes no determination concerning the purported July 15, 2026 service. Plaintiff is ordered, within 5 court days, to file any proof of service upon which Plaintiff relies concerning the purported July 15, 2026 substituted service of Defendant Fang Li.

PETITION FOR APPROVAL OF COMPROMISE OF CLAIM OF MINOR AYLIAHNA PEREZ

 

Motion filed on July 31, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Karen Bastian, as Guardian Ad Litem for Ayliahna Perez

RESPONDING PARTY: None

NOTICE: Defective. No proof of service of the petition appears in the filing provided to the Court.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Approval of the $25,000 settlement of minor Ayliahna Perez's claims against Lira S. Torres, including $10,000 in attorney's fees, $435 in costs, $4,537.23 in medical expenses, and deposit of the $10,027.77 net recovery into a blocked account.

RULING: Denied without prejudice.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On July 31, 2026, Petitioner filed a Petition for Approval of Compromise of Pending Action on behalf of Ayliahna Perez, together with proposed forms MC-351 and MC-355.

 

The petition reflects a gross settlement of $25,000.00, with $4,537.23 in medical expenses, $10,000.00 in attorney’s fees, and $435.00 in costs, leaving $10,027.77 for deposit into a blocked account. (Petition, MC-350, items 15-16.)

 

No proof of service of the petition was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Notice

 

The petition is not accompanied by a proof of service. The Court therefore cannot make the finding required by form MC-351 that notice was given as required by law.

 

Current Condition and Medical Report

 

Item 8(a) states that the minor has recovered completely and has no permanent injuries. (Petition, MC-350, item 8(a).) The medical records submitted do not establish that representation. Although item 7 describes only chiropractic treatment ending in 2023, the attached records show additional evaluation and treatment in 2025. The most recent medical record concerning the minor, dated August 4, 2025, states that she continued to experience neck and right trapezius pain, had begun physical therapy, and reported that the pain interfered with daily life. The provider recommended continued physical therapy and follow-up after completion of therapy. (Petition, PDF pp. 89-93.) No later medical report concerning the minor establishes that she subsequently recovered completely.

 

Item 8 requires a doctor’s report containing a diagnosis or prognosis and a report of the claimant’s current condition. The renewed petition must accurately complete items 7 and 8 and include as Attachment 8 a current medical report addressing the minor’s diagnosis, prognosis, and current condition.

 

Allocation of Settlement

 

Item 11 reflects that $50,000 is being paid to Karen Bastian and $25,000 to the minor arising from the same accident. (Petition, MC-350, item 11b(5), PDF p. 3.) Petitioner is identified as a plaintiff in the same action, but the petition does not include the required Attachment 11b(3) explaining the circumstances and the effect of Petitioner’s claim and its disposition on the minor’s compromise. The petition also does not include Attachment 11b(6) explaining the reasons for the apportionment. Item 11b(2), concerning whether Petitioner has a claim against the minor’s recovery, is not completed.

 

The renewed petition must complete item 11 and provide the required Attachments 11b(3) and 11b(6).

 

Medical Expenses and Medi-Cal

 

Item 12 is incomplete. Item 12a(2) is blank even though the attached DHCS final lien reflects that Medi-Cal paid $49.64. Item 12b(4) likewise does not state the amount paid by Medi-Cal, and neither item 12b(4)(a) nor 12b(4)(b) concerning notice to the Director of Health Care Services is selected. Item 12b(5)(a) also does not indicate whether there are statutory or contractual liens of medical service providers. (Petition, MC-350, item 12, PDF pp. 4-5.)

 

The attached February 2, 2024 DHCS letter states that Medi-Cal paid $49.64 and reduced its reimbursement claim to $37.23. (Petition, PDF pp. 101-104.) The letter also requires notification of additional injury-related services and states that DHCS may be entitled to additional reimbursement for Medi-Cal benefits not included in the stated amount. Because the petition contains treatment records from 2025, the renewed petition should include updated Medi-Cal lien information or confirmation that $37.23 remains the final amount.

 

Attorney’s Fees

 

Petitioner requests $10,000.00 in attorney’s fees, representing 40 percent of the minor’s gross recovery. Counsel’s declaration identifies the work performed and generally cites the nature and complexity of the matter, contingent risk, time and labor, and the result obtained. (Ghevondyan Decl. ¶¶ 4-6, Petition PDF p. 120.) Any renewed petition should provide sufficient factual information addressing the applicable factors under California Rules of Court, rule 7.955 to permit the Court to determine the reasonableness of the requested fee.

 

Proposed Orders

 

The proposed MC-351 cannot be signed as submitted. Item 8a(2) authorizes $14,972.23 as reimbursement for expenses paid by Petitioner or counsel, while item 8a(1) separately authorizes $10,000.00 in attorney’s fees and item 8a(3) separately authorizes $4,537.23 in direct medical payments. (Proposed MC-351, item 8a, PDF p. 2.) These amounts do not add up to the $14,972.23 total allowance stated in item 8a(5). A corrected MC-351 must accurately reflect the intended disbursements and total allowance.

 

The proposed MC-351 also leaves items 5, 6b, and 11 incomplete. Item 9a identifies Bank of America and its address but does not state the $10,027.77 amount to be deposited as required by that item. (Proposed MC-351, PDF pp. 1, 3-4.) A corrected MC-351 must accurately reflect the deductions and complete the blocked-account provisions.

 

The proposed MC-355 otherwise reflects the $10,027.77 deposit, the minor’s date of birth, and the appropriate blocked-account withdrawal restrictions. (Proposed MC-355.) A corrected MC-355 consistent with the amended petition and corrected MC-351 should be submitted with any renewed petition.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Petition to Approve Compromise of Pending Action on behalf of Ayliahna Perez is denied without prejudice.

 

Any renewed petition must correct the matters identified above and be accompanied by corrected proposed forms MC-351 and MC-355.

(1) PETITIONER’S MOTION FOR ORDER COMPELLING RESPONDENT’S VERIFIED ANSWERS TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE
(2) PETITIONER’S MOTION FOR ORDER COMPELLING RESPONDENT’S VERIFIED ANSWERS TO SPECIALLY PREPARED INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE
(3) PETITIONER’S MOTION FOR ORDER COMPELLING RESPONDENT’S VERIFIED RESPONSES TO DEMAND FOR INSPECTION AND PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE

 

Motions filed on July 16, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company (State Farm)

RESPONDING PARTY: Araik Khacatryan (Respondent)

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: State Farm seeks orders compelling Respondent to serve verified responses without objections to Form Interrogatories, Set One, Specially Prepared Interrogatories, Set One, and Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One, and to produce responsive documents within 20 days.

RULING: Granted. Respondent shall serve verified responses without objections to Form Interrogatories, Set One, Specially Prepared Interrogatories, Set One, and Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One, and produce all responsive documents within 20 days.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On July 15, 2026, State Farm filed a Petition to Open Unlimited Civil Court File to Establish Jurisdiction Over Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Arbitration Matters Under Insurance Code section 11580.2(f). 

 

On July 16, 2026, State Farm filed the present motions to compel responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, Specially Prepared Interrogatories, Set One, and Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One. The motions are supported by the declarations of Leesa A. Freed and exhibits.

 

No opposition was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

“[T]he uninsured motorist law grants the superior court the exclusive jurisdiction to hear discovery matters arising under uninsured motorist arbitrations.” (Miranda v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 913, 926.)

 

Insurance Code section 11580.2(f) provides that “Title 4 (commencing with Section 2016.010) of Part 4 of the Code of Civil Procedure shall be applicable to these determinations, and all rights, remedies, obligations, liabilities, and procedures set forth in Title 4 (commencing with Section 2016.010) of Part 4 of the Code of Civil Procedure shall be available to both the insured and the insurer at any time after the accident, both before and after the commencement of arbitration, if any.”

 

Here, the Petition was served on Respondent’s counsel by mail and electronic service. (7/16/26 Proofs of Service.) Case law holds that mail service is sufficient to acquire personal jurisdiction over the insured when the insured has initiated the contractual arbitration. (Miranda, supra, 117 Cal.App.4th at p. 927.) Respondent initiated the underinsured motorist arbitration on May 28, 2025. (Freed Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.) Thus, service on Respondent’s counsel is sufficient.

 

As to interrogatories, Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290 provides that “[i]f a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response,” the party waives the right to exercise the option to produce writings under section 2030.230, as well as any objection to the interrogatories, including one based on privilege or work product. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).) “The party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling response to the interrogatories.” (Id., subd. (b).) All that need be shown is that the interrogatories were properly served, the time to respond expired, and no response of any kind was served. (Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)

 

As to document demands, a party may move for an order compelling responses “[i]f a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to serve a timely response.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) Failure to timely respond waives all objections to the demand. (Id., subd. (a).)

 

Here, State Farm served Respondent with Form Interrogatories, Set One, Specially Prepared Interrogatories, Set One, and Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One, on July 17, 2025. (Freed Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. B.) Responses were originally due August 18, 2025. (Ibid.) State Farm granted several extensions and additional opportunities to respond, ultimately through February 13, 2026. (Freed Decl., ¶¶ 4-11, Exhs. C-I.) On May 1, 2026, State Farm again requested a date certain for the responses and advised that it would proceed with motions to compel if no date was provided. (Freed Decl., ¶ 13, Exh. J.) As of July 16, 2026, Respondent had served no responses to any of the three sets of discovery. (Freed Decl., ¶ 14.)

 

The unopposed motions are granted.

 

CONCLUSION

 

State Farm’s Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, is GRANTED. Respondent Araik Khacatryan is ordered to serve verified responses, without objections, within 20 days.

 

State Farm’s Motion to Compel Responses to Specially Prepared Interrogatories, Set One, is GRANTED. Respondent Araik Khacatryan is ordered to serve verified responses, without objections, within 20 days.

 

State Farm’s Motion to Compel Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One, is GRANTED. Respondent Araik Khacatryan is ordered to serve verified responses, without objections, and produce responsive documents in accordance with those responses, within 20 days.
