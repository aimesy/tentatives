Case Number: 24AVCV01664    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: A14

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES – NORTH DISTRICT

 

 

DATA MORTGAGE, INC. DBA ESSEX

MORTGAGE, 

Plaintiff, 

v. 

 

BRANDON ALAN GORMAN, an

individual; OCMBC, INC., a California

corporation; SECRETARY OF HOUSING

AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT, a

government agency; and DOES 1 through 25, inclusive,

 

Defendants. 

  Case Number 24AVCV01664

 

  [TENTATIVE] 

  STATEMENT OF DECISION 

 

 

  Date of Hearing: August 13, 2026

  Dept. A-14 

  Judge William H. Forman 

 

 

      I.          Background

 

This is a foreclosure action. Plaintiff Data Mortgage, Inc., dba Essex Mortgage (Plaintiff) moves the Court to vacate the default judgment of foreclosure entered against Defendants on March 9, 2026.

 

On December 17, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Brandon Alan Gorman (Gorman), OCMBC, Inc. (OCMBC), and the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development (SHUD), asserting two causes of action for (1) Judicial Foreclosure and (2) Declaratory Relief. Plaintiff alleges that on January 17, 2023, Gorman obtained title to real property known as 16233 Valeport Ave., Lancaster, CA 93535 (the Property) and on January 18th, executed and delivered a Promissory Note and a Deed of Trust to lender OCMBC. (Compl., ¶¶ 11-12.) Plaintiff was designated beneficiary under the Deed of Trust in an Assignment recorded on June 12, 2023, and presently owns and hods the Promissory Note and Deed of Trust. (Compl., ¶¶ 14-15.) Plaintiff asserts Gorman defaulted in payments owed to Plaintiff and elected to accelerate all amounts due and owing under the Note and Deed of Trust and requested judicial foreclosure of the Property. (Compl., ¶¶ 18-19, 25.)

 

On April 28, 2025, Plaintiff and SHUD entered a stipulated judgment.

 

On July 23, 2025, default was entered against Gorman and OCMBC.

 

On March 9, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment of foreclosure against Gorman and OCMBC.

 

On July 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed the present motion to vacate the default judgment of foreclosure.

 

This motion is unopposed.

 

-----

 

    II.          Request for Judicial Notice

 

Plaintiff’s Request for Judicial Notice – Plaintiff requests that the Court take judicial notice of (1) the complaint filed December 17, 2024 in this case, (2) a copy of the Grant Deed recorded on September 25, 2024 in the Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office as Document No. 2024065344, and (3) the default judgment of foreclosure filed in this action on March 9, 2026.

 

California Evidence Code section 452 permits judicial notice being taken of records of any California court. (Evid. Code., § 452, subd. (d).) California Evidence Code section 453 mandates a court take judicial notice of a matter specified in section 452 if a party so requests, gives the adverse party sufficient notice through pleadings or otherwise, and furnishes the court with sufficient information to enable it to take judicial notice. (Evid. Code, § 453, subd. (a), (b).)

 

A Court may properly take judicial notice of recorded documents. (Md. Casualty Co. v. Reeder (1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 961, 977; Evans v. California Trailer Court, Inc. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 540, 549 (overruled in part on other grounds) [courts may take judicial notice of recorded deeds].) The Court may take judicial notice “not only of the existence and recordation of recorded documents but also of a variety of matters that can be deduced from the documents.” (Fontenot v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 256, 265 (overruled in part on other grounds).) However, while the Court may take judicial notice of the legal effect of a recorded document where it is clear and not subject to reasonable dispute, the Court may not take judicial notice of factual matters stated therein. (Poseidon Development, Inc. v. Woodland Lane Estates, LLC (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 1106, 1117.)

 

Plaintiff requests judicial notice be taken of a recorded grant deed and the legal effect thereof and two documents within this Court’s records. Copies of the documents are attached to the request and have been provided to both the Court and Defendants. Therefore, Plaintiff’s request for judicial notice is granted.

 

-----

 

 III.          Legal Standard

Standard for Motion to Set Aside Default – Per Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), the Court may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.

“Long-standing Supreme Court precedent dictates that ‘a judgment is not void if the court has jurisdiction of the parties and of the subject matter, irrespective of whether or not the complaint states a cause of action so long as it apprises the defendant of the nature of the plaintiff's demand.” [Citation.] It thus appears that entering a default judgment based on a complaint that apprises a defendant of the nature of the plaintiff's demand but does not adequately state a cause of action is an act in excess of jurisdiction that renders a judgment voidable rather than void.” (First American Title Ins. Co. v. Banerjee (2022) 87 Cal. App. 5th 37, 44.) “If the court has fundamental jurisdiction but exceeds its jurisdiction by acting contrary to its statutory duties, the judgment is merely voidable, not void. [Citations.]” (W. Bradley Electric, Inc. v. Mitchell Engineering (2024) 100 Cal. App. 5th 1, 13.) A “trial court has no statutory power under section 473, subdivision (d) to set aside a judgment that is not void.” (Cruz v. Fagor America, Inc. (2007) 146 Cal.App.4th 488, 495–96.)

-----

 

 IV.          Discussion

 

Application – Plaintiff moves to vacate the default judgment of foreclosure entered on March 9, 2026 against Gorman and OCMBC pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) on the grounds that the judgment is void. Plaintiff asserts that after judgment was entered, Plaintiff learned that title to the subject Property had been transferred before the operative complaint was filed and the current title holder was not a named defendant in this action. (Motion, p. 1:24-2:3.)

 

The operative complaint in this case was filed December of 2024 due to Gorman’s default in failing to make the payment owed on March 1, 2024, naming only Gorman, OCMBC, and SHUD as Defendants. However, prior to the filing of the complaint, Gorman transferred title to the Property to AV Home Rescue, LLC by Grant Deed, which was recorded on September 25, 2024. (Motion, p. 3:14-16; RJN, Ex. 2.) Plaintiff argues the judgment of foreclosure of the Deed of Trust should be vacated on the grounds that it is void as to AV Home Rescue, LLC, title holder to the Property. (Motion, p. 3:27-4:1.)

 

To start, “if a party admits facts showing that a judgment is void, or allows such facts to be established without opposition, then, as a question of law, a court must treat the judgment as void upon its face. [Citations.]” (OC Interior Services, LLC v. Nationstar Mortgage, LLC, 7 Cal. App. 5th 1318, 1328-1329.) Further, the Court notes that a sale of encumbered real property does not affect the interest of a person who holds a conveyance from or under the mortgagor of the real property if (1) the conveyance or lien appears of record in the proper office at the time of the commencement of the action and (2) the person holding the recorded conveyance or lien is not made a party to the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 726, subd. (c).) Finally, Courts have long held that where a judgment of foreclosure is entered and the holder of title of the Property is not a named Defendant, the foreclosure proceedings are void. (See Burns v. Hiatt (1906) 149 Cal. 617, 620; Boggs v. Fowler & Hargrave (1860) 16 Cal.559, 563-64 [“the owner of the estate, whether mortgagor or his grantee, has a right to be heard, and no valid decree for the sale of the estate can pass until this right has been afforded to him”; judgment of foreclosure is void where the title holder was not named as a party, as the court never acquired jurisdiction over the estate.].)

 

Here, the judgment entered on March 9, 2026 ordered that a Writ of Sale shall issue ordering and directing the Sheriff of Los Angeles County to conduct a sale of the Property, including all of the right, title, and interest the Defendants and any successor thereafter had in and to the Property. (See Judgment of Foreclosure, ¶ 6.) The judgment also states that “Defendants, and all parties claiming through or under them as purchasers, encumbrancers, or otherwise, are forever barred and foreclosed of all interest, liens, or claims in the Property and every portion thereof, excepting only any statutory right of redemption provided by the laws of the State of California.” (Id at ¶ 11.)

 

AV Home Rescue, LLC, successor and title-holder of the Property, is a necessary party to this action, and any “order rendered without an indispensable party is void as to that party.” (In re Marriage of Ramirez, 198 Cal. App. 4th 336, 345; see also Fraser-Yamor Agency, Inc. v. County of Del Norte (1977) 68 Cal.App.3d 201, 214 [“If [indispensable] persons are not before the court, the court is without jurisdiction to adjudicate their rights because the failure to join an indispensable party constitutes a jurisdictional defect.”].) AV Home Rescue, LLC was not named as a party and never appeared, and the Court therefore lacked personal jurisdiction over the adjudication of AV Home Rescue, LLC’s rights and interest in the Property.

 

Therefore, in light of the information and evidence presented, the Court finds that the judgment entered on March 9, 2026 is void, and Plaintiff’s motion to vacate the default judgment entered in its favor is GRANTED.

 

-----

 

    V.          Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Data Mortgage Inc.’s Motion to Vacate Default Judgment of Foreclosure is GRANTED.

 


Case Number: 25AVCV00750    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: A14

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES – NORTH DISTRICT

 

 

ALBERT RODRIGUEZ, an individual,

 

Plaintiff, 

v. 

 

SHAY YACOBI, an individual; and OMEGA DESIGN, INC.,

 

Defendants. 

 

 

SHAY YACOBI, an individual; and OMEGA DESIGN, INC.,

 

Cross-Complainants, 

v. 

 

ALBERT RODRIGUEZ, an individual,

 

Cross-Defendant. 

  Case Number 25AVCV00750

 

  [TENTATIVE] 

  STATEMENT OF DECISION 

 

 

  Date of Hearing: August 13, 2026

  Dept. A-14 

  Judge William H. Forman 

 

 

      I.          Background

 

This is a breach of contract case. Counsel for Defendants Robert F. Smith (Counsel) moves to be relieved as counsel.

 

On June 11, 2025, Plaintiff Albert Rodriguez (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendants Shay Yacobi (Yacobi) and Omega Design, Inc. (Omega) (together Defendants), asserting a cause of action for breach of contract. Plaintiff alleges that on December 23, 2020, Plaintiff entered into a written contract with Omega, executed by its principal Yacobi, for the construction of a residential perimeter wall on Plaintiff’s property. (Compl., Attachment 2, ¶ 1.) Plaintiff asserts he paid, and Omega accepted, $41,200.00 under the contract, but that no materials were delivered, no labor was performed, and no visible activity ever began on the project site. (Id at ¶¶ 2-3.)

 

On July 28, 2025, Defendants filed a general denial to the complaint and a cross-complaint against Plaintiff for breach of contract.

 

On August 1, 2025, Plaintiff filed his answer to the cross-complaint.

 

On February 23, 2026, the Court held a Case Management Conference, at which the parties stated they were amenable to mediation. The Court referred the parties to the Mediation Volunteer Panel, a court-connected program administered by the Los Angeles Superior Court’s Alternative Dispute Resolution Office. The mediation was ordered to be completed by October 16, 2026.

 

On June 29, 2026, Counsel filed the present motions to be relieved as counsel for Omega and Yacobi.

 

This motion is unopposed.

 

-----

 

    II.          Legal Standard

 

Standard for Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – The Court has discretion to permit an attorney to withdraw from representation, and such a motion should be granted if there is no undue prejudice to the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 prescribes the following requirements of a movant seeking to be relieved as counsel: (1) The notice of motion and motion must be directed to the client using form MC-051 (“Notice of Motion and Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”); (2) The motion must be accompanied by a declaration using form MC-052 (“Declaration in Support of Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”) and “must state in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1).”; (3) A proposed order must be lodged with the court along with the moving papers and must use form MC-053 (“Order Granting Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”) and “must specify all hearing dates scheduled in the action or proceeding, including the date of trial, if known. If no hearing date is presently scheduled, the court may set one and specify the date in the order.”; (4) “The notice of motion and motion, the declaration, and the proposed order must be served on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case” either by personal service, electronic service, or by mail.

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a) – (e).)  

 

“If the notice is served on the client by mail under Code of Civil Procedure section 1013, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating facts showing that either: (A) the service address is the current residence or business address of the client; or (B) The service address is the last known residence or business address of the client and the attorney has been unable to locate a more current address after making reasonable efforts to do so within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d)(1).) “‘[C]urrent’ means that the address was confirmed within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved. Merely demonstrating that the notice was sent to the client’s last known address and was not returned…is not, by itself, sufficient to demonstrate that the address is current.” (Id at rule 3.1362(d).)

 

-----

 

 III.          Discussion

 

Application – The Court finds that Counsel has complied with the requirements of Rule 3.1362. Counsel has filed MC-051 forms directed at Omega and Yacobi. Counsel has also filed MC-052 Declarations as to each Defendant, stating that Counsel’s health has declined, making it almost impossible for him to represent Defendants in this case, and that Counsel has not heard back from Defendants since mid-April. As to Yacobi, Counsel states Yacobi did not respond to Counsel’s last effort at communication. Counsel has provided the next hearing date in this matter on the moving forms and has declared that both Defendants were served by mail at their verified last known addresses. Counsel confirmed Omega’s last known address within the last 30 days by electronic mail, and has confirmed Yacobi’s by conversation.

 

The Court also finds minimal prejudice to Defendants. Omega is a corporation, and it is well established law in California that a company may not represent itself in legal proceedings and must be represented by counsel. (See Merco Construction Engineers, Inc. v. Municipal Court (1978) 21 Cal.3d 724, 729 [“‘A corporation cannot represent itself in court, either in propria persona or through an officer or agent who is not an attorney’”.) However, the Court is not estopped from granting counsel’s motion for relief solely because it will leave Plaintiff without representation. (Gamet v. Blanchard (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 1276, 1284 fn. 5 [the ban on corporate self-representation does not prevent a court from granting a motion to withdraw as attorney of record, even if it leaves the corporation without representation].)

 

Furthermore, while the parties are actively engaged in mediation efforts, Omega need not have legal representation, as the mediation attendance requirements permit corporations and other entities to send “a representative familiar with the facts of the case, and that person shall have the authority to negotiate and to effectively recommend settlement to the governmental or corporate entity involved.” (1 Cal. Code Reg., 1222.) Thus, Defendants may have an attorney present for mediation, but this is not a requirement for either. (Ibid.) The Court also notes that trial has not yet been set in this case, it does not appear that Counsel is currently engaged in mediation on behalf of Defendants, as he has not been in contact with either since mid-April of this year, and the only upcoming hearings are (1) an OSC re Mediation and (2) a trial-setting conference, both set for October 19, 2026.

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s motions to be relieved are GRANTED. All upcoming hearing dates shall remain on calendar.

 

-----

 

 IV.          Conclusion

 

Robert F. Smith’s Motions to Be Relieved as Counsel are GRANTED.


Case Number: 26AVCV00485    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: A14

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES – NORTH DISTRICT

 

 

BRODY MAESTAS, an individual,

 

Plaintiff, 

v. 

 

OSCAR JAVIER VALDEZ; ZAIRA CAMACHO; BOMBARDIER RECREATIONAL PRODUCTS, INC.; BRP US, INC.; CAN-AM; and DOES 1-100, inclusive, 

 

Defendants. 

 

  Case Number 26AVCV00485

 

  [TENTATIVE] 

  STATEMENT OF DECISION 

 

 

  Date of Hearing: August 13, 2026

  Dept. A-14 

  Judge William H. Forman 

 

 

      I.          Background

 

This is a negligence and products liability action. Defendant Bombardier Recreational Products, Inc. moves to strike punitive damages allegations in the complaint.

 

On April 2, 2026, Plaintiff Brody Maestas (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Oscar Javier Valdez (Valdez), Zaira Camacho (Camacho), Bombardier Recreational Products, Inc. (Bombardier), BRP US, Inc. (BRP), and Can-Am (collectively Defendants), asserting four causes of action for (1) Negligence, (2) Strict Products Liability, (3) Failure to Warn, and (4) Negligent Recall.

 

Plaintiff alleges that on June 26, 2025, he was the passenger of a powersports vehicle owned by Valdez and Camacho, and manufactured and sold by Bombardier, BRP, and Can-Am when the vehicle, operated by Valdez, rolled over, causing Plaintiff to be ejected from the vehicle. (Compl., ¶¶ 14, 19-24.) Plaintiff alleges that Defendants breached their duties of care by negligently or recklessly operating, permitting use of, controlling, designing, manufacturing, marketing, selling, and providing the vehicle, causing the incident and Plaintiff’s injuries. (Compl., ¶ 31.)

 

On May 12, 2026, BRP filed a motion to strike.


On May 29, 2026, Valdez and Camacho filed a joint answer.

 

On May 29, 2026, Camacho and Valdez filed a cross-complaint against Bombardier, BRP, and Can-Am for (1) Declaratory Relief, (2) Apportionment, and (3) Implied Indemnity.

 

On June 18, 2026, Bombardier filed the present motion to strike punitive damages allegations in Plaintiff’s complaint.

 

On June 30, 2026, BRP and Bombardier answered the cross-complaint.

 

On July 31, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

On August 6, 2026, Bombardier filed a reply.

 

On August 6, 2026, the Court granted BRP’s motion to strike punitive damages from the complaint with leave to amend.

 

-----

 

      I.          Preliminary Procedural Issues

 

Meet and Confer – Before filing a motion to strike, the moving party is required to meet and confer at least five days before the date the responsive pleading is due in person, by telephone, or by video conference, with the party who filed the pleading for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the motion to strike. (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a).) The moving party shall file and serve with the motion to strike a declaration stating the means by which the parties met and conferred and that the parties did not reach an agreement to resolve the issues raised, or that the party who filed the pleading objected to failed to respond or otherwise failed to meet and confer in good faith. (Id at § 435.5, subd. (a)(3).)

 

Bombardier provides the Declaration of Patrick R. Ball, in which he declares that on May 7, 2026, his Partner Bryan Martin spoke telephonically to Plaintiff’s counsel about the allegations in the complaint and Bombardier’s intent to file a motion to strike, but despite a robust meet and confer process, no resolution was made. (Ball Decl., ¶ 3.) The Court finds the meet and confer requirement has been satisfied.

 

-----

 

    II.          Legal Standard

 

Standard for Motion to Strike – A motion to strike lies where either (1) there is “irrelevant, false or improper matter inserted in any pleading,” or (2) where a pleading or part thereof is “not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule or order of court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) When the pleadings are defective, “the defect may be raised by demurrer or motion to strike, or by motion for judgment on the pleadings.” (Coyne v. Krempels (1950) 36 Cal.2d 257, 262.) The grounds for a motion to strike shall appear on the face of the challenged pleading or form any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437, subd. (a).) When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend. (Perlman v. Municipal Court¿(1979) 99 Cal.App.3d 568, 575.)

 

To succeed on a motion to strike punitive damages allegations, it must be said as a matter of law that the alleged behavior was not so vile, base, or contemptible that it would not be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people. (Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal. App. 4th 1217, 1228-1229.) “Under the statute, malice does not require actual intent to harm.  Conscious disregard for the safety of another may be sufficient where the defendant is aware of the probable dangerous consequences of his or her conduct and he or she willfully fails to avoid such consequences.  Malice may be proved either expressly through direct evidence or by implication through indirect evidence from which the jury draws inferences.”  (Pfeifer v. John Crane, Inc. (2013) 220 Cal. App. 4th 1270, 1299 (internal quotations omitted).) 

 

-----

 

 III.          Discussion

 

Application – Bombardier moves to strike certain allegations and the request for punitive damages in paragraphs 48, 62, and 68 of the complaint and paragraph 5 of the prayer for damages pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 435 and 436 on the grounds that the complaint fails to sufficiently plead any specific facts that would warrant an award of punitive damages against Bombardier, especially in light of its corporate status. (Motion, p. 2:4-26, 5:2-7.)

 

Bombardier argues that while Plaintiff’s complaint asserts that Bombardier intentionally, recklessly, and maliciously failed to warn Plaintiff and others of the risks associated with the alleged vehicle defects, Plaintiff has not included any facts to support the allegation, and fails to allege specifically what dangerous defects existed in the vehicle or its rollover protection system or restraints or what Bombardier knew. (Motion, p. 8:23-25, 10:4-6.) Bombardier also argues that Plaintiff has failed to adequately plead facts sufficient to support his punitive damages request pursuant to Civil Code section 3294, subdivision (b), which requires advance knowledge and conscious disregard, authorization, ratification, or act of oppression, fraud, or malice on the part of an office, director, or managing agent of the corporation. (Motion, p. 10:16-24, 11:4-14.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues he has sufficiently pled facts to support his request for punitive damages, as he has alleged that the defects were known or knowable at the time of design, manufacture, and sale of the vehicle and its components, that Bombardier had prior knowledge of the dangers and risks, that Bombardier intentionally, maliciously, and recklessly failed to make changes or corrective measures, and Bombardier was well aware of the defects but failed to act or warn Plaintiff or any other user of the dangers. (Opp., p. 4:16-5:10.) Plaintiff also argues that he has adequately alleged punitive damages against Bombardier as a corporate defendant, as the complaint alleges that Bombardier had knowledge of the dangers and risks and deliberately declined to make corrective changes, warn consumers, or conduct a recall, and such decisions are generally made by managing agents, whose authority is a question of fact not resolvable at the pleadings stage. (Opp., p. 8:1-7.)

 

Plaintiff’s argument is unavailing, as it is well established in California that a request for punitive damages must be supported by specific facts at the pleading stage and not based merely on conclusory statements or recitation of the standard in general terms. “In order to state a prima facie claim for punitive damages, a complaint must set forth the elements as stated in the general punitive damage statute, Civil Code section 3294.” (Turman v. Turning Point of Central California, Inc. (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 53, 63.) “In addition to the requirement that the operative complaint set forth the elements as stated in section 3294, it must include specific factual allegations showing that defendant's conduct was oppressive, fraudulent, or malicious to support a claim for punitive damages. [Citation.] Punitive damages may not be pleaded generally.” (Today's IV v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (2022) 83 Cal. App. 5th 1137, 1193.) “Not only must there be circumstances of oppression, fraud or malice, but facts must be alleged in the pleading to support such a claim.” (Grieves v. Superior Court (1984) 157 Cal. App. 3d 159, 166.)

 

Furthermore, pursuant to Civil Code section 3294, subdivision (b), and per College Hospital Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 721, the availability of punitive damages against corporate entities like Bombardier must be based on (1) the wrongful act being committed by an officer/managing agent, (2) advanced knowledge of the employee’s unfitness by an officer/managing agent, (3) an officer/managing agent authorized the wrongful act, or (4) an officer/managing agent ratified the wrongful act.  

 

As currently drafted, the compliant does not provide any facts constituting the basis for the allegations that Bombardier acted with any malice, oppression, or fraud, any facts establishing that Bombardier knew or should have known of the defects. The complaint provides no factual allegations that satisfy the requirements of Civil Code section 3294 for recovery of punitive damages against a corporate entity. As Bombardier points out in its motion and reply, there are no factual allegations as to what the defects were, who knew of them, who ratified or authorized a wrongful act, or even that any officer or managing agent authorized a wrongful act. A mere conclusory allegation that Bombardier acted with malice, oppression, or fraud does not satisfy the specific factual pleading requirement for the maintenance of a punitive damages claim. While Plaintiff argues that the specific factual information of Bombardier’s knowledge, authorization or ratification, and malice, oppression, or fraud is in Bombardier’s possession and will be obtained through discovery, Plaintiff still must be able to supply the relevant factual basis upon which he is requesting punitive damages. (See G. D. Searle & Co. v. Superior Court (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 22, 29.) If Plaintiff has no factual basis on which to request punitive damages, to do so is inappropriate. If Plaintiff does have a factual basis on which this request is grounded, it is within his ability to so plead such facts, and he must do so to maintain this request.

 

-----

 IV.          Conclusion

 

Defendant Bombardier Recreational Products, Inc.’s Motion to Strike is GRANTED with leave to amend.
