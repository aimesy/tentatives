DEPARTMENT SED LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22STCV31611    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: SED

ROSA ROMERO, ET AL. V. LUIS LINARES

CASE NO.:  22STCV31611

HEARING:  08/03/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#8

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Luis Linares’s unopposed motion for terminating sanctions against Plaintiffs Rosa Romero, Anna Crespin, and Victor Castillo is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Luis Linares (Defendant) moves for an order granting terminating sanctions against Plaintiffs Rosa Romero, Anna Crespin, and Victor Castillo (collectively, Plaintiffs). 

 

Defendant also seeks an order dismissing Plaintiffs’ complaint pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) sections 2023.030(d).

 

Background

 

On September 27, 2022, Plaintiffs filed the instant action against Defendant and Does 1 through 100, inclusive, asserting four causes of action: (1) Strict Liability; (2) Negligence; (3) Premises Liability; and (4) Vicarious Liability.

 

On March 10, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiffs to serve responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One.  The Court ordered Plaintiffs to serve verified code-compliant responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, without objections by March 30, 2026.  The Court denied Defendant’s request for sanctions.

 

On March 11, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiffs to serve responses to Demand for Production of Documents, Set One.  The Court ordered Plaintiffs to serve verified code-compliant responses to Demand for Production of Documents, Set One, without objections by April 1, 2026 and to produce the documents, electronically stored information, and other things requested in Demand for Production of Documents, Set One, without objections by April 1, 2026.  The Court denied Defendant’s request for sanctions. 

 

On July 10, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for terminating sanctions against Plaintiffs.

 

Plaintiffs filed an opposition on July 30, 2026.  Plaintiffs’ opposition was due on or before July 21, 2026.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b) [“All papers opposing a motion so noticed shall be filed with the court and a copy served on each party at least nine court days”].)  Accordingly, the opposition is untimely and the Court declines to consider it in its tentative ruling.

 

Legal Standard

 

Where a party willfully disobeys a discovery order, courts have discretion to impose terminating, issue, evidence, or monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, subd. (g), 2025.450, subd. (h); R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486, 495.)  An evidence sanction prohibits a party that misused the discovery process from introducing evidence on certain designated matters into evidence.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (c).)  Ultimate discovery sanctions are justified where there is a willful discovery order violation, a history of abuse, and evidence showing that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with discovery rules.  (Van Sickle v. Gilbert (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1495, 1516.)  “[A] penalty as severe as dismissal or default is not authorized where noncompliance with discovery is caused by an inability to comply rather than willfulness or bad faith.”  (Brown v. Sup. Ct. (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 701, 707.)  The court may impose a terminating sanction by one of the following orders:

 

(1) An order striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings of any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process.

(2) An order staying further proceedings by that party until an order for discovery is obeyed.

(3) An order dismissing the action, or any part of the action, of that party.

(4) An order rendering a judgment by default against that party.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (d).)

 

Analysis

 

Defendant argues that terminating sanctions are warranted because Plaintiffs failed to comply with the Court’s March 10 and March 11, 2026 orders requiring objection-free, verified responses to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production of Documents, and because Plaintiffs thereafter refused to participate in the meet-and-confer process.  (Motion, p. 3.)

 

As discussed above, the Court granted Defendant’s motions to compel and ordered Plaintiffs to serve objection-free, verified responses by March 30, 2026 and April 1, 2026.  (3/10/2026 & 3/11/2026 Minute Orders.)  The Court also ordered Defendant to give notice of the rulings and file proofs of service within five days.  (Ibid.)  Defendant served notice of the rulings that same day and filed the corresponding proofs of service on March 19, 2026.  (Nason Decl., ¶ 4; 3/19/2026 Notices of Ruling.)

 

On May 7, 2026, Defendant’s counsel emailed Plaintiffs’ counsel regarding the overdue discovery responses.  (Nason Decl., ¶ 5, Ex. A.)  Defendant’s counsel followed up again on May 8, 2026 and June 3, 2026.  (Id. at ¶¶ 6–7, Exs. B–C.) According to Defendant’s counsel, Plaintiffs have not served any responses to the outstanding discovery requests.  (Id. at ¶ 9.)

 

Plaintiffs were personally served with the instant motion on July 10, 2026, but have not filed an opposition.  In addition, at the June 4, 2026 Status Conference Re: Mediation, Defendant advised the Court that it had filed a unilateral declaration in lieu of a joint status report because Plaintiffs had been nonresponsive, and mediation could not be scheduled for the same reason.  (6/4/2026 Minute Order.)

 

The record therefore reflects that Plaintiffs have failed to comply with the Court's discovery orders and have remained unresponsive despite Defendant's repeated efforts to obtain compliance.

 

Nevertheless, the Court finds that terminating sanctions are not warranted at this time.  The Court's March 10 and March 11, 2026 orders did not impose monetary sanctions or any other lesser sanctions for Plaintiffs' discovery violations.

 

As the court explained in Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604, "a terminating sanction should generally not be imposed until the court has attempted less severe alternatives and found them to be unsuccessful and/or the record clearly shows lesser sanctions would be ineffective."  Under the circumstances presented here, the Court concludes that terminating sanctions are premature because lesser sanctions have not yet been attempted.

 

Accordingly, the Motion for Terminating Sanctions is DENIED.

 

Although the Court finds that Plaintiffs have disregarded the Court's discovery orders, the Court declines to impose monetary sanctions in connection with the instant motion because Defendant did not request such relief or provide notice of the amount sought.  This ruling is without prejudice to Defendant seeking monetary or other appropriate discovery sanctions through a properly noticed motion.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Luis Linares’s unopposed motion for terminating sanctions against Plaintiffs Rosa Romero, Anna Crespin, and Victor Castillo is DENIED without prejudice to Defendant seeking monetary or other appropriate discovery sanctions through a properly noticed motion.   

Case Number: 25NWCV03668    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: SED

KISHA LASHAUN MCTHIRSTY V. GENERAL MOTORS LLC

CASE NO.: 25NWCV03668

HEARING:  08/03/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#9

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Kisha LaShaun McThirsty’s motion for attorneys’ fees is GRANTED in the total reduced amount of $24,378.00.

 

Defendant General Motors, LLC is ordered to pay the awarded amount within 90 days of this Order.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Kisha LaShaun McThirsty (Plaintiff) moves for an order granting attorney’s fees, costs and expenses in the total amount of $31,642.62. 

 

Background

 

On October 15, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative Complaint against Defendant General Motors LLC (Defendant) and Does through 100, inclusive, asserting four causes of action: (1) Breach of Implied Warranty (2) Breach of Express Warranty; (3) Violation of the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; and (4) Violation of California Civil Code Section 1793.2(b).

 

On June 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses.

 

On July 21, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

 

On July 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

Legal Standard

 

If a buyer-plaintiff prevails in an action under Civil Code section 1794, “the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorneys’ fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.”  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).)¿

 

Analysis

 

Plaintiff moves for an order granting attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses in the total amount of $31,642.62.

 

Here, it is undisputed that Plaintiff is the prevailing party in this action and is entitled to reasonable attorneys’ fees.  The only issue pending before this court is the amount of the award.

 

          Reasonableness of Hourly Rate

 

Plaintiff's counsel request hourly rates of $350 for attorney Michael Josephson and $450 for attorney Bryan Geoulla.  (Geoulla Decl., ¶ 53.)  In support of the requested rates, counsel cites cases approving their hourly rate in comparable matters.  (Id. at ¶¶ 13-20; Josephson Decl., ¶ 9-12.) 

 

The matter of the reasonableness of attorney fees is within the sound discretion of the trial court.  (Bruckman v. Parliament Escrow Co. (1989) 190 Cal.App.3d 1051, 1062.)  Normally, a reasonable hourly rate is the prevailing rate charged by attorneys of similar skill and experience in the relevant community.  (PLCM Group, Inc. v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.)

 

Based on counsel's supporting evidence and the Court's familiarity with prevailing market rates for comparable warranty litigation in this community, the Court finds that the requested hourly rates are reasonable.  Additionally, Defendant does not challenge the reasonableness of the requested hourly rate in its opposition.

 

          Reasonableness of Hours Billed

 

The trial court has an obligation to award only those attorneys’ fees that are reasonable.¿  (See¿PLCM Group v. Drexler¿(2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1096; see also¿Ketchum v. Moses¿(2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 (Ketchum).)  Although a verified fee bill is “prima facie evidence the costs, expenses and services listed were necessarily incurred,” (Hadley v. Krepel (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 677, 682), counsel still has the burden to demonstrate the reasonableness of charges.  (Mikhaeilpoor v. BMW of North America, LLC (2020) 48 Cal.App.5th 240, 247.)

 

Plaintiffs seek an award for attorney’s fees representing a total amount of 67.7 hours.  (Motion p. 3.)

 

The Court has reviewed and considered Plaintiff’s counsel's attached billing records, together with Defendant's objections to the reasonableness of certain billing entries.  (Geoulla Decl., ¶ 50, Ex. 3; Opp. pp. 3-7.)

 

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 1.7 hours for pre-engagement work on September 22, 2025.  In opposition, Defendant relies on Hensley v. Eckerhart (1983) 461 U.S. 424, 434 (Hensley), which instructs courts to exclude hours that are not reasonably expended and that would not properly be billed to a client.  The Court agrees that Hensley supports excluding time attributable to client intake or other pre-engagement administrative tasks that are not properly shifted to an opposing party.  However, the Court does not find that Hensley supports striking the entire 1.7 hours because the billing entries also reflect compensable legal work, including explaining Plaintiff's legal rights, discussing potential damages, advising Plaintiff regarding the representation, and corresponding regarding documents necessary to evaluate the claim.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 0.9 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 1.1 hours for reviewing Defendant's Answer on November 17, 2025.  While counsel was entitled to review Defendant's Answer and affirmative defenses, this is a routine Song-Beverly matter and the Answer is largely standardized.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 0.6 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 2.8 hours for preparing initial disclosures on January 15, 2026.  The disclosures required by Assembly Bill 1755 are largely standardized, although some case-specific information must be incorporated.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 1.3 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 13.8 hours reviewing Defendant's initial disclosures and document production.  While review of Defendant's production is compensable, the Court finds the time excessive in light of the routine nature of this Song-Beverly action, the limited litigation conducted, counsel's familiarity with similar manufacturer document productions, and the extensive internal memoranda and strategy notes prepared in connection with the review.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 6.3 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 2.6 hours on February 17 through 19, 2026 to review the video recording of Plaintiff's deposition, analyze the deposition transcript, and discuss the deposition with Plaintiff.  While the Court finds that post-deposition review and client communication are compensable, the Court concludes that the time billed is excessive in light of the straightforward issues presented.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 1.1 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 1.1 hours on March 4, 2026 to review Defendant's Code of Civil Procedure section 998 offer.  While the Court finds that counsel was entitled to review and analyze the settlement offer, the Court concludes that the time billed is excessive given the routine nature of the offer and issues presented.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 0.6 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 5.9 hours preparing for Defendant's Person Most Knowledgeable deposition.  While preparation for a PMK deposition is compensable, the Court finds the time billed excessive.  The Court finds that 3.5 hours was reasonably necessary to prepare for the deposition.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 2.4 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

·       Plaintiff's counsel billed 10.9 hours throughout the litigation communicating with Plaintiff regarding the status of the case, ongoing vehicle issues, litigation strategy, discovery obligations, and settlement.  The Court recognizes that counsel has an obligation to keep a client reasonably informed and that attorney-client communications are compensable.  However, the Court finds that the total time billed is excessive in light of the relatively straightforward nature of this routine Song-Beverly action, which resolved within approximately five months and involved limited litigation activity.  Accordingly, the Court will strike 3.9 hours from Plaintiff's motion.

 

Defendant argues that all pre-litigation work should be excluded because it was performed before the Complaint was filed and relies on Dominguez v. American Suzuki Motor Corp. (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 53 (Dominguez).  (Opp., pp. 4-5.)  The Court is not persuaded.  Although Dominguez held that the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act does not require a manufacturer to reimburse a consumer for attorney fees incurred in successfully obtaining a repurchase or replacement before litigation is commenced, the pre-litigation timing of the manufacturer's repurchase offer was central to that decision.  (Dominguez, supra, 160 Cal.App.4th at 58-59.)  There, plaintiff's counsel sent a pre-suit demand letter requesting repurchase, the manufacturer responded with a repurchase offer before suit was filed, and litigation ensued only after the parties were unable to agree on attorney fees.  (Ibid.)

 

Here, by contrast, Defendant did not extend its repurchase offer until after litigation had commenced.  (Geoulla Decl., ¶¶ 37-41, Ex. 1; Kay Decl., ¶ 10.)  Moreover, Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d) permits recovery of attorney fees reasonably incurred "in connection with the commencement and prosecution" of the action.  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).)  Counsel's pre-litigation work, including communicating with Plaintiff, reviewing and organizing the vehicle's repair history, evaluating potential claims, analyzing warranty and purchase documents, and preparing the matter for filing, was reasonably necessary to investigate the claims and facilitate the commencement of this action.  

 

Accordingly, the Court will strike a total of 17.1 hours from the fee request, consisting of 13.6 hours billed by attorney Bryan Geoulla at $450 per hour and 3.5 hours billed by attorney Michael Josephson at $350 per hour, for a total reduction of $7,345.00.  The Court therefore awards attorney's fees in the amount of $23,010.00, representing 50.6 hours reasonably incurred.

 

Memorandum of Costs

 

A verified memorandum of costs is prima facie evidence concerning the reasonableness of costs and expenses claimed by the Plaintiff.  (See Hadley v. Krepel (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 677, 682.)  “If the items appearing in a cost bill appear to be proper charges, the burden is on the party seeking to tax costs to show that they were not reasonable or necessary.”  (Ladas v. California State Auto. Assn. (1993) 19 Cal. App. 4th 761, 774.)  “On the other hand, if the items are properly objected to, they are put in issue and the burden of proof is on the party claiming them as costs”.  (Id.)¿

 

Plaintiff filed a costs memorandum seeking $1,447.62, in litigation costs related to filing and motion fees, service of process, and electronic filing or service.  

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that Plaintiff has failed to establish that all requested costs were reasonably incurred or recoverable under Code of Civil Procedure sections 1032 and 1033.5 and Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d).  (Opp., pp. 7–9.)  Specifically, Defendant seeks to tax $152.62 in costs, consisting of: (1) $23.00 in One Legal fees, arguing the charges include avoidable convenience fees incurred for counsel's benefit rather than necessary litigation expenses; (2) $50.00 for service of process, arguing Plaintiff failed to provide sufficient documentation demonstrating the service was statutorily authorized and reasonably necessary; and (3) $79.62 listed as "Other," arguing the entry is vague, unsupported, and not recoverable because it is neither itemized nor identified as an allowable statutory cost.  

 

In reply, Plaintiff argues that Defendant has not rebutted the presumption that the requested costs were reasonably incurred.  Plaintiff contends that the challenged $152.62 is supported by the One Legal invoices attached to counsel's declaration and consists of recoverable service-of-process, electronic filing, and related court technology fees.  

 

The Court finds that although Plaintiff attaches One Legal invoices documenting various e-filing-related expenses, the invoices do not clearly correspond to the $79.62 identified in the Memorandum of Costs as "Other," nor does Plaintiff explain how that amount was calculated.  Therefore, Plaintiff has not adequately substantiated that specific cost, and the Court taxes costs in the amount of $79.62.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff is awarded litigation costs in the amount of $1,368.00.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Kisha LaShaun McThirsty’s motion for attorneys’ fees is GRANTED in the total reduced amount of $24,378.00, representing attorney’s fees in the amount of $23,010.00 and litigation costs in the amount of $1,368.00.

 

Defendant General Motors, LLC is ordered to pay the awarded amount within 90 days of this Order.
