DEPARTMENT 514 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22STCV38521    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 514

Doe v. Defendant Doe Archdiocese
22STCV38521
Cross-Defendants’ Motion to Strike Cross-Complaint
Defendant’s Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint

Tentative Ruling

The motion to strike is granted .

The motion for leave to file a cross-complaint is granted.

Background

This action arises out of allegations of sexual assault, harassment, abuse, and molestation of a minor.

On December 9, 2022, John Mdo Doe (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against Defendant Doe Archdiocese, Defendant Doe Diocese, Defendant Doe School, Defendant Doe School District, and Defendant Doe Perpetrator, and Does 6 through 500, asserting causes of action for (1) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (2) [omitted]; (3) negligence; (4) negligent supervision; (5) negligent retention/hiring; (6) negligent failure to warn, train or educate; (7) [omitted]; (8) constructive fraud (Civil Code section 1573); (9) sexual harassment (Civil Code section 51.9); (10) [omitted]; (11) sexual battery (Civil Code section 1708.5), and (12) sexual assault. 

On December 19, 2022, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), asserting the same causes of action and naming as defendants The Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles (“Archdiocese”); The Archdiocese of Los Angeles Education & Welfare Corporation (“ALAEWC”); Alliance College-Ready Public Schools (“Alliance” or “Defendant”); Los Angeles Unified School District (“LAUSD”); Rosalio Medrano (“Medrano”), and Does 6 through 500.

In February and April 2023, Medrano, Archdiocese, and ALAEWC filed answers to the FAC.

In April and December 2024, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed Archdiocese, ALAEWC, and LAUSD.

In October 2024, the case was transferred to this department (then Department 56, now renamed Department 514). 

On December 17, 2024, the Court set trial for August 17, 2026.

On April 16, 2026, Defendant Alliance filed an answer to the FAC.  (Defendant Alliance had appeared in this action and filed documents as early as July 2024 but had not previously answered the FAC.)

On April 16, 2026, Alliance College-Ready Public Schools (“Alliance”) filed an answer to the FAC.  Among the fifteen affirmative defenses in the answer is the Second Affirmative Defense (comparative negligence/apportionment of fault).

Also on April 16, 2026, Defendant Alliance filed a cross-complaint for apportionment of fault against Roes 1 and 2.

Twelve days later, on April 28, 2026, Defendant Alliance filed a First-Amended Cross-Complaint (“FACC”) for apportionment of fault against Roes 1 and 2.

On July 8, 2026, the Court (on the ex parte application of Defendant Alliance supported by a stipulation of the parties) continued the trial date to October 26, 2026.

There are currently two motions before the Court and set for hearing on August 3.

First, on May 19, 2026, Cross-Defendants Roe 1 and Roe 2 (identified as Plaintiff’s parents), filed a motion to strike the cross-complaint filed on April 16.  Defendant Alliance filed an opposition on June 30, and Roes 1 and 2 filed a reply on July 7.

Second, on June 16, 2026, Defendant Alliance filed a motion for leave to file the FACC that was accepted for filing on April 28.  Plaintiff and Roes 1 and 2 filed an opposition on June 30, and Defendant Alliance filed a reply on July 7.

The hearing on both motions was initially set for July 14 and was continued by the Court to August 3.

Legal Standard

The Code of Civil Procedure distinguishes between compulsory and permissive cross-complaints.

A compulsory cross-complaint, under Code of Civil Procedure section 426.30, is one that asserts (1) a “related cause of action”; (2) that a party against whom a “complaint” has been filed and served has against the “plaintiff”; and (3) that exists as of the time the answer to the “complaint” is served. (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.30, subd. (a).)

As used in section 426.30, a “related cause of action” is defined as “a cause of action which arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause of action which the plaintiff alleges in his complaint.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.10, subd. (c).) “Complaint” is defined to include a cross-complaint, and “Plaintiff” is defined as “a person who files a complaint or cross-complaint.” (Id., subds. (a) & (b).)

Subject to the exceptions set forth by statute, if a party fails to assert fails to assert a related cause of action against the plaintiff that exists as of the time the party answers the complaint, “such party may not thereafter in any other action assert against the plaintiff the related cause of action not pleaded.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.30, subd. (a).)

A permissive cross-complaint, in contrast, may include a much broader group of pleadings. Under Code of Civil Procedure section 428.10, in a permissive cross-complaint a party may assert an unrelated cause of action “against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (a).) Alternatively, in a permissive cross-complaint a party may assert a “cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such a person is already party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.” (Id., subd. (b).) Cross-complaints for contribution or indemnity against new parties fall within the definition of a permissive cross-complaint in section 428.10.

A party must file a compulsory cross-complaint at the same time as (or before) the answer to the complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.30, subd. (a).) If a party fails to do so, and then later on seeks leave to file the cross-complaint, the court “after notice to the adverse party, shall grant, upon such terms as may be just to the parties, leave to amend the pleading, or to file the cross-complaint, to assert such cause if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.) The “good faith” standard must “be liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of causes of action.” (Ibid.)

A party must file a permissive cross-complaint before the court has set a date for trial, or, if the causes of action in the cross-complaint are asserted against “any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her,” at or before the party files the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subds. (a) & (b).) If a party fails to do so, and then later on seeks leave to file the cross-complaint, the court may grant leave “in the interests of justice.” (Id., subd. (c).)

Cross-complaints against a person or entity that is not already a party in the action are always permissive, rather than compulsory. (Insurance Co. of North America v. Liberty Mutual Ins. Co. (1982) 128 Cal.App.3d 297, 303; 1 Weil & Brown, California Practice Guide: Civil Procedure Before Trial [The Rutter Group 2026], ¶ 6:524.)

Request for Judicial Notice

Cross-Defendants ask the Court to take judicial notice of (1) Plaintiff’s Complaint; (2) the Order dated December 17, 2024, setting a trial date; and (3) Alliance’s Answer filed on April 16, 2026.  The request is granted.

Objections to Evidence

Alliance asserts one objection to the Declaration of Anthony M. DeMarco attached to Cross-Defendants’ opposition to the motion for leave.  The objection is overruled.

Discussion

Motion to Strike

Cross-Defendants move to strike the Cross-Complaint filed by Alliance on April 16.  (See Notice of Motion and Motion, at 2:3-4.)  The Cross-Complaint was not timely filed under Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50, and Alliance did not then (and does not now) seek a court order permitting the filing of this Cross-Complaint.

Accordingly, the motion is granted.

Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint

Alliance moves for an order granting leave to file the Cross-Complaint that was accepted for filing and stamped as filed on April 28.  (See Notice of Motion and Motion, at 2:6-7; Young Decl., Exh. A.)  As this pleading seeks to add new parties to the action, it is classified by the Code of Civil Procedure as a permissive cross-complaint.  And because the cross-complaint was not timely filed, Alliance must show that the filing of the proposed cross-complaint is “in the interest of justice.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (c).)

Rightly or wrongly, Defendant Alliance has asserted in its answer an affirmative defense regarding allocation of fault.  Defendant Alliance now also seeks to file a cross-complaint against Plaintiff’s parents asserting a cause of action for apportionment of fault.  Alliance is entitled to an adjudication of its defense and cross-complaint in some forum.

In this procedural posture, the Court expresses no views on the merits of the proposed cross-complaint.  It may have merit.  It may have none.  The merits of this (or any) pleading will be decided at a trial – or, where appropriate, on a pre-trial motion for summary judgment or summary adjudication. 

As a legal matter, if the Court denies the motion of Alliance for leave to file its cross-complaint, Alliance will be entitled to file a new, separate action asserting the same cause of action against Roe 1 and Roe 2.  Although the new action could be related to this one, the practical effect of denying the motion is that there could need to be two separate trials arising out this same subject matter and involving the same witnesses and underlying facts.  Given the nature of the allegations and facts, this will be a difficult trial under the best of the circumstances; forcing the parties and witnesses to endure two trials on the same facts is not in the interests of justice.

The Court recognizes that if the motion is granted, there may need to be a further continuance of the trial.  Cross-Defendants may need to take discovery, and they may seek to file a motion for summary judgment.  Given the late date on which Cross-Defendants have been added to the case, the Court is likely to grant any reasonable request by Cross-Defendants for an appropriate trial continuance.  Although delaying the trial again raises real concerns for the parties – and particularly the Plaintiff – those concerns are outweighed by the risk of having two separate trials involving the participants (including Plaintiff). 

Under all of the circumstances present here, the Court finds that it is in the interests of justice to permit Defendant Alliance to file a cross-complaint.

The motion for leave is granted.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS the motion of Cross-Defendants Roes 1 and 2 to strike the cross-complaint filed on April 16, 2026.

The Court STRIKES the cross-complaint filed on April 16, 2026.

The Court GRANTS the motion of Defendant Alliance College-Ready Public Schools to file the First Amended Cross-Complaint accepted for filing on April 28, 2026.  The First Amended Cross-Complaint accepted for filing on April 28, 2026, remains on filed.

Cross-Defendants are to file a response to the First Amended Cross-Complaint by no later than August 24, 2026.

Counsel for Defendant Alliance is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV31043    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 514

Jones v. CVS Pharmacy, Inc.
25STCV31043
Plaintiff’s Motion for Trial Preference

Tentative Ruling

The motion is denied without prejudice on procedural grounds.

Background

This case arises out of an alleged slip and fall at a CVS Pharmacy on West Slauson Avenue in Los Angeles on August 1, 2025.

On October 23, 2025, Helena Hammond Jones (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against CVS Pharmacy, Inc.; Garfield Beach CVS, LLC; CVS Solutions, LLC; Bryant Marroquin; and Does 1 through 50, asserting causes of action for (1) negligence, (2) negligent hiring, supervision, and/or retention, and (3) premises liability.

On December 1, 2025, Defendant Garfield Beach CVS, LLC (“Defendant”) (on its own behalf and as erroneously sued as CVS Pharmacy, Inc. and CVS Health Solutions, LLC) filed an answer.

On January 5, 2026, the Court, on the stipulation of the parties, issued an order dismissing CVS Pharmacy, Inc. and CVS Health Solutions, LLC without prejudice.

On March 3, 2026, Bryant Marroquin filed an answer.

On April 2, 2026. the Court set this matter for trial on February 16, 2028.

On June 12, 2026, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed Bryant Marroquin without prejudice.

On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed this motion for a trial preference.  Defendant filed an opposition on July 21, and Plaintiff filed a reply on July 27.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (a) provides:

“A party to a civil action who is over 70 years of age may petition the court for a preference, which the court shall grant if it makes both of the following findings:

(1)  The party has a substantial interest in the action as a whole.

(2)  The health of the party is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudicing the party’s interest in the litigation.” 

A motion for a preference must be “supported by a declaration of the moving party [stating] that all essential parties have been served with process or have appeared.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 36, subd. (c)(1).  The motion may be supported by a declaration by the attorney for the party seeking the preference “based upon information and belief as to the medical diagnosis and prognosis of any party.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 36.5.)

“Upon the granting of such a motion for preference, the court shall set the matter for trial not more than 120 days from that date and there shall be no continuance beyond 120 days from the granting of the motion for preference except for physical disability of a party or a party’s attorney, or upon a showing of good cause stated in the record.” 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 36, subd. (f).)  “Any continuance shall be for no more than 15 days and no more than one continuance for physical disability may be granted to any party.”  (Ibid.)

Discussion

Plaintiff was born in 1948 and is 78 years old. (See Meislin Decl., ¶ 5.)  She “has been diagnosed with a major neurocognitive disorder and anxiety, both due to the traumatic brain injury” that Plaintiff associates with the events at issue.  (Meislin Decl., ¶ 4.)  Plaintiff’s counsel states that she faces a “shortened life expectancy” and that a preference is necessary so that “she can participate in her case and have the opportunity to benefit from any recovery.”  (Meislin Decl., ¶¶ 6-7.)

There is one procedural defect in the moving papers, however. The statute requires that a motion for a preference must be supported by a declaration stating “that all essential parties have been served with process or have appeared.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 36, subd. (c)(1). No such declaration has been filed.

This is not a mere formality. Once the Court grants a preference, the Court’s power to grant any continuance is extremely limited. Before granting a preference, the Court must receive assurances – in the form of a written declaration, signed under penalty of perjury by the plaintiff or counsel – that that plaintiff will not seek to add new essential parties to the litigation in the limited time available prior to trial.

Plaintiff has not complied with the statutory requirements. The motion is denied without prejudice.

Conclusion

The Court DENIES WITHOUT PREJUDICE the motion of Plaintiff Helena Hammond Jones for a trial preference.

Moving Party is ordered to give notice.
