TENTATIVE RULING

 

            The Court is inclined to GRANT final approval, contingent on the parties addressing the following concerns:

 

a.      Notice:  The Court has concerns about the adequacy of the notice given in light of the report that more than 40% of the 170 Class Members did not receive notice of the settlement.  According to Plaintiff’s counsel, there are 170 Class Members. (Castillejos Decl., ¶5.) Simpluris mailed the Notice Packet to 119 Class Members with valid mailing addresses. (Id. at ¶7.) This means that Notice was not mailed out to 30% of the Class. Further, 12 Notice Packets were determined to be undeliverable because no updated address was available. (Id. at ¶9.) This means that 70 Class Members out of 170 did not receive Notice, which is over 40%. The Court orders the parties to provide a supplemental declaration explaining why the notice given was the best practicable notice under the circumstances and, if not, what additional measures could be taken to reach a larger percentage of Class Members.

b.      Proof of Occupancy:  According to counsel, Class members who do not receive the Notice Packet, but believe they are eligible to participate in the Settlement will need to provide proof that they stayed at the Hotel for 21 to 30 consecutive days during the Class period.  One method of proof is to provide a declaration under penalty of perjury that the Class member stayed at the Hotel for 21 to 30 consecutive days during the Class period along with another form of proof that he or she stayed at the hotel for at least one day.  (Singh Decl. ISO Preliminary Approval, ¶6.) The Court needs a supplemental declaration disclosing whether the administrator received any such declarations or alternate proof of occupancy and, if so, what conclusions were reached about the class members’ ability to participate in the settlement. 

c.       Average Class Payment:  The Court needs a supplemental declaration disclosing the average class payment and the projected highest and lowest class payments.  

d.      Attorneys’ Fees:  Plaintiff’s Counsel seeks a 35-percent fee award but had not provided any justification for an award greater than the one-third recovery usually granted by the Court. If Plaintiff will pursue the 35-percent award, counsel must submit a supplemental declaration justifying this higher recovery. 

e.      Incentive Award: Plaintiff Flores does not provide information with specificity in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of any financial or other risks he incurred.  Plaintiff Flores is ordered to provide a declaration attesting to why he or she should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.

 

Subject to the Court need for additional information, the Court is inclined to GRANT final approval and award the following: (1) $166,666.67 for attorney fees to Class Counsel; (2) $15,000.00 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) enhancement payment of $5,000 to the class representative; and (4) $10,000 for settlement administration costs to Simpluris, Inc.  Plaintiff’s Requests for Judicial Notice are denied as irrelevant.

BACKGROUND

This is a consumer class action regarding a practice called the “28 Day Shuffle”. Plaintiff brings this action against the owners and operators of the residential hotel called the “Leo’s Motel” (the “Hotel”) (collectively, “Defendants”[1]), located in Lomita, California.

On March 28, 2023, Plaintiff, on behalf of himself and all others similarly situated, filed the Complaint against Defendants to initiate the Action.  The Complaint asserted the following four causes of action:  (1) violation of California Civil Code section 1940.1; (2) violation of California Civil Code section 52.1; (3) negligence; and (4) violation of California Unfair Competition Law (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 17200, et seq.). On May 4, 2023, Plaintiff filed an Amendment to Complaint to correct the name of defendant 1879 Lomita L.L.C.

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants required guests, including Plaintiff, to move out or check out of the Hotel prior to the 30th consecutive day of occupancy in order to keep them in transient occupancy status, the “28-day shuffle.”  By prohibiting guests from occupying a unit at the Hotel for more than 30 consecutive days in violation of California Civil Code 1940.1(a), Defendants sought to deprive guests of protections and rights they would have been afforded had they not been unlawfully forced to vacate the hotel.  In particular, Defendants could avoid the traditional landlord-tenant relationship with guests and cause the eviction of tenants without unlawful detainer proceedings.  The same acts, omissions, and course of conduct give rise to Plaintiff’s causes of action for violation of California Civil Code section 52.1, negligence, and violation of the California Unfair Competition Law. 

Counsel represents that the Parties engaged in months-long settlement communications directly among counsel and participated in two mediation sessions with Hon. Thierry P. Colaw (Ret.), occurring on October 9, 2024 and then on January 23, 2025.  At the conclusion of the second mediation session, Hon. Thierry P. Colaw (Ret.) tendered a Mediator’s Proposal, which the Parties ultimately accepted. A fully executed copy of the resulting Settlement Agreement was filed with the Court on April 24, 2025. (See Stipulation of Settlement.)

Preliminary Approval was granted on October 29, 2025. An Amended Preliminary Approval Order was entered on March 4, 2026. Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered. (See Declaration of Orlando Castillejos (“Castillejos Decl.”).)

 

 Now before the Court is the motion for final approval of the settlement agreement.

C.      Attorney Fees and Costs

Class Counsel requests an award of $175,000 (35%) in fees and $12,000 in costs. (Motion ISO Fees, 10:5-6.) The Settlement Agreement provides for fees up to $175,000 (35%) and costs up to $12,000 (Settlement Agreement, ¶44.a); the class was provided notice of the requested awards and none objected. (Castillejos Decl., ¶15 and Exhibit A thereto.)

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba at 254.)  Here, class counsel requests attorney fees using the percentage method. The fee request represents 33% of the gross settlement amount which is the average generally awarded in class actions.  See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 (“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”).

Therefore, counsel has spent 286.4 hours of time on the matter for an aggregate loadstar of $243,440, which would require a negative multiplier to yield the requested attorney’s fees. (Ibid.)   

As for costs, class counsel has incurred costs of $12,866.90. (Singh Decl. ISO Fees, ¶8 and Exhibit 3 thereto.)  Class Counsel is requesting $12,000.00  in costs which is equal to the settlement cap of $12,000. (Ibid.) The costs include, but are not limited to, mediation ($9,250), Case Anywhere ($1,697.53), and filing fees ($1,492.68). (Ibid.)

Based on the above, the recommendation is to award $166,666.67 (1/3)  for fees and $12,000 for litigation costs.

 

D.     Incentive Award to Class Representative

The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $5,000 for a Service Payment to the Named Plaintiff. (Settlement Agreement, ¶44.b.)

In connection with the final fairness hearing, the named Plaintiff must submit a declaration attesting to why he or she should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.  The named Plaintiff must explain why he or she “should be compensated for the expense or risk she has incurred in conferring a benefit on other members of the class.”  (Clark v. American Residential Services LLC (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 785, 806.)  Trial courts should not sanction enhancement awards of thousands of dollars with “nothing more than pro forma claims as to ‘countless’ hours expended, ‘potential stigma’ and ‘potential risk.’ Significantly more specificity, in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of financial or other risks incurred by the named plaintiff, is required in order for the trial court to conclude that an enhancement was ‘necessary to induce [the named plaintiff] to participate in the suit . . . .’”  (Id. at 806-807, italics and ellipsis in original.) 

Plaintiff Flores represents that he has “actively participated, and will continue to actively participate, in the prosecution of this action.” (Declaration of Plaintiff Flores, ¶3.)

Plaintiff  Flores does not provide information with specificity in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of financial or other risks incurred by the named plaintiffs. Plaintiff Flores is to provide a declaration attesting to why he or she should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.

 

 

E.      Claims Administration Costs

The claims administrator, Simpluris, Inc. requests $10,000 for the costs of administering the settlement. (Castillejos Decl., ¶17.) This is equal to the $10,000 maximum amount estimated in the Settlement Agreement; (Settlement Agreement, ¶44.c); and disclosed in the notice to class members, to which there were no objections. (Castillejos Decl., ¶15 and Exhibit A thereto.)

Based on all the work performed by the Claims Administrator, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $10,000.
