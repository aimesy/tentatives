TENTATIVE RULING

 

COUNTY OF SANTA CRUZ HEALTH AGENCY,

 

                                  Petitioner,

 

         v.

 

 

CALIFORNIA DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH CARE SERVICES, et al.,

 

                                  Respondents.

 

 Case No:  25STCP03898

 

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  July 3, 2026

 Calendar Number:  9

 

Petitioner County of Santa Cruz Health Agency petitions the Court for a writ of mandate to set aside and reverse an audit adjustment by respondents Department of Health Care Services and its Director, Michele Baass, which reduced Petitioner’s Mental Health Plan program’s entitlement to federal funds.

 

The Court does not issue a tentative but will hear argument.

 

Tentatively, the Court understands that the correct analysis of the issue in this case to be as follows:

 

1.      The parties agree that “[t]here are three federal awards or cost objectives  [at issue] in this case:” (1) an “actual direct services award”; (2) a “mental health services quality [assurance award]; and an “administrative costs [award].” (Respondents’ Opp. at p. 6:4-6, citing 1 HT 43:11-20.)

2.      Before assigning or allocating costs by objective, a decision must initially be made as to whether a cost is “direct” or “indirect.” (10 AR 8501 [2 C.F.R. § 200.413]; 10 AR 8494 [2 C.F.R. § 200.1].)

3.      If the cost is direct, it must be assigned to the objective it specifically benefits. (See Petitioner’s Opening Br. at p. 10:23-24.)

4.      If the cost is indirect, it must be “equitably” allocated across all three categories. (See Petitioner’s Opening Br. at p. 10:5-6, citing 10 AR 008411.)

The Court asks the parties to address the following at the hearing:

 

A.    Do the parties agree with the analytic framework set forth above?

B.    Did the ALJ follow this framework?

C.    How should a “multi-benefit” cost be treated?

a.      May it be treated as direct and wholly assigned to a single primary objective?

b.      May it be treated as direct but still apportioned among the multiple objectives benefitted?

c.      May it be treated as indirect and equitably allocated across all objectives?

D.    If the Court grants the writ, what instructions should the Court provide to the ALJ on remand?


Case Number: 26STCP01256    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 833

 

SUPERIOR COURT OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES

 

DEPARTMENT 72

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

ANTONY GLEN KROO,

 

                                  Petitioner,

 

         v.

 

 

LOS ANGELES COUNTY DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY (LACDA),

 

                                  Respondent.

 

 Case No:  26STCP01256

 

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  July 1, 2026

 Calendar Number:  12

 

 

 

 

Respondent  Los Angeles County Development Authority moves to quash the service of summons and complaint filed on April 8, 2026 and set for hearing on July 1, 2026.

Respondent  Los Angeles County Development Authority’s motion to quash service of summons and complaint is GRANTED.

Background

 

            On March 30, 2026, Petitioner Antony Glen Kroo (“Petitioner”) filed a Petition for Writ of Mandate and Complaint for Declaratory Relief against Respondent Los Angeles County Development Authority (“LACDA” or “Respondent”) and EAH Housing, Hojas De Plata Owners (“Real Party-in-Interest”).

 

            On June 9, 2026, LACDA filed the current Motion for Quash Service of Summons. On June 11, 2026, Petitioner filed an Opposition. To date, no reply has been filed.

 

 

 

 

Legal Standard

 

“A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: [¶](1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her. [¶](2) To stay or dismiss the action on the ground of inconvenient forum. [¶](3) To dismiss the action pursuant to the applicable provisions of Chapter 1.5 (commencing with Section 583.110) of Title 8.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a).)

 

On a motion to quash service of the summons and complaint, the moving party must first present some admissible evidence, such as declarations or affidavits, to place the issue of minimum contacts before the Court. (School Dist. of Oskaloosa County v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 1126, 1131.) “When a motion to quash is properly brought, the burden of proof is placed upon the plaintiff to establish the facts of jurisdiction by a preponderance of the evidence.”  (Aquila, Inc. v. Sup. Ct. (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 556, 568; see also Elkman v. National States Ins. Co. (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 1305, 1312-13 [“Where a nonresident defendant challenges jurisdiction by way of a motion to quash, the plaintiff bears the burden of establishing by a preponderance of the evidence that minimum contacts exist between the defendant and the forum state to justify imposition of personal jurisdiction.”].) Evidence of the facts giving rise to personal jurisdiction or their absence may be in the form of declarations. (Arensen v. Raymond Lee Organization, Inc. (1973) 31 Cal.App.3d 991, 995.)

 

California’s long-arm statute permits a court to exercise personal jurisdiction on any basis consistent with state or federal constitutional principles. (Code Civ. Proc. [“CCP”] §410.10.)

 

Discussion

 

LACDA contends that the summons and complaint were not properly served through personal service, substituted service, and/or a properly executed Notice of Acknowledgment and Receipt under Code of Civil Procedure Section 415.30, or other authorized method of service. (Mot. at 4:14-17.) The Court agrees. 

“A summons may be served by mail as provided in this section. A copy of the summons and of the complaint shall be mailed (by first-class mail or airmail, postage prepaid) to the person to be served, together with two copies of the notice and acknowledgment provided for in subdivision (b) and a return envelope, postage prepaid, addressed to the sender.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.30, subd. (a).) Here, Plaintiff served LACDA via first-class mail on April 7, 2026, thereafter emailed courtesy copies. (Mot. at 4:13-14; POS, 4/8/26.) However, there is no indication that two copies of the Notice of Acknowledgment and Receipt form were mailed to LACDA. (Id.) Although Plaintiff asserts that LACDA had actual notice of the lawsuit and filed a purported Notice of Acknowledgment and Receipt, it is not in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure Section 415.30, subdivision (b). (Reyes Decl., ¶6.) In fact, Plaintiff’s “Notice of Acknowledgment and Receipt” merely includes email correspondence between Plaintiff and LACDA’s Risk Manager stating that the complaint was being referred to LACDA’s legal counsel. (Not. of Acknowledgment and Receipt, 5/18/26.) Furthermore, neither party contends that LACDA completed and return any Notice of Acknowledgment and Receipt form per Code of Civil Procedure Section 415.30, subdivision (c). (Dawson Decl., ¶3; Reyes Decl., ¶4; Opp. at 2:4-14.) As such, Plaintiff failed to effectuate service via mail on LACDA.

Next, Plaintiff has not filed any proof that LACDA was served via personal or substituted service in accordance with Code of Civil Procedure Sections 415.10 and 416.50, subdivision (a). (Dawson Decl., ¶2.) Additionally, Plaintiff cites to no legal authority to support the contention once a request for entry of default is filed, a defendant cannot move to quash the service of summons. Contrarily, when default has been entered against a defendant, the only remedy is to move to set aside default judgment per Code of Civil Procedure Sections 473, subdivision (b) or (d), and Code of Civil Procedure Section 437.5. To the extent that Plaintiff also contends that the present motion is untimely because he filed a request for entry of default first, the time to file a responsive pleading is triggered by proper service of the summons and complaint. No proper service of the summons and complaint was executed in this case. Thus, the motion to quash is timely.

Based on the foregoing, Respondent  Los Angeles County Development Authority’s motion to quash service of summons and complaint is GRANTED.


Case Number: 26STCV00590    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 833

Ally Bank,                                                                  Case No. 26STCV00590

v.                                                                     Hearing: July 1, 2026

                                                                        Location: Stanley Mosk Courthouse

                                                                                    Department: 833                                   John Eric Jones Jr., et al.                                             Judge: Joseph Lipner                         

                                     

 

[Tentative] Order Denying Applications for Writ of Possession

 

 

INTRODUCTION

 

Plaintiff Ally Bank (“Plaintiff”) moves for writs of possession against Defendants John Eric Jones Jr. (“Jones”) and Timothy Hunter Staton (“Staton”) (collectively, “Defendants”) as to the following property: 2024 Honda Civic, motor vehicle, Vehicle Identification Number 2HGFE1F7RH313017, with a value of $24,000.00 (the “Vehicle”). Defendants have not opposed the applications.

 

The court entered dismissal without prejudice as to Staton on May 7, 2026. Therefore, the application against Staton is moot.

 

The court denies the application against Jones because Plaintiff fails to provide proper notice to Jones, file proof of service of summons and complaint on Jones with the court, and show probable cause to believe the Vehicle is located at the locations specified in the application.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

“Upon the filing of the complaint or at any time thereafter, the plaintiff may apply pursuant to this chapter for a writ of possession by filing a written application for the writ with the court in which the action is brought.”  (Code Civ. Pro. § 512.010(a).)

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 512.010(b), the application must be submitted under oath and include:

 

(1) A showing of the basis of the plaintiff's claim and that the plaintiff is entitled to possession of the property claimed. If the basis of the plaintiff's claim is a written instrument, a copy of the instrument shall be attached.

 

(2) A showing that the property is wrongfully detained by the defendant, of the manner in which the defendant came into possession of the property, and, according to the best knowledge, information, and belief of the plaintiff, of the reason for the detention.

 

(3) A particular description of the property and a statement of its value.

 

(4) A statement, according to the best knowledge, information, and belief of the plaintiff, of the location of the property and, if the property, or some part of it, is within a private place which may have to be entered to take possession, a showing that there is probable cause to believe that such property is located there.

 

(5) A statement that the property has not been taken for a tax, assessment, or fine, pursuant to a statute; or seized under an execution against the property of the plaintiff; or, if so seized, that it is by statute exempt from such seizure.

 

Before the hearing on the Writ of Possession, the Defendant must be served with (1) a copy of the summons and complaint; (2) a Notice of Application and Hearing; and (3) a copy of the application and any affidavit in support thereof.  (Code Civ. Pro. § 512.030.)

 

“The writ will be issued if the court finds that the plaintiff's claim is probably valid and the other requirements for issuing the writ are established.”  (Code Civ. Pro. § 512.040(b).)  “A claim has ‘probable validity’ where it is more likely than not that the plaintiff will obtain a judgment against the defendant on that claim.”  (Code Civ. Pro. § 511.090.) 

 

DISCUSSION

           

A.                Notice

 

Plaintiff did not provide proof of service of the application for writ of possession on Jones.  Additionally, the court notes that it rejected entry of default against Jones for failure to file proof of service of the summons and complaint.  Plaintiff has not filed proof of service since the court’s rejection of Plaintiff’s request for entry of default.  Therefore, Plaintiff’s application is not in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 512.030.

 

B.                 Probable Validity of Plaintiff’s Claim

 

Plaintiff’s application for writ of possession is based on the causes of action for claim and delivery and money due on contract.

 

Plaintiff is a bank which entered into a contract with Defendants, created by Findlay Honda Henderson dealership (the “Dealership”), providing financing for the purchase of the Vehicle.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 6; Exh. A.)  The Dealership assigned all its interest in the contract to Plaintiff, and all payments were to be made to Plaintiff.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 6; Exh. A.)  Plaintiff became the owner and “holder” of the contract and acquired its interest in the collateral, the Vehicle. (Nunez Decl. ¶ 7; Exh. B.)  Under the contract, if Defendants defaulted on any provision, Plaintiff held the right to immediately possess the Vehicle.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 8.)

 

Defendants defaulted on the contract by failing to make their regular monthly payment on December 11, 2024.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 8.)  Before Plaintiff brought this action, Plaintiff noticed Defendants of their default, and Defendants failed, refused, or neglected to return the Vehicle to Plaintiff.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 9; Exh. C.)  Defendants owe Plaintiff a total of $35,125.75.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 10.)  The average wholesale and retail values of the Vehicle as of November 2025 were $24,000.00 and $28,400.00, respectively.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 11; Exh. E.)  Defendants have possession of the Vehicle and are concealing possession from Plaintiff.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 12.)

 

C.                 Wrongful Detention

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 512.010(b)(2), the application must include “a showing that the property is wrongfully detained by the defendant, of the manner in which the defendant came into possession of the property, and, according to the best knowledge, information, and belief of the plaintiff, of the reason for the detention.”  Plaintiff makes this showing. 

 

D.                Description and Value of Property

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 512.010(b)(3), the application must include a particular description of the property and a statement of its value.  Plaintiff provided the particular description of the property, by make, and serial number.  Plaintiff also provided a statement as to value and attached as Exhibit E a copy of the J.D. Power Report for November 2025, which is an industry standard index listing approximate values of motor vehicles and is a reliable guide as to value.  Plaintiff therefore satisfies section 512.010(b)(3).

 

E.                 Statutory Statement

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 512.010(b)(5), Plaintiff must provide a statement that the property has not been taken for a tax, assessment, or fine, pursuant to statute and has not been seized under an execution against the Plaintiff’s property. Plaintiff complied with this requirement.

 

F.                  Location of the Property  

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 512.010(b)(4), Plaintiff must identify the probable location of the Vehicle and establish probable cause to that effect.  Plaintiff’s application for writ of possession states that the Vehicle is located “[i]n a garage, or other parking area, windowless, locked or otherwise, at: 312 W. 5th St., #1009, Los Angeles, CA 90013,” or “617 Garden St., #406, Santa Barbara, CA 93101.”  (Appl. ¶ 6.)  Because Plaintiff seeks an order authorizing the levying officer to enter private property, Plaintiff must establish “probable cause” to believe that the Vehicle is located at the property specified in the application.  (See Code Civ. Pro. §§ 512.010(b)(4), 512.080.)  Plaintiff failed to meet this requirement.

 

Plaintiff’s representative declares that “[t]he file of Plaintiff reflects that defendant John Jones resides at 312 W. 5th St., #1009, Los Angeles, CA 90013.  Timothy Staton resides at 617 Garden St., #406, Santa Barbara, CA 93101.  In reliance upon the information contained in the file and computer system of Plaintiff pertaining to the subject motor vehicle, to the best of my knowledge, information and belief, the current location of the subject motor vehicle is as follows: 312 W. 5th St., #1009, Los Angeles, CA 90013 or 617 Garden St., #406, Santa Barbara, CA 93101.”  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 14.)  However, Plaintiff does not show probable cause to support finding that the Vehicle is located in Los Angeles or Santa Barbara.  Significantly, Plaintiff was not served with proof of service, which could confirm Jones’ address for the Court.  Plaintiff does not submit any declarations from process servers or other agents attesting that the Vehicle has been spotted at the specified locations in Los Angeles or Santa Barbara.  Plaintiff does not provide a further explanation past reliance on Plaintiff’s computer system and file, which the court cannot readily verify.  Therefore, on this record, Plaintiff has not established probable cause to believe that the Vehicle is located at the private property specified in the application.

 

G.                Undertaking

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 515.010 requires an undertaking to be filed before the writ issues in the amount of “not less than twice the value of the defendant’s interest in the property.”  Section 515.010(a) states that the value of the defendant’s interest “is determined by the market value of the property less the amount due and owing on any conditional sales contract or security agreement and all liens and encumbrances on the property, and any other factors necessary to determine the defendant’s interest in the property.”

 

Plaintiff submits evidence that the fair market value of the Vehicle is between $24,000.00 and $28,400.00.  (Nunez Decl. ¶ 11.)  Because the Court denies the application on other grounds, the Court does not decide whether Plaintiff must post an undertaking.

 

H.                Turnover Order

 

Plaintiff requests a turnover order.  (See Memorandum 2.)  Section 512.070 states: “If a writ of possession is issued, the court may also issue an order directing the defendant to transfer possession of the property to the plaintiff. Such order shall contain a notice to the defendant that failure to turn over possession of such property to plaintiff may subject the defendant to being held in contempt of court.”  (emphasis added.) “Thus a ‘turnover’ order, issued pursuant to section 512.070, is not a separate remedy but rather an alternative means of enforcing a writ of possession.”  (Edwards v. Sup.Ct. (1991) 230 Cal.App.3d 173, 178.)

 

Because not all requirements for issuance of the writ of possession are met, the court denies the request for a turnover order.

 

I.                    Oral Testimony

 

Plaintiff “anticipates the need for extremely brief, but very important, examination of the defendant” regarding the Vehicle’s location.  (See Memorandum 3.)  However, Jones has not responded to the complaint, and Plaintiff does not demonstrate that it will be able to obtain Jones’ testimony at the hearing.  Furthermore, “[a] party seeking permission to introduce oral evidence, except for oral evidence in rebuttal to oral evidence presented by the other party, must file, no later than three court days before the hearing, a written statement stating the nature and extent of the evidence proposed to be introduced and a reasonable time estimate for the hearing.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1306(b).)  Plaintiff has not complied with this rule.  Therefore, the court denies the request for oral testimony.

 

CONCLUSION

           

Based on the foregoing, the court orders as follows:

 

1.      The court denies the applications for a writ of possession.

 

2.      The court denies Plaintiff’s request for a turnover order.

 

3.      The court denies Plaintiff’s request for oral testimony.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
