Case Number: 24STLC02435    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Hu Trans, LLC’s Motion for Reconsideration is denied.

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Hash-Ulzii Erdenekhuu (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for breach of contract against Defendant Hu Trans, LLC (“Defendant”) on April 3, 2024. On May 16, 2024, Defendant filed a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff and EZH, Inc. (Cross-Defendants”).

 

On July 25, 2024, the Court sustained Plaintiff’s Demurrer to the second cause of action in the Cross-Complaint and deemed its Motion to Strike Portions of the Cross-Complaint moot. (Minute Order, 07/25/24.) Defendant filed the First Amended Cross-Complaint on August 14, 2024. On October 15, 2024, the Court sustained Plaintiff’s Demurrer to the second cause of action in the First Amended Complaint without leave to amend. (Minute Order, 10/15/24.)

 

On April 17, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Defendant/Cross-Complainant’s Attendance at Deposition, Request for Monetary Sanctions. (Minute Order, 04/17/25.) Plaintiff filed a Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication on May 23, 2025. On August 7, 2025, Plaintiff’s also filed a Motion for Leave to Amend Answer to the First Amended Cross-Complaint.

 

The Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication, came for hearing on August 20, 2025. The basis of the motion was Plaintiff’s third affirmative defense, asserting that the breach of contract cross-claim was barred by the statute of limitations. The Court granted Cross-Defendant’s Motion for Leave to Amend Answer on September 23, 2025. (Minute Order, 09/23/25.)

 

On November 18, 2025, the Court granted Defendant’s ex parte application to continue the trial date and set it for January 20, 2026. (Minute Order, 11/18/25; Clerk’s Certificate of Service, 11/18/25.) On January 20, 2026, the date was again continued to February 19, 2026, with both parties appearing through counsel and waiving notice of the order. (Minute Order, 01/20/26.) On February 19, 2026, both parties appeared and upon the completion of the trial the following day, the Court ordered judgment for Plaintiff in the amount of $24,000.00 principal and interest of $5,608.76. (Minute Order, 02/20/26.)

 

Defendant, in propria persona, filed a Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment on March 19, 2026. Judgment in favor of Plaintiff was entered on March 26, 2026. The Court denied the Motion to Set Aside on April 14, 2026, and granted Attorney William Hanley’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant on April 29, 2026. On April 24, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion for Reconsideration of Order Denying Motion to Vacate Judgment through Attorney Michael S. Lee. Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 16, 2026 and Defendant replied on July 22, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves for reconsideration of the Court’s order denying its Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment on March 19, 2026. The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008. When a party seeks the same relief that was previously denied, it must bring a renewed motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (b). (California Correctional Peace Officers Ass’n v. Virga (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 30, 43, fn. 11; see also Tate v. Wilburn (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 150, 156-157.) When a motion has been denied in whole or in part, the moving party may apply again for the same relief at a later time only upon “new or different facts, circumstances or law.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (b); see Graham v. Hansen (1982) 128 Cal.App.3d 965, 969-970.) This is described as a jurisdictional requirement for the Court to grant reconsideration. Also, the statute requires the moving party “to show a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence earlier, which can only be described as a strict requirement of diligence,” the purpose of which is to incentivize parties “to efficiently marshal their evidence.” (Baldwin v. Home Sav. of America (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 1192, 1199 [citing Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 689-690].)

 

Jurisdiction to Reconsider Post-Judgment Motions

 

Plaintiff’s opposition first challenges the Court’s jurisdiction to reconsider the order denying the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment on the grounds that judgment has already been entered. Plaintiff cites APRI Ins. Co. v. Superior Ct. (1999) 76 Cal.App.4th 176 for the proposition that “[o]nce judgment has been entered, however, the court may not reconsider it and loses its unrestricted power to change the judgment.” (APRI Ins. Co. v. Superior Ct. (1999) 76 Cal.App.4th 176 182.) However, this does not directly address the Court’s jurisdiction to reconsider a post-judgment motion to vacate judgment. Courts can reconsider post-judgment orders regarding authorized post-judgment motions, like a motion to vacate default judgment. (D.R.S. Trading Co., Inc. v. Barnes (2009) 180 Cal.App.4th 815, 817, 820 (“We are unconvinced by the contention the existence of a judgment somehow precludes the court from reconsideration of a motion it is explicitly empowered to consider after judgment.”].) The Court has the power to reconsider its ruling denying Defendant’s Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment.

 

New or Different Facts, Circumstances or Law

 

Defendant, proceeding pro se, brought its Motion to Set/Aside Vacate Judgment under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) on the basis of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Specifically, the Motion argued that Defendant did not receive proper notice of the trial, and had it received proper notice, would have appeared and defended the case. (Motion to Set Aside, filed 03/19/26, Boldbaatar Decl., ¶¶2-3.)  The Motion did not address or raise any issues with respect to communication with Defendant’s attorney.  The Court denied the Motion on the grounds that Defendant cannot proceed without counsel.  (Minute Order, p. 2, 04/14/2026.)  The Court also observed that the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate was without merit because the record established notice to Defendant through counsel.  (Id., p. 3.)

 

Defendant now contends that the lack of notice resulted from the failure of its former attorney, William B. Hanley, to inform it of the trial date. (Motion, Boldbaatar Decl., ¶¶7-10.) The Boldbaatar Declaration in support of the Motion attests that there was a breakdown in communication between Defendant and Attorney Hanley at the end of 2025, which is evidenced by Attorney Hanley serving Defendant with a Motion to be Relieved as Counsel on or around February 20, 2026, the same day as trial. (Ibid.) The Declaration states that on an unspecified date, Declarant Boldbaatar reviewed the docket and learned that trial had been held in his absence, at which time he attempted to contact Mr. Hanley without success.  (Ibid.)  Until these events, Declarant Boldbaatar believed Mr. Hanley was adequately litigating the case, despite the breakdown in communication.  (Id. at ¶13.)  Declarant Boldbaatar drafted and filed the initial Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment without an attorney in desperation.  (Id. at ¶11.)  Defendant retained attorney Michael S. Lee on or around March 26, 2026.  (Id. at ¶15.)  The Motion to Set Aside/Vacate was denied April 14, 2026.  The Declaration of Defendant’s new counsel, Michael S. Lee, attests that Mr. Lee endeavored to have Mr. Hanley sign a Substitution of Counsel after Mr. Lee was retained and before the April 14, 2026 hearing date, but Mr. Hanley was non-responsive.  (Motion, Lee Decl., ¶¶2-4, Exh. 1.)

 

These details, describing a breakdown in the relationship and the lack of communication between Defendant and its former counsel in the time leading up to trial, were known to Defendant, through its declarant at the time the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate was filed, and therefore are not new or different.  (See In re Marriage of Herr, (2009) 174 Cal. App. 4th 1463, 1468 (“facts of which the party seeking reconsideration was aware at the time of the original ruling are not ‘new or different.’”) (citation omitted).)

Requirement of Diligence with respect to New Facts

 

Even if the information can be considered “new” because it was not previously presented to the Court, Defendant does not sufficiently explain why the information was not previously presented. Declarant Boldbaatar attests that following the trial and entry of judgment, Defendant was unable to contact its attorney and therefore filed the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment on his own in desperation. (Motion, Boldbaatar Decl., ¶¶8-11 and Exh. 3, pp. 6-7.)  Defendant then promptly retained Mr. Lee as counsel, who attempted to reach Mr. Hanley to substitute in as counsel prior to a decision on the Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel but received no response.  (Motion, Lee Decl., ¶¶2-4, Exh. 1.) 

 

The Court does not find that these circumstances show reasonable diligence on Defendant’s part.  All of the facts now presented about his attorney’s alleged failure to keep him in the loop were necessarily within Defendant’s knowledge at the time the Motion was filed but they were not presented to the Court in the Motion to Set Aside/ Vacate.  Defendant’s decision to file the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate without the assistance of counsel is no excuse.  Moreover, Defendant retained Mr. Lee on March 26, 2026, while the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate was still pending, and yet did not withdraw pro se Motion before it was decided nor seek to have the hearing on the Motion continued until after Attorney Hanley was relieved.  Under these circumstances, the Court finds that Defendant has not shown that it “could not, with reasonable diligence,” have earlier brought the newly raised facts to the Court’s attention. (Baldwin, 59 Cal.App.4th at 1998.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Motion for Reconsideration of the Court’s order dated March 19, 2026 is denied.

 

Reconsideration of the March 19, 2026 Order Denying Defendant’s Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment

 

Even if reconsideration were granted, the Court would again deny the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment.

 

The Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment was brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) on the basis of Defendant’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. The Motion is supported by Declarant Boldbaatar’s declaration, which states that the declarant and Attorney Hanley began experiencing a breakdown in communication at the end of 2025.  (Boldbataar Decl., ¶6.)  The declarant only learned that trial had occurred upon checking the docket after receiving his attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel.  (Boldbataar Decl., ¶¶7-8.)  Thus, the communication breakdown resulted in Defendant’s surprise regarding the actual date of trial, which passed without Defendant’s appearance. 

 

These facts do not warrant relief from the judgment.  As Plaintiff argues, Attorney Hanley’s alleged neglect does not provide a basis for relief.  (Buckert v. Briggs (Ct. App. 1971) 15 Cal. App. 3d 296, 301 (discretionary relief under § 473(b) “may not be predicated upon the neglect of the party’s attorney unless shown to be excusable because the negligence of the attorney in the premises is imputed to his client and may not be offered by the latter as a basis for relief.”) (internal citations omitted).) There is a recognized exception “where the attorney’s neglect is of that extreme degree amounting to positive misconduct, and the person seeking relief is relatively free from negligence.”  (Id.)  But that exception plainly does not apply here.  The record is quite clear that Attorney Hanley persisted in actively litigating the case, including – after requests for continuance were overruled – appearing at trial and trying the case in his client’s absence.  (See Opp., Hardacre Decl., ¶¶15-16.)  This conduct is not abandonment.  (See Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal. App. 4th at 683 (“For the exception to apply, the attorney’s misconduct must be sufficiently gross to effectively abrogate the attorney-client relationship.”)  The facts here distinguish this case from Buckert, supra, where the trial judge was found to have properly vacated judgment against the plaintiffs after their attorney not only failed to inform them of the trial date after promising to do so, but also failed himself to appear at trial. (Buckert, 15 Cal. App. 3d at 299; see also Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal. App. 4th 674, 682–83) (describing Buckert); Carroll v. Abbott Lab’ys, Inc., (1992) 32 Cal. 3d 892, 900 (collecting cases and stating: “What [each of these cases] have in common is a total failure on the part of counsel to represent the client: each attorney had de facto substituted himself out of the case.” (emphasis added).)   

 

Separately, the Court declines to draw any inference that Attorney Hanley in fact failed to attempt to alert Defendant regarding the trial date.  It defies credulity that, although Attorney Hanley persisted in actively litigating the case, he for some reason would not attempt to notify Defendant of the trial date, nor contact Declarant Boldbaatar to appear as a witness, as Defendant claims.  

 

Finally, however, even crediting that there was a break in communication that could be attributed to Attorney Hanley, the exception is still inapplicable because Defendant is not “relatively free from negligence.” (Buckert, 15 Cal. App. 3d at 301.) Defendant’s own surprise regarding the trial date and loss of communication without counsel is not excusable.  Nothing in the record indicates that Defendant had received any assurances that trial would not take place or was yet to be scheduled.  (Cf., Buckert, 15 Cal. App. 3d at 299.)  A reasonably prudent party to litigation under these circumstances would attend to trial dates and, even if represented, would not let the trial date come and go without checking in with counsel.  

 

For these reasons, were reconsideration granted, the Court would again deny the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate.

 

Conclusion

 

Accordingly, Defendant Hu Trans, LLC’s Motion for Reconsideration is denied.

Case Number: 24STLC04194    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Ajitananda Barua’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal is denied.

 

On its own motion, the Court vacates the dismissal entered on 12/23/2025.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Entry of Default and Default Judgment against Defendants Mirna Lorena Lopez Barrietos and Mario Pacheco is scheduled for 08/26/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service of Summons and Complaint as to Defendant Rolando Rafael Fuentes Orizabal is scheduled for 08/26/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Ajitananda Barua (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for motor vehicle negligence against Defendants Mirna Lorena Lopez Barrietos, Mario Pacheco, and Rolando Rafael Fuentes Orizabal (“Defendants”) on June 11, 2024. Upon the action being filed, trial was set for December 9, 2025. (Third Amended Standing Order, 06/11/24.) Proofs of Substitute Service with respect to Defendants Barrietos and Pacheco were filed on May 15 and 22, 2025. On November 26, 2025, the trial date was continued from December 9, 2025 to December 23, 2025 on account of the Court being unavailable on the earlier date. (Notice Re: Continuance of Hearing and Order, 11/26/25.)

 

Following Plaintiff’s failure to appear for trial on December 23, 2025, the Court dismissed the action without prejudice. (Minute Order, 12/23/25.) Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Vacate Dismissal on June 15, 2026. No Defendant has appeared in this action, nor has any opposition to the instant Motion been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b) on the basis of surprise, excusable neglect, and inadvertence. (Notice, p. 2:24-27.) Under this statute, an application for relief must be made within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the party or attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

 

The Motion is supported by a declaration from Plaintiff’s counsel, who declares that he was unaware of the trial date because of “bugs and glitches” in the office computer system that prevented it from printing out emails from the Court on a reliable basis.  (Motion, Canter Decl., ¶¶6-8.)  As a result, Plaintiff’s counsel contends that he had no way of knowing about a court appearance for December 23, 2026. (Ibid.) And further – far from admitting error or inadvertence – that Defendant and counsel “have been denied due process due to the computer glitches.”  (Ibid.) This explanation does not admit any mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or error, and is insufficient for that reason.  (Code. Civ. Proc., §473(b); Credit Managers Assn. v. Nat'l Indep. Bus. All. (Ct. App. 1984) 162 Cal. App. 3d 1166, 1173 (“ ‘Surprise’ referred to [473(b)] is ‘some condition or situation in which a party to cause is unexpectedly placed to his injury, without any default or negligence of his own, which ordinary prudence could not have guarded against.’ ”). 

 

Further, the explanation does not credibly address Plaintiff’s counsel’s failure to appear on December 23, 2025.  Trial in this matter was set for December 9, 2025 when the case was filed. This was long before the purported computer glitches.  The December 9, 2025 trial date was stated in the Third Amended Standing Order provided to Plaintiff’s counsel at the time the case was filed. Plaintiff certainly received it, and in fact served it on Defendants with the other case-opening documents.  (See Proofs of Service, 5/15/25, 5/22/25.)  Plaintiff’s counsel does not explain why he did not attempt to appear for the December 9, 2025 trial date, of which he indisputably had notice.  Had he attempted to appear on December 9, 2025 – or even consulted the docket in preparation for that date – he would have been alerted that the trial date had been continued to December 23, 2025.  For these reasons, the Declaration does not credibly establish that whatever mistake or surprise counsel admits here, if any is admitted, was the cause of the dismissal.  (See State Farm Fire & Cas. Co. v. Pietak (2001)90 Cal. App. 4th 600, 609 (affirming denial of 473(b) motion, where “neither [attorney] declaration contains any sworn admission of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or error that resulted in a dismissal of claims”).)

 

Nor does the Motion demonstrate that it was timely filed within a reasonable time, as required by the moving statute. Where the statute requires that “ ‘ “the application must be made within a ‘reasonable time’ … what is a reasonable time in any case depends upon the circumstances of that particular case.” While in “the determination of that question, a large discretion is necessarily confided to [the trial] court” ... there must be some showing—some evidence—as the basis for the exercise of such discretion.’ ” (Caldwell v. Methodist Hospital (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1521, 1524 [citing Carrasco v. Craft (1985) 164 Cal.App.3d 796, 805].) For unknown reasons, Plaintiff’s counsel’s paralegal did not look at the Register of Actions for this action until June 8, 2026, almost six months after the originally scheduled trial date. (Motion, Orellana Decl., ¶3.) As Plaintiff does not address the requirement that the Motion be filed within a reasonable time, the Court has no basis to find that it was so filed.

 

However, although the Court finds no basis for discretionary relief pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), it notes that dismissal pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 581, subdivision (b)(3) requires 30 days’ notice of the trial date. Here, the continuance was ordered on November 26, 2025, which did not give Plaintiff the requisite 30 days’ notice required for dismissal under section 581, subdivision (b)(3). Therefore, the Court vacates the dismissal on its own motion and sets orders to show cause regarding requests for entry of default against Defendants Barrietos and Pacheco and filing proof of service of the Summons and Complaint as to Defendant Orizabal.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Ajitananda Barua’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal is denied.

 

On its own motion, the Court vacates the dismissal entered on 12/23/2025.

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service of Summons and Complaint as to Defendant Rolando Rafael Fuentes Orizabal is scheduled for 08/26/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 24STLC08974    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Motion of Plaintiffs Nancy Angeles and Beverli I. Rodriguez, a minor by and through her Guardian Ad Litem, Nancy Angeles, to Vacate Dismissal is granted. The dismissal entered on 06/05/2026 is hereby vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service is scheduled for 08/26/2026 at 09:30 AM in Department 26 at Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiffs Nancy Angeles and Beverli I. Rodriguez, a minor by and through her Guardian Ad Litem, Nancy Angeles (“Plaintiffs”), filed the instant action for motor vehicle negligences against Defendants Lien Hong Doan, Care Dimensions LLC, and Care Dimensions Healthcare, Inc. (“Defendants”) on December 6, 2024. Following Plaintiffs’ failure to appear for trial on June 5, 2026, the Court dismissed the action without prejudice. (Minute Order, 06/05/26.)

 

Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion to Vacate Dismissal on June 9, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b). Under this statute, an application for relief must be made no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the party or attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) A timely request for relief from an entry of default, default judgment, or involuntary dismissal, based on attorney fault, must be granted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Plaintiffs timely filed the Motion four days after dismissal of the action and included an attorney affidavit of fault explaining that their counsel failed to appear due to a calendaring error. (Motion, Fu Decl., ¶¶5-6.) Based on the timely request for relief and supporting attorney affidavit of fault, the case must be reinstated pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).

 

Conclusion

 

Motion of Plaintiffs Nancy Angeles and Beverli I. Rodriguez, a minor by and through her Guardian Ad Litem, Nancy Angeles, to Vacate Dismissal is granted. The dismissal entered on 06/05/2026 is hereby vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service is scheduled for 08/26/2026 at 09:30 AM in Department 26 at Spring Street Courthouse.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s (1) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admission, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions; (2) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (3) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are stayed until disposition of Defendant Balaciano Group’s Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security Pursuant to CCP § 391.1.

 

Status Conference Re: Stay remains scheduled for 10/13/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On February 11, 2026, Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Balaciano Group (“Defendant”). The Complaint alleges causes of action for (1) Pre-Employment Retaliation in Violation of FEHA (Government Code § 12940(h)); (2) Pre-Employment Retaliation in Violation of California Labor Code Section 98.6 (Labor Code § 98.6 with SB 497 Rebuttable Presumption); and (3) Ultra Vires Acts Exceeding Corporate Authority.

 

On April 6, 2026, Defendant filed its Answer to the Complaint; Plaintiff filed a Demurrer to Answer on April 13, 2026. Concurrent with the Demurrer, Plaintiff filed a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings scheduled for July 7, 2026. The Demurrer to Answer originally came for hearing on May 13, 2026, at which time the Court continued the matter to July 7, 2026. (Minute Order, 05/13/26.) The Court addressed Defendant’s opposition request that Plaintiff be ordered to post security and found it premature. (Ibid.) If Defendant filed a motion for Plaintiff to furnish security, the Court ruled, it would hear and determine that motion before reaching the merits of the Demurrer to Answer and the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, or any other pending motions. (Ibid.)

 

On June 23, 2026, Defendant filed a Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security Pursuant to CCP § 391.1, which is set for hearing on September 29, 2026. As the Court previously noted, Code of Civil Procedure section 391.6 expressly provides that “when a motion pursuant to Section 391.1 is filed prior to trial the litigation is stayed … until 10 days after the motion shall have been denied, or if granted, until 10 days after the required security has been furnished and the moving defendant given written notice thereof.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.6.) At the hearing on July 7, 2026, the Court stayed the litigation as a whole until disposition of the Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security.

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s (1) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admission, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions; (2) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (3) Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are stayed until disposition of Defendant Balaciano Group’s Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security Pursuant to CCP § 391.1.

 

Status Conference Re: Stay remains scheduled for 10/13/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 26STLC01756    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Farzin Fahimi’s Motion to Compel Arbitration and Dismiss Action with Prejudice is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On March 16, 2026, Plaintiff Kinecta Federal Credit Union (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Farzin Fahimi (“Defendant”) for breach of contract, money lent, and account stated. On April 27, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion to Compel Arbitration and Dismiss Action with Prejudice. Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 6, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to the Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”), which provides in relevant part:

 

A written provision in any maritime transaction or a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce to settle by arbitration a controversy thereafter arising out of such contract or transaction, or the refusal to perform the whole or any part thereof, or an agreement in writing to submit to arbitration an existing controversy arising out of such a contract, transaction, or refusal, shall be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract or as otherwise provided in chapter 4.

 

(9 USC, § 2.) It is alternatively made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2 and 1281.7.

 

On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party thereto refuses to arbitrate such controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists, unless it determines that: (a) The right to compel arbitration has been waived by the petitioner; or (b) Grounds exist for the revocation of the agreement.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1281.2, subds. (a)-(b).)

 

Existence of an Arbitration Agreement

 

Defendant contends that they are alleged in the Complaint to be liable on a consumer credit account that is governed by a Cardmember Agreement. (Motion, p. 9:2-28.)[1] The Motion goes on to argue that paragraph 43 of the Cardmember Agreement is an arbitration provision. (Ibid.) In support, the Motion attempts to rely on “the accompanying Declaration of Farzin Fahimi,” however, no such declaration is attached or concurrently filed. (See id. at p. 9:5-8.) The Motion, therefore, provides no evidence of the Cardmember Agreement or the purported arbitration provision therein.

 

Furthermore, Plaintiff disputes that the Cardmember Agreement governs this dispute. It points to the Loan and Security Agreement entered into by the parties as the controlling document. (Opp., Marquez Decl., ¶3 and Exh. A.) The Loan and Security Agreement mentioned in the opposition is the same one upon which the Complaint is based. (Compl., ¶6.) A review of the Loan and Security Agreement attached to the opposition, which reflects the terms alleged in the Complaint, reveals no arbitration provision that binds the parties. Without the existence of an arbitration agreement binding the parties, the Court finds no basis to compel Plaintiff to arbitration or to dismiss the action.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Farzin Fahimi’s Motion to Compel Arbitration and Dismiss Action with Prejudice is denied.
