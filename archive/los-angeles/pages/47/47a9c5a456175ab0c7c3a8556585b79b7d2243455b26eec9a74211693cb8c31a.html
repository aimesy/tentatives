ANALYSIS:

 

            Kimiko Mukai owns or leases the property occupied by Juan’s Rotisserie Chicken restaurant, where Abner Reyes encountered various obstacles that he alleges violated the Americans with Disabilities Act.  Non-compliance with the ADA can serve as a predicate for a claim under California’s Unruh Civil Rights Act.  Reyes sued Mukai, and later amended his complaint to name Andres Jaramillo.  Reyes propounded five special interrogatories on Jaramillo.  Jaramillo failed to respond timely, so Reyes now moves to compel responses. 

 

A party must respond to interrogatories within 30 days after their service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).)  If the party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the interrogatories. (Id. § 2030.290, subds. (a), (b).)

 

Reyes served Jaramillo with the special interrogatories in April 2026.  To date, no responses have been served.  Jaramillo has not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Reyes’s motion to compel responses to the special interrogatories.  Jaramillo is ordered to serve verified responses to the special interrogatories without objections within 30 days of this order.

 

Jaramillo must also pay sanctions for forcing Reyes to move to compel responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)  Reyes seeks $500.00—for one hour of his counsel’s work—from Jaramillo and his counsel, Douglas R. MacLeith.  The Court finds this request reasonable.  Jaramillo and MacLeith, jointly and severally, must pay Reyes $500.00 within 30 days of this order.

 

Reyes is to give notice.
