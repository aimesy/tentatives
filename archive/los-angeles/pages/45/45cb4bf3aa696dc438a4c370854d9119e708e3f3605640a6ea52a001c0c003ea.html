Case Number: 21STCV19052    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Case Name:               S&I Construction, Inc. v. Noah Tal

 

Case Number:           21STCV19052

 

Proceeding:               OSC re Default Judgment on Tal Cross-Complaint

 

Date of Hearing:       September 23, 2026

 

Party Against Whom Default Judgment is Sought:          Cross-Defendant David Hazan

 

Summary of Ruling:  Default Judgment is denied; Order to Show Cause is continued to __________, 2026, at 8:30AM to give Cross-complainant Noah Tal time to correct the deficiencies in the default judgment request. 

 

 

The request for Default Judgment by defendant and cross-complainant Noah Tal against cross-defendant David Hazan is denied.  Tal provides several exhibits to support his claim of damages.  The evidence regarding the costs to correct the deficiencies in Hazan’s work, including $272,075.90 for costs of repair of multiple defects, $29,500.00 for damage to the roof of the front property, and loss of rental income, in the amount of $400,000.00for the back property and $25,000.00 for the front property, in the amount of $25,000.00, is sufficient. 

 

But Tal’s claim of loss of financial income is not properly supported.  He submitted federal income tax summaries, but it is unclear whether the loss of income is attributable to Hazan.  In addition, Tal’s request to recover the amounts paid to Hazan for the work performed is not supportable.  Other than the costs he had to incur to correct deficiencies and damage caused by Hazan, he has not offered evidence that the work for which he paid was not performed.  Tal has also failed to submit exhibits supporting claimed costs, and the costs claimed on the CIV-100 form ($1,591.34) do not match the costs claimed in Tal’s declaration in support.  Finally, the amount of interest on the proposed judgment submitted 8/11/26 does not match that on the CIV-100 filed 6/29/26.  As previously pointed out by the Court, Tal has failed to show a statutory or contractual basis for recovery of attorneys’ fees.  Despite having an opportunity to provide a basis, he has not done so; therefore, the Court will not award attorneys’ fees. 

 

The Court will continue the Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment to ____________, 2026, so that Tal may correct the deficiencies as to all of the foregoing except for attorneys’ fees and amounts paid to Hazan, which shall not be awarded in any default judgment that is entered. 

 

 


Case Number: 25STCV15589    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 September 23, 2026

           

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV15589

CASE NAME:                        Kimberly Cruz v. FCA US, LLC, et al.

                                                                                                                                                           

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Kimberly Cruz

                                               

OPPOSING PARTY:             Defendant FCA US, LLC

PROCEEDING:                      MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL DISCLOSURES; AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff’s motion to compel and for sanctions is denied. 

                                                                                                                                                           

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant FCA US, LLC

                                               

OPPOSING PARTY:             Plaintiff Kimberly Cruz

PROCEEDING:                      MOTION FOR SANCTIONS

OPPOSITION:                        September 10, 2026

REPLY:                                  September 16, 2026

 

RULING SUMMARY:          FCA’s motion for sanctions is granted.  The Court orders Plaintiff’s counsel, Strategic Legal Practices, APC, to pay monetary sanctions of $1,500.00 to FCA’s counsel within 30 days of this Ruling.

                                                                                                                                                           

 

 

Background

 

On May 29, 2025, Kimberly Cruz (“Plaintiff”) filed this Lemon Law action against Defendants FCA US LLC (“FCA”); Bravo Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram of Alhambra (“Bravo”) (together, “Defendants”); and Does 1 to 10.  

The Complaint alleges the following causes of action for (1) Violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(d); (2) Violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(b); (3) Violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(a)(3); (4) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability (Civ. Code, §§ 1791.1, 1794, 1795.5); (5) Negligent Repair, and (6) Fraudulent Inducement.    

On June 15,  2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel initial disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and request for monetary sanctions. 

On July 9, 2026, FCA filed a motion for an order for sanctions based on Plaintiff’s failure to fully comply with mandatory disclosures set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (b).  

Oppositions were filed to both motions

LEGAL STANDARDS 

“Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (b).)

 

Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: “A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff’s attorney…paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j).)

 

FCA’s MOTION FOR SANCTIONS

 

FCA argues Plaintiff was required to provide all mandatory disclosures and documents within 60 days after the filing of an answer or other responsive pleading and failed to do so. (Mot., p. 2.)  Defendants filed a responsive pleading on August 11, 2025, triggering the mandatory disclosure deadline and making mandatory disclosures due on October 10, 2025. (Coughlin Decl., ¶¶ 2, 3.)  Plaintiff served initial disclosures on January 30, 2026. (Coughlin Decl., ¶ 4, Ex. A.)  FCA argues Plaintiff failed to provide documentation and to fully comply with the mandatory disclosures. (Mot., p. 3.)

 

Plaintiff failed to provide initial disclosures within the statutory timeframe.  Because Defendants filed a responsive pleading on August 11, 2025, Plaintiff’s initial disclosures were due by October 10, 2025.  Plaintiff did not provide initial disclosures until January 30, 2026, well beyond the 60-day timeframe, and Plaintiff failed to produce any documents with her January 30, 2026, disclosures. 

 

Further, Plaintiff failed to carry the good-cause burden.  Plaintiff’s disclosures asserted a “diligent search,” and that responsive documents sit in FCA’s own portal.  (Coughlin Decl. ¶ 4, Ex. A.)  That explanation does not constitute good cause.  Plaintiff has not explained the delay or the absence of document production.  On this record, Plaintiff has not shown good cause.

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL AND FOR SANCTIONS

 

Plaintiff’s motion is based on assertions that Defendant failed to comply timely with the disclosure requirements under section 871.26 and failed to serve a verified response with the disclosures that it did produce.  Defendant contends that it did provide the required disclosures under the statute and that the statute does not require a verified response. 

 

Plaintiff has failed to show that section 871.26 requires a verified statements with the disclosures provided.  Plaintiff contends: “California’s mandatory initial disclosure statute expressly provides that initial disclosures ‘shall be verified either in a written declaration by the party or the party’s authorized representative, or signed by the party’s counsel.’”  But Plaintiff cites section 2016.090(a)(5) of the Code of Civil Procedure to support this contention; that provision is in the civil discovery provisions of the Code, and Plaintiff offers no authority to show that it is applicable to the initial disclosures under section 871.26.  Plaintiff’s contention is entirely without merit. 

 

With regard to the documents that Defendant did and did not produce, Plaintiff argues that, had Defendant conducted electronic searches in various databases, it would have found other documents to be produced with the initial disclosures.  Plaintiff has offered no evidence to support the contention that an electronic search would produce additional documents.  Plaintiff has also not shown that the initial disclosures that Defendant provided failed to comply with Defendant’s obligations under section 871.26.

 

Conclusion

 

FCA’s motion for sanctions is granted.  The Court orders Plaintiff’s counsel, Strategic Legal Practices, APC, to pay monetary sanctions of $1,500.00 to FCA’s counsel within 30 days of this Ruling. Plaintiff’s motion to compel and for sanctions is denied.
