Case Number: 25PSCV00111    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: H

Ramirez v. Amazon Logistics Inc., et al., Case No. 25PSCV00111

 

ORDER ON MOTION FOR ALTERNATIVE SERVICE OF PROCESS

Plaintiff Pedro Ramirez’s Motion for Alternative Service of Process is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Background 

Plaintiff Pedro Ramirez (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows: On January 14, 2023, Plaintiff’s vehicle was struck by Defendant Yesenia C. Munguia (“Munguia”), who was operating the work vehicle for Defendants Amazon Logistics Inc. (“Amazon”) and Metro Deliveries & Logistics LLC (“Metro”). Plaintiff further alleges that Munguia failed to yield the right of way while attempting to make a left or U-turn, leading to the collision. 

On May 2, 2025, Plaintiff filed an amended motor vehicle complaint, asserting causes of action against Munguia, Amazon, Metro, and Does 1-50 for: (1) General Negligence; and (2) Motor Vehicle. 

A Case Management Conference is set for August 31, 2026.

  

Legal Standard 

Code of Civil Procedure Section 413.30 provides in part: 

“(a) (1) If no provision is made in this chapter or other law for the service of summons, or if a plaintiff, despite exercising reasonable diligence, has been unable to effect service of the summons by any of the methods authorized under this chapter, the court in which the action is pending may, upon motion, direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the party to be served, including by electronic mail or other electronic technology, and that proof of such service be made as prescribed by the court. 

(2) A plaintiff seeking to establish reasonable diligence under this section shall set forth facts detailing all attempts to serve the defendant by each of the methods prescribed by statute, including facts demonstrating why each method was unsuccessful at every address or location where the defendant is likely to be found.”  

(Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30(a).) 

 

Discussion 

Plaintiff moves the Court for an order permitting alternative service of process upon Munguia. 

Plaintiff alleges that he has been unable to serve Munguia through authorized methods. (Mot., at p. 3.) Plaintiff submits a declaration of reasonable diligence attesting to two attempts (Feb. 26 & March 3, 2025) at service at the same address. (Mot., Exh. A.) However, Plaintiff states he has made 10 service attempts upon Munguia in the motion. (Mot., at p. 3.) Plaintiff further alleges that a search was conducted for Munguia’s most recent address, but the resident at 960 Stichman Ave, Basset, CA 91746 stated that Munguia did not reside there. (Mot., at p. 4.) Plaintiff requests leave to serve Munguia by publication, certified mail, email, or any other method the Court would deem appropriate. 

Plaintiff’s application contains the same defects as his previous one: (1) the motion is not accompanied with a supporting declaration; and (2) the Declaration of Reasonable Diligence attached to the motion only addresses the February 26 and March 2, 2025 service attempts and lacks information regarding the September 14, 17, 20, 23, 25, 28, and 30, 2025 and May 19, 2026 alleged attempts. Plaintiff’s motion is almost identical to the previous application that was denied sans the addition of the 10th attempt. Thus, as previously stated, the Court is not satisfied, based upon the information presented, that Munguia cannot be served with the summons and complaint in a manner provided for pursuant to Code. 

The motion, then, is denied without prejudice. 

Plaintiff is directed to submit any future request to serve Munguia by alternative means by filing an application, rather than a noticed motion. Moreover, while the Superior Court of Los Angeles County (SCLAC) forms are only optional, in any future application, Plaintiff is directed review the Application for Publication (SCLAC LACIV 108) and the Order for Publication (SCLAC LACIV 145) and either use those forms or include all the information requested in those forms.

Case Number: 25PSCV01282    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: H

Vasquez v. Ford Motor Company, et al., Case No. 25PSCV01282

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES

Plaintiff Andrea Vasquez’s Motion for Order Compelling Further Responses to Requests for Production [Set One] is GRANTED in part (i.e., as to Nos. 45 & 46) and DENIED in part (i.e., at to Nos. 16-18 & 20-30). Ford is to provide further, Code-compliant responses to Nos. 45 & 46 within 20 days from the date of the hearing. Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

Background

Plaintiff Andrea Vasquez (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows:

On December 19, 2024, Plaintiff entered into a warranty contract with Ford Motor Company (“FMC”) regarding a 2024 Ford Mach-E (“subject vehicle”). The subject vehicle suffers from various defects and has not been repaired after a reasonable number of attempts.

On April 10, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against FMC, Puente Hills Ford, LLC dba Puente Hills Ford (“Puente Hills Ford”) and Does 1-10 for: (1) Violation of Song-Beverly Act—Breach of Express Warranty, (2) Violation of Song-Beverly Act—Breach of Implied Warranty, and (3) Negligent Repair.

On July 28, 2026, the Court granted a stipulated protective order.

The Final Status Conference is set for April 20, 2027.

Trial is set for May 4, 2027.

Legal Standard

“[T]he demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply:

(1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete.

(2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive.

(3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).) 

The motion must include a meet and confer declaration which “state[s] facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2016.040, 2031.310, subd. (b)(2).) “In lieu of a separate statement required under the California Rules of Court, the court may allow the moving party to submit a concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(3).) 

A motion to compel further responses to a demand for inspection or production of documents must “set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).) If the moving party has shown good cause for the production of documents, the burden is on the objecting party to justify the objections. (Kirkland v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 98.) 

Notice of the motion must be provided “within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or any specific later date to which the requesting party and the responding party have agreed in writing . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).) The responding party has the burden of justifying the objections to the requests. (Coy v. Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 210, 220-221.) 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).) 

Discussion 

Plaintiff moves the court for an order compelling FMC to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production, Set No. One (i.e., Nos. 16-18, 20-30, and 45-46). Plaintiff also seeks sanctions against FMC and its counsel of record in the amount of $2,545.00. 

Plaintiff’s counsel Matias Leite (“Leite”) represents as follows:

On November 19, 2025, Plaintiff served the subject discovery. (Leite Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.) FMC initially served unverified responses on December 23, 2025, then sent verified responses on January 12, 2026. (Id., ¶ 4, Exh. B.) On January 13, 2026, Plaintiff sent FMC a meet and confer letter identifying the deficiencies in FMC’s responses. (Id., ¶ 5, Exh. C.) On January 30, Plaintiff was granted an extension for the motion to compel filing deadline in order to schedule an IDC. (Id., ¶ 6, Exh. D.) On February 25, 2026, during the IDC, the Court ordered the parties to meet and confer regarding a protective order. (Id., ¶ 7.) The parties met and conferred multiple times regarding a protective order and extended the discovery motion filing deadline to April 20, 2026, but Leite claims that FMC has “gone silent” since March 17, 2026, and has not moved for a protective order. (Id., ¶¶ 8-12.) The instant motion followed on April 20, 2026.

On June 23, 2026, FMC’s counsel states that he followed up with Plaintiff regarding the protective order. (Perez Decl., ¶ 9.) Subsequent to the filing of the motion, the parties entered into and filed a stipulated protective order with the Court. Additionally, FMC’s counsel, Raymond Perez, Jr., represents that on July 27, 2026, FMC produced any outstanding confidential documents subject to the protective order. (Perez Jr. Decl., ¶ 10.) 

Request for Production (“RFP”) No. 16 asks FMC to produce each written warranty it provided for the Subject Vehicle. RFP No. 17 asks FMC to produce all documents that constitute its warranty claims policies and procedure from 2025 to the present. RFP No. 18 asks FMC to produce its workshop manual(s) for the Subject Vehicle. 

RFP No. 20 asks FMC to produce all documents that describe its procedures for evaluating and responding to California consumers’ complaint regarding its manufactured or distributed vehicles since 2025. RFP No. 21 asks FMC to produce all documents that describe the policies, procedures, and/or instructions FMC’s employees and agents should follow starting from 2025 when evaluating a customer request for a refund for money paid towards or owed on a motor vehicle it manufactured or distributed. RFP No. 22 asks FMC to produce all documents describing its policies, procedures, or guidelines for determining whether a vehicle is eligible for a vehicle repurchase pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act since 2025. RFP No. 23 seeks all training materials given to the individual(s) evaluating the vehicle repurchase request for the Subject Vehicle. RFP No. 24 asks FMC to produce all training materials for the handling of consumer requests for a vehicle repurchase in California starting in 2025. RFP No 25 seeks all scripts and flow charts that FMC used in handling California consumer requests for vehicle repurchase or replacement since 2025. 

RFP Nos. 26-27, 29 ask FMC to produce all documents starting from 2025 evidencing its policies, procedures, and parameters for determining what constitutes a repair presentation, a “non-conformity,” and/or a reasonable number of repair attempts to determine vehicle repurchase eligibility pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act. RFP No. 28 seeks all documents starting from 2025 evidencing FMC’s policies, procedures, and parameters for determining what constitutes a “substantial impairment” of a vehicle’s use, value, or safety to determine vehicle repurchase eligibility pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act. RFP No. 30 asks FMC to produce all documents evidencing its policies, procedures, and parameters for establishing the time frame to respond to a vehicle repurchase request. 

RFP No. 45 seeks sufficient documents showing all of the 2024 Ford Mustang Mach E vehicle owners’ complaints that were the same or substantially similar to any of the complaints the Subject Vehicle was presented to FMC’s authorized repair facilities for during the warranty period. RFP No. 46 seeks sufficient documents showing all warranty repairs made to 2024 Ford Mustang Mach E vehicles that were the same or substantially similar to the repairs FMC’s authorized repair facilities performed on the Subject Vehicle. 

Here, the requests seek documents regarding warranties and manuals related to the Subject Vehicle, policies, procedures, guidelines, and training materials regarding vehicle repurchase requests pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act, and substantially similar complaints and warranty repairs associated with other 2024 Ford Mustang Mach E vehicles. In response to RFP Nos. 16-18 and 20-30, FMC states that it had already agreed to produce or has produced the responsive documents, including: the New Vehicle Limited Warranty Guide for the 2024 Ford Mustang Mach-E; the Workshop Manual applicable to the Subject Vehicle; Ford’s Warranty Policy and Procedural Manual; policies and procedures for Ford’s Customer Relationship Center (handles customer communications regarding vehicle concerns); and its RAV Policy and Procedure manual (re: vehicle refunds and replacements). As to RFP No. 25, FMC complied in part but stated it did not locate scripts responsive to the request after a reasonable and diligent search. FMC also provided objections to the aforementioned requests on the grounds they are overbroad, unduly burdensome, and seek irrelevant documents. Based on the produced documents and FMC’s code-compliant responses, the Court finds that further responses are not warranted for RFP Nos. 16-18 and 20-30. 

As to requests No. 45 and 46, FMC objected on the basis that the requests are overbroad, unduly burdensome, and seek irrelevant documents and information, especially because the requests are not limited in temporal scope, to any specific component at issue, or to any specific warranty repair. FMC also objects on privilege grounds. The Court disagrees with FMC’s objections based on relevance and privilege; as to relevance, the requests seek documents that could reveal whether the Subject Vehicle contained a warranty-covered defect and whether said defects or nonconformities were known prior to Plaintiff’s purchase. As to the privilege objection, FMC does not adequately identify how the requests implicate privilege issues and has not provided a privilege log. However, the Court agrees with FMC that the requests as currently written are overbroad; as such, the Court limits the requests to complaints and warranty repairs associated with other 2024 Ford Mustang Mach E vehicles starting from 2024 and involving the same or substantially similar symptoms as those found in Plaintiff’s repair records. 

The motion is denied in part as to RFP Nos. 16-18, 20-30, and granted in part as to RFP Nos. 45-46. FMC is to provide further, Code-compliant responses to RFP Nos. 45-46 (as narrowed by the Court) within 20 days from the date of the hearing.

Sanctions

Plaintiff seeks sanctions against FMC and her counsel of record in the amount of $2,545.00 [calculated as follows: 3.1 hours preparing motion and supporting documents, plus 2 hours for reviewing the opposition and drafting a reply, plus 2 additional hours preparing for/attending hearing at $350.00/hour, plus $60.00 in filing fees.]

Because the motion was only partially successful, the Court determines that the imposition of sanctions against FMC would be unjust and denies the request for sanctions in its discretion.

Case Number: 26PSCP00245    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: H

Gutierrez v. Pomona Unified School District, et al., Case No. 26PSCP00245

 

ORDER ON PETITION FOR PERMISSION TO FILE LATE CLAIM

Petitioner Issac Gutierrez’s Petition Seeking Permission to File a Late Claim is DENIED.

 

Background 

Petitioner Issac Gutierrez, a minor, by and through her Guardian Ad Litem Rosa Yvette Gutierrez, (“Petitioner”) alleges that on November 20, 2024, Petitioner sustained personal injuries after encountering dangerous conditions on public property (Alcott Elementary School). 

On June 11, 2026, the instant petition was filed.

 

Legal Standard 

Prior to filing a suit against a public entity, a plaintiff must comply with the Government Tort Claims Act, which states, in part: “no suit for money or damages may be brought against a public entity on a cause of action for which a claim is required to be presented . . . until a written claim therefor has been presented to the public entity and has been acted upon by the board, or has been deemed to have been rejected by the board . . .”  (Gov. Code, § 945.4.)  

A claim for death or injury to person or personal property shall be presented not later than six months after the accrual of the cause of action.  (Gov. Code, § 911.2, subd. (a).)  When a claim required to be presented not later than six months after the accrual of the cause of action is not presented within that time, a written application may be made to the public entity for leave to present that claim.  (Gov. Code, § 911.4, subd. (a).) The application shall be presented to the public entity within a reasonable time not to exceed one year after the accrual of the cause of action and shall state the reason for the delay in presenting the claim.  The proposed claim shall be attached to the application. (Gov. Code, § 911.4, subd. (b).)  

If an injured party who has failed to timely file a claim has submitted a written application to the public entity for leave to present such claim and the application has been denied, the injured party may petition to the court for relief from the claim requirements. (Gov. Code, § 946.6, subd. (a); Munoz v. State of California (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1767, 1777.) The petition shall be filed within six months after the application to the board is denied or deemed denied pursuant to Section 911.6.  (Gov. Code, § 946.6, subd. (b).)  

The court shall relieve the petitioner from the requirements of Section 945.4 and grant the petition under Section 946.6, subdivision (c) if the petitioner demonstrates by a preponderance of the evidence the application to the public entity under Section 911.4 was made within a reasonable time not exceeding one year after the accrual of the cause of action and was denied or deemed denied, and that one or more of the following is applicable: (1) the failure to present the claim was due to mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect unless the public entity establishes that it would be prejudiced in the defense of the claim, (2) the person who sustained the injury was a minor during all of the time specified in Section 911.2 for presentation of the claim, (3) the person who sustained the injury was physically or mentally incapacitated during all of the time specified in Section 911.2 and failed to present a claim by reason of that disability, or (4) the person who sustained the injury died before the expiration of the time specified in Section 911.2.  (Gov. Code, § 946.6, subd. (c); Munoz, supra, 33 Cal.App.4th at p. 1778.)  A petitioner may not successfully argue excusable neglect when she fails to take any action in pursuit of the claim within the six-month period.  (Id., at 1778-1779.)  

“The mere recital of mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect is not sufficient to warrant relief. Relief on grounds of mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect is available only on a showing that the claimant’s failure to timely present a claim was reasonable when tested by the objective ‘reasonably prudent person’ standard.” (Department of Water & Power v. Superior Court (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 1288, 1293 (DWP).) “A petitioner or counsel for a petitioner must show more than the mere failure to discover a fact until too late; he or she must establish the failure to discover that fact in the use of reasonable diligence.  [Citation.]” (Munoz v. State of California (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1767, 1783.) Where the lateness of the claim is attributable to the failure of the parties to conduct a reasonably prudent investigation of the circumstances, relief from filing a late claim is properly denied. (Leake v. Wu (1976) 64 Cal.App.3d 668, 673; DWP, supra, 82 Cal.App.4th at p. 1296 [where petitioner and counsel either had actual possession of or readily available access to information and the lateness of a claim is attributable to lack of diligence, the relief is properly denied].) 

 

Discussion 

Judicial Notice 

Pomona Unified School District (“PUSD”) requests that the Court take judicial notice of the Petitioner’s December 23, 2025 Application for Leave to present Late Claim, and attached documents, attached as Exhibit 1 to PUSD’s opposition. (Evidence Code §§ 452, subd. (c)-(d), (h), and 453.) PUSD also requests that the Court take judicial notice of the Court’s file from LASC Case No. 25PSCV03911, especially the documents attached as Exhibits 2-4 to PUSD’s opposition (Summons & Complaint, April 30, 2026 Demurrer Order, & Request for Dismissal). 

These documents were previously filed in a Los Angeles County Superior Court case and can be found in LASC Case No. 25PSCV03911’s file. Accordingly, PUSD’s requests for judicial notice are granted. However, the Court takes judicial notice only as to “the existence, content and authenticity of public records and other specified documents” and the legal effect of the documents’ language when that effect is clear. The Court does not take judicial notice of the truth of the factual matters asserted in those documents. (Dominguez v. Bonta (2022) 87 Cal.App.5th 389, 400; Fontenot v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 256, 265; Sosinsky v. Grant (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1548, 1568 [“[N]either a finding of fact made after a contested adversary hearing nor a finding of fact made after any other type of hearing can be indisputably deemed to have been a correct finding. As we have noted, ‘[u]nder the doctrine of judicial notice, certain matters are assumed to be indisputably true, and the introduction of evidence to prove them will not be required.’ (1 Witkin Cal. Evidence (3d ed. 1986) § 80.) Taking judicial of the truth of a judge’s factual finding would appear to us to be tantamount to taking judicial notice that the judge’s factual finding must necessarily have been correct and that the judge is therefore infallible. We resist the temptation to do so.”].) 

Merits 

Petitioner moves the Court for an order granting leave to file a late claim pursuant to Government Code section 946.6.

Petitioner alleges that on November 20, 2024, he sustained personal injuries when he encountered a dangerous condition at his public elementary school. (Mot., at p. 4.) On May 7, 2025, Petitioner directed a government claim to PUSD and the other named Defendants. (Id.) After not receiving a response, Petitioner filed a complaint on October 29, 2025 (Case No. 25PSCV03911); however, after receiving the complaint, PUSD claimed it did not receive a Government Tort Claim form. (Id.) Petitioner issued a late claim on December 23, 2025. (Id.) The Court in Case No. 25PSCV03911 sustained PUSD’s demurrer with leave to amend on April 30, 2026, on the grounds that Petitioner failed to allege sufficient facts demonstrating substantial compliance with the Government Claims Act’s claim presentation requirement and the December 2025 application for leave to present a late claim was untimely. (Id., Exh. D.) On June 12, 2026, the Court in Case No. 25PSCV03911 entered Petitioner’s request for dismissal of the entire action of all parties and all causes of action without prejudice. Petitioner filed this application on June 11, 2026. 

Petitioner alleges that the Court in 25PSCV03911 was wrong when it found that the late claim previously filed on December 23, 2025 is the first notice that PUSD had of the claim’s particulars. (Mot., at p. 9.) Specifically, Petitioner argues that the complaint filed on October 29, 2025 (that was found to be defective for failure to exhaust administrative remedies) was sufficient to substantially comply with the Government Tort Claims Act. (Id.) Thus, the petition is timely. (Id.) 

Petitioner further argues that relief must be liberally granted when the claimant is a minor. (Id.) In addition, Petitioner argues that the failure to timely present claims was due to excusable neglect, that he acted diligently and within the statutory timeline when he learned of the neglect, and that PUSD will not be prejudiced by a grant. (Id., at p. 14-22.) 

In opposition, PUSD argues that the petition must be denied due to res judicata because Judge Lynette Gridiron Winston dispensed a prior judgment on the same issues and dismissed the claims on April 30, 2026. (Opp., at pp. 8-10.) PUSD further argues that even if the Court finds res judicata to not apply here, the petition should still be denied because Petitioner has failed to timely submit the application. (Id., at pp. 10-11.) 

“Three elements must exist for res judicata (or claim preclusion) to apply: ‘(1) the decision in the prior proceeding is final and on the merits; (2) the present proceeding is on the same cause of action as the prior proceeding; and (3) the parties in the present proceeding or parties in privity with them were parties to the prior proceeding.’ [citation omitted].” (Association of Irritated Residents v. Department of Conservation (2017) 11 Cal.App.5th 1202, 1219.) Here, the first suit did not result in a final decision on the merits, because the complaint was dismissed without prejudice. Thus, claim preclusion does not apply here. 

Issue preclusion depends on three questions: “‘Was the issue decided in the prior adjudication identical with the one presented in the action in question? Was there a final judgment on the merits? Was the party against whom the plea is asserted a party or in privity with a party to the prior adjudication?’ [citation omitted].” (Gurrola v. County of Los Angeles (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 145, 151.) As noted, the first suit did not result in a final decision on the merits, because the complaint was dismissed without prejudice and the demurrer ruling was on pleading sufficiency. Thus, issue preclusion does not apply here either. 

The Court, however, finds that Petitioner did not timely submit an Application for Leave to file a late claim. Pursuant to Government Code section 911.4, an application must be presented to the public entity within one year after the accrual of the cause of action. PUSD alleges that Petitioner submitted his December 2025 Application to PUSD on January 5, 2026, 13.5 months after the alleged incident. (Opp., at p. 11.) Petitioner does not dispute that the December 23, 2025 late claim was untimely, but asserts that the complaint filed on October 29, 2025 in Case No. 25PSCV03911 constitutes timely notice under the claim presentation statutes. (Mot., at p. 9.) The Court finds this argument unpersuasive. 

Pursuant to Government Code section 911.4, subdivision (b), “the application shall be presented to the public entity.” Section 915, subdivision (a) specifies how the claim or application must be presented to a local public entity:

(1) “Delivering it to the clerk, secretary, or auditor thereof.

(2) Mailing it to the clerk, secretary, auditor, or to the governing body at its principal office.

(3) If expressly authorized by an ordinance or resolution of the public entity, submitting it electronically to the public entity in the manner specified in the ordinance or resolution.”

Therefore, even if the complaint was considered a valid first notice, the Proof of Service filed by Petitioner in Case No. 25PSCV03911 states that PUSD was served the summons and complaint on November 24, 2025, more than a year after the alleged injury. The Court finds Petitioner’s Application for Leave to file a late claim to PUSD was untimely. 

In reply, Petitioner argues that Government Code section 946.4 presents an independent statutory basis for relief, and PUSD has failed to carry its burden pursuant to the Code. (Reply at p. 4.) However, “[p]oints raised for the first time in a reply brief will ordinarily not be considered, because such consideration would deprive the respondent of an opportunity to counter the argument.” (American Drug Stores, Inc. v. Stroh (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1446, 1453). 

The petition is denied.
