I.            INTRODUCTION

         Defendants Atlas Voluntary Benefits Financial & Insurance Solutions, Inc. (“Atlas”) and Marisol Flores (collectively, “Defendants”) move for orders compelling plaintiff Employee Retention Benefits, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) to serve further responses to Special Interrogatories, Set Two and Requests for Production of Documents, Set Two. The Court previously asked the parties to provide a joint statement identifying and summarizing the requests at issue. Although the documents filed on August 14, 2026, are entitled “joint status conference report”, the parties do not seem to have prepared them “jointly” because there are overlapping areas of agreement for certain discovery requests where Plaintiff “proposed” a resolution and Defendants state that they are “agreeable” to the proposal. If the report was actually the product of joint efforts, those statements should have been omitted as irrelevant. Therefore, the Court considers the motions moot with respect to the requests which appear to no longer be in dispute and does not include any discussion of them in this ruling.

Plaintiff to identify employees, agents, and independent contractors who materially participated in selling, enrolling, servicing, administering, or transitioning insurance policies sold to Los Angeles County employees who had their premiums deducted from their payroll by PPOA.

SROG Nos. 98, 114, 118: DENIED.

2.   Website/LinkedIn Testimonial Witnesses

6.   Employee Complaints and ERB Financial Information

B.          Requests for Production of Documents, Set Two

1.   Corporate, Financial, Website, and Marketing Records

          RFP Nos. 37-38, 41-42: DENIED. Defendants fail to show good cause for the production of documents relating to ERB North American LLC, which is not a party to the case.

RFP Nos. 53-61: DENIED. The objection that the requests are overbroad is SUSTAINED.

RFP No. 181: DENIED. No showing of good cause.

RFP No. 46: GRANTED. Requests are appropriately narrowed to address the 2020 data breach incident.

4.   Other Employee Associations and Third Parties

RFP No. 151: DENIED. Overbroad, request is not limited to a specific set of employees relevant to this action and broadly asks for documents regarding “any customer complaints or cancellation requests.”

RFP Nos. 182, 187: DENIED. No showing of good cause why other employee associations are relevant, i.e., whether former ERB members are part of these organizations.

RFP Nos. 152-153: GRANTED. Statement of inability to comply is not complete. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.230.)

RFP Nos. 184-185: GRANTED. Requests concern an entity identified as the “policyholder” for customers who allegedly requested refunds and cancellations due to Defendants’ conduct. organizations that former ERB customers are part of.

5.   Sydney Consulting Group/William Bade

RFP Nos. 160-161: GRANTED, Plaintiff to produce ordinary-course consulting communications and documents concerning ERB customers who were LA County Employees with insurance premium payroll deductions previously made through PPOA, Atlas/Flores, policy migrations, cancellations/refunds, and damages.

6.   Carrier, Policy, Contract, Compensation, and Indemnity Documents

Plaintiff Raul Hernandez (“Plaintiff”) seeks a default judgment against defendants Aryh Industries, LLC, Aryh Portable Restrooms, LLC, and Misael Hernandez-Delgado (collectively, “Defendants”). On February 27, 2026, the Court noted that the statement of damages was not properly served on any of the Defendants and vacated the default improperly entered against them.

On June 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a proof of service purporting to show that the statement of damages was served on Aryh Portable Restrooms, LLC. The proof of service shows that the documents were left with an agent, but there is no declaration of mailing. Thus, the Court cannot find that the statement of damages was appropriately served via substituted service on Aryh Portable Restrooms, LLC.

Then, on August 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed “proof of personal service” of the statement of damages on Misael Hernandez-Delgado. But there was no personal service effected, rather, the statement of damages was left with “Kevin H., Owner Postal Service.” There is no declaration of reasonable diligence showing that personal service was attempted or a statement that the address served is a private mailbox. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20.)

There is also no proof of service showing that the statement of damages was served on Aryh Industries, LLC.

However, even if Plaintiff had properly served Defendants with the statement of damages, Plaintiff’s declaration, filed on February 6, 2026, is insufficient to substantiate Plaintiff’s request for $160,000 in damages. First, the declaration refers to Exhibits 1 and 2 attached to a Brief Summary of the Case, even though no such summary is in the Court’s record; Plaintiff submits no evidence supporting his special damages. Second, Plaintiff’s declaration is entirely conclusory and contains no description about the treatment he received, the injuries he sustained, or the extent of any pain and suffering or emotional distress he suffered.

Accordingly, since the statement of damages has not yet been properly served on any of the Defendants, the Court VACATES the default that was improperly entered on June 22, 2026 and sets an OSC re: entry of default for _________________ at 8:30 a.m. in Department 3 of the Alhambra Courthouse.

I.            INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff Benjamin Steinberg (“Plaintiff”) filed this action on January 10, 2024, and filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) on April 16, 2025. The FAC asserts causes of action for premises liability and general negligence against 673 E Col Properties, LLC (“Defendant”). On June 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed an amendment naming Defendant as Doe 2.

Defendant filed this motion for summary judgment on May 28, 2026. Defendant argues it owed no duty to Plaintiff to maintain a tree well or remove a homeless person and their belongings from an adjacent sidewalk. The motion is unopposed.

II.          FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Plaintiff alleges he was walking on the sidewalk when he moved to the right of the sidewalk to avoid trash, debris, and a homeless person in his path. When he moved, he stepped into a tree well that was not level, lost his balance, and fell. (FAC, ¶ 17.)

III.        LEGAL STANDARD

In reviewing a motion for summary judgment, courts must apply a three-step analysis: “(1) identify the issues framed by the pleadings; (2) determine whether the moving party has negated the opponent’s claims; and (3) determine whether the opposition has demonstrated the existence of a triable, material factual issue.” (Hinesley v. Oakshade Town Center (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 289, 294.)

IV.        REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Defendant’s request for judicial notice of City’s Code of Ordinances is GRANTED.

V.          DISCUSSION

The elements of a negligence and premises liability cause of action are the same: duty, breach, causation, and damages.  (Castellon v. U.S. Bancorp (2013) 220 Cal.App.4th 994, 998.) Those who own, possess, or control property generally have a duty to exercise ordinary care in managing the property in order to avoid exposing others to an unreasonable risk of harm.  (Annocki v. Peterson Enterprises, LLC (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 32, 37.) “A defendant cannot be held liable for the defective or dangerous condition of property which it did not own, possess, or control. Where the absence of ownership, possession, or control has been unequivocally established, summary judgment is proper.  [Citation.]” (Isaacs v. Huntington Memorial Hospital (1985) 38 Cal.3d 112, 134.) There are exceptions to this general rule where a person has voluntarily assumed a duty to act, either by contract or by his or her actions (Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club of Southern California (2002) 161 Cal.App.3d 571, 575), or where a person creates the danger that causes the harm or increases a foreseeable risk of harm (Vasilenko v. Grace Family Church (2017) 3 Cal.5th 1077, 1081).

Courts have held that in the absence of a staute a landowner is under no duty to maintain in a safe condition a public street abutting upon the landowner’s property unless the landowner created the danger. (See, e.g., Sexton v. Brooks (1952) 39 Cal.2d 153, 157.) “The reason for this rule is that a landowner generally has no right to control another’s property, including streets owned and maintained by the government.”  (Vasilenko, supra, 3 Cal.5th at p. 1084.)

Here, it is undisputed that the City of Pasadena (“City”), not Defendant, designed and constructed the tree well. (UMF Nos. 2-3.) City also owned, controlled, maintained the sidewalk and tree well – not Defendant. (UMF Nos. 4, 7.) Defendant never repaired the subject tree well, but City repaired the tree well in 2023 and added dirt to reduce or eliminate the elevation differential between the tree well and the sidewalk. (UMF Nos. 5-6, 11-12.) Defendant also did not own, maintain, or control the public sidewalk and therefore had no duty to keep a homeless man and his belongings off the sidewalk. (UMF Nos. 13-14, 16.) Defendant did not permit or cause any homeless person to occupy the public sidewalk and did not place any trash or debris on the public sidewalk. (UMF Nos. 16, 18.)

Defendant meets its burden to show Plaintiff cannot establish that it owed him a duty to maintain the tree well or keep the sidewalk clear. Plaintiff filed a notice of non-opposition in which he agreed that Defendant did not owe him a duty and no act or omission by Defendant caused his injuries. Accordingly, Plaintiff does not raise a triable issue of material fact and summary judgment is appropriate.

VI.    CONCLUSION

          Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion for summary judgment is GRANTED.
