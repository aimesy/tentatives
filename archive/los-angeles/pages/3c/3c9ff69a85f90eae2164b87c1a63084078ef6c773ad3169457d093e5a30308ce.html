DEPARTMENT A LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

INSTRUCTIONS: If the parties wish to submit on the tentative ruling and avoid a court appearance on the matter, the moving party must:

1. Contact the opposing party and all other parties who have appeared in the action and confirm that each will submit on the tentative ruling.

2. No later than 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing, call the Courtroom (310-761-4302) advising that all parties will submit on the tentative ruling and waive hearing; and

3. Serve notice of the Court's ruling on all parties entitled to receive service.

If this procedure is followed, when the case is called the Court will enter its ruling on the motion in accordance with its tentative ruling. If any party declines to submit on the tentative ruling, then no telephone call is necessary, and all parties should appear at the hearing. If there is neither a telephone call nor an appearance, then the matter may either be taken off calendar or ruled on.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: CITY’S MOTION FOR ATTORNEY’S FEES

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: August 3, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 06/01/2023

TRIAL: 11/10/2025

 

MOVING PARTY:              Plaintiff City of Compton

RESPONDING PARTY:     None

1.     Background

This is a narcotics abatement action. Plaintiff City of Compton (“City”) alleged that defendant Green House Cure operated an unlicensed marijuana dispensary business on the property located at 2007 E. Compton Blvd., Compton, CA 90221 (the “Property”). City alleged that defendants FS International, Inc. and Mehdi Javadiaghdam were at all relevant times the owner of the Property. A non-jury trial was held in this action on November 10, 2025. Judgment was entered for City and against defendants FS International, Inc. and Mehdi Javadiaghdam on February 26, 2026.

On February 8, 2024, the court appointed a receiver to take control of and operate the Property in order to terminate the illegal business and repair and rehabilitate the Property. John S. Rey (“Receiver”) was appointed as receiver for the Property.

On September 10, 2025, Receiver moved for an order approving the sale of the Property for $950,000.00. The court granted Receiver’s motion on October 8, 2025.

On April 23, 2026, the court granted Receiver’s motion for discharge and terminated the receivership.

2.     Discussion

City moves for attorney’s fees as the prevailing party pursuant to Health and Safety Code § 17980.7, subd. (c). In support of this motion, Plaintiff provides the declaration of attorney David R. Welch. Attorney Welch declares the hourly rates of the individuals who worked on this action and provides a set of itemized time entries for work on the instant action. Plaintiff also filed a memorandum of costs. No parties have opposed this motion. 

“[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate. . . .¿ The reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work.¿ The lodestar figure may then be adjusted, based on consideration of factors specific to the case, in order to fix the fee at the fair market value for the legal services provided.”¿ (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095 (internal citations omitted); Reck v. FCA US LLC (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 682, 691 [“To determine a reasonable attorney fee award, the trial court applies the lodestar method”].) 

The prevailing party is entitled to “’compensation for all the hours reasonably spent” in litigating the action to a successful conclusion. (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc.¿(2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 821 (Robertson).) ‘Reasonably spent’ means that time spent ‘in the form of inefficient or duplicative efforts is not subject to compensation.’ (Horsford v. Board of Trustees of California State University (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 394 (Horsford).) A plaintiff who receives monetary recovery as part of a settlement agreement is considered to be the prevailing party. (DeSaulles v. Community Hospital of Monterey Peninsula (2016) 62 Cal.4th 1140, 1156.)

“[T]he verified time statements of the attorneys, as officers of the court, are entitled to credence in the absence of a clear indication the records are erroneous.”¿ (Horsford, supra, 132 Cal.App.4th at p. 396.)  However, the court may rely on his or her own experience and is given broad discretion in calculating reasonable attorney’s fees. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 ["The experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court...”].)

The lodestar may be augmented or diminished “by taking various relevant factors into account including (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved and the skill displayed in presenting them; (2) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys; and (3) the contingent nature of the¿¿fee award, based on the uncertainty of prevailing on the merits and of establishing eligibility for the award.” (Ibid.) The multiplier is a risk enhancement based on the probability of loss. (Robertson, supra, 144 Cal.App.4th at p. 821.)

Plaintiff requests $ 77,967.00 in previously-incurred attorney’s fees and $4,737.50 in fees anticipated to be incurred in bringing the instant motion, totaling $82,704.50.

Plaintiff is the prevailing party in this action, based on the court’s decision in its favor following trial. Plaintiff is therefore entitled to reasonable and actually incurred attorney’s fees and statutorily available costs, pursuant to Health and Safety Code § 17980.7, subd. (c)(11).

Upon the court’s review of counsel’s declarations, the court finds that the following hourly rates are reasonable: $350 for partners David R. Welch and Kelley Bond; $275 for associates Marius Jungk, Eddie Pitluck, Julian Hasenfratz, Marcus Whittemore, and Seungjai Oh; $225 per hour for paralegals Latasha Bonaparte, Emilia Lillo, Karen Galoyan, and George Krboyan; and $175 for legal assistants Alexaundria Gaines-Southwood and David Pease.

Along with the Welch Declaration, the court has reviewed each entry on the billing statements submitted by City’s counsel. Based on the court’s review of the billing statements and City’s arguments, the court finds the requested hours to have been reasonably spent.

Attorney Welch declares that he spent 1.5 hours working on the instant motion and that Attorney Jungk spent 6.5 hours preparing the instant motion. (Welch Decl. ¶12.) Attorney Welch also declares that he anticipates spending 1.5 hours and Attorney Jungk anticipates spending five hours to review any opposition and prepare a reply, and that Attorney Welch anticipates spending 1.5 hours to attend the hearing. In light of the lack of opposition or reply, and to avoid duplicative billing, the court will reduce the requested hours, for a total amount awarded in connection with the instant motion of $2,500.

City also requests that its attorney’s fees be satisfied from the funds on deposit with the court from the sale of the Property before Non-Party Newtek Small Business Finance, LLC (“Newtek”)’s claim on those funds. However, the court has already found that Newtek’s claim on the funds on deposit with the court precedes City’s claim. Furthermore, a motion for attorney’s fees is not the proper procedural vehicle to determine the priority of claims on funds deposited with the court. The court will therefore decline to consider this request.

3.     Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court GRANTS IN PART City’s motion for attorney’s fees. The court awards City attorney’s fees in the total amount of $80,467. 

The clerk is directed to interlineate the fee award in favor of City on the previously-entered judgment.

City is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION QUESTIONS

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: August 3, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 03/11/2024

1st AMENDED COMPLAINT FILED: 04/24/2024

TRIAL: 08/31/2026

 

MOVING PARTY:              Plaintiff E.G.

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District

1.     Background

Plaintiff E.G. (“Plaintiff”) alleges that on September 11, 2023, Plaintiff was a student at King/Drew Magnet High School of Medicine and Science, a school within defendant Los Angeles Unified School District (“District”). Plaintiff alleges that, on September 11, 2023, Plaintiff was assaulted by a fellow student. Plaintiff alleges that staff members of the school failed to adequately supervise Plaintiff, failed to administer medical care of Plaintiff after the incident, and failed to timely notify Plaintiff’s parents that he was assaulted.

2.     Discussion

a. Motion to Compel Further Responses to Deposition Questions

Plaintiff moves to compel further responses from District’s retained expert, Dr. Anita Herrera-Hamilton, to deposition questions. Plaintiff contends that Dr. Herrera-Hamilton refused to answer discoverable questions during her deposition. In opposition, District contends that the parties have already stipulated, and the court has entered an order, that access to the testing data and related testimony would be limited to designated and professionally qualified experts.

A party may move to compel a further answer when a deponent fails to answer a deposition question. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subds. (a), (b).) If the court determines that the answer or production sought is subject to discovery, it shall order that the answer be given or the production be made on the resumption of the deposition. (Id., subd. (i).)

Plaintiff contends that Dr. Herrera-Hamilton refused to answer questions concerning which performance validity tests Plaintiff passed or failed; Plaintiff’s performance data; the cutoff score Dr. Herrera-Hamilton used for each test; the adjusted cutoff score for an examinee with an intellectual disability; and the task demands of the tests. However, District contends and Plaintiff concedes that the parties have agreed to provide further discovery as to Plaintiff’s performance data and which tests Plaintiff passed or failed. Therefore, at issue are the cutoff scores that were used, the adjusted cutoff score, and the task demands.

On July 24, 2025, the court entered an order granting District’s application to compel an independent medical examination of Plaintiff, pursuant to the terms of a stipulation between the parties. The stipulation stated that “[a]t the conclusion of any testing of Plaintiff by Dr. Herrera-Hamilton, a copy of the actual test pages, raw data generated and all test results regarding Plaintiff shall be provided to Plaintiff’s expert… With respect to the raw data generated and all test results regarding Plaintiff (hereinafter “Data”), the parties mutually agree to the following:… Access to Data and testimony concerning Data is limited to experts designated by the parties who are professionally qualified to use and interpret the Data.” (Stein Decl., Ex. 1.)

It is apparent, and conceded by Plaintiff, that the test information at issue constitutes Data within the meaning of the joint stipulation, and is therefore subject to this provision. Plaintiff contends that the stipulation’s statement that “Access to Data and testimony concerning Data is limited to experts designated by the parties” is properly interpreted as concerning two separate subjects; that is to say, “access to Data” and “testimony concerning Data.” Plaintiff contends that, therefore, any testimony from Dr. Herrera-Hamilton, as a designated expert, is proper. Plaintiff misinterprets the language of the stipulation. It is apparent to the court that the parties’ meaning in setting forth this language was that access to Data and access to testimony concerning Data would be limited to designated experts, not that access to Data would be so limited and that only experts could testify concerning Data but that the expert testimony would be fully accessible non-experts, even where the testimony itself exposed Data and thereby provided access to it.

District contends that all raw data, testing materials, and results, including the information at issue in this motion, were provided to Plaintiff’s designated expert. Therefore, District has complied with the terms of the court’s July 24, 2025 order.

The court will therefore deny Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses from Dr. Herrera-Hamilton to deposition questions.

    b. Sanctions

District requests monetary sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $6,107.40, based on attorney Shiva E. Stein’s hourly rate of $377 across 11.2 hours spent preparing the opposition, three hours in meet-and-confer correspondence, and an anticipated two hours to attend the hearing.

Code of Civil Procedure § 2023.030 provides that, “[t]o the extent authorized by the chapter governing any particular discovery method . . . , the court, after notice to any affected party, person, or attorney, and after opportunity for hearing, may impose . . . [monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating] sanctions against anyone engaging in conduct that is a misuse of the discovery process . . . .”

Monetary sanctions shall constitute reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees incurred as a result of the sanctionable conduct. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (a).) Monetary sanctions are mandatory in connection with a motion to compel further responses to deposition questions, unless the court finds that the offending party acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust. (Code of Civ. Proc. § 2025.480, subd. (j).)

There is no indication that Plaintiff had substantial justification to bring the instant motion or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust. Plaintiff was fully aware of the stipulation between the parties and nonetheless chose to file the instant motion. Sanctions are therefore warranted against Plaintiff.

The court finds the requested hourly rate to be reasonable. The court finds the requested hours to be unreasonable and will reduce them accordingly. The court will therefore award monetary sanctions in a total of $4,524, which the court finds to be reasonable under the circumstances.

3.     Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses from Dr. Herrera-Hamilton to deposition questions.

The court GRANTS District’s request for monetary sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $4,524, to be paid not later than September 3, 2026.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Based on the foregoing, the application is denied without prejudice.

Case Number: 25CMCV01944    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: A

The court has reviewed the parties' filings in response to the Order to Show Cause re Dismissal. 

With respect to the third cause of action for declaratory relief, plaintiff failed to file a motion for leave to file an amended complaint as required by the court's June 3, 2026 order and Code of Civil Procedure section 473.  Plaintiff further failed to comply with Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, including but not limited to the requirement that a proposed amended pleading and a sworn declaration accompany any motion for leave to file an amended pleading as provided by that rule. 

With respect to the first and second causes of action, the court concludes that plaintiff's Response to the Order to Show Cause re Dismissal constitutes an improper motion for reconsideration prohibited by Code of Civil Procedure section 1008.  The court's June 3, 2026 order did not grant plaintiff permission to amend or to seek leave to amend the first or second cause of action.  As stated in the conclusion, the demurrer to these causes of action was sustained without leave to amend.  The court's reference in the body of the order to the demurrer to the first cause of action being sustained with leave to amend was inadvertent and in error, and will be corrected nunc pro tunc to conform to the final order set forth in the conclusion.  Nonetheless, plaintiff's response brief makes clear he did not rely on any such error to his detriment because he acknowledges his understanding that the demurrer was sustained without leave to amend, and he failed to file an amended pleading which is consistent with the order denying leave to amend.  

Plaintiff requests that the court overlook his procedural failures and instead rely on the policy that provides for liberality in the amendment of pleadings.  However, that policy does not relieve plaintiff from his duty to comply with all governing statutes and court rules, which the court expressly brought to his attention and emphasized in bolded and italicized font in the June 3, 2026 order.  Moreover, under California law, a self-represented (in propria persona, or "in propria persona") litigant must expect and receive the same treatment as if represented by an attorney — no different, no better, no worse. This rule is firmly established by the California Supreme Court in Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal. 4th 975 and traces back to the foundational Court of Appeal decision in Taylor v. Bell (1971) 21 Cal. App. 3d 1002. Except where a particular rule expressly provides otherwise, the rules of civil procedure apply equally to parties represented by counsel and those who forgo attorney representation. Judges are not required to act as counsel for self-represented parties, and mere self-representation is not a ground for exceptionally lenient treatment.  To overlook plaintiff's repeated and unjustified procedural failures in this case would be to deprive defendants of fundamental fairness and due process protections.  

Based on the foregoing, the court dismisses the action with prejudice, and shall enter judgment of dismissal in favor of defendants and against plaintiff.  Defendants are ordered to give notice.
