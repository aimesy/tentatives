23SMCV01072 (Yaghoubzadeh v. Moradi, et. al.)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            On March 9, 2023, plaintiff Crystal Yaghoubzade (Plaintiff) filed the initial Complaint. The first amended complaint (FAC) was filed on May 18, 2023, against David R. Moradi (Moradi), Meir Agaki D.D.S. Inc. (Agaki) and does 1-50, alleging dental malpractice, violation of Health and Safety Code sections 123100, et seq., negligent hiring and supervision, fraudulent misrepresentation, negligent misrepresentation, fraudulent concealment, intentional infliction of emotional distress, and declaratory relief.

 

            On September 5, 2024, Moradi’s Motions to Compel were granted, and Plaintiff was ordered to provide responses by September 15, 2024. (9/5/24 Minute Order) In addition, Plaintiff was ordered to pay the monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,140 due October 21, 2024. (Id.)

 

            On March 18, 2025, Moradi’s motion for terminating sanctions was granted in part, as follows: “the Court again orders the Plaintiff to file verified code compliant responses to SROGS and RFPs within 10 days of the hearing and to pay additional sanctions in the amount of $1,485.00.” (3/18/25 Minute Order.)

 

            On November 20, 2025, the Court granted Agaki’s motion for summary judgment. (1//20/25 Minute Order.) Moradi’s motion for summary judgment was granted in part as to the 1st, 2nd, and 4th-8th causes of action and denied as to the third cause of action. (Id.)

 

            On March 18, 2025, Moradi’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions and Monetary Sanctions was heard and granted in part as follows:

 

1. Defendant DAVID R. MORADI, D.D.S.’s Motion for an Order of terminating sanctions against Plaintiff CRYSTAL YAGHOUBZADEH and Request for Monetary Sanctions is hereby GRANTED in part.

 

2. Plaintiff CRYSTAL YAGHOUBZADEH is ORDERED again to serve verified, code compliant responses to Defendant DAVID R. MORADI, D.D.S.’s Special Interrogatories, Set Two, and Defendant DAVID R. MORADI, D.D.S.’s Request for Documents, Set Two, within 10 days of the hearing on March 18, 2025.

 

3. Plaintiff CRYSTAL YAGHOUBZADEH and Plaintiff’s counsel, Armand J. Jaafari, Esq., jointly and severely is further ORDERED to pay Defendant DAVID R. MORADI, D.D.S. monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,485.00 by way of check to Ford, Walker, Haggerty & Behar [predecessor firm to Behar Gibbs Savage and Paulson] within 10 days of the signed order of the Court.

 

4. The Court further ordered Plaintiff CRYSTAL YAGHOUBZADEH to be available for deposition on April 8, 2025, April 9, 2025, April 14, 2025, or April 17, 2025 as agreed by the parties at the hearing on March 18, 2025

 

(3/18/26 Minute Order.)

                       

INSTANT MOTION

 

            On April 23, 2026, Moradi filed the instant motion for judgment on the pleadings or alternatively a motion to strike.

 

            No opposition has been filed.

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

            The Court finds that Moradi has satisfied the meet and confer requirement. (Bridwell Decl. ¶ 13.)

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

            Moradi’s requests for judicial notice of court filings in the instant action are granted.

 

DISCUSSION

 

I.       MOTION FOR JUDGMENT ON THE PLEADINGS / MOTION TO STRIKE

            A motion for judgment on the pleadings has the same function as a general demurrer but is made after the time for demurrer has expired and, except as provided by Code of Civil Procedure section 438, the rules governing demurrers apply. (Cloud v. Northrop Grumman Corp. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 995, 999; Southern Calif. Edison Co. v. City of Victorville (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 218, 227; Templo v. State of Calif. (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 730, 735; Code Civ, Proc. § 438.)

 

            “The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time at its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or any order of the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.)

 

A.    Negligent Hiring and Supervision

 

            The elements of a cause of action for negligent hiring, retention, or supervision are: (1) the employer’s hiring, retaining, or supervising an employee; (2) the employee was incompetent or unfit; (3) the employer had reason to believe undue risk of harm would exist because of the employment; and (4) harm occurs. (Evan F. v. Hughson United Methodist Church (1992) 8 Cal.App.4th 828, 836-837.)

 

            Here, the instant motion is made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438 on the ground that the Plaintiff’s entire First Amended Complaint fails to sufficiently state a cause of action against Moradi for Negligent Hiring and Supervision based on this court’s Order dated March 27, 2026 on Moradi’s request for issue and evidence sanctions, and made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 436. Morardi argues that the only remaining cause of action, Negligent Hiring and Supervision, is not legally cognizable, improper, and contrary to this Court’s Order. The Court agrees.

            The Court’s March 27, 2026 Order precludes Plaintiff “from introducing at trial evidence in support of its cause of action against Defendant Moradi for negligent hiring and supervision of employees” thus Plaintiff cannot sufficiently present a legally cognizable claim for Negligent Hiring and Supervision against the Defendant.

            Accordingly, Moradi’s unopposed motion is GRANTED.

Case Number: 24SMCV01661    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: O

TENTATIVE RULING

July 21, 2026

24SMCV01661 (Vasquez v. American Honda Motor Co.)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

             This is a lemon law action brought by Plaintiff Blanca Vasquez (Plaintiff) concerning a 2024 Honda CRV, (the Subject Vehicle), which Plaintiff allege was purchased new on October 8, 2023. (Knighton Decl. ¶ 2.) Plaintiff filed the instant action asserting claims for breach of express warranty and breach of implied warranty under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (Act) against Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. (Defendant)

           

INSTANT MOTION

 

            On February 13, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to compel Plaintiff’s vehicle inspection. Plaintiff filed an opposition. No reply has been filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

I.       MOTION TO COMPEL VEHICLE INSPECTION

 

            “Unless otherwise limited by order of the court in accordance with this title, any party may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, that is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action or to the determination of any motion made in that action, if the matter either is itself admissible in evidence or appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence. Discovery may relate to the claim or defense of the party seeking discovery or of any other party to the action. Discovery may be obtained of the identity and location of persons having knowledge of any discoverable matter, as well as of the existence, description, nature, custody, condition, and location of any document, electronically stored information, tangible thing, or land or other property.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.010.)

 

            Any party may obtain discovery by “inspecting, copying, testing, or sampling documents, tangible things, land or other property, and electronically stored information in the possession, custody, or control of any other party to the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010, subd. (a).) If a party filing a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling thereafter fails to permit the inspection, copying, testing, or sampling in accordance with that party's statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance. (Id. § 2031.320, subd. (a).)

 

            On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete; (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive; (3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)  The court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust. (Id., subd. (h).) The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Id., subd. (b)(2).)

 

            If a party filing a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling under Sections 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, 2031.240, and 2031.280 thereafter fails to permit the inspection, copying, testing, or sampling in accordance with that party's statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.320, subd. (a).) The Court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel compliance with a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust. (Id., subd. (c).)

 

            Here, on November 21, 2025, Defendant served a Notice of Vehicle Inspection, setting the inspection to take place on January 30, 2026. (Knighton Decl. ¶ 5; Exhibit A) On January 13, 2026, Plaintiff served objections to the Notice of Vehicle inspection. (Id. ¶ 7; Exhibit B). Defendant asserts that it has attempted to schedule an inspection of the Subject Vehicle, but its efforts have been obstructed by Plaintiff. (Knighton Decl. at ¶ 4-7.)

           

            But Plaintiff’s counsel has shown that he sent five communications to Defendant’s counsel between January 23, 2026, and February 18, 2026, offering dates for the vehicle inspection and requesting a response. (Romero Decl., ¶¶ 6–10; Ex. 1.) Defendant’s counsel did not respond. (Id. ¶ 12-14.) Defendant did not propose any alternative dates, did not explain any unavailability, and did not provide any communication. (Id.)

 

            No reply has been filed. Defendant has failed to address why the instant motion was filed on February 13, 2026, given that Plaintiff’s counsel sent correspondence to defense counsel on January 29, 2026, February 4, 2026, February 9, 2026, and February 18, 2026 to schedule the vehicle inspection. (Id., ¶¶ 6–14; Ex. 1 [emails dated January 29, 2026, February 4, 2026, February 9, 2026, and February 18, 2026].)

 

            Defendant’s motion to compel vehicle inspection is DENIED on the basis that Defendant’s meet-and-confer efforts are inadequate. (Code Civ. Proc., § 20310, subd. (b)(2).)

 

            Plaintiff requests that this Court award monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,650.00 (5.3 hours x $500.00). (Romero Decl., ¶¶ 14–16). The Court finds that sanctions are appropriate, and the amount requested is reasonable, except that the amount should be reduced by 0.8 hours for the anticipated time since no reply was filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).)

 

            Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED, IN PART, in the reduced amount of $2,250 (4.5 hours at $500/hr.).  Such amount to be paid by Defendant to Plaintiff's counsel within thirty days. 

 


Case Number: 26SMCV02048    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: O

TENTATIVE RULING

July 21, 2026

26SMCV02048 (LWK Partners LLC v. 629 Palisades LLC, et al.)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            On April 13, 2026, plaintiff LWK Partners LLC f/k/a LightWell Keystone I, LLC (Plaintiff) filed the complaint against defendant 629 Palisades, LLC, a California limited liability company (Defendant), and Does 1-10, alleging breach of contract and specific performance.

 

            Plaintiff alleges that it submitted a Residential Income Purchase Agreement and Joint Escrow Instructions to purchase real property located at 1233 14th Street, Santa Monica, California 90202 (the “Property”) from defendant 629 Palisades, LLC (“Defendant”). [See Request for Judicial Notice (“RJN”) at Exh. A at ¶¶ 1-2 (Pl.’s Compl.).] As relevant here, Lightwell Keystone I, LLC was organized in California and changed its name to LWK Partners LLC. [See RJN at Exhs. B-C (LightWell Keystone I, LLC’s Articles of Organization and Amendment of the Articles of Organization).]

           

INSTANT MOTION

 

            On May 20, 2026, Defendant filed the instant demurrer to the complaint. Plaintiff filed an opposition, and Defendant filed a reply.

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

            The Court finds that Defendant has satisfied the meet and confer requirement. (Shiri Declaration ¶ 3.)

 

REQUESTS FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

            Plaintiff’s requests for judicial notice (Ex. A-C [complaint, articles of incorporation, and article of organization filed with Secretary of State]) are granted. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d), (h).)

 

EVIDENTIARY OBJECTIONS

 

            The Court declines to rule on Plaintiff’s objections to the Shiri Declaration with respect to the substance of the letters. But the Court considers the exhibits to the extent that they demonstrate a meet-and-confer effort.

 

           

DISCUSSION

 

            Here, Defendant asserts the following: “Buyer/ Plaintiff LWK Partners LLC claims that they entered into a contract with Defendant, 629 Palisades, LLC for the sale of a property located at 1233 14" Street, Santa Monica, CA 90404 (the “Property”). Plaintiff claims there is a dually signed Residential Income Purchase Agreement (“RIPA’). If that was true why hasn't Plaintiff provided the RIPA in the complaint…. there was never a dually sign RIPA [Plaintiff] nor could there every be a legitimate dually signed contract with entity as the entity never existed, i.e. [Plaintiff] was never registered with the California Secretary of State ever, nor was it a registed LLC in any other state,” (Demurrer at p. 1.)

 

            In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the complaint alleges the parties entered into a written RIPA and began to perform until Defendant canceled the transaction without justification, causing Plaintiff at least $163,000.00 in damages. (Compl. ¶ 1-3.) Plaintiff contends that these allegations must be accepted as true at the pleading stage. The Court agrees. Defendant improperly attempts to resolve purported factual disputes rather than attack the sufficiency of the allegations in the complaint, which are accepted as true at this stage. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].)

 

            Plaintiff is also correct that the RIPA need not be attached to the complaint and that the case cited by Defendant, Consolidated World Investments, Inc. v. Lido Preferred Ltd. (1992) 9 Cal. App. 4th 373, 380, does not concern whether a contract must be attached to a Complaint. California law specifically permits a plaintiff suing on a written contract to plead the contract by legal effect rather than attaching the full contract. (Construction Protective Services, Inc. v. TIG Specialty Ins. Co. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 189, 198-199 [a plaintiff may “plead the legal effect of the contract rather than its precise language.”].)

 

            Lastly, on the issue of standing, Plaintiff states that Defendant again raises factual disputes unsuitable for resolution on demurrer and asserts that LWK Partners LLC is merely the updated name of Lightwell Keystone I, LLC, as specifically alleged in the complaint as “LWK Partners LLC f/k/a LightWell Keystone I, LLC.” (Compl. at p. 1.) On this issue, Plaintiff also provides Plaintiff’s Articles of Organization and Amendment of the Articles of Organization. (RJN Ex. B-C.)

 

            The Court is not persuaded by Defendant’s arguments in reply regarding the statute of frauds or Plaintiff’s standing. Defendant’s arguments do not change the fact that the allegations in the complaint are accepted as true at the pleading stage.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer is OVERRULED.  Defendant may have thirty (30) days in which to file and serve a response to the complaint.
