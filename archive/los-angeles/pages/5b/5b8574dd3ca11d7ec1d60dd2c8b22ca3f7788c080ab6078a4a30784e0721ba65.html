Case Number: 24STLC06155    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING: 

 

Plaintiff Alan Tyson Zorthian’s Motion to Vacate Order of Dismissal is denied.

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

On August 26, 2024, Plaintiff Alan Tyson Zorthian (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the instant action for breach of contract and common counts against Defendants Matthew Angrum and Andrea Angrum (“Defendants”). Following Defendants’ failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered their default on October 22, 2024. Default judgment was entered against Defendants on January 27, 2025. On May 28, 2025, the Court granted Defendants’ Motion to Vacate the Default and Default Judgment. (Minute Order, 05/28/25.)

 

Plaintiff filed a Motion for Summary Judgment or Adjudication, which the Court denied on November 18, 2025. (Minute Order, 11/18/25.) Defendants filed their trial brief on January 20, 2026 and Plaintiff filed his trial brief the following day. Trial was held on January 27, 2026, at which time the issues regarding collateral estoppel were heard and argued. (Minute Order, 01/27/26.) The Court took the matter under submission and dismissed the matter without prejudice after finding that collateral estoppel applied to the case from an earlier Small Claims judgment. (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Vacate Order of Dismissal on June 8, 2026. Defendants filed their opposition on June 22, 2026 and Plaintiff replied on June 29, 2026. 

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to vacate the judgment of dismissal entered by the trial court on January 27, 2026. The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivisions (b) and (d).

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d)

 

Plaintiff first argues that the judgment of dismissal must be vacated as void because he had no notice that the issue of collateral estoppel would be determined at trial. The record shows, however, that Plaintiff did have notice of the issue. Collateral estoppel was listed as an affirmative defense in Defendants’ Answers. (Answers, filed 11/27/24, ¶4.) The issue was raised again in Defendants’ trial brief, filed on January 20, 2026, which stated that, at trial, the Court will be asked to determine “whether Plaintiff's claims are barred or limited by the prior Small Claims judgment.” (Trial Brief, filed 01/20/26, § IV.) It is the first trial issue raised in the brief, and Defendants’ devote a full section to it. (Id. at § II.) Additionally, Plaintiff addressed the defenses, including collateral estoppel, albeit in one sentence, in his trial brief. (Trial Brief, filed 01/21/26, § IV, ¶C.) The parties then argued the issue before the trial court. (Minute Order, 01/27/26.) Therefore, the Court finds Plaintiff had notice that the issue would be raised.   

 

Plaintiff also argues that the trial court’s ruling was made without testimony, an identified record, or a noticed, dispositive motion. (Motion, p. 8:8-26.) Plaintiff provides no authority to find that the manner in which the trial judge handled the proceedings rendered the ruling void. In reply, Plaintiff defines “void” as where the court “lacked jurisdiction over the parties, lacked subject matter jurisdiction, or lacked authority to act” and relies on the lack of notice to argue the trial judge had no authority to act. (Citing Lee v. An (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 558, 563-565.) As discussed above, there is no basis to find Plaintiff lacked notice of the basis of the judgment. Therefore, the judgment of dismissal is not void or voidable.

 

 Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b)

 

Plaintiff alternatively moves for relief from the judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Relief under this statute must be sought within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

 

Plaintiff does not show through his declaration that the dismissal of judgment resulted from his mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  Plaintiff declares that he did not have notice that a dispositive theory would be heard at the trial call and that he believed that when the Court stated it would take the matter under submission, he would be able to file additional papers.  (Motion, Zorthian Decl., ¶¶9, 11.) As discussed above, the issue of collateral estoppel was an affirmative defense timely raised by Defendants in their Answer and identified as an issue for trial in Defendants’ trial brief.  Plaintiff’s failure to realize that the issue would be litigated at trial is therefore not excusable and not a basis to vacate the judgment.  Plaintiff’s unspoken expectation that he could file additional briefing after the Court took the issue under submission was not reasonable, and also not a basis to vacate judgment.  (See Index of Record Exhibits, Exh. 10 (“Transcript of Proceedings”) at 172-82.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Alan Tyson Zorthian’s Motion to Vacate Order of Dismissal is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC05444    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendants Shaun Ross and David Wallace’s Motion for Summary Judgment is denied without prejudice to bringing a motion in full compliance with the statutory requirements.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On July 15, 2025, Plaintiff Rommy Shy (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed this action for recovery of COVID-19 rental debt against Defendants Shaun Ross (“Defendant Ross”) and David Wallace (“Defendant Wallace”). Defendants, also in propria persona, filed Answers on August 29, 2025. On October 14, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Substitution of Attorney indicating he would be represented by Attorney Yoel Hanohov. A second Substitution of Attorney was then filed on November 5, 2026, switching Plaintiff back to a self-represented litigant, and listing their email address as [email protected].

 

On April 9, 2026, Defendants filed the instant Motion for Summary Judgment. The Motion was served on Plaintiff by electronic mail the day before. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move for summary judgment on the Complaint pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 437c. A defendant seeking summary judgment must show either (1) that one or more elements of the cause of action cannot be established; or (2) that there is a complete defense to that cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(2).) The plaintiff is under no evidentiary burden to produce rebuttal evidence until the moving defendant meets its initial moving burden. (Binder v. Aetna Life Insurance Company (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 832, 839-840.)

 

Initially, the Court addresses service of the Motion on Plaintiff by electronic mail. The email address to which the Motion was served was [email protected], which does not match the email address listed in Plaintiff’s Substitution of Attorney form. (Motion, p. 125.) Defendants, therefore, have not demonstrated service of the Motion to the correct address. Failure to give notice of a motion is not only a violation of the statutory requirements but of due process. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.) The Court cannot grant the Motion without a showing of proper service.

 

Second, under Code of Civil Procedure section 437c, a separate statement must be filed and served in support of, and in opposition to, the motion for summary judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subds. (b)(1), (b)(3).) This is echoed in the California Rules of Court. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(c) and (e).) The separate statements must plainly and concisely set forth “all material facts that the moving party contends are undisputed” and the opposing party’s response to each of the material facts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subds. (b)(1), (b)(3).) The moving separate statement of material facts must be set forth in a two-column format with facts on the left-hand side and evidence on the right-hand side. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(h).) Failure to include a separate statement of undisputed facts is grounds to deny a motion for summary judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (b)(1).)

 

Although Defendants’ Motion states that it is supported by a Separate Statement of Undisputed Material Facts, no such document is in the Court’s docket as having been filed or served upon Plaintiff. (See Notice of Motion, p. 2:13-14.) The moving memorandum of points and authorities includes a section that lists “undisputed facts,” but it does not comply with the formatting and evidentiary requirements. (Motion, pp. 7:20-8:21.) As the Court of Appeals has explained: “Separate statements are required not to satisfy a sadistic urge to torment lawyers, but rather to afford due process to opposing parties and to permit trial courts to expeditiously review complex motions for ... summary judgment to determine quickly and efficiently whether material facts are in dispute.” (Parkview Villas Assn., Inc. v. State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. (2005) 133 Cal.App.4th 1197, 1210, citing United Community Church v. Garcin (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 327, 335.)

 

Furthermore, it has long been the rule that self-represented litigants are held to the same standards as practicing attorneys. (Lombardi v. Citizens Nat. Trust etc. Bank (1955) 137 Cal.App.2d 206, 208–209.) The lack of a supporting separate statement for the instant Motion for Summary Judgment renders it so procedurally defective that the Court cannot evaluate the parties’ arguments in the manner dictated by the Code of Civil Procedure and the California Rules of Court.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendants Shaun Ross and David Wallace’s Motion for Summary Judgment is denied without prejudice to bringing a motion in full compliance with the statutory requirements.

Case Number: 25STLC07790    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Defendant Deandre Parks aka Deandre Parks, Sr. aka Deandre Lamont Parks, Sr. is ordered to serve responses to the Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s requests for sanctions are denied.

 

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) served Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, on Defendant Deandre Parks aka Deandre Parks, Sr. aka Deandre Lamont Parks, Sr. (“Defendant”) on October 13, 2025. (Motions, Freed Decl., Exh. 1.) Despite a meet and confer effort, to date, Defendant has not served verified responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶¶4-5 and Exh. 2) Plaintiff filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions on May 28, 2026. The Motions were served on Defendant through Attorney Lynda Jacobs, Esq. by electronic and first-class mail the day before. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Further, the motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Based on Defendant’s failure to timely respond to the propounded discovery, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling service of verified responses to the discovery requests without objections.

 

Plaintiff moves for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030. In City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, the Supreme Court observed:

 

It is already well-established that a court may not rely on [Code of Civil Procedure] section 2023.030 to override the limitations prescribed by any other applicable sanctions provision in the [Civil Discovery] Act. A court may invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 only when confronted with an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision. And where it invokes that authority, it is constrained by the long-settled rules generally governing the imposition of discovery sanctions under the Act.

 

(City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 74-75.) The moving statutes, Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300, only make sanctions available when a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); § 2031.300, subd. (c).) Defendant filed no opposition to the instant Motions and cannot be sanctioned under sections 2030.290 or 2031.310.

 

Nor has Plaintiff shown that Defendant’s failure to serve discovery responses is “an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision.” Therefore, the Court declines to invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Defendant Deandre Parks aka Deandre Parks, Sr. aka Deandre Lamont Parks, Sr. is ordered to serve responses to the Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s requests for sanctions are denied.

Case Number: 25STLC08173    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant U S Pacific
Construction, Inc.’s Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is
denied.

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On October 27, 2025, Plaintiff Creditors
Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant collections action
against Defendant U S Pacific Construction, Inc. (“Defendant”). Following
Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered its
default on March 26, 2026.

 

On April 27, 2026, Defendant
filed a Substitution of Attorney, which was its first filing in the case.
Plaintiff filed its Request for Default Judgment two days later. Defendant then
filed an Objection to Plaintiff’s Request for Court Judgment on May 1, 2026 and
a Cross-Complaint on May 5, 2026.

 

Plaintiff’s Request for Default
Judgment was entered by the Court on May 7, 2026. Defendant then filed the
instant Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment on May 11, 2026.
Plaintiff filed its opposition on June 22, 2026, to which Defendant replied on
June 23, 2026. Defendant filed an Answer on June 24, 2026 and a supplemental
brief and declaration on June 26, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment as Void and/or
Voidable

 

Defendant first moves to vacate
the default judgment as void or voidable because it was entered after Defendant
appeared in this action. Code of Civil Procedure section 473,
subdivision (d) provides that “[t]he court may, .... on motion of either party
after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code
Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) In California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn
(2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, the California Supreme Court held that there is no
two-year limitation to challenge a void default judgment, regardless of whether
it is valid on its face. (California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn (2024) 17
Cal.5th 207, 225-226.) A motion for relief under Code of Civil Procedure
section 473, subdivision (d), therefore, can be brought at any time.

 

In support of its request
to find the default judgment void, the Motion points to Code of Civil Procedure
section 585 for the proposition that default judgment could not have been
entered after Defendant filed a cross-complaint. Section 585, however,
specifically delineates the filings by which a defendant can prevent a default
judgment, and a cross-complaint is not one of those filings. Instead, the
statute refers to:

 

Answer,
demurrer, notice of motion to strike of the character specified in subdivision
(f), notice of motion to transfer pursuant to Section 396b, notice of motion to
dismiss pursuant to Article 2 (commencing with Section 583.210) of Chapter 1.5
of Title 8, notice of motion to quash service of summons or to stay or dismiss
the action pursuant to Section 418.10, or notice of the filing of a petition
for writ of mandate as provided in Section 418.10 . . . .

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 585,
subd. (a).) Defendant cites no authority for expanding this list to
cross-complaints.  To the contrary, courts
have held that a party in default may not file a cross-complaint.  (See Dakota Payphone, LLC v. Alcaraz (2011)
192 Cal. App. 4th 493, 502 (“Knowing that he was in default, [defendant] should
also have known that he could not file a cross-complaint,” and collecting
cases).)    

 

Vacate
Default and Default Judgment Due to Mistake, Inadvertence, Surprise, or Excusable
Neglect

 

Defendant also argues
briefly that the default and default judgment should be vacated on account of
Defendant’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  (Mot. at 9.) 
Relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) must be sought within a reasonable time, no more than six
months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be
accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s mistake,
inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b);
English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

 

The Motion was timely
filed less than two months after entry of default, and is accompanied by a copy
of Defendant’s proposed Answer. (Motion, Liu Decl., Exh. G.)  The moving papers, however, do not establish any
basis for relief under §473(b).  The moving
papers attach the declaration of Defendant’s CEO, Tiezhu Liu, but the
declaration does not address mistake or excusable neglect.  (Mot.. Liu Decl., ¶¶1-10.)   Liu’s
declaration in support of the Reply brief states that, upon being served with
this action, Liu sent a settlement proposal to Plaintiff’s counsel on February
9, 2026, and asked for an extension of time to respond, and that Plaintiff did
not agree to settle. (Reply, Liu Decl., ¶¶3-6 and Exh. A.)  The declarations do not include information
sufficient for the Court to find that Defendant’s failure to respond was
excusable.  They establish only that Defendant
took some affirmative action in the case in February, and then nonetheless did
not appear in the case until April 29, 2026. 
The gap is unexplained.  Without
any explanation for the delay, the Court has no basis to determine that it was
the result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect, as opposed
to Defendant’s inexcusable neglect of this action.

 

Supplemental Brief and
Declaration

 

Defendant filed an unauthorized
supplemental brief and supporting declaration on June 26, 2026, in an attempt
to add a new argument in support of the Motion to Vacate. Not only is filing a
supplemental brief without leave not authorized by the Code of Civil Procedure,
it also violates Plaintiff’s right to due process by depriving it of timely
notice of the basis of the Motion. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1005, 1010.)  The Court will not consider it.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant
U S Pacific Construction, Inc.’s Motion to Set Aside Default and Default
Judgment is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC00627    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Malwarebytes, Inc.’s Motion to Require Plaintiff to Furnish Security is denied.

 

Analysis:

 

On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Malwarebytes, Inc. (“Defendant”). Defendant filed its Answer on March 9, 2026. On March 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed (1) the Application for Leave to File Memorandum of Points and Authorities in Excess of Page Limit; (2) the Demurrer to Answer; and (3) the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings. On April 22, 2026, the Court sustained the Demurrer to the Answer with leave to amend as to the fifth through ninth and twenty-second affirmative defenses, and otherwise overruled the Demurrer. (Minute Order, 04/22/26.) Defendant filed its Amended Answer on May 12, 2026.

 

On May 14, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion to Require Plaintiff to Furnish Security. Plaintiff filed an opposition and objection on June 24, 2026, and Defendant replied on June 29, 2026. The opposition was untimely filed and served only eight court days before the hearing, however, the Court finds Defendant provided a substantive reply and was not prejudiced by the delay. The Court will consider all the papers.

 

Discussion

 

Request for Judicial Notice and Evidentiary Objections

 

Defendant asks the Court to take judicial notice of the following filings:

 

(1) May 4, 2026 Court Order in Tsang Han Wang v. Electronic Waveform Lab, Inc., Los Angeles Superior Court, Case No. 26STLC00659, re: Ruling on Submitted Matter Denying Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s Discovery Motions; (2) Sustaining, in Part, Overruling, in Part, Defendant Electronic Waveform Lab, Inc.’s Demurrers to Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s Complaint; (3) Granting Defendant Electronic Waveform Lab, Inc.’s Motion to Declare Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang a Vexatious Litigant;

 

(2) February 2, 2026 Complaint in Tsang Han Wang v. Electronic Waveform Lab, Inc., Los Angeles Superior Court, Case No. 26STLC00659; and

 

(3) May 5, 2026 Court Order in Tsang Han Wang v. The Superior Court of the State of California for the County of Los Angeles, Appellate Division of the Los Angeles Superior Court, Case No. 26APCP00087, denying Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s Writ Petition.

 

The Court takes judicial notice of these filings as to the fact of their existence and the orders made therein. The Court does not take judicial notice of the hearsay statements. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d); Sosinsky v. Grant (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1548, 1564–1565.) Plaintiff’s evidentiary objections to the Bustos declaration in support of Defendant’s Motion are overruled.

 

Order to Post Security

 

A defendant may move the court “for an order requiring the plaintiff to furnish security shall be based upon the ground, and supported by a showing, [1] that the plaintiff is a vexatious litigant and [2] that there is not a reasonable probability that he or she will prevail in the litigation against the moving defendant.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.1.) “If the court finds in the defendant's favor on these points, it orders the plaintiff to furnish security in an amount fixed by the court. (§ 391.3, subd. (a).) Failure to provide the security is grounds for dismissal. (§ 391.4.)” (In re Marriage of Rifkin and Carty (2015) 234 Cal.App.4th 1339, 1345.) “At the hearing upon the motion the court shall consider any evidence, written or oral, by witnesses or affidavit, as may be material to the ground of the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.2.) Furthermore, a motion brought under section 391.1 prior to trial stays the litigation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.6)

 

On May 4, 2026, in Tsang Han Wang v. Electronic Waveform Lab, Inc., Los Angeles Superior Court, Case No. 26STLC00659 (the “Waveform Lab case”) the Court ruled that Plaintiff met the definition of a vexatious litigant under Code of Civil Procedure section 391, subdivisions (b)(1) and (b)(3). (Motion, RJN, Exh. A, pp. 13-15.) Although Plaintiff argues that the vexatious litigant statute does not allow the Court to impose security on a plaintiff who was not deemed vexatious at the time the case was filed, the opposition cites no authority for such a limitation. (Opp., p. 7:20-21.) As Defendant points out, the security statute allows any defendant to seek an order furnishing security, which necessarily includes defendants in concurrent cases regardless of when they were filed. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 391, subd. (b)(4).) The first basis for an order of security is satisfied based on the Court’s ruling in the Waveform Lab case. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 391(b)(4).)

 

The Court does not find the second grounds for a security satisfied, as to whether Plaintiff has a reasonable probability of prevailing on the merits of this action.  The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: (1) Pre-Employment Retaliation for Complaints of Unlawful Testing (Labor Code § 98.6); (2) Pre-Employment Retaliation under FEHA (Gov’t Code § 12940(h)); and (3) Ultra Vires Acts Exceeding Corporate Authority. Defendant points to the Complaint in the Waveform Lab case, which asserts identical claims, and where the Court found no reasonable probability of prevailing.

 

As explained below, Defendant’s invocation of the Waveform Lab decision is sufficient to address Counts One and Two but not Count Three.  In Waveform Lab, as to Count One, brought under Labor Code section 98.6, the Court found that alleged violations of regulations promulgated under the FEHA were inapplicable as a matter of law. (Motion, RJN, Exh. A, pp. 5-6.)  That legal determination is equally applicable here.  Count One of the Complaint in this case likewise relies on alleged violations under FEHA which are not a basis for relief under Labor Code § 98.6.  (See Complaint, ¶¶ 85, 88, 89; Grinzi v. San Diego Hospice Corp. (2004) 120 Cal. App. 4th 72, 87 (“the Legislature . . .  intended the phrase, “any rights,” in the final portion of section 98.6, subdivision (a), to similarly refer to rights “otherwise protected by the Labor Code.”).)  Because the allegations are insufficient as a matter of law, Plaintiff is not reasonably likely to success on that claim.  Plaintiff does not argue to the contrary in his opposition.  (See Opp. at 14.) 

 

As to Count Three in Waveform Lab, the Court found Plaintiff’s cause of action for ultra vires corporate activity, which is also brought in this action on the same grounds, was unsupportable as pled because no California authority has recognized that a job applicant can bring such a claim. (Motion, RJN, Exh. A, pp. 8-9; see Cal. Corp. Code, § 208(a) (limiting the ability to bring such a claim.)  Again, Plaintiff does not defend this cause of action in his opposition. 

 

As to Count Two in Waveform Lab, for FEHA pre-employment retaliation, the Court determined Plaintiff was not reasonably likely to prevail based on weighing the evidence in the record in that case. The Court concluded that Plaintiff could not prove the survey he was required to take was a prohibited “medical or psychological’ examination or inquiry” within the meaning of California Code of Regulations, Title 2, § 11071 or that he had a good-faith believe that the survey was discriminatory. (Id. at Exh. A, p. 17.) Among other things, the Court concluded that it did not appear that Plaintiff could establish that the examination was performed by a health care provider, and therefore it was not a prohibited “examination” (2 CCR § 11065(m)); nor did it appear that Plaintiff could establish that it  was a medical or psychological “inquiry” based on the attachments to the Complaint.  (Id. at Exh. A, p. 17.)  Further the Court found that Plaintiff was not reasonably likely to be able to show that he had a good faith belief that the survey was discriminatory because he had not seen the questions, and had, based on matters submitted for judicial notice in that case, engaged in a pattern of filing similar cases against a variety of employers. (Id. at Exh. A, pp. 17-18.)

 

As Plaintiff argues, the Court’s factual findings based on evidence in Waveform Lab cannot simply be transported into this case.  (Opp. at 15.)  As discussed above, Counts One and Three are deficient as a matter of law, and Plaintiff offered nothing to support a contrary conclusion.  As to Count Two, however, the Court relied on matters submitted for judicial notice and the specific attachments to the Complaint in that action, which included a screenshot of the test interface used by the Defendant in that case.  The same materials are not before the Court here.  For example, Defendant in this action did not ask the Court to take judicial notice of other strikingly similar cases filed by Plaintiff to support their contention that he is unlikely to be able to demonstrate that he acted in good faith.  (See Exh. A, pp. 17 (taking notice for that purpose.)  The Complaint in his case attaches generally available information off the internet about the Predictive Index, but not the page that Plaintiff was allegedly directed to by Defendant.  The Court finds the record here comparable to that in Muller v. Tanner  (1969) 2 Cal.App.3d 445, 464–465, cited by Plaintiff.  (See Opp. at 15.)  There, the court reversed the order for security where “[t]he only evidence on this point is the complaint itself and the conclusion, expressed in the statutory language, in defendant’s affidavit.”  (Id. at 464.) Of course, here, the Court took judicial notice of the Court’s decision in Waveform Lab, but that notice does not extend to the truth of the Court’s findings in that case, or prove by fiat that they apply in this case.  (See Steed v. Department of Consumer Affairs, 204 Cal.App.4th 112 (2012), (“A court may take judicial notice of (another) court’s action, but may not use it to prove the truth of the facts found and recited.”).  Defendant’s reference to the Complaint and the Court’s decision in another case was not enough to meet its burden where matters of fact are at issue.

 

Accordingly, the Court finds that Defendant has not met its burden to show no reasonable likelihood of success on the merits.  Defendant is a vexatious litigant but Defendant has not demonstrated that a security should be required.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Malwarebytes, Inc.’s Motion to Require Plaintiff to Furnish Security is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC00871    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

Case Number: 26STLC00941    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s (1) Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings; and (2) Demurrer to Answer, are stayed.

 

The action is stayed until disposition of Defendant Balaciano Group’s Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security Pursuant to CCP § 391.1.

 

Status Conference Re: Stay is scheduled for 10/13/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On February 11, 2026, Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Balaciano Group (“Defendant”). The Complaint alleges causes of action for (1) Pre-Employment Retaliation in Violation of FEHA (Government Code § 12940(h)); (2) Pre-Employment Retaliation in Violation of California Labor Code Section 98.6 (Labor Code § 98.6 with SB 497 Rebuttable Presumption); and (3) Ultra Vires Acts Exceeding Corporate Authority.

 

On April 6, 2026, Defendant filed its Answer to the Complaint; Plaintiff filed the instant Demurrer to Answer on April 13, 2026. Concurrent with the Demurrer, Plaintiff filed a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings set to be heard on July 7, 2026. Defendant filed an opposition to the Demurrer on April 30, 2026 and Plaintiff replied on May 4, 2026. Defendant filed an opposition to evidentiary objections and an objection to Plaintiff’s request for judicial notice on May 8, 2026.

 

The Demurrer to Answer originally came for hearing on May 13, 2026, at which time the Court continued the matter to July 7, 2026. (Minute Order, 05/13/26.) The Court addressed Defendant’s opposition request that Plaintiff be ordered to post security and found it premature. (Ibid.) If Defendant filed a motion for Plaintiff to furnish security, the Court ruled, it would hear and determine that motion before reaching the merits of the Demurrer to Answer and the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, or any other pending motions. (Ibid.)

 

On June 23, 2026, Defendant filed a Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security Pursuant to CCP § 391.1, which is set for hearing on September 29, 2026. As the Court previously noted, Code of Civil Procedure section 391.6 expressly provides that “when a motion pursuant to Section 391.1 is filed prior to trial the litigation is stayed … until 10 days after the motion shall have been denied, or if granted, until 10 days after the required security has been furnished and the moving defendant given written notice thereof.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.6.) Accordingly, the Court stays the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings and Demurrer to Answer, and the litigation as a whole, until disposition of the Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s (1) Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings; and (2) Demurrer to Answer, are stayed.

 

The action is stayed until disposition of Defendant Balaciano Group’s Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post Security Pursuant to CCP § 391.1.

 

Status Conference Re: Stay is scheduled for 10/13/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: LAM14K02141    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Anthony Daetwan Smith’s Motion to Pay Judgment in Installments is placed off calendar.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On February 7, 2014, Plaintiff Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Anthony Daetwan Smith (“Defendant Smith”) and Angela Ronita Butler (“Defendant Butler”). Defendants filed an Answer on April 1, 2014, and on June 30, 2015, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case. In accordance with that settlement, the Court dismissed the action on August 7, 2015, but retained jurisdiction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. (Judgment Order of Dismissal, 08/07/25.) On November 30, 2015, the Court entered judgment against Defendant Smith. (Judgment 11/30/15.)

 

The Court renewed the judgment on October 30, 2025. On April 3, 2025, Defendant Smith filed the instant Motion for Order to Pay Judgment in Installments. However, the Motion does not include a proof of service showing it was served on Plaintiff, or that Plaintiff was given notice of the hearing date. Failure to give notice of the papers and hearing date is a violation of due process and the statutory notice requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.) Furthermore, Plaintiff then filed an Acknowledgment of Full Satisfaction of Judgment on June 15, 2026, rendering the relief sought in the Motion moot.

 

Defendant Anthony Daetwan Smith’s Motion to Pay Judgment in Installments, therefore, is placed off calendar.
