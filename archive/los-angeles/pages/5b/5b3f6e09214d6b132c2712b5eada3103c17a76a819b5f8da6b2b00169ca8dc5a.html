Case Number: 24PSCV01989    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  Brandon Diaz v. Memito Trucking, Inc, et al. 

Motion of Jeffrey Y. Choi and Mollyanne Wincek of Snyder Burnett Egerer, LLP to be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant Luis Gerardo Santa Maria 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court GRANTS the Motion of Jeffrey Y. Choi and Mollyanne Wincek of Snyder Burnett Egerer, LLP to be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant Luis Gerardo Santa Maria. The Court will sign the proposed order. Counsel must then serve the signed order on Defendant Luis Gerardo Santa Maria and file proof of service of same within five calendar days of this order. 

             Counsel is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is an auto accident case. On June 20, 2024, plaintiff Brandon Diaz (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendants Memito Trucking, Inc., Luis Gerardo Camou Santa Maria[1] (collectively, Defendants) and Does 1 to 50, alleging causes of action for general negligence and motor vehicle. 

On June 23, 2026, Jeffrey Y. Choi and Mollyanne Wincek of Snyder Burnett Egerer, LLP moved to be relieved as counsel for Defendant Luis Gerardo Camou Santa Maria. The Motion is unopposed. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

The Court has discretion to allow an attorney to withdraw, and such a motion should be granted, provided that there is no prejudice to the client, and it does not disrupt the orderly process of justice. (See Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915; People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 403-407.)

A motion to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Council Form MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion), MC-052 (Declaration), and MC-053 (Proposed Order). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) The requisite forms must be served “on the client and on all parties that have appeared in the case.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d).) 

DISCUSSION 

            Jeffrey Y. Choi and Mollyanne Wincek of Snyder Burnet Egerer, LLP (collectively, Counsel) seek to be relieved as counsel for Defendant Luis Gerardo Santa Maria on the grounds that they have last all contact with their client. (Choi/Wyncek Decl., ¶ 2.) A breakdown in the attorney-client relationship is grounds for allowing the attorney to withdraw. (Estate of Falco (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004, 1014.) Grounds for permitting an attorney to withdraw from representation also include the client’s conduct that, “renders it unreasonably difficult for the lawyer to carry out the representation effectively[.]” (Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16, subd. (b)(4).) 

Counsel has also submitted the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on Judicial Council forms MC-051, MC-052, and MC-053. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) Proofs of service are attached to each document as well. 

            The Court finds good cause exists. Accordingly, the Court GRANTS the Motion. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court GRANTS the Motion of Jeffrey Y. Choi and Mollyanne Wincek of Snyder Burnett Egerer, LLP to be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant Luis Gerardo Santa Maria. The Court will sign the proposed order. Counsel must then serve the signed order on Defendant Luis Gerardo Santa Maria and file proof of service of same within five calendar days of this order. 

             Counsel is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order.


[1] The Complaint spells Defendant Santamaria’s last name as “Santamaria.” (Compl., ¶ 1.) The Court assumes the Complaint’s spelling was a typo and that the correct spelling is “Santa Maria.”

Case Number: 25PSCV00293    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  Ke Jiang v. Longzhu Wang 

Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang’s Motion for an Order Expunging Lis Pendens’ [sic] and for Attorneys’ Fees and Costs in the Amount of $13,860 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court GRANTS in part Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang’s Motion for an Order Expunging Lis Pendens’ [sic] and for Attorneys’ Fees and Costs. Cross-Complainant Longzhu Wang’s lis pendens recorded against that certain real property located at 15713 Dimity Avenue, Chino Hills, California 91709, is/are hereby EXPUNGED. Cross-Complainant Wang must record a notice of said expungement and file proof of same with the Court within 30 calendar days of the Court’s order. 

Cross-Complainant Longzhu Wang and Wang’s Counsel of Record must further pay Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang’s  attorney fees and costs in the amount of $3,060.00 to Jiang’s counsel within 30 calendar days of the Court’s order. 

             Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is a business dispute. On January 27, 2025, plaintiffs Ke Jiang and Wanvo Holdings Inc. filed this action against defendant Longzhu Wang (Defendant or Wang) and Does 1 through 50, alleging causes of action for breach of contract, assault, and intentional infliction of emotional distress. On April 17, 2025, plaintiff Ke Jiang (Jiang) filed the operative First Amended Complaint (FAC) against defendants Longzhu Wang, Chengdu Duchengnanzhuang Fushi Co., and Does 1 through 50, alleging causes of action for breach of contract, intentional infliction of emotional distress, and specific performance. 

On September 3, 2025, after the Court sustained Wang’s demurrer to the FAC, Plaintiff filed the operative Second Amended Complaint (SAC) against Wang and Does 1 through 50, alleging causes of action for breach of contract and intentional infliction of emotional distress. 

On November 17, 2025, after the Court sustained Wang’s demurrer to the SAC, Plaintiff filed the operative Third Amended Complaint (TAC) against Wang and Does 1 through 50, alleging one cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress. 

On May 29, 2025, cross-complainant Longzhu Wang (Wang or Cross-Complainant) filed a cross-complaint against Ke Jiang (Jiang), Bao Yan Guo (Guo), Wanvo Holdings Inc. (collectively, Cross-Defendants), and Does 1 through 50, alleging causes of action for breach of contract, fraud/intentional misrepresentation, constructive fraud, money had and received, sexual battery, battery, false imprisonment, intentional infliction of emotional distress, the Bane Act violation, and imposition of constructive trust. 

On June 20, 2026, Cross-Defendant Jiang moved to expunge Cross-Complainant Wang’s lis pendens. On July 27, 2026, Wang opposed the Motion. Jiang did not reply to Wang’s Opposition. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

At any time after notice of pendency of action has been recorded, any party, or any nonparty with an interest in the real property affected thereby, may apply to the court in which the action is pending to expunge the notice. However, a person who is not a party to the action shall obtain leave to intervene from the court at or before the time the party brings the motion to expunge the notice. Evidence or declarations may be filed with the motion to expunge the notice. The court may permit evidence to be received in the form of oral testimony, and may make any orders it deems just to provide for discovery by any party affected by a motion to expunge the notice. The claimant shall have the burden of proof under Sections 405.31 and 405.32. 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 405.30.) 

“In proceedings under this chapter, the court shall order the notice expunged if the court finds that the pleading on which the notice is based does not contain a real property claim. The court shall not order an undertaking to be given as a condition of expunging the notice where the court finds the pleading does not contain a real property claim.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.31.) 

PRELIMINARY ISSUES 

            Jiang’s Motion cites an unpublished trial court decision, namely Pawnee Leasing Corp. v. Island Pac. Hayward, 2018 Cal. Super. LEXIS 50164. (Motion, 6:15-16.) This is improper. (See Aguirre v. Amscan Holdings, Inc. (2015) 234 Cal.App.4th 1290, 1298, fn. 5.) The Court admonishes Jiang to cite proper cases going forward. 

            Wang’s Opposition was untimely filed on July 27, 2026, when the deadline was July 24, 2026. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).) The Court will still consider Wang’s Opposition, but admonishes Wang to comply with the Code of Civil Procedure going forward. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300, subd. (d); Juarez v. Wash Depot Holdings, Inc. (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 1197, 1202.)     

DISCUSSION 

Summary of Arguments 

Cross-Defendant Jiang moves to expunge Cross-Complainant Wang’s lis pendens recorded against Jiang’s real property located at 15713 Dimity Avenue, Chino Hills, California 91709 (the Property). Jiang argues that a lis pendens may be recorded only by a party asserting a “real property claim,” i.e., one that, if meritorious, would affect title to or the right to possession of specific real property under Code of Civil Procedure sections 405.4, 405.20, and 405.31, and that the claimant bears the burden of showing the pleading contains such a claim. Jiang contends Wang’s Cross-Complaint alleges only that Jiang fraudulently obtained roughly $600,000 and used those funds to buy the Property, which under Lewis, Urez, and Shoker is a claim for money that serves merely as a collateral means to secure a debt and cannot support a lis pendens. Jiang points to paragraph 92 of the Cross-Complaint, which requests for a constructive trust “to the extent of the funds wrongfully taken from her” as showing Wang seeks money rather than the Property. Jiang contends that Wang never owned, occupied, or held title to the parcel, and argues her remedy lies in attachment law, not a lis pendens.  Jiang further argues that section 405.38 directs the Court to award the prevailing party its reasonable fees and costs, and that fees are just here because he attempted to meet and confer and Wang declined to withdraw the lis pendens. Jiang requests $13,860 in attorney fees and costs. 

In opposition, Wang argues that because Jiang moved solely under section 405.31, review is limited to a demurrer-like analysis of the pleading under Kirkeby, and that Jiang improperly asks the Court to look past the allegations to find a debt-collection purpose. Wang contends the Cross-Complaint identifies the specific parcel, alleges that fraudulently obtained funds (including $300,000 paid for an undelivered 60% interest in Wanvo) were traced into its purchase, alleges Jiang and Guo hold title as involuntary trustees under Civil Code section 2224, and seeks a declaration that title is held in trust for Wang’s benefit, which if meritorious would affect title and satisfy section 405.4. Wang argues that Lewis and Shoker do not permit a court to disregard express title allegations, that Lewis never reconciled section 2224’s tracing principles with section 405.4’s “if meritorious” standard, and that the California Supreme Court has twice declined to decide whether a constructive-trust claim may support a lis pendens. On fees, Wang argues that a denied motion leaves Jiang without prevailing-party status under section 405.38, and that in any event Wang acted with substantial justification given the unresolved doctrinal question and the alleged risk of transfer of the property. Wang further contends the $13,860 demand is excessive for a five-page motion, that rests on duplicative and clerical billing and not-yet-incurred anticipated hours, and is supported by declarations executed March 6, 2026 (months before the July 2026 motion), and a claimed $60 fee contradicted by the court's $0.00 "NOFEE" receipt. Wang contends Jiang improperly seeks fees against counsel without statutory authority. 

Analysis – Lis Pendens 

The Court finds the Cross-Complaint fails to allege facts supporting Wang’s maintenance of the lis pendens on the Property. In a motion to expunge lis pendens under Code of Civil Procedure section 405.31, the trial court engages in a “demurrer-like analysis” limited to “whether the pleading states a real property claim.” (Kirkeby v. Superior Ct. (2004) 33 Cal.4th 642, 647-648 (Kirkeby).) Jiang brought this Motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 405.31, so the “demurrer-like analysis” applies to this Motion. (See ibid.; see generally, Motion.) 

“’A lis pendens may be filed by any party in an action who asserts a ‘real property claim.’ [Citation.]” (Newell v. Superior Ct. of Los Angeles Cnty. (2024) 107 Cal.App.5th 728, 734 (Newell).) “The party opposing a motion to expunge has the burden of showing the existence of a real property claim. [Citation.]” (Id. at p. 735.) “’Real property claim’ means the cause or causes of action in a pleading which would, if meritorious, affect (a) title to, or the right to possession of, specific real property…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.4; Newell, supra, 107 Cal.App.5th at p. 735.) 

Wang’s lis pendens is predicated on the Tenth Cause of Action for imposition of constructive trust. (Cross-Compl., ¶¶ 85-93.) There are two subtypes of constructive trust claims: (1) “[those] that are akin to fraudulent conveyance claims because they seek a constructive trust on real property that was itself wrongfully taken and, if successful on the merits, plaintiffs would regain title to or possession of the same property”; and (2) “[those] seeking a constructive trust or equitable lien on different property, merely as a means to secure collection of a debt”. (Shoker v. Superior Ct. of Alameda Cnty. (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 271, 282–283 (Shoker).) The latter subtype cannot support a lis pendens. (See id. at p. 283.) “The fact that someone buys property with stolen money does not make the victim the owner of that property as a matter of real property law. It merely entitles the victim to pursue the thief and to recover a money judgment, perhaps seeking to attach the property along the way…” (Lewis v. Superior Ct. (1994) 30 Cal.App.4th 1850, 1863 (Lewis).) No real property claim exists where the complaint only alleges that the defendant wrongfully took the plaintiff’s money and used it to buy, i.e., not convey, the property. (Id. at p. 1865.) 

Here, the Cross-Complaint expressly alleges that Cross-Defendants Jiang and Guo “misappropriated the money to purchase the residential property located at 15713 Dimity Avenue, Chino Hills, California 91709, which they currently occupy as their primary residence.” (Cross-Compl., ¶ 89.) This is precisely the subtype of constructive trust claim that the law says is not a real property claim and therefore fails to support maintaining a lis pendens. (See Shoker, supra, 81 Cal.App.5th at pp. 282–283; Lewis, supra, 30 Cal.App.4th at p. 1863.) The Cross-Complaint also alleges that it seeks a constructive trust “to the extent of the funds wrongfully taken from [Plaintiff].” (Cross-Compl., ¶ 92.) This is one of the exact same problems addressed in Lewis. The claimant claimed an “interest in the property ‘only to the extent the monies it alleges were wrongfully obtained have been invested therein’”. The Court of Appeal found this undermined the claimant’s lis pendens. (Lewis, supra, 30 Cal.App.4th at p. 1865.) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the Motion. Cross-Complainant Wang’s lis pendens is/are hereby EXPUNGED. Cross-Complainant Wang must record a notice of expungement of all lis pendens within 15 calendar days of this order. 

Analysis – Attorney Fees and Costs 

With respect to attorney fees and costs, Jiang is the prevailing party and therefore entitled to attorney fees and costs unless the Court finds Wang acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make their imposition unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.38; Castro v. Superior Ct. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 1010, 1018.) The Court finds Wang did not act with substantial justification and that there are no other circumstances make their imposition unjust. As discussed above, the allegations in the Cross-Complaint make it abundantly clear that Wang’s constructive trust claim is one for money and not for real property, which the law says does not support the maintenance of a lis pendens. 

Nevertheless, the Court finds Jiang’s counsel’s claimed attorney fees excessive. The Court finds that 15 hours on a seven-page motion and two short declarations with one exhibit attached is excessive and unreasonable. (See generally Castaneda Decl., Musto Decl.) As for the $60.00 filing fee, the CRS reservation receipts do not always show the fee being charged at the time the reservation is made. The Motion would not have been scheduled for hearing if Jiang had not paid the filing fee. The Court awards Jiang $3,060.00 in attorney fees and costs against Wang and Wang’s counsel, jointly and severally, comprised of 5.0 hours preparing the Motion and 1.0 hour appearing at the hearing on this Motion, multiplied by a $500.00 hourly rate, plus the $60.00 filing fee. Wang and Wang’s counsel must pay said attorney fees and costs to Jiang’s counsel within 30 calendar days of the Court’s order. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court GRANTS in part Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang’s Motion for an Order Expunging Lis Pendens’ [sic] and for Attorneys’ Fees and Costs. Cross-Complainant Longzhu Wang’s lis pendens recorded against that certain real property located at 15713 Dimity Avenue, Chino Hills, California 91709, is/are hereby EXPUNGED. Cross-Complainant Wang must record a notice of said expungement and file proof of same with the Court within 30 calendar days of the Court’s order. 

Cross-Complainant Longzhu Wang and Wang’s Counsel of Record must further pay Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang’s attorney fees and costs in the amount of $3,060.00 to Jiang’s counsel within 30 calendar days of the Court’s order. 

            Cross-Defendant Ke Jiang is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order.

Case Number: 25PSCV04322    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME: Crystal Lynne Saxton v. The Calvo Family Housing Limited Partnership, et al. 

Motion to Substitute Fawntaine La France Saxton, Laronda Jeanette Whitaker, Rondal Van Saxton, Jr., and Roshawn Anthony Saxton for Deceased Plaintiff Crystal Lynn Saxton as Successors in Interest 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court GRANTS the Motion to Substitute Fawntaine La France Saxton, Laronda Jeanette Whitaker, Rondal Van Saxton, Jr., and Roshawn Anthony Saxton for Deceased Plaintiff Crystal Lynn Saxton as Successors in Interest. Moving Parties are hereby substituted in as the Successors in Interest for Plaintiff under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.31. 

             Moving Parties are ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is a premises liability case. On December 2, 2025, plaintiff Crystal Lynne Saxton (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendants The Calvo Family Housing Limited Partnership, Paul Calvo, Peggy Calvo (collectively, Defendants), and Does 1 through 50, alleging causes of action for negligence and premises liability. 

On May 28, 2026, Fawntaine La France Saxton, Laronda Jeanette Whitaker, Rondal Van Saxton, Jr., and Roshawn Anthony Saxton (collectively, Moving Parties) filed a motion to be substituted as successors in interest for deceased Plaintiff. The Motion is unopposed. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

“On motion after the death of a person who commenced an action or proceeding, the court shall allow a pending action or proceeding that does not abate to be continued by the decedent's personal representative or, if none, by the decedent's successor in interest.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.31.) 

“(a) The person who seeks to commence an action or proceeding or to continue a pending action or proceeding as the decedent’s successor in interest under this article, shall execute and file an affidavit or a declaration under penalty of perjury under the laws of this state stating all of the following: 

(1) The decedent's name. 

(2) The date and place of the decedent's death. 

(3) “No proceeding is now pending in California for administration of the decedent's estate.” 

(4) If the decedent's estate was administered, a copy of the final order showing the distribution of the decedent's cause of action to the successor in interest. 

(5) Either of the following, as appropriate, with facts in support thereof: 

(A) “The affiant or declarant is the decedent's successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) and succeeds to the decedent's interest in the action or proceeding.” 

(B) “The affiant or declarant is authorized to act on behalf of the decedent's successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) with respect to the decedent's interest in the action or proceeding.” 

(6) “No other person has a superior right to commence the action or proceeding or to be substituted for the decedent in the pending action or proceeding.” 

(7) “The affiant or declarant affirms or declares under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct.” 

(b) Where more than one person executes the affidavit or declaration under this section, the statements required by subdivision (a) shall be modified as appropriate to reflect that fact. 

(c) A certified copy of the decedent’s death certificate shall be attached to the affidavit or declaration. 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 377.32.) 

DISCUSSION 

Moving Parties seek to be substituted in place of Plaintiff, who died on December 21, 2025, under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.31. Moving Parties, who are Plaintiff’s children, indicate that they are Plaintiff’s only surviving heirs and successors in interest. Moving Parties represent that there are no probate proceedings or other heirs. Moving Parties provided a copy of Plaintiff’s death certificate and a declaration setting forth the information required by Code of Civil Procedure section 377.32. 

The Court finds the Motion proper. Moving Parties’ declaration sets forth the information required under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.32, subdivision (a), namely: (1) Plaintiff’s name; (2) Plaintiff’s death on December 21, 2025, in Baldwin Park, California; (3) a statement that no action is pending in California for administration of Plaintiff’s estate; (4) a statement that Moving Parties are Plaintiff’s successor in interest and succeed to Plaintiff’s interest in this action; (5) a statement that no other person has a superior right to commence this action or proceeding or to be substituted for Plaintiff in this action; and (6) a declaration under penalty of perjury. (Moving Parties Decl., ¶¶ 1-7.) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the Motion. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court GRANTS the Motion to Substitute Fawntaine La France Saxton, Laronda Jeanette Whitaker, Rondal Van Saxton, Jr., and Roshawn Anthony Saxton for Deceased Plaintiff Crystal Lynn Saxton as Successors in Interest. Moving Parties are hereby substituted in as the Successors in Interest for Plaintiff under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.31. 

            Moving Parties are ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order.

Case Number: 26PSCV00117    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  Western University of Health Sciences v. All Persons Claiming Any Legal or Equitable Right, Title, Estate, Lien, or Interest in the Property Described in this Complaint, Which is Adverse to Plaintiff’s Title or Creates Any Cloud on Plaintiff’s Title 

Plaintiff’s Motion for Order Authorizing Service of Summons by Publication 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion for Order Authorizing Service of Summons by Publication. 

BACKGROUND 

This is a quiet title action. On January 12, 2026, plaintiff Western University of Health Sciences (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendants All Persons Claiming Any Legal or Equitable Right, Title, Estate, Lien, or Interest in the Property Described in this Complaint, Which is Adverse to Plaintiff’s Title or Creates Any Cloud on Plaintiff’s Title, alleging cause the sole cause of action for quiet title. 

On April 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (FAC) against All Persons Claiming Any Legal or Equitable Right, Title, Estate, Lien, or Interest in the Property Described in this Complaint, Which is Adverse to Plaintiff’s Title or Creates Any Cloud on Plaintiff’s Title, Douglas Bryan, Lucinda Bourne, Russell Bryan, Katherine Bryan, Karen Donato, Sarah Roumas, Laura Jenks Robinson, Jacqueline Wender, Nicole Bryan Byrd, Hilary Bryan, Greyson Bryan, Shelley Bryan, Leslie Bryan, Scott Bryan, Wendy Bryan, and Jason Bryan (collectively, Defendants), alleging  the sole cause of action for quiet title. On June 2, 2026, Plaintiff dismissed Defendants Shelley Bryan, Douglas Bryan, Katherine Bryan, Scott Bryan, and Russell Bryan without prejudice. 

On July 1, 2026, Plaintiff moved for an order authorizing service of summons by publication. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

(a) A summons may be served by publication if upon affidavit it appears to the satisfaction of the court in which the action is pending that the party to be served cannot with reasonable diligence be served in another manner specified in this article and that either: 

(1) A cause of action exists against the party upon whom service is to be made or he or she is a necessary or proper party to the action. 

(2) The party to be served has or claims an interest in real or personal property in this state that is subject to the jurisdiction of the court or the relief demanded in the action consists wholly or in part in excluding the party from any interest in the property. 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 415.50, subd. (a).) 

(b) If upon affidavit it appears to the satisfaction of the court that the plaintiff has used reasonable diligence to ascertain the identity and residence of and to serve summons on the persons named as unknown defendants and persons joined as testate or intestate successors of a person known or believed to be dead, the court shall order service by publication pursuant to Section 415.50 and the provisions of this article. The court may, in its discretion, appoint a referee to investigate whether the plaintiff has used reasonable diligence to ascertain the identity and residence of persons sought to be served by publication, and the court may rely on the report of the referee instead of the affidavit of the plaintiff in making the order for service by publication. 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 763.010, subd. (b).) 

DISCUSSION 

Plaintiff seeks to serve Defendants Lucinda Bourne, Jason Bryan, Leslie Bryan, Wendy Bryan, and Laura Jenks Robinson on the grounds that they cannot with reasonable diligence be located and served in any other many specified by the Code of Civil Procedure and that a cause of action exists against them. Plaintiff represents that, following the Court’s prior order, it dismissed deceased Bryan Family heirs and effected service on other family members through Notices and Acknowledgment of Receipt, leaving only these five unserved. As to four of them, Plaintiff states that the only available contact information is an email address, that counsel emailed each a Notice and Acknowledgment of Receipt on May 28, 2026, and that none was returned. As to Lucinda Bourne, Plaintiff states it has no email or mailing address, that cooperating relatives cannot confirm she is alive, and that counsel’s single attempt to reach her at an international telephone number believed to be in Portugal was unsuccessful. Plaintiff argues these efforts constitute reasonable diligence and that, because this is a quiet title action in which the defendants claim or may claim an interest in the subject real property, the statutory predicate for publication under Code of Civil Procedure § 415.50, subdivision (a)(2), is satisfied. 

            The Court finds Plaintiff has sufficiently demonstrated reasonable diligence in attempting to effect service on Defendants Lucinda Bourne, Jason Bryan, Leslie Bryan, Wendy Bryan, and Laura Jenks Robinson. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 415.50.) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the Motion. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion for Order Authorizing Service of Summons by Publication.

Case Number: 26PSCV00780    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 6

Plaintiff Lucy Lu’s Request for Entry of Default Judgment 

Defendants: Robinson Reese, LP, IMAG Holdings, Inc., FDH Custom Homes, CEJ Construction, Inc., FS Builders LLC 

TENTATIVE RULING           

Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is GRANTED in the amount of $319,208.00 as to Defendants IMAG Holdings, Inc. and FDH Custom Homes. Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is DENIED with prejudice as to Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC. The Court hereby enters judgment in favor of Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC, and Plaintiff takes nothing by way of the Complaint against Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC. The Court directs Plaintiff to submit a revised proposed judgment consistent with this ruling for the Court’s review and signature.           

BACKGROUND           

            This is a breach of contract case arising from a settlement agreement concerning a residential construction dispute. On March 5, 2026, plaintiff Lucy Lu (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendants Robinson Reese, LP, IMAG Holdings, Inc., FDH Custom Homes, CEJ Construction, Inc., FS Builders LLC (collectively, Defendants), and Does 1 to 20, alleging causes of action for breach of contract, violation of Business & Professions Code § 7108 (willful diversion of construction funds), and violation of Business & Professions Code § 7159.5(a)(3) (unlawful advance payment in home improvement contract).   

            On May 7, 2026, default was entered against each of the Defendants. 

            On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff requested entry of default judgment against Defendants.           

LEGAL STANDARD 

Code of Civil Procedure section 585 permits entry of a default judgment after a party has failed to timely respond or appear. (Code Civ. Proc., § 585.) A party seeking judgment on the default by the court must file a Request for Court Judgment and: (1) a brief summary of the case; (2) declarations or other admissible evidence in support of the judgment requested; (3) interest computations as necessary; (4) a memorandum of costs and disbursements; (5) declaration of nonmilitary status; (6) a proposed form of judgment; (7) a dismissal of all parties against whom judgment is not sought or an application for separate judgment under Code of Civil Procedure section 579, supported by a showing of grounds for each judgment; (8) exhibits as necessary; and (9) a request for attorneys’ fees if allowed by statute or by the agreement of the parties. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1800, subd. (a).) 

ANALYSIS 

Plaintiff seeks default judgment against Defendants in the total amount of $319,208.00, including $258,188.00 in damages, $60,125.00 in interest,[1] and $895.00 in costs. The Court finds the Complaint deficient in certain respects.. First, Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC are not parties to the settlement agreement underlying this action. (Lu Decl., Ex. 1.) Second, Plaintiff has not adequately shown alter ego liability against all the named Defendants. (See Compl., ¶¶ 8-20; Vasey v. California Dance Co. (1977) 70 Cal.App.3d 742, 749 [conclusory allegations of alter ego liability in complaint, coupled with evidence presented at default hearing, were insufficient to show lack of separateness between persons and corporation and to show that inequity would result if alter ego liability were not imposed]; Grappo v. McMills (2017) 11 Cal.App.5th 996, 1015 [“It is well established a default judgment cannot properly be based on a complaint which fails to state a cause of action against the party defaulted because, as Witkin explains, ‘[a] defendant who fails to answer admits only facts that are well pleaded.’”].) The allegations in the Complaint and Plaintiff’s evidence is otherwise insufficient to support the imposition of alter ego liability. (See ibid.; see generally, Lu Decl.) 

The Court otherwise finds the default judgment request proper as to Defendants IMAG Holdings, Inc. and FDH Custom Homes. 

CONCLUSION           

            Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is GRANTED in the amount of $319,208.00 as to Defendants IMAG Holdings, Inc. and FDH Custom Homes. Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is DENIED with prejudice as to Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC. The Court hereby enters judgment in favor of Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC, and Plaintiff takes nothing by way of the Complaint against Defendants Robinson Reese, LP, CEJ Construction, Inc., and FS Builders LLC. The Court directs Plaintiff to submit a revised proposed judgment consistent with this ruling for the Court’s review and signature.


[1] Based on the supplemental declaration Plaintiff submitted on July 30, 2026. (See Counts Decl. (7/30/26).)
