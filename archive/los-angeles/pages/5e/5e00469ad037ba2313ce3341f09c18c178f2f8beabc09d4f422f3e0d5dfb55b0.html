            Plaintiffs

            Plaintiffs move
for leave of Court to file a Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”). Defendant

Legal Standard

 

            The court
may, in furtherance of justice, allow a party to amend any pleading upon any
terms as may be proper. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 473, subd. (a),
576.) Courts liberally grant leave to amend based on a
strong policy favoring the resolution of all disputes between parties in the
same case. (Nestle v. Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920,
939; Morgan v. Superior Court (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.)
Thus, requests for leave to amend will generally be granted unless the party
seeking to amend has been dilatory in bringing the proposed amendment, and the
delay will cause prejudice to the opposing party if leave to amend is granted. (Hirsa v.
Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490; see also Armenta
ex rel. City of Burbank (2006) 142 Cal.App.4th 636, 642 [“instances
justifying the court’s denial of leave to amend are rare.”].) Absent
prejudice, delay alone is insufficient to deny leave to amend. (Higgins
v. Del Faro (1981) 123 Cal.App.3d 558, 564-565.) 

 

            A party
requesting leave to amend must state what allegations in
the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted and added,
as well as specify where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the changes are
located.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule
3.1324(a)(1)-(3).) The moving
party must also attach the proposed amended
pleading with a declaration by counsel, describing (1) the effect of
the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the
facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) why the
request was not made earlier.  (Cal. Rules of Court,
rule 3.1324(b)(1)-(4).)   

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

            Defendant
requests that the Court take judicial notice of six exhibits. The Court
declines to take judicial notice of the six documents because it is unclear
what they are supposed to be or where they were acquired. Thus, as presented to
the Court, the Court is unable to take judicial notice of the exhibits pursuant
to Evidence Code section 452.

 

Analysis

 

                  Plaintiffs have complied with the Rules
of Court. Plaintiffs attached the proposed SAC as Exhibit 1. (See Liberation
Decl., Ex. 4.)

 

            Plaintiffs
seek leave to amend to correct a typographical error in Civil Code section 911,
subdivisions (a) and (b), to Civil Code section 936, and to name TNT Building
Corporation doing business as TNT Simmonds (“TNT”) as a defendant to their
action.

 

           Plaintiffs argue
that leave to amend is warranted because the cause of action will not change
their claims or prejudice Defendant. Plaintiffs contend that they learned of
TNT’s involvement in the events that led to their claims on December 5, 2025.
(Libertino Decl., ¶ 5.) Moreover, no discovery has been conducted in this
action, and the case is in the early stages. (Libertino Decl., ¶ 7.) Plaintiffs
assert leave to amend is necessary and proper because the SAC arises out of the
same transactions, occurrences, and subject matter of the operative complaint,
the motion is brought in good faith, granting leave to amend will promote the
efficient resolution of all claims, and denial will result in irreparable harm
to Plaintiffs. (Libertino Decl., ¶ 8(b).) Defendant disputes leave to amend
because Plaintiffs’ delay in seeking leave to amend is unreasonable and
unwarranted. The Court is not persuaded by Defendant’s argument. Defendant
relies on the exhibits of which the Court denied taking judicial notice.

 

           Thus, Plaintiffs
will be permitted to file the SAC.

 

Conclusion

 

            Plaintiffs
Bryant Waters, et al.’s motion for leave to file a Second Amended Complaint is
granted.

 

            Plaintiff must
file the SAC within five (5) calendar days of this ruling.

Case Name: Andrade v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan 

Case No.: 25STCV25110 

Motion: Motion to be Relieved as Counsel 

Moving Party: Counsel for Plaintiff Patricia Andrade

Opposing Party: None

Tentative Ruling:  The Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is denied.

Plaintiff’s counsel –Jose Alfonso Ramirez-Gomez on behalf of Jacoby & Meyers Attorneys, LLP– seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for Plaintiff Patricia Andrade. No opposition was filed.  

The motion to be relieved as counsel is denied.    

Discussion

An attorney is entitled to withdraw upon the consent of the client, or without that consent if approved by the court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 284; Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.) In the latter case, counsel must make a motion to be relieved as attorney of record.  The motion must be made using mandatory forms: Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil (MC-051), Declaration (MC-052), and Proposed Order (MC-053). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (a), (b), (e).) 

The declaration accompanying the motion to be relieved as counsel must state “in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1).” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (b).)

The notice to the client must be delivered by personal service, electronic means, or mail. If it is done via mail under Code of Civil Procedure section 1013, it must be accompanied by a declaration confirming the service address to be the most current residence or that it is the client’s last known address, and the attorney has been unable to locate a more current address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(1)(A)-(B).) If the notice is served on the client by electronic service under Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6 and rule 2.251, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating that the electronic service address is the client's current electronic service address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(2).) “If the party's residence is not known, any attempt of service pursuant to this subdivision may be made by delivering the notice or papers to the clerk of the court, for that party.” Code Civ. Proc., § 1011, subd. (b)(3)

Here, Attorney Ramirez-Gomez (“Counsel”) has complied with the Rules of Court by filing forms MC-051, MC-052, and MC-053. Counsel has submitted this motion to be relieved as counsel for a second time. 

Counsel submitted a supporting declaration that provides an adequate explanation for his request to be relieved as counsel. Counsel asserts that, since January 7, 2026, he has not been able to contact Plaintiff after repeated efforts. Counsel also asserts that a private investigator was hired to locate Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s emergency contact but was unable to locate either of them. 

Counsel contends that he has made diligent efforts to locate Plaintiff and confirm the best method to serve her. The Court previously denied Counsel’s motion for this same basis on May 4, 2026. Counsel must comply with the statute to properly serve Plaintiff with his motion and supporting papers. 

Therefore, the Court denies Counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel. 






Case Number: 25STCV28466    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             June 30, 2026             

Case Name:               Casillas v. Mahammadi, et al.

Case No.:                    25STCV28466  

Matter:                        Demurrer  

Moving Party:             Defendant Eusebio Valles Sanchez

Responding Party:       None

 

Tentative Ruling:      Defendant Eusebio Valles Sanchez’s demurrer is granted in part and denied in part.

 

 

            Plaintiff Martin Casillas (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Babak Mahammadi (“Mahammadi”), Las Lomas Investments, LLC (“Lomas”), Jose Talavera, Stucco Guys, Eusebio Vallez Sanchez (“Sanchez”), and Does 1 through 20. The Complaint alleges four causes of action for: (1) negligence; (2) statutory employer liability (Labor Code Sections 3706 and 2750.5); (3) premises liability; and (4) declaratory relief.

 

            Sanchez, in pro per, demurs to Plaintiff’s Complaint. Plaintiff did not file an opposition.

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading by raising questions of law.¿(Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.)¿“In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)¿The court “ ‘ “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .” ’ ”¿ (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.)¿In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated.¿(Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.)

 

Analysis

 

            Meet and Confer

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, “a demurring party is required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or video conference with the party that filed the pleading. . . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

            Sanchez did not attach a declaration claiming that the parties met and conferred. Thus, the parties did not meet the statutory requirements. In the interest of judicial efficiency, the Court will address the merits of Sanchez’s demurrer.

 

            Demurrer

 

 

                        Uncertainty

           

Per Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (f), a demurrer must be sustained if a pleading is uncertain. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (f).) The term uncertain means “ambiguous and unintelligible.” (Ibid.) However, demurrers for uncertainty will not be sustained unless “‘the pleading is so incomprehensi[ve] that a defendant could not reasonably respond.’” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695 , quoting Mahan v. Charles W. Chan Ins. Agency, Inc. (2017) 14 Cal.App.5th 841, 848, fn.3 (A.J. Fistes Corp.).)

 

            In looking at the Complaint as a whole, it is not incomprehensible to sustain on demurrer. The Complaint alleges sufficient facts for Sanchez to respond.

 

            Thus, the Court overrules the demurrer on this basis.

 

            Failure to Allege Sufficient Facts

 

            Sanchez demurs to the entire Complaint for failure to allege sufficient facts. The Court notes that only the first and fourth causes of action are directed as to Sanchez.

 

                                    Negligence

 

            ““The elements of a cause of action for negligence are well established. They are ‘(a) a legal duty to use due care; (b) a breach of such legal duty; [and] (c) the breach as the proximate or legal cause of the resulting injury.’”” (Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.)

 

            Sanchez contends that the Complaint does not allege that he owed Plaintiff a duty. However, the Court is not persuaded by Sanchez’s argument. The Complaint alleges that Defendants Mahammadi and Lomas hired Sanchez, doing business as Stucco Guys. (Compl., ¶¶ 4, 9.) The Complaint also alleges that “Stucco Defendants” were unlicensed contractors and failed to carry workers’ compensation insurance. (Id., ¶ 10.) Stucco Defendants hired Plaintiff, and on June 10, 2019, Plaintiff was injured while working. (Id. at ¶ 13.) Moreover, the Complaint alleges that Defendants owed Plaintiff a duty of care to provide a safe work environment and comply with California labor, safety, and licensing laws. Thus, the Complaint alleges sufficient allegations to constitute a cause of action.

 

            Therefore, the Court overrules Sanchez’s demurrer as to the first cause of action.

 

                                    Declaratory Relief

 

            ““To qualify for declaratory relief, [a party] would have to demonstrate its action presented two essential elements: ‘(1) a proper subject of declaratory relief, and (2) an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to [the party's] rights or obligations.’”” (Jolley v. Chase Home Finance, LLC (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 872, 909, as modified on denial of reh'g (Mar. 7, 2013).)

 

            The Complaint does not contain any allegations regarding a subject of declaratory relief. While the Complaint alleges the fourth cause of action against all Defendants, it contains allegations only against Defendants Mahammadi and Lomas.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains the demurrer as to the fourth cause of action.

 

Leave to Amend

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that the pleading can be successfully amended. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

Plaintiff did not file an opposition to show the Court how the pleading can be amended as to the fourth cause of action. Thus, leave to amend is not granted.

 

Conclusion

 

            Defendant Eusebio Valles Sanchez’s demurrer as to the first cause of action is overruled.

 

            Defendant Eusebio Valles Sanchez’s demurrer as to the fourth cause of action is sustained. 

 

            Plaintiff is not granted leave to amend.


Case Number: 25STCV31513    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             June 30, 2026 

Case Name:               Blankwoods LLC v. Garrison, et al.  

Case No.:                    25STCV31513

Matter:                        Motion for Preliminary Injunction  

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Blankwoods LLC

Responding Party:       Defendants Michael Garrison, Zachary Powers, Mint Management LLC, and Crafton Select LLC

 

Tentative Ruling:      Plaintiff Blankwoods LLC’s motion for preliminary injunction is denied.

 

 

            Plaintiff Blankwoods LLC (“Plaintiff”), a Florida Limited Liability Company, filed this

action against Defendants Michael Garrison, Zachary Powers, Mint Management LLC, Crafton

Select LLC (collectively “Defendants”), and Does 1 through 20. The Complaint alleges eight causes of action for: (1) breach of contract; (2) fraud; (3) trademark infringement; (4) unfair business practices per Business and Professions Code section 1700, et seq.; (5) conversion; (6) civil theft per Penal Code section 496; (7) internal interference with prospective economic advantage; and (8) constructive trust and restitution.

 

            Plaintiff moves for a preliminary injunction enjoining and restraining Defendants from manufacturing, selling, distributing, transferring, or promoting any product bearing the word mark Quickies. Defendants oppose. 

 

Legal Standard

 

            The standards governing a preliminary injunction are well known. “[A] court will deny a preliminary injunction unless there is a reasonable probability that the plaintiff will be successful on the merits, but the granting of a preliminary injunction does not amount to an adjudication of the merits.” (Beehan v. Lido Isle Community Assn. (1977) 70 Cal.App.3d 858, 866 (Beehan).) “The function of a preliminary injunction is the preservation of the status quo until a final determination of the merits.” (Ibid.) 

 

            “Trial courts traditionally consider and weigh two factors in determining whether to issue a preliminary injunction. They are (1) how likely it is that the moving party will prevail on the merits, and (2) the relative harm the parties will suffer in the interim due to the issuance or nonissuance of the injunction.” (Dodge, Warren & Peters Ins. Services, Inc. v. Riley (2003) 105 Cal.App.4th 1414, 1420.) “[T]he greater the ... showing on one, the less must be shown on the other to support an injunction.” (Ibid. [quoting Butt v. State of California, (1992) 4 Cal.4th 668, 678].) The burden of proof is on the moving party “to show all elements necessary to support issuance of a preliminary injunction.” (O'Connell v. Superior Court (2006) 141 Cal.App.4th 1452, 1481 (O'Connell).) 

 

            Preliminary injunctive relief requires the use of competent evidence to create a sufficient factual showing on the grounds for relief. (See e.g., Ancora-Citronelle Corp. v. Green (1974) 41 Cal.App.3d 146, 150.) ‘“The scope of available preliminary relief is necessarily limited by the scope of the relief likely to be obtained at trial on the merits.”’” (O'Connell, supra, 141 Cal.App.4th at p. 1463.)  

 

Analysis

 

            Plaintiff moves for a preliminary injunction to stop and restrain “Defendants[’] wholesale theft and use of Plaintiff’s valuable intellectual property rights, including Plaintiff’s common-law trade dress and trademarks.[1]   

 

            Likelihood of Prevailing on the Merits

 

            “An injunction may be granted in the following cases: (1) When it appears by the complaint that the plaintiff is entitled to the relief demanded, and the relief, or any part thereof, consists in restraining the commission or continuance of the act complained of, either for a limited period or perpetually.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 526, subd. (a)(1).)

 

             Plaintiff argues that it will likely prevail on its eight causes of action. However, the Court is not persuaded by Plaintiff’s claim. Plaintiff grouped its causes of action into three groups: (1) breach of contract; (2) infringement of trade dress, trademarks, and unfair business practices; and fraudulent concealment. As a preliminary matter, Plaintiff does not address the following causes of action: (5) conversion; (6) civil theft per Penal Code section 496; (7) internal interference with prospective economic advantage; and (8) constructive trust and restitution. The burden is on Plaintiff to show that it will likely prevail on the merits of its action. (See O'Connell, supra, 141 Cal.App.4th at p. 1481.) Here, Plaintiff has not addressed its fifth through eighth causes of action.

 

            Moreover, “the general purpose of such an injunction is the preservation of the status quo until a final determination of the merits of the action.” (Continental Baking Co. v. Katz (1968) 68 Cal.2d 512, 527.) A mandatory injunction that will grant the moving party “all of the injunctive relief requested in its complaint and to accomplish the main purpose of the action in advance of a trial on the mertis. Rarely will such a mandatory injunction issue.” (Paramount Pictures Corp. v. Davis (1964) 228 Cal.App.2d 827, 838–839.) Here, Plaintiff’s Proposed Order makes the following request for relief.

 

IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that Defendants Michael Garrison, Zachary Powers, Mint Management LLC, and Crafton Select LLC (“Defendants”) pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, § 525 et seq. are hereby enjoined and restrained from:

(i) the manufacture, sale, distribution, transfer, or promotion of any product

bearing the word mark Quickies, the use of the color red, and the Blankwoods’

common-law trade dress, which consists of: the word Quickies, in a white

cursive script, with an underscoring white line widening to form a cigarette

paper “Quickies” cone, twisted at the end, against a red background (the

“Quickies trade dress” See, e.g. Exhibit “A” attached hereto as reference),

(collectively the “Blankwoods’ intellectual property”), and particularly as

applied to any packaging, or any designs or illustrations thereof, including any

Grasshoppers branded products or materials used for marketing and sales

which utilize Blankwoods’ intellectual property;

 

(ii) the possession and/or control of any item, including packaging or marketing

materials, that bears the word mark Quickies, the trade dress or the color red,

including those used in conjunction with any Grasshoppers branding,

marketing or sales;

 

(iii) approving or causing to approve consigns for goods bearing the word mark

Quickies, the trade dress or the color red, including those used in conjunction

with the Grasshoppers branding;

 

(iv) collecting payment for or from any sales of goods bearing the word mark

Quickies, the trade dress or the color red, including those payments owed or

received in conjunction with the Grasshoppers branding and its use of the

Blankwoods’ intellectual property;

 

(v) from collecting for or on behalf of any Defendant, and each of them, any past,

current, or future gross revenue to be received through or from the sale of

items bearing the word mark Quickies, the trade dress or the color red,

including those used conjunction with the Grasshoppers brand, and which

Defendants are ordered to place said revenue in an escrow account designated

by Plaintiff, pending an accounting and/or trial, including any accounts

payable due and owing to Defendants, or any of them, from any customer,

merchant, other purchaser or other business associate or subsidiary or

independent company under Defendants ownership or control;

 

(vi) the use of the word mark Quickies, the trade dress or the color red, including

those used in conjunction with the Grasshoppers brand, in conjunction with

the sale or promotion of goods within California;

 

(vii) further unlawful and unlicensed possession of Blankwoods’ digital assets,

identified minimally as “a” through “c” below, (“Blankwoods’ Digital

Assets”), and the Other Digital Assets, identified minimally as “d” through

“f” below and infringing upon or derivative of Blankwoods’ intellectual

property, inclusive of, but not limited to, domains and social media accounts

such as:

 

a. the domain “quickiesbrand.com;”

b. the domain “quickiesprerolls.com;”

c. the Instagram account “quickieprerolls;”

d. utilizing the domains “grasshoppers.shop” and “groasshoppers

                brand.com” as destination domains from any domain associated with                      the Quickies Mark or trade dress;

e. utilizing any Instagram account in association with the word mark Quickies, the trade dress or color red in association with any social media account, inclusive of the Instagrams accounts bearing the unique identifiers: i. officialgrasshopperlosangeles; ii. official_grasshoper; iii. grasshopper_la; and iv. grasshoppersbrand_official; and f. any other digital asset related to the Quickies or Grasshoppers brand; and

 

(viii) the causing of any association between Defendants and the word mark Quickies, the corresponding trade dress or the color red, by which Defendants indicate, or cause the public to perceive, that Defendants’ or any one of them are the owners of word mark Quickies or other Blankwoods’ intellectual property; or any use of such intellectual property as a source identifier of any of Defendants’ goods or services.

 

As Blankwoods has also presented facts clearly establishing its rights over the Digital Assets, the Court further enjoins Defendants’ infringing conduct and orders Defendants, and each of them, their agents, servants, employees, representatives, and all person acting in concern or participation with them to:

 

(i) deliver to Blankwoods access to the Digital Assets using the word mark Quickies, the trade dress or the color red, including those used in conjunction with the Grasshoppers brand, by providing Blankwoods with the respective login credentials; and

a. institute Blankwoods as the registered owner of any domains bearing the Quickies trademark or other intellectual property, including, but not limited to, “quickiesbrand.com,” “quickiesprerolls.com,” and the Instagram account “quickieprerolls.”

 

(Plaintiff’s Proposed Order, 2:9-4:27.)

 

What Plaintiff is seeking in this motion is the injunctive relief it seeks in its Complaint, not to maintain the status quo.

 

             The Complaint alleges that Plaintiff is the “sole owner of intellectual property concerning the Quickies™ trademark, inclusive of the background color red with white fonts in conjunction with its brand. Its Class 25 registered wordmark covers articles of clothing, as specified above. Said marks are valid and protectable.” (Compl., ¶ 64 (emphasis added).) But Defendants’ evidence as to its packaging and marketing materials does not use the color red. (Garrison Decl., ¶¶ 11, 17-20; Ex. B-C.) Whether Defendants infringed or did not infringe on the trademark is not a determination the Court can make at this time, but what is before the Court weighs in favor of Defendants, not Plaintiff. There is a question of who holds the Quickies trademark. Defendants contend that the trademark holder is Canna Culture Corporation. (See Trojan Decl., ¶ 2; Ex. 1; Garrison Decl., Ex. 1.)

 

            Thus, the Court finds that Plaintiff did not meet its burden for the likelihood of prevailing on the merits. Because Plaintiff has not met its burden as to the first element, the Court will not address the second element regarding the balance of the harms. (See O'Connell, supra, 141 Cal.App.4th at p. 1467.)

 

            Therefore, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion for a preliminary injunction.

 

Conclusion

 

            Plaintiff Blankwoods LLC’s motion for preliminary injunction is denied.

 

 

 


[1] However, the Plaintiff’s Proposed Order is far more expansive than the motion. The Court notes that Plaintiff’s Proposed Order seeks more than to maintain the status quo of the parties until the resolution of the case.
