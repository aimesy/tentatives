I.            INTRODUCTION

On May 29, 2026, defendants/cross-complainants Los Angeles County Metropolitan Authority (“LACMTA”) and Alfred Murrillo (“Murrillo”) (collectively, “Defendants”) filed this motion for an order bifurcating trial in this matter to address issues of liability before damages.

Plaintiff John Morales (“Plaintiff”) filed an opposition brief on June 15, 2026.

Defendants filed a reply brief on June 18, 2026.

II.          DISCUSSION

This action arises from a multi-vehicle collision on the westbound Interstate 10 near Fremont Avenue involving a LACMTA bus operated by Murrillo. Plaintiff was a passenger on the bus, which collided with a stopped vehicle in the No. 1 HOV/HOT lane. The stopped vehicle, according to its owner, Valeria Garcia (“Garcia”), had sustained a flat tire and was unable to be moved from the HOV lane to the emergency lane. Garcia’s vehicle was located west of a blind curve in the freeway when co-defendant Octavio Covarrubias (“Covarrubias”) collided with Garcia’s vehicle after rounding and coming out of the same blind curve. The LACMTA bus then came upon the scene and Plaintiff sustained injuries as a result.

Under Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 598, the court may, when the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economy and efficiency of handling the litigation would be promoted thereby, on motion of a party after notice and hearing, order that the trial of any issue or any part thereof shall precede the trial of any other issue or any part thereof in the case. The objective of the bifurcation statute is the avoidance of waste of time and money caused by unnecessary trial of damage questions in cases where the liability issue is resolved against the plaintiff. (Trickey v. Superior Court In and For Sacramento County (1967) 252 Cal.App.2d 650, 653.)

Defendants contend that bifurcating trial into separate phases to address issues of liability before damages will conserve judicial resources because there would be no need to introduce lengthy testimony and evidence of Plaintiff’s injuries if no liability is found. Defendants argue that, without bifurcation, there is the risk that sympathy from extensive damages evidence will influence the liability determination.

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that bifurcation will not conserve judicial resources because the issues of liability and damages overlap and cannot be easily untangled. However, Plaintiff identifies no particular witness or evidence that would need to be presented twice if bifurcation were granted and liability determined first. Issues of medical treatment, the reasonableness of medical expenses, causation, and Plaintiff’s future care could all be reserved for a damages phase and do not need to be introduced in conjunction with evidence that is relevant to a liability determination, which involves roadway conditions, visibility, prior collisions and disabled vehicles, the bus operator’s perception and response, and whether the incident was avoidable.  

III.        CONCLUSION

Defendants’ motion for bifurcation is GRANTED.

I.            INTRODUCTION

In this reverse validation proceeding and action for declaratory relief challenging water rates, Defendant City of Pasadena (“Defendant”) seeks a protective order prohibiting plaintiffs Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association and John Gregory Haines (collectively, “HJTA”) from obtaining responses to HJTA’s First Set of Special Interrogatories (Nos. 1-2), First Set of Requests for Production of Documents (Nos. 1-5), and First Set of Requests for Admission (Nos. 1-2), served on April 15, 2026. Defendant also requests the Court order that discovery is limited to the administrative record before the Pasadena City Council at the time

II.          DISCUSSION

A reverse validation action is a challenge to an agency's “legislative type activity.” (Morgan v. Community Redevelopment Agency (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 243, 258.) Defendant argues that conventional civil discovery is inapplicable in this case because judicial review of legislative acts is limited to the administrative record, with narrow exceptions set forth in Government Code section 53759.2 (b)(2).) Government Code section 53759 governs Proposition 218 challenges and section 53759.2 sets forth the evidence that can be considered by the Court. The Court’s review is limited to “the record of proceedings before the local agency” and evidence outside the record may be admitted only if the evidence is relevant to: (1) issues other than the validity of the fee or assessment, such as standing or capacity, (2) affirmative defenses, such as laches, estoppel, and res judicata, (3) the accuracy and completeness of the administrative record, and (4) the local agency’s compliance with the procedures set forth in Government Code section 53759.1. (Gov. Code, §§ 53759.2, subd. (b)(2)(A)-(D).) Extrinsic evidence may also be admissible where it is necessary to explain information in the administrative record. (Gov. Code, § 53759.2, subd. (b)(2)(E).)

Given the limited amount of evidence that is admissible at trial, the scope of discovery set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 2017.010 is necessarily narrowed and does not encompass HJTA’s pending discovery. First, an interrogatory answer is a sworn statement created for litigation that did not exist when the City Council acted — it develops new facts, not expert interpretation of existing ones. Second, a request for admission does not seek to understand the record; it seeks a binding pretrial concession that would conclusively establish a factual predicate regardless of what the administrative record shows, effectively overriding it. Third, a document request under HJTA’s sweeping definitions — encompassing emails, schematics, and electronically stored information in the possession of City employees, contractors, and consultants — seeks evidence entirely outside the record regarding wells, water towers, reservoirs, pumping stations, and water sources. As Defendant asserts, “[n]one of these vehicles aids the parties or the court in analysis of the record. All three seek to develop and introduce substantive facts that were never before the City Council.” (Reply, p. 10.)

The Court has considered HJTA’s other arguments expressed in its opposition brief and finds them unpersuasive in light of Defendant’s reply brief. Defendant distinguishes the cases cited by HJTA and points out that HJTA does not address the statutory framework for reverse validation proceedings in the Government Code or show how the requested discovery is reasonably calculated to lead to any of the evidence made admissible by Government Code section 53759.2. HJTA also fails to address section 867 of the Code of Civil Procedure, which mandates expedited resolution of validation actions and is incompatible with HJTA’s claim that ordinary civil discovery rules apply.

III.        CONCLUSION

In light of the foregoing, Defendant’s motion for a protective order is GRANTED. Defendant need not respond to HJTA’s First Set of Special Interrogatories (Nos. 1-2), First Set of Requests for Production of Documents (Nos. 1-5), and First Set of Requests for Admission (Nos. 1-2), served on April 15, 2026. Discovery as to evidence outside the administrative record is limited to matters relevant to or reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of evidence admissible under Government Code § 53759.2.
