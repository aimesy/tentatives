Tentative
Ruling:      Defendant Black Tide Events, LLC’s motion for summary judgment, or in
the alternative summary adjudication, is denied.





 





 



            Plaintiff
Darrylene Corniel (“Corniel”) and Estate of Darrell Caldwell (“Estate”)
(collectively “Plaintiffs”) filed their action against Live Nation Worldwide
Inc., C3 Presents LLC, Bobby Dee Presents, Inc., Snoop Dogg’s LLC, Jeff Shuman,
University of Southern California (“USC”), LAFC Sports LLC, Major League
Soccer, LLC, CISS Management Group, CI Security Specialists, Inc. and Does 1 to
100. The FAC alleges three causes of action on behalf of the Estate: (1)
wrongful death sounding in negligence; (3) wrongful death – premises liability;
and (5) wrongful death – negligent hiring, supervision, training, and/or
retention. The FAC alleges three causes of action on behalf of Corniel for: (2)
survival action sounding in negligence; (4) survival action – premises
liability; and (6) survival action – negligent hiring, supervision, training,
and/or retention. The FAC alleges that on December 18, 2021, Darrell Caldwell,
also known as Drakeo the Ruler (“Decedent”), was set to perform at the Once
Upon A Time in LA Music Festival (“Festival”), where, allegedly, a dispute
arose that resulted in his wrongful death (“Subject Incident”). 



 



            On June 26,
2022, Plaintiffs’ case was consolidated with Caldwell v. Live Nation World
Wide, Inc., et al.



 



            On March
22, 2026, Plaintiffs named Defendant Black Tide Events, LLC (“Defendant”) as
Doe 3.



 



            Defendant moves
for summary judgment or, in the alternative, summary adjudication as to all six
of Plaintiffs’ causes of action. Plaintiffs oppose.



 



Legal Standard



 



            “The party
moving for summary judgment bears the burden of persuasion that there is no
triable issue of material fact and that he is entitled to judgment as a matter
of law.”¿(Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co.¿(2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850.)
A triable issue of material fact exists if the evidence would allow a
reasonable trier of fact to find the underlying fact in favor of the party
opposing the motion in accordance with the applicable standard of proof.
(Ibid.)¿ 



¿ 



            “When
deciding whether to grant summary judgment, the court must consider all of the
evidence set forth in the papers (except evidence to which the court has
sustained an objection), as well as all reasonable inferences that may be
drawn from that evidence, in the light most favorable to the party opposing
summary judgment.”¿(Avivi v. Centro Medico Urgente Medical Center¿(2008)
159 Cal. App. 4th 463, 467; Code Civ. Proc. § 437c, subd. (c).)

Plaintiffs and Defendant made evidentiary objections.

makes 33 evidentiary objections to Plaintiff’s evidence. The Court overrules
all 33 evidentiary objections.

also make four evidentiary objections to Dominique Sicari’s declaration. The

Request for Judicial Notice



 



            Defendant
requests that the Court take judicial notice of two court documents: (1)

            The Court
grants Defendant’s request for judicial notice pursuant to Evidence Code

Analysis



 



            Defendant
moves for summary judgment or in the alternative, summary adjudication on the
grounds that: (1) Defendant is entitled to summary judgment as to all causes of
action; (2) Defendant did not owe a duty to Decedent and did not proximately
cause Decedent’s death as to the first cause of action; (3) Defendant did not
owe a duty to Decedent and did not proximately cause Decedent’s death as to the
second cause of action; (4) Defendant did not owe a duty to Decedent and did
not proximately cause Decedent’s death as to the third cause of action; (5)
Defendant did not owe a duty to Decedent and did not proximately cause
Decedent’s death as to the fifth cause of action; (6) Defendant is entitled to
judgment as to the fifth cause of action because Defendant did not hire,
retain, or any security personnel; and (7) Defendant is entitled to judgment as
to the sixth cause of action because Defendant did not hire, retain, or any
security personnel. The FAC alleges three causes of action: a wrongful death claim
and a separate survivor claim. Because the evidence used in each cause of
action is identical, the Court will address the three causes of action based on
their elements.



 



            Preliminary
Issues



 



            In
opposition, Plaintiffs raise two procedural issues in Defendant’s moving
papers. First, Plaintiffs argue that the separate statement is untimely, and
second that Defendant’s motion for summary adjudication is defective.



 



            Code of
Civil Procedure section 437c, subdivision (a)(2) requires that “[n]otice of the
motion and supporting papers shall be served on all other parties to the action
at least 81 days before the time appointed for hearing.” (Code Civ. Proc., §
437c, subd. (a)(2).)



 



            Defendant
admits that when it initially served the moving papers with the incorrect draft
used and subsequently filed and served the updated separate statement. Statutorily,
the deadline to timely serve all moving papers on the parties was May 24, 2026.
The amended proof of service attached states that Plaintiffs were served
through messenger and email on May 22, 2026. While the email service was
untimely, the messenger service was timely.



 



            Regarding
Defendant’s motion for summary adjudication, the Court finds that there is
confusion on the part of both of the parties.



 



            “A party
may move for summary adjudication as to one or more causes of action within an
action, one or more affirmative defenses, one or more claims for damages, or
one or more issues of duty, if the party contends that the cause of action has
no merit, . . . A motion for summary adjudication shall be granted only if it
completely disposes of a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for
damages, or an issue of duty.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (f)(1).)



 



            Defendant
states that it requests summary adjudication in the alternative because Plaintiffs
cannot show causation. (Mot., 11:28-12:1.) Code of Civil Procedure section
437c, subdivision (f) makes it clear that the Court cannot grant a motion for
summary adjudication unless it completely disposes of a cause of action, unlike
if a moving party seeks summary adjudication as to the issue of duty without
completely disposing of a cause of action. Thus, for the Court to grant summary
adjudication as to causation it must dispose of the entire cause of action. However,
most importantly, Defendant’s notice of motion moves for summary judgment and in
the alternative summary adjudication, and identifies the issues in each of the
causes of action that it argues warrant granting of its motion. Additionally,
the Defendant’s Amended Separate Statement reflects each issue as written on
the notice of motion in compliance with California Rules of Court, rule
3.1350(b).



 



            Thus,
Plaintiff’s procedural objections are inapplicable and incorrect. The Court
will address the merits of Defendant’s motion for summary judgment and summary
adjudication.



 



First and Second Cause of Action
– Negligence (Wrongful Death) and (Survival Action)



 



            ““To
establish a cause of action for negligence, the plaintiff must show that the
‘defendant had a duty to use due care, that he breached that duty, and that the
breach was the proximate or legal cause of the resulting injury.’ [Citation.]
Recovery for negligence depends as a threshold matter on the existence of a
legal duty of care.” [Citation.] “‘[T]he existence and scope’ of the
defendant's duty is one of law to be decided by the court, not by a jury, and
therefore it generally is ‘amenable to resolution by summary judgment.’
[Citation.]”” (Murphy v. Pina (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 305, 322 (citations
omitted).)



 



            Defendant
contends that it did not owe a duty of care to Plaintiffs with respect to
Decedent’s death because it: (1) did not own, possess or control the Music
Festival Premises, including the area where the Subject Incident occurred; (2)
did not owe a duty to protect from third-party criminal conduct; and (3) was
not engaged in a joint venture with the Festival Promotors. 



 



            “A
defendant cannot be held liable for the defective or dangerous condition of
property which it did not own, possess, or control. Where the absence of
ownership, possession, or control has been unequivocally established, summary
judgment is proper.” (Isaacs v. Huntington Memorial Hospital (1985) 38
Cal.3d 112, 134 (Isaacs).)



 



            As an
initial matter, Defendant does not provide any legal argument to support its
proposition that it was not engaged in a joint venture with the Festival
Promoters. Thus, this argument is undeveloped and not a basis for supporting
Defendant’s motion.  



 



            The
undisputed material facts in the present motion are that Defendant did
not own lease, or licensed the premises where the Subject Incident occurred. (Undisputed
Material Facts (“UMF”) 1) It is also undisputed that Defendant was hired under
the title of “Festival Director.” (UMF 2) Defendant repeatedly contends that it
had a limited role as an independent contractor for the Festival; however,
Defendant’s own evidence establishes that a triable issue of material fact
exists as to what its involvement and role was within the operations of the
Festival.



 



            “The
“‘crucial element’” for imposing a duty in such circumstances is control
([Citation.] [“‘[w]hoever controls the land is responsible for its safety’”]),
the rationale being that whoever has the means to control the property can take
steps to prevent the harm.” (Soto v. Union Pacific Railroad Co. (2020)
45 Cal.App.5th 168, 177.) Defendant relies on Sean Akhavan’s Declaration and
the Agreement between C3 Presents, LLC and Black Tide Events, LLC, dated
December 10, 2021 (“Agreement”). The Court sustained evidentiary objections as
to Sean Akhavan’s Declaration; thus, reliance on paragraph 3 of the declaration
is improper as that portion of the declaration is inadmissible. Second, the
Agreement that Defendant relies on creates a triable issue of material fact instead
of showing that no triable issue exists. The Agreement expressly states that
Defendant will:



 



. . . propose, manage, and execute
vendor relationships and project deadlines on behalf of C3/Live Nation, with
the goal of resulting in various revenue streams to include:



. . .



 



[Defendant] will provide operational
management services, including but not limited to:



. . .



 



·        
Securing all event permits per venue and local
jurisdiction guidelines



·        
Coordinating with local public authorities,
government agencies, and risk management team to create and implement a:



o  
Traffic and Parking plan



o  
Event Safety Plan



           . . .



 



·      Festival
management of vendors including, but not limited to:



o   Police



o   Security



 



(Black Tide Ex., A, p. 13-15 (emphasis added).)



 



            Moreover,
Defendant argues that the Agreement language references the terms “security” or
“safety” as “administrative oversight coordination.” (Mot., 6: 25-28.) However,
the burden is on Defendant to support this claim, and not Plaintiffs. Defendant
relies on the same 11 undisputed material facts throughout its separate
statement. Some of the “undisputed material facts” are not undisputed material
facts but rather legal conclusions of the triable issues that are in dispute Defendant’s
role in the production of the Festival and the day of the Festival. (See UMF 3,
5, 8.) Per California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(a) “‘[m]aterial facts’ are
facts that relate to the cause of action, claim for damages, issue of duty, or
affirmative defense that is the subject of the motion and that could make a
difference in the disposition of the motion.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule
3.1350(a)(2); Code Civ. Proc., §437c, subd. (b)(1).) “The point of the separate
statement is not to craft a narrative, but to be a concise list of the material
facts and the evidence that supports them. ‘The separate statement serves two
important functions in a summary judgment proceeding: It notifies the parties
which material facts are at issue, and it provides a convenient and expeditious
vehicle permitting the trial court to hone in on the truly disputed facts.’” (Beltran
v. Hard Rock Hotel Licensing, Inc. (2023) 97 Cal.App.5th 865, 875.)



 



            Additionally,
Defendant asserts that Co-Defendant C3 was responsible for contracting with the
security entities, and attaches the executed contracts. (Sicari Decl., ¶ 9.)
However, the dispute is whether Defendant had any control regarding the
production of the Festival and execution of the security plans, if any control.



 



            Defendant
also argues that at most awareness of the security plan does not show control.
(Mot., 7:9-15.) However, Defendant’s reliance on Sea Horse Ranch, Inc. v.
Superior Court (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 446 is questionable because the case
is based on criminal liability. The Sea Horse Ranch, Inc. case involves
the Court’s review of the evidence presented in a preliminary hearing regarding
criminal charges. (Sea Horse Ranch, Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 24
Cal.App.4th 446, 449.) Plaintiffs bring forth civil claims against Defendant.



 



             Furthermore,
Defendant’s reliance on the Court’s ruling on USC’s motion for summary judgment
is not a basis to grant Defendant’s motion. USC separately met its burden of
proof and established that no triable issue of material fact existed, and the facts
of its role at the Festival were different than Defendant’s.



 



           For the reasons
listed above in this ruling, Defendant did not meet its burden to establish
that it did not owe Decedent a duty of care. Based on the Separate Statement
and evidence submitted by Defendant, there is a triable issue as to what its
role was leading up to the Festival and the Festival. The Court does not need
to address the remaining arguments of Defendant’s motion regarding criminal
conduct and causation. The burden does not shift to Plaintiffs.



 



            Thus,
Defendant’s motion for summary judgment is denied. Defendant’s motion for
summary adjudication as to the first and second causes of action is denied.



 



            Third
and Fourth Cause of Action – Premises Liability (Wrongful Death) and
(Survival



            Action)



 



            Defendant
relies on the same arguments as the first and second causes of action because
the claims are all based on negligence. Defendant also relies on the same 11
undisputed material facts to support its arguments. Because Defendant provides
no other arguments or evidence to support its claims, Defendant does not meet
its burden to establish that no triable issue of material facts exists as to
the third and fourth causes of action. The burden does not shift to Plaintiffs
to establish that a triable issue of material fact exists.



 



            Thus,
Defendant’s motion for summary adjudication as to the third and fourth causes
of action is denied.



 



           



            Fifth
and Sixth Causes of Action – Negligent Hiring/Retention/Supervision Training         (Wrongful Death) and (Survival Action)



 



            “‘A
principal who conducts an activity through an agent is subject to liability for
harm to a third party caused by the agent's conduct if the harm was caused by
the principal's negligence in selecting, training, retaining, supervising, or
otherwise controlling the agent....’ [Citation.] ‘Liability under this rule is
limited by basic principles of tort law, including requirements of causation
and duty.’ [Citation.] Furthermore, ‘[l]iability under this rule also requires
some nexus or causal connection between the principal's negligence in selecting
or controlling an actor, the actor's employment or work, and the harm suffered
by the third party.’ ” (Phillips v. TLC Plumbing, Inc. (2009) 172
Cal.App.4th 1133, 1140.)



 



            Defendant
argues that because it did not contract or retain any security providers for
the Festival, and “had minimal passive oversight over the Festival,” it cannot
be held liable for the fifth and sixth causes of action. However, as addressed
in this ruling, Defendant has not met its burden to establish how much of a
role it played leading up to the Festival and at the Festival. While Defendant
has continued to assert this proposition that it had “minimal control” throughout
its motion, the separate statement does not support this basis. Because
Defendant has not met its burden, the burden does not shift to Plaintiff.



 



           Defendant’s motion
for summary adjudication as to the fifth and sixth causes of action is denied.



 



           Therefore,
Defendant has failed to demonstrate it is entitled to summary judgment or, in
the alternative, summary adjudication.  



           



Conclusion



 



            Defendant
Black Tide Events, LLC’s motion for summary judgment is denied.  



 



            Defendant
Black Tide Events, LLC’s motion for summary adjudication is denied.



 

Case Number: 25STCV17808    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             August 13, 2026

Case Name:                 Garsinii v. Growth Management, Inc. et al.

Case No.:                    25STCV17808

Motion:                       Motion to be Relieved as Counsel

Moving Party:             Counsel for Defendants Joy Lewis, John Lewis, Adolescent Growth, Inc., and Growth Management, Inc.

Opposing Party:          Plaintiff Namahn Garsinii

 

Tentative Ruling:      The Motions to be Relieved as Counsel is granted in part and denied in part.

                                   

 

Albright, Yee & Schmit APC (“Firm”) seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for Defendants Joy Lewis, John Lewis, Adolescent Growth, Inc. and Growth Management, Inc., (collectively “Defendants”). Plaintiff Namahn Garsinii (“Plaintiff”) filed an opposition to Albright, Yee & Schmit APC’s motion.

 

Analysis

 

            An attorney is entitled to withdraw upon the consent of the client, or without that consent if approved by the court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 284; Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.) In the latter case, counsel must make a motion to be relieved as attorney of record.  The motion must be made using mandatory forms: Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil (MC-051), Declaration (MC-052), and Proposed Order (MC-053). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (a), (b), (e).)

 

            The declaration accompanying the motion to be relieved as counsel must state “in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship, why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1).” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (b).)

 

            The notice to the client must be done by personal service, electronically, or by mail. If it is done via mail under Code of Civil Procedure section 1013, it must be accompanied by a declaration confirming the service address to be the most current residence or that it is the client’s last known address, and the attorney has been unable to locate a more current address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(1)(A)-(B).)

 

            Here, Firm filed forms MC-051, MC-052, and MC-053 for each Defendant. However, forms MC-051, incorrectly labeled Defendants Joy Lewis and John Lewis as corporations.

 

            Firm has complied with the procedural and statutory requirements to be relieved as counsel for Defendants Adolescent Growth, Inc., and Growth Management, Inc. However, this is not a legal basis to deny the motions.

 

Therefore, Albright, Yee & Schmit APC’s motion to be relieved as counsel is granted as to Defendants Adolescent Growth, Inc., and Growth Management, Inc.

 

Albright, Yee & Schmit APC’s motion to be relieved as counsel is denied as to Defendants Joy Lewis and John Lewis.

 

 

 

 


Case Number: 25STCV33983    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

 

Hearing Date:             August 13, 2026

Case Name:               Spigen, Inc. v. Genesis Motor America, LLC, et al.  

Case No.:                   25STCV33983

Matter:                        Motion for Attorney Fees

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Spigen Inc.

Responding Party:      Defendant Genesis Motor America  

 

Tentative Ruling:      Plaintiff Spigen Inc.’s motion is granted in part and denied in part.

 

 

            Plaintiff Spigen Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Genesis Motor America (“GMA”), GENSM, LLC, and Does 1 through 50. The Complaint alleged two causes of action for: (1) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (d); and (2) breach of implied warranty of merchantability.

 

            Plaintiff filed a motion for attorney’s fees. GMA opposes.

 

Legal Standard

 

            Where the buyer of a vehicle prevails in an action under the Song-Beverly Act, he “shall be allowed by the court to recover . . . a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred . . . .” (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).) 

 

            “[T]he starting point of every fee award . . . must be a calculation of the attorney's services in terms of the time he has expended on the case. Anchoring the analysis to this concept is the only way of approaching the problem that can claim objectivity, a claim which is obviously vital to the prestige of the bar and the courts.”¿(In re Vitamin Cases (2003) 110 Cal. App. 4th 1041, 1058, [citation omitted].) “[T]he primary method for establishing the amount of ‘reasonable’ attorney fees is the lodestar method. The lodestar (or touchstone) is produced by multiplying the number of hours reasonably expended by counsel by a reasonable hourly rate.”¿(Thayer v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2001) 92 Cal. App. 4th 819, 833 (Thayer).) Under this approach, a base amount is calculated from a compilation of time reasonably spent and reasonable hourly compensation of each attorney. (Serrano v. Priest (1977) 20 Cal.3d 25, 48; Serrano v. Unruh (1982) 32 Cal.3d 621, 639 (Serrano).) A fee request that appears unreasonably inflated is a special circumstance that permits the trial court to reduce the award or deny it altogether. (Serrano, supra, 32 Cal. 3d at p. 635.)

 

Analysis

 

            Plaintiff requests fees in the amount of $20,661.72 in attorney’s fees, plus $1,800 for Plaintiff to read Defendant’s opposition and file a reply for the current motion. Plaintiff also requests $921.72 in costs.

 

            Reasonableness of Plaintiff’s Counsel’s Rates

 

            “A prevailing party has the burden of showing that the fees incurred were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation, and were reasonable in amount.” (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 817–818 (Robertson).) “[T]he lodestar is the basic fee for comparable legal services in the community; it may be adjusted by the court based on factors including, as relevant herein, (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award.” (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 (Ketchum).)

 

             Plaintiff requests that the Court accept Counsel Alex Cha’s (“Counsel”) $600 hourly rate. (Cha Decl., ¶ 13.) However, Counsel does not provide the Court with any evidence to support his hourly rate is reasonable. In Plaintiff’s moving papers and reply, it asserts that Counsel has 25 years of experience, but this assertion is not made in Counsel’s declaration. (Mot., 7:4-5.) Moreover, other rulings where Counsel’s rate was granted in not binding in this Court. Thus, without evidence to support the demand for a $600 hourly rate, the Court will reduce Counsel’s hourly rate to $450 based on the facts of the case and the Court’s knowledge of what a reasonable hourly rate is for these types of cases.

 

           Thus, the Court reduces Counsel’s hourly rate from $600 to $450. The Court imposes a total reduction of $4,485.

           

            Reasonableness of Plaintiff’s Counsel’s Hours        

 

            The Court must base the attorneys’ fees on the “actual time expended on the case

 as long as such fees are reasonably incurred—both from the standpoint of time spent and the amount charged.” (Robertson, supra, 144 Cal.App.4th at p. 817.) The Court must ascertain “whether under all the circumstances of the case the amount of actual time expended and the monetary charge being made for the time expended are reasonable.” (Ibid.)

 

            Plaintiff contends that Counsel spent 29.9 hours to litigate its case.

 

            In opposition, GMA argues that the number of hours requested is unreasonable and excessive because the several entries were excessive, some hours have not been earned, and other entries are not recoverable.

 

           As an initial matter, Plaintiff does not present the Court with any argument that its case was novel or complex that would justify the amount of hours requested. The case also lasted six months, and no substantial motions were filed in this action or vehicle inspection conducted. Plaintiff filed several entries for initial consultations with Plaintiff as a “potential client” and execution of the attorney-client agreement. (Cha Decl., Ex. B.) These fees cannot be imposed on Defendant; thus, the Court reduces the amount by $2,040.

            GMA also disputes Plaintiff’s entries for drafting the Complaint and service of the Complaint because the Complaint was only four pages and two causes of action. (Compl., 1:1-4:26.)

 

            In reply, Plaintiff argues that even if Counsel uses a template, time is still needed to update the Complaint with the new facts.

 

            The Court finds that the amount requested for the drafting, research, and service of the Complaint is excessive; therefore, the Court imposes a reduction of $150.

 

               Additionally, there are several administrative entries at Counsel’s $600 hourly rate. The Court finds that the hourly rate for the administrative entries is unreasonably high. To address this issue, the Court imposes a reduction of $660.

 

           The Court finds that GMA’s arguments about unearned fees are irrelevant since Plaintiff has reviewed GMA’s opposition, filed a reply, and will attend the hearing on its motion.

 

           Therefore, the Court will impose a total reduction of $3,350.

 

            Costs

 

            Plaintiff requests $921.72 for costs and expenses incurred in connection with the action.

The costs requested are recoverable. (Code Civ. Proc., 1033.5.) The Court finds Plaintiff’s request for costs is reasonable.

 

            Thus, the Court grants Plaintiff’s request for costs.

 

Conclusion

 

            Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys' fees is granted in part and denied in part. Plaintiff is awarded fees in the total amount of $15,126.72. Plaintiff's request for costs is granted in its $921.72 entirety.

 

 


Case Number: 26STCV02203    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             August 13, 2026

Case Name:               Salcedo, et. al. v. Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc., et al.

Case No.                     26STCV02203

Matter:                        Motions to Compel Plaintiff Pablo Salcedo to respond to Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents and deem admitted Requests for Admission

Moving Party:             Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.                

Responding Party:       None

Matter:                        Motions to Compel Plaintiff Olivia Fabian to respond to Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents, Form and Special Interrogatories, and deem admitted Requests for Admissions

Moving Party:             Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.                

Responding Party:       None

 

Tentative Ruling:      Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s motion to compel Plaintiff Pablo Salcedo to serve responses is granted. Defendant’s motion to deem admitted Requests for Admissions is granted.

 

                                  Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s motion to compel Plaintiff Olivia Fabian to serve responses is granted. Defendant’s motion to deem admitted Requests for Admissions is granted.

 

 

Plaintiffs Pablo Salcedo (“Salcedo”) and Olivia Fabian (“Fabian”) (“Plaintiffs”) brought this action against Defendants Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc. (“Defendant”) and Us Bank National Association, as Trustee for JP Morgan Mortgage Acquisition Trust 2006 Cw2 and Does 1-10.

 

A.     Motions Against Plaintiff Salcedo

 

Defendant moves to compel Plaintiff Salcedo to respond to Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents and deem admitted Requests for Admissions served on Salcedo. Defendant also requests the Court to impose monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,550 against Salcedo for each motion. Salcedo did not file an opposition.

 

 Defendant contends that on February 3, 2026, it propounded discovery on Salcedo, and to date, Salcedo has not served responses. (Ramos Decl., ¶¶ 3-6.)

 

Thus, the Court grants Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff Pablo Salcedo to respond to Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents (Set One). Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Admissions (Set One) are deemed admitted against Plaintiff Pablo Salcedo. The Court also grants Defendant’s request to impose monetary sanctions against Plaintiff in the reduced amount of $1,000 for both motions.

 

Plaintiff Pablo Salcedo is to serve Code-compliant, verified responses to the entire set of  Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents without objection, on or before September 14, 2026.

 

Sanctions are granted against Plaintiff Salcedo in the total amount of $1,500. Sanctions are to be paid on or before September 14, 2026.

 

B. Motions Against Plaintiff Fabian

 

Defendant moves to compel Plaintiff Fabian to respond to Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents, Form and Special Interrogatories, and deem admitted Requests for Admissions served on Fabian. Defendant also requests the Court to impose monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,550 against Fabian for each motion. Fabian did not file an opposition.

 

 Defendant contends that on February 3, 2026, it propounded discovery on Fabian, and to date Fabian has not served responses. (Ramos Decl., ¶¶ 3-6.)

 

Thus, the Court grants Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff Olivia Fabian to respond to Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Production of Documents, Form and Special Interrogatories (Set One), and Defendant Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.’s Request for Admissions (Set One) are deemed admitted against Plaintiff Pablo Salcedo. Case Number: 26STCV02259    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             August 13, 2026         

Case Name:              Mallen, et al. v. Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc., et al.

Case No.:                   26STCV02259

Matter:                        Demurrer with Motion to Strike  

Moving Party:             Defendants Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc. and Christina Wyant

Responding Party:       Plaintiffs Blake Mallen, Karis Mallen, and Bailey’s LLC

 

Tentative Ruling:      Defendants Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc. and Christina Wyant’s demurrer is sustained.

 

                                    Defendants Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc. and Christina Wyant’s motion to strike is rendered moot.

 

 

            Plaintiffs Blake Mallen, Karis Mallen, and Bailey’s LLC (“Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendants Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc., Christina Wyant (“Wyant”) (collectively “Defendants”), and Does 1 through 10. The First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleges three causes of action for: (1) negligence; (2)  negligent representation; and (3) fraudulent representation.

 

            Defendants demur to Plaintiffs’ third cause of action for failure to allege sufficient facts. Defendants also move to strike the prayer for punitive damages. Plaintiffs oppose.

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading by raising questions of law.¿(Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.)¿“In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)¿The court “‘“treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .”’”¿ (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.)¿In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated.¿(Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) 

            “The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) “Immaterial” or “irrelevant” matters include allegations not essential to the claim, allegations neither pertinent to nor supported by an otherwise sufficient claim or a demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 431.10, subds. (b)(1)-(3).) 

 

Analysis

 

            Meet and Confer

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, “a demurring party is required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or video conference with the party that filed the pleading. . . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).) A moving party in a motion to strike must meet and confer before filing the motion. (Code Civ. Proc. §435.5, subd. (a).)

 

            Defendants contend that the parties met and conferred over the telephone. (Suh Decl., ¶ 4.) Thus, the parties fulfilled their statutory meet-and-confer obligations.

 

            Preliminary Issues

 

            As an initial matter, the Court notes that Defendants reserved this Court hearing only as a motion to strike. The Court advises the parties that, moving forward, they must ensure they reserve the correct reservation slot required for their respective motions. The Court cannot allow parties to bypass the Court’s reservation system to have their motions heard at an earlier date, nor can the Court accommodate such requests out of error by the parties. However, in this situation, because Plaintiffs have not opposed the demurrer on this basis and at this time the Court has the capacity to hear the demurrer, the Court will exercise its discretion to hear both motions.

 

            Demurrer

 

            Defendants demur to Plaintiffs’ third cause of action for failure to allege sufficient facts.

 

            ““The elements of fraud, which give rise to the tort action for deceit, are (a) misrepresentation (false representation, concealment, or nondisclosure); (b) knowledge of falsity (or ‘scienter’); (c) intent to defraud, i.e., to induce reliance; (d) justifiable reliance; and (e) resulting damage.”” (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 638 (Lazar).) “In California, fraud must be pled specifically; general and conclusory allegations do not suffice.” (Id. at p. 645.) “Section 525 of the Restatement Second of Torts states: ‘One who fraudulently makes a misrepresentation of fact, opinion, intention or law for the purpose of inducing another to act or to refrain from action in reliance upon it, is subject to liability to the other in deceit for pecuniary loss caused to him by his justifiable reliance upon the misrepresentation.’” (Small v. Fritz Companies, Inc. (2003) 30 Cal.4th 167, 174.) “”Every element of the cause of action for fraud must be alleged in the proper manner (i.e., factually and specifically), and the policy of liberal construction of the pleadings ... will not ordinarily be invoked to sustain a pleading defective in any material respect.”” (Committee On Children's Television, Inc. v. General Foods Corp. (1983) 35 Cal.3d 197, 216.)

 

            Defendants argue that the FAC fails to allege facts sufficient to establish that they knowingly made false misrepresentations or acted with the intent to deceive Plaintiffs, and the FAC lacks specificity.

 

            The FAC alleges that Wyant told Plaintiffs that their home was “too high to insure” under the California FAIR plan. (FAC, ¶ 21.) Plaintiffs further allege that Wyant’s prior statement was false because they purchased a subsequent house and were able to purchase a property in the same neighborhood and purchased a FAIR plan. (Id. at ¶ 45.) Plaintiffs allege that Wyant made a false representation “without verifying it,” but also that she has actual or constructive knowledge of their eligibility as an insurance professional. (Id. at ¶ 54.)

 

            In opposition, Plaintiffs contend that every element for their cause of action is alleged. As an initial matter, Plaintiffs rely on Engalla v. Permanente Medical Group, Inc. (1997) 15 Cal.4th 951, to support their claim that assertions without regard for the truth satisfy the scienter element of fraud. However, the Court finds their reliance on Engalla in inapplicable. The court in Engalla addressed false representations in the scope of arbitration agreements. (Engalla v. Permanente Medical Group, Inc. (1997) 15 Cal.4th 951, 973.) But in Bily, the Court stated that “[i]f the defendant has no belief in the truth of the statement, and makes it recklessly, without knowing whether it is true or false, the element of scienter is satisfied.” (Bily v. Arthur Young & Co. (1992) 3 Cal.4th 370, 414.)

 

            Plaintiffs argue that they allege that Wyant made the misrepresentation without taking any steps to verify it. (FAC, ¶ 54.) However, while  allegations of scienter are alleged, Plaintiffs do not allege facts to support the element that Wyant intended to induce reliance or intent deceive Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs contend that Defendants intended to refrain Plaintiffs from taking an action upon reliance of Wyant’s assertion. However, this argument is unclear as to how the alleged statement regarding coverage fulfilled the intent element. The FAC alleges that Wyant was working with Plaintiffs to help find coverage; the Court is not persuaded by Plaintiff’s allegations that the FAC alleges sufficient facts for a viable cause of action. Moreover, the FAC is also conclusory, and depends on allegations that do not support its cause of action, but instead served more as evidentiary assertions. (See FAC, ¶¶45-46.)

 

            Defendants state that the FAC is not clear as to how “Defendant Martplan would be liable for fraudulent misrepresentation along with Defendant Wyant.” (Mot., 8:23-25.) However, Defendants do not provide further support for this assertion. The Court will not address this assertion, as the burden of proof is on Defendants to make their argument.

 

            Thus, the demurrer is sustained as to the third cause of action.

 

            Motion to Strike

 

            Defendants move to strike the prayer for punitive damages as to Plaintiff’s third cause of action.

 

            Because the demurrer has been sustained to the third cause of action, the motion to strike is rendered moot.

 

Leave to Amend

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that the pleading can be successfully amended. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

The Court finds that Plaintiffs have presented the Court with sufficient basis to grant leave to amend. Plaintiffs will be given the opportunity to amend.

 

Conclusion

           

            Defendants Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc. and Christina Wyant’s demurrer is sustained.

 

            Defendants Martplan Insurance Agency, Inc. and Christina Wyant’s motion to strike is moot.

 

            Plaintiffs shall have leave to amend and must file an amended complaint by September 14, 2026.


Case Number: 26STCV14602    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             August 13, 2026         

Case Name:              Guzman v. General Motors Company

Case No.:                   26STCV14602


Matter:                        Demurrer with Motion to Strike  

Moving Party:              Defendant General Motors Company

Responding Party:       None

 

Tentative Ruling:      Defendant’s demurrer is sustained.

 

                                    Defendant’s motion to strike is rendered moot.

 

 

            Plaintiff Joshua Guzman (“Plaintiff”) filed his action against General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 10. The Complaint alleges eight causes of action against Defendant for: (1) breach of implied warranty; (2) breach of express warranty; (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; (4) fraud—intentional misrepresentation; (5) negligent misrepresentation; (6) promissory fraud; (7) promissory estoppel; (8) violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200.

 

            Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh and eighth causes of action, and moves to strike portions of Plaintiff’s Complaint for requesting punitive damages. No opposition was filed by Plaintiff.

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading by raising questions of law.¿(Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.)¿“In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)¿The court “‘“treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .”’”¿ (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.)¿In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated.¿(Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) 

            “The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) “Immaterial” or “irrelevant” matters include allegations not essential to the claim, allegations neither pertinent to nor supported by an otherwise sufficient claim or a demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 431.10, subds. (b)(1)-(3).)

Analysis

            Meet and Confer

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, “a demurring party is required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or video conference with the party that filed the pleading. . . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).) A moving party in a motion to strike must meet and confer before filing the motion. (Code Civ. Proc. §435.5, subd. (a).)

 

Defendant contends that on June 4, 2026, the parties met and conferred telephonically. (Kay Decl., ¶ 2.) Thus, the parties fulfilled their meet-and-confer obligations.

 

            Demurrer

 

            Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, and eighth causes of action on the grounds that Plaintiff fails to allege sufficient facts to state a viable cause of action.

 

            Fourth and Fifth Causes of Action—Intentional Misrepresentation and Negligent Misrepresentation

 

            The facts¿constituting¿the alleged fraud must be alleged factually and specifically as to every element of¿fraud, as the policy of “liberal construction” of the pleadings will not ordinarily be invoked. (Lazar v. Superior Court¿(1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 645 (Lazar).) To properly allege fraud against a corporation, the plaintiffs must plead the names of the persons allegedly making the false representations, their authority to speak, to whom they spoke, what they said or wrote, and when it was said or written. (Tarmann¿v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.¿(1991) 2 Cal.App.4th 153, 157 (Tarmann).)¿ “Fraud is never presumed. So in the instant case, if the plaintiff would charge the defendant corporation with making fraudulent misrepresentations, it was necessary for him to allege the name of the person who spoke, his authority to speak, to whom he spoke, what he said or wrote, and when it was said or written.” (Mason v. Drug, Inc. (1939) 31 Cal.App.2d 697, 703.)

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff has not alleged sufficient facts to conform with the specificity requirement as required for fraud-based claims. The Court is persuaded by Defendant’s argument that Plaintiff does not meet the required pleading standard. There are no allegations in the Complaint that the representative they spoke with had the authority to speak on behalf of Defendant. (Compl., ¶ 12, 48, 57.) The Complaint alleges that a misrepresentation was made by, or that the alleged misrepresentation was about, the source, sponsorship, approval, or certification of his vehicle. But, the allegations are conclusory and do not meet the particularity requirement. In the absence of a timely opposition to dispute the arguments made by Defendant, the Court finds that the Complaint is deficient.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s fourth and fifth causes of action.

 

            Sixth Cause of Action – Promissory Fraud/Fraudulent Inducement

 

            ““Promissory fraud” is a subspecies of the action for fraud and deceit. A promise to do something necessarily implies the intention to perform; hence, where a promise is made without such intention, there is an implied misrepresentation of fact that may be actionable fraud.” (Lazar, supra, 12 Cal.4th at p. 638.) “An action for promissory fraud may lie where a defendant fraudulently induces the plaintiff to enter into a contract.” (Ibid.)

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff did not plead fraud with the requisite specificity to allege that Defendant knew of the facts and allegations that were made with the intent to deceive Plaintiff. The Court is persuaded by Defendant’s argument. Moreover, the sixth cause of action is related to the fourth and fifth causes of action and also fails for failure to allege with specificity.  In the absence of a timely opposition to dispute the arguments made by Defendant, the Court finds that the Complaint is deficient.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s sixth cause of action.

 

            Seventh Cause of Action—Promissory Estoppel

 

            “‘The elements of a promissory estoppel claim are “(1) a promise clear and unambiguous in its terms; (2) reliance by the party to whom the promise is made; (3)[the] reliance must be both reasonable and foreseeable; and (4) the party asserting the estoppel must be injured by his reliance.”’” (Aceves v. U.S. Bank, N.A. (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 218, 225.)

 

            Defendant contends that Plaintiff fails to allege a clear and unambiguous promise. The Court agrees. As addressed throughout this ruling, it is unclear whether the people Plaintiff spoke with had the authority to speak on behalf of Defendant. In the absence of a timely opposition to dispute the arguments made by Defendant, the Court finds that the Complaint fails to allege sufficient facts.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s seventh cause of action.

           

            Eight Cause of Action—Violation of California’s Unfair Competition Laws (“UCL”)

 

            “A plaintiff alleging unfair business practices under these statutes must state with reasonable particularity the facts supporting the statutory elements of the violation.” (Khoury v. Maly's of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 618 (italics added).) Business and Professions Code section 17200 prohibits unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent business acts or practices. (See Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17200.)

 

            Here, the Complaint alleges all three prongs under the UCL, but neither is sufficiently alleged to survive demurrer. Also, the allegations of fraudulent conduct throughout the demurrer have been determined not to meet the pleading standard, and the eighth cause of action is derivative of the fourth, fifth, and sixth causes of action. As to the unfair and unlawful prong, the allegations are also deficient. In the absence of a timely opposition to dispute the arguments made by Defendant, the Court finds that the Complaint does not allege sufficient facts for a viable cause of action.

 

            The Court notes that Defendant raises the argument that Plaintiff has not alleged sufficient facts for a claim for injunctive relief. The Court finds that this argument is procedurally improper for purposes of demurrer.

 

           Thus, the Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s eighth cause of action.

 

                        Motion to Strike

 

            Defendant moves to strike portions of Plaintiff’s Complaint relating to the allegations and prayer for punitive damages under the fourth, fifth, and sixth causes of action.

 

            The motion to strike is rendered moot because the Court sustained the demurrer on the three causes of action.

 

Leave to Amend

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that the pleading can be successfully amended. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

Plaintiff failed to oppose the demurrer. Plaintiff has made no showing to meet his burden to demonstrate the pleading can be successfully amended. Thus, leave to amend will not be granted.

 

Conclusion

 

            Defendant’s demurrer is sustained as to the fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, and eight causes of action without leave to amend.

 

            Defendant’s motion to strike is rendered moot.
