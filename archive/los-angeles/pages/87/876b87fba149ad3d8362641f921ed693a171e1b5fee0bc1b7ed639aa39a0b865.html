DEPARTMENT NWI LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 26VECV03948    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: NWI

26VECV03948 STABILE v. MIDKIFF, et al.

September 14, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

 Motion to Quash Service of Summons

 

I.        Background

 

This is a landlord-tenant action arising out of the rental agreement between Plaintiff Joel Stabile a/k/a Joe Stabile (“Plaintiff”) and Defendants David Midkiff, Augusta Midkiff a/k/a Agusta Midkiff, and DOES 1 through 10 (collectively, “Defendants”), for renting the real property located at 19934 Kittridge Street, Winnetka, CA 91306 (the “Subject Property”) in exchange for $2,695.00 per month. On July 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against Defendant, alleging causes of action for: (1) Fraud; (2) Ejectment; (3) Breach of Contract; (4) Unjust Enrichment; (5) Conversion; and (6) Trespass.

 

On August 20, 2026,  Defendants David Midkiff, Augusta Midkiff a/k/a Agusta Midkiff (jointly, the “Midkiff Defendants”) filed the instant Motion to Quash Service of Summons. On August 25, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition. Per the Court’s August 26, 2026 order, Midkiff Defendants were ordered to file a reply brief at least five court days in advance of this hearing date, which would have been September 4, 2026. (Min. Order, 8/26/26.) To date, no reply has been filed.

 

II.     Discussion

 

“A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: [¶](1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her. [¶](2) To stay or dismiss the action on the ground of inconvenient forum. [¶](3) To dismiss the action pursuant to the applicable provisions of Chapter 1.5 (commencing with Section 583.110) of Title 8.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a).)

 

Midkiff Defendants move for an order quashing the service of the summons and complaint upon them on the grounds that neither of them were served according to any of the statutorily authorized methods for service of process. (Mot. at 2:3-4.) Specifically, Midkiff Defendants contend that (1) they were not personally served the summons and complaint; (2) substituted service was effected; and (3) no subsequent mailing of the summons and complaint was made. (Id. at 2:6-9.)

 

With respect to personal service, “[a] summons may be served by personal delivery of a copy of the summons and of the complaint to the person to be served. Service of a summons in this manner is deemed complete at the time of such delivery…The date upon which personal delivery is made shall be entered on or affixed to the face of the copy of the summons at the time of its delivery. However, service of a summons without such date shall be valid and effective.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.10.)

 

As to substituted service, “If a copy of the summons and complaint cannot with reasonable diligence be personally delivered to the person to be served, as specified in Section 416.60, 416.70, 416.80, or 416.90, a summons may be served by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint at the person’s dwelling house, usual place of abode, usual place of business, or usual mailing address other than a United States Postal Service post office box, in the presence of a competent member of the household or a person apparently in charge of their office, place of business, or usual mailing address other than a United States Postal Service post office box, at least 18 years of age, who shall be informed of the contents thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and of the complaint by first-class mail, postage prepaid to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left. Service of a summons in this manner is deemed complete on the 10th day after the mailing.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (b) [emphasis added].)

 

Here, Midkiff Defendants attest that on or about July 22, 2026, papers appearing to include documents for this instant lawsuit were thrown or left at the front door of their residence but no one personally handed these documents to either of them. (D. Midkiff Decl., ¶4; A. Midkiff Decl., ¶4.) In opposition, Plaintiff indicates that proof of service states that Defendant David Midkiff was personally served the summons and complaint and Defendant August Midkiff was served via substituted service on Defendant David Midkiff. (Opp. at 3:7-16; Tamer Decl., ¶10, Ex. A.) The summons and complaint were thereafter mailed with a notice of acknowledgement and receipt. (Id.)

 

The Court notes that the proof of service documents Plaintiff attaches to the opposing papers were not filed with the Court. “The complaint must be served on all named defendants and proofs of service on those defendants must be filed with the court within 60 days after the filing of the complaint.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.110, subd. (b) [emphasis added].) As such, the alleged proofs of service are not properly before this Court. Even assuming arguendo the proofs of service were filed with the Court as required, they indicate that the summons and complaint were served by a third party who is not a Registered Process Server in California or any other state, thus not entitled to the presumption of valid service under Evidence Code Section 647.

 

Nevertheless, Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel of record, Steven Tamer aver under penalty of perjury that they were present when personal service was made. In fact, Plaintiff and Mr. Tamer state that they personally observed Defendant David Midkiff be served with the summons and complaint. (Stabile Decl., ¶3; Tamer Decl., ¶6.) Furthermore, Plaintiff and Mr. Tamer state that they saw Defendant Augusta Midkiff come out of the residence several times that day while Plaintiff, Mr. Tamer, and the others were waiting for the Los Angeles Police Department (“LAPD”) to arrive. (Stabile Decl., ¶4; Tamer Decl., ¶7.) The LAPD notes given to Plaintiff and Mr. Tamer indicate that “residents refused entry to residence….” (Tamer Decl., ¶11, Ex. B.) The Midkiff Defendants have not presented any evidence on reply contradicting and/or refuting the documentary evidence provided by Plaintiff and his counsel, Mr. Tamer.

 

Taking these facts altogether, the Midkiff Defendants fail to demonstrate that personal service and substituted service was not properly completed.

 

Request for Attorney’s Fees and Sanctions

 

Plaintiff’s counsel of record, Mr. Tamer seeks $4,900.00 in attorney’s fees and sanctions against the Midkiff Defendants consisting of: (1) 5.5 hours drafting the opposition and supporting papers and (2) 1.5 hours preparing for and attending the hearing at an hourly rate of $700.00/hour. (Tamer Decl., ¶12.) However, Mr. Tamer cites to no legal authority for seeking sanctions on a motion to quash service of summons. Additionally, although the Court has discretionary power to order sanctions under the Code of Civil Procedure Section 128.5, subdivision (a), the Court declines to impose sanctions at this time as this case is still in the pleading stages. The parties are advised that any conduct moving forward that fall within the parameters of Code of Civil Procedure Section 128.5, subdivision (a) and upon noticed motion may result in discretionary sanctions.

 

III.  Conclusion

 

Accordingly, Defendants David Midkiff and Augusta Midkiff’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons is DENIED. Plaintiff Joel Stabile’s Request for Discretionary Sanctions is also DENIED.

 

Defendants David Midkiff and Augusta Midkiff are ordered to file and serve their Answer to the Complaint within 15 days of this order.

 

Defendants David Midkiff and Augusta Midkiff to give notice.

I.       BACKGROUND  

On May 20, 2014, Plaintiffs Yevgenya Shevtsov and Tatyana Shevtsov, as heirs and successor-in-interest to Nadezhda, deceased (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) commenced this personal injury action against Defendants Country Villa Health Services, Inc. (“CVH”); West Hollywood Healthcare & Wellness Centre, LP (“WHW”); SR & DR Operating Company, LLC (“SDO”); Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (“CSMC”); RRT Enterprises, LP (“RRT”); Abraham Ishaaya (“Ishaaya”); Michael Borookhim (“Borookhim”); William Stanford (“Stanford”); Fannu Rodriguez (“Rodriguez”); Bradley Rosen (“Rosen”); Eurlyn Villamor (“Villamor”); Lourdes Uy (“Uy”); Eleanor Lavretsky (“Lavretsky”); Igor Shnaider (“Shnaider”); Gadson Johnson (“Johnson”); Niceto Lopez (“Lopez”); Marina Vaysburd (“Vaysburd”); Jay Gladstein (“Gladstein”); Jon Kea (“Kea”); Marina Kovalevsky (“Kovalevsky”); Carolyn Bell (“Bell”); Shaun Miller (“Miller”); Demi Penera (“Penera”); and DOES 1 to 10 (collectively, “Defendants”).

On April 18, 2025, Plaintiffs dismissed Villamor and Uy with prejudice.

On January 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against the remaining Defendants, asserting causes of action for: (1) Intentional Tort; (2) Wrongful Death; (3) Elder Abuse and Neglect; (4) Battery; (5) Civil Conspiracy to Commit Elder Abuse and Battery; (6) Fraud; (7) Constructive Fraud; (8) Fraud by Misrepresentation; (9) SNF Patient Rights’ Violations; and (10) Willful Misconduct.

On April 23, 2026, CSMC filed a Demurrer to the FAC. On April 24, 2026, SDO filed a Demurrer with Motion to Strike the FAC. These matters were fully briefed and came for hearing on June 30, 2026; the demurrers and motion to strike were sustained without leave to amend.

On April 29, 2026, Plaintiffs dismissed Ishaaya, Borookhim, Stanford, Rodriguez, Rosen, Lavretsky, Shnaider, Johnson, Lopez, Vaysburd, Gladstein, Kea, Kovalevsky, Bell, Miller, Penera with prejudice.

On August 19, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion to Strike Memorandum of Costs. On August 31, 2026, CSMC filed an Opposition. On September 4, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Reply.

II.          LEGAL STANDARD

            “Except as otherwise expressly provided by statute, a prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs in any action or proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (b).) “Allowable costs shall be reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation rather than merely convenient or beneficial to its preparation.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (c)(2).)  “If the items appearing in a cost bill appear to be proper charges, the burden is on the party seeking to tax costs to show that they were not reasonable or necessary.” (Ladas v. California State Auto. Assn. (1993) 19 Cal. App. 4th 761, 774.) “On the other hand, if the items are properly objected to, they are put in issue and the burden of proof is on the party claiming them as costs.” (Ibid.) 

            “A prevailing party who claims costs must serve and file a memorandum of costs within 15 days after the date of service of the notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5 or the date of service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal, or within 180 days after entry of judgment, whichever is first. The memorandum of costs must be verified by a statement of the party, attorney, or agent that to the best of his or her knowledge the items of cost are correct and were necessarily incurred in the case.” (Cal. Rules Court, Rule 3.1700, subd. (a)(1).) A verified cost memorandum is “prima facie evidence of the propriety of the items listed on it, and the burden is on the party challenging these costs to demonstrate that they were not reasonable or necessary.” (Adams v. Ford Motor Co. (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 1475, 1486.)

            “Any notice of motion to strike or to tax costs must be served and filed 15 days after service of the cost memorandum. If the cost memorandum was served by mail, the period is extended as provided in Code of Civil Procedure section 1013. If the cost memorandum was served electronically, the period is extended as provided in Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6(a)(4).” (Cal. Rules Court, Rule 3.1700, subd. (b)(1).) 

III.       DISCUSSION

            Plaintiffs moves to tax and/or strike CSMC’s memorandum of costs (“MOC”) filed on August 4, 2026 on the grounds that: (1) it was untimely filed and (2) contains costs that were either inflated, not paid, or not incurred by CSMC. CSMC opposes.

            With respect to timeliness, Plaintiffs calculate the time for filing the MOC based on the Notice of Entry of Dismissal, which was entered on June 30, 2025. (Mot. at 3:22-4:3.) However, the Certificate of Mailing regarding the Notice of Entry of Dismissal indicates that it was served on July 1, 2026. (See Cert. of Mailing, 6/30/26; Not. of Entry of Dismissal, 6/30/26.) Furthermore, the Notice of Entry of Dismissal and Certificate of Mailing only include the addresses for Plaintiffs. (Ibid.) Thus, it does not appear on the face of the Notice of Entry of Dismissal nor the Certificate of Mailing that it was served on CSMC as to trigger the fifteen (15) day timeline under California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1700, subdivision (a)(1).

            Subsequently, Judgment was entered for CSMC on July 20, 2026. (Judgment re Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, 7/20/26.) The following day, CSMC filed the Notice of Entry of Judgment. (Not. of Entry of Judgment, 7/21/26.) Fifteen calendar days from July 21, 2026, would be August 5, 2026. Therefore, CSMC timely filed its MOC as it was filed on August 4, 2026, which is one day before the deadline to file it would have expired.

            As to CSMC claimed costs of $1,027.45 consisting of: (1) $877.45 for filing and motion fees and (2) $150.00 for jury fees, Plaintiff argue that these are grossly inflated or were not actually incurred by CSMC. (Mot. at 4:5-9.) However, the MOC is verified by CSMC’s counsel of record, Sara R. Sabzerou, Esq., thus is prima facie evidence that these costs were incurred. Furthermore, costs for filing and motion fees are expressly recoverable under Code of Civil Procedure Section 1033.5, subdivision (a)(1). Likewise, jury fees are also recoverable under Code of Civil Procedure Section 1033.5, subdivision (a)(1). Nevertheless, CSMC implores the Court to look to its own records in support of finding that these costs were reasonably and necessarily incurred. (Opp. at 4:12-14.)

            In reviewing the Court’s records, there is no Notice of Jury Fee Deposit nor Jury Fee Deposit filed with the Court. Therefore, the Court will strike $23.70 for Notice of Jury Fee Deposit and $150.00 for Jury Fee Deposit, for total of $173.70. To the extent that CSMC contends that the fees sought for proof of service, reply brief, case management conference statements, judgment, and notice of entry of judgment were electronic filing fees recoverable under Code of Civil Procedure Section 1033.5, subdivision (a)(14) (See Opp. at 4:1-6), the MOC does not seek these costs under Item 14 as required but sought them under Item 1, which is only for statutory filing fees. Accordingly, the Court will strike $48.50 for proof of service of the demurrer/motion to strike, $48.50 for reply briefs, $23.70 for CMC statements, and $48.50 for judgment for total of $169.20. Overall, the Court strikes $342.90 in costs leaving a remaining recovery of $684.55 in costs.

IV.       CONCLUSION

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs Yevgenya Shevtsov and Tatyana Shevtsov’s Motion is GRANTED IN PART. Defendant Cedars-Sinai Medical Center is awarded costs in the reduced amount of $684.55.

            Plaintiffs Yevgenya Shevtsov and Tatyana Shevtsov to give notice.
