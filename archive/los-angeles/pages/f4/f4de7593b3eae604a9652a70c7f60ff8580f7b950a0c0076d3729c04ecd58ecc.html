24VECV06495 GREGORIO M GUILLEM vs FCA US, LLC, et al.

August 5, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling -- Motion for Relief As Counsel

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On December 26, 2024, Plaintiff Gregorio M. Guillem (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants FCA US, LLC (“FCA”), Dependable Dodge Canoga Park (“Dependable Dodge”), and Does 1 to 10 alleging five causes of action: (1) violation of Cal. Civ. Code § 1793.2(d); (2) violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(b); (3) violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(a)(3); (4) breach of the implied warranty of merchantability; (5) fraudulent inducement through concealment; and (6) negligent repair.

 

On July 24, 2026, Strategic Legal Practices, APC (hereinafter “Counsel”) filed the instant unopposed motion to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff.

 

II.     Relief As Counsel

 

The court may order that an attorney be changed or substituted at any time before or after judgment or final determination upon request by either client or attorney and after notice from one to the other. (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 284(2).) “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133.)   

 

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Counsel Form MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion)(Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1362(a)), MC-052 (Declaration)(CRC Rule 3.1362(c)), and MC-053 (Proposed Order)(CRC Rule 3.1362(e)). The proposed order must specify all hearing dates scheduled in the action or proceeding, including the date of trial, if known. (CRC Rule 3.1362(e).)

 

Further, the requisite forms must be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case. (CRC Rule 3.1362(d).) The court may delay the effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (CRC Rule 3.1362(e).) The Court has discretion on whether to allow an attorney to withdraw, and a motion to withdraw will not be granted where withdrawal would prejudice the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.) 

 

Counsel declares that there has been a “complete breakdown of the attorney-client relationship” and that Plaintiff has remained unresponsive. (See MC-052.) Counsel provides all requisite forms and trial in this action is set for September 28, 2026, giving Plaintiff adequate time to find alternative counsel. Accordingly, the Court finds good cause for relief.

 

 

Based on the foregoing, Counsel’s motion for relief as counsel is GRANTED effective upon the filing of the proof of service of the signed order on all parties and client.

 

Counsel is ordered to give notice.


Case Number: 25VECV02393    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: I

25VECV02393 BLANCA JUDITH HERRERA vs CITY OF LOS ANGELES, et al.

August 5, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling – Motions to Compel Further Responses

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On April 29, 2025, Plaintiff Blanca Judith Herrera (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants City of Los Angeles (“City”), State of California (“State”), Judicial Council of California (“JCC”), and Does 1 to 50 alleging one cause of action for premises liability.

 

On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel the City’s further responses to her Form Interrogatories, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, and Requests for Admissions, Set One. Plaintiff also requests sanctions.

 

On July 23, 2026, the City filed its opposition.

 

To date, Plaintiff has not filed a reply.

 

II.     Motion to Compel Further Responses

 

Interrogatories

 

Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.300 provides, in pertinent part, that “[o]n receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete. (2) An exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate. (3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.” (CCP § 2030.300(a).)

 

Unless notice of a motion to compel further responses is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (CCP § 2030.300(c).)

 

Request for Production of Documents

 

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete. (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive. (3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 2031.310(a).)

 

Requests for Admissions

 

If a party to whom requests for admissions are directed fails to serve a timely response, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted. (CCP § 2033.280(b).) The party to whom the requests for admission are directed waives any objection to the requests upon their failure to timely respond. (CCP § 2033.280(a).)

 

Sanctions

 

The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with CCP § 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the party acted with substantial justification. (CCP § 2031.310(h).) Under California law, the objecting party has the burden of justifying its objections when the propounding party requests that the Court order further responses. (Coy v. Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 210, 220-221.) Further, under CCP § 2023.010, it is a misuse of discovery to make unmerited objections. (CCP § 2023.010(e).) 

 

III.  Discussion

 

Meet and Confer Requirement

 

A motion to compel further responses shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (CCP §§ 2030.300(b)(2) and 2030.310(b)(2).) On June 25, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel “submitted formal attempts to meet and confer regarding Defendant’s insufficient discovery responses.” (Young Decl. ¶ 8.) Defense counsel did not respond. (Id. at ¶ 9.) Plaintiff’s counsel attempted to contact defense counsel via email and telephone, but counsel still did not respond. (Ibid.) Accordingly, the Court finds that Plaintiff satisfies the meet and confer requirement.

 

Timeliness

 

As a preliminary matter, the Court addresses the timeliness of the instant motions. The City argues that these motions are barred by CCP §§ 2030.300(c), 2031.310(c), and 2033.290(c). (See Oppositions at p. 5.) These code sections provide that motions to compel further responses shall be noticed within 45 days of service of the verified response, or any specific later date to which the demanding party and the responding party have agreed in writing; otherwise, the demanding party waives any right to compel a further response. (See CCP §§ 2030.300(c), 2031.310(c), and 2033.290(c).)

 

Here, the City served its initial discovery responses on May 13, 2026. (Young Decl. at ¶ 5.) Accordingly, any motion to compel further responses must have been noticed by June 27, 2026. (See CCP §§ 2030.300(c), 2031.310(c), and 2033.290(c).) Plaintiff noticed the instant motions on July 13, 2026.

 

Notably, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that, on June 17, 2026, after learning of defense counsel’s unavailability to meet and confer, counsel asked for a three-week extension to July 20, 2026. (Young Decl. at ¶ 7.) Defense counsel’s paralegal could not provide an extension in the absence of counsel. (Id. at ¶ 7.)

 

Notwithstanding the foregoing, while Plaintiff attempted to meet and confer in good faith, the deadlines imposed by CCP §§ 2030.300(c), 2031.310(c), and 2033.290(c) are mandatory. Once the statutory period expires without a timely motion or written agreement extending the deadline, the court lacks authority to compel further responses. (See Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1410.) Plaintiff could have timely filed the motions with a showing of a good faith effort to meet and confer but did not do so. Therefore, Plaintiff’s motions are denied.

 

Furthermore, even assuming, arguendo, Plaintiff’s motions were timely, Plaintiff fails to submit separate statements as required by Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1345(a). (See CRC Rule 3.1345(a).) Without separate statements, the Court is unable to ascertain the sufficiency of the City’s responses. Plaintiff’s motions are also denied on these grounds.

 

Sanctions

 

Pursuant to CCP § 2031.310(h), the City requests sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel, jointly and severally, in the total amount of $3,000.00, or $750.00 per opposition, representing three hours of attorney work at an hourly rate of $250.00 per motion. (See Sawaf Decl. at ¶ 16.) Considering Plaintiff filed time-barred motions which fail to comply with the California Rules of Court, the Court finds sanctions appropriate pursuant to CCP § 2031.310(h). Additionally, the Court finds the requested amount reasonable.

 

 

 

 

 

 

IV.  Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s motions to compel further responses are DENIED.

 

Additionally, pursuant to CCP § 2031.310(h), sanctions are awarded against Plaintiff and her counsel, jointly and severally, in the total amount of $3,000.00, payable to defense counsel within 20 days of this hearing.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25VECV03851    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: I

25VECV03851 ANIKA MCFALL vs DELUXE TRANSPORTATION & LIVERY, LLC, et al.

August 5, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling – Motion to Compel Initial Responses

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On July 10, 2025, Plaintiff Anika McFall (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Deluxe Transportation & Livery, LLC (“Defendant”) and Does 1 to 10 alleging motor vehicle negligence.

 

On November 3, 2025, Plaintiff filed her first amended complaint (“FAC”) alleging three causes of action: (1) negligence; (2) negligent entrustment; and (3) violation of vehicle codes.

 

On May 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to compel Plaintiff’s responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One.

 

To date, the motion remains unopposed.

 

II.     Motion to Compel Initial Responses

 

Interrogatories

 

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses and for a monetary sanction. (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.290(b).) The statute does not contain a time limit for a motion to compel where no responses have been served. All that need be shown in the moving papers is that a set of interrogatories was properly served on the opposing party, that the time to respond has expired, and that no response of any kind has been served. (See Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)

 

Where there has been no timely response to interrogatories, the demanding party may seek an order compelling a response. (CCP § 2031.300(b).) Failure to timely respond waives all objections, including privilege and work product. (CCP § 2031.300(a).) Thus, unless the party to whom the demand was directed obtains relief from waiver, he or she cannot raise objections to the interrogatories. Likewise, for failure to respond, the moving party need not attempt to resolve the matter outside court before filing the motion.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant served their discovery request on Plaintiff on November 17, 2025. (Cotter Decl. ¶ 5; Exh. A [discovery request].) Defendant provided Plaintiff with various extensions to respond until May 8, 2026. (Id. at ¶¶ 6-11; Exh. B [email correspondence].) Ultimately, upon filing this motion, Plaintiff had not served her responses. (Id. at ¶ 11.) Accordingly, Defendant’s motion is granted.

 

Defendant requests sanctions in the total amount of $952.00, representing a $60.00 filing fee and 3.5 hours of attorney work at an hourly rate of $255.00. (Cotter Decl. at ¶ 12.) The Court finds this amount reasonable.

 

III.  Conclusion

 

Defendant’s motion is granted. Plaintiff is to serve Defendant with substantive, code-compliant, objection-free responses to Defendant’s Special Interrogatories, Set One, within fifteen days of this order.

 

Additionally, Plaintiff and her counsel, jointly and severally, are to pay defense counsel sanctions in the amount of $952.00 within fifteen days of this hearing.

 

Moving party to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT CLUB DEMONSTRATION SERVICES, INC.’S MOTION TO COMPEL ARBIRATION AND STAY PROCEEDINGS

TRIAL DATE: NONE

 

I.       BACKGROUND  

On April 20, 2026, Plaintiff Ahron Piell (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants Club Demonstration Services, Inc. (“Club Demonstration”), Abner Morales (“Morales”), and Does 1 to 10 alleging nineteen causes of action: (1) disability discrimination; (2) retaliation for protected activity; (3) harassment and hostile work environment; (4) failure to prevent discrimination, harassment, and retaliation; (5) failure to provide reasonable accommodation; (6) failure to engage in the interactive process; (7) wrongful termination in violation of public policy; (8) whistleblower retaliation; (9) retaliation for exercising labor code rights; (10) intrusion into private affairs; (11) failure to provide meal periods; (12) failure to provide rest periods; (13) failure to timely pay wages when due; (14) failure to timely pay final wages at separation; (15) negligent hiring, supervision, and retention; (16) failure to provide accurate itemized wage statements; (17) failure to maintain required payroll and employment records; (18) failure to provide employment records upon request; and (19) unfair business practices.

On July 10, 2026, Club Demonstration filed the instant motion to compel arbitration and stay proceedings.

On July 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed his opposition.

On July 29, 2026, Club Demonstration filed its reply.

 

II.          LEGAL STANDARD

In deciding a motion to compel arbitration, trial courts must first decide whether an enforceable arbitration agreement exists between the parties and then determine the second gateway issue of whether the claims are covered within the scope of the agreement. (See Omar v. Ralphs Grocery Co. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 955, 961.)(See also Chiron Corp. v. Ortho Diagnostic Systems, Inc. (9th Cir. 2000) 207 F. 3d 1126, 1130 [“The court’s role under the [Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”)] is therefore limited to determining (1) whether a valid agreement to arbitrate exists and, if it does, (2) whether the agreement encompasses the dispute at issue.”]. “The petitioner bears the burden of proving the existence of a valid arbitration agreement by the preponderance of the evidence, and a party opposing the petition bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence any fact necessary to its defense.” (Engalla v. Permanente Medical Group, Inc. (1997) 64 Cal.Rptr.2d 903, 915-16.) “In these summary proceedings, the trial court sits as a trier of fact, weighing all the affidavits, declarations, and other documentary evidence, as well as oral testimony received at the court’s discretion, to reach a final determination.” (Id. at 916.) No jury trial is available for a petition to compel arbitration. (Ibid.)

Accordingly, under both the FAA and California Law, arbitration agreements are valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, except on such grounds that exist at law or equity for voiding a contract. (Winter v. Window Fashions Professions, Inc. (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 943, 947.) 

 

III.      DISCUSSION

Evidentiary Objections

            As a preliminary matter, Club Demonstration’s July 29, 2026, evidentiary objections to the Takash declaration are overruled in their entirety. Takash, as Plaintiff’s counsel, has personal knowledge of Club Demonstration’s pre-suit communications and general preservation of right to compel arbitration. (See Cal. Evid. Code § 702.)

 

Existence of a Valid Arbitration Agreement

Club Demonstration proves the existence of a valid arbitration agreement with Plaintiff.

Plaintiff’s claims arise from his employment with Club Demonstration as a Sales Associate and Sampler. (See Complaint at ¶¶ 11-31.) At the time of his onboarding, Plaintiff completed a Mutual Agreement to Arbitrate Claims (hereinafter the “Agreement”). (Shaddock Decl. at ¶ 7.) Completion of the Agreement was tracked through an electronic Applicant Tracking System called Paradox. (Id. at ¶ 8.) “Paradox is secured through a user-based permission process which allows only certain employees, including [Club Demonstration]’s High Volume Talent Acquisition Pre-hire Onboarding Team and Paradox system administrators, to access and view candidate or employee documentation in the system, including those documents completed in the onboarding process.” (Ibid.) Paradox has various secure electronic verification methods to ensure only a prospective candidate can access their onboarding portal. (Id. at ¶¶ 10-13.) Club Demonstration has provided evidence that Plaintiff completed the Agreement through his secure Paradox onboarding portal. (See Shaddock Decl. Exhs. A-B and D.)

The Agreement provides, under a bold header “Arbitration Agreement Acknowledgement,” the following:

“. . . [Y]ou and Advantage Sales & Marketing LLC (hereinafter the ‘Company’ agree to

resolve in binding arbitration all claims or controversies (‘Claims’) that the Company

may have against you, or that you (and no other party) may have against any of the

following: (1) the Company, (2) the Company’s parents, subsidiaries, and affiliated

entities, including but not limited to Daymon Worldwide Inc. (hereinafter ‘Affiliated

Entities’), (3) the Company’s and its Affiliated Entities’ benefit plans or the plans’

sponsors, fiduciaries, administrators, affiliates and agents, (4) all clients and/or customers

of the Company or its Affiliated Entities, (5) the officers, directors, employees or agents

of the Company, the Affiliated Entities, and clients and/or customers, in their capacity as

such or otherwise, and (6) all successors and assigns of any of them. Under this

Agreement, any Claims will be decided by an arbitrator rather than by a judge or jury.

Both you and the Company may seek to enforce this Agreement.”

 

(See Shaddock Decl. Exh. E.)

Notably, Club Demonstration is a “wholly-owned subsidiary of Daymon Worldwide Inc.” (See Shaddock Decl. at ¶ 5.) Therefore, Club Demonstration proves the existence of a valid arbitration agreement with Plaintiff.

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Club Demonstration’s “entire standing theory depends on the assertion that [Club Demonstration] is an ‘Affiliated Entity’ of Advantage. But [Club Demonstration] submits no corporate records, ownership documents, Secretary of State records, parent-subsidiary documents, or other competent proof establishing the relevant corporate relationship.” (Opposition at 4:10-15.) However, Plaintiff misstates the burden of evidence regarding arbitration agreements. Club Demonstration need only establish the existence of a valid arbitration agreement by a “preponderance of the evidence.” (Engalla v. Permanente Med. Grp. Inc. (1997) 15 Cal.4th 951, 972.) Here, Club Demonstration submits the declaration of Katy Shaddock, Vice President of Talent Acquisition for Advantage Sales & Marketing LLC, who declares that Club Demonstration is indeed an affiliated entity. (See Shaddock Decl. at ¶¶ 3 and 5.) Therefore, Club Demonstration meets its burden of proof.

Next, Plaintiff argues that “[t]he agreement expressly excludes ‘Any claim involving your rights that currently is pending in litigation in in any forum.’ Plaintiff’s claims are currently pending in this Court.” (Opposition at 5:2-5.) However, a plain reading of the Agreement indicates that this clause applies to pending claims at the time the Agreement was signed. Otherwise, the Agreement itself would be made illusory since, as Club Demonstration notes, “[e]very motion to compel arbitration is, by definition, filed after a claim has been brought in litigation.” (Reply at 6:12-20.) This argument fails.

Next, Plaintiff argues that Club Demonstration waived its right to arbitration because “Plaintiff’s November 16, 2025 demand letter requested that [Club Demonstration] identify and produce any arbitration agreement within twenty-one days. [Club Demonstration] did not produce the agreement.” (Opposition at 5:15-18.) However, Plaintiff provides no authority to support his argument that this nonaction constitutes waiver. Additionally, the party asserting waiver “bears the substantial burden of proof” and waiver of arbitration “is not to be lightly inferred.” (St. Agnes Med. Ctr. v. PacifiCare of Cal. (2003) 31 Cal.4th 1187, 1195.) This argument as well fails.

Finally, Plaintiff argues that the Agreement’s “Bellwether Arbitration Procedures” are unconscionable because “[t]hey permit the Company to delay employees’ claims whenever more than ten similar cases exist.” (Opposition at 6:9-19.) However, as Club Demonstration argues, the Bellwether Provision is not at issue in this action because “Plaintiff offers no evidence that ten or more substantially similar cases are pending. The bellwether provisions are therefore not triggered, and Plaintiff’s challenge to them is premature and speculative.” (Reply at 9:6-11.)

Based on the foregoing, Club Demonstration proves the existence of a valid arbitration agreement with Plaintiff.

 

Applicability

            Next, Club Demonstration shows that the arbitration agreement applies to Plaintiff’s claims.

As discussed supra, the arbitration agreement applies to any disputes arising from Plaintiff’s employment. (See Shaddock Decl. at Exh. E [Agreement].) Therefore, the arbitration provision applies to Plaintiff’s claims.

 

III.    CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the instant motion to compel arbitration is GRANTED and the action is stayed pending arbitration.

Moving party to give notice.
