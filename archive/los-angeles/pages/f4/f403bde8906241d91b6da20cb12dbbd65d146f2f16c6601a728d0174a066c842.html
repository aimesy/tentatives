QUASH SUBPOENA

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant, Gabriel René and Third Party Mariam Saffarian

RESPONDING PARTY: Unopposed/Plaintiff, David Thomson

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion to Quash Subpoena Directed to Judgment Debtor and Third Party

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff David Thomson filed a complaint against Defendants Verses Technologies USA, Inc., Cyberlab, LLC, Daniel Mapes, and Gabriel René for: (1) Unregistered Sale of Securities; (2) Violation of Section 25401 Of California Corporations Code; (3) Violation of Sections 45001 and 25504 of the California Corporations Code; (4) Breach of Contract; (5) Breach of the Covenant of Good Faith And Fair Dealing; and (6) Unjust Enrichment. On March 30, 2023, the Court granted Defendants’ motion to compel arbitration. On May 17, 2024, an arbitrator found in favor of Plaintiff and awarded $9,921,298.24. The Court confirmed the arbitration award on April 29, 2025. On May 28, 2025, the Court entered judgment, permitting Plaintiff to “recover his ongoing attorney’s fees and expenses incurred after the 5/17/2024 Award, as may be determined by this Court in the future, until this Judgment is fully paid, and that any such award of attorney’s fees and expenses shall be jointly and severally against each Defendant.” The Court granted Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys’ fees on April 21, 2026.

 

RULING: Granted in part.

Defendant Gabriel René and his spouse, Mariam Saffarian, move to quash and limit the subpoenas served on them by Plaintiff. They argue that the subpoenas are overly broad, request documents not within their possession, custody, and control, and seek privileged tax records as well as documents for unreachable trust property. Plaintiff does not oppose the motion. René and Saffarian’s motion is granted in part.

 

A judgment debtor examination enables a judgment creditor to “obtain an order requiring the judgment debtor to appear before the court, or a court-appointed referee, to furnish information that will aid in the enforcement of the money judgment.” (Lee v. Swansboro Country Property Owners Assn. (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 575, 581; Code Civ. Proc., § 708.110, subd. (a).) “At the examination, the judgment creditor has the opportunity to inquire of the judgment debtor regarding the property the debtor has, or may acquire in the future, that may be available to satisfy the judgment.” (Lee, supra, 151 Cal.App.4th at p. 581.) The judgment creditor may also examine a third party that may have “possession or control of property in which the judgment debtor has an interest or is indebted to the judgment debtor in an amount exceeding two hundred fifty dollars ($250) . . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.120, subd. (a).) “A judgment debtor examination is intended to allow the judgment creditor a wide scope of inquiry concerning property and business affairs of the judgment debtor.” (Lee, supra, 151 Cal.App.4th at p. 581.)

An examination may be conducted with a subpoena of documents. (Lee, supra, 151 Cal.App.4th at p. 582.) “If a subpoena requires the . . . production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by [a party] . . . may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).)

 

René and Saffarian first contend that the subpoenas are overly broad in that they seek documents from the past 5 years. Given the “wide scope” of inquiry afforded to judgment creditors regarding the judgment debtor’s property, the Court declines to categorically limit the subpoenas in this regard. Rather, the Court opts to limit the subpoenas in accordance with specific objections to certain requests. The Court therefore addresses each of René and Saffarian’s particular objections in turn.

 

René and Saffarian argue that the subpoenas seek documents not within their possession, custody, and control because they require them to create documents that do not exist and to produce documents that are in the possession of Verses Technologies and other entities. The Court agrees. Subpoenas may be directed to documents and electronically stored information “under the witness’s control.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1985, subd. (a).) René and Saffarian represent that documents such as inventories of personal property, lists of wages and commissions, and lists of all monies from an estate or inheritance do not exist. [René Decl. ¶ 3; Saffarian Decl. ¶ 3.] They also state that they do not have possession or control or the right to access documents from Verses Technologies or its affiliates. [René Decl. ¶ 3(i); Saffarian Decl. ¶ 3(i).] This includes René’s stock, which he is unable to access or transfer without some form of legal process. [René Decl. ¶ 5.] The subpoena is thus limited to the extent it seeks documents not in the possession or control of the subpoenaed parties.

 

René and Saffarian next object that the subpoenas require production of privileged tax records and documents concerning Saffarian’s interest in unreachable trust property. These objections have merit. There is a privilege against disclosing tax returns. (Li v. Yan (2016) 247 Cal.App.4th 56, 66.) The privilege “will not be upheld” when: “(1) the circumstances indicate an intentional waiver of the privilege; (2) the gravamen of the lawsuit is inconsistent with the privilege; or (3) a public policy greater than that of the confidentiality of tax returns is involved.” (Id. at p. 67.) There is no indication that any of these exceptions apply here. The subpoenaed parties thus cannot be compelled to produce their tax returns. Saffarian’s interest in trust property cannot be reached because the trust was established by her mother before Saffarian’s marriage and there has been no transmutation. [Saffarian Decl. ¶ 4.] (See Steinhart v. County of Los Angeles (2010) 47 Cal.4th 1298, 1319 [“Under general principles of trust law, trust beneficiaries hold ‘the equitable estate or beneficial interest in’ property held in trust and are ‘regarded as the real owner[s] of [that] property’”].) Saffarian therefore cannot be required to produce a copy of the trust. Plaintiff’s subpoena is therefore limited to the extent it seeks tax returns and a copy of the trust for which Saffarian is a named beneficiary.

 

The unopposed motion is therefore granted in part and Plaintiff’s subpoena is limited in accordance with this order.

 

René and Saffarian to give notice.

DEMURRER

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant, Jason Kilpatrick

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Kelly Nicole Deckers

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Demurrer to Fourth and Seventh Causes of Action

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff Kelly Nicole Deckers sued her former employer, Defendant City of Redondo Beach, asserting numerous violations of the Fair Employment and Housing Act. After two demurrers were sustained, Plaintiff filed a Third Amended Complaint on November 21, 2025. Plaintiff alleges that the City discriminated against her and that its employees harassed her because of her need to attend oncology appointments due to her prior cancer diagnosis. Plaintiff also brings a defamation claim against the City, Redondo Beach Police Department Communications Supervisor Jason Kilpatrick, and City Director of Human Resources Diane Strickfaden. On April 6, 2026, the Court overruled the City’s demurrer to the fourth and seventh causes of action in Plaintiff’s Third Amended Complaint for harassment and defamation.  

 

RULING: Overruled.

Defendant Kilpatrick demurs to the harassment and defamation claims in Plaintiff’s Third Amended Complaint. He argues that Plaintiff fails to allege facts sufficient to state her claims. Plaintiff opposes the demurrer, contending that the harassment claim is not asserted against Kilpatrick and that she has adequately pled her defamation claim. Kilpatrick’s demurrer is overruled.

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading “by raising questions of law.” (Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.) “In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.) The court “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .” (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.) In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated. (Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) “If the complaint states a cause of action under any theory, regardless of the title under which the factual basis for relief is stated, that aspect of the complaint is good against a demurrer.” (Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title Guaranty Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26, 38.)

 

Fourth Cause of Action: Harassment

Kilpatrick’s demurrer to the harassment cause of action is overruled because he does not have standing to demur to this claim. The harassment claim is only asserted against the City and Kilpatrick cannot demur on its behalf. (See Carter v. Prime Healthcare Paradise Valley LLC (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 396, 410 [“Allegations of misconduct directed against one defendant, however, do not state a cause of action against another defendant against whom the allegations of misconduct are not directed”].) Allegations of Kilpatrick’s harassing conduct are only alleged for the purposes of establishing the City’s liability. [TAC ¶ 55.] Kilpatrick’s demurrer to this claim is thus overruled.

 

Seventh Cause of Action: Defamation

Kilpatrick argues that Plaintiff fails to state a defamation claim because she does not allege who made the purportedly defamatory statements, the contents of the statements, and the manner and context in which they were made. He contends that Plaintiff also fails to adequately allege that the statements were made with malice and thus outside the scope of an applicable privilege. Kilpatrick further claims that assertion of the defamation claim against him violates the Court’s prior January 27, 2025 ruling prohibiting Plaintiff from adding new causes of action in her amended complaint.

 

Kilpatrick’s arguments as to the insufficiency of the defamation claim are unavailing for the same reasons discussed in the Court’s April 6, 2026 order overruling the City’s demurrer. Plaintiff adequately alleges the content and context of the purportedly defamatory statements because she states that Kilpatrick told her prospective employers that “(1) Plaintiff was in litigation against City, (2) Plaintiff was not eligible for rehire by City, and (3) Plaintiff had been disciplined while employed by City.” [TAC ¶¶ 80-83.] Plaintiff’s allegations are also sufficient whether she is pleading libel or slander. Slander “can be charged by alleging the substance of the defamatory statement” (Okun v. Superior Court (1981) 29 Cal.3d 442, 458) and less particularity is needed for alleging libel where, as here, “it appears that defendant has superior knowledge of the facts, so long as the pleading gives notice of the issues sufficient to enable preparation of a defense.” (Id. at p. 458.) Again, even if Kilpatrick made the statements via written references, there is no indication that Plaintiff had access to the references to spell out the words verbatim in her complaint. And Plaintiff sufficiently alleges malice because she claims that Kilpatrick knew the statements were false and published them “with the intention of blackballing Plaintiff from employment with any police department.” [TAC ¶ 82.] These allegations therefore defeat Kilpatrick’s arguments regarding the inadequacies of Plaintiff’s claim.

 

Plaintiff has also not violated the Court’s prior order by asserting the defamation claim against Kilpatrick. That order only restricted Plaintiff from adding new causes of action. Kilpatrick was a named defendant in the First Amended Complaint, and the Third Amended Complaint merely adds specificity to the purportedly defamatory statements. Kilpatrick’s demurrer to the defamation cause of action is thus overruled.

 

Kilpatrick to answer the Third Amended Complaint within 10 days of this order.

 

The Court declines Plaintiff’s invitation to rule on Strickfaden’s pending demurrer here.

 

Kilpatrick to give notice.

LEAVE TO AMEND

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff, Dinora Yaneth Martinez

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendants, Ron Teeguarden Enterprises, Inc., Jimmy Telles, and Jesus Marquez

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion for Leave to File First Amended Complaint

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff Dinora Yaneth Martinez sued her employer, Defendant Ron Teeguarden Enterprises, Inc. (RTE), alleging that Defendant failed to accommodate her disabilities, compensate her for overtime work, and provide her with required meal and rest periods. Plaintiff also sues Defendants Jimmy Telles and Jesus Marquez, individuals with responsibility to act on Plaintiff’s grievances. Plaintiff asserts multiple causes of action under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) and the Labor Code.

 

RULING: Granted in part.

Request for Judicial Notice: Granted.

The Court takes judicial notice of the existence of its February 23, 2026 minute order and the truth of the results reached therein. (Lockley v. Law Office of Cantrell, Green, Pekich, Cruz & McCort (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 875, 885-86.) The Court also takes judicial notice of the existence of the docket for Plaintiff’s workers’ compensation claim. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d).)

 

Evidentiary Objections: Sustained in part.

Objection Nos. 1-2 and 4-5 are overruled. Objection No. 3 is sustained under the mediation privilege (Evid. Code, § 1119, subdivision (a)), but only as to the following: “In later discussions with counsel, I learned that Ron Teeguarden was taking a back seat in operations of the business and Yanlin was the key point of contact.”

 

Plaintiff moves for leave to file a First Amended Complaint adding her former joint-employer, Sourcing Logistics, Inc. (SLI), and Yanlin Teeguarden, owner and chief of operations for SLI and RTE, as parties. Plaintiff also seeks to add 7 causes of action: (1) FEHA Disability Discrimination; (2) Interference with CFRA Rights; (3) Failure to Provide Personnel File; (4) Failure to Provide Wage Statements; (5) Failure to Reimburse/Indemnify for Work-Related Expenditures; (6) Failure to Pay All Wages Due Upon Separation; and (7) Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public Policy. The basis for the amendments is discovery of the additional parties through Defendants’ document production and Yanlin’s participation in the litigation in April-June 2025 and the inadvertent omission of the additional causes of action from the original complaint. Defendants oppose the motion, arguing that Plaintiff’s delay in moving for leave prejudices them and that certain claims are futile. Plaintiff’s motion is granted in part, and she is only granted leave to add the additional causes of action.

 

A motion for leave to amend must comply with the requirements set forth in California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, which states as follows:

 

(a)        Contents of motion

A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:

(1)               Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;

(2)               State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and

(3)               State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.

 

(b)        Supporting declaration

A separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify:

(1)        The effect of the amendment;

(2)        Why the amendment is necessary and proper;

(3)        When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and

(4)               The reasons why the request for amendment was not made

earlier.

 

Dilatory delays and prejudice to the opposing parties is a valid ground for denial. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.) Prejudice exists where the amendment would require delaying the trial, resulting in loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation such as an increased burden of discovery. (Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 486-88.)

 

Leave to amend is generally liberally granted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a); Mesler v. Bragg Management Co. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 290, 296.) The Court will not generally consider the validity of the proposed amended pleading in ruling on a motion for leave, instead deferring such determinations for a demurrer or motion to strike, unless the proposed amendment fails to state a valid claim as a matter of law. (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal. App.3d 1045, 1048; California Casualty Gen. Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1985) 173 Cal.App.3d 274, 280-81 disapproved of on other grounds by Kransco v. American Empire Surplus Lines Ins. Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 390.)

 

Defendants are not prejudiced by amendment to add the 7 additional causes of action. The proposed claims all relate to Plaintiff’s employment with RTE, her disability, RTE’s alleged failure to accommodate her disability (including via a leave of absence), and RTE’s failure to pay wages. Contrary to Defendants’ claim, the wrongful termination cause of action is not barred by the statute of limitations because it arises from the same alleged failure to accommodate Plaintiff’s disability and thus “relates back” to the allegations in her original complaint. (Barrington v. A. H. Robins Co. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 146, 151.) With trial a little less than 3 months away, Defendants will have sufficient time to conduct any further discovery on these related claims. Amendment to add the additional causes of action is thus proper.

 

On the other hand, adding SLI and Yanlin severely prejudices Defendants. Plaintiff has been aware of SLI’s joint-employer status since at least mid-2025 and of Yanlin’s role at RTE since at least June 5, 2024 when she appeared as RTE’s representative at Plaintiff’s workers’ compensation deposition. [Forman Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. A, p. 3.] SLI and Yanlin’s need to conduct discovery and the provision of sufficient time to enable them to exercise their statutory right to bring a summary judgment motion entail another continuance of trial. (Polibrid Coatings, Inc. v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 920, 923-24 [belatedly-added defendant should be given “at least enough time for that party to reasonably complete discovery and bring a summary judgment motion”].) Plaintiff’s justification for delay in amendment due to ongoing settlement discussions up until December 17, 2025 is insufficient, especially in the face of a looming trial. [Jensvold-Rumage Decl. ¶ 8.] Plaintiff is therefore not granted leave to add SLI and Yanlin to the instant action.

 

Plaintiff’s motion is thus granted in part. Plaintiff to file a First Amended Complaint in accordance with this order within 10 days.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.

VACATE DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant, Jesus Adrian Carrizoza

RESPONDING PARTY: Unopposed/Plaintiff, Juan Manuel Vargas

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion to Vacate Default Judgment

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff Juan Manuel Vargas purchased 2 mobile homes from Defendant Jesus Adrian Carrizoza. Plaintiff alleges that Defendant failed to convey title, registration, or ownership of the mobile homes to him. Default judgment was entered against Defendant on January 27, 2026.  

 

RULING: Granted.

Defendant moves to vacate the default and default judgment entered against him on grounds of improper service and lack of actual notice. The Court does not consider Plaintiff’s 7-court day late opposition because of prejudice to Defendant’s ability to respond. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).) Because Defendant establishes he lacked actual notice of the instant action, Defendant’s motion is granted.

 

A court may set aside any void judgment or order. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) “[A] default judgment entered against a defendant who was not served with a summons in the manner prescribed by statute is void.” (Dill v. Berquist Construction Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1444.) Where service is proper but does not result in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action, a court has discretion to grant relief from a default judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5; Anastos v. Lee (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1314, 1319.) “The notice of motion shall be served and filed within a reasonable time, but in no event exceeding the earlier of: (i) two years after entry of a default judgment against the party; or (ii) 180 days after service on the party of a written notice that the default or default judgment has been entered.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (a).) The motion must be accompanied by “an affidavit showing under oath that the party’s lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party’s avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect. The party shall serve and file with the notice a copy of the answer, motion, or other pleading proposed to be filed in the action.” (Id. § 473.5, subd. (b).)

 

Defendant first argues that the default judgment should be vacated because he was served at his business address rather than his residence. In lieu of personal service, a party may be served via substituted service “by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint at the person’s dwelling house, usual place of abode, usual place of business, or usual mailing address other than a United States Postal Service post office box, in the presence of a competent member of the household or a person apparently in charge of their office, place of business, or usual mailing address other than a United States Postal Service post office box, at least 18 years of age, who shall be informed of the contents thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and of the complaint by first-class mail, postage prepaid to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (b).) The proof of service of a registered process server creates a rebuttable presumption that service was proper. (Evid. Code, § 647.)

 

Defendant was properly served via substituted service. The proof of service was completed by a registered process server and indicated that service was made at Defendant’s usual place of business at 11420 Long Beach Blvd, Lynwood, CA 90262. Service was made by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint with “Bille N.,” the person apparently in charge at the business. The process server also submitted a “Declaration of Due Diligence” showing that personal service was attempted on April 24 and 29, 2025, before leaving the papers with Bille on May 3, 2025. While Defendant avers in his declaration that Bille is not one of his employees but works in the neighboring barbershop, this is insufficient to establish that Bille was not a “person apparently in charge.” Defendant admits that the place of service was his business address [Carrizoza Decl. ¶ 3] and the Declaration of Due Diligence states that on both April 24 and 29 Bille was present at the address and knew whether Defendant was in or out of the premises. Based on these facts, the process server could reasonably deem that Bille was apparently in charge at the business and that Bille was “reasonably likely” to deliver the documents to Defendant. (Chinese Theater, LLC v. Starline Tours USA, Inc. (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 1048, 1059-60.) Service on Defendant was therefore proper.

 

Defendant next contends that the default and default judgment should be set aside because he did not receive actual notice of the suit. He states in his declaration that he “never received a Summons, Complaint, or any other lawsuit papers in this case” and that he only found out about the action on January 29, 2026 when he went to the courthouse to file a restraining and searched his name in the court system. [Carrizoza Decl. ¶¶ 2, 5.] On that same day, he requested a fee waiver and a copy of the entire case file and filed the instant motion 2 weeks later on February 13, 2026. [See id. ¶ 6.] These facts indicate that Defendant’s lack of actual notice was due to some failure in the method of service to provide him knowledge of the action and not because of any avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect on his behalf. Defendant’s motion is therefore granted, and the default and default judgment are vacated.

 

Defendant to separately file the proposed answer within 10 days.

 

Defendant to give notice.
