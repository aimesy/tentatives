MOTION FOR RELIEF FROM POTENTIAL WAIVER OF TRIAL BY JURY

 

Motion filed on April 9, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiffs Raul Gutierrez and Maria Angelica Gutierrez

RESPONDING PARTY: None

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Relief from any potential waiver of trial by jury.

RULING: Granted.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On April 9, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion for relief from any potential waiver of trial by jury. No timely opposition has been filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 631, subdivision (g) provides that “[t]he court may, in its discretion upon just terms, allow a trial by jury although there may have been a waiver of a trial by jury.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 631, subd. (g).) “[G]iven the public policy favoring trial by jury, the trial court should grant a motion to be relieved of a jury waiver unless, and except, where granting such a motion would work serious hardship to the objecting party.” (Gann v. Williams Brothers Realty, Inc. (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 1698, 1703-1704.) “[T]he primary consideration is indeed whether granting relief from waiver would result in any hardship to other parties or to the court, such as delay in rescheduling the trial for a jury or inconvenience to witnesses.” (TriCoast Builders, Inc. v. Fonnegra (2024) 15 Cal.5th 766, 780.)

 

Here, Plaintiffs seek relief from any potential jury waiver based on their failure to timely post jury fees. Plaintiffs requested a jury trial in their February 5, 2024 Case Management Statement. Defendants also requested a jury trial in their February 2, 2024 Case Management Statement. At the February 21, 2024 case management conference, the Court (Dept. F43) set the matter for jury trial on March 17, 2025. Plaintiffs’ counsel states that the failure to pay the jury fees was inadvertent, that counsel discovered the omission during a review of the Court’s register of actions at the end of March 2026, and that Plaintiffs posted jury fees and served notice on April 9, 2026. (Eiden Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) The motion is unopposed. Trial is currently set for August 31, 2026. The Court finds granting relief will not otherwise require a continuance, inconvenience witnesses, prejudice any party, or impose any hardship on the Court.


CONCLUSION

 

Plaintiffs’ motion for relief from potential waiver of trial by jury is GRANTED.

DEFENDANT HAMID FAYAZ-RAD’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER DEPOSITION AND TESTIMONY OF PLAINTIFF SAMMY MOUSAVI/FOR LEAVE TO TAKE SUBSEQUENT DEPOSITION; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

Motion filed on May 13, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Hamid Fayaz-Rad

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Sammy Mousavi

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Order compelling Plaintiff to appear for further deposition testimony, or alternatively granting leave to take a subsequent deposition, and monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and his counsel.

RULING: Granted in part. Plaintiff shall appear for one limited deposition session. Sanctions denied.

 

BACKGROUND

On February 1, 2024, Plaintiff Sammy Mousavi filed this premises liability action against Defendant Hamid Fayaz-Rad, individually and as trustee for the Fayazrad Family Trust, the Fayazrad Family Trust, and Does 1 through 50. On June 6, 2025, Defendant filed a prior motion to compel further deposition testimony. On September 15, 2025, the Court granted that motion in part, ordered Plaintiff to answer questions seven through sixteen, twenty, and twenty-two, denied the motion as to questions one through six, seventeen through nineteen, and twenty-one, and denied Defendant’s request for sanctions. On March 6, 2026, the Court denied Defendant’s ex parte application for clarification of the September 15, 2025 order without prejudice to noticed motion practice. Defendant filed this motion on May 13, 2026. Plaintiff opposed on June 18, 2026. Defendant replied on June 23, 2026.

 

DISCUSSION

“Any party may obtain discovery . . . by taking in California the oral deposition of any person, including a party to the action. The person deposed may be a natural person, an organization such as a public or private corporation, a partnership, an association, or a governmental agency.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.) “[A] deposition examination of the witness by all counsel, other than the witness’ counsel of record, shall be limited to seven hours of total testimony.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.290, subd. (a).) “The court shall allow additional time, beyond any limits imposed by this section, if needed to fairly examine the deponent or if the deponent, another person, or any other circumstance impedes or delays the examination.” (Ibid.)

 

The seven-hour limit is “merely presumptive” and applies only if the court does not order otherwise. (Certainteed Corp. v. Superior Court (2014) 222 Cal.App.4th 1053, 1060.) Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.290 not only authorizes the court to allow additional time, but requires it if the additional time is needed to fairly examine the deponent or if any person or circumstance impedes or delays the examination, unless the court determines the deposition should be limited for another reason. (Id. at pp. 1061-1062; Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.290, subds. (a), (c).)

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.610, once a party has taken the deposition of a natural person, that party may not take a subsequent deposition without leave of court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.610, subd. (a).) The court may grant leave “for good cause shown.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.610, subd. (b).) “Good cause requires a showing of ‘specific facts justifying discovery,’ and the objective of such discovery is not merely the discovery of admissible evidence but also effective preparation for trial and the prevention of surprise at trial.” (Associated Brewers Distributing Co. v. Superior Court (1967) 65 Cal.2d 583, 586-588.)

 

Here, Defendant has shown that limited additional time is needed to fairly examine Plaintiff and that the prior examination was impeded or delayed, but not that an unrestricted subsequent deposition is warranted. Plaintiff has appeared for three deposition sessions. Volume III was limited to the questions compelled by the Court’s September 15, 2025 order, and Plaintiff answered those questions. (Page Decl. ¶ 10, Ex. F.) Defendant identifies remaining areas of examination, including the incident, Plaintiff’s claimed injuries, prior and post-incident medical history and treatment, and damages. (Mot. at pp. 4-5, 8.) Defendant does not show specific facts justifying discovery broader than the limited further examination ordered here. (Associated Brewers, supra, 65 Cal.2d at pp. 586-588; Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.610, subd. (b).)

 

Defendant contends Plaintiff provided five hours and twenty-five minutes of testimony in Volumes I and II, and that Volume III took approximately 30 minutes. (Page Decl. ¶¶ 9-10.) Plaintiff contends the first two sessions totaled six hours and fifteen minutes, leaving approximately 45 minutes before reaching seven hours, and that Volume III took approximately 20 minutes, leaving approximately 25 minutes before reaching the seven-hour presumptive limit. (O’Hara Decl. ¶¶ 7-9.) Thus, under either calculation, the deposition had not reached seven hours of total testimony under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.290, subdivision (a). The record also reflects that the examination was impeded or delayed by Farsi interpretation, technical or audio issues, objections, and colloquy. (Page Decl. ¶¶ 9-11.) The Volume III transcript also shows that part of that session was consumed by record statements, objections, and colloquy rather than examination. (Page Decl., Ex. F.) The Court finds limited additional time is therefore needed to fairly examine Plaintiff. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.290.)

 

Plaintiff argues Defendant forfeited any further questioning because defense counsel suspended Volume II over objection, did not state that the suspension was to seek a protective order, and did not file a protective order motion. Section 2025.470 governs suspension of testimony so a party or deponent may seek protective relief. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.470.) Where a deponent fails to answer, however, the examining party may complete the deposition on other matters or adjourn the deposition and move to compel answers. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.460, subd. (e).)

 

Here, defense counsel ended Volume II after Plaintiff’s counsel instructed Plaintiff not to answer and after stating he would seek a discovery referee or special master and move to compel. (O’Hara Decl., Ex. C at 134:24-135:11.) Defendant then filed the prior motion, which the Court granted in part. The Court’s March 6, 2026 order later denied ex parte relief and made no determination regarding the permissible duration or scope of further questioning under section 2025.290. (Page Decl., Ex. E.)

 

Plaintiff's reliance on Safechuck v. MJJ Productions, Inc. (2023) 94 Cal.App.5th 675 does not require a different result. Safechuck affirmed, for abuse of discretion, the denial of a deposing party's motion to compel a further deposition of a nonparty witness and the sanctions imposed on that party's counsel, on the grounds that counsel failed to meet and confer in good faith and reacted disproportionately to opposing counsel's conduct. (Id. at pp. 699-700.) The deposing party's failure to state a protective-order purpose for the suspension was a secondary consideration, not the basis of the ruling. (Id. at p. 700 & fn. 5.) Safechuck announced no rule that an irregular suspension forfeits the statutory examination time or bars a later motion to compel further testimony.

 

The Court finds good cause to permit an additional deposition session of Plaintiff limited to 90 minutes of on-the-record testimony, subject to the limitations ordered below. To the extent that time exceeds Plaintiff’s calculation of remaining time before reaching seven hours of total testimony, the additional time is warranted because the prior examination was impeded or delayed within the meaning of section 2025.290.

 

Sanctions

 

Both sides request monetary sanctions. Defendant seeks sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010, 2023.030 and 2025.480, subdivision (j). Plaintiff seeks sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.020, 2023.030, and 2025.480, subdivision (j). The Court grants limited further deposition testimony under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.290. Section 2025.290 does not independently require sanctions.

 

Defendant was substantially justified in seeking noticed relief because the March 6, 2026 order left the section 2025.290 issue unresolved, the prior record included instructions not to answer that the Court partially rejected, and both sides' calculations show some deposition time remained. Plaintiff was substantially justified in opposing broader relief because the September 15, 2025 order was limited to specified questions, Plaintiff appeared for Volume III, and the parties had a legitimate dispute regarding the effect of the end of Volume II.

 

Both requests for monetary sanctions are denied.

 

CONCLUSION

Defendant’s motion is granted in part. Plaintiff Sammy Mousavi shall appear for an additional deposition session within 30 days of notice of this order, at a mutually agreeable date, time, and location arranged by counsel. If the parties cannot agree, Defendant may serve a deposition notice consistent with this order and the Code of Civil Procedure.

 

The deposition is limited to 90 minutes of on-the-record testimony. The examination is limited to the alleged incident, Plaintiff’s claimed injuries, prior medical history and treatment reasonably related to the claimed injuries, post-incident treatment, and claimed damages. This Court’s September 15, 2025 order remains in effect. Defendant may not re-ask questions as to which that order denied compulsion.

 

Counsel shall not use the final session for opening statements, record speeches, speaking objections, or colloquy. Objections shall be stated concisely. Defendant’s counsel shall ask nonduplicative, nonargumentative questions within the scope of this order.

 

Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is denied. Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is denied.

DEFENDANT LINE 204, LLC’S DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF’S FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Motion filed on April 1, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Line 204, LLC

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Michael S. Lichstein, Co-Trustee of the Michael and Debra Lichstein Family Trust dated June 4, 2025

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Defendant Line 204, LLC demurs to the first cause of action for breach of contract under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivisions (e) and (f).

RULING: Sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

BACKGROUND

On October 31, 2025, Michael S. Lichstein filed the original unlawful detainer complaint against Line 204, LLC. On January 9, 2026, the Court set aside and vacated the December 18, 2025 judgment, found possession was no longer at issue pursuant to the parties’ oral stipulation, and ordered Plaintiff to file an amended complaint within 30 days. On January 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint against Defendants Line 204, LLC, Alton Butler, and Sira Butler, asserting causes of action for breach of contract and breach of commercial guaranty. Line 204 filed this demurrer on April 1, 2026, supported by the declaration of Jeffrey S. Goodfried. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 16, 2026. Line 204 filed a reply on June 25, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

A demurrer should be overruled if the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action “under any possible legal theory.” (Aubry, supra, 2 Cal.4th at p. 967.) Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”].)

 

DISCUSSION

Real Party In Interest

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 367 requires every action to be prosecuted in the name of the real party in interest. The real party in interest is “the person possessing the right sued upon by reason of the substantive law[.]” (Powers v. Ashton (1975) 45 Cal.App.3d 783, 787.) “Where the complaint states a cause of action in someone, but not in the plaintiff, a general demurrer for failure to state a cause of action will be sustained.” (Parker v. Bowron (1953) 40 Cal.2d 344, 351.) A trust itself is not a separate legal entity and cannot sue in its own name. (Portico Management Group, LLC v. Harrison (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 464, 473.) Where a cause of action is prosecuted on behalf of an express trust, “the trustee is the real party in interest because he is the one in whom title to the cause is vested.” (Powers, supra, 45 Cal.App.3d at p. 787.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 369, subdivision (a)(2) permits a trustee of an express trust to sue without joining the persons for whose benefit the action is prosecuted. Probate Code section 16249 gives a trustee the power to prosecute actions for the protection of trust property. Probate Code section 15620 provides that, unless the trust instrument provides otherwise, a power vested in two or more trustees may be exercised only by their unanimous action.

 

Here, the FAC alleges the existence of a lease, Plaintiff’s performance, Line 204’s breach, and resulting damages. (FAC, ¶¶ BC-1 to BC-6.) The FAC names Plaintiff as “Michael S. Lichstein, Co-Trustee of the Michael and Debra Lichstein Family Trust dated June 4, 2025,” but the lease attached to the FAC identifies “Michael S. Lichstein, a Married Man as His Sole and Separate Property” as lessor. (FAC, p. 1, Ex. A.) The FAC alleges that the lease was made between Michael S. Lichstein and Line 204. (FAC, ¶ BC-1.) It does not allege that the premises were transferred to the Trust, that the lease rights became trust property, that the lease claims became trust claims, or that Plaintiff otherwise acquired the right to enforce the lease in his capacity as co-trustee.

 

The FAC also does not allege facts showing Plaintiff’s authority to prosecute the action as the sole appearing co-trustee. Plaintiff identifies himself as co-trustee, but the FAC does not allege that the trust instrument authorizes him to act unilaterally or that the co-trustees acted unanimously in bringing this action. The trust instrument is not attached to the FAC. If the Trust, pursuant to its terms, gives Plaintiff authority to act unilaterally, or if the co-trustees unanimously authorized the action, those facts may be pleaded. As pleaded, the FAC does not allege the facts necessary to show that Plaintiff may exercise the trustee power to prosecute this action for the Trust.

 

Because the alleged transfer is not pleaded in the FAC, it does not establish that the named trustee plaintiff is the real party in interest. The FAC therefore fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a breach of contract cause of action by Plaintiff in the capacity in which he sues.

 

The demurrer is sustained under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e).

 

Uncertainty

 

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally, reasonably, and in context. (MKB Management, Inc. v. Melikian (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 796, 802.) “[D]emurrers for uncertainty are disfavored, and are granted only if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695 (internal quotation omitted).)

 

Here, the FAC alleges unpaid base rent, late fees, CAM charges, insurance, real estate taxes, other rent-related charges, physical damage to the premises, and damages of at least $880,000. (FAC, ¶¶ 10, BC-2, BC-4.) The damages allegations are thin, but they identify the categories of damages claimed and state an amount demanded. Line 204 can respond to the pleading and test the calculation through discovery.

 

The demurrer is overruled under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (f).

 

Leave to Amend

 

The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

Here, Plaintiff’s opposition identifies a potentially curative amendment by asserting that the premises were transferred into the Trust. Plaintiff may also be able to allege facts showing authority to prosecute the action as co-trustee. Therefore, the Court grants leave to amend.

 

CONCLUSION

Defendant Line 204, LLC’s demurrer to the first cause of action for breach of contract is sustained under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e), with 20 days leave to amend. The demurrer is overruled under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (f).
