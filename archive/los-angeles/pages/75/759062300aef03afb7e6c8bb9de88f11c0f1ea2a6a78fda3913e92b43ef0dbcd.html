Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     July 1, 2026                                        TRIAL DATE:  October 5, 2026

                                                          

CASE:                         Casetta Signater v. Keppel Union School District

 

CASE NO.:                 23STCV28732

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE (NOS. 16, 17, AND 41), AND TO COMPEL PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS IDENTIFIED IN DEFENDANT’S MAY 4, 2026 SUPPLEMENTAL PRIVILEGE LOG; REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

AGAINST DEFENDANT AND ATTORNEY OF RECORD IN THE AMOUNT OF $12,000.00

 

PLAINTIFF'S MOTION TO COMPEL FIVE PROPERLY NOTICED DEPOSITIONS

 

DEFENDANT KEPPEL UNION SCHOOL DISTRICT’S MOTION TO COMPEL THE DEPOSITION OF THE PERSON MOST KNOWLEDGABLE FOR WONDERFUL COLLEGE PREP ACADEMY – H.R. AND REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS

 

DEFENDANT KEPPEL UNION SCHOOL DISTRICT’S MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF CASETTA SIGNATER’S APPEARANCE FOR A DEPOSITION AND FOR SANCTIONS IN THE AMOUNT OF $5,963.20 AGAINST PLAINTIFF CASETTA SIGNATER AND HER ATTORNEY, DARRYL LUCIEN, ESQ

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

            Plaintiff Casetta Signater (“Plaintiff”) brings this FEHA employment action against defendant Keppel Union District School (“Defendant”). 

Before the court are four motions: (1) Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further; (2) Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Five Depositions; (3) Defendant’s Motion to Compel Person Most Knowledgeable (PMK) Deposition; and (4) Defendant’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Deposition.  The court addresses these motions in turn.

II.        DISCUSSION RE MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER

            A.  Background

            On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel Defendant’s further responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 16, 17, and 41.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendant and its counsel of record.

            On June 17, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. Defendant requests sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel of record.

            On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

            B.  Legal Standard

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.310, a party may move for a further response to production demands where an answer to the discovery requests is evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general.¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

Notice of the motions must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(c).)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(a)(3) requires that all motions for responses involving further discovery contain a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses.¿¿ 

 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

Sanctions shall be awarded against any party, person or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further responses, unless the Court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(h).)¿¿ 

¿ 

Sanctions may also be imposed against counsel if a party seeking sanctions against an attorney offers sufficient evidence of a misuse of the discovery process, and the attorney fails to demonstrate that he or she did not recommend that conduct. (Kwan Software Engineering, Inc. v. Hennings (2020) 58 Cal.App.5th 57, 81 (Kwan Software).)¿ 

            C.  Application

            Plaintiff moves for a further response from Defendant to Request for Production of Documents (RFP) Nos. 16, 17, and 41.  These RFPs concern the investigation file of Chris Reynolds (“Reynolds Investigation File”).  Mr. Reynolds conducted an investigation into Plaintiff’s complaint of discrimination and retaliation pursuant to Defendant’s request.

            The motion is denied.  Driving this conclusion is the court’s view that Defendant has not waived the attorney-client privilege nor work product protections.  Plaintiff argues Defendant has waived these privileges by relying on the Reynolds Investigation File in its motion for summary judgment.  Not so.  Consistent with the Judge Bensinger’s April 3, 2025 order denying Plaintiff’s motion to compel further, Defendant has not placed the adequacy of its investigation at issue.  Defendant’s motion for summary judgment merely noted that the investigation took place and Mr. Reynolds’s findings.[1]

            Monetary Sanctions

            Notwithstanding the ruling, the court declines to award sanctions against Plaintiff. 

            D.  Conclusion

            The motion is DENIED.

            Defendant’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

III.      DISCUSSION RE MOTION TO COMPEL FIVE DEPOSITIONS

A.  Background

On June 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel Defendant to produce the following individuals for deposition: (1) Superintendent Priyadarshini Darbari (“Darbari”) for the continuation and completion of her PMK deposition; (2) Ms. Darbari in her individual capacity; (3) Defendant’s PMK on Board of Education communications, deliberations, and decisions relating to the non-reemployment and termination of Plaintiff; (4) Dr. Jacqueline Cardenas; and (5) Dr. Terry Walker.  Plaintiff also requests that the court extend the discovery cut-off for the exclusive purpose of taking the foregoing depositions and an award of sanctions.

On June 17, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.  Defendant requests sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel.

On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

            B.  Legal Standard

Any party may obtain discovery by taking in California the oral deposition of any person.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.)¿ “If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action…without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450(a).)¿¿¿¿¿¿

            C.  Application

            The court will grant the motion.  The court finds Plaintiff sufficiently met and conferred prior to filing this motion, and importantly, established good cause to reopen discovery for the purpose of taking the requested depositions. (See Mot., pp. 10-11.)  Moreover, Defendant does not oppose the continuance/taking of Ms. Dabari’s deposition in her individual capacity and as Defendant’s PMK.[2]

            Monetary Sanctions

Given the ruling, the court considers only Plaintiff’s request for sanctions.  If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿Here, Plaintiff does not identify against whom sanctions are sought in her notice of motion.

            D.  Conclusion

            The motion is GRANTED.  Defendant is ordered to produce Ms. Darbari, Dr. Walker, and Ms. Cardenas for deposition within 30 days of this order. 

            The requests for sanctions are DENIED.  

IV.       DISCUSSION RE MOTION TO COMPEL PMK DEPOSITION

A.  Background

On June 11, 2026, Defendant filed a motion to compel Plaintiff’s former employer, Wonderful College Prep Academy – H.R., to produce a PMK for deposition.  Defendant does not request sanctions.

On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.  Plaintiff does not request sanctions.

On June 24, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

            B.  Legal Standard

A party is entitled to obtain discovery regarding any non-privileged matter that is relevant to the subject matter of the action.¿ (Code of Civil Procedure § 2017.010.)¿ A party seeking discovery from a person who is not a party to the action may obtain discovery by oral deposition, written deposition, or deposition subpoena for production of business records.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.010.)

¿¿¿¿ 

If the nonparty deponent is an organization, to any officer, director, custodian of records, or to any agent or employee authorized by the organization to accept service of a subpoena.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.220(b)(1).)¿ Personal service of any deposition subpoena is effective to require the personal attendance and testimony of the nonparty deponent, if the subpoena so specifies.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.220(c)(1).)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

“A written notice and all moving papers supporting a motion to compel an answer to a deposition question or to compel production of a document or tangible thing from a nonparty deponent must be personally served on the nonparty deponent unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail or electronic service at an address or electronic service address specified on the deposition record.”¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1346.)¿¿ 

            C.  Application

            The court will deny the motion.  Defendant does not provide proof of service of this motion on the nonparty deponent.  Ruling on this motion without proof of service would deny nonparty deponent of notice and an opportunity to be heard.

            D.  Conclusion

            The motion is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

V.        DISCUSSION RE MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S DEPOSITION

A.  Background

On June 11, 2026, Defendant filed this motion to compel Plaintiff to complete her deposition.  Defendant requests sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel of record.

On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

On June 24, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

            B.  Legal Standard

            See above.

            C.  Application

            The motion is granted.  It is undisputed Plaintiff did not complete her deposition.  Further, Plaintiff does not oppose the completion of her deposition.  Rather, if the court is inclined to grant the motion, Plaintiff requests that her continued deposition be ordered to take place in a single or consecutive days as necessary.  The request is reasonable.  The court will order the deposition subject to these conditions.  Discovery is likewise reopened and extended for this purpose.

            Monetary Sanctions

            Defendant’s request for sanctions is denied.  Although Plaintiff untimely objected to the notice of deposition for her continued deposition, Defendant was required to obtain leave of court to depose Plaintiff more than once.  The court finds Plaintiff had substantial justification in opposing this motion to ensure any continued deposition is completed in a single day, or alternatively, over consecutive days.

            D.  Conclusion

            The motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to appear for her continued deposition within 30 days of this order.  The deposition is to be completed in a single day, or alternatively, over consecutive days.

            Defendant’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

VI.       DISPOSITIONS

 

            1.  Plaintiff’s motion to compel further is Denied.  The requests for sanctions are Denied.

 

            2.  Plaintiff’s motion to compel five depositions is Granted.   Defendant is ordered to produce Ms. Darbari, Dr. Walker, and Ms. Cardenas for deposition within 30 days of this order.  Discovery is reopened and extended for this purpose.  The request for sanctions is Denied.

 

3.  Defendant’s motion to compel PMK deposition is Denied Without Prejudice.

 

            4. Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s deposition is Granted.  Plaintiff is ordered to appear for her continued deposition within 30 days of this order.  The deposition is to be completed in a single day, or alternatively, over consecutive days.  Discovery is likewise reopened and extended for this purpose.  The request for sanctions is Denied.

 

            Moving parties to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 25STCV25686    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

CASE:                         Anna Pink v. Joe Contreras, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV25686

 

 

MOTIONS TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO CATERPILLAR INC.’S FORM INTERROGATORIES, SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES, REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant Caterpillar, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

                       

On June 3, 2026, Defendant, Caterpillar, Inc., filed these motions to compel responses from Plaintiff, Anna Pink, to Defendant’s first set of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production of Documents.  Defendant also seeks sanctions against Plaintiff. 

            The motions are unopposed.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

¿ 

If a party to whom interrogatories and inspection demands were directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order to compel responses without objections.¿(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(b), 2031.300(b).)  Failure to timely serve responses waives objections to the requests. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a), 2031.300(a).)¿A party moving to compel discovery responses under these statutory provisions is not required to meet and confer prior to filing the motion.¿(See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(b), 2031.300(b); see also Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411 [citing Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 906 for the proposition that “meet and confer” requirement “did not apply when propounding party sought order compelling responses to interrogatories and sanctions for responding party's failure to respond ‘within the statutorily permitted time’”].)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

Monetary Sanctions¿ 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

If the court finds that a party has unsuccessfully made or opposed a motion to compel responses, the court “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(c); 2031.300(c).) With respect to untimely responses to admissions requests, sanctions are mandatory.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(a).)    

 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

Defendant moves the court for an order compelling Plaintiff to provide responses to Defendant’s first set of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production of Documents.¿¿¿¿ 

 

It is undisputed that Defendant properly served Plaintiff with the at-issue discovery requests on April 9, 2026.¿ (See Guest Decls., ¶ 2, Exs. A.)  To date, Plaintiff has not served responses.¿ (Guest Decls., ¶¶ 3.)  Accordingly, all objections to the discovery requests are waived.  Defendant is entitled to an order compelling responses to the interrogatories and inspection demands.¿¿ 

¿¿ 

¿Monetary Sanctions¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Defendant request sanctions against Plaintiff.¿ Given the court’s ruling, sanctions are warranted.¿ Accordingly, sanctions are imposed against Plaintiff in the total requested amount of $1,145.50.¿¿¿ 

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

The unopposed motions are GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to provide verified, objection-free responses to Defendant’s first set of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, Requests for Production of Documents within 30 days of this order.¿ 

 

 

Defendant’s request for sanctions is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to pay sanctions in the sum of $1,145.50 within 30 days of the date of this order. 

 

Defendant to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 26STCV01060    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:       July 1, 2025                                                  TRIAL DATE:   May 15, 2028

                                                          

CASE:                         Zhao Zehao v. BMW of North America, LLC

 

CASE NO.:                 26STCV01060

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S DEMURRER TO ANSWER

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Zhao Zehao

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant BMW of North America, LLC

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

On January 12, 2026, Plaintiff, Zhao Zehao, commenced this Song-Beverly action against Defendant, BMW of North America, LLC, arising from the purchase of a new 2024 BMW iX (the “Vehicle”).

 

On February 13, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint.

 

On February 25, 2026, Plaintiff filed this demurrer to Defendant’s Answer.  

 

On June 16, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

 

On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

¿A demurrer to an answer may be¿appropriate if¿“[t]he answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense [or] [t]he answer is uncertain.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.20, subds. (a), (b).)[1] An affirmative defense is considered “new matter” beyond a general denial. (§ 431.30, subd. (b)(2).)  The defendant bears the burden of proof to¿establish¿any new matters. (Harris v City of Santa Monica¿(2013) 56 Cal.4th 203, 239.)  The answer must allege the facts on which the defense is founded. (See¿California Trust Co. v. Gustason¿(1940) 15 Cal.2d 268, 273.)

Ultimate facts¿are¿generally sufficient,¿rather than evidentiary matters or legal conclusions.¿¿(See¿Doe v. City of Los Angeles¿(2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550.) ¿Even where a defense is defectively pled, it may be allowed if the¿Answer¿gives sufficient notice to enable the plaintiff to prepare to meet the defense, in part because unpled defenses are waived. (See¿Harris v. City of Santa Monica,¿supra,¿56 Cal.4th¿at p.¿240.)

 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

            A.  Timeliness

 

            Defendant argues the demurrer is untimely.  Defendant is incorrect.  A demurrer must be filed and served within ten days after service of the answer to a pleading.  (§ 430.40.)  Here, Defendant served and filed its Answer to the Complaint on February 13, 2026.  (Souto Decl., Ex. A.) Electronic service extends the time to file the demurrer by two days.  (§§ 430.40; 1010.6(a)(3)(B).)  Here, Defendant served and filed its Answer on February 13, 2026 by electronic service.  Plaintiff’s demurrer followed twelve days later.  The demurrer is timely filed.  The court proceeds to consider the merits of the demurrer.

 

            B.  Merits of the Demurrer

 

The demurrer lacks merit.  Plaintiff relies on conclusory, boilerplate language to show that Defendant failed to plead enough facts to articulate an affirmative defense.  Plaintiff’s efforts are frivolous.  All of Defendant’s affirmative defenses are pled sufficiently to put Plaintiff on notice and tailor its discovery to the defenses. In the court’s view, this is a clear attempt to drive up legal fees and delay the speedy resolution of this case.  Since all unpled defenses are waived, Defendant need not plead specific facts to support each defense; it need only plead enough facts to put Plaintiff on notice, which it has done here. 

           

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

The demurrer is OVERRULED.

 

Defendant to give notice, unless waived.

CASE:                              Edmund Lee, et al. v. Adobe, Inc.

 

CASE NO.:                 26STCV01352

 

 

VERIFIED APPLICATIONS TO APPEAR AS COUNSEL PRO HAC VICE

 

 

I.         BACKGROUND 

On June 1, 2026, Applicants, Morris Dweck and Eric Freiman, filed these verified applications to appear pro hac vice in this action on behalf of Plaintiffs Edmund Lee, Samantha Harris, Victoria Donovan, Ciara Holt, Vicky Dobbin, Andrea-Kayle Andaya and Zachary Place.

The applications are unopposed. 

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD 

 

California Rules of Court, rule 9.40 provides that an attorney in good standing in another jurisdiction may apply to appear as counsel pro hac vice in the State of California by filing a verified application together with proof of service by mail of a copy of the application and notice of hearing on all parties who have appeared in the case and on the State Bar of California at its San Francisco office, with payment of a $50.00 fee, so long as that attorney is not a resident of the State of California, and is not regularly engaged in substantial business, professional, or other activities in the State of California.¿ (Cal. R. Ct., rule 9.40.)¿¿ 

¿¿ 

The application must state: (1) the applicant’s residence and office addresses; (2) the courts to which the applicant has been admitted to practice and the dates of admission; (3) that the applicant is a member of good standing in those courts; (4) that the applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court; (5) the title of each court and cause in which the applicant has filed an application to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this state in the preceding two years, the date of each application, and whether or not it was granted; and (6) the name, address, and telephone number of the active member of the State Bar of California who is attorney of record in the local action.¿ (Cal. R. Ct., rule 9.40(d).)¿¿ 

 

 

 

III.      DISCUSSION 

 

Morris Dweck and Eric Freiman seek a court order allowing them to appear as counsel pro hac vice to represent Plaintiffs in this action.¿¿ 

¿¿  

Upon review, the court finds the applications comply with California Rules of Court, rule 9.40.¿¿¿ 

 

IV.       CONCLUSION 

Accordingly, the unopposed applications are GRANTED.¿ It is ordered that Morris Dweck and Eric Freiman be admitted to appear as counsel pro hac vice for the purpose of representing Plaintiffs Edmund Lee, Samantha Harris, Victoria Donovan, Ciara Holt, Vicky Dobbin, Andrea-Kayle Andaya and Zachary Placein this action.¿ Applicants shall be subject to all applicable rules of this Court.¿ 

Moving party to give notice.¿¿
