The Motion for Summary Adjudication. Cross-complainant/defendant Nancy Zelaya (“Zelaya”), individually and as co-trustee of the Jose A. Zelaya Trust (“Zelaya Trust”), moves for summary adjudication on her cause of action for partition by sale. The action involves the real property located at 1200 N. Orchard Drive, Burbank, California 91506 (“Property”). Cross-defendant/plaintiff Gladis Ortiz (“Ortiz”), appearing as the trustee of the Gladis Ortiz Living Trust (“Ortiz Trust”), does not oppose the motion with respect to partition by sale. Accordingly, the Court grants the motion for summary adjudication of the partition action and enters an interlocutory judgment determining that Nancy Zelaya, as trustee of the Zelaya Trust, and Ortiz, as trustee of the Ortiz Trust, each have a 50% interest in the Property. Zelaya’s motion for summary adjudication on her cross-complaint’s First Cause of Action for Partition by Sale of the Property is GRANTED.

Next, Zelaya moves for “summary adjudication” of her entitlement to attorneys’ fees and costs incurred in this action for the common benefit. (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.010.) Attorneys’ fees in a partition action may be awarded when the services were for the common benefit. However, the award of fees should not be made in an interim order but should be reserved for determination in the final judgment. (Williams v. Wells Fargo Bank & Union Trust Co. (1943) 56 Cal.App.2d 645, 652.) The statute cited by Zelaya, C.C.P. 874.110(a) allows the costs to be paid prior to judgment, but entitlement to attorneys’ fees is not a proper subject for summary adjudication, which is limited to the categories enumerated in Code of Civil Procedure section 437c: one or more causes of action, one or more affirmative defenses, one or more claims for damages, or one or more issues of damages (as specified in Civil Code § 3294). Accordingly, the motion for summary adjudication on the issue of attorneys’ fees and costs is DENIED.

I.            INTRODUCTION

On March 21, 2025, plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against defendant Kristina Kotova (“Defendant”) for the collection of a personal loan debt in the sum of $73,741.93. Defendant filed an answer on August 15, 2025. On June 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed this motion for judgment on the pleadings against Defendant. The motion is unopposed.        

II.          LEGAL STANDARD

A motion for judgment on the pleadings by the plaintiff can be made on the ground that the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against the defendant and “the answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense to the complaint.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 438, subd. (c).) The grounds for a motion for judgment on the pleadings must appear on the face of the challenged pleading or be based on facts the court may judicially notice. (Id., subd. (d); Tung v. Chicago Title Co. (2021) 63 Cal.App.5th 734, 758-759.)

III.        DISCUSSION

The elements of a breach of contract claim are: (1) the existence of a contract, (2) the plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) the defendant’s breach or anticipatory breach, and (4) resulting damage.) (Wall Street Network, Ltd. v. N. Y. Times Co. (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1171, 1178.)

“[A]ny matter admitted in response to a request for admission is conclusively established against the party making the admission in the pending action, unless the court has permitted withdrawal or amendment of that admission.” (Wilcox v. Birtwhistle (1999) 21 Cal.4th 973, 978-979 (italics omitted).)

Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s deemed admissions in discovery warrant a judgment on the pleadings because Defendant has admitted that they were issued the subject personal loan, that they were to repay the Plaintiff, that they received monthly statements and never disputed the accuracy of the statements, that they have not repaid the balance due on the loan, and that they do not have any defenses to Plaintiff’s Complaint. Accordingly, Plaintiff has shown that judgment on the pleadings is appropriate against Defendant and the motion for judgment on the pleadings is GRANTED.

IV.        CONCLUSION

Judgment is entered against Defendant in the amount of $74,236.93, consisting of $73,741.93 for the principal amount owed and $495 in court costs.
