Motion for Order Establishing Truth of Requests for Admissions

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.  

DISCUSSION

Defendant Susan L. Caldwell (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order establishing the truth of matters stated in requests for admissions served on plaintiff Aaron Celious (“Plaintiff”).

The court denies the motion as untimely. The last date this motion could be heard was Tuesday, January 13, 2026, 15 days before the previous January 28, 2026 trial date. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2024.020 subd. (a); Stipulation entered May 14, 2025 [setting trial and continuing cutoff dates].) To be heard on that date with proper statutory notice, Defendant would have had to file and serve this motion no later than December 18, 2025. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).) Moreover, the court’s continuance of trial at the January 15, 2026 Final Status Conference did not reopen discovery or extend the discovery motion cutoff date. (Minute Order entered January 15, 2026.) Defendant’s motion is beyond the discovery motion cutoff and must be denied.

 

ORDER

The court DENIES defendant Susan L. Caldwell’s Motion for Order Establishing the Truth of Admissions.

The court orders defendant Susan L. Caldwell to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion for Terminating Sanctions

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Dolores Kent

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   No response.

Motion for Terminating Sanctions

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.  

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff and cross-defendant Dolores Kent (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order (1) imposing terminating sanctions against defendant and cross-complainant Lydia Long Li Perkins (“Defendant”); and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,837.89.

If a party engages in a misuse of the discovery process, the court may impose monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating sanctions.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030.)¿ “Misuses of the discovery process include, but are not limited to, the following: [¶¶] (d) Failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery. [¶¶] (g) Disobeying a court order to provide discovery.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subds. (d), (g).)¿ “When exercising its discretion to determine which form of sanction is most appropriate for a discovery violation, a trial court should consider various factors, including ‘the importance of the materials that were not produced—from the perspective of the offended party’s ability to litigate the case—and what prejudice, if any, the offended party suffered….’”¿ (Victor Valley Union High School District v. Superior Court of San Bernardino County (2023) 91 Cal.App.5th 1121, 1158.)¿¿¿¿ 

First, the court finds that Plaintiff has shown that Defendant has violated a court order to provide discovery.

On April 6, 2026, the court issued an order that (1) granted Plaintiff’s motions to compel responses to requests to produce, form interrogatories, and special interrogatories, (2) granted Plaintiff’s motion for order establishing admissions of the truth of the matters stated in requests for admissions; (3) granted Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions, but not terminating sanctions; and (4) ordered Defendant to serve on Plaintiff full and complete verified responses, without objections, to Plaintiff’s Requests to Produce, Set One, Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Special Interrogatories, Set One, and to produce documents responsive to the Requests to Produce within 20 days of the date of the April 6, 2026 order.  (April 6, 2026 order, p. 5.)  Plaintiff served Defendant with the court’s April 6, 2026 ruling on April 7, 2026. (April 7, 2026 Notice of Ruling.)  However, Plaintiff has presented evidence showing that Defendants had not, as of the date that Plaintiff filed this motion on May 20, 2026, served responses to the subject discovery as ordered by the court.  (Siegel Decl., ¶ 5.)  

Thus, the court finds that Plaintiff has shown that Defendant violated the court’s April 6, 2026 order to provide discovery.  Defendant did not file opposition papers or other evidence with the court (1) disputing her failure to comply with the court’s order, or (2) showing that responses have since been served.  

Second, the court finds that Defendant’s failure to serve the requested discovery is prejudicial to Plaintiff because the demands sought basic information pertaining to the allegations in the Complaint and Cross-Complaint. (E.g., Siegel Decl. ISO Mot. Compel Special Interrogs. Ex. A.) The absence of these materials precludes Plaintiff from pursuing her case in chief or mounting a defense to the cross-complaint. (Victor Valley Union High School District, supra, 91 Cal.App.5th at p. 1158 [court should consider the importance of materials not produced and what prejudice has been suffered, if any].)¿ 

Finally, the court has considered that it has already imposed the less severe sanction of monetary sanctions against Defendants, which has not produced Defendants’ compliance with the discovery rules or the court’s order.  (Victor Valley Union High School District, supra, 91 Cal.App.5th at p. 1158; April 6, 2026 Order, p. 5.)  

Based on the evidence presented, and in light of the fact that Defendant did not file opposition papers disputing her conduct or showing their belated compliance, the court finds that (1) Defendant has engaged in conduct that is a misuse of the discovery process (i) by failing to respond or submit to an authorized method of discovery, and (ii) by disobeying a court order to provide discovery, (2) Plaintiff has been prejudiced by Defendant’s misconduct, and (3) the evidence shows that the less severe sanction of monetary sanctions has not produced compliance with the discovery rules.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subds. (d), (g).)   

The court therefore finds that it is appropriate, and exercises its discretion, to impose terminating sanctions against Defendant.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (d)(1).)  

The court also grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant in the amount of $1,060.00.  (Code Civ. Proc.,  § 2023.030, subd. (a); Siegel Decl., ¶ 6 [2 hours of attorney time expended at $500 per hour + $60 filing fee for the moving papers].)  

ORDER

The court GRANTS plaintiff Dolores Kent’s motion for terminating sanctions.

The court orders that the First Amended Answer filed March 14, 2025 by defendant Lydia Long Li Perkins is stricken. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (d)(1).)

The court orders that the Cross-Complaint filed February 19, 2025 by cross-complainant Lydia Long Li Perkins is dismissed. 

The court orders defendant Lydia Long Li Perkins to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Dolores Kent in the amount of $1,060.00 within 30 days of the date of this order.

The court orders Plaintiff Dolores Kent to file a Request for Entry of Default (Judicial Council Form CIV-100) as to defendant Dolores Kent no later than August 24, 2026.

The court orders plaintiff Dolores Kent to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion to Strike Portions of Complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant General Motors, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Daniel Stoddard

Motion to Strike Portions of Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order striking portions of the Complaint filed by plaintiff Daniel Stoddard (“Plaintiff”).

The court finds that Plaintiff has not alleged facts entitling her to request civil penalties under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.24 because she has not alleged compliance with the vehicle retention requirement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.24, subd. (c).) 

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.24, subdivision (a) states that a party seeking to commence an action for civil penalties under the Song-Beverly Act must, at least 30 days before commencing the action,  “(1) [n]otify the manufacturer of the consumer’s name, the accurate Vehicle Identification Number (‘VIN’) of the motor vehicle, and a brief summary of the repair history and problems with the motor vehicle [¶]” and “(2) [d]emand that the manufacturer repurchase or replace the motor vehicle.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.24, subd. (a).) The consumer must also have possession of the vehicle at the time the notice is sent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.21, subd. (c).) 

The court finds that Plaintiff has not alleged that he has retained possession of the subject vehicle. Although Plaintiff asserts that possession can be inferred from the allegation that he made a buyback request, that argument would subsume the possession requirement of subdivision (c) within the general notice requirement of subdivision (a). Tellingly, subdivision (h), which authorizes claims for relief without civil penalties when there is no valid buyback request, also requires the consumer to have possession of the vehicle at the time of filing of the complaint. Therefore, a complaint under the Song-Beverly Act which, as here, does not allege continued possession of the vehicle at all does not demonstrate conformity with the laws of this state, whether it seeks civil penalties or not.

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  Plaintiff has not specifically shown how he could amend her pleading to correct the deficiency that requires striking Plaintiff’s requests. The court therefore orders the requests for civil penalties stricken without leave to amend.

For the reasons stated above, the court grants Defendant’s motion to strike Plaintiff’s requests for civil penalties as not in conformity with the laws of this state without leave to amend. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 436, 871.24 subd. (c).)

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendant General Motors, LLC’s motion to strike without leave to amend. The court orders that paragraphs 29-31, 36, 39, and the prayer for civil penalties set forth on section e, page 11 line 28 and page 12 line 1 of the Complaint filed by plaintiff Daniel Stoddard are stricken.

The court orders defendant General Motors, LLC to file an answer to plaintiff Daniel Stoddard’s Complaint within 10 days of the date of this order.

The court orders defendant General Motors, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
