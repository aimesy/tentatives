22STCV15026

ROCIO AYALA, et al. vs VALENCIA FULFILLMENT INC., et al.

June 25, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: DEFENDANTS’ MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FORMALLY ADD OR LEAVE TO FORMALLY ADD AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE TO ANSWER.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the Motion.

Defendant may serve and file the proposed Answer as a separate document on or before June 29, 2026.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

ROCIO AYALA and GARY RAND & SUZANNE E. RAND-LEWIS (Plaintiffs) bring suit against VALENCIA FULFILLMENT, INC., JOSE CORONA, and MARISOL CORONA (Defendants), alleging the following causes of action:

 1. BREACH OF EXPRESS CONTRACT;

 2. CONSTRUCTIVE TRUST/UNJUST ENRICHMENT;

 3. VIOLATION OF BUSINESS AND PROFESSIONS CODE §17200; and

 4. INTERFERENCE WITH PROSPECTIVE ECONOMIC ADVANTAGE & BUSINESS RELATIONS.

Allegedly, Defendants refuse to pay the $42,500.00 owed pursuant to a Settlement Agreement having an attorneys’ fees provision.

On May 6, 2026, Defendants filed the Motion to Amend the Answer, to add collateral estoppel as an affirmative defense.

Plaintiffs have opposed, based on several arguments further addressed below.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

“[D]iscretion should be exercised liberally in favor of amendments, for judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters in the same lawsuit.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047.)

Reconsideration is inapposite, where different issues are raised. (E.g., Reedy v. Bussell (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1272, 1291 [reconsideration inapposite where motion for discovery sanctions based on conduct occurring after rulings]; Farber v. Bay View Terrace Homeowners Assn. (2006) 141 Cal.App.4th 1007, 1013 [motion for judgment on the pleadings did not involve reconsideration where court declined to consider unclearly raised issue in overruling a prior demurrer]; Community Memorial Hospital v. County of Ventura (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 199, 204-205 [“But a motion for summary judgment or adjudication is not a reconsideration of a motion overruling a demurrer. They are two different motions.”].)

“[T]o comply with the safe harbor provisions of section 128.7, a party may not bring a motion for sanctions unless there is some action that the offending party may take to withdraw the improper pleading.” (Martorana v. Marlin & Saltzman (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 685, 699.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Defendants request to add this paragraph as an affirmative defense:

“That on July 22, 2022, this Court, Hon. Teresa Traber, Judge, presiding, granted plaintiffs’ motion to enforce the settlement agreement between plaintiff Rocio Ayala and defendants, and ordered defendants to pay the amount set forth in the settlement agreement, as well as attorneys fees and costs incurred in the making of the motion. Judge Traber denied plaintiffs’ request, as set forth in the motion to enforce the settlement agreement, to award additional fees to plaintiffs. Plaintiffs did not appeal that Order, and that Order is final. The parties to that motion to enforce the settlement agreement are the same parties as are litigants in this case, or are in privity with them.”

(Motion, 1:26-2:4.)

Plaintiffs respond with points including these:

• Defendants’ motion is untimely as the law and motion cut off date expired on July 7, 2025. Defendants did not seek leave of court to file this motion almost ten (10) months after the motion cut off date.

• Defendants’ motion violates California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, and is not accompanied by the mandatory attorney Declaration attesting to when the need for amendment was discovered.

• Defendants’ argument that re judicata precludes all of Plaintiffs’ claims has already been rejected by this Court on multiple occasions. Defendants cannot show that claim preclusion and/or issue preclusion apply to this case.

• Defendants waived and forfeited any right to assert claim preclusion and/or issue preclusion by their failure to plead it in their original Answer filed after their default was vacated.

• Defendants’ delay in filing this motion is unreasonable and is severely prejudicial to Plaintiffs. The Court has already held approximately ten (10) Final Status Conferences, and Plaintiffs have been ready for Trial since July, 2025.

• Defendants’ motion is an improper motion for reconsideration in violation of Code of Civil Procedure §1008.

• Defendants’ proposed Answer is procedurally defective, not properly verified, and improperly seeks affirmative relief beyond the proposed amendment. Defendants did not seek leave to assert any cross-claim or affirmative claim for relief.

• Sanctions are warranted against Defendants and their counsel for bringing this frivolous, bad faith motion.

(Opposition, pp. 16-17.)

A court can deny leave to amend after long, inexcusable delay, where there is cognizable prejudice, such as discovery needed, trial delay, critical evidence lost, or added preparation expense. (Solit v. Tokai Bank (1999) 68 Cal.App.4th 1435, 1448.)

A newly raised argument or court ruling may be justification for delay in moving for leave to amend. (Cf. Foundation for Taxpayer and Consumer Rights v. Nextel Communications, Inc. (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 131, 136 [“The argument fails because the FTCR had no reason to amend its complaint until Nextel sought to apply Proposition 64 to this case.”]. Cf. also Richter v. Adams (1941) 43 Cal.App.2d 184, 187 [determining abuse of discretion in denial of leave to amend complaint, partly because recent substitution of attorney was one good reason for delay].)

Courts generally do not consider the validity of proposed amendments to a pleading. (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047; Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2006) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 760 [“the better course of action would have been to allow … [plaintiff] to amend the complaint and then let the parties test its legal sufficiency in other appropriate proceedings.”].)

“Collateral estoppel as a defense need not be alleged in an answer or other responsive pleading,…” (E.g., Rodgers v. Sargent Controls & Aerospace (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 82, 89.)

Here, Defendants state that the Motion is filed, “in an abundance of caution,…” (Motion, 4:9.) And Plaintiffs recognize that, “[o]n November 21, 2025, Defendants substituted new counsel to represent them in this case.” (Opposition, 20:28). However, Plaintiffs argue on the merits that, “Defendants cannot show that claim preclusion and/or issue preclusion apply to this case.” (Opposition, 16:15-16).

All relevant things considered, the Court finds and concludes that the Motion otherwise would have been unnecessary where issue preclusion need not be included in any answer; however, the amendment is now justified because Plaintiffs have filed the Opposition contesting that applicable rule of law. Further, Plaintiffs have failed to show cognizable prejudice by an amended answer, because the issue of collateral estoppel is not triggered by filing the Answer, but could have been injected into the case at any time during its pendency, as analyzed above.

Additionally, the Court rejects Plaintiffs’ procedural objections, as Courts may overlook harmless procedural errors made in the documents or proceedings that do not affect substantial rights of parties. (See, e.g., Code Civ. Proc., § 475; Morgan v. AT & T Wireless Services, Inc. (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1235, 1252; Reedy v. Bussell (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1272, 1289 [“‘Procedural defects which do not affect the substantial rights of the parties do not constitute reversible error….’”]; McAllister v. County of Monterey (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 253, 283 [court is bound to ignore any “ ‘defect … in the pleadings or proceedings which, in the opinion of said court, does not affect the substantial rights of the parties.’ “ ].)

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court grants the Motion, for reasons stated.

Finally, the opposing request for sanctions is denied, in the Court’s sound discretion, as not complying with “safe harbor” requirements, and not addressing any frivolous issues or unnecessary delay.

23STCV22021

LANISHA SIMS vs TRINITY MANAGEMENT

June 25, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO VACATE DISMISSAL.

 

RULING:

The Court continues the Motion hearing to July 24, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Department 311.

Within 5 days Plaintiff shall file Proof of Service showing defense counsel’s address of record.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On September 13, 2023, LANISHA SIMS (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against TRINITY MANAGEMENT (Defendant), listing Causes of Action for:

1. NEGLIGENT MAINTENANCE OF PREMISES [Civ. Code §§ 1941.1; 1714(a); 3333];  and

2. BREACH OF WARRANTY OF HABITABILITY.

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant leased an uninhabitable apartment at 8325 S. Western Ave, Los Angeles, having mold, rat and cockroach infestation, plumbing issues, flooding, leaks, broken walls and deteriorated carpet.

On September 2, 2025, the Court entered Dismissal pursuant to an unopposed Order to Show Cause regarding Settlement.

On January 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion to Vacate Dismissal entered September 2, 2025, on grounds that the parties never completed the settlement for $5,000.00, and Plaintiff’s counsel failed to attend the hearing because of staff error.

No Opposition has been filed, possibly due to lack of notice at the address of record.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

To constitute grounds for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, generally attorneys’ exceptional workload must be accompanied by a factor outside attorneys’ control in an unmanageable situation, such as mistakes of clerical staff such as failure to calendar leading to failure to appear. (Huh v. Wang (2007) 158 Cal.App.4th 1406, 1424.) Generally, regarding miscalendaring, excusable neglect and a basis for relief have been found where an attorney relies on an employee or calendaring system to calendar deadlines, and the employee or system fails. (Renteria v. Juvenile Justice, Dept. of Corrections & Rehabilitation (2006) 135 Cal.App.4th 903, 911.) Attorneys’ or staff’s calendaring errors can support relief, if excusable under appropriate circumstances. (Comunidad en Accion v. Los Angeles City Council (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 1116, 1133-1135 [“trial court abused its discretion in denying … relief from default. The one-week delay in requesting a hearing was an isolated mistake in an otherwise vigorous and thorough presentation of … claims…. [T]he lead attorney made a single calendaring error, not a series of errors resulting from disorganization.”].)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiff moves to vacate dismissal caused by staff miscalendaring, leading to counsel’s failure to appear at the OSC re dismissal after a settlement that the parties never completed.

A mailing or proof of service lacking a fully correct address is ineffective where it “resulted, or could have resulted, in a lack of actual notice….” (Adaimy v. Ruhl (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 583, 587. Accord, Moghaddam v. Bone (2006) 142 Cal.App.4th 283, 288 [“In the absence of proof notice was actually received, the … failure to use the correct ZIP Code invalidates what would have otherwise been sufficient notice.”]. See also Lee v. Placer Title Co. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 503, 509-510 [where notice was not mailed to the “‘office address as last given by that person on any document filed in the cause,’” per Code Civ. Proc., § 1013, subd. (a), notice was ineffective, and order entered was void].)

On February 5, 2024, Defendant filed the last address of record as being in Agora Hills, which the Clerk also used on the Certificate of Mailing, filed July 29, 2025. However, the instant Proof of Service by Mail, attached to the Motion, states another address in Los Angeles.

Therefore, the Court requires proof that Defendant had notice and an opportunity to oppose.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court continues the Motion, for a corrected Proof of Service.

EDITH GUTIERREZ vs RALPHS GROCERY COMPANY, et

June 25, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: DEFENDANT, RALPHS GROCERY COMPANY DBA RALPHS’ MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT.

 

RULING:

The Court denies the Motion.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On January 19, 2024, EDITH GUTIERREZ (Plaintiff) filed a form Complaint against RALPHS GROCERY COMPANY (Defendant), listing Causes of Action for: 1) Premises Liability; and 2) General Negligence.

Plaintiff alleges that she suffered serious injuries from a slip-and-fall, causing injuries, due to negligence in maintenance, occurring at Ralphs at 3410 W. 3rd Street, Los Angeles.

On January 15, 2026, Defendant filed the Motion for Summary Judgment, on summarized grounds that (1) Defendant conducted reasonable inspections of the premises and a sweep shortly before Plaintiff’s alleged incident, (2) Defendant did not create any hazard that caused Plaintiff’s fall, and (3) Defendant lacked a reasonable time to discover and correct any hazard.

Plaintiff opposes, based upon several counterarguments, including that Plaintiff’s Deposition evidences that the slippery fruit was a smashed peach or plum, and Defendant’s representatives have provided some incompetent, impeachable and contradictory testimony. 

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

“[T]he defendant must make an affirmative showing that the plaintiff will be unable to prove its case by any means.” (Weber v. John Crane, Inc. (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 1433, 1439.) “‘It is the defendant's burden on its motion for summary judgment to negate the matters which the plaintiff will bear the burden of proving at the trial.’” (Segura v. Brundage (1979) 91 Cal.App.3d 19, 28-29.) Defendants moving for summary judgment must make a prima facie showing by presenting evidence that a reasonable trier of fact could not find that any underlying material fact is more likely than not. (Y.K.A. Industries, Inc. v. Redevelopment Agency of City of San Jose (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 339, 352.) “The submission of evidence which is equivocal or from which conflicting inferences may be drawn is insufficient to meet the movant's burden.” (Anderson v. Metalclad Insulation Corp. (1999) 72 Cal.App.4th 284, 297.)

“[T]he role of the court in summary judgment proceedings is not to weigh the evidence, but to determine whether there exists a triable issue of material fact.” (Habitat Trust for Wildlife, Inc. v. City of Rancho Cucamonga (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 1306, 1342.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

 

A.    Evidentiary Objections

As to summary judgment or adjudication motions, judges need only rule on evidentiary objections deemed material to the disposition. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (q).) As to summary judgment rulings, the law does not require explanations for rulings on evidentiary objections. (Mora v. Big Lots Stores, Inc. (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 496, 512, fn.15, disapproved of on other grounds by Noel v. Thrifty Payless, Inc. (2019) 7 Cal.5th 955.) “‘[A]n appellate court reviews a court's final rulings on evidentiary objections by applying an abuse of discretion standard.’” (Miranda v. Bomel Const. Co., Inc. (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 1326, 1335.)

The Court overrules Plaintiff’s Evidentiary Objections, received June 5, 2026, only as to the first unnumbered one, and otherwise sustains them.

 

B.     Untimely Reply Documents

“The [summary judgment] reply shall not include any new evidentiary matter, additional material facts, or separate statement submitted with the reply and not presented in the moving papers or opposing papers.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (a)(4).)

The Court strikes Defendant’s Reply evidence and Separate Statement but considers the Reply Memorandum. Moreover, Defendant counterproductively has admitted factual disputes in the Reply Separate Statement, at additional fact numbers 4, 5, 8, 11 through 23, 25, 26, 28 through 36, 38, 40, 42 through 44, and 46 through 54.

 

C.     Merits

Defendant seeks summary judgment based on arguments including: 1) Plaintiff offers no evidence that Defendant knew of the grape Plaintiff slipped on; 2) video clips show the material times and that Defendant lacked notice of fruit on the floor; 3) just three minutes elapsed from when grapes were dropped until Plaintiff fell; and 4) other opposing points are just misleading distractions. 

Plaintiff makes counterarguments including: 1) Defendant failed to prove (a) the area where Plaintiff fell, (b) insufficient time to notice prior to the incident and (c) inspections that were reasonable; 2) a triable issue of material fact exists regarding whether Defendant had sufficient notice of the dangerous condition; and 3) reasonableness of premises inspections is a question of fact.

The elements of the Cause of Action for Premises Liability including Negligence, are these:

  1. Defendant was owner, occupant or lessor of premises;
  2. defendant was negligent in the use, maintenance or management of premises;
  3. negligence was a cause of injury, damage, loss or harm to plaintiff.


(Brooks v. Eugene Burger Management Corp. (1989) 215 Cal.App.3d 1611, 1619.) See also Ambriz v. Kelegian (2007) 146 Cal.App.4th 1519, 1532 [triable issue of fact as to the issues of duty and causation in premises-liability case and error to rule as a matter of law]; Peralta v. Vons Companies, Inc. (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 1030, 1037 [“‘a defendant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law if the plaintiff fails to show that the dangerous condition existed for at least a sufficient time to be discovered by ordinary care and inspection.’”].)

The Court readily determines that there are triable issues of material fact, particularly as to (1) what fruit Plaintiff stepped on, which may not have been the grape that Defendant emphasizes, and (2) whether Defendant had sufficient notice of the dangerous condition in time to have a duty to address the dangerous condition. (See, e.g., attorney Justin K. Renge’s Declaration, filed 6/5/26. Exhibit B (Plaintiff’s Deposition), 54:10-24 (Yes, it was like -- like a peach.  It looked like a peach.  I don't know.  It was either a plum or peach.”); Walter Lozano Declaration, filed 1/15/26, ¶¶ 8-9 (“At 2:24:00 p.m. a lady dropped 2 grapes on the floor in the produce section where the plaintiff fell three (3) minutes later.. [¶] “At 2:27 p.m. plaintiff, Edith Gutierrez slipped on the grapes and fell.”).)

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the Court denies the Motion, for reasons set forth above.

MAYA KOGAN BY AND THROUGH HER SUCCESSOR-IN-INTEREST, SAMAIL KOGAN, et al. vs GRANADA HILLS CONVALESCENT HOSPITAL, INC.

June 25, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: MOTION TO COMPEL DEFENDANT GRANADA HILLS CONVALESCENT HOSPITAL, INC. DBA GRANADA HILLS CONVALESCENT HOSPITAL TO PRODUCE A PERSON MOST KNOWLEDGEABLE.

 

RULING:

The Court orders the Motion off calendar as moot.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On September 13, 2024, MAYA KOGAN, SAMAIL KOGAN, IRINA BRANDALIS, and MARINA KOGAN AKOPNIK, (Plaintiffs) filed a Complaint against GRANADA HILLS CONVALESCENT HOSPITAL, INC. (Defendant), listing Causes of Action for:

1.      ELDER ABUSE/NEGLECT  (Pursuant to Welfare and Institutions Code §§15657 & 15610.57);

2.      NEGLIGENCE;

3.      VIOLATION OF RESIDENT’S RIGHTS (Pursuant to Health and Safety Code § 1430); and 

4.      WRONGFUL DEATH. 

Plaintiffs allege that MAYA KOGAN (Deceased) was a dependent elder who suffered preventable injuries, including pulmonary embolism, cerebral edema, anoxic brain encephalopathy, multiple infections and death, and who Defendant convalescent hospital had neglected and denied.

On May 29, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the Motion to Compel a Deposition of Defendant’s Person Most Knowledgeable (PMK), on grounds that Defendant failed to produce a deponent. Plaintiffs additionally request monetary sanctions in the amount of $3,060.00 against Defendant.

Defendant opposes, based upon counterarguments such as that the deposition date has been set for June 18, 2026, such that the Motion is moot, and meeting and conferring was inadequate.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

A motion lies to compel deposition attendance or document production, after service of a deposition notice, where a deponent fails to appear at, or proceed with, a deposition, without having served a valid objection under section 2025.410. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

Regarding discovery sanctions, courts may determine substantial justification based on the “whole picture” of the discovery dispute, including where parties partially prevailed as to some discovery items in dispute, or where discovery concessions occurred only after improperly asserted objections. (In re Marriage of Moore (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 1275, 1297.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiffs move to compel the PMK’s deposition attendance, and persist in advancing the request in the Reply filed June 17, 2026, based upon uncertainty as to whether the deposition attendance would occur the next day.

Defendant counterargues that Plaintiffs unilaterally set a deposition date, and the Motion to Compel is moot after a mutual agreement to the date. Additionally, Defendant requests sanctions in the amount of $1,351.20 against Plaintiffs and/or their counsel, for failing to meet and confer.

No meet and confer is required to compel initial deposition attendance, but instead there must be a declaration showing that moving party inquired about the nonappearance. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).) "Implicit in the requirement that counsel contact the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance is a requirement that counsel listen to the reasons offered and make a good faith attempt to resolve the issue," including by rescheduling. (Leko v. Cornerstone Bldg. Inspection Serv. (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 1109, 1124. See also Super. Ct. LA County, Local Rules, rule 3.26 [“guidelines adopted by the Los Angeles County Bar Association are adopted as civility in litigation recommendations….”].)

The Court orders the Motion off calendar, as moot, after the deposition date has passed.

Additionally, the Court does not grant either side’s sanctions request, it finding substantial justification as to whether meeting and conferring is even required, and as to the debatable issue whether the parties sufficiently interacted timely in setting the deposition date.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court orders the Motion off calendar and denies sanctions.

RULING:

The Court grants the Motion.

The Court will complete, sign and file the Form Order received-stamped on May 27, 2026.

 

ANALYSIS:

By the Motion, filed May 27, 2026, counsel moves to be relieved as attorney of record for Defendant PEGASUS TRUCKING, LLC, by declaring, in the form Declaration, at paragraph 2: “There has been a breakdown in the relationship.”

Here, the form notice, declaration, proposed order, and proof of service sufficiently comply with the requirements for a motion to be relieved as counsel. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)

Further, moving counsel’s declaration shows a cognizable ground for withdrawal:

·         A breakdown in the attorney-client relationship. (Estate of Falco v. Decker (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004, 1014.)

Finally, no opposing document is filed in order to show any prejudice caused by attorney withdrawal. (See Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(d); Vann v. Shilleh (1975) 54 Cal.App.3d 192, 197.)

Therefore, the Court grants the Motion and will sign the proposed Order counsel submitted.

CHAI GROUP LLC VS CITY OF LOS ANGELES ET AL

June 25, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: DEFENDANT CITY OF LOS ANGELES’ MOTION TO CHALLENGE THE GOOD FAITH SETTLEMENT BETWEEN SOIL ENGINEERING CONSTRUCTION, INC.’S AND PLAINTIFF CHAI GROUP, LLC, AND CROSS-COMPLAINANTS JOHN AND LINDA BEKEREDJIAN.

 

RULING:

The Court denies the Motion to Challenge the Good Faith Settlement.

The Court finds that the settlement was made in good faith.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

In an Amended Complaint, filed July 5, 2019, CHAI GROUP, LLC (Plaintiff) alleges that it sustained property damage in 2027, as to some hillside properties in the vicinity of Laurel Canyon Boulevard in Los Angeles, as a result of the landslide involving contributing responsibilities of CITY OF LOS ANGELES, JOHN BEKERDJIAN, LINDA BEKERDJIAN, and SOIL ENGINEERING CONSTRUCTION, INC. (Defendants), including for previous slope remediation work.

Defendants HOVANES JOHN BEKEREDJIAN and LINDA BEKEREDJIAN filed a Cross-Complaint asserting claims for property damage, indemnity, and related relief.

On May 19, 2026, CITY OF LOS ANGELES (Moving Defendant) filed the Motion to Challenge the Good Faith Settlement Between SOIL ENGINEERING CONSTRUCTION, INC., Plaintiff CHAI GROUP, LLC, and Cross-Complainants JOHN and LINDA BEKEREDJIAN.

In opposition, SOIL ENGINEERING CONSTRUCTION, INC. (Opposing Defendant) advocates denying the Motion, and instead finding a Good Faith Settlement, based upon counterarguments such as that Moving Defendant did not file evidence that meets the burdens of proof, whereas Opposing Defendant filed supportive proof.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

In an action involving allegations that parties are joint tortfeasors or co-obligors on contract debt, “a settling party may give notice of settlement to all parties and to the court, together with an application for determination of good faith settlement and a proposed order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (a)(2).) “Within 25 days of the mailing of the notice, application, and proposed order, or within 20 days of personal service, a nonsettling party may file a notice of motion to contest the good faith of the settlement. If none of the nonsettling parties files a motion within 25 days of mailing of the notice, application, and proposed order, or within 20 days of personal service, the court may approve the settlement.” (Ibid.)

The Tech-Bilt factors are:

  • A rough approximation of plaintiffs' total recovery and the settlor's proportionate liability;
  • The amount paid in settlement;
  • A recognition that a settlor should pay less in settlement than if found liable after a trial;
  • The allocation of the settlement proceeds among plaintiffs;
  • The settlor's financial condition and insurance policy limits, if any; and
  • Evidence of any collusion, fraud, or tortious conduct between the settlor and the plaintiffs aimed at making the nonsettling parties pay more than their fair share.

(Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499.)

Parties challenging the good faith nature of a settlement agreement have the burden to demonstrate that it lies so far “out of the ballpark” of the Tech-Bilt factors that it is inconsistent with the equitable objectives of the statute. (Nutrition Now, Inc. v. Superior Court (2003) 105 Cal.App.4th 209, 213-214.) “‘[A] defendant's settlement figure must not be grossly disproportionate to what a reasonable person, at the time of the settlement, would estimate the settling defendant's liability to be.’” (Long Beach Memorial Medical Center v. Superior Court (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 865, 874 (Long Beach).) “[A] disproportionately low settlement amount may be reasonable where the settling joint tortfeasor is relatively insolvent and uninsured or underinsured….” (Ibid., at p. 875.)

“The trial court is given broad discretion in deciding whether a settlement is in 'good faith' for purposes of section 877.6, and its decision may be reversed only upon a showing of abuse of discretion.” (TSI Seismic Tenant Space, Inc. v. Superior Court (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 159, 165.) Judges’ have discretion in ruling upon a motion for good faith settlement, and the rulings must be made “in view of the equitable goals of the statute, in conformity with the spirit of the law and in a manner that serves the interests of justice”, and must serve the goals of “encouraging settlement among all interested parties” and “equitably allocating costs among multiple tortfeasors”, as opposed to allowing a party to obtain “protection from its indemnification obligation at bargain-basement prices.” (Long Beach, supra, 172 Cal.App.4th at pp. 873, 876.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Moving Defendant reasons that there is a woefully inadequate settlement meant to leave it potentially for the contracted remedial repairs to private property.

Opposing Defendant counterargues that Moving Defendant did not file evidence to support that Opposing Defendant’s remedial repair of an initial landslide had any effect, or that Moving Defendant’s work did not contribute to the landslide. Thus, it concludes that settling for policy limits of $1,000,000 constitutes a good faith settlement. (See Scott Monroe Decl., filed 4/30/26. ¶ 11).

“[T]he determination whether the settlement was in good faith must be based on competent, admissible evidence.” (Brehm Communities v. Superior Court (2001) 88 Cal.App.4th 730, 736.) A determination of a good faith settlement must be supported by substantial evidence. (Norco Delivery Service, Inc. v. Owens Corning Fiberglas (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 955, 962.)

The Court has read the evidence filed by Moving Defendant and Opposing Defendant, and finds that Moving Defendant’s proof is inconclusive, and speculative, as to whether Opposing Defendant contributed to the alleged property damages, and whether Moving Defendant lacks exposure to liability such that the settlement would lie so far “out of the ballpark” of Tech-Bilt factors, inconsistent with the statutory objectives.

For example, the Reply evidence consists of portions of Dr. Philip Shaller’s deposition testimony, dated July 30, 2024 (Exhibit H) that primarily shows the deponent’s lack of knowledge and inability to ascertain causation, and Chai Group LLC’s PMK Moshe Zemach, dated October 23, 2023 (Exhibit I, p. 161) that demonstrates the deponent’s lack of knowledge as to what planting and irrigation work Opposing Defendant had performed. So, Moving Defendant has filed substantial background information about parties involved in arrangements, but which at most is only speculative as to the relative faults of the parties.

In contrast, Opposing Defendant filed evidence, including expert opinions and evidentiary foundation, supporting a finding that it has caused none of the alleged damages.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the Court denies the Motion.
