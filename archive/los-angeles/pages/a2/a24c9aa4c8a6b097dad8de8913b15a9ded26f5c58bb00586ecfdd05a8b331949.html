Case Number: 24STLC03282    Hearing Date: August 27, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Enforce Settlement is granted. Judgment is to be entered in Plaintiff’s favor and against Defendant ERLA Construction in the amount of $4,000.00 principal, $1,000.00 attorney’s fees, and $73.65 costs.

 

Plaintiff is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this ruling within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant ERLA Construction (“Defendant”) on May 7, 2024. Defendant filed an answer to the Complaint on October 25, 2024. On April 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case. On June 10, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Enforce Settlement Pursuant to CCP §664.6; Request for Fees and Costs. The Motion was served on the same day via first-class and electronic mail to Defendant through their attorney. To date, no opposition has been filed.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion is brought under Code of Civil Procedure, section 664.6, which states in relevant part:

 

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (a).) “Parties” include “an attorney who represents the party” and an insurer’s agent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (b).) The settlement must include the signatures of the parties seeking to enforce the agreement, and against whom enforcement is sought. (J.B.B. Investment Partners, Ltd. v. Fair (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 974, 985.) The settlement agreement complies with the statutory requirements set forth above because it was signed by both parties. (Motion, Hernandez Decl., Exh. 1, p. 3.)

 

The settlement provides that Defendant would pay Plaintiff $5,000.00 in four installment payments starting on April 17, 2026. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶1.) Defendant made the first payment of $1,000.00, but thereafter defaulted. (Id. at ¶4 and Exh. 2.) Plaintiff moves for a judgment in the amount of $4,000.00, plus reasonable attorney’s fees and costs in the amount of $1,873.65. (Motion, p. 3:23-25.) In light of Defendant’s default, Plaintiff is entitled to a judgment of $4,000.00 principal. The settlement also specifically provides for an award of reasonable attorney’s fees and costs on a motion to enforce the agreement. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶10.) The Court finds the amount of attorney’s fees sought unreasonable given that the instant action is a straightforward collections case, the Motion is unopposed, and Plaintiff’s counsel can appear for the hearing remotely. (Id. at ¶7.) Nor does the supporting declaration demonstrate that $600.00 is a reasonable hourly rate for the work performed. (Id. at ¶6.)  Attorney’s fees are awarded at an hourly rate of $400.00 for 2.5 hours of time, in the amount of $1,000.00. Costs of $73.65 are also awarded.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Enforce Settlement is granted. Judgment is to be entered in Plaintiff’s favor and against Defendant ERLA Construction in the amount of $4,000.00 principal, $1,000.00 attorney’s fees, and $73.65 costs.

 

Plaintiff is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this ruling within 20 days.

Case Number: 25NWLC23115    Hearing Date: August 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. Motion to Enforce Settlement is granted. Judgment is to be entered in Plaintiff’s favor and against Defendant Paul Hutcherson aka Paul Henry Hutcherson dba Hutcherson Construction in the amount of $20,550.63 principal, less payments in the sum of $6,174.76, for the total sum of $14,375.87, plus $895.37 in interest, $1,750.00 in attorney’s fees, and costs of $527.74, for a total judgment of $20,548.98.

 

Plaintiff is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this ruling within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 6, 2025, Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed this subrogation action against Defendant Paul Hutcherson aka Paul Henry Hutcherson dba Hutcherson Construction (“Defendant”). On February 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Mutual General Release and Settlement Agreement and the instant Motion to Set Aside Dismissal and Enter Judgment. The Motion initially came for hearing on July 22, 2026, at which time the Court continued the matter for further briefing addressing (1) the basis for the costs claimed in the sum $717.84, and (2) the calculation of the claimed interest in the sum of $2,347.68. (Minute Order, 07/22/26.)

 

Plaintiff filed and served a supplemental declaration on July 27, 2026. To date, no opposition to the Motion has been filed.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion is brought under Code of Civil Procedure, section 664.6, which states in relevant part:

 

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (a).) “Parties” include “an attorney who represents the party” and an insurer’s agent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (b).) The settlement must include the signatures of the parties seeking to enforce the agreement, and against whom enforcement is sought. (J.B.B. Investment Partners, Ltd. v. Fair (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 974, 985.) The settlement agreement complies with the statutory requirements set forth above because it was signed by both parties. (Motion, Aposhian Decl., Exh. 1, p. 3.)

 

The settlement provides that Defendant would pay Plaintiff $9,262.13 in three installment payments due on July 14, 2025, August 14, 2025, and September 14, 2025. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶1.) The settlement agreement also provides that in the event of Defendant’s default, Plaintiff may seek judgment in the full amount of liability ($20,550.63), plus interest, costs, and attorney’s fees. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶2.) A total of $6,174.76 was paid towards the settlement, after which Defendant defaulted. (Id. at ¶3.) Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to judgment in the amount of $20,550.63 principal, less payments in the sum of $6,174.76. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶2.)  Plaintiff is also entitled to the attorney’s fees and costs it incurred to enforce the Settlement Agreement. (Id., at Exh. 1, ¶10.) Counsel’s declaration attests to 2.5 hours of attorney time billed at $700 per hour, for a total of $1750.00. (Motion, Aposhian Decl., ¶¶4-6.)  The supplemental declaration now demonstrates that Plaintiff incurred costs on the action in the amount of $527.74 for filing fees and service of process. (Motion, Supp. Aposhian Decl., ¶3.) Finally, Plaintiff seeks interest in the revised amount of $895.37, calculated at ten percent per annum on the defaulted amount. (Id. at ¶4 and Exh. 2.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. Motion to Enforce Settlement is granted. Judgment is to be entered in Plaintiff’s favor and against Defendant Paul Hutcherson aka Paul Henry Hutcherson dba Hutcherson Construction in the amount of $20,550.63 principal, less payments in the sum of $6,174.76, for the total sum of $14,375.87, plus $895.37 in interest, $1,750.00 in attorney’s fees, and costs of $527.74, for a total judgment of $20,548.98.

 

Plaintiff is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this ruling within 20 days.

Case Number: 26STLC00586    Hearing Date: August 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

Petitioner Maria Gutierrez’s Petitions to Approve Compromise of Disputed Claim on behalf of Minor Merida Bucio and Sahara Bucio are denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Minor Plaintiffs Merida Bucio and Sahara Bucio (“Claimants”), by and through Plaintiff Maria Gutierrez (“Petitioner”), filed the instant action for motor vehicle negligence against Defendants Susan Zurita and Norma Carrillo (“Defendants”) on January 28, 2026.

 

On March 23, 2026, Petitioner filed Petitions for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Minor or Person with a Disability. The Petitions were set for hearing on June 11, 2026, then continued at Petitioner’s request. (Minute Order, 03/30/26.) At the hearing on June 23, 2026, the Court continued the Petitions again to allow for supplemental papers to be filed. (Minute Order, 06/23/26.)

 

On June 30, 2026, Petitioner filed declarations regarding Claimants’ medical treatment and recovery, as well as Applications for Appointment of Guardian Ad Litem. The Applications for Appointment of Guardian Ad Litem were rejected by the Court on July 16, 2026, but Petitioner filed new ones on July 30, 2026. Petitioner also filed orders with respect to the Petitions for Approval of Compromise and a Notice of Continued Hearing on July 1 and 2, 2026, respectively.

 

At the next hearing on August 13, 2026, the Court again continued the hearing and ordered Petitioner to file the Declaration of Maria Gutierrez in Support of Sahara Bucios, as well as revised applications for Appointment of Guardian Ad Litem, no later than five days before the new hearing date. (Minute Order, 08/13/26.) Petitioner filed the Declaration of Maria Gutierrez in Support of Sahara Bucios on the same day, but it remains that no revised applications for Appointment of Guardian Ad Litem have been filed, let alone approved for Claimants.

 

The Court cannot grant the Petitions until a guardian ad litem has been appointed on behalf of each Claimant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 372(a).)  The Notices of Rejection explain the necessary corrections and the Court set a deadline for resubmission. In light of the lack of any appointed guardian ad litem for either Claimant, the Petitions cannot be granted at this time.

 

The Court repeats the corrections to be made with respect to the applications for guardian ad litem here. The issues concern the proposed order: (1) The box labeled “Ex Parte” must be checked; (2) Item 2 should be blank because hearings are not held on these applications; (2) Item 4 should be filled out and include the minor’s date of birth.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner Maria Gutierrez’s Petitions to Approve Compromise of Disputed Claim on behalf of Minor Claimants Merida Bucio and Sahara Bucio are denied.

Case Number: 26STLC00747    Hearing Date: August 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

Petitioner Anayeli Osorio’s Petition
for Approval of Compromise of Disputed Claim on behalf of Minor Plaintiff Priscilla
Lopez is GRANTED.

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Court
approval is required for all settlements
of a minor’s claim. (Prob. Code §§ 3500, 3600, et seq.; Code Civ. Proc., § 372.) “‘[W]ithout trial court approval
of the proposed compromise of the ward’s claim, the settlement cannot be valid.
[Citation.]  [¶] Nor is the settlement
binding [on the minor] until it is endorsed by the trial court.’” (Pearson
v. Superior Court (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1333, 1338.) A minor, like
Claimant, “shall appear either by a guardian or conservator of the estate or by
a guardian ad litem appointed by the court in which the action or proceeding is
pending, or by a judge thereof, in each case.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 372, subd.
(a)(1).) Alternatively, the petitioner may file a declaration demonstrating
that they have a right to compromise the minor’s claim under Cal. Probate Code
section 3500.  The Court excuses the
appearance of the petitioner and claimant at the hearing.  (Cal. R. Ct., rule 7.952(a).)

 

Minor Plaintiff Priscilla Lopez
(“Minor”), by and through her Guardian ad Litem Anayeli Osorio (“Petitioner”),
filed this action against Defendant Kathleen Galan for injuries arising out of
a motor vehicle incident that occurred on March 28, 2025, in Sylmar,
California. The complaint alleges a cause of action for motor vehicle
negligence. Petitioner was appointed Guardian ad Litem for the Minor in this
action.

 

Petitioner filed a Petition for
Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of
Judgment for Minor. The petition seeks approval of a settlement in the gross
amount of $15,000.00, representing the total settlement payable by Defendant.

 

The petition includes medical
records and a physician’s report reflecting that the Minor sustained injuries
to her spine, right thigh, shoulders and cervix, and that she has since
recovered without permanent impairment. The Court finds the settlement amount
reasonable in light of the nature of the incident, the injuries sustained, and
the Minor’s recovery.

 

Attorney’s fees in the amount of
$3,750.00 (25% of the gross settlement) and medical expenses totaling $4000.00
are reasonable and supported by the declarations and documentation submitted.
After payment of approved fees and expenses, the net balance of $6,550.00
remains for the benefit of the Minor.

 

The Court finds that the petition
satisfies the requirements for expedited approval under California Rules of
Court, rule 7.950.5. The settlement amount does not exceed $50,000; no portion
of the proceeds is to be placed in a trust; there are no unresolved lien
disputes; all appearing defendants participated in the settlement; and
Petitioner is represented by counsel independent of the defendant and insurer.

 

Petitioner also filed a proposed Order
Approving Compromise of Disputed Claim or Pending Action or Disposition of
Proceeds of Judgment for Minor or Person with a Disability (Judicial Council
Form MC-351), and a proposed Order to Deposit Money into Blocked Account
(Judicial Council Form MC-355). The expedited petition is granted with proposed
orders to be signed this date.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner
Laura Rosales’s Petition for Expedited Approval of Compromise of Disputed Claim
on behalf of Minor Plaintiff Carlos A. Morales is GRANTED.

 

Court
clerk to give notice.

















































 

Case Number: 26STLC01724    Hearing Date: August 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendants Maria Quiroz Merlos, Emma Solache and Lilia Sanche’s Demurrer to the Complaint is placed off calendar.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Why This Action Should Not Be Reclassified as an Unlimited Civil Action and Transferred to the Reclassification/Transfer Desk for Collection of Fees and Reassignment to an Unlimited Civil Court is scheduled for 09/24/2026, at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiffs Araceli Vera, Humberto Solache, Leticia Padilla (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Maria Quiroz Merlos, Emma Solache and Lilia Sanche (“Defendants”) on March 13, 2026. Defendants filed the instant Demurrer to the Complaint on June 23, 2026. No opposition to the Demurrer has been filed to date.

 

 

Discussion

 

A few issues prevent the Court from addressing the Demurrer.

 

First, the Demurrer is not accompanied by a proof of service demonstrating service of the moving papers or notice of the hearing date upon Plaintiffs. Failure to give notice of the papers and hearing date is a violation of due process and of the statutory notice requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.)

 

Additionally, the Demurrer is not accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. Although failing to comply with the meet and confer requirement is not grounds to overrule the Demurrer, the Court retains the discretion to place the hearing off calendar or continue the matter to ensure compliance.  

 

Finally, to the extent that the Complaint for breach of contract seeks specific performance as a remedy, this action is not properly before the Limited Civil Court. (Compl., ¶¶10d, BC-6, p. 3:12-4:14.) Specific performance is an equitable remedy that is not permitted in the Limited Civil Court. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 85, 86, subd. (b).) The Court will set an Order to Show Cause regarding transfer of this action to the correct department.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants Maria Quiroz Merlos, Emma Solache and Lilia Sanche’s Demurrer to the Complaint is placed off calendar.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Why This Action Should Not Be Reclassified as an Unlimited Civil Action and Transferred to the Reclassification/Transfer Desk for Collection of Fees and Reassignment to an Unlimited Civil Court is scheduled for 09/24/2026, at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.
