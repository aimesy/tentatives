Case Number: 21LBCV00639    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: S27

 

  1. Background Facts

Plaintiff, Palmcrest Grand Home Assisted Living, LLC filed this action against Defendant, The Palms Real Estate, LLC for breach of lease and declaratory relief.  The complaint was filed on 12/07/21.

 

On 7/25/22, Defendant filed a cross-complaint against Palmcrest House, LLC, which was the tenant to whom Defendant leased the property, and the tenant who in turn subleased the property to Plaintiff.  The cross-complaint includes causes of action for declaratory relief, breach of contract, and indemnity. 

 

On 10/03/22, Palmcrest House, LLC filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff and Defendant, alleging claims for declaratory relief, indemnity, and contribution. 

 

On 1/03/24, the Court approved the parties’ stipulation and ordered a referee to “hear and determine all issues of fact and/or law” in this action.

 

  1. Motion re: Application of Five-Year Dismissal Rule

a.     Recent Hearings and Briefing

On 6/03/26, the Court held a status conference regarding the status of the judicial reference.  At the conclusion of the conference, the Court ordered the parties to submit a joint stipulation extending the five-year rule or submit briefing on the issue.

 

On 7/29 and 7/30/26, the parties submitted status reports re: the five-year statute.  They did so because of the then-scheduled 7/31/26 OSC re: stipulation of five-year statutory time. 

 

Plaintiff submitted a report wherein it took the position that “trial” commenced on 7/03/25 when the referee issued a Phase One Interim Ruling.  Alternatively, it indicated that, if the issue needs to be determined as a disputed issue, the issue should be submitted to the referee for ruling.

 

Defendant submitted a report wherein it indicated it had been in communication with Plaintiff to execute a stipulation re: the five-year dismissal statute, but needed more time to evaluate its options.  It did not meaningfully brief any issue, did not provide citations to authorities, and just generally indicated it was evaluating various issues before substantively responding.

 

On 7/31/26, the Court set the matter for hearing on noticed motion and scheduled the hearing on 9/17/26.  The Court ordered a briefing schedule per statute.

 

On 8/25/26, Plaintiff filed an opening brief re: the issue.  Plaintiff’s opening brief re-states its position in the status conference report – that the five-year statute is satisfied by the commencement of trial, and that, to the extent satisfaction of the statute is disputed, the issue is one for the referee.

 

Any responsive brief was due nine court days prior to the hearing, which fell on 9/03/26.  Defendant filed a grossly untimely brief on 9/09/26, which rendered review difficult to timely prepare for the hearing.  The Court has read and considered the untimely responsive brief.

 

b.     Referee Determination

The threshold issue on this motion is whether the Court or the Referee should hear this motion.  In its original status report, Defendant argued, without citation to authority, that the referee did not have jurisdiction to hear a major legal question that will determine liability.  In its moving papers, Plaintiff submits authority conceding that this is the case if the trial court acts on its own motion, but it is NOT the case in the event the parties, as they did here, stipulate to the reference.  See O’Donoghue v. Superior Court (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 245, 255. 

 

In the response brief, Defendant argues Plaintiff is confusing a Referee with a temporary judge.  Defendant again cites no authority for the position.  The parties clearly agreed to submit all issues of fact and law to the Referee.  The Court finds the Referee must make this determination, and orders the parties to submit the issue to the Referee for determination.      

 

c.     Future Hearings

The Court has a status conference re: judicial reference scheduled on 2/10/27.  The status conference remains scheduled.  The parties must submit the issue of the five-year rule for determination prior to the status conference. 

 

d.     Notice

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV07341    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiffs, Jesus and Diana Renteria filed 23LBCV07341 against Defendants, Pinner Construction Co., Inc.., et al. on 6/06/23.  Plaintiffs’ FAC alleges the defendants, and each of them, provided negligent drawings, plans, specifications, safety instructions, and designs for Plaintiff to use in constructing a building.  As a result, the building Plaintiff was working on collapsed, causing him to sustain grave injuries.  His wife sues for loss of consortium. 

 

On 3/22/24, Rika Corporation filed this related action against Kern Steel Fabrication, Inc., Pinner Construction Co., Inc., and Hartford Fire Insurance Company for damages including enforcement of claim on public works payment bond.  Kern and Pinner have each filed cross-complaints, and ultimately First Amended Cross-Complaints, against numerous cross-defendants, including the City.

 

2.     4/21/26 Ruling on Demurrer

On 4/21/26, the Court ruled on the City’s demurrer to Kern Steel’s First Amended Cross-Complaint.  The Court ruled, in pertinent part, as follows:

The City contends each and every claim pled against it is barred by Kern’s failure to allege compliance with the Government Tort Claims Act.  In opposition to the demurrer, Kern contends Pinner submitted a government tort claim on behalf of itself and all subcontractors.  Kern argues, “This part may need to be addressed more in the complaint between CITY and KERN STEEL, but there is compliance. (Dec. of Johnson, ¶ 16.)”

 

The Johnson Declaration summarily states that Pinner submitted a tort claim on behalf of itself and all subcontractors. 

 

The issue presented, therefore, is whether one entity can submit a government tort claim on behalf of another entity.  Kern does not address this issue in its opposition papers.  Kern indicates it “may need to be addressed more,” but that is the purpose of opposition papers.  The Court briefly researched the issue, and could not find any authority permitting one legal entity to submit a government tort claim on behalf of another legal entity.  Additionally, the Court notes that the Kern FACC does not even attempt to allege compliance with the Act, and instead the opposition relies on Pinner’s FACC to support its contention that it complied with the Act.

 

The demurrer is sustained.  Leave to amend is granted if and only if Kern can both allege compliance with the Act and, in opposition to a future demurrer in this regard, provide applicable and on-point authority permitting Pinner to submit a claim on its behalf.

 

3.     Demurrer to Second Amended Cross-Complaint

a.     SACC

Kern Steel filed an SACC, wherein it essentially alleges that Pinner Construction filed a government tort claim on its behalf, and therefore it was excused from filing one on its own behalf.  The City demurs, contending this is improper.  Kern Steel, despite being ordered to do so, has not cited any authority supporting its position. 

 

b.     Governing Law

Because neither party meaningfully briefed the issue, the Court conducted independent research in this regard.  The Rutter Guide on Personal Injury litigation explains the following:

Where two or more persons suffer separate and distinct injuries from the same act or omission, each person must submit a claim; one cannot rely on a claim presented by another. This is so even where one person's injuries are “derivative” of the other's. [Castaneda v. Department of Corrections & Rehab. (2013) 212 CA4th 1051, 1062-1063, 151 CR3d 648, 656; Nelson v. County of Los Angeles (2003) 113 CA4th 783, 796-798, 6 CR3d 650, 661-662; Nguyen v. Los Angeles County Harbor/UCLA Med. Ctr. (1992) 8 CA4th 729, 10 CR2d 709—parents alleging negligent infliction of emotional distress could not rely on daughter's medical malpractice claim]

 

(1) [5:11.22] Loss of consortium claimant: A loss of consortium claimant cannot rely on the spouse's (or registered domestic partner's) personal injury claim. Each must file a separate claim (or, alternatively, the claim must indicate it is filed on behalf of both, seeking damages for their respective personal injury and loss of consortium). [Shelton v. Sup.Ct. (State of Calif.) (1976) 56 CA3d 66, 82, 128 CR 454, 464-465]

 

(2) [5:11.23] Wrongful death and survival claimants: Similarly, an heir's wrongful death claim seeks damages that are separate and distinct from the damages suffered by the victim (and recoverable by the victim's personal representative in a survival action on behalf of the estate). Hence, neither a wrongful death claimant nor a personal representative pursuing a survival cause of action may rely on the other's claim. [Castaneda v. Department of Corrections & Rehab. (2013) 212 CA4th 1051, 1062-1063, 151 CR3d 648, 656; Nelson v. County of Los Angeles (2003) 113 CA4th 783, 796-798, 6 CR3d 650, 661-662; see ¶ 5:122.1]

 

(3) [5:11.24] Employer and employee claimants: A claim filed by an employer (or its workers' comp carrier) seeking recoupment of workers' comp benefits paid to an injured employee does not excuse claim filing by the employee (or, where the employee was killed, by the wrongful death claimants). [Pacific Tel. & Tel. Co. v. County of Riverside (1980) 106 CA3d 183, 190-192, 165 CR 29, 33-34; Home Ins. Co. v. Southern Calif. Rapid Transit Dist. (1987) 196 CA3d 522, 526, 241 CR 858, 859-860 (dictum; distinguishing employee's right to intervene in employer's lawsuit, ¶ 5:36.3)]

(But an employer (or workers' comp carrier) seeking recoupment of benefits paid to an injured employee claimant need not file a separate claim; see ¶ 5:11.26.)

 

(4) [5:11.25] Compare—insurer need not file separate subrogation claim: A liability insurer with a subrogation claim need not file a “separate and redundant” claim where its insured has already timely filed with the entity. The insurer's claim is not “independent and separate” but, rather, is “equal to and limited by” the insured's right of action. Hence, the insured's filing is sufficient to apprise the entity of the insurer's claim. [Smith v. Parks Manor (1987) 197 CA3d 872, 878-882, 243 CR 256, 258-261]

(a) [5:11.26] Includes employer and workers' comp subrogee claims: Similarly, a claim filed by an employee includes any claim that the employer or workers' comp carrier may have for benefits paid to the employee based on the same injury. The employer (or carrier) is entitled to rely on the employee's timely-filed claim to meet its filing requirements, and need not file a separate, in effect duplicative, claim for benefits reimbursement. [San Diego Unified Port Dist. v. Sup.Ct. (Marriott Corp.) (1988) 197 CA3d 843, 852, 243 CR 163, 168]

 

c.     Analysis

Kern Steel’s argument is that Pinner, the general contractor, was told by the City that the City had a Builder’s Risk Policy in place when it actually did not.  Pinner and all of its subcontractors suffered losses because the City did not have a policy in place.  Pinner then filed a government tort claim on behalf of itself and all subcontractors, and this was sufficient to satisfy the requirement. 

 

Each subcontractor, however, suffered its own unique losses.  Even if those losses were in some way related to, or even derivative of, the general contractor’s losses, the losses were suffered by the subcontractors directly.  Per the above law, this means each subcontractor was required to file its own government tort claim.

 

Kern Steel admits it did not do so, and therefore the City’s demurrer to the SACC is sustained.  Because Kern Steel was previously given leave to amend to address and resolve this issue, but failed to do so, leave to amend is denied. 

 

d.     Additional Issues

The Court notes that the City advances other arguments on demurrer, but resolution of those issues is not necessary to a resolution of the demurrer, which is sustained without leave to amend as detailed above.  The Court therefore declines to address the remaining arguments.

 

4.     Motion to Compel

Hi-Q Design moves to compel Kern Steel to respond to RPDs.  Hi-Q has similar motions on calendar on 10/06/26 and 10/22/26.  The Court wishes to hear all motions together, and therefore continues today’s motion and the 10/06/26 motion to 10/22/26 at 8:30 a.m. in Department S27.  The Court asks the parties to work together to resolve the discovery issues without the need for the 10/22/26 hearing if at all possible.

 

5.     Notice

The City is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV01655    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff, Lyric Barnes filed this action against Defendants, ABC Liovin Drilling, Inc. and Marcos Cano for damages arising out of an automobile accident.  Plaintiff alleges Cano was driving the automobile that caused the accident and ABC owned the automobile Cano was driving.

 

2.     Discovery Motions

a.     Relief Sought

Plaintiff propounded RPDs, FROGs, SROGs, and RFAs on Defendants, and Defendants served objections-only responses.  Plaintiff attempted to meet and confer, but the issues were not resolved, and Plaintiff filed these motions to compel further responses.  Four motions are directed at Cano and four are directed at ABC. 

 

b.     Objections Only Responses

The burden to justify an objection to a discovery request is on the party asserting the objection.  See Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255. 

 

c.     ABC Liovin

ABC opposes the motions, contending further responses have been served and the motions are moot, but Plaintiff has refused to withdraw them despite an agreement that she would do so.  Plaintiff did not file reply papers as to these four motions.  The Court takes them off calendar as moot.

 

d.     Cano

Cano also opposes the motion.  Counsel declares he cannot reach his client, and asks that the Court deny the motion and/or not impose sanctions. 

 

Parties have an independent obligation to stay in contact with their attorneys while litigation is pending.  There is no authority presented for the position that, in the event an attorney cannot locate his client, a motion to compel can or should be denied, and discovery obligations can or should be in limbo indefinitely.  The motions to compel are granted.  Cano must serve further responses, without objections, within twenty days. 

 

e.     Sanctions

Plaintiff seeks sanctions in varying amounts in connection with each of the eight motions.  Because the ABC motions are taken off calendar as moot, no sanctions are imposed in connection with those motions.

 

The Court finds sanctions against Cano and his attorney of record are appropriate.  Counsel, in his declaration, does not establish that he communicated his inability to locate his client to Plaintiff.  Absent such communication, the Court finds sanctions are appropriate.

 

 

 

Plaintiff’s attorney declares his billing rate is $700/hour.  Because this is an automobile accident case, the case is likely based on a contingency fee.  The Court finds $700/hour is grossly inflated for a basic personal injury case, and also finds Counsel has not stated anything in the declaration to support such an excessive hourly rate.  The Court awards two hours of attorney time to prepare each motion to compel, one hour to prepare each reply, and one hour to appear, for a total of 13 hours.  The Court awards the time at the rate of $300/hour.  The Court awards a total of $3900 in attorneys’ fees.  The Court also awards four filing fees of $60 each, for a total of $240 in costs.  Sanctions are sought and imposed against Cano and his attorney of record, jointly and severally, and must be paid within twenty days.

 

f.      Notice

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV01897    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: S27

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff sues Defendant for violations of Song-Beverly, breach of implied warranty, and fraud – concealment. 

 

2.     Motion for Summary Adjudication

a.     Procedural Defect

Defendant’s notice of motion and separate statement do not comply with CRC 3.1350(b) and (h), which require the notice of motion to set forth each issue to be adjudicated and the separate statement to repeat the issues verbatim.  Department S27 has admonished Honda of this defect myriad times in the past.  Because the issues presented by way of this motion are simple and the defect does not defeat the Court’s ability to rule on the motion, the Court will rule despite the defect.  Honda is advised that Department S27 will summarily deny any motion with this defect filed after today’s date, and may deny previously filed motions that suffer from this defect if it renders ruling on the motion difficult.

 

b.     Request for Judicial Notice

Defendant requests judicial notice of Plaintiff’s complaint.  The Court takes judicial notice of the fact that the complaint exists and alleges what it alleges.  The Court does not judicially notice, as Defendant appears to suggest, that the lack of detail in the complaint is conclusive proof that such detail does not exist.  This will be discussed further below.

 

c.     Breach of Implied Warranty

Plaintiff purchased her automobile on 12/27/19 and filed suit on 6/26/25.  Defendant contends the implied warranty runs for one year from the date of purchase and the four-year statute of limitations runs from the date the defect is discovered, which must be during the one-year period, rendering the absolute last possible day for filing this action to have been 12/27/24. 

 

Defendant relies on Civil Code §1791.1 and Soro v. FCA US, LLC, 2026 WL 473050 to support its position.  §1791.1(c) makes clear that an implied warranty has a duration of no more than one year from the date of purchase.  The statute of limitations on a claim for breach of an implied warranty of merchantability is four years. (Montoya v. Ford Motor Co.¿(2020) 46¿Cal.App.5th 493, 495.) The claim “accrues when the breach occurs, regardless of the aggrieved party’s lack of knowledge of the breach. A breach of warranty occurs when tender of delivery is made. . . . .” (Commercial Code § 2725, subd. (2).) In short, Section 2725 of the Commercial Code is clear: there is no delayed-discovery rule with respect to accrual of a claim for breach of an implied warranty.¿(Cardinal Health 301, Inc. v. Tyco Electronics Corp.¿(2008) 169 Cal. App. 4th 116, 129, 134.) If the contract expressly extends the warranty to “future performance” of the goods, then the claim accrues when the breach is discovered or should have been discovered.¿But absent such an explicit extension, accrual occurs at the time of the breach, which is the time of delivery of the goods in question.¿ (Id. at p. 129.)

¿ 

Plaintiff contends that¿Mexia v. Rinker Boat Co. (2009) 174¿Cal.App.4th 1297 stands for the proposition that the statute of limitations on a claim for breach of the implied warranty of merchantability of a consumer good is tolled for the duration of the period of the express warranty on the good when the good’s defect is latent.¿That is not so. The Court in¿Mexia¿said flat out that “[i]n the case of a latent defect, a product is rendered unmerchantable, and the warranty of merchantability is breached, by the existence of the unseen defect, not by its subsequent discovery.” (Id. at 1304-1305.) 

 

The motion for summary adjudication of the cause of action for breach of implied warranty is therefore granted.

 

d.     Fraud -- Concealment

Defendant also argues the fraud cause of action is barred by the statute of limitations.  Defendant concedes the delayed discovery rule applies to fraud claims, but argues Plaintiff has not pled facts sufficient to support operation of the rule.  Notably, Defendant did not demur to the complaint in this regard.  Plaintiff alleges the delayed discovery rule applies.  This is sufficient, on a summary adjudication motion, to require Defendant to disprove the allegation.  While Defendant could have demurred based on lack of specificity, it failed to do so.  It also failed to provide any evidence at all to defeat the allegation.  The motion for summary adjudication of this cause of action is therefore denied.

 

e.     Conclusion

The motion for summary adjudication of the breach of implied warranty cause of action is granted.  The motion for summary adjudication of the fraud cause of action is denied.

 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.
