DEPARTMENT C LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25NWCV02081    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: C

COREAS vs TOYOTA MOTOR SALES U.S.A., INC.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV02081

HEARING:  9/23/26 @ 9:30 AM

 

#9

TENTATIVE RULING

 

I.             Defendant TOYOTA MOTOR SALES U.S.A., INC.’s motion to compel deposition attendance and testimony of Plaintiff ELENA D. COREAS is GRANTED.

II.            Defendant TOYOTA MOTOR SALES U.S.A., INC.’s motion to compel production of Plaintiff’s vehicle for inspection is GRANTED.

III.          Sanctions in the amount of $720.00 are imposed upon Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel, jointly and severally, payable within 30 days. 

 

Moving Party to give NOTICE.

 

 

Background

 

This is a lemon law case involving a 2023 Toyota Corolla, VIN No.: JTDBCMFE4P3015362 (“Subject Vehicle”), which Plaintiff purchased on or about March 30, 2023. 

 

Motion to Compel Deposition Attendance

 

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.230, without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”  (CCP § 2025.450(a).)  “The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”  (CCP § 2025.450(b)(1).)  The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration.  (CCP § 2025.450(b)(2).)

 

On June 2, 2026, Defendant served a Notice of Taking Deposition of Plaintiff Elena D. Coreas, with a request for the production of documents, setting Plaintiff’s deposition for June 16, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. via videoconference. (Kuredjian Decl. ¶ 4; Exhibit A.) Defendant’s notice requested that Plaintiff produce documents at the time of deposition. (Id.)

 

Plaintiff served objections to Defendant’s notice on June 9, 2026 stating Plaintiff and/or Plaintiff’s counsel were unavailable. (Kuredjian Decl. ¶ 4; Exhibit B.) Defendant emailed Plaintiff’s Counsel requesting alternate dates. Plaintiff never responded. Defendant has attempted to secure the deposition of Plaintiff since March 31, 2026, with follow-up emails on April 6, May 4, May 13, May 26, June 4, June 9, and June 15, 2026. (Kuredjian Decl. ¶ 6-7.)

 

As of the filing of this motion, Defendant’s counsel has yet to receive a response from Plaintiff’s counsel regarding the availability of Plaintiff for a deposition. (Kuredjian Decl. ¶ 8.) Plaintiff’s counsel argues that Defendant failed to meet and confer by telephone as required, but Plaintiff’s counsel’s offers no reason why counsel failed to respond to any of Defendant’s seven emails.  Additionally, Plaintiff’s counsel failed to oppose to the instant motion in a timely manner. 

 

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to appear at the next noticed deposition to be scheduled at a mutually agreeable time between the parties and within 30 days.

 

Motion to Compel Inspection of Vehicle

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.310 allows a party to compel a further response to a demand for inspection if it finds that the response is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive, or an objection in the response is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc. (CCP), § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

 

To establish good cause, the burden is on the moving party to show: (1) relevance to the subject matter and (2) specific facts justifying discovery. (Glenfed Develop. Corp. v. Sup. Ct. (National Union Fire Ins. Co. of Pittsburgh, Penn.) (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1117; see also Kirkland v. Sup. Ct. (Guess?, Inc.) (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 98.)

 

Here, Defendant seeks to inspect Plaintiff’s vehicle in order to evaluate the claims and defenses in this matter, but Plaintiff has not confirmed the inspection or meaningfully participated in Defendant’s efforts to coordinate the inspection. (Kuredjian Decl., ¶ 3.) Plaintiff fails to oppose this motion.

 

The Court finds that good cause exists for inspection of Plaintiff’s vehicle.  An inspection will allow Defendant to investigate Plaintiff’s claims, determine whether third party or outside influence had an impact on any alleged defects, safeguard against surprise, and develop defenses.

 

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to submit the Subject Vehicle for inspection within 30 days, absent an agreement by the parties to a longer period. 

 

Sanctions

When a party fails to permit an inspection demanded under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 et seq., the Court may order compliance and may impose monetary sanctions against any party or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes the motion, unless the Court finds substantial justification or other circumstances making the imposition of sanctions unjust.

 

Here, sanctions in the reasonable sum of $360.00 are imposed for each motion, for a total of $720.00, upon Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel, jointly and severally, payable in 30 days. 


Case Number: 26NWCV02171    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: C

TYLER v. VOLKSWAGEN GROUP OF AMERICA, INC, et al.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV02171

HEARING:  9/23/26 @ 9:30 AM

 

#10

TENTATIVE RULING

 

     I.        Defendant McKenna Motor Company, Inc. dba McKenna Audi’s Demurrer to Plaintiff JUSTINE BETTS TYLER’s Complaint is SUSTAINED with 20 days leave to amend.

 

    II.        The Case Management Conference scheduled for October 8, 2026 is ADVANCED to today and VACATED.  Trial is set for March 16, 2028 at 10:00 a.m. in Dept. SE-C.  A Final Status Conference is set for March 2, 2028 at 10:00 a.m. in Dept. SE-C. 

 

Moving Party to give NOTICE.

 

 

Defendant McKenna Motor Company, Inc. dba McKenna Audi (erroneously sued as McKenna Motor Company, Inc. dba Audi McKenna Norwalk) (Defendant) demurs to Plaintiff JUSTINE BETTS TYLER’s (Plaintiff) fourth cause of action for negligent repair on the ground that it fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action and is barred by the economic loss rule.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a lemon law case. Plaintiff purchased a 2023 Audi Q5, on June 24, 2026. (Complaint ¶ 9.)  Defects and nonconformities to warranty manifested themselves within the applicable express warranty period. The nonconformities substantially impaired the use, value and/or safety of the Subject Vehicle. (Complaint, ¶ 32.) Based thereon, the Complaint asserts causes of action for:

 

1.    Violation of the Song-Beverly Act – Breach of Express Warranty

2.    Violation of the Song-Beverly Act – Breach of Implied Warranty

3.    Violation of the Song-Beverly Act – Section 1793.2

4.    Negligent Repair

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (CCP §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732, internal citations omitted.) 

“Liberality in permitting amendment is the rule, if a fair opportunity to correct any defect has not been given.” (Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1227.) It is an abuse of discretion for the court to deny leave to amend where there is any reasonable possibility that plaintiff can state a good cause of action. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) The burden is on plaintiff to show¿in what manner¿plaintiff can amend the complaint, and¿how¿that amendment will change the legal effect of the pleading. ¿(Id.) 

DISCUSSION

 

Fourth Cause of Action: Negligent Repair

 

NEGLIGENT REPAIR: The elements are: 1) Legal duty owed to plaintiffs to use due care; 2) breach of duty; 3) causation; and 4) damage to plaintiff. (Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.)   

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s negligent-repair cause of action lacks the requisite factual allegations to substantiate the claim and merely states legal conclusions.   

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Plaintiff alleges that he delivered the vehicle for professional service in the form of a repair and reasonably expected that Defendant MCKENNA AUDI would perform the repair with care and skill. (Complaint ¶¶ ¶¶ 9-20, 68-72.). Plaintiff alleges the following:

 

Plaintiff first presented the Subject Vehicle for repairs in July 2025, with approximately 30,008 miles on the odometer, and reported the sunroof exhibiting structural nonconformities. Additionally, the Subject Vehicle exhibited stalling. Furthermore, excessive shaking emitted while driving. Additionally, the door locks malfunctioned. Moreover, the driver’s window emitted an abnormal noise while active. (Complaint, ¶ 15.)

 

In August 2025, with approximately 30,895 miles on the odometer, Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle again and reported stalling recurrently. Additionally, the driver’s window continued to emit an abnormal noise. Furthermore, the Dome light malfunctioned. (Complaint, ¶ 16.)

 

Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle again in November 2025, with approximately 32,798 miles on the odometer, and reported the infotainment system malfunctioned. Additionally, the “Lane Assist Malfunction” message appeared on the instrument cluster. Furthermore, the Subject Vehicle excessively shook while turning. Moreover, the driver’s window emitted an abnormal noise for the third time. (Complaint, ¶ 17.)

 

In December 2025, with approximately 33,240 miles on the odometer, Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle again and reported an abnormal noise emitting while turning. Additionally, the Dome light malfunctioned recurrently. Furthermore, the driver’s window emitted an abnormal noise for the fourth time. (Complaint, ¶ 18.)

 

Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle in January 2026, with approximately 34,073 miles on the odometer, and reported the “Lane Assist Malfunction” message reappearing. Additionally, an abnormal noise continued to emit while turning. Furthermore, the Dome light malfunctioned for the third time. (Complaint, ¶ 19.)

 

In March 2026, with approximately 35,135 miles on the odometer, Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle again and reported the “Lane Assist Malfunction” message appearing for the third time. In addition, the Freeway Lane Assist feature malfunctioned. Additionally, an abnormal noise emitted while turning for the third time. (Complaint, ¶ 20.)

 

Plaintiff delivered the Subject Vehicle to Defendant AUDI MCKENNA NORWALK for repairs on numerous occasions. (Complaint, ¶ 69.)

 

Therefore, Plaintiff apprises Defendant of the factual basis for the claim against it. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) 

 

Economic Loss Rule

 

The economic loss rule provides that “where a purchaser’s expectations in a sale are frustrated because the product he bought is not working properly, his [or her] remedy is said to be in contract alone, for he [or she] has suffered only economic losses.” (Robinson Helicopter Co., Inc. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 988.)  “Tort damages have been permitted in contract cases where a breach of duty directly causes physical injury; for breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing in insurance contracts; for wrongful discharge in violation of fundamental public policy; or where the contract was fraudulently induced…. in each of these cases, the duty that gives rise to tort liability is either completely independent of the contract or arises from conduct which is both intentional and intended to harm.”  (Robinson, supra, 34 Cal.4th at 989–990.) 

 

In Robinson, the California Supreme Court carved out a “narrow” and “limited” exception to the economic loss rule, holding that “a defendant's affirmative misrepresentations on which a plaintiff relies and which expose a plaintiff to liability for personal damages independent of the plaintiff's economic loss” is excluded from the economic loss rule. (Robinson, supra, 34 Cal.4th at 993.) 

 

Plaintiff argues there is an exception to the economic loss rule where the contract is services rather than the sale of goods. (North American Chemical Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 764.) North American Chemical recognized the “fundamental principle” that “accompanying every contract is a common-law duty to perform with care, skill, reasonable expedience, and faithfulness the thing agreed to be done, and a negligent failure to observe any of these conditions is a tort, as well as a breach of contract.” (Id. at p. 774.) 

 

Since North American Chemical was decided, however, the California Supreme Court has narrowed the exceptions to the economic loss rule.  In Erlich v. Menezes (1999) 21 Cal.4th 543, the court determined that homeowners could not recover tort damages for emotional distress caused by a contractor’s negligent construction of their home.  A breach of contract is tortious “only when some independent duty arising from tort law is violated. [citation] If every negligent breach of a contract gives rise to tort damages, the limitation [upon tort damages] would be meaningless, as would the statutory distinction between tort and contract remedies.” (Id. at p. 554.) “The benefits of broad compensation must be balanced against the burdens on commercial stability.  ‘Courts should be careful to apply tort remedies only when the conduct in question is so clear in its deviation from socially useful business practices that the effect of enforcing such tort duties will be … to aid rather than discourage commerce.’” (Id. (citing Freeman & Mills, Inc. v. Belcher Oil Co. (1995) 11 Cal.4th 85, 109).) 

 

The Robinson court held “the economic loss rule does not bar [Plaintiff’s] fraud and intentional misrepresentation claims because they were independent of [Defendant’s] breach of contract.” (Robinson Helicoptor v. Dana Corp, supra, 34 Cal.4th 979, 991.)  The court carved out a “narrow” and “limited” exception to the economic loss rule, holding that “a defendant's affirmative misrepresentations on which a plaintiff relies and which expose a plaintiff to liability for personal damages independent of the plaintiff's economic loss” is excluded from the economic loss rule. (Id. at 993.) Here, Plaintiff alleges a claim for negligent repair which does not fall within the narrow exceptions carved out by the California Supreme Court in Robinson.  Plaintiff does not allege that Defendant engaged in any intentional misconduct or made any affirmative misrepresentations.  Nor does Plaintiff adequately allege an independent duty giving rise to tort liability. (Erlich v. Menezes, supra, 21 Cal.4th 543, 554.) As alleged, the fourth cause of action is barred by the economic loss rule. 

 

Lastly, Plaintiff relies on Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905 for the proposition that the economic loss rule does not bar a negligence cause of action for professional services. (Sheen, at p. 933.) However, Plaintiff cites to no authority which supports a negligent repair cause of action in the context of automotive repairs.   

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the fourth cause of action SUSTAINED with 20 days leave to amend.

 

 

 


Case Number: 26NWCV02685    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: C

ANSIBOY vs LOPEZ

CASE NO.:  26NWCV02685

HEARING:  9/23/26 @ 9:30 AM

#11

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Defendant Fabiola Lopez’s Unopposed Demurrer is OVERRULED.  Defendant to answer within five days. 

Moving Party to give NOTICE.

 

Pro Per Defendant Fabiola Lopez (Defendant) generally demurs to Pro Per Plaintiff Dennis Ansiboy’s Complaint.

Background

This is an unlawful detainer action. Plaintiff alleges that Defendant entered into a month to month tenancy on or about September 1, 2025. Plaintiff further alleges that Defendant failed to pay rent in the monthly amount of $6,999.00. Plaintiff alleges he served a Three-Day Notice to Pay Rent or Quit on multiple dates: 07/28/26 & 07/21/26, 07/22/26 & 07/15/26. (Complaint, p. 2–4.)

Legal Standard

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (CCP §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732, internal citations omitted.) 

 

“Liberality in permitting amendment is the rule, if a fair opportunity to correct any defect has not been given.” (Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1227.) It is an abuse of discretion for the court to deny leave to amend where there is any reasonable possibility that plaintiff can state a good cause of action. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) The burden is on plaintiff to show¿in what manner¿plaintiff can amend the complaint, and¿how¿that amendment will change the legal effect of the pleading. ¿(Id.) 

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

Defendant’s Request for Judicial Notice is GRANTED.

 

Meet and Confer

 

Under Code Civ. Proc., 430.41, subd. (d)(2), the meet-and-confer requirement does not apply to unlawful-detainer proceedings.

 

Discussion

I.             DEFENDANT’S DEADLINE TO RESPOND TO THE PLEADINGS

Generally, unless otherwise ordered by the court, the tenant has only five days after the summons is served to demur. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1167, 1170.) Default may be entered if no responsive pleading is filed “within the time specified in the summons, or within further time as may be allowed.” (CCP § 585(a) (emphasis added)).

However, where plaintiff has not yet applied for entry of default, plaintiff in effect, has allowed the defendant further time to plead. (Fiorentino v. City of Fresno (2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 596, 605.) But a pleading or motion filed after expiration of the time to respond is subject to a motion to strike. Although discretionary with the court, if the motion is granted, the defendant's default can then be entered. (Buck v. Morrossis (1952) 114 CA2d 461, 464-465.)

Here, Plaintiff filed his Complaint and served Defendant on August 10, 2026.  Defendant filed her Demurrer to the Complaint on August 31, 2026. Although the demurrer did not meet the five-day deadline, Plaintiff had not applied for entry of default when Defendant demurred.  Plaintiff applied for entry of default as to All Unknown Occupants on September 10, 2026. Thus, Plaintiff allowed the defendant further time to plead. (Fiorentino v. City of Fresno (2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 596, 605.) Plaintiff failed to move to strike Defendant’s Demurrer. Therefore, the Court shall consider Defendant’s Demurrer on the merits.

II.            FACTS SUFFICIENT TO STATE A CAUSE OF ACTION

A.   Standing

A cause of action for unlawful detainer must “set forth the facts on which the plaintiff seeks to recover” (Stancil v. Superior Court (2021) 11 Cal.5th 381, 395,; accord, Code Civ. Proc., § 1166, subd. (a)(2)), including the allegation of ownership or some other enforceable right to possession (Staudigl v. Harper (1946) 76 Cal.App.2d 439.) A failure-to-pay unlawful detainer action may be brought by “the landlord, or the successor in estate of the landlord, ...” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1161, subd. (2).) Except as otherwise authorized by statute, every action must be prosecuted in the name of the real party in interest. (Code Civ. Proc., § 367.) The real party in interest is typically a party who has title to the cause of action, but “in many instances ... one who is not the owner of the property nonetheless may be the real party in interest if that person's interests in the property are injured or damaged.” (Vaughn v. Dame Construction Co. (1990) 223 Cal.App.3d 144, 148.)

Defendant argues that the Complaint identifies Plaintiff as “Dennis Ansiboy,” whereas the recorded Grant Deed concerning the Subject Property reflects a conveyance by Dennis Ansiboy and Esmeralda Ansiboy to the “Dennis and Esmeralda Ansiboy Revocable Living Trust.” (RJN, Ex. A.) The Court finds that at the pleadings stage Plaintiff’s allegations suffice. Plaintiff alleges his interest in the premises is “as owner.” (Complaint, ¶4.) Plaintiff’s allegations regarding his status as landlord are supported by the lease agreement, which he signed in his individual capacity. (Complaint, Ex. 1.) Defendant’s argument concerning the Grant Deed refers to matters outside of the four corners of the Complaint and cannot be considered by the Court when ruling on a demurrer. 

Accordingly, the demurrer as to Plaintiff’s standing is OVERRULED.

B.   Unlawful Detainer Cause of Action

The basic elements of unlawful detainer for nonpayment of rent are (1) the tenant is in possession of the premises; (2) that possession is without permission; (3) the tenant is in default for nonpayment of rent; (4) the tenant has been properly served with a written three-day notice; and (5) the default continues after the three-day notice period has elapsed. (Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 1161(2).)

Because of the summary character of unlawful detainer proceedings, the Unlawful Detainer statutes are strictly interpreted and applied. Landlords who fail to follow the statutory prerequisites are likely to find their cases dismissed and may be deemed to have forfeited the preexisting right to evict. (Group XIII Properties LP v. Stockman (2022) 85 Cal.App.5th Supp. 1, disapproved of on other grounds by 360 So Reeves, LLC v. Dutton (2026) 119 Cal.App.5th Supp. 1.)

Defendant argues the Complaint is uncertain and internally inconsistent because it alleges Defendant occupied the premises pursuant to a month-to-month tenancy whereas the Lease provides for a fixed-term tenancy beginning September 1, 2025 and ending October 31, 2028. (Complaint, ¶6e, Ex. 1, Residential Lease Agreement, para. 1.4.) To the extent there is any ambiguity in the tenancy, the express terms of the Lease are unambiguous and controlling. 

Defendant argues there is uncertainty regarding which three-day notice forms the basis for this action.  Plaintiff attaches three separate three-day notices to the Complaint in Exhibit 2: 1) “Three Day Notice to Quit Premises for Failure to Perform Covenants” served July 22, 2026; 2) “Three Day Notice to Pay or Quit” served on July 15, 2026, and 3) “California Three (3) Day Notice to Perform Covenants or Quit” served on July 15, 2026.  Defendant cites to no authority limiting Plaintiff to only one three-day notice.  The notice to pay or quit and the notices to perform or quit relate to separate and distinct grounds for eviction, and each ground forms a basis for this action. 

Defendant argues the manner of service as alleged in the Complaint is confusing, and the proofs of service do not reflect an authorized method of service pursuant to CCP § 1162.  The Complaint states notice was served: “By posting a copy on the premises on (date):7/22/26 & 7/15/26 … AND giving a copy to a person found residing at the premises and mailing a copy to the defendant at the premises on (date) 7/22/26 & 7/15/26. (Complaint, ¶10a(3).  However, the Complaint also states: “because no person of suitable age or discretion can be found there.” (Ibid.) To the extent there is any ambiguity in the Complaint, the proofs of service contained in Exhibit 3 of the Complaint clearly state the notices were served by “posting and mail” on July 15, 2026 and July 22, 2026. 

Finally, Defendant argues the Three-Day Notice to Perform Covenants or Quit is overbroad and uncertain.  The Court has reviewed the Three-Day Notice to Perform Covenants or Quit served on July 15, 2026, and finds that it sufficiently gives notice to Defendant of the alleged violations of the lease.  These include failure to provide and maintain renter’s insurance, failure to provide the landlord with lawful access to the property, failure to cooperate with all repair and maintenance work, etc.  Moreover, the notice provides a detailed chronology of events from October 17, 2025 through March 16, 2026. 

Accordingly, the demurrer is OVERRULED.  Defendant shall answer within five days.
