MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR DOCUMENTS; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 708.020, 2031.300)

The Court GRANTS Pensco Trust Company LLC’s motion to compel responses to the requests for inspection.  Felix and Yelena are ordered to served verified responses without objections to the requests within 30 days of this order. 

 

Felix and Yelena must pay Pensco $2,198.41, jointly and severally, within 30 days of this order.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Pensco Trust Company LLC sued Felix Bobritsky, Yelena Bobritsky, Cooper Dietz, Daniala Bobritsky, and Shoshanah Bobritsky for disobedience of subpoenas and court orders.  Shoshanah was dismissed; default judgment was entered against Felix, Yelena, Daniala, and Dietz; and this action was reclassified as a civil limited jurisdiction matter.

 

            On May 2, 2024, this Court granted Pensco’s motion to compel Felix and Yelena to respond to interrogatories to enforce the judgment and imposed monetary sanctions against them both in the amount of $1,823.91.  Felix and Yelena failed to obey the Court’s order.  Pensco then propounded requests for inspection to enforce the May 2, 2024 order, Felix and Yelena failed to respond, so Pensco now moves to compel responses to the requests for inspection.

 

            A monetary sanctions order has the force and effect of a money judgment and is enforceable under the Enforcement of Judgments Law.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 680.230, 680.270; Newland v. Superior Court (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 608, 615; Lucky United Properties Investment, Inc. v. Lee (2010) 185 Cal.App.4th 125, 144.)  A judgment creditor may demand a judgment debtor produce, permit to inspect, and copy a document if the demand requests information to aid in the enforcement of the money judgment, in the manner prescribed in Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 et seq.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.030, subd. (a).)  The judgment debtor must answer within the time prescribed in Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.030 et seq.  (Id., § 708.030, subd. (a).) 

 

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).)  If a party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the requests for production of documents.  (Id., § 2031.300, subds. (a), (b).)

 

            Pensco served Felix and Yelena with the requests for inspection in July 2026.  To date, no responses have been served.  Felix and Yelena have not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Pensco’s motion.  Felix and Yelena are ordered to served verified responses without objections to the requests for inspection within 30 days of this order.

 

            Felix and Yelena must also pay sanctions for causing Pensco to compel responses to the requests for inspection.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.030, subd. (c); id., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)  Pensco requests $2,198.41 from Felix and Yelena, reflecting 2.5 hours of counsel’s work at $850.00 per hour plus $73.41 in filing fees.  This request is reasonable.  Felix and Yelena must pay Pensco $2,198.41, jointly and severally, within 30 days of this order.

 

Pensco is to give notice.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 685.040)

The Court GRANTS Maricopa Meadows Homeowners Association’s motion for attorney fees and costs in the total amount of $1,454.73, comprising $1,125.00 in attorney fees and $329.73 in costs.

ANALYSIS:

 

            In March 2022, the Court domesticated an Arizona judgment in favor of Maricopa Meadows Homeowners Association and against Chanel Coopwood.  Maricopa now moves for an award of attorney fees and costs incurred post-judgment.

 

            Attorney fees are allowable as costs when authorized by contract, statute, or law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10).)  Code of Civil Procedure section 685.040 entitles a judgment creditor to reasonable and necessary costs of enforcing a judgment, including attorney fees, if the underlying judgment includes a fee award.  The judgment creditor may claim those costs through a noticed motion before the judgment is satisfied in full, but not later than two years after the costs have been incurred.  (Id., § 685.080, subd. (a).) 

 

            Notice of Maricopa’s motion was served properly on judgment debtor Coopwood.  The judgment provides for “all costs and attorney fees incurred by [Maricopa] after submission of [the] judgment for entry by the Court in collecting the amounts listed in [the] Judgment.”  The judgment has not yet been satisfied, and the fees and costs sought by Maricopa were incurred between June 4, 2024 and now.  

 

The Court’s objective is to award those fees at the fair-market value for the attorneys’ work. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132.)  That can be done by multiplying the number of hours the attorneys reasonably expended by a reasonable hourly rate.  (Id. at p. 1132, 1134.)  Maricopa seeks attorney fees based on its counsel, B. Austin Baillio’s, hourly rate $375.00 per hour until January 1, 2025, after which the rate increased to $395.00 per hour, with some services performed for a flat fee; counsel Garren R. Laymon’s work performed for a flat fee of $500.00; and law clerk Elijah Swolgaard’s work performed for a flat fee of $400.00.  With his declaration, Baillio provides a ledger for this matter, with itemized hours demonstrating the specific tasks completed and the fees thereby incurred.  In total, the ledger reflects $1,283.00 in fees, consisting of three hours of total work, including 0.4 hours for attending this hearing.  Both the rates and number of hours expended are reasonable—minus the time spent remotely attending this hearing on an unopposed motion.  The Court deducts that time, valued at $158.00, for a total fee award of $1,125.00.

 

Maricopa also requests costs in the amount of $329.73, which includes electronic monitoring fees, filing fees, fees for official certification, and recording fees.  This amount is supported by the ledger provided by Baillio.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Maricopa’s motion for attorney fees and costs in the total amount of $1,454.73, comprising $1,125.00 in attorney fees and $329.73 in costs.

 

Maricopa is to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Messeret Tenna sued Annahshem Ramones for a motor vehicle cause of action.  Tenna personally served Ramones in November 2025.  Tenna did not file proof of service until February 2026 and did not obtain a default against Ramones.[1]  Tenna’s counsel then failed to appear for the March 18, 2026 non-jury trial, and the Court ordered Tenna’s complaint dismissed without prejudice pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 581, subdivision (b)(3).  Tenna now moves to have the dismissal vacated pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b)’s mandatory attorney-fault provision. 

 

            A motion pursuant to the mandatory relief provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), must be (1) timely, (2) in proper form, and (3) accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)  A court must grant relief if the party satisfies those requirements, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not actually caused by the attorney’s fault.  (Ibid.)

 

            Tenna’s motion, filed August 17, 2026, satisfies each of those requirements.  Tenna’s counsel declares that the failure to appear “was due to [his] calendaring and scheduling mistake and inadvertence.”  And while Tenna’s motion is not accompanied by papers curing the lapse that led to the dismissal, Tenna’s counsel represents to the Court that he will “work diligently to…submit and serve the Request for Entry of Default...within 30–45 days” after this hearing.  The Court accepts that representation.  (See Carmel, Ltd. v. Tavoussi (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 393, 401–402; Austin v. Los Angeles Unified School Dist. (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 918, 933.)

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Tenna’s motion.  The March 18, 2026 dismissal is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.

 

            Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (c)(1)(A), the Court imposes a penalty of $250.00 on Tenna’s counsel, payable to the Court within 30 days of this order.  The Court further ORDERS Tenna to SHOW CAUSE, on Thursday, November 19, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse, why default and a default judgment have not yet been entered in this matter.  And the Court ORDERS Tenna to SHOW CAUSE, on the same day and at the same time, why Tenna’s counsel should not be sanctioned $250.00 for failing timely to secure a default and default judgment in this action.

 

            Tenna is to give notice.

 


[1] Tenna’s counsel states that he failed to file proof of service, but proof of personal service on Ramones was filed on February 19, 2026.

MOTION FOR ATTORNEY FEES AND COSTS

(Civ. Code §§ 798.85, 1717)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Court GRANTS Ricberay, Inc.’s motion for attorney fees and costs in the reduced total amount of $9,777.79, comprising $9,209.50 in attorney fees and $568.29 in costs.

 

Ricberay is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

ANALYSIS:

 

            Ricberay, Inc. petitioned the Court for a permanent injunction against Martin Apolinar pursuant to Civil Code section 798.88.  The Court granted the petition and signed the submitted order on May 21, 2026.  Ricberay now moves for attorney fees and costs pursuant to Civil Code section 798.85.  Apolinar has not opposed this motion.

 

Attorney fees are allowable as costs when authorized by contract, statute, or law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10).)  Civil Code section 1717 authorizes fees for a prevailing party when a party sued on a contract that allows for them.  The rental agreement between Ricberay and Apolinar provides that “the prevailing party shall be entitled to reasonable attorney[] fees and costs.”  Additionally, Civil Code section 798.85 allows for recovery of reasonable attorney fees and costs by the prevailing party in any action pursuant to Civil Code section 798.88.  Given that Ricberay is the prevailing party in this action, Ricberay is entitled to recover reasonable attorney fees and costs on contractual and statutory grounds.

 

             The Court’s objective is to award those fees at the fair-market value for the attorneys’ work. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132.)  That can be done by multiplying the number of hours the attorneys reasonably expended by a reasonable hourly rate. (Id. at p. 1132, 1134.) 

 

            Ricberay seeks attorney fees based on attorney Gregory B. Beam’s hourly rate of $350.00 up until January 1, 2026, increased to $375.00 after that date; attorney Norman Paul Breen’s hourly rate of $350.00, and paralegal Sandra Beck’s hourly rate of $140.00. These hourly rates are reasonable.  Beam’s declaration states the services rendered by his firm include issuing two separate notices, preparing and filing the petition, communicating with witnesses, preparing and filing declarations, attending the injunction hearing, and obtaining the injunction order.  The number of hours expended before this motion include Beam’s work of six hours, Breen’s work of 13.3 hours, and Beck’s work of 11.8 hours, which amounts to $8,459.50.  This amount is reasonable.

 

            Ricberay also seeks attorney fees for bringing this motion, in 1.5 hours of Breen’s work drafting it, one hour of Beam’s work reviewing and revising, two hours for Beam’s appearance at this hearing, and if an opposition is filed, two hours for Beam’s review and preparation of a reply.  As there was no opposition filed, two hours of Beam’s total work are deducted.  Likewise, the Court does not anticipate this to be a long hearing.  It will allow $562.50 for time preparing the motion and half an hour of Beam’s time for the hearing, for a total of $750.00.

 

            That makes a total of $9,209.50 in attorney fees.

 

            The $568.29 in costs Ricberay requests are set forth in the memorandum of costs and include filing and motion fees, service of process, and fees for electronic filing or service.  These requests are proper and reasonable.

 

            Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Ricberay’s motion for attorney fees and costs in the reduced total amount of $9,777.79, comprising $9,209.50 in attorney fees and $568.29 in costs.

 

            Ricberay is to give notice.
