DEPARTMENT 836 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCP04981    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 836

MOTION TO AUGMENT RECORD

 

Date:               9/24/26 (1:30 AM)

Case:               Federation of Hillside and Canyon Associations, Inc., et al. v. City of Los Angeles (25STCP04981)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Federation of Hillside and Canyon Associations, Inc.’s Motion to Augment the Administrative Record is GRANTED.

 

Petitioner Federation of Hillside and Canyon Associations, Inc. seeks to augment “the administrative record for the First Cause of Action to include eight (8) emails exchanged between July 18, 2025 and October 14, 2025 among Chiharu Suzuki, P.E., the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety (“LADBS”) Building Civil Engineer who served as the lead staff person processing the Federation’s administrative appeal, and staff of the City’s Bureau of Engineering (“BOE”).” (Notice of Mtn. at 2.) Petitioner does not seek to include later messages within the email threads that are dated October 15, 2025 and beyond, i.e., after the Board made its decision.

 

I.          BACKGROUND

 

Real party in interest Josephson Investments, Inc. (“Josephson Investments”) owns the real property located at 10453 Sandal Lane in Los Angeles and is constructing a single-family dwelling on the property (“Sandal Lane Project”). (Pet. ¶ 13; Hall Decl. ¶ 6.) On April 22, 2025, the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety issued permits relating to the Sandal Lane Project. (Pet. ¶ 58.) On May 7, 2026, petitioner Federation of Hillside and Cayon Associations, Inc. (“Hillside Federation”) filed a building permit appeal concerning the Sandy Lane Project. (Pet. ¶ 7; Hall Decl. ¶7.) On September 16, 2025, the Board of Building and Safety Commissioners denied petitioner’s appeal. (Pet. ¶ 64; Hall Decl. ¶ 7.) Following a request for reconsideration, the Board denied this request on October 14, 2025 and issued a Final Action letter on October 16, 2025. (Pet. ¶ 65; Hall Decl. ¶ 8.)

 

Petitioners Hillside Federation and State Alliance for Firesafe Road Regulations (“SAFRR”) initiated the instant action for a writ of mandamus on December 23, 2025 against the City of Los Angeles (“City”) and named Josephson Investments as a real party in interest. Petitioner Hillside Federation claims that it is “dedicated to preserving safe and living communities in the hillside and canyon areas of Los Angeles,” and petitioner SAFRR claims that its “mission is to ensure that new California development complies with laws relating to firefighting and fire safety.” (Pet. ¶¶ 10, 11.) The overarching thrust of their writ petition is to address “[City’s] systemic failures in the administration and enforcement of the State Minimum Fire Safe Regulations.” (Hall Decl. ¶ 4; Pet. ¶¶ 1-8, 66-92.) The petition also alleges that the Sandal Lane Project is “one example of the City’s failure to apply the Fire Safe Regulations.” (Pet. ¶¶ 9, 57.) In the petition, the first cause of action is for a claim for writ of administrative mandate that concerns a challenge to the Sandy Lane Project (Pet. ¶¶ 93-103; Hall Decl. ¶ 5.)

 

Following the commencement of this action, the administrative record for the first cause of action was certified by the City on April 16, 2026, consisting of 1,209 pages. (Hall Decl. ¶ 9.) On April 17, 2026, a supplemental record, consisting of 5 pages was certified by the City. (Hall Decl. ¶ 9.) Based on petitioner’s various concerns about this record, the City certified a second supplemental record on May 26, 2026. (Hall Decl. ¶ 10.) Petitioners now seek to augment the record because the City did not include the above-referenced internal e-mail communications pertaining to the appeal. (Hall Decl. ¶¶ 15-17.)

 

II.        DISCUSSION

 

A.                Legal Standard

 

Per CCP § 1094.6(c), “the complete record of the proceedings shall be prepared by the local agency. . . . Such record shall include the transcript of the proceedings, all pleadings, all notices and orders, any proposed decision by a hearing officer, the final decision, all admitted exhibits, all rejected exhibits in the possession of the local agency or its commission, board, officer, or agent, all written evidence, and any other papers in the case.”

 

“As a general rule, a hearing on a writ of administrative mandamus is conducted solely on the record of the proceeding before the administrative agency.” (Richardson v. City and County of San Francisco (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 671, 702.)  However, “[w]here the court finds that there is relevant evidence that, in the exercise of reasonable diligence, could not have been produced or that was improperly excluded at the hearing before respondent, it may enter judgment as provided in subdivision (f) remanding the case to be reconsidered in the light of that evidence; or, in cases in which the court is authorized by law to exercise its independent judgment on the evidence, the court may admit the evidence at the hearing on the writ without remanding the case.” (CCP § 1094.5(e).) “Augmentation of the administrative record is permitted only within the strict limits set forth in section 1094.5, subdivision (e)….” (Pomona Valley Hospital Medical Center v. Superior Court (1997) 55 Cal.App.4th 93, 101.) “In the absence of a proper preliminary foundation showing that one of the exceptions noted in section 1094.5, subdivision (e) applies, it is error for the court to permit the record to be augmented.” (Ibid.)

 

B.                 Judicial Notice

 

Petitioner’s requests for judicial notice of LAMC §§ 98.0403.1 and 98.0403.2 are GRANTED pursuant to Evidence Code § 452(b).

 

C.                Analysis

 

Petitioner contends that the omitted emails pertaining to the appeal should have been included within the administrative record pursuant to CCP § 1094.6(c) because they fall within the categories of “all written evidence” and “any other papers in the case.” (Mtn. at  6-7.) The Court agrees.

 

The Board’s determination on the appeal was based on the report submitted by LADBS and LADBS Building Civil Engineer Chiharu Suzuki’s presentation of the report. (AR 92-96, 1152.) Because LADBS authored the report, it follows that its investigation and materials generated as part of that investigation leading to the creation of the report would be part of the proceedings. (RJN, Ex. A [LAMC § 98.0403.1(b)(2)]; Ex. B [LAMC § 98.0403.2(b)(3)].) In addition, the pertinent emails that are the subject of this motion appear to be written evidence that was relied upon by Suzuki at the administrative hearing. (Compare AR 1164:24-1165:3, 1165:19-1166:2, 11673-9, 1187:9-17 with Mot., Hall Decl., Ex. 4; see Reply re: City’s Opp. at 4.)  Thus, the emails at issue fall constitute “all written evidence” and “any other papers in the case” as contemplated by CCP § 1094.6(c).

 

Contrary to City and Josephson Investments’ contentions, the Court does not find that the pertinent emails should be deemed “extra-record” evidence simply because they were not made available to the Board. Extra-record evidence typically pertains to evidence that was never considered by the administrative agency, which is not the case here. (See CCP § 1094.5(e).) Because Suzuki’s testimony relied on the pertinent emails, it follows that the Board considered the contents of that evidence, even though those emails were not formally submitted to the Board. Indeed, if it were necessary for the emails to be submitted as an exhibit to be considered part of the complete record for purposes of CCP § 1094.6(c), such an interpretation would render the inclusion of “all written evidence” as a superfluous provision of the statute when read together with “all admitted exhibits” and “all rejected exhibits.” Such a result is not the proper way to interpret a statute. (Committee for Responsible School Expansion, supra, 142 Cal.App.4th 1178, 1189.) Statutes must be interpreted “so as to give force and effect to every provision and not in a way that would render words or clauses nugatory, inoperative or meaningless.” (Committee for Responsible School Expansion v. Hermosa Beach City School Dist. (2006) 142 Cal.App.4th 1178, 1189.)

 

By interpretating CCP § 1094.6(c) to include the pertinent emails here, the Court is not opening the door for any and all internal staff communication to be treated as part of a complete administrative record. Rather, as explained above, the pertinent emails are treated as “all written evidence” because the content of those emails was presented to and considered by the Board.

 

Lastly, the Court notes that City does not raise any argument that the emails are precluded from disclosure based on any stated privilege. As for such assertion by Josephson Investments, the privilege does not belong to Josephson Investments to assert. (See Josephson Investments Opp. at 11:15-26.)

 

Accordingly, petitioner Federation of Hillside and Canyon Associations, Inc. Motion to Augment the Administrative Record is GRANTED on the ground that the pertinent emails are part of the complete record as contemplated by CCP § 1094.6. Consequently, in the interest of judicial economy, the Court declines to address whether CCP § 1094.5(e) applies.

 

 

 

 

 


Case Number: 26STCP01094    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 836

PETITION FOR RELIEF FROM GOVERNMENT CLAIMS PRESENTATION REQUIREMENT

  

Date:               9/24/26 (1:30 PM)

Case:               Zoe S. Nole, et al. v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, et al. (26STCP01094)

  

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioners Zoe S. Noel and Haven Nole’s Petition for Relief from Government Claims Act Claim Presentation Requirement is DENIED AS MOOT.

 

Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s evidentiary objections are OVERRULED.

 

As alleged in the petition, petitioner Zoe S. Noel sustained injuries from an incident involving a bus operated by respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority on March 18, 2024. (Pet. at 2:1.) Petitioner Haven Nole may have witnessed the incident and suffered psychological injury. (Yehoshua Decl. ¶¶ 6-7 & Ex. B at 2.) A claim relating to a cause of action for personal injury must be presented to the public entity no later than six months after accrual of the cause of action.¿ (Gov. Code § 911.2; Munoz v. State of California¿(1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1767, 1776.)

 

Before and after the incident, petitioner claims to have been having cognitive ability problems. (Pet. at 1:27-28, 2:2-28.) Petitioner further claims to still be dealing with cognitive limitations. (Pet. at 3:4-5.) Nonetheless, petitioners were represented by counsel following the incident, and respondent’s records show that claims were timey filed for both petitioners with respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority in August 2024, which was within six months from the date of the incident. (Yehoshua Decl. ¶¶ 6-7 & Ex. B.) These claims were rejected on April 30, 2025, with notices mailed to petitioners and their counsel of record. (Yehoshua Decl. ¶¶ 8-10 & Exs. C-D; Pet. Ex. A.)

 

Respondent’s records and the petition show that petitioner was advised that, if she wanted to file a lawsuit, she was required to do so by October 30, 2025. (Yehoshua Decl. ¶¶ 11-12; Pet. Ex. B; see also Gov. Code § 945.6(a) [suit against public entity must be commenced within six months after notice of claim denial].) Petitioners did not file any such lawsuit against respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority.  Instead, petitioners filed a government claim with the City of Los Angeles on October 14, 2025, which was rejected by the City on November 5, 2025. (Pet., Ex. C.)

 

Given that petitioners did not file any lawsuit against respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority within six months after denial of their claim, any relief this Court might provide under Government Code § 946.6 is of no moment.  Simply put, section 946.6 may provide for relief from the government claims presentation requirement, but it provides for no relief from the failure to timely file suit within six months of rejection of the claim, as required is by section 945.6.

 

Even though petitioner claims that she did not possess “a complete copy of the claim materials or clear documentation establishing the status of any rejection applicable to Plaintiff individually” (Pet. at 3:7-8), those facts, even if true, would not alter the result here.  To begin with, petitioners still have not brought any suit for damages against respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority.  In any event, it is undisputed that petitioners were informed on May 27, 2025, of the obligation to file a lawsuit by October 30, 2025. (Pet. Ex. B.)  Furthermore, the timing of when a lawsuit must be filed is triggered by the date the notice was “deposited in the mail,” which occurred on April 30, 2025. (Gov. Code § 945.6(a)(1); Yehoshua Decl. ¶¶ 8-10 & Exs. C-E.)

 

Lastly, petitioner’s claim filed with the City of Los Angeles on October 14, 2025 does not resuscitate her ability to file a lawsuit against respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, an entirely different public entity. The Court is unaware of any legal authority that would support such a proposition.  Indeed, the rejection notice by the City for petitioner’s claim, dated November 5, 2025, informed petitioner explicitly that the respondent is a separate public entity and not part of the City of Los Angeles. (Pet. Ex. C.)

 

Accordingly, because petitioners did not timely file suit against respondent Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority as required by Government Code § 945.6(a), their request for relief from the government claims presentation requirement under Government Code § 946.6 is MOOT and must be DENIED.


Case Number: 26STCP01647    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 836

DEMURRER TO THE

PETITION FOR WRIT OF MANDAMUS

 

Date:               9/24/26 (1:30 PM)

Case:               David Ferrer v. Public Employment Relations Board (26STCP01647)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Respondent Public Employment Relations Board’s Demurrer to the Petition for Writ of Mandamus is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

 

I.          BACKGROUND

 

On April 28, 2026, petitioner David Ferrer initiated this action against respondent Public Employment Relations Board (“PERB”). Pursuant to the petition, petitioner seeks a writ of mandate under CCP § 1085 compelling PERB to (1) vacate PERB Decision No. 3018, issued April 24, 2026, in Case No. LA-CE-6977-E; (2) reconsider the matter “under the correct prima facie standard;” and (3) “permit further administrative processing consistent with the Court’s ruling.” (Pet. ¶ 1; Prayer for Relief at ¶¶ 1-3..)

 

As alleged, petitioner is a deaf educator who was formerly employed by Downey Unified School District (the “District”). (Pet. ¶ 20.) In separating from the District, petitioner signed a settlement agreement. (Pet. ¶ 22.) Petitioner also filed administrative charges and complaints against the District, which included an Unfair Practice Charge with PERB (the “Charge”). (Pet. ¶¶ 12, 21.) On April 8, 2025, the District’s counsel issued a written demand to petitioner, contending that the Charge violated the settlement agreement and demanded a withdrawal of the Charge, or else litigation would be pursued. (Pet. ¶ 22-23.) On August 11, 2025, the District filed a lawsuit against petitioner for breach of contract. (Pet. ¶¶ 24-26.)

 

On December 11, 2025, PERB’s Office of General Counsel issued a Warning Letter that identified deficiencies within the Charge and gave petitioner a change to correct the issues. (Pet. ¶ 13.) On January 2, 2026, petitioner filed a Fifth Amended Charge, narrowing the issue to the allegation that the District “retaliated against and interfered with protected PERB activity by threatening and filing civil litigation based on petitioner’s administrative filings. (Pet. ¶ 14.) On January 15, 2026, PERB’s Office of General Counsel dismissed the Fifth Amended Charge on the grounds that petitioner lacked standing as he was not an employee at the time the District filed its lawsuit and that petitioner failed to state a prima facie case as “he had not shown the District’s lawsuit lacked a reasonable basis.” (Pet. ¶ 15.)

 

Petitioner appealed the dismissal. (Pet. ¶ 16.) On April 24, 2026, PERB issued Decision No. 3018, holding that post-separation acts by a former employer are actionable if the allegations show that they are “integrally tied to the employment relationship and satisfy substantive and procedural requirements.” (Pet. ¶ 17.) Ultimately, PERB upheld the dismissal because petitioner did not allege facts to show that the retaliatory lawsuit was objectively baseless. (Pet. ¶¶ 18-19.)

 

Petitioner alleges that PERB committed a legal error in upholding the dismissal by reasoning that “[o]nce PERB recognized that [p]etitioner adequately alleged retaliation for protected activity, the proper course was to permit further administrative processing unless the charge was legally incapable of proceeding.” (Pet. ¶ 39.) It is further alleged that PERB improperly applied a heightened pleading standard and required petitioner to litigate the merits of his case. (Pet. ¶¶ 41-49.) It is also alleged that PERB “overlooked the legal significance of a public employer’s lawsuit seeking damages and injunctive relief.” (Pet. ¶ 53.)

 

On June 17, 2026, PERB filed the instant demurrer to the petition on the grounds that it fails to “show a colorable claim that PERB violated [petitioner’s] constitutional rights, exceed[ed] a grant of its authority, or embraced an erroneous statutory construction” per Internat. Assn. of Fire Fighters, Local 188, AFL-CIO v. Public Employment Relations Bd. (2011) 51 Cal. 4th 259, 271. (Notice at pg. 2.) Petitioner opposes the demurrer.

 

II.        DISCUSSION

 

As a preliminary matter, the Court notes that petitioner’s opposition is oversized by seven pages. (CRC rule 3.1113(D). Therefore, the Court shall only consider the opposition’s first fifteen pages, which are marked Page 4 through Page 18.

 

As an additional preliminary matter the Court GRANTS PERB’s requests for judicial notice pursuant to Evidence Code § 452(c). Consequently, the Court need not take judicial notice of the documents identified by petitioner, as they largely consist of the same documents identified by PERB.

 

Where pleadings are defective, a party may raise the defect by way of a demurrer or motion to strike or by motion for judgment on the pleadings. (CCP §430.30(a); Coyne v. Krempels, (1950) 36 Cal.2d 257.) The party against whom a complaint or cross-complaint has been filed may object by demurrer or answer to the pleading. (CCP §430.10.) A demurrer tests the sufficiency of a pleading, and the grounds for a demurrer must appear on the face of the pleading or from judicially noticeable matters. (CCP §430.30(a); Blank v. Kirwan, (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

PERB argues that the petition is subject to demurrer because it fails to make out a claim that is subject to judicial review under International Ass’n of Fire Fighters, Local 188, AFL–CIO v. Public Employment Relations Bd. (“Fire Fighters”) (2011) 51 Cal.4th 259. The Court agrees.

 

A Court’s review of PERB’s determination under traditional mandamus is governed by the California Supreme Court’s decision in Fire Fighters. (See also Government Code § 3509.5(a).) In Fire Fighters, our Supreme Court held that mandamus review of PERB’s decision not to issue a complaint is available in only three limited circumstances, stating:

 

[W]hen PERB refuses to issue a complaint under the MMBA [Meyers-Milias Brown Act], a superior court may exercise mandamus jurisdiction to determine whether PERB's decision [1] violates a constitutional right, [2] exceeds a specific grant of authority, or [3] is based on an erroneous statutory construction.

 

(Id. at 271.)  In emphasizing that these circumstances permitting judicial review of a PERB decision not to issue a complaint was truly “limited,” the Court further stated:

 

We stress, however, that it remains true that a refusal by PERB to issue a complaint under the MMBA is not subject to judicial review for ordinary error, including insufficiency of the evidence to support the agency’s factual findings and misapplication of the law to the facts, or for abuse of discretion. Also, to avoid undue interference with the discretion that the Legislature has intended PERB to exercise, courts must narrowly construe and cautiously apply the exceptions we here recognize.

 

(Ibid.)

 

Here, the petition sets forth ordinary error by PERB not subject to review by this Court.  The petitioner alleges that PERB erred because, “[o]nce PERB recognized that [p]etitioner adequately alleged retaliation for protected activity, the proper course was to permit further administrative processing unless the charge was legally incapable of proceeding.” (Pet. ¶ 39.)

The petition further alleges that PERB improperly applied a heightened pleading standard and required petitioner to litigate the merits of his case. (Pet. ¶¶ 41-49.) The petition additionally alleges that PERB “overlooked the legal significance of a public employer’s lawsuit seeking damages and injunctive relief” and should not have treated the settlement agreement as dispositive (Pet. ¶¶ 53, 56.) None of these allegations fall within the narrow exceptions provided within Fire Fighters. A disagreement with the outcome of a case does not establish a failure of PERB to perform its statutory function. (See Williams v. Public Employment Relations Bd. (2012) 204 Cal.App.4th 1119, 1127 [“To the extent appellants seem to suggest that PERB did not perform its statutory function with regard to their charges, they are incorrect; what appellants disagree with is PERB’s conclusion they had not established a prima facie case.”].)

 

In opposition, petitioner contends that his claims of error fall within the Fire Fighters’ exceptions for exceeding a specific grant of authority to PERB and for an erroneous statutory construction. (Opp. at 12:1-4.)  Although he claims such exceptions, petitioner identifies no specific grant of authority that was exceeded and no statute that was erroneously construed.  Instead, petitioner simply repeats the ordinary legal error alleged in the petitioner, arguing that “PERB improperly elevated Petitioner's pleading burden, prematurely resolved issues reserved for later administrative proceedings, and effectively converted the complaint issuance process into a determination on the merits.” (Opp. at 12:8-10.) Petitioner simply conflates PERB’s purported legal error in evaluating his claim with conduct equating to acting in excess of “a specific grant of authority” or to “an erroneous statutory construction.” But, as instructed in Fire Fighter, “courts must narrowly construe and cautiously apply the exceptions we here recognize,” not expand the limited exceptions to swallow up the general prohibition against judicial review or ordinary error. (Fire Fighters, 51 Cal.4th at 271.)

 

With respect to the exception for an erroneous statutory construction, petitioner argues that PERB’s decision was based on an erroneous statutory construction of the EERA because PERB is required petitioner to address the merits of his charge, rather than accept his allegations as true. (Opp. at 12-14, 16-17.) Petitioner, however, fails to show how EERA imposes any particular pleading standard. To the contrary, where a charge includes a claim of interference with EERA rights or retaliation via litigation, PERB’s prior precedent requires a showing that the respondent acted without any reasonable basis and for an unlawful purpose. (Operating Engineers Local Union No. 3, AFL-CIO (Wagner) (2021) PERB Decision No. 2782-M, at pg. 8.) When PERB applied that same precedent to petitioner’s claim here, it engaged in no erroneous application or EERA or any other statute.

 

As for exceeding a specific grant of authority, as noted above, petitioner identifies no such specific grant of authority that was exceeded.  Instead, petitioner argues that PERB exceeded its authority by “applying its litigation-retaliation standard in a manner that creates practical immunity for employer lawsuits targeting protected PERB activity. (Opp. at 18:3-4.)  It is not clear how PERB’s application of a standard for pleading to evaluated petitioner’s claim exceeded PERB’s authority.  Moreover, PERB’s requirement of compliance with particular pleading requirements does not, as petitioner claims, immunize PERB from responsibility for its unlawful conduct.

 

Accordingly, because the petition fails to allege adequately that any of the limited circumstances permitting judicial review of PERB”s decision under Fire Fighter, the Court SUSTAINS the Demurrer.  Furthermore, based on the arguments presented by petitioner in opposition, the Court does not find that it is reasonably possible that such defect could be cured by amendment.  Thus, leave to amend is DENIED. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)
