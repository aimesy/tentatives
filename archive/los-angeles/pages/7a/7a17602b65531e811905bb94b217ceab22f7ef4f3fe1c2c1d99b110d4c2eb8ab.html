MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 473(b), (d))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court DENIES John Steven Vo’s motion to set aside the default judgment entered against him.

 

The Court’s Judicial Assistant is to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

            The Court previously continued this hearing to allow John Steven Vo to file and serve a declaration in support of his motion to set aside the default judgment against him, or to give Vo and Progressive West Insurance Company the chance to meet to resolve the issue.  Since then, neither party has filed any document with the Court.  The Court therefore adopts its previous tentative ruling as follows:

 

Progressive West Insurance Company sued John Steven Vo over a car accident.  It purported to have made substituted service on Vo when a registered server left process with “Tim Vo Co-Occupant” at Vo’s home address, and then mailed the same process to Vo.  Vo failed to answer Progressive’s complaint, so Progressive obtained a default judgment against him in October 2024.  Vo now moves to set aside the judgment and the underlying default.  The Court DENIES the motion.

 

            Vo seeks relief from the default judgment on two alternative grounds.  Under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), if a party’s own mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect causes it to suffer a default judgment, that party can seek relief from the judgment within six months of its entry.  But the default judgment against Vo was entered more than six months before he filed his motion.

 

            Under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), a party can move at any time to set aside a void judgment.  (California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn (2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, 225–226.)  A default judgment is void if it’s entered against a party who was never served with process.  (County of San Deigo v. Gorham (2010) 186 CalApp.4th 1215, 1226.)  Because there is proof of service on Vo, and because it’s signed by a registered process server, the burden is on Vo to prove that he wasn’t served in order to be relieved of the default judgment against him.  (American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)  Vo’s motion papers contain his representation that he was incarcerated at the time he was purportedly served, but there is no evidence of that—not even a sworn-under-penalty-of-perjury declaration from Vo himself.  Without evidence, Vo cannot meet an evidentiary burden.

 

            The Court therefore DENIES Vo’s motion.

Case Number: 23STLC08229    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Thurs., July 9, 2026                                        JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           Macadangdang v. Maravilla, et al.                                        

COMP. FILED:         12-29-23

CASE NUMBER:      23STLC08229                       

NOTICE:                   OK                                                                 

                                               

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION FOR ORDER VACATING AND SETTING ASIDE DISMISSAL OF CASE

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Josie Macadangdang

RESP. PARTY:         Defendants Novalita Maravilla and California Care Referral Services, Inc.

 

MOTION TO SET ASIDE DISMISSAL

(CCP § 473(b))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court GRANTS Josie Macadangdang’s motion to vacate and set aside the dismissal entered against them.

 

The November 20, 2025 dismissal is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Josie Macadangdang sued Novalita Maravilla and California Care Referral Services, Inc. over unpaid wages.  Macadangdang failed to appear for the November 20, 2025 trial of the action, and the Court granted Maravilla and CCRS’s request to dismiss Macadangdang’s complaint without prejudice.  Macadangdang now moves to have that dismissal vacated under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), because the dismissal was the result of surprise.  The Court GRANTS the motion.

 

            Under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), a party can seek relief from a dismissal caused by its own mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  Macadangdang seeks relief under the “surprise” prong of the statute.  “Surprise” refers to a condition in which the party is unexpectedly placed, to their detriment and through no fault of their own, which ordinary care could not have guarded against.  (State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Pietak (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 600, 611.)  Macadangdang’s spouse, George Clifton, experienced a medical emergency on the morning of trial, which prevented Macadangdang from appearing.  Macadangdang called both opposing counsel and the Court that morning to explain the situation. 

 

This may or may not be surprise, but it is, in any event, excusable neglect.  Neglect is excusable if a reasonably prudent person under similar circumstances might have made the same error. (Austin v. Los Angeles Unified School District (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 918, 929.)  The moving party must show a reasonable excuse for the dismissal.  (Shapiro v. Clark (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1141–1142.)  Macadangdang has done that here.  Macadangdang provides evidence of Clifton’s ongoing medical condition and attests to Clifton having a medical emergency the morning of trial.  Macadangdang has a reasonable excuse for not appearing at trial, and a reasonably prudent person under similar circumstances may have done the same thing Macadangdang did.

 

            Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Macadangdang’s motion.  The November 20, 2025 dismissal is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.

 

            Macadangdang is to give notice.

PROCEEDINGS:      PETITION TO CORRECT CONTRACTUAL ARBITRATION AWARD

 

MOVING PARTY:   Petitioner Walter Young

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

PETITION TO CORRECT ARBITRATION AWARD

(CCP § 1285, et seq.)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

            The Court previously continued this hearing on Walter Young’s petition to correct an arbitration award to allow him the opportunity to address deficiencies in the petition.  Young has now properly filed proof of service of the petition and notice of hearing on Pacific Blue Roofing.  But Young was also ordered to file and serve documents addressing the various deficiencies in his papers by 16 court days before today’s hearing.  Instead, he filed a declaration—containing arithmetic with no narrative explanation—only two court days before the hearing, with no proof of service on anyone. 

 

The Court previously warned Young that this was his “one last time” to address the deficiencies in his petition, which was in any event untimely filed as an initial matter.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288.)   

 

The Court DENIES Young’s petition.

 

            Young is to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

            State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company filed this subrogation action against Juan Montesmoreno and Jennifer Hernandez.  Hernandez defaulted and Montesmoreno answered.  State Farm then propounded nine requests for admission on Montesmoreno. 

 

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)  If a party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted.  (Id. § 2033.280, subd. (a), (b).) 

 

State Farm served Montesmoreno with requests for admission in March 2026.  To date, no responses have been served.  Montesmoreno has not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED and State Farm’s first set of requests for admission are deemed admitted. 

 

            Montesmoreno must also pay sanctions for causing State Farm to move to deem the requests admitted.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)  State Farm requests $460.00—for two hours of its counsel’s work at $200.00 per hour plus $60.00 in filing fees—from Montesmoreno.  The Court finds this request reasonable.  Montesmoreno must pay State Farm $460.00 within 30 days of this order.

 

            State Farm is to give notice.
