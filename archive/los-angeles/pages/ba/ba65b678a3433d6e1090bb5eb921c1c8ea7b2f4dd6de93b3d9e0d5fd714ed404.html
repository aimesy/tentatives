MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

 

Motion filed on 6/1/26.

 

MOVING ATTORNEY: Jeffrey S. Kramer

CLIENTS: Defendants Vivian Almache and Jonathan Alexander Almache

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order relieving Jeffrey S. Kramer as Defendants Vivian Almache and Jonathan Alexander Almache’s counsel in this action. 

 

RULING: The motion is denied without prejudice. 

 

On 6/1/26, attorney Jeffrey S. Kramer filed the instant motion seeking to be relieved as counsel for Vivian Almache and Jonathan Alexander Almache in this action.  The declaration filed in support of the motion indicates that attorney Kramer is in the process of retiring and closing his law practice.  Additionally, the declaration states that arrangements have been made for new counsel who has associated into the subject litigation.  Attorney Kramer goes on to state that Mr. Almache has been unresponsive throughout most of the litigation and Ms. Almache stopped responding in early 2024.  Mr. Kramer further indicates that numerous attempts to communicate with the clients have been made via phone, mail, email and retaining a private investigator to contact them.  However, none of the efforts have been successful. 

 

The motion was originally scheduled for hearing on 10/27/26.  Pursuant to counsel’s ex parte application, the hearing on the motion was advanced to 7/8/26.  (See 6/24/26 Minute Order; 6/25/26 Nunc Pro Tunc).  The proofs of service attached to the moving papers do not show service of the motion on the clients.  The proofs of service attached to the moving papers do show service on attorney Jay Van De Poel on behalf of the clients who appears to be the associated counsel referenced in the declaration.  However, the email address listed for attorney Van De Poel in the proofs of service ([email protected] does not match the email address in eCourt [email protected]).  The proof of service for the notice of the Court’s 6/24/26 ruling on the ex parte application advancing the hearing on the motion does indicate that the notice was emailed to the clients.  However, there is no evidence that the motion itself was served on the clients or that the electronic service address is the client’s current electronic service address.  See CRC 3.1362(d)(2).  The declaration merely states that the emails have not been returned or bounced.    

 

It is also not clear whether the clients will be representing themselves if the motion is granted or whether attorney Jay Van De Poel of Van De Poel & Levy LLP will take over representing the clients in this matter as the proposed order on this motion sets forth the clients address and telephone number.   

 

 

 

    

 


Case Number: 24CHCV00674    Hearing Date: July 8, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 7/8/26                                                               TRIAL DATE: 10/12/26

Case #24CHCV00674

 

MOTION TO COMPEL NEUROLOGICAL IME

 

Motion filed on 3/2/26.

 

MOVING  PARTY: Defendants Smart & Final Stores LLC & Chedraui USA, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Carmen Harper

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Plaintiff Carmen Harper to appear for a physical neurological independent medical examination. 

 

RULING: The motion is denied. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of a slip and fall incident that occurred on 7/4/22 at a Smart & Final store in Northridge, California.  Plaintiff Carmen Harper (Plaintiff) alleges that she slipped and fell on ice resulting in personal injuries.  Plaintiff’s complaint alleges one cause of action for premises liability against Defendants Smart & Final LLC and Chedraui USA, Inc. (collectively, Defendants).

 

In discovery responses, Plaintiff identified injuries including “myelomalacia, and/or aggravation of myelomalacia,” and claims affected body areas include neck, back, and legs, with gait and balance issues and intermittent cane use.  (Kosch Decl., Ex.2).  Plaintiff also indicated she experienced paralysis on the date of the incident.  Id.  Further, Plaintiff has indicated that she continues to treat with neurosurgeon Rollin Hu, M.D.  Id.  Defendants have already completed an orthopedic IME of Plaintiff. 

 

Defendants requested that Plaintiff stipulate to another neurological IME; however, Plaintiff refused.  Therefore, on 3/2/26, Defendants filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling Plaintiff to appear for a physical neurological independent medical examination.  Plaintiff has opposed the motion.  Defendants have not filed a reply to the opposition. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

In personal injury actions, defendants are entitled to one physical examination of the plaintiff.  CCP 2032.220(a).  The court may order additional examinations upon a showing of good cause.  CCP 2032.310; CCP 2032.320.  When a plaintiff claims injuries crossing medical specialties, courts allow additional IMEs when reasonably necessary to test the nature, extent and causation of claimed damages.  See Vinson (1987) 43 C3d 833, 840; Shapira (1990) 224 CA3d 1249, 1255.  Without any authority (e.g. legal or expert), Defendants conclude:

 

“An orthopedic exam is not a substitute for a dedicated neurological evaluation. Orthopedic assessment centers on musculoskeletal structure and mechanics; neurology evaluates cord/nerve dysfunction, neurological deficits, and related functional sequelae. Because Plaintiff claims spinal cord-related pathology and neurologic deficits, a neurologist’s evaluation is materially distinct and necessary.”

 

(See Motion, p.5:11-16).

 

However, Defendants fail to acknowledge that the report from Ardalan Nourian, M.D., a board-certified orthopedic surgeon, who conducted the defense medical examination on 8/15/25, includes a section which specifically addresses “Neurologic Examinations.”  (See Oran Decl., Ex.1, p.4).  Defendants fail to explain why the examination by Dr. Nourian and his subsequent report are insufficient to cover Plaintiff’s neurological injuries.   

 

Additionally, a motion for an additional medical examination must “specify the time, place, manner, conditions, scope, and nature of the examination, as well as the identity and the specialty, if any, of the person or persons who will perform the examination.”  CCP 2032.310(b).  Here, the motion leaves the time and location of the proposed examination to be determined.  (See Motion, p.6:3-6; proposed Order, p.2:8-9).  The motion also fails to adequately describe the manner, conditions, scope and nature of the examination.  Instead, the motion vaguely states that the examination will be limited “to neurological conditions/injuries allegedly caused or aggravated by the incident, including claimed myelomalacia, gait/balance deficits, cervical neurologic complaints, and related functional limitations” and “[c]onfirming compliance with CCP section 2032 requirements (no invasive procedures absent further order/stipulation; no imaging unless agreed or separately ordered).  (See Motion, p.6:7-11; proposed Order, p.2:10-17).

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is denied. 


Case Number: 24CHCV00702    Hearing Date: July 8, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 7/8/26                                                         TRIAL DATE: 6/1/27

Case #24CHCV00702

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE A SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Motion filed on 5/21/26. 

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Lev Katsman

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Mahle Filter Systems North America Inc. dba Mahle Letrika USA

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order granting Plaintiff leave to file a Second Amended Complaint. 

 

RULING: The motion is denied without prejudice.    

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of an incident that occurred on 12/8/23 at Defendant Clubcorp Porter Valley Country Club, Inc. (The Club) when Plaintiff Lev Katsman (Plaintiff) was playing a round of golf.  (See FAC ¶¶10, 18).  Plaintiff alleges that while driving the E-Z-Go Elite golf cart (the golf cart) assigned and provided to him by the Club, “the front right wheel of the golf cart suddenly and without any warning locked up, which then caused the rear-end of the golf cart to swerve left in a clockwise direction, which then caused plaintiff  to be ejected from the driver’s side of  the golf cart” resulting in Plaintiff suffering serious and permanent injuries. (emphasis added) (FAC ¶18).

 

On 3/5/24, Plaintiff filed this action against The Club and Does 1-50 alleging one cause of action for negligence.  Plaintiff’s original complaint made the same allegations regarding the right front wheel locking up and causing the incident.  (See Complaint ¶¶14, 17-19).  On 4/18/24, The Club answered the complaint and filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff and Roes 1-25.  On 10/16/24, The Club filed a First Amended Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, Textron Specialized Vehicles, Inc. and Textron E-Z-G0, LLC (collectively, Textron) and Roes 1-50.  On 12/13/24, Textron answered The Club’s First Amended Cross-Complaint.  On 12/13/24, The Club filed a Roe amendment naming Mahle Filter Systems North America, Inc. dba Mahle Letrika USA (Mahle) as Roe 1.  On 1/13/25, Textron filed a cross-complaint against Mahle.  On 3/7/25, Mahle answered Textron’s cross-complaint. 

 

On 3/7/25, The Club dismissed Mahle as Roe 1 from its First Amended Cross-Complaint as to the 1st – 4th causes of action and filed a Roe amendment naming Mahle as Roe 13 as to the 5th – 8th causes of action.

 

On 3/12/25, Plaintiff answered The Club’s First Amended Cross-Complaint.  On 4/4/25, Mahle answered The Club’s First Amended Cross-Complaint.  On 6/9/25, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file a First Amended Complaint was granted and on 6/10/25, Plaintiff filed his operative First Amended Complaint which alleges causes of action for: (1) Negligence against The Club and Does 1-25, (2) Products Liability against Textron and Does 26-50, (3) Strict Products Liability against Textron and Does 26-50, (4) Breach of Express and Implied Warranties against Textron and Does 26-50, and (5) Breach of Warranties of Fitness for a Particular Purpose against Textron and Does 26-50.  On 6/11/25, Plaintiff filed a Doe amendment naming Mahle as Doe 1 in his First Amended Complaint. 

 

On 7/14/25, Textron dismissed Mahle from its cross-complaint without prejudice.  On 7/15/25, The Club dismissed Mahle from its First Amended Cross-Complaint without prejudice.  On 7/23/25, Plaintiff dismissed Mahle from his First Amended Complaint without prejudice.  On 9/10/25, Plaintiff filed a Doe amendment naming Mahle as Doe 27 in his First Amended Complaint.

 

On 4/17/26, the Court overruled Mahle’s demurrer to the First Amended Complaint.  In its ruling on the demurrer, the Court noted that:

 

“the Complaint and the First Amended Complaint repeatedly allege that the incident resulted from “the front right wheel of the golf cart suddenly and without any warning lock[ing] up, which then caused the rear-end of the golf cart to swerve left in a clockwise direction, which then caused plaintiff to be ejected from the driver’s side of the golf cart.” (emphasis added) (See Complaint ¶¶14,17; First Amended Complaint ¶¶18, 19, 21-23, 28, 34, 49). However, Plaintiff’s opposition to the demurrer claims that the incident occurred when “one of the rear wheels locked without warning, causing the cart to violently rotate, eject Plaintiff….” (emphasis added) (See Opposition, p.5:14-16, p.7:15-17). In the reply, Mehle claims that this is the first time Plaintiff has claimed that the action arises from a mechanical failure relating to the rear wheel of the golf cart. (See Reply, p.2:3-18).

 

If Plaintiff intends to now base his claims on these new facts, it would seem prudent that he seek leave to amend his pleading by filing a properly noticed motion for leave to amend which would allow Mahle Letrika USA, as well as the other defendants, to address the propriety of adding such allegations at this point in the proceedings.”

 

(See 4/17/26 Minute Order, pp.4-5).

 

 

 

On 5/5/26, Mahle answered the First Amended Complaint.  On 5/21/26, Plaintiff filed and served the  instant motion for leave to file a Second Amended Complaint.  Mahle has  opposed the motion and Plaintiff has filed a reply to the opposition.    

 

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

Leave to amend is to be liberally granted so that matters may be resolved on their merits.  CCP 473(a)(2); CCP 576; Nestle (1972) 6 C3d 920; Hirsa (1981) 118 CA3d 486, 488-489; Mabie (1998) 61 CA4th 581, 596.  If a motion to amend is timely and granting same will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to deny leave to amend and where the refusal results in a party being deprived of the right to assert a meritorious cause of action, it is an abuse of discretion.  California Casualty General Insurance Co. (1985) 173 CA3d 274, 278.

 

A motion for leave to amend must be accompanied by a declaration that specifies: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.  CRC 3.1324(b).

 

Here, the declaration of Plaintiff’s attorney, Ronald M. Binder, submitted in support of the motion fails to meet the foregoing requirements.  The declaration merely sets forth attorney Binder’s qualifications; identifies the attached exhibits (e.g. complaint, First Amended Complaint, proposed Second Amended Complaint); indicates that written discovery has been conducted, some depositions have been taken and more will take place in the near future; and indicates that a physical inspection of the golf cart has been completed.  (See Binder Decl. ¶¶1-6).  The declaration concludes that since the trial is not scheduled until 6/1/27, allowing the amendment will not prejudice any other party.  (Id. ¶7).

 

Contrary to the assertion in the reply, the opposition does not argue that the motion should be denied because Plaintiff “did not provide a redline showing additions, deletions, and alterations between the first and second amended complaints.”  (See Reply, p.9:7-9).  With regard to Plaintiff’s failure to comply with CRC 3.1324, the opposition focuses on the deficiencies the attorney Binder’s declaration.  (See Opposition, p.8:16-21).  It is not until the reply that attorney Binder includes a declaration pursuant to the requirements of CRC 3.1324(b).  (See Reply, p.5:17-p.8:6 and Ex.A thereto).  The foregoing is not a mere technical defect as the Binder declaration filed with the reply provides the substantive basis for the motion depriving the other parties, particularly Mahle, of the opportunity to respond to same in the opposition.      

 

Additionally, contrary to Plaintiff’s assertion, with regard to the sham pleading doctrine, Mahle does not rely “nearly exclusively on discovery responses as its source of alleged inconsistencies.”  (See Reply, p.3:24-25).  The opposition points out allegations in the original and First Amended Complaint regarding the right front wheel locking up which are inconsistent with the proposed new allegations in the Second Amended Complaint regarding the rear wheels locking up.  (See Opposition, p.3:4-16, p.4:18-20).   

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is denied without prejudice.
