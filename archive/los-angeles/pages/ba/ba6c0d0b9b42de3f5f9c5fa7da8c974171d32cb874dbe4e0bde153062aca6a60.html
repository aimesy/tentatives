Case Number: 25STCV27898    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 729

Superior Court of California

DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

JOHN DOE,

 

         vs.

 

BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE CALIFORNIA STATE UNIVERSITY.

 Case No.:  25STCV27898

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  September 28, 2026

 

Defendant The Board of Trustees of the California State University’s demurrer to Plaintiff John Doe’s First Amended Complaint is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to the 2nd and 3rd causes of action and overruled as to the 1st cause of action.

Defendant The Board of Trustees of the California State University’s motion to strike the entirety and portions of Plaintiff John Doe’s First Amended Complaint is denied as moot.

 

Defendant The Board of Trustees of the California State University (“CSU”) (“Defendant”) demurs Plaintiff John Doe’s (“Doe”) (“Plaintiff”) 1st, 2nd, and 3rd causes of action in his first amended complaint (“FAC”) on the basis they fail to state facts sufficient to constitute any cause of action against Defendant.  (Notice of Demurrer, pgs. 1-2; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)

Defendant also moves to strike the entirety and portions of Plaintiff’s FAC.  (Notice of MTS, pgs. 1-2; C.C.P. §§436(a), (b).)

 

Request for Judicial Notice

Defendant’s 6/3/26 request for judicial notice of (1) Ruling on Verified Petition for Writ of Mandate in Doe v. Board of Trustees of the California State University, LASC Case No. 24STCP01952 (D-RJN, Exh. A); and (2) the appellate opinion in Court of Appeal, Second District B344378 Jane Roe v. Jenna Smith (D-RJN, Exh. B), is granted, however, the Court does not take judicial notice of the truth of the matters asserted therein.

 

1.     Demurrer

Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, the demurring party must meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading to attempt to reach an agreement that would resolve the objections to the pleading and obviate the need for filing the demurrer.  (C.C.P. §430.41(a), emphasis added.)

Defendant’s counsel declares that “the parties to this litigation met and conferred . . . via telephone on June 3, 2026, but they did not reach an agreement.”   (Decl. of Winder ¶2.)  Defendant’s counsel’s declaration is sufficient under C.C.P. §430.41(a).  Therefore, the Court will consider Defendant’s demurrer.

 

Procedural Background

          Plaintiff filed his initial Complaint on June 23, 2025, against Defendant alleging three causes of action: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Negligence; and (3) Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing.

          On March 4, 2026, this Court sustained Defendant’s demurrer to the Complaint with 20 days leave to amend.  (3/4/26 Minute Order.)

          Plaintiff filed the operative FAC on April 2, 2026, against Defendant, alleging the same three causes of action.

Defendant filed its demurrer and accompanying motion to strike on June 3, 2026.  Plaintiff filed his oppositions on September 28, 2026.  Defendant filed its replies on September 21, 2026.

 

Summary of Demurrer

Defendant demurs to each cause of action on the basis they fail to state facts sufficient to constitute causes of action against it.  (Demurrer, pgs. 5-6.)

 

Legal Standard

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.)  A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable.  (See Donabedian v. Mercury Insurance Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].)  For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law.  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital District (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

Failure to State a Cause of Action

Breach of Contract (1st COA)

“To prevail on a cause of action for breach of contract, the plaintiff must prove (1) the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance of the contract or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) resulting damage to the plaintiff.”  (Richman v. Hartley (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1182, 1186.)

Plaintiff alleges that he was told by CSUCI administrators that if he did not sign the Settlement and Release Agreement, Plaintiff would be terminated immediately with no evidentiary hearing, no appeal, and no corresponding salary in lieu of three months’ notice of termination.  (FAC ¶72.)

Plaintiff alleges that the University represented that the Settlement Agreement and General Release would fully and finally settle any differences between the University and Plaintiff and that the harassment investigation and Mr. Nutt’s investigation report and findings would be kept confidential.  (FAC ¶73.)

Plaintiff alleges that the University represented that the Title IX complaint and investigation report would not be included in Plaintiff’s personnel file.  (FAC ¶74.)

Plaintiff alleges that on May 17, 2023, Plaintiff and CSU signed the Settlement Agreement and General Release and Plaintiff left his employment at CSUCI effective May 17, 2023, and received the sum of $50,400 salary less all appropriate payroll deductions and withholdings as the corresponding salary in lieu of the required three months’ notice before the separation date.  (FAC ¶75.)

Plaintiff alleges as of May 2023, all issues and differences concerning Plaintiff’s employment with CSUCI were fully and finally resolved by the Settlement Agreement whereby CSU agreed to place Plaintiff’s letter of resignation in his personnel file and agreed that Mr. Nutt’s investigation report, issued without an evidentiary hearing, would be kept confidential.  (FAC ¶76.)

Plaintiff alleges the Settlement Agreement was a valid written contract supported by consideration.  (FAC ¶103.)  Plaintiff alleges he performed all obligations required of him under the Settlement Agreement, except as excused or waived.  (FAC ¶104.) 

Plaintiff alleges Defendants owed contractual duties to Plaintiff under the Settlement Agreement, including duties to honor the agreement’s finality, confidentiality, and personnel-file-related terms, and to refrain from disclosing, reviving, republishing, or using the settled matter in a manner inconsistent with the agreement.  (FAC ¶105.) 

Plaintiff alleges that in or about March 2024, Defendant, through one or more officers, employees, agents, and/or representatives, disclosed, communicated, relied upon, republished, and/or otherwise revived the settled matter.  (FAC ¶85.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant thereby asserted, directly or indirectly, that Plaintiff had violated the CSU Nondiscrimination Policy and/or had engaged in sexual harassment, notwithstanding the Settlement Agreement’s promise of finality and confidentiality.  (FAC ¶86.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendant materially breached the Settlement Agreement by, among other things, disclosing or republishing the settled matter, asserting that Plaintiff had violated CSU policy and/or engaged in sexual harassment notwithstanding the agreement, repudiating the agreement’s promise of final resolution, and using the settled matter in connection with later personnel decisions affecting Plaintiff.  (FAC ¶106.)  Plaintiff alleges Defendant’s breaches were material and deprived Plaintiff of the benefits for which he bargained, including confidentiality, finality, closure, and freedom from later use of the settled matter against him.  (FAC ¶107.) 

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and proximate result of Defendant’s breach, Plaintiff suffered damages according to proof, including but not limited to lost compensation, lost benefits, lost employment opportunities, injury to professional reputation with economic consequences, and other consequential damages.  (FAC ¶108.)

Plaintiff’s opposition concedes that “the written Settlement Agreement does not contain a general confidentiality or nondisclosure provision.”  (Opposition, pg. 9.)  Nonetheless, the FAC alleges that “Mr. Nutt’s investigation report, issued without an evidentiary hearing, would be kept confidential.”  (FAC ¶76.)  Plaintiff therefore alleges that the confidentiality of the investigation report is a material term the Settlement Agreement Defendant argues confidentiality of the report was not a term of the Settlement Agreement.  Since the Settlement Agreement was not attached to the Complaint or FAC, the Court cannot make this determination.  Accepting the pleadings as true, within the four corners of the FAC, Plaintiff sufficiently alleges a cause of action for breach of contract.

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the 1st cause of action is overruled.

 

Negligence (2nd COA)

“The elements of a cause of action for negligence are well established. They are ‘(a) a legal duty to use due care; (b) a breach of such legal duty; [and] (c) the breach as the proximate or legal cause of the resulting injury.’”  (Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendants owed Plaintiff a duty to maintain confidentiality of various matters pursuant to the settlement agreement.  (FAC ¶112.)  Plaintiff alleges the foregoing duty was breached when Plaintiff did not receive the full benefit of the bargained for settlement agreement.  (FAC ¶113.)  Plaintiff alleges he suffered injuries as a direct result of CSU’s breach, including loss of career opportunities, economic losses, emotional harm and damages, and reputational damages.  (FAC ¶114.)

Government Code §815(a) provides, “Except as otherwise provided by statute: (a) A public entity is not liable for an injury, whether such injury arises out of an act or omission of the public entity or a public employee or any other person.”  (Gov. Code § 815(a).)

In Miklosy v. Regents of University of California (2008) 44 Cal.4th 876, the California Supreme Court confirmed that Government Code §815 effectively abolishes all common law tort claims against public entities.  (Miklosy v. Regents of University of California (2008) 44 Cal.4th 876, 899 [“[O]ur own decisions confirm that section 815 abolishes common law tort liability for public entities.”].)

Here, Defendant is a government entity pursuant to Education Code §§899000, et seq., and therefore, a common law tort claim cannot be asserted against Defendant.  The FAC fails to allege negligence based on a statutory duty. 

Plaintiff’s opposition acknowledges that the duty he asserts is derived from a statute and not an independent tort duty of confidentiality; the FAC does not currently asserted this statutory duty, namely, the Information Practices Act of 1977.  (Opposition Demurrer, pg. 15.) 

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the 2nd cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing (3rd COA)

“The covenant of good faith and fair dealing, implied by law in every contract, exists merely to prevent one contracting party from unfairly frustrating the other party’s right to receive the benefits of the agreement actually made. The covenant thus cannot ‘be endowed with an existence independent of its contractual underpinnings.’ It cannot impose substantive duties or limits on the contracting parties beyond those incorporated in the specific terms of their agreement.”  (Guz v. Bechtel National, Inc. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 317, 349-350, internal citations omitted.)

Plaintiff alleges that in or about March 2024, Defendant, through one or more officers, employees, agents, and/or representatives, disclosed, communicated, relied upon, republished, and/or otherwise revived the settled matter.  (FAC ¶85.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendant thereby asserted, directly or indirectly, that Plaintiff had violated the CSU Nondiscrimination Policy and/or had engaged in sexual harassment, notwithstanding the Settlement Agreement’s promise of finality and confidentiality.  (FAC ¶86.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendant communicated such assertions and/or relied on such assertions in connection with later CSU employment decisions affecting Plaintiff.  (FAC ¶87.)

Plaintiff alleges that under the Settlement Agreement, Plaintiff had a right to receive the benefits of the parties’ bargain, including finality, confidentiality, closure, and freedom from later revival or use of the settled matter in a manner that would impair his professional opportunities.  (FAC ¶118.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant breached the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing by acting in a manner that unfairly interfered with Plaintiff’s right to receive the benefits of the Settlement Agreement, including by reviving, disclosing, republishing, relying upon, and/or using the settled matter in later employment-related decisions and communications.  (FAC ¶119.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant’s conduct was contrary to the purposes of the Settlement Agreement and deprived Plaintiff of the agreement’s intended benefits even apart from, or in addition to, the express breaches alleged herein.  (FAC ¶120.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and proximate result of Defendant’s breach of the implied covenant, Plaintiff suffered damages according to proof, including economic and consequential damages.  (FAC ¶121.)

Plaintiff fails to allege a cause of action for breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing that is additional to the claims asserted in the breach of contract cause of action.  (Careau & Co. v. Security Pacific Business Credit, Inc. (1990) 222 Cal.App.3d 1371, 1395 [“If the allegations do not go beyond the statement of a mere contract breach and, relying on the same alleged acts, simply seek the same damages or other relief already claimed in a companion contract cause of action, they may be disregarded as superfluous as no additional claim is actually stated. Thus, absent those limited cases where a breach of a consensual contract term is not claimed or alleged, the only justification for asserting a separate cause of action for breach of the implied covenant is to obtain a tort recovery.”].)  Because Plaintiff’s claim is based on the same alleged violation of contract terms, namely that “Mr. Nutt’s investigation report, issued without an evidentiary hearing, would be kept confidential,” the claim is duplicative.  (FAC ¶76.)

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the 3rd cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ FAC is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to the 2nd and 3rd causes of action and overruled as to the 1st cause of action.

Moving Party to give notice.

 

2.     Motion to Strike

In light of the Court’s ruling on the demurrer, Defendant’s motion to strike is denied as moot.

 

Conclusion

Defendant’s motion to strike is denied as moot.

Moving Party to give notice.

Case Number: 26STCP02675    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 729

Superior Court of California

DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

WATERLOO PAYS, LLC, 

 

         vs.

 

CERTAIN STATUTORILY DEFINED INTERESTED PARTIES.

 Case No.:  26STCP02675

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  September 28, 2026

 

Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC’s unopposed first amended petition for approval of transfer of structured settlement payment rights is granted. 

 

Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC (“Waterloo”) (“Petitioner”)[1] petitions this Court to approve its First Amended Petition and enter an order in accordance with Insurance Code §10139.5, approving this transfer of structured settlement payment rights and ordering the Payment Obligor BrowerGreer PLC (“BrowerGreer”) (“Payment Obligor” and “Payment Issuer”), seeking approval of the transfer of certain structured settlement payment rights by Payee Raymond Gutierrez (“Gutierrez”) (“Payee”) to Petitioner or its assignee.  (Notice Hearing on First Amended Petition, pg. 2.)

 

Legal Standard

Insurance Code §10137 provides, “[a] transfer of structured settlement payment rights is void unless a court reviews and approves the transfer and finds the following conditions are met: (a) [t]he transfer of the structured settlement payment rights is fair and reasonable and in the best interest of the payee, taking into account the welfare and support of his or her dependents [; and] (b) [t]he transfer complies with the requirements of this article, will not contravene other applicable law, and the court has reviewed and approved the transfer as provided in Section 10139.5.”  (Ins. Code §10137.)  The transfer agreement is effective only upon approval in a final court order.  (Ins. Code §10139.5(a).)

Insurance Code §10139.5(a) requires the court to make the following findings:

(1)   The transfer is in the best interest of the payee, taking into account the welfare and support of the payee’s dependents.

(2)   The payee has been advised in writing by the transferee to seek independent professional advice regarding the transfer and has either received that advice or knowingly waived, in writing, the opportunity to receive the advice.

(3)   The transferee has complied with the notification requirements pursuant to paragraph (2) of subdivision (f), the transferee has provided the payee with a disclosure form that complies with Section 10136, and the transfer agreement complies with Sections 10136 and 10138.

(4)   The transfer does not contravene any applicable statute or the order of any court or other government authority.

(5)   The payee understands the terms of the transfer agreement, including the terms set forth in the disclosure statement required by Section 10136.

(6)   The payee understands and does not wish to exercise the payee’s right to cancel the transfer agreement.

 

(Ins. Code §10139.5(a).)

In addition, pursuant to Insurance Code §10139.5(c):

Every petition for approval of a transfer of structured settlement payment rights, except as provided in subdivision (d), shall include, to the extent known after the transferee has made reasonable inquiry with the payee, all of the following:

 

(1) The payee’s name, address, and age.

(2) The payee’s marital status, and, if married or separated, the name of the payee’s spouse.     

(3) The names, ages, and place or places of residence of the payee’s minor children or other dependents, if any.

(4) The amounts and sources of the payee’s monthly income and financial resources and, if presently married, the amounts and sources of the monthly income and financial resources of the payee’s spouse.

(5)  Whether the payee is currently obligated under any child support or spousal support order, and, if so, the names, addresses, and telephone numbers of any individual, entity, or agency that is receiving child or spousal support from the payee under that order or that has jurisdiction over the order or the payments in question.

(6) Information regarding previous transfers or attempted transfers, as described in paragraph (11), (12), or (13) of subdivision (b).  The transferee or payee may choose to provide this information by providing copies of pleadings, transaction documents, or orders involving any previous attempted or completed transfer or by providing the court a summary of available information regarding any previous transfer or attempted transfer, such as the date of the transfer or attempted transfer, the payments transferred or attempted to be transferred by the payee in the earlier transaction, the amount of money received by the payee in connection with the previous transaction, and generally the payee’s reasons for pursuing or completing a previous transaction. 

 

(Ins. Code §10139.5(c).)

 

Discussion

Payee became entitled to certain structured settlement payments in an abuse case titled Jane BP1-B Doe, et al. vs. Doe 1, et al., LASC Case No. 22STCV25961 (filed August 11, 2022).  (Amended Petition ¶3.)  Payee has entered into a transfer agreement with Petitioner (“Transfer Agreement”) to transfer those certain structured settlement payments described in Paragraph 5.17 of the Transfer Agreement (“Transferred Payments”).[2]  (Amended Petition ¶4, Exh. 1.)

The Transfer Agreement states that Payee agrees to sell, transfer, deliver and irrevocably assign to Petitioner and Petitioner’s successors and assigns at Closing 4 annual payments of $43,000.00 due beginning January 1, 2027, through and including January 1, 2030.  (Amended Petition ¶4, Exh. 1 at Schedule A.)  The net amount paid to Payee would be $101,019.66.  (Amended Petition ¶4, Exh. 1 at Schedule A.)  The future structured settlement payments would total one hundred seventy-two thousand dollars ($ 172,000.00), and Payee’s transaction at issue exchanges those payments for the purchase price of one hundred one thousand nineteen dollars and sixty-six cents ($101,019.66).  (Amended Petition ¶5, Exh. 2.)  The discounted present value of the amount Payee is selling (the value of Payee’s structured settlement in current dollars) is $155,885.34.  (Amended Petition ¶4, Exh. 1 at Addendum to Contract §5.22.)  The net amount that Payee will receive from Petitioner, in exchange for future structured settlement payments represents 64.8 percent of the estimated current value of the payments and is calculated by dividing the net payment amount by the discounted present value of the payments.  (Amended Petition ¶5, Exh. 1 at Addendum to Contract §5.25.)

Pursuant to Insurance Code §10136, at least ten days prior to the date that Payee signed the Transfer Agreement, Petitioner provided to Payee, and Payee acknowledged receipt thereof, a separate written disclosure statement, (“Disclosure Statement”), completed with the information which applies to this Transfer Agreement.  (Amended Petition ¶5, Exh. 2.)

All of this information has been properly disclosed to Payee by way of the disclosure form in compliance with Insurance Code §10136(b), as required by Insurance Code §10139.5(a)(3).  (Amended Petition ¶5, Exh. 2 at pg. 2; Decl. of Payee ¶15.)  Payee was also advised in writing to seek independent legal, tax, and financial professional advice.  (Amended Petition ¶5, Exh. 2 at pg. 2; Decl. of Payee ¶16.)  Payee chose not to seek an independent professional’s advice.  (Decl. of Payee ¶16.)  Therefore, Petitioner has complied with the statutory disclosure requirements in Insurance Code §10139.5(a)(2).

The transfer agreement also complies with Insurance Code §§10136 and 10138 as required by Insurance Code §10139.5(a)(3).  The transfer agreement does not contravene any applicable statute or order of any court or other governmental authority.  (Ins. Code §10139.5(a)(4).)

¿ ¿ 

Information About Payee

First, the Payee is 31 years old.  (Amended Petition, Exh. 3 at ¶3.)  The Payee resides in Lancaster and provides his address.  (Amended Petition, Exh. 3 at ¶2.)  Therefore, the petition meets the requirements of Insurance Code §10139.5(c)(1).

Second, the Payee’s marital status is single.  (Amended Motion, Exh. 3 at ¶4.)

Third, the Payee has no minor children or other dependents.  (Amended Petition, Exh. 3 at ¶5.) 

Fourth, the Payee is currently unemployed.  (Amended Petition, Exh. 3 at ¶6.)  Therefore, the petition meets the requirements of Insurance Code §10139.5(c)(4).

Fifth, the Payee does not have any court-ordered child support obligations.  (Amended Petition, Exh. 3 at ¶7.)  Therefore, the petition meets the requirements of Insurance Code §10139.5(c)(5).

Sixth, the Payee has not previously assigned a portion of his structured settlement payments rights.  (Amended Petition, Exh. 3 at ¶12.) 

Petitioner has provided all of the information required by Insurance Code §10139.5(c)(6).

 

Analysis of Transfer

The terms of the proposed Transfer Agreement are as follows:

You have agreed to sell to the transferee future payments totaling one hundred seventy-two thousand and 00/100 ($172,000.00), in exchange for a purchase price of one hundred one thousand nineteen and 66/100 dollars ($101,019.66).

 

Those future payments have a discounted present value equal to one hundred fifty-five thousand eight hundred eighty-five and 34/100 dollars ($155,885.34), calculated by applying the discount rate of 5.0 percent utilized by the Internal Revenue Service to value annuities in probate proceedings.

 

The purchase price to be paid to you was calculated using a discount rate of 29.56 percent.

 

The purchase price payable to you is less than the present value of the future payments stated above because the discount rate of your transaction is greater than the rate utilized by the Internal Revenue Service.

 

For comparison purposes:

If you did not sell your right to receive structured settlement payments, but instead borrowed the net amount of $101,019.66 and paid that loan back in installments with each of the payments you are now selling, the equivalent interest rate you would be paying for that loan would be 29.56% per year.

 

(Amended Petition, Exh. 2.)

Pursuant to Insurance Code §10139.5(b)(2), Payee intends to use the money he receives from Petitioner to put a down payment on a home and purchase reliable transportation.  (Amended Motion, Exh. 3 ¶11.)

The Court finds the transfer is in the best interest of the Payee.  The Court determines the transfer is fair and reasonable his intended use of the funds.  The Payee was fully informed of his rights and has knowingly waived independent professional advice regarding the transfer.  (Amended Motion, Exh. 3 ¶16.) 

Accordingly, Petitioner’s petition is granted.

 

Conclusion

Petitioner’s unopposed petition is granted.

Moving Party to give notice.

TENTATIVE RULING

 

EJEME AIRE, 

 

         vs.

 

ANTHONY EGBASE.

 Case No.:  26STCV18501

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  September 28, 2026

 

Pro per Plaintiff Ejeme Aire’s motion for a limited order to preserve potentially relevant documents and ESI pending ordinary discovery is granted. 

 

Plaintiff Ejeme Aire (“Aire”) (“Plaintiff”), in pro per, moves for a limited order requiring Defendants Anthony O. Egbase (“Egbase”) and A.O.E. Law & Associates, APC (“AOE Law”) (collectively, “Defendants”) to preserve potentially relevant documents and ESI pending ordinary discovery.  (Notice Motion, pgs. 1-2; C.C.P. §§128, 526.)

 

Background

Plaintiff filed the operative Complaint in this matter on June 11, 2026, against Defendants.

Plaintiff filed the instant motion on August 4, 2026.  Defendants filed their opposition on September 15, 2026.  Plaintiff filed her reply on September 15, 2026.

 

Legal Standard

C.C.P. §128(a) provides, in part:

(a)  Every court shall have the power to do all of the following:

(1)   To preserve and enforce order in its immediate presence.

(2)   To enforce order in the proceedings before it, or before a person or persons empowered to conduct a judicial investigation under its authority.

(3)   To provide for the orderly conduct of proceedings before it, or its officers.

(4)   To compel obedience to its judgments, orders, and process, and to the orders of a judge out of court, in an action or proceeding pending therein.

 

(C.C.P. §128(a)(1)-(4).)

 

Discussion

Plaintiff’s motion is granted.  This Court errs on the side of caution to ensure all potential discovery within Defendants’ possession, custody, and control in this matter is preserved, irrespective of any determination of the likelihood of success on the merits or issues of res judicata in related cases.  This Court does not interpret the instant motion to be one for a preliminary injunction; rather, it requests the preservation of evidence.

 

Conclusion

Plaintiff’s motion is granted.
