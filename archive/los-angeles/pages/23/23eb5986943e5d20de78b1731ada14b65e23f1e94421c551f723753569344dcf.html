Case Number: 23STCV04379    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 311

SUPERIOR COURT OF CALIFORNIA, COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES

Civil Division

Central District, Stanley Mosk Courthouse, Department 311

Tentative Ruling

23STCV04379

KRISTINA RIVAS vs NATURES IMAGE, INC., et al.

 

July 29, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PETITIONER/DEFENDANT’S PETITION TO CONFIRM CONTRACTUAL ARBITRATION AWARD.

 

RULING:

The Court continues the Petition hearing to August 28, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department 311, for Petitioner to file a Proof of Service addressed to Plaintiff’s counsel of record.

Petitioner/Defendant to give notice.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

On February 28, 2023, KRISTINA RIVAS (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against NATURES IMAGE, INC., AJ FOX, JACKIE MORALES, and SARA KOENIG (Defendants). Plaintiff alleges that Defendants wrongfully terminated her employment, after other adverse job actions, because of her age, gender, disability, race, national origin, ancestry, and complaints about sexual harassment.

On June 23, 2026, Petitioner and Defendant NATURES IMAGE, INC. filed the Petition to Confirm Contractual Arbitration Award based on the Arbitration Award.

No Opposition or Counter-Petition has been filed.

 

II.              LEGAL STANDARD

Unless a petition or response seeking to correct or vacate an arbitration award was filed properly based upon valid grounds, or the proceeding is dismissed, courts are required to enter judgment in conformity with the award. (Valsan Partners Ltd. Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.) “‘[T]he court shall confirm the award as made …, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.’” (Law Offices of David S. Karton v. Segreto (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 1, 8.)

 

III.            ANALYSIS

Petitioner seeks to confirm the Arbitration Award served on June 8, 2026, and enter Judgment in favor of Defendant. The Proposed Judgment was received on June 23, 2026, but is missing the referenced Arbitration Award attachment.

“Successful service by mail requires strict compliance with all statutory requirements, including those set forth in section 1013; the failure to comply deprives a court of jurisdiction to act.” (Lee v. Placer Title Co. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 503, 509-510 [where notice was not mailed to the “‘office address as last given by that person on any document filed in the cause,’” per Code Civ. Proc., § 1013, subd. (a), notice was ineffective, and order entered was void]. Accord, Silver v. McNamee (1999) 69 Cal.App.4th 269, 279.)

Courts and litigants have the right to rely on court records in ascertaining attorneys of record until they receive a notice of substitution of attorney. (McMillan v. Shadow Ridge at Oak Park Homeowner’s Assoc. (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 960, 965.) In case proceedings, judges should not recognize clients or attorneys who are not the attorneys of record. (McMillan v. Shadow Ridge at Oak Park Homeowner’s Assoc. (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 960, 966.) “‘The attorney of record has the exclusive right to appear in court for his client and neither the party himself nor another attorney should be recognized by the court in the conduct or disposition of the case.’” (Younessi v. Woolf (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 1137, 1146.)

Although Plaintiff’s counsel reportedly was granted permission to be relieved as attorney during arbitration (Emil W. Herich Declaration, filed June 23, 2026, ¶ 8), there is no Substitution of Attorney or Notice of Change of Address filed in this case. In fact, on June 25, 2026, the Clerk served notice upon Plaintiff’s counsel of record.

Thus, the Court has nothing filed in this case showing that Plaintiff was properly served by email as a self-represented litigant, as the penultimate page of the Petition reveals.

 

IV.           CONCLUSION

The Court continues the Petition to ensure that Plaintiff is validly served with notice of the hearing on the Petition.

 


Case Number: 26STCV10790    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 311

SUPERIOR COURT OF CALIFORNIA, COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES

Civil Division

Central District, Stanley Mosk Courthouse, Department 311

Tentative Ruling

26STCV10790

JAE YEAL KIM, AN INDIVIDUAL vs JI HYUN CHUNG, AN INDIVIDUAL, et al.

 

July 29, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: DEMURRER OF DEFENDANT JI HYUN CHUNG TO PLAINTIFF'S COMPLAINT.

 

RULING:

The Court sustains the unopposed Demurrer, with leave to amend.

Plaintiff may serve and file a First Amended Complaint (FAC) no later than on August 28, 2026.

 Defendant to give notice.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

On April 6, 2026, JAE YEAL KIM (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against JI HYUN CHUNG, YONG SUNG KIM, and ALL PERSONS UNKNOWN CLAIMING ANY LEGAL OR EQUITABLE RIGHT, TITLE, ESTATE, LIEN OR INTEREST IN THE PROPERTY DESCRIBED IN THE COMPLAINT ADVERSE TO PLAINTIFFS’ TITLE OR INTEREST OR ANY CLOUD ON PLAINTIFFS’ TITLE OR INTEREST THERETO.

Plaintiff lists these Causes of Action:

1.     QUIET TITLE;

2.     DECLARATORY RELIEF;

3.     CANCELLATION OF INSTRUMENTS AND DAMAGES;

4.     SLANDER OF TITLE; and

5.     FINANCIAL ELDER ABUSE.

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants induced Plaintiff to execute, as a gift, a Grant Deed to a condominium at 32 South Serrano Avenue #301, Los Angeles, but Defendants subsequently acted contrary to representations that no transfer of a property interest and no recording would be done.

On June 8, 2026, Defendants filed the Demurrer to the Complaint, on grounds emphasizing the fraud must be specifically pled, and addressing some other claim elements.

Plaintiff filed no Opposition to this matter.

 

II.              LEGAL STANDARD

 

A.    Demurrer Procedure

Demurrers are to be sustained where a pleading fails to plead adequately any essential element of the cause of action. (Cantu v. Resolution Trust Corp. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 857, 879-880.) A complaint does not necessarily need to be a model of pleading but needs sufficient factual allegations. (E.g., State v. The Superior Court (2001) 87 Cal.App.4th 1409, 1411.)

 

B.    Quiet Title

 

The elements of the Cause of Action are these:

  1. Description of the property;
  2. plaintiff's title or interest and the basis;
  3. defendant’s asserting adverse claim or antagonistic property interest;
  4. date as of which the determination is sought; and
  5. prayer for determination of title.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 761.020. See also Lucas v. Sweet (1956) 47 Cal.2d 20, 22 [sufficient to plead that plaintiff is the owner of described property, defendant wrongfully claims interest, unless complaint reveals a defect in title]; Leeper v. Beltrami (1959) 53 Cal.2d 195, 214 [“general rule that a cause of action to quiet title may be stated in general terms.”]; Dreher v. Rohrmoser (1955) 134 Cal.App.2d 196, 198 [complainants can specifically plead the invalidity of defendants’ legal title and courts may enter such judgment as the equities require]; Lewis v. Superior Court (1994) 30 Cal.App.4th 1850, 1866 [citing Code Civ. Proc., § 761.020 (complaint to quiet title must be verified)]; Leeper v. Beltrami (1959) 53 Cal.2d 195, 216 [in order for Plaintiff to show the required element of having title where there is a contract between the parties, there must be something legally wrong with the contract justifying relief or rescission]; Kroeker v. Hurlbert (1940) 38 Cal.App.2d 261, 267 [requirement to specifically allege quiet title based on fraud applies only where defendants have clear record title]; Tsasu LLC v. U.S. Bank Trust, N.A. (2021) 62 Cal.App.5th 704, 716 [plaintiffs must file a verified complaint for quiet title naming as defendants all persons having adverse claims to the title and those known to the plaintiffs or reasonably apparent from inspection of the property, and judgment requires joinder of all parties having an interest in the property].)

 

C.    Declaratory Relief

 

The elements of the Claim are these:

  1. Person interested under a written instrument or a contract; or
  2. a declaration of his or her rights or duties;
    1. with respect to another; or
    2. in respect to, in, over or upon property; and,
  3. an actual controversy.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1060; Ludgate Ins. Co. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 592, 605-606. See also City of Cotati v. Cashman (2002) 29 Cal.4th 69, 80 [“‘an actual, present controversy must be pleaded specifically" and "the facts of the respective claims concerning the [underlying] subject must be given.’”]; Cordoba Corp. v. City of Indus. (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 145, 157 [“There is no basis for declaratory relief where only past wrongs are involved…. [¶] No declaration from the trial court could help the parties prevent a future breach.”]; Osseous Technologies of Amer., Inc. v. DiscoveryOrtho Partners LLC (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 357, 372 [“‘in a dispute involving an alleged breach of contract, courts may provide declaratory relief under section 1060 if the relief sought would also govern the future conduct of the parties.…’”]; Cal. Ins. Guar. Ass'n v. Superior Court (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 1617, 1624 [“availability of another form of relief that is adequate will usually justify refusal to grant declaratory relief” but “[t]he refusal to exercise the power is within the court's legal discretion….”]; Hood v. Superior Court (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 319, 324 [declaratory relief unavailable where duplicates other cause of action].)

 

D.    Cancellation Of Instruments and Damages

 

The elements of the Cause of Action are the following:

  1. Grantor's signature was forged, or grantor was unaware of the nature of what is signed (void deed); or
  2. grantor was aware of what is executed, but was induced through fraudulent misrepresentations to deliver the deed (voidable deed).

 

(Schiavon v. Arnaudo Brothers (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 374, 378; Fallon v. Triangle Management Services (1985) 169 Cal App 3d 1103, 1106; Smith v. Williams (1961) 55 Cal.2d 617, 620; Civ. Code, § 3412. See also La Jolla Group II v. Bruce (2012) 211 Cal.App.4th 461, 477 [“‘A deed is void if the grantor's signature is forged or if the grantor is unaware of the nature of what he or she is signing. [Citation.] A voidable deed, on the other hand, is one where the grantor is aware of what he or she is executing, but has been induced to do so through fraudulent misrepresentations.’”].)

 

“ ‘Ordinarily a suit to set aside and cancel a void instrument is governed by section 343 of the Code of Civil Procedure.’… The only exception to this rule … would be as and when fraud or mistake were involved, in which case the three-year period of section 338(4) would apply.” (Robertson v. Superior Court (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 1319, 1326.)

 

E.    Slander of Title

 

The elements of this Cause of Action are these:

  1. Publication;
  2. without privilege or justification;
  3. which is false; and
  4. causes direct and immediate pecuniary loss.

 

(M.F. Farming, Co. v. Couch Distributing Co. (2012) 207 Cal.App.4th 180, 198-199 [“‘If the matter is reasonably understood to cast doubt upon the existence or extent of another's interest in land, it is disparaging to the latter's title where it is so understood by the recipient….’”], disapproved on other grounds by Baral v. Schnitt (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376, 392; Manhattan Loft, LLC v. Mercury Liquors, Inc. (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 1040, 1051. But see Glass v. Gulf Oil Corp. (1970) 12 Cal.App.3d 412, 419 [elements are (1) false and unprivileged disparagement, (2) of title to property and (3) resulting in actual pecuniary damage].)

 

F.     Financial Elder Abuse

 

A claim for financial elder abuse includes taking, secreting, appropriating, obtaining, or retaining real or personal property of an elder for a wrongful use, or with intent to defraud, or assisting with those. (Knox v. Dean (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 417, 423, 431. See also Munoz v. Patel (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 761, 782–783 [allegations that defendant obtained property of an elder for a wrongful purpose or with fraudulent intent sufficiently stated a claim for financial elder abuse.]; Wood v. Santa Monica Escrow Co. (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 1186, 1189 [“Section 15610.30, subdivision (a), provides that financial abuse of an elder or dependent adult occurs where a person takes or assists in taking real or personal property of such an adult to a wrongful use or with an intent to defraud, or both.”]; Das v. Bank of America, N.A. (2010) 186 Cal.App.4th 727, 735 [addressing statutory provisions re financial abuse of an elder]; Stebley v. Litton Loan Servicing, LLP (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 522, 527-528 [“bad faith or intent to defraud is no longer required in elder or dependent adult abuse cases…. But they still must allege at least a ‘wrongful use’ of property.”].)

 

III.            ANALYSIS

Defendants opine that all Plaintiff’s claims are based on fraud but fail to plead with specificity, as to how, when, where, to whom, and by what means. Defendants also state that Quiet Title must be verified and plead present title; Declaratory Relief is duplicative and backward-looking; Cancellation is time-barred; Slander of Title claim attacks a privileged recordation; and Elder Abuse alleges no facts of a wrongful taking.

A judge in a civil case is not “‘obligated to seek out theories [a party] might have advanced, or to articulate … that which … [a party] has left unspoken.’” (Mesecher v. County of San Diego (1992) 9 Cal.App.4th 1677, 1686.)

Rarely should judges sustain demurrers to initial complaints without giving leave to amend. (Tarrar Enterprises, Inc. v. Associated Indemnity Corp. (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 685, 689.) “If the plaintiff has not had an opportunity to amend the complaint in response to the demurrer, leave to amend is liberally allowed as a matter of fairness, unless the complaint shows on its face that it is incapable of amendment.” (City of Stockton v. Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 730, 747.) Courts generally allow at least one time to amend a complaint, after sustaining a demurrer, even without any request for leave to amend. (McDonald v. Superior Court (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 297, 303.)

Here, Plaintiff filed no Opposition for the Court’s consideration. However, applicable policy is to allow at least one opportunity to amend a complaint after a demurrer.

 

IV.           CONCLUSION

Therefore, the Court sustains the unopposed Demurrer, with leave to amend.
