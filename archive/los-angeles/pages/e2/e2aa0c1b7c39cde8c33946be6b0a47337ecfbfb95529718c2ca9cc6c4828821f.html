Motion to Compel Further Deposition Answers

The court considered the moving and opposition papers filed in connection with this motion.  

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Tamara Jones (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order compelling defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s (“Defendant”) retained expert to provide further answers to questions at his deposition, and for sanctions.

The court denies Plaintiff’s motion because the deadline to have expert discovery motions heard was May 5, 2026, pursuant to the court’s order resetting trial for May 15, 2026. (Minute Order entered January 8, 2026; Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.030.) The court’s subsequent order continuing trial did not extend the discovery motion cutoff. (Minute Order entered April 9, 2026.) Plaintiff’s motion is beyond the discovery motion hearing cutoff. Moreover, Plaintiff’s motion does not include a separate statement as required by Rule of Court 3.1345 subdivision (a)(4).

          For these reasons, the court denies Plaintiff’s motion to compel further deposition answers.

 

ORDER

The court DENIES plaintiff Tamara Jones’s motion to compel further deposition answers.

The court orders plaintiff Tamara Jones to give notice of this ruling.

MOtion for Provisional Certification of Settlement Class and Preliminary Approval of Class Action Settlement

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Marcela Martinez De Baeza

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response

Motion for Provisional Certification of Settlement Class and Preliminary Approval of Class Action Settlement

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Marcela Martinez De Baeza (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order provisionally certifying a Settlement Class and preliminarily approving a class and PAGA action settlement.

The parties have reached a settlement of $135,000.00.  (Boyamian Decl., Ex. 1 [Settlement Agreement] ¶ 1.22 [defining gross settlement amount].)

Labor Code section 2699, subdivision (s)(2) provides that “[t]he superior court shall review and approve any settlement of any civil action pursuant to” PAGA.¿ The court’s review of PAGA settlements “ensur[es] that any negotiated resolution is fair to those affected.”¿ (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 549.)¿“[A] presumption of fairness exists where: (1) the settlement is reached through arm’s-length bargaining; (2) investigation and discovery are sufficient to allow counsel and the court to act intelligently; (3) counsel is experienced in similar litigation; and (4) the percentage of objectors is small.”¿ (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 244-245 [disapproved on other grounds].)¿ “[T]he test is not the maximum amount plaintiffs might have obtained at trial on the complaint, but rather whether the settlement is reasonable under all of the circumstances.”¿ (Id. at p. 250.)¿¿¿¿¿

            The court must also review and approve settlements of class litigation. (Cal Rules of Court, Rule 3.769, subd. (a).) The court has a “fiduciary responsibility” as guardian of absent class members’ rights to ensure the settlement is fair.  (Luckey v. Superior Court (2014) 228 Cal.App.4th 81, 95.) Under California law, a presumption of fairness attaches to a settlement where: “(l) the settlement is reached through arm’s-length bargaining; (2) investigation and discovery are sufficient to allow counsel and the court to act intelligently; (3) counsel is experienced in similar litigation; and (4) the percentage of objections is small.” (Dunk v. Ford Motor Co. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 1794, 1802.)

In California, there are two certification prerequisites: (1) the existence of an “ascertainable class;” and (2) “a well-defined community of interest in the questions of law and fact involved affecting the parties to be represented.”  (Sotelo v. MediaNews Group, Inc. (2012) 207 Cal.App.4th 639, 647.)  To certify a class, the party advocating class treatment must demonstrate: (1) a numerous class; (2) predominant common questions of law or fact; (3) class representatives with claims or defenses typical of the class; and (4) class representatives who can adequately represent the class.”  (Brinker Restaurant Corp. v. Superior Court (2012) 53 Cal.4th 1004, 1021.)

          The court finds that all requirements for class treatment have been met and that equity warrants class treatment. Plaintiff has demonstrated a numerous class with predominant common questions of law or fact relating to these wage and hour claims such that joinder of all members would be impracticable. (Boyamian Decl. ¶ 56.) As one of the aggrieved employees, Plaintiff’s claims are typical of the class, and Plaintiff has demonstrated that she can adequately represent the class. (De Baeza Decl. ¶¶ 7-14.)

The court has reviewed (1) the terms of the Settlement Agreement, including (i) the gross settlement amount of $135,000, the $10,000 enhancement payment to Plaintiff, attorney’s fees not to exceed 35 percent of the gross settlement amount ($47,250.00) and litigation expenses (not to exceed $15,000), the administrator expense payment ($3.990), (ii) the $10,000 PAGA payment, of which 75 percent, or $7,500, will be paid to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency, and of which the remaining 25 percent, or $2,500, will be allocated to the aggrieved employees, (iii), the net settlement amount of $51,260.00 to be distributed to the proposed class members, (iv) the settlement funding procedures, and (v) the release of the claims by Plaintiffs and the aggrieved employees, the latter of which are deemed to release all claims that were or reasonably could have been alleged based on the facts alleged in the Complaint and the PAGA notice; (2) the declarations of Plaintiff, in which Plaintiff attests to her involvement in this action; and (3) the declaration of Plaintiffs’ counsel, Michael Boyamian, in which Boyamian asserts that (i) Plaintiff’s counsel investigated the claims against Defendants, (ii) the parties attended an all-day mediation, at which the parties reached an agreement in principle to settle this action, (iii) the settlement was reached through arms-length bargaining, and (iv) counsel is experienced in employment law and wage and hour claims.  (Boyamian Decl., Ex. 1 [Settlement Agreement], ¶¶ 1.22, 3.2,-5.4; De Baeza Decl. ¶¶ 7-14; Boyamian Decl., ¶¶ 5-20.)

The court has also considered counsel’s declaration to determine the reasonableness of the request for attorney’s fees in the amount of $47,250.00, which is the maximum amount permitted by the Settlement Agreement.  For the reasons set forth below, the court finds that an award of reasonable attorney’s fees is $47,250.00 and will therefore approve an award in that amount. 

“[W]hen class action litigation establishes a monetary fund for the benefit of the class members, and the trial court in its equitable powers awards class counsel a fee out of that fund, the court may determine the amount of a reasonable fee by choosing an appropriate percentage of the fund created.”  (Laffitte v. Robert Half Internat. Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.5th 480, 503.) “A lodestar cross-check . . . provides a mechanism for bringing an objective measure of the work performed into the calculation of a reasonable attorney fee.  If a comparison between the percentage and lodestar calculations produces an imputed multiplier far outside the normal range, indicating that the percentage fee will reward counsel for their services at an extraordinary rate even accounting for the factors customarily used to enhance a lodestar fee, the trial court will have reason to examine its choice of a percentage.”  (Id. at p. 504.)

The requested amount of attorney’s fees is 35 percent of the gross settlement amount.  The court (1) has exercised its discretion to conduct a lodestar cross-check on the percentage fee, and (2) finds that Plaintiff has established a lodestar of $50,150 (59 hours x Attorney Boyamian’s $850 hourly rate). (Laffitte, supra, 1 Cal.5th at p. 506 [trial courts have discretion to conduct lodestar cross-check] Boyamian Decl. ¶ 59.)  As the fee award requested in the settlement is less than the lodestar calculation to which Attorney Boyamian testifies, the court finds that the requested attorney’s fees are reasonable.

The court finds that Plaintiff has supported the request for $11,417.04 in litigation costs.  (Boyamian Decl., Ex. 3.)  The remaining $3,582.96 will be allocated to the net settlement.  (Boyamian Decl., Ex. 1, Settlement Agreement, ¶ 3.2.2.) 

Based on the arguments and evidence set forth in Plaintiff’s motion and supporting declarations, the court finds that the Settlement Agreement is fair, adequate, and reasonable. 

          The court has also reviewed the Settlement’s procedures for giving notice to class members and finds that it conforms to the requirements of the Rules of Court. (Boyamian Decl. Ex. 1 ¶¶ 7.1-7.8; Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.769, subd. (f).)

The court therefore grants in part Plaintiff’s motion, as modified to reduce the award of litigation expenses to the requested amount of $11,417.04.  The court has calculated the new net settlement amount to be $54,842.96 ($135,000 gross settlement -- $47,250.00 in attorney’s fees -- $11,417.04 in litigation expenses -- $10,000 total enhancement payment to Plaintiff -- $3,990 in administration expenses to Apex -- $10,000 in PAGA payments). 

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS plaintiff Marcela Martinez De Baeza’s motion for provisional class certification and preliminary approval of class and PAGA settlement subject to the following:

The court orders that plaintiff Marcela Martinez De Baeza shall be awarded reduced litigation expenses in the amount of $11,417.04, with the remaining $3,582.96 reallocated to the net settlement amount.

 

 

 

 

The court orders plaintiff Marcela Martinez De Baeza to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

DISCUSSION

Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories - Employment

Plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez moves the court for an order (1) compelling defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (“Defendant”) to serve responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories -Employment, Set 1, and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion because (1) Plaintiff has shown that Defendant did not serve timely responses to their Form Interrogatories, and (2) Defendant did not submit opposition papers or other evidence with the court establishing that responses have since been served on Plaintiff.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b); Kasis Decl., Ex. 1 [proof of service of Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories on counsel for Defendant] and ¶¶ 4-11.)

          The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)  “The court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed . . . or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.”  (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 3.1348(a).) The court finds that $750 (1.5 hours x $500 hourly rate)is a reasonable amount of sanctions to impose against Defendant in connection with this motion.

Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production

Plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez moves the court for an order (1) compelling defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (“Defendant”) to serve responses to Plaintiff’s Requests for Production, Set 1, and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion because (1) Plaintiff has shown that Defendant did not serve timely responses to their Requests for Production, and (2) Defendant did not submit opposition papers or other evidence with the court establishing that responses have since been served on Plaintiff.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b); Kasis Decl., Ex. 1 [proof of service of Requests for Production on counsel for Defendant] and ¶¶ 4-11.)

          The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)  “The court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed . . . or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.”  (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 3.1348(a).) The court finds that $750 (1.5 hours x $500 hourly rate) is a reasonable amount of sanctions to impose against Defendant in connection with this motion.

The court GRANTS plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez’s motion to compel responses to Form Interrogatories - Employment, set 1.

          Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, the court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority to serve on plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez full and complete verified answers, without objections and which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.220-2030.250, to plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez’s Form Interrogatories - Employment, Set 1, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.

          The court GRANTS plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez’s motion to compel responses to Requests for Production, set 1.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300, the court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (1) to serve on plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez written responses, without objections and which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2031.210-2031.250, to plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez’s Requests for Production of Documents, Set 1 and (2) to produce to plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez all documents and things in defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority’s possession, custody, or control which are responsive to those requests, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.

          The court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez in the amount of $1,500.00 within 30 days of the date of service this order.

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant Contractors State License Board

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   No response

Motion to Allow Interpleader of Funds and Discharge of Liability

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.

DISCUSSION

Defendant Contractors State License Board (“Defendant” or “CSLB”) moves the court for an order granting interpleader of funds in the amount of $50,000.00, discharging Defendant of liability, and dismissing Defendant from the action.

“Any person, firm, corporation, association or other entity against whom double or multiple claims are made, or may be made, by two or more persons which are such that they may give rise to double or multiple liability, may bring an action against the claimants to compel them to interplead and litigate their several claims.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 386(b).) Once a party admits liability and deposits the money with the court, that party is discharged from liability and freed from the obligation of participating in the litigation between the claimants. (City of Morgan Hill v. Brown (1999) 71 Cal.App.4th 1114, 1122.)

The court finds that interpleader of the disputed funds and dismissal of the moving Defendant is proper. Defendant’s sole involvement in the underlying dispute is through plaintiff Arthur Bender (“Plaintiff”)’s fourth cause of action for recovery of a disciplinary cash deposit. (Complaint ¶¶ 72-73.) This cause of action alleges that the contractor defendants in this action were ordered by Defendant CSLB to obtain a disciplinary bond in connection with a license probation, and, in lieu of obtaining that bond, the contractor defendants deposited $50,000.00 with Defendant CSLB. (Complaint ¶ 72.) In the event Plaintiff prevails on the underlying claims, Plaintiff seeks to claim against the deposit. (Complaint ¶ 73.) Defendant CSLB disclaims any interest in the action beyond holding the cash deposit. (Edwards Decl. ¶¶ 5-9.) As this showing is sufficient to demonstrate the application of the interpleader statute, the court grants Defendant CSLB’s motion.

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendant Contractor State License Board’s motion to interplead funds.

The court orders that defendant Contractor State License Board deposit with the Clerk of the Court defendant LA Solar Group, Inc.’s cash deposit in the amount of $50,000.00.

The court orders that defendant Contractor State License Board is discharged from further liability to any party to this action relating to the deposited funds, contingent on defendant Contractor State License Board filing proof of deposit.

The court orders that, upon defendant Contractor State License Board’s filing proof of deposit of the disputed funds, defendant Contractor State License Board is dismissed from this action with prejudice.

The court orders defendant Contractor State License Board to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

RESPONDING PARTY:   No response.

(1) Motion to be Relieved as Counsel for Plaintiff Marlen Solano

(2) Motion to be Relieved as Counsel for Plaintiff Ulises Rangel

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with these motions. No opposition was filed.

DISCUSSION

Dolan Law Firm, P.C. (“Plaintiffs’ Counsel”) moves to be relieved as counsel for plaintiffs Marlen Solano and Ulises Rangel (“Plaintiffs”) in this action.  

“The question of granting or denying an application of an attorney to withdraw as counsel (Code Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. 2) is one which lies within the sound discretion of the trial court¿‘having in mind whether such withdrawal might work an injustice in the handling of the case.’”¿ (People v. Prince¿(1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 406 [internal quotations omitted].)¿ The court should also consider whether the attorney’s “withdrawal can be accomplished without undue prejudice to the client’s interests.”¿ (Ramirez v. Sturdevant¿(1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

For a motion to be relieved as counsel under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2), California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) the proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-053)).¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

The court finds that Plaintiffs’ Counsel (1) has served Plaintiffs with the moving papers filed in connection with these motions (i) at Plaintiffs’ last known addresses, which Plaintiffs Counsel has confirmed by email is current, and (ii) by electronic service, directed to the emails used to communicate during this action, and (2) has served the defendants with the moving papers filed in connection with these motions by mail. (MC-052, ¶¶ 3, subds. (a)(2), (b)(2)(b), (b)(2)(d), 7; July 17,  2026 Proof of Service.)  The court also finds that Plaintiffs’ Counsel has shown sufficient reasons why the motions¿should be granted, and why¿Plaintiffs’ Counsel has brought the motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) instead of filing a consent under section 284, subdivision (1).¿ (MC-052, ¶ 2.) 

The court therefore grants Plaintiffs’ Counsel’s motions to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiffs.  

Dolan Law Firm, P.C. will be relieved as counsel of record for plaintiffs Marlen Solano and Ulises Rangel effective upon the filing of the proof of service of the signed “Order¿Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil” on the clients. 

The court orders Dolan Law Firm, P.C. (1) to give notice of this ruling and the signed “Order¿Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil” to plaintiffs Marlen Solano and Ulises Rangel, and (2) to file a proof of service of the notice of this ruling and the signed “Order¿Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil” on plaintiffs Marlen Solano and Ulises Rangel no later than August 27, 2026.  

IT IS SO ORDERED.
