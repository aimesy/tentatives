DEPARTMENT D LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25NWCV02811    Hearing Date: August 31, 2026    Dept: D

KAUSHIKKUMAR P. PATEL, ET AL. V. TOYOTA MOTOR SALES, U.S.A, INC.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV02811

HEARING:  08/31/26 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#12

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Toyota’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff Kaushikkumar P. Patel is GRANTED.  Plaintiff Patel is ordered to appear for deposition within 30 days.  Plaintiff Patel and his counsel of record, Quill & Arrow, LLP, are ordered to pay $455.00 within 20 days. 

 

Defendant Toyota’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff Shiva Corporation is GRANTED.  Plaintiff Shiva is ordered to appear for deposition within 30 days.  Plaintiff Patel and his counsel of record, Quill & Arrow, LLP, are ordered to pay $455.00 within 20 days. 

 

Moving party(s) to give notice.

 

Defendant Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc. moves to compel the depositions of Plaintiffs Kaushikkumar P. Patel (“Patel”) and Shiva Corporation (“Shiva”) pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2025.450 subdivision (a) and 2025.480 subdivision (a) based on two separate motions. 

 

Background

 

Song Beverly Warranty Act action.  Plaintiffs filed a complaint on August 5, 2025 against Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc. (“Toyota”) alleging (1) violation of Song Beverly Act – Breach of Express Warranty; (2) violation of Song Beverly Act – Breach of Implied Warranty; (3) violation of the Song Beverly Act Section 1793.2. 

 

Plaintiffs purchased a 2022 Lexus on June 10, 2022 subject to a warranty issued by Defendant Toyota.  Plaintiffs presented the vehicle for repairs in February 2025, March 2025, April 2025 and May 2025 for an abnormal noise in the engine and exhaust area.  Plaintiffs revoked acceptance of the sales contract.

 

On June 8, 2026, Defendant Toyota filed a Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff Patel and a Motion to Compel Deposition of Shiva.  No oppositions or replies have been filed as of August 26, 2026. 

 

Legal Standard

 

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.230, without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent's attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”  (Code of Civ. Proc.,  §2025.450, subd. (a).)

 

“A motion under subdivision (a) shall comply with both of the following: (1) The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice. (2) The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040, or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code of Civ. Proc., §2025.450, subd. (b).)   

 

Meet and Confer

 

          Plaintiff Patel

 

          Defendant Toyota served Plaintiff Patel with a deposition notice on November 18, 2025 for deposition on December 5, 2025.  (Memo of Points and Authorities ISO of Motion to Compel Patel Depo., Miner Dec., ¶2.)  Plaintiff Patel served objections to the deposition on November 28, 2025 and indicated that he would not attend the deposition because it was unilaterally set by Defendant.  (Id. at ¶3.)  Defendant thereafter made multiple requests for available deposition dates.  (Id. at ¶¶4-6.)  Plaintiff did not respond to any of these requests.  (Id.) 

 

          Defendant served Plaintiff Patel with a First Amended Deposition Notice on May 4, 2026 for deposition on May 21, 2026.  (Id. at ¶7.)  Plaintiff again objected on grounds that the deposition was unilaterally set and refused to attend.  (Id. at ¶8.)  Defendant requested available dates for deposition and Plaintiff again did not respond.  (Id. at ¶9.) 

 

          Defendant satisfies the meet and confer requirement of Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450 subdivision (b)(2) as to Plaintiff Patel.  Defendant contacted Plaintiff regarding his non-appearance and requested available dates.

 

          Defendant also satisfies the good cause requirement under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450 subdivision (b)(1) as to the 46 Requests for Production set forth in the Notice of Deposition.  Defendant explains that the documents are directly relevant to Plaintiffs’ claims and are necessary to evaluate Plaintiffs’ allegations and damages.  (Id. at 2:7-13.) 

 

          Plaintiff Shiva

 

          Defendant served Plaintiff Shiva with a deposition notice on May 4, 2025 for deposition on May 21, 2026.  (Memo of Points and Authorities ISO Motion to Compel Shiva Depo., Miner Dec., ¶6.)  Plaintiff Shiva objected to the deposition on May 14, 2026 on grounds that the deposition date was set unilaterally.  (Id. at ¶7.)  Defendant responded with a request for available dates on May 29, 2026.  (Id.)  Plaintiff Shiva failed to respond. 

 

          Defendant satisfies the meet and confer requirement of Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450 subdivision (b)(2) as to Plaintiff Shiva.  Defendant contacted Plaintiff regarding his non-appearance and requested available dates.

 

          Defendant also satisfies the good cause requirement under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450 subdivision (b)(1) as to the 46 Requests for Production set forth in the Notice of Deposition.  Defendant explains that the documents are directly relevant to Plaintiffs’ claims and are necessary to evaluate Plaintiffs’ allegations and damages.  (Id. at 2:7-13.) 

 

Procedural Issues

 

          Defendant cites to both sections 2025.450 and 2025.480.  The applicable section is section 2025.450, because no deposition took place, no objections were made to the form of the deposition notices pursuant to section 2025.410 and Defendant is not moving to compel responses to particular questions that Plaintiffs refused to answer at deposition. 

 

Analysis

 

          Plaintiffs Patel and Shiva failed to appear at a properly noticed deposition.  Plaintiffs have also refused to provide available dates for deposition despite Defendant’s request for such dates and Plaintiffs’ objection that the original depositions were unilaterally set.  Defendant’s Motions to Compel Depositions of Plaintiffs Patel and Shiva are granted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450 subdivision (a).  Plaintiffs are ordered to appear for deposition within 30 days of this order.

 

          Defendant is also entitled to monetary sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450 subdivision (g)(1), “[i]f a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7…in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” 

 

          Defendant requests sanctions against Plaintiff Patel and his counsel in the amount of $1,245.00 based on 2 hours motion preparation and 1 hour opposition review, reply preparation and hearing attendance at $395.00/hr and a $60.00 filing fee.  Defendant also requests $1,245.00 in sanctions for the motion to compel deposition of Shiva based on the same time expenditures set forth in the Patel Motion to Compel.  The Court finds the moving papers are largely duplicative. For example, the Memorandum filed as to the Patel motion is virtually identical to the Memorandum filed as to the Shiva motion. The Court finds similar duplicative work in the remaining moving papers. Additionally, no opposition was filed. The Court reduces the number of hours to those reasonably attributable to the moving papers reviewed and compared by the Court.

 

As to each motion, the Court awards $395.00 in sanctions based on 1.0 hour of motion preparation and hearing time and a $60 filing fee. 

 

Plaintiff Patel and his counsel of record, Quill & Arrow, LLP, are ordered to pay $455.00 within 20 days. 

 

Plaintiff Shiva and its counsel of record, Quill & Arrow, LLP, are ordered to pay $455.00 within 20 days. 

 

Conclusion

 

          Defendant Toyota’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff Kaushikkumar P. Patel is GRANTED.  Plaintiff Patel is ordered to appear for deposition within 30 days.  Plaintiff Patel and his counsel of record, Quill & Arrow, LLP, are ordered to pay $455.00 within 20 days. 

 

          Defendant Toyota’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff Shiva Corporation is GRANTED.  Plaintiff Shiva is ordered to appear for deposition within 30 days.  Plaintiff Patel and his counsel of record, Quill & Arrow, LLP, are ordered to pay $455.00 within 20 days.
