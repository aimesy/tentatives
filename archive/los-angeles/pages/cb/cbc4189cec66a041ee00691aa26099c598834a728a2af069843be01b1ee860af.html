Motion to Quash Service of Summons

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Specially appearing defendant Luis Mendez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   No response.

Motion to Quash Service of Summons

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.

DISCUSSION

Specially appearing defendant Luis Mendez (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order quashing service of the summons and complaint in this action, filed by plaintiff Mases Nazarian (“Plaintiff”).

“A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or¿more of the following purposes: [¶] (1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §¿418.10, subd. (a)(1).)¿ “[A] motion to quash under section 418.10, subdivision (a)(1) is a limited procedural tool to contest personal jurisdiction over the defendant where the statutory requirements for service of process are not fulfilled.”¿ (Stancil v. Superior Court¿(2021) 11 Cal.5th 381, 390.)¿¿¿

Plaintiff filed, on February 17, 2024, proof of service of the summons, complaint, and case management documents on Defendant.  The proof of service (1) states that Defendant was served by substituted service on January 14, 2026, at 4:41 p.m., at his putative home address 1360 Foxdale Loop, Apt. 609, San Jose, California, by leaving the documents in the presence of Luis Mendoza, and (2) was signed by a registered California process server on January 15, 2026. (POS-010, ¶¶ 3-5, 7.)

Because the proof of service was completed by a registered process server, there is a presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence of the facts stated in the proof of service.  (Evid. Code, § 647; American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)  The court finds that Defendant’s declaration is sufficient to rebut the presumption of the facts stated in the proof of service. Defendant categorically denies that the Foxdale Loop address is or has ever been his home address, usual mailing address, or usual place of residence or business. (Mendez Decl. ¶¶ 2-3.) Defendant also categorically denies ever having seen the summons and complaint. (Mendez Decl. ¶ 5.)

For the reasons stated above, the court grants Defendant’s motion.

ORDER

The court GRANTS specially appearing defendant Luis Mendez’s motion to quash service of summons.

The court orders that service of summons on specially appearing defendant Luis Mendez is quashed. 

 

The court orders specially appearing defendant Luis Mendez to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[Tentative] Order RE:

 

 

Demurrer to Complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendants Ford Motor Company and Envision Ford of Oxnard

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  Plaintiffs Richard Odonnell and Gary Cooksey

Demurrer to Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendants Ford Motor Company and Envision Ford of Oxnard (“Defendants”) demur to the Complaint filed by plaintiffs Richard Odonnell and Gary Cooksey (“Plaintiffs”) in its entirety.

The court sustains Defendants’ demurrer to the first cause of action for violation of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (d) because Defendants have shown that it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have not alleged facts establishing that the vehicle that is the subject of this action is a new motor vehicle within the meaning of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) 

Plaintiffs allege that they have been damaged by Defendant’s failure to comply with its obligations under Civil Code section 1793.2 on the ground that Defendant failed to promptly replace the subject vehicle or to make restitution (Compl. ¶ 30).  Under section 1793.2, subdivision (d)(2), the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act provides that a manufacturer shall promptly replace a new motor vehicle, or make restitution to the buyer, if the manufacturer is unable to service or repair a new motor vehicle to conform to the applicable express warranties.  (Civ. Code, § 1793.2, subd. (d)(2).)  As noted by Defendants, the California Supreme Court recently held that “a motor vehicle purchased with an unexpired manufacturer’s new car warranty does not qualify as a ‘motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer’s new car warranty’ under section 1793.22, subdivision (e)(2)’s definition of ‘new motor vehicle’ unless the new car warranty was issued with the sale.”  (Rodriguez v. FCA US LLC (2024) 17 Cal.5th 189, 196.)  Here, Plaintiffs have alleged that they “entered into a warranty contract with Defendant FORD regarding a 2020 Ford Mustang . . . , which was manufactured and/or distributed by Defendant FORD” (Compl. ¶ 7). 

Thus, because Plaintiffs have alleged facts establishing that the car was used (i.e., by alleging that it was a model-2020 vehicle purchased in 2023 (Compl. ¶ 7), Plaintiffs must allege that Ford’s new car warranty accompanied the sale.1  (Rodriguez, supra, 17 Cal.5th at pp. 196, 199-200 [“the phrase ‘other motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer’s new car warranty’ is most naturally understood to mean other vehicles for which such a warranty is issued with the sale’”].)  While Plaintiffs have alleged that they “entered into a warranty contract with Defendant FORD” regarding the subject vehicle (Comp. ¶ 7), which included bumper-to-bumper, powertrain, and emission warranties (Compl. ¶ 8), Plaintiffs did not allege the warranty contract to be Ford’s new car warranty that was issued with the sale. (Ibid.)  

The court sustains Defendants’ demurrer to the second cause of action for violation of subdivision (b) of Civil Code section 1793.2 because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have not alleged, for the reasons set forth above, facts establishing that the subject vehicle is a new motor vehicle within the meaning of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Civ. Code, §§ 1793.2, subd. (b), 1791, subd. (a) [defining consumer good to mean any new product that is used, bought, or leased primarily for personal, family, or household purposes].) 

The court sustains Defendants’ demurrer to the third cause of action for violation of subdivision (a)(3) of Civil Code section 1793.2 because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have not alleged, for the reasons set forth above, facts establishing that the subject vehicle is a new motor vehicle within the meaning of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Civ. Code, §§ 1793.2, subd. (a)(3), 1791, subd. (a).)  

The court sustains Defendants’ demurrer to the fourth cause of action for breach of the implied warranty of merchantability because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have not alleged, for the reasons set forth above, facts establishing that the subject vehicle is either a new motor vehicle within the meaning of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act, or that Ford issued a new warranty at the point of purchase. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Civ. Code, §§ 1791.1, 1794, 1795.5.)

The court overrules Envision Ford’s demurrer to the fifth cause of action for negligent repair because it states facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since (1) the court finds that it is not barred by the economic loss rule because it is based on Envision Ford’s breach of its duty to use ordinary care and skill in the storage, preparation, and repair of the subject vehicle in accordance with industry standards (Compl., ¶ 49), and therefore does not arise from any underlying contract between Plaintiffs and Envision Ford, and (2) Plaintiffs have sufficiently alleged that Envision Ford’s breach of its duties proximately caused Plaintiffs’ damages (Compl., ¶ 50).  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905, 923; Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 527 [“Ordinarily, negligence may be alleged in general terms . . . .”].) 

The court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the sixth cause of action for fraudulent inducement – concealment because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have not alleged that Defendants had a duty to disclose the facts regarding the subject defects in the vehicle, including because Plaintiffs have not alleged facts establishing that they purchased the vehicle directly from Defendants or its agent and that, during the course of those direct dealings, Defendants concealed facts regarding the defects from Plaintiffs.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 40-41 [circumstances imposing a duty to disclose “presuppose a preexisting relationship between the parties” which “must necessarily arise from direct dealings between the plaintiff and the defendant; it cannot arise between the defendant and the public at large”]; Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 844, review dism. Dec. 18, 2024 [finding complaint sufficient and declining to hold claim barred on the ground that there was no relationship requiring Nissan to disclose known defects where plaintiffs alleged they bought the car from a Nissan dealership, that Nissan backed the car with its warranty, and Nissan’s authorized dealerships are its agents for purposes of the sale of Nissan vehicles to consumers].) 

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  The court finds that Plaintiffs have not articulated the facts that they could allege to render their causes of action against Ford sufficient and therefore sustains the demurrer without leave to amend. 

 

ORDER

The court SUSTAINS defendants Ford Motor Company and Envision Ford of Oxnard’s demurrer to plaintiffs Richard Odonnell and Gary Cooksey’s first through fourth and sixth causes of action without leave to amend.

The court OVERRULES defendants Ford Motor Company and Envision Ford of Oxnard’s demurrer to plaintiffs Richard Odonnell and Gary Cooksey’s fifth cause of action.

The court orders defendants Ford Motor Company and Envision Ford of Oxnard to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion to Compel Deposition and Production of Documents

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Antreas Hindoyan, M.D.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Defendant University of Southern California.

Motion to Compel Deposition and Production of Documents

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Antreas Hindoyan (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order compelling Defendant University of Southern California (“Defendant”) to produce eight witnesses for deposition and to produce documents.

The court denies Plaintiff’s motion for failure to submit a proper meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).)

A reasonable and good faith attempt at informal resolution “requires that counsel attempt to talk the matter over, compare their views, consult, and deliberate.” (In re Marriage of Moore (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 1275, 1293 [internal quotation marks and citation omitted].)  “[T]he parties must present to each other the merits of their respective positions with the same candor, specificity and support during informal negotiations as during the briefing of discovery motions. Only after all the cards have been laid on the table, and a party has meaningfully assessed the relative strengths and weakness of its position in light of all available information, can there be a sincere effort to resolve the matter.”  (Id. at pp. 1293-1294 [internal quotation marks and citation omitted].)

The declaration submitted in support of Plaintiff’s motion reveals that Plaintiff’s counsel did not engage in a reasonable and good-faith attempt at informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion. In response to Defendant’s objection to the eight deposition notices at issue here, Plaintiff’s counsel demanded by email that Defendant provide new dates for the depositions to take place. (Zilifyan Decl. Ex. 3 [PDF page 105].) Defendant’s counsel invited Plaintiff’s counsel to schedule a telephone conversation to discuss the matter informally. (Zilifyan Decl. Ex. 3 [PDF pages 102-104].) Nothing in the record indicates that this telephone conversation occurred, and the next email from Plaintiff’s counsel was a unilateral demand for alternative dates and threatening to file a motion. (Zilifyan Decl. Ex. 3 [PDF page 101].) Based on the court’s review of the Zilifyan declaration filed by Plaintiff, the court finds that Plaintiff has not shown that they satisfied the meet and confer requirement before filing this motion.

 

ORDER

The court DENIES plaintiff Antreas Hindoyan’s motion to compel deposition and production of documents.

The court orders defendant University of Southern California to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
