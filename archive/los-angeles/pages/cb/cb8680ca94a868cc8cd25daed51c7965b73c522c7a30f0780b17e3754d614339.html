Case Number: 23STLC07422    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Destiny C. Erazo-Lopez’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On November 21, 2023, Plaintiff Progressive West Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Destiny C. Erazo-Lopez (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered default on April 28, 2025 and default judgment on May 9, 2025.

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Default on July 6, 2026. The Motion did not include a proof of service showing it was served on Plaintiff, or that Plaintiff was given notice of the hearing date. Plaintiff filed a declaration regarding the improper service on July 20, 2026. At the hearing on July 27, 2026, the Court continued the matter to allow for proper service of the Motion papers. (Minute Order, 07/27/26.) Defendant filed proof of service by mail of the papers on August 6, 2026, and Plaintiff filed a substantive opposition on August 28, 2026. 

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to vacate the entry of default and default judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, on the basis that she did not receive actual notice of the action in time to appear. Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5 states in relevant part:

 

When service of a summons has not resulted in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action and a default or default judgment has been entered against him or her in the action, he or she may serve and file a notice of motion to set aside the default or default judgment and for leave to defend the action.  The notice of motion shall be served and filed within a reasonable time, but in no event exceeding the earlier of: (i) two years after entry of a default judgment against him or her; or (ii) 180 days after service on him or her of a written notice that the default or default judgment has been entered.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (a).) Additionally, the motion “shall be accompanied by an affidavit showing under oath that the party’s lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by his or her avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect. The party shall serve and file with the notice a copy of the answer, motion, or other pleading proposed to be filed in the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (b).)

 

The Motion is supported by Defendant’s declaration, which attests that the person described in the proof of service did not reside or accept service at her residence. (Motion, Erazo-Lopez Decl., ¶5.) The declaration acknowledges that the service address is Defendant’s residence. (Ibid.) The proof of service describes the person to whom the papers were delivered as “‘John Doe’, Male, Hispanic, 56 Years, Black Hair, Brown Eyes, 5’07”, 165 lbs, co-occupant.” (Proof of Substitute Service, filed 02/23/24, ¶5.) Defendant declares she only learned of this action on April 3, 2026, upon receiving a notice from the Department of Motor Vehicles that her driver’s license had been suspended. (Motion, Erazo-Lopez Decl., ¶3.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff points out that after delivery of the papers to “John Doe” at Defendant’s residence, the Summons and Complaint were also mailed to the service address: 14530 Wolfskill St. Mission Hills, CA 91345. (Opp., Nivinskus Decl., ¶6 and Exh. A.) This is also attested to in the proof of service. (Id. at Exh. A, p. 3.) Plaintiff mailed the Summons and Complaint on January 22, 2024, and mailed a Request for Default in April 2025, to the service address, and none of the papers were returned as undeliverable. (Id. at ¶¶8-9.)

 

Defendant has not demonstrated she lacked actual notice of the action in time to respond to the Complaint or prevent default judgment. Defendant’s supporting declaration states in only a single conclusory sentence that no one matching the description in the proof of service “resided at or accepted service at” her residence.  (Mot., Erazo-Lopez Decl., ¶5.)  But the Declaration does not address why Defendant did not receive actual notice of the lawsuit when the Summons and Complaint were mailed to her residence.  Defendant concedes that service address was her residential address at the relevant time, she received the DMV notice in the mail at that address, and no mail sent by Plaintiff to that address was returned as undeliverable.  In light of those facts, Defendant’s brief assertion contesting that the papers were delivered to “John Doe” does not convincingly establish she did not receive notice of the lawsuit, or that the lack of notice was not due to her avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5(b).)  The Court finds that relief from the entry of default or default judgment is not available to Defendant under Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Destiny C. Erazo-Lopez’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is denied.

Case Number: 24STL07314    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Crown Poly, Inc.’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is granted. The Court hereby vacates the default entered on March 7, 2025, the default judgment entered on August 12, 2025, and the order to appear for examination entered on July 20, 2026. The Court likewise quashes the Civil Subpoena Duces Tecum served on Defendant on or about July 24, 2026.

 

As requested, Defendant is ordered to file and serve its response to the Complaint within 30 days of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On October 10, 2024, Plaintiff Complete Forklift Parts, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Crown Poly, Inc. (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered default on March 7, 2025, and default judgment on August 12, 2025. Plaintiff filed an Application and Order for Appearance and Examination on July 20, 2026, with the examination set for September 1, 2026. According to Defendant, it was served with a Civil Subpoena Duces Tecum in connection with the examination. (Motion, Joseph Decl., ¶10 and Exh. H.)

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Default on August 6, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on August 31, 2026 and Defendant replied on September 4, 2026.

 

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to vacate the entry of default and default judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), on the basis that it was never served with the Summons and Complaint. Defendant also moves pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, for lack of actual notice of the action in time to appear.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d)

 

Defendant argues that it was never served with the Summons and Complaint, such that the Court did not obtain personal jurisdiction over it, rendering the default and default judgment void. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 410.50; Ridec LLC v. Hinkle (2023) 92 Cal.App.5th 1182, 1202.) Defendant also argues that the proofs of service of the Summons and Complaint are facially void.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) provides that “[t]he court may, .... on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) Plaintiff incorrectly argues in its opposition that the deadline to file this motion for relief was six months from entry of default judgment, citing Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Defendant, however, does not move under subdivision (b), but rather brings its motion under subdivision (d). The language of subdivision (d) contains no deadline.

 

Plaintiff filed Proofs of Personal Service of the Summons and Complaint on February 11, 2025 and March 7, 2026. Both proofs of service state that Defendant was personally served by delivery of the papers to “John Doe” at 5700 Bickett St, Huntington Park, CA 90255, on December 12, 2024. (Proofs of Personal Service, filed 02/11/25 and 03/07/25, ¶¶3-5.) Plaintiff does not address Defendant’s argument that service upon “John Doe” is facially invalid. Proof of personal service upon a corporation must identify the relationship of the person served to the corporation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 416.10, subd. (a).) Plaintiff’s proofs of service do not demonstrate that the papers were delivered to a person authorized to accept service on Defendant’s behalf, as required to achieve personal service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 416.10.)

 

Plaintiff in opposition argues that service upon “John Doe” was proper because, according to the contemporaneous notes of the registered process server, “John Doe” identified himself as the person authorized to accept service.  Plaintiff attaches the declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel Frank Marchetti who testified to obtaining the notes from the online portal of ABC Legal Services, which he retained to perform service.  (Marchetti Decl., ¶¶3-6.)  The declaration also attaches a photograph taken by the process server, showing her location at the place of service.  (Id., ¶¶6-8.)  Counsel also attests to receiving a call on February 24, 2025, from a representative of Defendant who stated that Defendant had “received the lawsuit” and was investigating the matter. The declaration also states that counsel advised the person of the need to file an answer.  (Id., ¶9.) 

 

No presumption of proper service applies to facially invalid service.  (Dill v. Berquist Constr. Co. (1994) 24 Cal. App. 4th 1426, 1441-1443.)  Plaintiff bears the burden to show that service was proper.  (Ibid.)  This evidentiary burden does not demand strict compliance with the statutory requirements; instead, the plaintiff can demonstrate substantial compliance. (Id. at 1437.) The evidence of substantial compliance must establish actual notice to the party to be served. (Ibid.) The Dill court explained that for a corporate defendant, this means evidence that the summons was actually received by one of the persons to be served under Code of Civil Procedure section 416.10. (Ibid.)

 

Defendant objects to the process server notes and the account of the phone call as hearsay.  The Court overrules the objection as to Counsel’s statements in the phone call; a declarant’s past statements may be established by declaration in support of the motion, and the statements are not being admitted for their truth.  The opposing party’s admissions are admissible.  (Evid. Code, § 1220.)  The Court sustains the objection as to the process server notes.  The Declaration is sufficient to establish that counsel found them in ABC Legal Service’s online portal, but they are not admissible for the truth of the matter stated—that the person served stated he was authorized to accept service.  If the process server had submitted a declaration to that effect, her account of that person’s statement would have been admissible, and if the statement had been included in her affidavit of service, it would also be admissible in this context.  (See Evid. Code, § 647.)  Neither circumstance is at issue.

 

On this evidence, the Court finds that Plaintiff has not met its burden to show that the summons was actually received by one of the persons to be served under Code of Civil Procedure section 416.10.  The purported admission by an unidentified person during the phone call that Defendant had “received the lawsuit” is not enough to show substantial compliance with the service statutes.  (Dill, 24 Cal. App. 4th at 1438–39 (“the mere fact that some employee of the corporation received the summons does not necessarily establish substantial compliance.”).  Moreover, even were the Court to credit the statement in the process server notes that “John Doe” stated he was authorized to accept service, that statement does not convince the Court that service was in fact made to a person in fact authorized to receive service. Defendant introduced relevant evidence on this point, including noting that service was made on December 21, 2024, a Saturday at 5:08 p.m., outside of business hours.  Declarants Browne and Kermani each state that the 5700 Bicket Street location was closed on December 21, 2024 for the holidays, and because it was a weekend.  (Mot., Brown, Kermani Decls., ¶8.)   The agent for service of process at the time was Ebrahim Simhaee.  The proofs of service accurately identify him as the registered agent but nonetheless identify the person to whom the documents were given as “John Doe,” suggesting that the person served was not Mr. Simhaee.  (See Mot., Browne Decl., ¶¶5-9; (Proofs of Personal Service, filed 02/11/25 and 03/07/25, ¶¶3(b), 6(d).)  Indeed, Defendant submits a declaration from Mr. Simhaee stating that he was not present at the service location on that date.  The Court has no basis in the record to conclude that John Doe was otherwise one of the persons to be served under Code of Civil Procedure section 416.10.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Crown Poly, Inc.’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is granted. The Court hereby vacates the default entered on March 7, 2025, the default judgment entered on August 12, 2025, and the order to appear for examination entered on July 20, 2026. The Court likewise quashes the Civil Subpoena Duces Tecum served on Defendant on or about July 24, 2026.

 

As requested, Defendant is ordered to file and serve its response to the Complaint within 30 days of this order.

 Analysis:

The Court sets an Order to Show Cause Why the Petitions Should Not Be Dismissed for Failure to Show Proper Service, on the date indicated below.  The parties are encouraged to appear and address the Court on this tentative at the hearing on the Petitions, which may negate the need for the Show Cause hearing.

 

Conclusion

 

The Court sets an Order to Show Cause Why the Petitions Should Not Be Dismissed for Failure to Show Proper Service, on October 5, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.  A party’s failure to appear may result in dismissal of that party’s Petition.

Case Number: 24STLC03866    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff The Hartford Underwriters Insurance Company’s Motion for Order to Correct Clerical Error in Judgment and Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc is granted.

 

Plaintiff is to file a proposed amended judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 29, 2024, Plaintiff The Hartford Underwriters Insurance Company filed the instant action against Defendant AW Collision of Monrovia, a California Corporation (“Defendant”). The Court entered default judgment on December 17, 2024. However, the default judgment does not include the parties’ names at paragraph 5 of the Judicial Council form JUD-100. (Judgment, 12/17/24, ¶5.) 

 

On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Order to Correct Clerical Error in Judgment and Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc. At the initial hearing, the Court noted that Plaintiff served Defendant with the Motion only by electronic mail and continued the matter to ensure proper service on Defendant in accordance with the statutory requirements. (Minute Order 08/03/26.) The Court ordered Plaintiff to file proper proof of service by August 17, 2026. Plaintiff filed Proof of Service of the Motion and Notice of Continuance of Hearing with proof of service of the same, on August 3, 2026. This demonstrates mail service of the Motion and notice of hearing upon Defendant as required.

 

Plaintiff moves pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) to amend the judgment entered on December 17, 2024 by adding the parties’ names at paragraph 5. The statute provides that “[t]he court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed….” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) The failure to include the parties’ names at paragraph 5 is a clear clerical error that may be corrected under the moving statute.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff The Hartford Underwriters Insurance Company’s Motion for Order to Correct Clerical Error in Judgment and Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc is granted.

 

Plaintiff is to file a proposed amended judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

Case Number: 25STLC05445    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services’ (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff's Demand for Identification, Production, Inspection and Copying of Documents and Other Tangible Things; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff's Second Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Defendant Shawn Fortune aka Shawn Michael Fortune is to serve verified responses without objections to the discovery within 20 days’ notice of this order. Plaintiff’s request for sanctions, however, is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On July 15, 2025, Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for breach of contract and common counts against Defendants Shawn Fortune aka Shawn Michael Fortune (“Defendant Fortune”) and Certified Cars of Huntington LLC aka Certified Cars of Huntington (“Defendant Certified Cars”). Defendant Fortune filed a General Denial to the Complaint on October 1, 2025. On June 2, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Demand for Production of Documents, Set One; and Request for Monetary Sanctions. (Minute Order, 06/02/26.)

 

On July 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff's Demand for Identification, Production, Inspection and Copying of Documents and Other Tangible Things; Request for Monetary Sanctions; (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff's Second Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (3) Motion for Order that Matters be Deemed Admitted; Request for Monetary Sanctions. The Motions were served on Defendant Fortune the same day by electronic and first-class mail. The Court granted Plaintiff’s ex parte application to advance the hearing dates to September 14 and 15, 2026. (Minute Order, 08/04/26.) Plaintiff filed notice of the new hearing dates and proof of service of the same on August 4, 2026. No opposition to the Motions has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an order compelling Defendant Fortune’s initial responses to Special Interrogatories, Set Two, and Demand for Identification, Production, Inspection and Copying of Documents and Other Tangible Things, Set Two, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300. Plaintiff propounded Special Interrogatories, Set Two, and Demand for Identification, Production, Inspection and Copying of Documents and Other Tangible Things, Set Two, on Defendant Fortune on June 3, 2026. (Motions, Brown Decl., Exh. 1.) Despite a meet and confer effort, Defendant Fortune did not serve responses. (Id. at ¶¶4-5 and Exh. 2.)

 

Based on Defendant Fortune’s failure to serve initial responses to the discovery, the Motions to compel responses to the interrogatories and demands are granted. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) The moving party is not required to meet and confer prior meet and confer to bringing a motion to compel initial responses. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Furthermore, the motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to respond. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling Defendant Fortune to serve verified responses to the interrogatories and demands, without objections.

 

Plaintiff also asks the Court to impose monetary sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030. In City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, the Supreme Court observed:

 

It is already well-established that a court may not rely on [Code of Civil Procedure] section 2023.030 to override the limitations prescribed by any other applicable sanctions provision in the [Civil Discovery] Act. A court may invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 only when confronted with an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision. And where it invokes that authority, it is constrained by the long-settled rules generally governing the imposition of discovery sanctions under the Act.

 

(City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 74-75.) Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300 limit the court’s authority to impose sanctions when (as here) the non-moving party does not oppose a successful motion to compel responses to interrogatories and requests for production. Defendant Fortune filed no opposition, and Plaintiff has not shown that his failure to serve discovery responses is “an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision.” Therefore, the request for sanctions is denied.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services’ (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff's Demand for Identification, Production, Inspection and Copying of Documents and Other Tangible Things; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff's Second Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Defendant Shawn Fortune aka Shawn Michael Fortune is to serve verified responses without objections to the discovery within 20 days’ notice of this order. Plaintiff’s request for sanctions, however, is denied.

Case Number: 26STCP01347    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Unifirst Corporation’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is Granted.

 

Petitioner is to file a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

 

Analysis:

 

On April 6, 2026, Petitioner Unifirst Corporation (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award (the “Petition”) against Respondent Elite Tires & Auto Care (“Respondent”).  This is a continued hearing on the Petition.  No response to the Petition has been filed to date.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party to an arbitration in which an award has been made may petition the court to confirm, correct or vacate the award.  The petition shall name as respondent all parties to the arbitration and may name as respondents any other persons bound by the arbitration award.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.) “If a petition or response under this chapter is duly served and filed, the court shall confirm the award as made, whether rendered in this state or another state, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.)

 

A response to a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award that seeks to vacate or correct the award must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent, or within ten days of service of the Petition to Confirm, whichever is sooner.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288.2, 1290.6; Valencia v. Mendoza (2024)103 Cal. App. 5th 427, 445.)

 

Discussion

 

Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing (CCP § 1290.4)

 

Code of Civil Procedure, section 1290.4 requires that the petition and notice of hearing be served on Respondent “in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice” or “[i]f the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made . . . [s]ervice within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4, subds. (a), (b).)  The arbitration agreement does not provide for the manner of service of the petition and notice of hearing, which therefore must be served in the manner of service for a summons. (Pet., Attachment 4(b), p. 2.)

 

At the initial hearing on August 10, 2026, the Court noted that service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing Date did not comply with the statutory requirements, in that service on the corporate defendant was made on the corporation’s agent at the agent’s home address, 810 W. 29th St, Long Beach, California, without any explanation why that was an appropriate address for substitute service. (Minute Order, 08/10/26 (citing Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20).) The Court continued the hearing and ordered Petitioner to file proof of service of the Petition and Notice of New Hearing Date in accordance with the statutory requirements by August 24, 2026.  (Ibid.)  Petitioner was also ordered to give Respondent notice of the ruling. (Ibid.)

 

On September 2, 2026, Petitioner filed a Notice of Motion on Petition to Confirm Contractual Arbitration Award and a Declaration in Support of Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award.

 

The Supporting Declaration states that Respondent did not participate in the arbitration proceeding and describes two prior unsuccessful attempts to serve Respondent through its agent for service of process, Isela Arteaga.  First, service was attempted at the Cedar Avenue address for service of process identified on the Secretary of State website, which was determined to be an invalid address.  (Cooper Decl., ¶ 5.)  Service was then attempted at a residential address where Mr. Arteaga no longer resides.  (Id., ¶6.)  Finally, service was attempted at the 810 W. 29th Street address, located through a public records search. (Id., ¶7.)  The Declaration says that the public records search is attached as Exhibit A, but it is not; Exhibit A are printouts from the Secretary of State website, identifying the Cedar Avenue address. 

 

The Court nonetheless finds this showing sufficient, considered in light of the original proof of service.   Substitute service on a corporation via its agent for service of process may be accomplished at the person’s usual mailing address by leaving the papers with a person apparently in charge thereof and thereafter mailing the documents to that address by First Class mail.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20(a).)  The proof of service identifies the 810 W. 29th address as the “home” address for Mr. Arteaga, and states that the papers were delivered to a competent member of the household, described as a “co-occupant to Mr. Arteaga.”  (Proof of Mailing (Substitute Service, 06/02/2026.)  The Declaration establishes unsuccessful efforts to identify another physical address at which papers could be served on Mr. Arteaga.  (Cooper Decl., ¶¶5-6.)  The Proof of Service documents that the papers were later delivered by First Class Mail, as required for substitute service.  (Proof of Mailing (Substitute Service), 06/02/2026.)  Based on this showing, the Court finds that service of the Petition complied with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4.   

 

The Notice of Motion on Petition filed on September 2, 2026 gave Defendant notice of the continued hearing date.  The Notice includes a proof of service by mail on Defendant on August 28, 2026 at the 810 W. 29th Street address.  (Notice of Motion, filed 09/02/26, p. 3.) Having found service of the Petition valid at that address, the Court finds service by mail of the Notice of Motion also sufficient.

 

Service of the Arbitration Award (CCP § 1283.6)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 requires that “[t]he neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1283.6.) This requirement may be satisfied by service by the arbitrator, or upon proper service of the award with the petition. (See Murray v. Civil Service Employees Ins. Co. (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 796, 799-800.) The arbitration award was served on the parties on November 10, 2025. (Pet., Attachment 8(c), p. 1.)

 

Confirmation of the Arbitration Award

 

An arbitration award is not directly enforceable until it is confirmed by a court and judgment is entered.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1287.6; Jones v. Kvistad (1971) 19 Cal.App.3d 836, 840.) The court must confirm the award as made, unless it corrects or vacates the award, or dismisses the proceeding.  (CCP § 1286; Valsan Partners Limited Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility, Inc. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.) Code of Civil Procedure, section 1285.4 states a petition under this chapter shall:

 

a)     Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

b)    Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

c)     Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4.) The Petition complies with these requirements. It sets forth the nature of the arbitration agreement, the name of the arbitrator, and attaches a copy of the arbitration award. (Pet., ¶¶4-6 and Attachment 8(c).) The arbitrator awarded Petitioner $24,521.47 principal, $269.83 pre-award interest, and $7,627.50 pre-award attorney’s fees. (Id. at Attachment 8(c), pp. 4-5.) Therefore, the arbitration award is confirmed in favor of Petitioner in that amount.

 

Attorney’s Fees, Costs and Interest

 

To the extent Petitioner requests attorney’s fees according to proof and costs of $500.00 (Pet., ¶10e-f), those are permitted on a petition to confirm arbitration award. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1293.2; MBNA America Bank, N.A. v. Gorman (2006) 147 Cal.App.4th.Supp. 1, 7.) Petitioner may file a memorandum of costs pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1700 and a noticed motion for attorney’s fees pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1702. Also, “a successful party to arbitration is entitled to post-award, pre-judgment interest under Civil Code section 3287, subdivision (a). (Pierotti v. Torian (2000) 81 Cal.App.4th 17, 27.) Petitioner’s proposed judgment must include the legal basis and calculation for the interest sought therein.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner Unifirst Corporation’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is granted.

 

Petitioner is to file a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

 

Judicial Assistant is to give notice.


Case Number: 26STCP01797    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Shelly G. Wolfsdorf ’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is granted.

 

Petitioner is to file a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Petitioner Shelly G. Wolfsdorf (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award against Respondents Dalmore Group LLC (“Respondent Dalmore”) and Finalis Securities LLC (“Respondent Finalis”) on May 8, 2026. The Petition seeks to confirm the arbitration award issued on January 20, 2026, in favor of Petitioner, requiring expungement of all references to certain customer dispute information maintained by the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (“FINRA”) in the Central Registration Depository (“CRD”). (Pet., Attachment 8(c), pp. 3-4.) FINRA waived its participation in the confirmation proceedings. (Id., Attachment 5.)

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party to an arbitration in which an award has been made may petition the court to confirm, correct or vacate the award.  The petition shall name as respondent all parties to the arbitration and may name as respondents any other persons bound by the arbitration award.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.) “If a petition or response under this chapter is duly served and filed, the court shall confirm the award as made, whether rendered in this state or another state, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.)

 

A response to a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award that seeks to vacate or correct the award must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent, or within ten days of service of the Petition to Confirm, whichever is sooner.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288.2, 1290.6; Valencia v. Mendoza (2024)103 Cal. App. 5th 427, 445.)

 

Discussion

 

Filing Requirements of a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award (CCP § 1285.4)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1285.4 states: “A petition under this chapter shall:

 

(a)   Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

(b)  Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

(c) Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4 (emphasis added).) The Petition sets forth the substance of the arbitration agreement and attaches a copy of the arbitration award with the names of the arbitrators. (Pet., ¶¶4-6 and Attachment 8(c).) This satisfies Code of Civil Procedure section 1285.4, subdivisions (a)-(c).

 

Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing (CCP § 1290.4)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4 states in pertinent part:

 

“(a) A copy of the petition and a written notice of the time and place of the hearing thereof and any other papers upon which the petition is based shall be served in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice.

 

(b) If the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made and the person upon whom service is to be made has not previously appeared in the proceeding and has not previously been served in accordance with this subdivision: ¶ (1) Service within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4.) The arbitration agreement does not provide for the manner of service. (Pet., Exh. 2.) On June 17, 2026, Petitioner filed Notices and Acknowledgement of Receipt with respect to service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing on both Respondents. (Notices and Acknowledgement of Receipt, filed 06/17/26.) The Notices and Acknowledgement of Receipt also include a Notice of Non-Opposition to the Petition. (Ibid.) Respondents also informed Petitioner that they would not appear at the hearing on the instant Petition. (Decl. of Non-Appearance, filed 09/10/26.) Based on Respondents’ acknowledgment of receipt of the papers and submission to the Court’s jurisdiction, the Court finds that compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4 is satisfied.

 

Service of the Arbitration Award (CCP § 1283.6)

         

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 provides that: “The neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.”  (Emphasis added.) This requirement may be satisfied by service by the arbitrator, or upon proper service of the award with the Petition. (See Murry v. Civil Service Employees Ins. Co. (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 796, 799-800.) The Petition demonstrates that the arbitration award was served on the parties on February 11, 2026. (Pet., Attachment 8(c), p. 6.) Therefore, the Court finds the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 have been met.

 

Timing of Service of Petition (CCP §§ 1288, 1288.4)

           

A party may seek a court judgment confirming an arbitration award by filing and serving a petition no more than four years, but not less than 10 days, after the award is served. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288, 1288.4.) The Petition was timely filed approximately three months after the award was issued.

 

Merits of the Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award

 

The court must confirm the award as made, unless it corrects or vacates the award, or dismisses the proceeding.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286; Valsan Partners Limited Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility, Inc. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.) Substantively, Petitioner has demonstrated that on January 20, 2026, the arbitrators issued an award in their favor and against Respondents requiring expungement of all references to certain customer dispute information as to Petitioner maintained by FINRA in Petitioner’s Central Registration Depository (“CRD”) record. (Pet., Attachment 8(c), pp. 4-5.)

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Petitioner Shelly G. Wolfsdorf ’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is granted.

 

Petitioner is to file a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

 

 

Moving party is to give notice.

Case Number: 26STLC03935    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Jay Kim, as Trustee of the Kim Revocable Living Trust dated September 16, 2008, Kim Revocable Living Trust dated September 16, 2008’s Demurrer to the Complaint is overruled as to the third cause of action and sustained without leave to amend as to the fourth and fifth causes of action.

 

Defendant is to file and serve an Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this ruling.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiffs Sammy K. Shin and Chung Ok Shin (“Plaintiffs”) filed the instant action against Defendant Jay Kim, as Trustee of the Kim Revocable Living Trust dated September 16, 2008, Kim Revocable Living Trust dated September 16, 2008 (“Defendant”), on June 15, 2026. Defendant filed and served the instant Demurrer to the Complaint on July 28, 2026. No opposition to the Demurrer has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Demurrer is accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. (Declaration, filed 07/28/26, ¶2.) Defendant demurs to the third, fourth, and fifth causes of action of the Complaint for failure to allege sufficient facts and uncertainty. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subds. (e), (f).) Special demurrers are not permitted in the Limited Civil Court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (c).) Therefore, the Court will not consider the Demurrer for uncertainty.

 

The Complaint alleges (1) Negligence; (2) Private Nuisance; (3) Trespass; (4) Property Damage; and (5) Loss of Rental Income, based on the following facts. Plaintiffs purchased the real property located at 4848 Wilshire Blvd. #102 Los Angeles, California (“Unit 102”). (Compl., ¶2.) Defendant owns the unit above Unit 102, located at 4848 Wilshire Blvd. #302, Los Angeles, California (“Unit 302”). (Id. at ¶3.) Defendant owned, controlled, maintained, repaired, occupied, and/or possessed Unit 302 and the plumbing, fixtures, appliances, water lines, and related components located therein. (Id. at ¶4.) Defendant owed a duty to exercise reasonable care in the ownership, maintenance, inspection, repair, and operation of Unit 302 so as not to cause damage to neighboring units. (Id. at ¶5.)

 

Plaintiffs purchased Unit 102 on or about July 10, 2024 and rented it out. (Id. at ¶7.) In mid-February 2025, Plaintiffs received tenant complaints of water leakage affecting Unit 102. (Id. at ¶8.) Plaintiffs discovered that water had escaped from Unit 302 and infiltrated Unit 102. (Id. at ¶9.) The water intrusion was caused by Defendants’ negligent maintenance, repair, inspection, operation, and/or failure to timely correct a dangerous condition within Unit 302. (Id. at ¶10.) The condition was mainly caused by construction work being done in Unit 302. (Ibid.) Plaintiffs sought a meeting with Defendant and the Homeowner’s Association. (Id. at ¶11.) One of the HOA board members tried to visit Unit 302, but Defendant refused them access. (Ibid.) Defendant knew or reasonably should have known about the water leak but failed to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. (Id. at ¶12.) As a direct result of the water leak, Unit 102 sustained extensive water damage to ceilings, walls, flooring, drywall, cabinetry, and other building components, and substantial mold contamination. (Id. at ¶¶13-14.) Plaintiff had to retain contractors, water mitigation specialists, mold assessors, mold remediation companies, and repair professionals to investigate and remediate the damage. (Id. at ¶15.) Plaintiff lost rental income during the remediation and repair process. (Id. at ¶17.) Plaintiff also incurred mold remediation costs, water mitigation expenses, repair and reconstruction costs, property damage, expert and inspection fees, lost rental income, loss of use damages, prejudgment interest, and other consequential damages. (Id. at ¶18.)

 

The third cause of action for trespass incorporates by reference the aforementioned allegations and further alleges that “water, moisture, mold spores, and related contaminants originating from Unit 302 physically invaded Plaintiff’s property” and that the “unauthorized invasion interfered with Plaintiff’s possessory interests” and directly caused Plaintiff to suffer damages. (Id. at ¶¶28-30.) Defendant demurs on the grounds that trespass requires a particular mental state or degree of culpability beyond ordinary negligence, which the Complaint fails to allege. (Citing Martin Marietta Corp. v. Insurance Co. of North America (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1132.) The case to which Defendant cites holds that “ ‘The essence of the cause of action for trespass is an “unauthorized entry” onto the land of another.’ [Citation.]” (Martin Marietta Corp. v. Insurance Co. of North America (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1132, citing Cassinos v. Union Oil Co. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 1770, 1778.) The Court of Appeal also held that an insurance policy referring to “wrongful entry” included trespass but was not persuaded by the argument that “wrongful entry” requires “intent to oust the plaintiff from possession.” (Id. at 1133.) Beyond this, the case did not set forth what “particular mental state or degree of culpability” is required to allege trespass.

 

Nor does Defendant’s Demurrer analyze any of the allegations in the Complaint to demonstrate that they fail to support the requisite intent. The Complaint alleges that Defendant knew, or should have reasonably known, about the water intrusion. (Compl., ¶12.) Defendant also allegedly refused to give an HOA member access to Unit 302. (Id. at ¶11.) The Demurrer does not explain why these allegations are insufficient. Therefore, Defendant has not demonstrated that Plaintiff’s allegations in support of the third cause of action are insufficient to allege trespass. The Demurrer to the third cause of action is overruled.

 

Defendant also demurs to the fourth and fifth causes of action on the grounds that they merely make claims for particular kinds of damages—property damage and loss of use and loss of rental income—which do not constitute a cause of action. As Defendant points out, these causes of action allege no additional breach or wrongful conduct and simply allege specific types of damages based on previously alleged conduct. (Compl., ¶¶32-38.) This does not constitute a cause of action. (See People ex rel. Feuer v. Superior Ct. (Cahuenga’s the Spot) (2015) 234 Cal. App. 4th 1360, 1376-77 (defining “cause of action”).  The Demurrer to the fourth and fifth causes of action is sustained.

 

As the pleading party, Plaintiff bears the burden to show that leave to amend should be granted, which the Court must allow where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) Plaintiff filed no opposition to the Demurrer to demonstrate on what basis leave to amend the last two causes of action might be granted. Therefore, leave to amend is denied.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Jay Kim, as Trustee of the Kim Revocable Living Trust dated September 16, 2008, Kim Revocable Living Trust dated September 16, 2008’s Demurrer to the Complaint is overruled as to the third cause of action and sustained without leave to amend as to the fourth and fifth causes of action.

 

Defendant is to file and serve an Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this ruling.
