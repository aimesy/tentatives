Case Number: 25STCV35056    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 23, 2026                                      TRIAL DATE:  June 5, 2028

                                                          

CASE:                         Shahram Pascal Abrar v. Tayfun Paul Kadioglu, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV35056

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM

INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE, FROM DEFENDANTS BELLEVUE OASIS LLC, FIT FOR A KING INC., AND PALOMA PALM SPRINGS, LLC, AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE, FROM DEFENDANTS BELLEVUE OASIS LLC, PALOMA PALM SPRINGS LLC, AND TAYFUN KADIOGLU LLC

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION, SET ONE, FROM DEFENDANTS BELLEVUE OASIS LLC, PALOMA

PALM SPRINGS LLC, AND FIT FOR A KING, INC.

I.         INTRODUCTION

            On July 21, 2026, Plaintiff, Shahram Pascal Abrar, filed three motions: (1) to compel Defendants, Bellevue Oasis LLC (“Bellevue”), Paloma Palm Springs LLC (“Paloma), and Fit for a King Inc. (“FFK”) to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories, Set One; (2) to compel Bellevue, Paloma and Tayfun Kadioglu LLC (“Tayfun”) to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s Requests for Production of Documents, Set One; (3) to compel Bellevue, Paloma and FFK to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s Requests for Admission, Set One.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendants and their (then) counsel of record.

            On August 26 and September 10, 2026, Defendants filed oppositions.  On the same day, Defendants’ former counsel also filed oppositions and joinder to Defendants’ oppositions.

            On September 1 and September 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed replies.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.300, 2031.310, and 2033.290, parties may move for a further response to interrogatories, requests for production of documents, and requests for admissions where an answer to the discovery requests are evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general.¿¿¿ 

 

The service of supplemental responses during the pendency of a motion to compel further does not moot the motion.  (See County of San Benito v. Superior Court (2023) 96 Cal.App.5th 243, 256 [“Moreover, to adopt the County's interpretation of the discovery act—that a party opposing a motion to compel further responses may unilaterally divest the court of authority to rule on the motion by serving a further response in the interim—would permit a party faced with a motion to compel to delay its adjudication indefinitely by successive supplemental but inadequate responses, each prompting a further motion to compel, each of which in turn would be defeated by piecemeal supplementation.”].)¿

¿ 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

Sanctions shall be awarded against any party, person or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further responses, unless the Court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(d); 2031.310(h); 2033.290(d).)¿¿ 

III.      DISCUSSION

            A.  Judicial Notice

            Defendants unopposed request for judicial notice is GRANTED.

            B.  Form Interrogatories (FROG) and Requests for Production of Documents (RFP)

            Defendants initially served objection-only responses and, during the pendency of this motion, served supplemental verified responses.  Contrary to Defendants’ position, the supplemental responses do not divest the court of its jurisdiction to rule upon Defendants’ initial responses.  (See County of San Benito v. Superior Court, supra, 96 Cal.App.5th at p. 256.)  However, based on Plaintiff’s reply, many of the issues may now be moot.  The court will therefore order the parties to meet and confer regarding Defendants’ supplemental verified responses.  Any remaining disputes are to be clearly detailed in a separate statement.  The court will rule on Plaintiff’s sanctions request pending further hearing on the motions to compel further responses to the FROGs and RFPs.

            C.  Requests for Admission (RFA)

            The only remaining RFA at issue is Defendants’ supplemental responses to RFA No. 31.  (See Reply ISO MTCF to RFAs, p. 2.)  Defendants provided the same responses to RFA No. 31.  The court has reviewed the supplemental responses.  It is evasive and does not admit, deny, or state an inability.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.220.) 

            Accordingly, the motion to compel further responses to RFA No. 31 is GRANTED.  The motion is MOOT as to RFA Nos. 1-30, 32-35.   

            The court also finds sanctions against Defendants only are warranted.  The court finds that former counsel did not engage in or counsel discovery abuse.  Accordingly, sanctions are imposed against Defendants in connection to the RFAs in the total sum of $2,310.

IV.       DISPOSITIONS

1.  The hearing for the motions to compel further responses to FROGs and RFPs are CONTINUED to October 23, 2026.  The parties are to meet and confer regarding Defendants’ supplemental responses.  Any remaining disputes are to be detailed in a separate statement to be filed no later than 7 court days before the hearing.

2.  The motion to compel further responses to the RFAs is GRANTED as to RFA No. 31.  Defendants are ordered to provide further responses to RFA No. 31 within 10 days of this order and to pay sanctions in the sum of $2,310 within 30 days of this order.

            Plaintiff to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 26STCV00973    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:       September 23, 2026                                                TRIAL DATE:  Not set

                                                          

CASE:                         Tori Chen v. Los Angeles Department of Water and Power, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 26STCV00973

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO SET ASIDE AND VACATE THE COURT’S MAY 26, 2026 ORDER SUSTAINING DEFENDANT’S DEMURRER WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND AND TO VACATE OR PREVENT ENTRY OF JUDGMENT

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Tori Chen

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant City of Los Angeles

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

            On January 12, 2026, Plaintiff, Tori Chen, filed a Complaint against Defendants, City of Los Angeles, acting by and through its Department of Water and Power (the “City”), Adriana Rubalcava, Walter Ramirez, Sumita Thappa, and Sandra Wallace for retaliation for whistleblowing (Labor Code § 1102.5), retaliation for safety complaints/whistleblower protection (Labor Code § 6310), and breach of contract/breach of written policy/MOU (unpaid accrued PTO).  

 

            On February 5, 2026, this case was deemed related to Case No. 23STCV19719.  Case No. 23STCV19719 is the lead case.

 

            On April 20, 2026, the City filed a demurrer to the Complaint.

 

            On May 26, 2026, the court sustained the demurrer without leave to amend.

 

            On June 25, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to set aside and vacate the court’s May 26, 2026 order and to vacate or prevent entry of judgment.

 

            On September 1, 2026, judgment was entered in the City’s favor.

 

            On September 10, 2026, the City filed an opposition.

 

            On September 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.        DISCUSSION & LEGAL STANDARD

 

The motion fails for two reasons.  First, it is procedural defective.  Plaintiff moves pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 663, which provides,“[a] judgment or decree, when based upon a decision by the court, or the special verdict of a jury, may, upon motion of the party aggrieved, be set aside and vacated by the same court, and another and different judgment entered, for either of the following causes, materially affecting the substantial rights of the party and entitling the party to a different judgment:  1. Incorrect or erroneous legal basis for the decision, not consistent with or not supported by the facts; and in such case when the judgment is set aside, the statement of decision shall be amended and corrected.  2. A judgment or decree not consistent with or not supported by the special verdict.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 663.)  By its very terms, Section 663 applies to a judgment or decree.  It does not apply to an interim order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend. 

 

Second, the motion is substantively defective.  The court sustained the City’s demurrer based on facts which the court judicially noticed.  The judicially noticed fact established that the City rejected Plaintiff’s claim on July 8, 2024, thereby triggered a six-month statute of limitation.  It is undisputed Plaintiff filed this action more than six months after July 2024.  Plaintiff fails to show any incorrect or erroneous legal basis for the court’s May 26, 2026 order.

 

III.      CONCLUSION

 

            The motion is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice, unless waived.

  Judge of the Superior Court 

 

 

           

 


Case Number: 26STCV13131    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 23, 2026                                                  TRIAL DATE:  N/A

                                                          

CASE:                              Katelyn Waco v. Jon’s Towing Inc.

 

CASE NO.:                 26STCV13131

 

APPLICATION FOR DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

On April 23, 2026, Plaintiff, Katelyn Waco, filed this action against Defendant, Jon’s Towing Inc., and Does 1 through 10 for pregnancy discrimination, sex discrimination, wrongful termination in violation of public policy, intrusion into private affairs, and constructive invasion of privacy.

 

On May 4, 2026, Defendant was served with the summons and complaint by substituted service.

 

            On July 13, 2026, default was entered against Defendant.

 

            On August 7, 2026, Does 1 through 10 were dismissed.  On the same day, Plaintiff filed a request for entry of default judgment against Defendant in the total sum of $492,225.49, consisting of $83,725.49 in special damages, $300,000 in general damages, and $108,500 in attorney fees.

 

            On August 25, 2026, the court denied the application without prejudice because (1) there was no proof of service of the statement of damages; (2) Plaintiff did not submit any evidence to support the damages sought; (3) Plaintiff did not submit any proof of Defendant’s financial condition to support a punitive damages award; (4) attorney’s fees were not calculated pursuant to the default schedule provided under LASC Local Rule 3.214. 

 

            On September 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed another request for default judgment.  Plaintiff now requests entry of default judgment against Defendant in the total sum of $389,452.74, consisting of $83,725.49 in special damages, $300,000 in general damages, and $5,727.25 in attorney fees.

 

 

II.        DISCUSSION

 

            After review, the court cannot approve the application for the following reasons:

 

1.     Plaintiff’s special damages claim is comprised, in part, of $38,674.08 in loss of earnings, $37,440.00 in loss of future earning capacity, and $7,611.41 in lost benefits.  Plaintiff merely states that she lost her income of $18.00 and that she suffered the foregoing economic losses.  This showing is insufficient to establish she suffered economic losses in the foregoing amounts.  For instance, there is no calculation or explanation provided to show how the stated figures represent Plaintiff’s special damages.  This is especially true of Plaintiff’s alleged loss of future earning capacity and lost benefits.  Plaintiff must provide additional explanation or proof.

 

2.     Plaintiff’s general damages claim is comprised of $125,000 for pain, suffering, and inconvenience, $125,000 for emotional distress, and $50,000 for invasion of privacy.  Plaintiff does not provide sufficient evidence to prove up her general damages claim. Further, the court is unable to ascertain the appropriate general damages award in the absence of proof to support the special damages claim. 

 

3.     The general damages requested make clear that emotional distress is at the heart of the action.  Therefore, Plaintiff should have served Defendant with a statement of damages prior to the entry of default.  (See Schwab v. Rondel Homes, Inc. (1991) 53 Cal.3d 428, 432.)  Here, Plaintiff purportedly served the statement of damages on September 21, 2026.  Having failed to serve the statement of damages prior to the entry of default, the default was entered in error and must be vacated.  (Sass v. Cohen (2020) 10 Cal.5th 861, 870-71.)

 

4.     The statement of damages was purportedly served by mailing and acknowledgement of receipt. However, the completed acknowledgement of receipt is not attached to the statement of damages.  Effectively, there is no proof that the statement was served on Defendant.

 

III.      CONCLUSION

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s application for entry of default judgment is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.  The default entered against Defendant on July 13, 2026 is VACATED.  Plaintiff is ordered to seek entry of default and to file a default judgment package addressing the defects noted herein forthwith.  If no default or a default judgment is entered before the next court date, Plaintiff must file a declaration explaining why no later than five court days before the next court date.
