DEPARTMENT D LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25NWCV00286    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: D

ARACELI ROMAN, ET AL. V. LUIS MOLINA CESMA, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV00286

HEARING:  September 28, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#16

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

City of Huntington Park’s demurrer to the third cause of action is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

 

Plaintiff shall file a second amended complaint within 30 days of this ruling.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant City of Huntington Park demurrers to Plaintiff’s third cause of action for dangerous condition of public property for failing to plead facts constituting a cause of action. Specifically, the claim lacks facts showing the existence of a dangerous condition, notice, and creation of a dangerous condition under Gov. Code §§ 835 and 835.2 for liability to attach. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.10(e).)

 

Background

 

This is an action regarding a motor vehicle-pedestrian accident that occurred on October 30, 2023. On January 24, 2025, Plaintiff Araceli Roman, as Guardian Ad Litem for Samantha Roman, a minor, (Plaintiff) filed this action against Defendants Luis Molina Cesma, Maria Lourdes Cesma Trevino, City of Huntington Park, and Does 1 to 50. The Complaint alleges that Araceli Roman was crossing the street with her granddaughter, Samantha Roman, when Defendant Luis Molina Cesma collided with them while making a left turn. The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Motor Vehicle Negligence (against all Defendants), 2) Premises Liability (against Defendant City of Huntington Park), and 3) General Negligence (against all Defendants).

 

On June 9, 2025, Defendant City of Huntington Park (City) filed an answer.

 

On July 14, 2025, Plaintiff filed a motion for leave to file a first amended complaint which the court DENIED.

 

On June 29, 2026, City filed a stipulation and proposed order permitting Plaintiff leave to file a first amended complaint which the court GRANTED.

 

On July 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC) with three causes of action for: 1) Negligence – Motor Vehicle (against Defendant Cesma), 2) Negligent Entrustment (against Defendant Trevino), and 3) Dangerous Condition of Public Property [Gov. Code § 835] (against City).

 

On September 1, 2026, City filed the instant demurrer to the FAC. On September 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed an untimely opposition. On September 18, 2026, City filed a timely reply.

 

Legal Standard

 

As a general matter, in a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. ¿ (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)  “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.”  (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.)  As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations.  (Id.)  The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action.  (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment.  (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.)  The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully.  (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.)  However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.”  (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245).   

 

Plaintiff is only required to allege ultimate facts, not evidentiary facts.  (See Committee on Children's Television, Inc. v. General Foods Corp. (1983) 35 Cal.3d 197, 212 [“the complaint should set forth the ultimate facts constituting the cause of action, not the evidence by which plaintiff proposes to prove those facts”); 1 Cal. Affirmative Def. § 10:2 (2d ed.) [allegations of agency, course and scope of employment, etc. qualify as ultimate facts].)  Plaintiff’s allegations must be accepted as true on demurrer.  (See Yvanova v. New Century Mortgage Corp. (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919, 924 [“For purposes of reviewing a demurrer, we accept the truth of material facts properly pleaded in the operative complaint”].) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, subdivision (a) requires the moving party to meet and confer “in person, by video conference, or by telephone” in an attempt to resolve a demurrer’s deficiencies five days before the responsive pleading is due.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(a)(2).) 

 

Here, the parties met and conferred telephonically on August 11, 2026. (Declaration of Jeremy M. Forsyth ¶ 3.) This requirement is therefore met.

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

City requests the court to take judicial notice of the following documents:

1.    Plaintiff’s complaint filed January 24, 2025;

2.    Plaintiff’s response to City’s special interrogatory No. 34;

3.    Plaintiff’s response to City’s form interrogatory No. 17.1.

 

Plaintiff requests the court to take judicial notice of the following documents:

1.    Joint stipulation for leave to file first amended complaint filed June 12, 2026.

 

The Court may take judicial notice of “[f]acts and propositions that are not reasonably subject to dispute and are capable of immediate and accurate determination by resort to sources of reasonably indisputable accuracy.”  (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (h).)  “The existence and contents of a written agreement may be the proper subject of judicial notice if there is no factual dispute that the document is genuine and accurate.”  (Chacon v. Union Pacific Railroad (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 565, 572; see Performance Plastering v. Richmond Am. Homes of Calif., Inc. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 659, 666 n.2 [judicial notice of settlement agreements and their contents proper in demurrer because there was no factual dispute concerning their contents].) 

 

The court GRANTS City’s request for judicial notice of Plaintiff’s complaint and GRANTS Plaintiff’s request for judicial notice of the joint stipulation for leave to file first amended complaint. (Evid. Code § 452(d).) The court DENIES City’s request for judicial notice of the discovery responses as immaterial to this ruling.

 

Procedural Issues

 

City substantially complied with Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1320(a) because their notice states each ground for demurrer despite appearing in the notice and not as a separate page.

 

Plaintiff’s opposition is one day late. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1005(b).) However, the court exercises its discretion to consider it since City still timely filed their reply.

 

Analysis

 

City contends the FAC fails to state a claim against them for six reasons: 1) Plaintiff failed to meet the heightened pleading standard of alleging the dangerous condition claim against City; 2) the sham pleading doctrine bars Plaintiff’s “new” allegations that the deficiencies included non-functioning rectangular rapid flash beacon warning devices and pedestrian push-button actuators; 3) City is immune from liability for failure to provide crossing equipment under Gov. Code § 830.4; 4) Plaintiff failed to adequately allege that the City had notice of a dangerous condition at the time of injury; 5) Plaintiff failed to allege that City is liable for creating a dangerous condition; and 6) Plaintiff’s conclusory allegations do not support causation between the dangerous condition and Defendant Molina’s driving.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that City improperly asks the court to resolve factual disputes at this stage, City possesses the maintenance records so less particularity is required as to those allegations, Plaintiff sufficiently identified the dangerous condition for the pleading stage, that the immunities articulated in Gov. Code § 830.4 do not apply here, the sham pleading doctrine does not apply or fully dispose of the claim. Plaintiff also argues that she pleads a dangerous condition in such a way as to foreseeably endanger those using the property or the danger arises from the interrelationship of its structural or natural features. Finally, she argues she plead City’s actual or constructive notice three ways, which in turn defeats City’s argument that Plaintiff must also plead they created the dangerous condition.

 

The City replies that Plaintiff confirmed the FAC is deficient via counsel’s declarations, the alleged malfunction is still unconnected to the collision, there are no facts that a physical defect in City property increased or intensified the danger of third party accidents, there are no allegations the City’s property created a trap by design, there are still no allegations of actual or constructive knowledge, no allegations City created the condition, and no allegations of causal connection. They add that they do challenge the entire claim, not a portion of it, and leave to amend would not cure these defects.

 

A claim against a public entity under Gov. Code § 835 requires the plaintiff to plead: “(1) a dangerous condition existed on the public property at the time of the injury;” “(2) the condition proximately caused the injury;” “(3) the condition created a reasonably foreseeable risk of the kind of injury sustained;” and “(4) the public entity had actual or constructive notice of the dangerous condition of the property in sufficient time to have taken measures to protect against it.” (Brenner v. City of El Cajon (2003) 113 Cal.App.4th 434, 439 (Brenner).)

 

“Dangerous condition” means a condition of property that creates a substantial...risk of injury when such property or adjacent property is used with due care in a manner in which it is reasonably foreseeable that it will be used.”  (Gov. Code, § 830.)  “With respect to public streets, courts have observed “any property can be dangerous if used in a sufficiently improper manner.  For this reason, a public entity is only required to provide roads that are safe for reasonably foreseeable careful use. [Citation.].”  (Sun v. City of Oakland (2008)166 Cal.App.4th 1177, 1183, quoting Chowdhury v. City of Los Angeles (1995) 38 Cal.App.4th 1187, 1196.) “A dangerous condition exists when public property ‘is physically damaged, deteriorated, or defective in such a way as to foreseeably endanger those using the property itself,’ or possesses physical characteristics in its design, location, features or relationship to its surroundings that endanger users.” (Hedayatzadeh v. City of Del Mar (2020) 44 Cal.App.5th 555, 562.)

 

i.                 Particularity of Allegations

 

“[A] claim alleging a dangerous condition [against a public entity] may not rely on generalized allegations [citation] but must specify in what manner the condition constituted a dangerous condition.” (Brenner, supra, 113 Cal.App.4th at p. 439-440.)

 

After reviewing the FAC, Plaintiff insufficiently alleged the dangerous condition with the requisite particularity.

 

Brenner discussed factors to determine whether a crosswalk is a dangerous condition at the pleadings stage. The first was an increased number of pedestrians coupled with allegations of “physical characteristics” of the crossing that “created a substantial risk that a driver using due care while traveling along [the road] would be unable to stop for pedestrians who were using due care while crossing” at the intersection. (Brenner, supra, 113 Cal.App.4th at p. 440.) The second was allegation of “some peculiar condition that makes it unsafe to cross [the crosswalk] even when motorists and pedestrians are exercising due care.” (Id. at p. 441.)

 

Here, Plaintiff’s FAC is like Brenner. Notably absent are allegations of physical characteristics of the intersection of Live Oak Street and State Street that create a condition such that a driver using due care would be unable to stop for pedestrians using due care. Instead, Plaintiff alleges that the intersection was regularly used by children as a school-route (i.e., increased foot traffic). (FAC ¶¶ 17, 21.) She additionally alleges, generally, a “continuing pedestrian-safety hazard at this specific location” based on a 2019 vehicle-pedestrian accident and a collision history report. (FAC ¶ 19.) The court is not convinced this is sufficient under Brenner.[1] However, this defect is reasonably capable of curing.

 

 

Conclusion

 

The court SUSTAINS City’s demurrer with leave to amend.

Plaintiff shall file a second amended complaint within 30 days of this ruling.


[1] The court declines to develop the remaining arguments.


Case Number: 25NWCV04143    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: D

GERARDO MONTES V. DAVID S. BARCLAY

CASE NO.:  25NWCV04143

HEARING:  09/28/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#18

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

 

Defendant David S. Barclay’s motion to set aside entry of default is GRANTED.

 

Defendant David S. Barclay’s motion to quash service of summons is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant David S. Barclay moves to set aside entry of default.

 

Defendant David S. Barclay moves to quash service of summons and complaint.   

 

Background

 

This action arises from a dispute as to ownership of two parcels of property.

 

On November 20, 2025, plaintiff Gerardo Montes (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against defendants David S. Barclay (“Defendant”) and DOES 1 through 10, inclusive, for (1) Allocation of Appropriate Interest Attributable to Plaintiff for Home Improvements and Appreciation in the Value of Properties Due to Said Home Improvements, (2) Partition by Sale of Real Properties, and (3) Conversion.

 

On May 12, 2026, Defendant was served with, inter alia, the summons and the complaint via personal service at 67710 Rio Arapaho Road, Cathedral City, CA 92234. (See 05/18/26 Proof of Service of Summons.) The proof of service is executed by a registered process server. (Id.)

 

On June 12, 2026, at the request of Plaintiff, default was entered against Defendant. (06/12/26 Request for Entry of Default.)

 

On June 13, 2026, Jeffrey Orr Law, PC substituted in as counsel of record for Defendant. (See 07/13/26 Substitution of Attorney.) Defendant was previously self-represented. (Id.)

 

On July 31, 2026, Defendant filed a: (1) motion to set aside entry of default; and (2) motion to quash service of summons (collectively, the “Motions”).

 

On September 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to the Motions. Any opposition was required to have been filed and served at least nine court days before the hearing. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).) Although the opposition is untimely, the Court exercises its discretion and will consider the untimely opposition. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1300(d).)

 

The Court will address the Motions in this ruling.

 

Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default

 

Defendant moves to set aside the entry of default on the following grounds: (1) the default is void because Defendant was never validly served with the summons and the complaint; (2) the default was taken against Defendant where service did not result in actual notice in time to defend, through no avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect; and (3) alternatively, the default was the product of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. (Not. of Mot. at p. 2:1-8.)

 

A.    Legal Standard

 

“The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) “Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding was taken.” (Ibid. [italics added].) 

 

“The court may, upon motion of the injured party . . . set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) “[C]ompliance with the statutory procedures for service of process is essential to establish personal jurisdiction.” (Ellard v. Conway (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 540, 544 [citation omitted].) “[A] default judgment entered against a defendant who was not served with the summons in the manner prescribed by statute is void.” (Ibid. [citation omitted].) Code of Civil Procedure § 473(d) empowers “the court [to] . . . set aside a default judgment which is valid on its face, but void, as a matter of law, due to improper service.” (Ibid.)

 

“When service of a summons has not resulted in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action and a default or default judgment has been entered against him or her in the action, he or she may file a notice of motion to set aside the default or default judgment and for leave to defend the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (a).) “The notice of motion shall be served and filed within a reasonable time, but in no event exceeding the earlier of: (i) two years after entry of a default judgment against him or her; or (ii) 180 days after service on him or her of a written notice that the default or default judgment has been entered.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (a).)  

 

Personal service is accomplished when a copy of the summons and complaint is delivered to the person to be served. (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.10.)¿¿ 

 

“In lieu of personal delivery of a copy of the summons and complaint to the person to be served . . . a summons may be served by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint during usual office hours in his or her office or, if no physical address is known, at his or her usual mailing address, other than a United States Postal Service post office box, with the person who is apparently in charge thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and complaint by first-class mail, postage prepaid to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (a).)¿ 

 

“It is well settled that appellate courts have always been and are favorably disposed toward such action upon the part of the trial courts as will permit, rather than prevent, the adjudication of legal controversies upon their merits.”  (Benjamin v. Dalmo Mfg. Co. (1948) 31 Cal.2d 523, 525.) “[T]he filing of a proof of service creates a rebuttable presumption that . . . service was proper.” (Dill v. Berquist Construction Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1441.) “[A] registered process server’s declaration establishes a presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence of the facts stated in the declaration.” (American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)   

 

B.    The Default Should Be Set Aside

 

Initially, the Court notes that no proposed answer or other pleading is attached to the declarations in support of the motion. The Court has reviewed all documents in support of the Motions, and no proposed answer or other responsive pleading has been filed. Accordingly, such fact precludes relief from entry of default under Code Civ. Proc. § 473(b).

 

Defendant attests that he was never personally served with the summons or the complaint in this action. (Declaration of David S. Barclay Filed July 21, 2026 (“Barclay Decl.”) ¶ 4.) No one ever attempted to leave the summons and complaint with any member of Defendant’s household or any person apparently in charge of his residence or place of business. (Id. ¶ 5.) The summons and the complaint were not mailed to Defendant’s residence. (Id. ¶ 6.) Defendant’s partner found a rolled-up stack of papers on the front porch of Defendant’s residence. (Id. ¶ 9.) Defendant contends that he has a meritorious defense to this action. (Id. ¶ 21.) Defendant attests that granting the Motions will not prejudice Plaintiff. (Id. ¶ 22.)

 

Jonathan Fuh, who is Defendant’s partner, provides a declaration and attests, inter alia, that “[o]n either Wednesday, May 13, 2026, or Thursday, May 14, 2026, [he] observed a rolled-up stack of papers, stapled together, sitting unattended on the front porch of the residence” he shares with Defendant. (Declaration of Jonathan Fuh Filed July 21, 2026 (“Fuh Decl.”) ¶ 4.) No process server, sheriff, marshal, or other person appeared at their residence and attempted to serve Defendant. (Id. ¶ 5.) No one ever asked Mr. Fuh to accept service on behalf of Defendant. (Id. ¶ 6.) No copy of the summons or complaint in this action was ever delivered to Defendant by mail, personally, or otherwise served upon him. (Id. ¶ 9.)

 

On opposition, Jonathan Charles Kraut (“Kraut”) provides a declaration. The declaration of Mr. Kraut relevantly recounts the filing of the complaint, sets forth the contents of the proof of service as to the summons and the complaint, and provides a copy of the request for entry of default form. (Kraut Decl. ¶¶ 1-3, Exs. A, B, C.)

 

The Court finds that relief is not warranted under Code Civ. Proc. § 473.5 as Defendant reviewed the summons and the complaint, at the latest, on May 14, 2026. (Barclay Decl. ¶ 8.) The Court recognizes that Defendant did not learn of the request for entry of default until June 18, 2026. (Barclay Decl. ¶¶ 19-20.) However, Defendant had actual notice in time to defend this action.  

 

Nevertheless, the Court finds that setting aside the default is proper under Code Civ. Proc. § 473(d).

 

The declarations of Defendant and Mr. Fuh collectively establish that service was not properly effectuated. Under Ellard, supra, 94 Cal.App.4th 540, 544, the default should be set aside. Based on the evidence presented in support of the Motions, Defendant was not served with the summons and the complaint either personally or by substitute service. The declaration of Attorney Kraut does not lead the Court to a finding that service was proper. For instance, Plaintiff has not provided a declaration from the registered process server in support of the opposition. Thus, relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d) is appropriate. Due to the lack of proper service, the default is void.

 

Accordingly, the motion to set aside entry of default is GRANTED.

 

 

 

 

Motion to Quash Service of Summons

 

A.    Legal Standard

 

A defendant who takes the position that the service of summons as made upon him did not bring him within the jurisdiction of the court, may serve and file a notice of motion to quash the service.” (Coulston v. Cooper (1966) 245 Cal.App.2d 866, 868.) “The effect of such notice is to place upon the plaintiff the burden of proving the facts that did give the court jurisdiction, that is the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Ibid.) “A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion . . . [t]o quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1).) 

 

B. Quashing Service of the Summons and the Complaint is Proper

 

The Court references its recitation of the evidence and its analysis from above and incorporates such recitation herein. Defendant is contesting service. Plaintiff therefore has “the burden of proof . . . to establish the facts of jurisdiction by a preponderance of the evidence.” (Aquila, Inc. v. Superior Court (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 556, 568.) The Court finds that the declarations in support of the Motions establish that service was improper. Finally, Plaintiff’s reliance on Trackman v. Kenney (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 175 is inapposite.

 

The Court therefore GRANTS the motion to quash service of summons.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant David S. Barclay’s motion to set aside entry of default is GRANTED.

 

Defendant David S. Barclay’s motion to quash service of summons is GRANTED. 

Case Number: 26NWCV01981    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: D

CHRISTOPHER GOMEZ V. NORDSTROM INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV01981

HEARING:  09/28/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#14

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

 

Defendant Nordstrom Inc.’s demurrer to the third cause of action for Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress is SUSTAINED WITH 30 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Defendant Nordstrom Inc.’s motion to strike is GRANTED WITH 30 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Plaintiff Christopher Gomez shall file and serve an amended complaint within 30 days of this ruling.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Nordstrom Inc. (Defendant) demurs to the third cause of action for Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress in the Complaint filed by Plaintiff Christopher Gomez (Plaintiff).

 

Defendant also moves to strike punitive damages allegations.  

 

Background

 

This action arises from an alleged assault and battery that occurred at or near 300 Los Cerritos Center, Cerritos, CA 90703 (the “Subject Premises”).  On June 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against defendants Nordstrom Inc. (“Defendant”), Macerich Cerritos, LLC (“MCL”), John Doe (“Doe”), and DOES 1 to 100, inclusive, alleging the following causes of action: (1) Assault; (2) Battery; (3) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress; (4) Negligence; and (5) Negligent Hiring, Supervision and Retention.

 

On September 4, 2026, at the request of Plaintiff, the Court dismissed defendant MCL without prejudice.

 

Legal Standard

 

As a general matter, in a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. ¿ (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)  “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.”  (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.)  As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Id.)  The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action.  (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment.  (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.)  The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully.  (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.)  However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.”  (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245). 

 

Plaintiff is only required to allege ultimate facts, not evidentiary facts.  (See Committee on Children's Television, Inc. v. General Foods Corp. (1983) 35 Cal.3d 197, 212 [“the complaint should set forth the ultimate facts constituting the cause of action, not the evidence by which plaintiff proposes to prove those facts”); 1 Cal. Affirmative Def. § 10:2 (2d ed.) [allegations of agency, course and scope of employment, etc. qualify as ultimate facts].)  Plaintiff’s allegations must be accepted as true on demurrer.  (See Yvanova v. New Century Mortgage Corp. (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919, 924 [“For purposes of reviewing a demurrer, we accept the truth of material facts properly pleaded in the operative complaint”].)

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, subdivision (a) requires the moving party to meet and confer “in person, by video conference, or by telephone” in an attempt to resolve a demurrer’s deficiencies five days before the responsive pleading is due.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(a)(2).)  Before filing a motion to strike, a similar meet and confer process must be effectuated.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a).)

 

The Court finds that Defendant has not fulfilled the meet and confer requirement.  (Farley Decls., ¶ 3, Ex. 1.) Meeting and conferring via email is not sufficient.

 

The Court will consider the merits of both the demurrer and the motion to strike. Defendant is reminded to comply with the requirements of the Code of Civil Procedure.

 

Demurrer

 

          Third Cause of Action – IIED

 

Defendant demurs to third cause of action for IIED on the grounds that Plaintiff: (1) has not met the “outrageous conduct” requirement for such claim; and (2) has not met the “severe emotional distress” element of such claim. Plaintiff contends that the third cause of action is sufficiently pleaded.

 

“A cause of action for IIED requires proof of: (1) extreme and outrageous conduct by the defendant with the intention of causing, or reckless disregard of the probability of causing, emotional distress; (2) the plaintiff suffered severe emotional distress; and (3) the defendant's extreme and outrageous conduct was the actual and proximate cause of the severe emotional distress.”  (Crouch v. Trinity Christian Center of Santa Ana, Inc. (2019) 39 Cal.App.5th 995, 1007.) Conduct, to be “outrageous” must be “so extreme as to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized community.”  (Ibid. [internal quotations omitted])  Similarly, “[s]evere emotional distress means emotional distress of such substantial quality or enduring quality that no reasonable [person] in civilized society should be expected to endure it.”  (Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, 1051 [internal quotations omitted].) ¿“ ‘[M]ere insults, indignities, threats, annoyances, petty oppressions, or other trivialities’ ” do not constitute extreme and outrageous conduct.”  (Okorie v. Los Angeles Unified School Dist. (2017) 14 Cal.App.5th 574, 597 [disapproved of by Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System (2021) 11 Cal.5th 995 on other grounds].) 

 

Plaintiff alleges, inter alia, that on July 22, 2024, Plaintiff was browsing the fragrance section at a Nordstrom store.  (Compl. ¶ 8.)  While examining a bottle of perfume, Plaintiff was approached by defendant Doe, a security guard employed at the Subject Premises.  (Id. ¶ 8.) Defendant Doe demanded that Plaintiff surrender the perfume, to which Plaintiff complied.  (Id.)  Plaintiff did so “immediately . . . without hesitation, resistance, or argument.”  (Id.)  Plaintiff alleges that defendant Doe suddenly and violently shoved Plaintiff against a glass wall with substantial force.  (Id.)  Plaintiff alleges that the glass wall was neither secure nor properly maintained.  (Id.)  Plaintiff suffered physical injuries and damages.  (Id. ¶ 9.)

 

The Court finds that the third cause of action for IIED is insufficiently alleged.  (Compl. ¶¶ 34-43.)  The mere act of physical contact, without more, does not give rise to extreme and outrageous conduct.  Additionally, Plaintiff has not pleaded facts showing that Plaintiff suffered “emotional distress of such substantial or enduring quality . . . .”  (Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1227.)  The allegation that Plaintiff has suffered “severe and extreme emotional distress including . . . highly unpleasant mental suffering and anguish” is insufficient to meet the pleading standard. Plaintiff has not set forth the nature of the emotional distress sustained.  (See Bogard v. Employers Casualty Co. (1985) 164 Cal.App.3d 602, 617.)

 

Accordingly, the Court SUSTAINS the Demurrer to Third Cause of Action for IIED with 30 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Motion to Strike

 

In order to¿state¿a prima¿facie¿claim for punitive damages, a complaint must set forth the elements as¿stated¿in the general punitive damage statute, Civil Code section 3294.  (College Hospital, Inc. v. Superior Court¿(1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 721.) These¿statutory elements include allegations that the defendant has been guilty of oppression,¿fraud¿or malice.  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).)  California¿Civil Code Section¿3294¿authorizes punitive damages upon a showing of malice, fraud, or oppression.  Malice is defined as either “conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff,” or “despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.”  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(1).)¿

 

“Despicable conduct is conduct which is so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people.”  (Mock v. Michigan Millers Mutual Ins. Co. (1992) 4 Cal. App. 4th 306, 331.)  California¿Civil Code Section 3294(c)(2) defines oppression as “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights.”  Fraud under California¿Civil Code Section 3294(c)(3) “means an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.”  Specific facts must be pled in support of punitive damages.¿ (Hillard v. A.H. Robins Co. (1983) 148 Cal.App.3d 374, 391-392.)¿¿¿¿

 

“An employer shall not be liable for [punitive] damages ..., based upon acts of an employee of the employer, unless the employer had advance knowledge of the unfitness of the employee and employed him or her with a conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others or authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct.... With respect to a corporate employer, the advance knowledge and conscious disregard, authorization, [or] ratification ... must be on the part of an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporation.”  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (b).)¿ The¿Supreme Court of California¿interpreted the latter statement as requiring the officer, director, or managing agent to¿be someone who¿“exercise[s] substantial discretionary authority over decisions that ultimately determine corporate policy.”  (White v. Ultramar, Inc.¿(1999) 21 Cal.4th 563, 577.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

The Court finds that the punitive damages allegations are insufficient.  Defendant is a corporation.  (Compl. ¶ 2.)  Plaintiff has not pleaded specific facts showing any ratification or advanced knowledge of the unfitness of defendant Doe as to Defendant.  Moreover, Plaintiff has not alleged that the conduct at issue was perpetuated by an officer, director, or managing agent of Defendant.  Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged oppression, malice, or fraud of Defendant.

 

Thus, the Court finds it appropriate to strike the punitive damages allegations.  The motion to strike is GRANTED WITH 30 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Nordstrom Inc.’s Demurrer to the Third Cause of Action for IIED is SUSTAINED with 30 days leave to amend.

 

Defendant Nordstrom Inc.’s Motion to Strike is GRANTED with 30 days leave to amend.

 

Plaintiff Christopher Gomez shall file and serve an amended complaint within 30 days of this ruling.
