ANALYSIS:

 

            Carlos Manuel Vanegas sued Anthony Busse and Meagan Busse.  The Busses failed to respond, and Vanegas obtained a default against them in May 2026.  The Busses now move to have the default set aside under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), subdivision (d), or section 473.5.

 

            Given that the Busses primarily argue that service of process was invalid, the Court starts with Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d). 

 

            In July 2025, a registered process server declared that after three attempts at serving Anthony and Meagan personally, he left copies of the summons, complaint, and various other case-opening materials for the Busses at the address “15601 Vandorf Pl, Encino, CA 91436” with “John Doe” as grandfather/co-occupant.  The server then mailed those materials to the Busses at the same address.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (b) [if a person can’t be personally served with reasonable diligence, he can be served in this manner].)  The process server’s declaration is prima facie evidence that Vanegas served the Busses.  (Evid. Code, § 647; see Lee v. Yan (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 975, 978.)  That places the burden on the Busses to offer evidence to the contrary.  (Palm Property Investments, LLC v. Yadegar (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 1419, 1428.)

 

            The Busses contend that the John Doe was not a “competent member of the household” as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 415.20, subdivision (b).  They argue that there was no co-occupant residing at the property and no person present with authority to accept service on their behalf:  The person present at the residence on the date of alleged service was only temporarily overseeing the home while they were on vacation.  Anthony attests that on the date of the attempted substitute service, he and Meagan were on vacation, his father-in-law was temporarily overseeing the residence, and that his father-in-law is not an occupant of the residence.  Additionally, father-in-law Steve Rojas attests that he was temporarily overseeing the Busses residence on the alleged service date, is not an occupant of the residence, and that “no copies of any documents, including the Summons or Complaint were left at the residence or within my possession.”  Anthony also attests that he did not receive any documents in the mail.  Furthermore, the process server’s proofs of service indicate that he was informed the Busses were “not home [as they were] on vacation,” two days before substitute service was purportedly effected. 

 

            These above facts demonstrate that substitute service was invalid as Rojas, “John Doe,” was not a “competent member of the [Busse] household” and no documents were mailed as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 415.20, subdivision (b).  Vanegas did not file an opposition arguing otherwise.  Accordingly, the Busses are entitled to relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) for invalid service of process.

 

            Given the above disposition, the Court need not address the Busses’ requests for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) or section 473.5.

 

            Thus, the Court GRANTS the Busses motion.  The May 6, 2026 default is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.  The Court ORDERS Venegas to SHOW CAUSE, on Thursday, October 15, 2026 at 9:30 a.m., in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse, why service has not been effected on the Busses.  Filing of proper proofs of service will discharge this order.

 

            The Busses are to give notice.


Case Number: 25STLC05354    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Thurs., August 13, 2026                                 JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           American Credit Acceptance LLC v. Crawford

COMP. FILED:         07-14-25

CASE NUMBER:      25STLC05354                                  

NOTICE:                  OK                                                                 

                                                                                   

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION FOR ORDER AUTHORIZING SERVICE OF SUMMONS AND COMPLAINT BY ELECTRONIC MAIL PURSUANT TO CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE SECTION 413.30

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff American Credit Acceptance LLC

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

MOTION FOR ORDER PERMITTING ALTERNATIVE SERVICE OF PROCESS

(Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

American Credit Acceptance LLC sued Theresa Crawford for claim and delivery of personal property, for per-trial writ of possession, and an order directing transfer of personal property and restraining order.  American Credit now moves for an order authorizing Crawford to be served with the summons and complaint by electronic mail pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 413.30.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 413.30 provides that “[i]f a plaintiff, despite exercising reasonable diligence” has been unable to serve the summons by any of the four usual methods of service—personal, substitute, mail with acknowledgement, and publication—the court may allow the summons be served in “a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the party to be served, including by electronic mail or other electronic technology.”  (Id., § 413.30, subd. (a)(1); Crane v. Dolihite (2021) 70 Cal.App.5th 772, 786.)  A plaintiff seeking to establish reasonable diligence must “set forth facts detailing all attempts to serve the defendant by each of the methods prescribed by statute, including facts demonstrating why each method was unsuccessful at every address or location where the defendant is likely to be found.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(2).)

 

American Credit contends it attempted to personally serve Crawford at three different addresses found by skip trace but was unable to effect serve at any of them.  American Credit also argues it sent a demand letter to Crawford’s purported email address but received no response.  However, American Credit does not provide the skip trace indicating the relevant addresses.  While there are declarations of reasonable diligence at three addresses, American Credit has failed to demonstrate reasonable diligence as defined by Code of Civil Procedure section 413.30 because it has not exhausted nor shown why it is impossible to perform the other methods of service prescribed by Code of Civil Procedure sections 415.10, et seq. 

 

            Accordingly, the Court DENIES American Credit’s motion for an order permitting service by electronic mail.
