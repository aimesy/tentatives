DEPARTMENT 735 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV04366    Hearing Date: August 26, 2026    Dept: 735

Chung, et al. v. Lee, et al.

Motion for Attorney Fees and Costs

 

BACKGROUND

In February 2024, Plaintiff Jae Min Chung filed this derivative action as an individual and on behalf of members of the Los Angeles Korean 1st Presbyterian Church Corporation. In October 2024, the Court denied Plaintiff’s motion for a preliminary injunction. She filed a Notice of Appeal of that order the same day. On April 7, 2025, Plaintiff’s appeal was dismissed at Plaintiffs’ request. In its remittitur, the Court of Appeal ordered that “[c]osts, if any, are awarded pursuant to California Rules of Court Rule 8.278.”

Defendant Hwa Chong Kang filed a motion for attorneys’ fees and costs on May 12, 2025, seeking $24,305.00 in attorneys’ fees and $24,305.00 in costs based on Civil Code section 1717, Rule of Court 3.1702, and a provision contained in the disputed Deed of Trust recorded January 10, 2023. Plaintiff opposes the motion, arguing, among other things, that it is premature.

DISCUSSION

            Whether or not fees are recoverable based on Civil Code section 1717, principles of reciprocity, or relevant Rules of Court, Defendant’s motion is denied without prejudice as premature.

As to costs: costs are recoverable after dismissal of an appeal pursuant to Rule of Court 8.278. Rule 8.278 applies the ordinary method for recovery of costs: a prevailing party awarded costs on appeal must file a memorandum of costs, which is subject to a motion to tax or strike. (Cal. Rule of Court, Rule 8.278(c)(1).) Defendant’s demand for costs by noticed motion does not conform to the proper procedure. It is not plain that Defendant is the prevailing party after Plaintiff’s voluntary dismissal. Regardless, for purposes of the appeal only, Defendant filed no memorandum, which is the proper method for recovery. The Court defers the question whether costs may be awarded to Defendant as a prevailing party or credited against any recovery by Plaintiff until after rendition of judgment.

            Attorneys’ fees incurred in an interim appeal are similarly not awardable until after entry of final judgment. (Chen v. Valstock Ventures, LLC (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 957, 970-983.) Plaintiff appealed the Court’s order on a preliminary injunction; the dismissal of the appeal did not finally resolve any claim against any party. Defendant’s request for prevailing party fees is premature.

CONCLUSION 

The court denies Defendant’s motion for attorney fees and costs without prejudice.

Plaintiff shall give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV19549    Hearing Date: August 26, 2026    Dept: 735

Leiva, et al. v. FCA US LLC, et al.

Defendant FCA US LLC’s Demurrer to Plaintiffs’ Complaint

 

Plaintiffs filed their complaint on June 30, 2025, asserting six claims arising from their purchase of a 2019 Jeep Cherokee on March 25, 2019. (Compl., ¶ 7.) Defendant FCA US LLC demurs to Plaintiffs’ sixth cause of action for fraudulent concealment. Instead of opposing the demurrer or timely filing as a matter of right a first amended complaint due on or before the due date for plaintiff’s opposition, plaintiff filed a late first amended complaint. The Court grants Defendant’s request to strike the untimely first amended complaint, but the court deems that first amended complaint to be Plaintiffs’ request for leave to amend.

DISCUSSION

            As pled, Plaintiffs’ claim is untimely. The statute of limitations for fraud is three years. (Code Civ. Proc., § 338(d).) The only operative date identified in Plaintiffs’ pleading is the date they purchased the Subject Vehicle, in March 2019. Without further facts establishing some other date of accrual or an applicable tolling doctrine, Plaintiffs’ claim is time-barred on its face as of March 2022. Plaintiffs filed their complaint in 2025, after the limitations period expired.

            The Court’s ruling does not depend on the economic loss doctrine or Plaintiffs’ failure to plead a transactional or fiduciary relationship, neither of which arguments is persuasive. (See Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 843-845 [economic loss rule]; OCM Principal Opportunities Fund, L.P. v. CIBC World Markets Corp. (2007) 157 Cal.App.4th 835, 859 [duty to disclose].) The demurrer is sustained solely based on the statute of limitations.

CONCLUSION 

The court sustains Defendant’s demurrer with 30 days’ leave to amend.

Defendant shall give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV31438    Hearing Date: August 26, 2026    Dept: 735

Supply & Demand, Inc. v. Kaye, et al.

Motion to Compel Defendant Anthony Kaye’s Further Responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One

 

On June 11, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel Defendant Anthony Kaye to provide further responses to Requests for Production, Set One. Kaye’s opposition filed on August 13, 2026, contends Plaintiff’s motion is largely moot because Kaye served supplemental responses on August 13 and, in any event, the responses subject to this motion were Code-compliant in any case. Plaintiff filed a reply on August 19, 2026, arguing Kaye’s responses remain insufficient and the substance of the motion therefore remains unresolved.

Effect of Supplemental Responses

The Court does not accept Kaye’s unilateral assertion that Plaintiff’s motion is moot, particularly given that Plaintiff’s reply contends otherwise. Plaintiff’s motion is properly and timely directed to the responses that were operative when the motion was filed and served. If Kaye’s recent supplement satisfies the instant order in certain respects, the parties can resolve the issue via meeting and conferring after the hearing.

The Court rules as follows on the Requests at issue.

RPD No. 1

            Denied. The objection based on overbreadth is sustained.

RPD No. 2

            Granted. The objections are overruled, except those based on privilege and work-product. If the response remains one of inability to comply, it must specify whether the documents have been lost or whether they never existed.

RPD No. 3

            Granted. The objections are overruled, except those based on privilege and work-product. If the response remains one of inability to comply, improper qualifications to that response must be omitted.

RPD Nos. 4-5

            Granted. The objections are overruled, except those based on privilege and work-product, and any documents withheld on that basis must be identified in a privilege log. The contention that “certain documents might be produced” subject to a protective order is not a response. If Kaye required a protective order, he should have filed a motion.

RPD No. 6

            Granted. The objections are overruled, except those based on privilege and work-product, and any documents withheld on that basis must be identified in a privilege log.

RPD Nos. 7-16

            The Court makes the same order as for RPDs Nos. 4-5.

RPDs Nos. 19-20

            The Court makes the same order as for RPD No. 6.

SANCTIONS

            A party prevailing on a motion to compel further discovery responses is entitled to monetary sanctions unless its opponent demonstrates substantial justification or other injustice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(h).) Plaintiff has prevailed almost entirely on its motion, and Kaye has failed to demonstrate substantial justification for his failure to provide timely substantive responses to any of Plaintiff’s requests, even where he agreed that “certain documents might be produced.” Plaintiff is entitled to sanctions.

            Plaintiff seeks $7,552.50 in sanctions against Kaye and his counsel, jointly and severally. Plaintiff has not demonstrated sanctions against counsel are warranted, and the amount Plaintiff demands is excessive. The Court awards Plaintiff 5.0 hours for motion, opposition, and reply, plus 1.0 hour to appear, at Plaintiff’s counsel’s $795.00 hourly rate, for total sanctions of $4,770.00 against defendant Anthony Kaye only.

CONCLUSION 

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion in part, on the terms set forth above.

Defendant Anthony Kaye is ordered to provide supplemental responses consistent with this Order and pay $4,770.00 to Plaintiff and/or its counsel of record within 30 days of this order.

Plaintiff to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV31552    Hearing Date: August 26, 2026    Dept: 735

Alesio v. Estate of Ulrich F. Keppler, et al.

Defendant’s Motion to Compel Compliance with Subpoena for Production of Personal Records from Peter J. Weingold, M.D. and Sanctions against Plaintiff

 

DISCUSSION

            The motion is denied for failure to give proper notice to the third-party deponent. For a nonparty subpoena to be effective, a deposition subpoena must be personally served. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.220(b).) Personal service is a condition both for enforcement of the subpoena and for attendance at any court session purporting to enforce the subpoena. (Id., subd. (c).) The Rules of Court require that “[a] written notice and all moving papers supporting a motion to compel an answer to a deposition question or to compel production of a document or tangible thing from a nonparty deponent ... be personally served on the nonparty deponent unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail or electronic service at an address or electronic service address specified on the deposition record.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1346.)

It is undisputed that the deponent is not a party to this action. Defendant’s exhibits do not establish the subpoena was personally served. (See Aldover Decl. Exh. A, p. 4 [blank proof of service].). The proof of service for Defendant’s motion indicates the deponent was served by U.S. Mail.

CONCLUSION 

The court denies Defendant’s motion to compel and for sanctions.

Defendant to give notice.
