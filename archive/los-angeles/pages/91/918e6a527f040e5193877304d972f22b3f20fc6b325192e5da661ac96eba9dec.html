DEPARTMENT 400 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV30997    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 400


The Court tenders the following tentative decision in the matter Elena Lohr v. Kathy Barbata, Los Angeles County Superior Court case number 23STCV30997, set for hearing on September 24, 2026. Elena Lohr (Plaintiff) moves for orders compelling Kathy Barbata (Defendant) to provide responses to Plaintiff’s form interrogatories, set two (FROGs) and requests for admissions, set three (RFAs). Plaintiff also requests a total $7,120 in sanctions. Plaintiff’s motion to compel FROGs is granted and her motion to compel RFAs is denied. The Court imposes $1,175 in sanctions on Defendant’s counsel.

 

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, that party waives any objection, the party making the demand may move for an order compelling response, and the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party who unsuccessfully opposes such a motion unless they acted with substantial justification. (Id., § 2030.290.) And if a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response, that party waives any objection, “[t]he requesting party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted,” and the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party who unsuccessfully opposes such a motion unless they acted with substantial justification. (Id., § 2033.280.)

 

            Here, Plaintiff filed her original complaint on December 20, 2023. She served all relevant written discovery requests via email on June 3, 2026. (Schell Decl. ¶ 5, Ex. A.) Defendant’s responses were due within 30 days. On July 13, Plaintiff sent a meet and confer letter. (Id. ¶ 6.) Defendant did not respond as of July 29. (Id. ¶ 7.) Because of Defendant’s failure to respond, Plaintiff’s motion to compel discovery pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 is proper. However, Plaintiff’s motion to compel responses to RFAs is improper, as section 2033.280 does not provide for such relief. Plaintiff moves for Defendant to provide verified and substantive responses without objection pursuant to section 2033.280 in her notice of motion. But section 2033.280 only allows for an order deeming the RFAs admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) Plaintiff’s only reference to the proper relief is contained within her description of the statute later in the motion. The notice of motion, introduction, and conclusion all request that the Court order Defendant to provide responses. Thus, Plaintiff’s motion related to RFAs is improper because she has not provided sufficient notice of the relief sought. Accordingly, only Plaintiff’s motion to compel FROGs is granted.

 

            “The court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).) Such sanctions are authorized even when no opposition to a motion to compel is filed. (Rules of Court, rule 3.1348(a).) Here, Defendant has offered no brief in opposition or explanation as to why responses were not provided. Thus, sanctions are warranted with respect to Plaintiff’s motion regarding FROGs.

 

Plaintiff’s counsel alleges an hourly rate of $500, which the Court credits. (Schell Decl. ¶ 9.) Counsel testifies to having spent 3 hours drafting the present motion, 1 hour gathering exhibits, 1 hour of anticipated preparation for the hearing, and 2 hours for attending the hearing. Because of the level of experience indicated by counsel’s hourly rate, the Court finds these hourly totals to be inflated. The Court credits counsel with 1 hour for preparing the simple motion, 0.25 hours for gathering exhibits, 0.1 hours for preparing for the hearing, and 1 hour for attending the hearing. Accordingly, the Court awards $1,175 in sanctions ([$500 per hour] x [2.35 hours] = $1,175).

Conclusion:

 

            The Court grants Plaintiff’s motion to compel FROGs. It denies Plaintiff’s motion to compel RFAs. The Court imposes $1,175 in sanctions on Defendant’s counsel, payable to Plaintiff.  

Case Number: 25STCV20008    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 400


       The Court tenders the following tentative decision in the matter Laura Alcantar, et al. v. Uber Technologies, Inc., et al., Los Angeles County Superior Court case number 25STCV20008, set for hearing on September 24, 2026. Uber Technologies, Inc., Rasier, LLC, and Rasier-CA, LLC (collectively, Defendants) move for an order compelling Laura Alcantar and Edell Dawkins (collectively, Plaintiffs) to submit to binding arbitration and staying the present action pending completion of arbitration, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2. The motion is granted.

 

A.    Legal Standard

 

“On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party thereto refuses to arbitrate such controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists, unless it determines that: (a) The right to compel arbitration has been waived by the petitioner; or (b) Grounds exist for the revocation of the agreement.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1281.2, subds. (a), (b).)

 

A proceeding to compel arbitration is in essence a suit in equity to compel specific performance of a contract. (Freeman v. State Farm Mutual Auto Insurance Co. (1975) 14 Cal.3d 473, 479.) Such enforcement may be sought by a party to the arbitration agreement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1280, subd. (e)(1).) A motion to compel arbitration requires the facts to be proven by affidavit or declaration and documentary evidence with oral testimony taken only in the court’s discretion. (Code Civ. Proc., §1290.2; Rosenthal v. Great Western Fin. Securities Corp. (1996) 14 Cal.4th 394, 413–414.) The motion must set forth the provisions of the written agreement and the arbitration clause verbatim, or such provisions must be attached and incorporated by reference. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1330; see Condee v. Longwood Mgmt. Corp. (2001) 88 Cal.App.4th 215, 218.) 

 

            Once the moving party alleges that an arbitration agreement exists, the burden shifts to the responding party to prove the falsity of the purported agreement, and no evidence or authentication is required to find the arbitration agreement exists. (See Condee, supra, 88 Cal.App.4th at p. 219.) However, if the existence of the agreement is challenged, “[movant] bears the burden of proving [the arbitration agreement’s] existence by a preponderance of the evidence.” (Rosenthal, supra, p. 413; see also Espejo v. Southern California Permanente Medical Group (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 1047, 1058–1060.)

 

B.     Discussion

 

Defendants argue that Plaintiffs should be compelled to arbitrate their claims against them pursuant to a written arbitration agreement (Agreement) between the parties. Defendants contend that the Agreement is governed by the Federal Arbitration Act (FAA), that it is valid and enforceable under that provision, and covers Plaintiffs’ claims. Plaintiffs have not filed a brief in opposition.

 

Defendants have presented evidence of an agreement to arbitrate which covers Plaintiffs’ claims.

 

Plaintiffs do not contest that Defendants have demonstrated the existence of a valid arbitration agreement. For a trial court to compel arbitration under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2, the moving party first bears the burden of producing “prima facie evidence of a written agreement to arbitrate the controversy.” (Rosenthal, supra, 14 Cal.4th 394 at p. 413.) This burden can be met by attaching a copy of the agreement purporting to be signed by the opposing party. (Gamboa v. Northeast Community Clinic (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 158, 165 (Gamboa).) Alternatively, the moving party may set forth the agreement’s provisions in the motion. (Ibid.) If the opposing party disputes the agreement, it bears the burden of producing evidence to challenge its authenticity. (Ibid.) The opposing party may “testify under oath or declare under penalty of perjury that the party never saw or does not remember seeing the agreement, or that the party never signed or does not remember signing the agreement.” (Ibid.) In that case, the moving party must then prove the existence of the agreement by a preponderance of the evidence. (Id. at pp. 165–166.)

 

Here, Defendants demonstrate that Plaintiffs have agreed to Defendants’ terms of use on various occasions. (Pare Decl. ¶¶ 18, 26.) The arbitration provision within these terms applies to disputes, claims, or controversies which occurred before or after the date the Plaintiffs agreed to the terms. (Id., Exs. G, K.) Under the agreement, Plaintiffs and Defendants agreed to arbitrate “any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to” the terms of use or Plaintiffs’ use of Defendants’ services. Plaintiffs allege negligence on the part of Defendants in providing the relevant service. (Compl.) Thus, Defendants have demonstrated the existence of an agreement to arbitrate the present controversy as required under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2. Plaintiffs offer no grounds for revocation of the agreement. Accordingly, Defendants’ motion is granted.

 

C.     Conclusion

 

The motion to compel arbitration is granted and this case is stayed pending outcome of the arbitration. 

Case Number: 26STCP02799    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 400


Case Name:  DRB CAPITAL, LLC vs A. J.

Case # 26STCP02799

 

PETITION TO TRANSFER STRUCTURED SETTLEMENT PAYMENT RIGHTS

 

Analysis

 

  1. Legal Standard

 

This Petition is governed by Insurance Code sections 10134–10139.5. (See also 321 Henderson Receivables Origination LLC v. Sioteco (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 1059, 1066.) Under Insurance Code section 10137, a transfer of structured settlement payment rights is void unless a court reviews and approves the transfer and finds the following conditions are met:

 

                        (a) The transfer of the structured settlement payment rights is

fair and reasonable and in the best interest of the payee, taking

into account the welfare and support of his or her dependents.

 

                        (b) The transfer complies with the requirements of this article,

will not contravene other applicable law, and the court has reviewed

and approved the transfer as provided in Section 10139.5.

 

Pursuant to Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivision (a), the Court must make the following express findings as to a transfer of structured settlement payment rights:

 

                        (1) The transfer is in the best interest of the payee, taking

into account the welfare and support of the payee's dependents.

                       

(2) The payee has been advised in writing by the transferee to

seek independent professional advice regarding the transfer

and has either received that advice or knowingly waived that

advice in writing.

 

(3) The Petitioner has complied with the notification requirements pursuant to Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivision (f)(2), and

transferee has provided the payee with a disclosure form

that complies with Section 10136 and the transfer agreement complies with sections 10136 and 10138.

                       

(4) The transfer does not contravene any applicable statute or the order

of any court or other government authority.

 

(5) The payee reasonably understands the terms of the transfer

agreement, including the terms set forth in the disclosure statement required by Section 10136.

 

(6) The payee reasonably understands and does not wish to exercise the payee's right to cancel the transfer agreement.

 

                  The transfer agreement is effective only upon approval in a final court order. (Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (a).) 

 

When determining whether the proposed transfer should be approved, including whether the transfer is fair, reasonable, and in the payee’s best interest, the Court shall consider the totality of the circumstances, including, but not limited to:

 

 (1) the reasonable preference and desire of the payee to complete the proposed transaction, taking into account the payee’s age, mental capacity, legal knowledge, and apparent maturity level;

 

(2) the stated purpose of the transfer;

 

(3) the payee’s financial and economic situation;

 

(4) the terms of the transaction, including whether the payee is transferring monthly or lump sum payments or all or a portion of his or her future payments;

 

(5) whether the future periodic payments were intended to pay for future medical care of the payee related to the incident that was the subject of the settlement;

 

(6) whether the payee has other means of income or support sufficient to meet the payee’s future financial obligations for support of payee’s dependents, such as child support;

 

(7) whether there were previous transactions involving payee’s structured settlement payments; and

 

(8) whether the payee and his or her dependents are facing a hardship situation. (Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (b).)

 

Procedurally, Insurance Code section 10136, subdivision (b) provides that ten or more days before the payee executes a transfer agreement, the transferee shall provide the payee with a separate written disclosure statement, accurately completed with the information that applies to the transfer agreement in at least 12-point type. 

 

The Court-approval process requires the transferee to file a petition in the county in which the transferor resides for approval of the transfer. (Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (f)(1).) In addition, the following elements are required to be served and filed not less than 20 days prior to the scheduled hearing on any application for approval of a transfer of structured settlement payment rights (id., § 10139.5, subd. (f)(2)):

 

(A) A copy of the transferee’s current and any prior petitions.

(B)  A copy of the transfer agreement.

(C)  A listing of each of the payee's dependents, together with each dependent's age.

(D) A copy of the disclosure required in subdivision (b) of section 10136.

(E)  A copy of the annuity contract.

(F)  A copy of any qualified assignment agreement.

(G) A copy of the underlying structured settlement agreement.

(H) Notification that any interested party is entitled to support, oppose, or otherwise respond to the transferee’s application, either in person or by counsel, by submitting written comments to the court or by participating in the hearing.

(I)    Notification of the time and place of the hearing and notification of the manner in which and the time by which written responses to the application must be filed, which may not be less than 15 days after service of the transferee’s notice, in order to be considered by the court.

(J)    If the payee entered into the structured settlement at issue within five years prior to the date of the transfer agreement, then the transferee shall provide notice to the payee’s attorney of record at the time the structured settlement was created, if the attorney is licensed to practice in California, at the attorney’s address on file with the State Bar of California. The notice shall be delivered by regular mail.

(K) Proof of service showing compliance with the notification requirements of this section.

 

Further, at the time of filing such a petition, the transferee shall file a copy of the petition with the California Attorney General. (Ins. Code, § 10139.) Lastly, the Court shall retain continuing jurisdiction to interpret and monitor the implementation of the transfer agreement as justice requires. (Id. § 10139.5, subd. (i).)

 

  1. Discussion

 

DRB Capital, LLC (Petitioner) seeks approval of the transfer of structured settlement payment rights to it by A. J. (Payee) in exchange for $110,726.60. (Ex. A.) Payee has agreed to sell the following settlement payments: 316 monthly payments of $1,500.00 commencing on or about September 10, 2034, and ending on or about December 10, 2060.

 

(Ex. A.)

 

The discounted present value of the payments is equal to $173,278.80. (Ex. A ¶ 3(f).) The annuity issuer is American General Life Insurance Company. (Ex. A.) The obligor with respect to the structured settlement payments is American General Assignment Corp. (Ibid.) The disclosure statement provided to Payee by Petitioner complies with section 10136, subdivision (b) and (c). (Petition, Ex. C.)

 

Petitioner provides a declaration from Payee in support. Payee is 30 years old, not married, has no minor children, and currently works as an Amazon Delivery driver. (Payee Decl. ¶¶ 1, 4.) Payee has previously assigned transfers of structured settlement payment rights. (Id. ¶ 7.) Within the last five years, Payee has attempted multiple previous transactions involving her structured settlement payment that were denied, dismissed, or withdrawn prior to a decision on the merits. (Ibid.) Payee would like to use the funds for satisfaction of debt, medical and dental procedures, and a downpayment on a house. (Id. ¶ 9.) Payee states that she understands the nature and terms of the transaction. (Id. ¶ 10.) Finally, Payee was advised to seek independent professional advice regarding the legal, tax, and financial implications of the transaction, did not seek such advice, and has waived any right or opportunity to seek and receive such advice. (Id. ¶ 12.)

 

Pursuant to Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivision (f)(2), the Petitioner has filed:

 

(A) A copy of the transferee’s current and any prior petitions. (YES, though no copies of other prior petitions have been filed because Petitioner could not locate them.)

(B)  A copy of the transfer agreement. (YES, Ex. A.)

(C)  A listing of each of the Payee’s dependents, together with each dependent's age. (NA.)

(D) A copy of the disclosure required in subdivision (b) of section 10136. (YES, Petition, Ex. C.)

(E)  A copy of the annuity contract. (YES, Ex. E.)

(F)  A copy of any qualified assignment agreement. (NA.)

(G) A copy of the underlying structured settlement agreement. (YES, Ex. E.)

(H) Notification that any interested party is entitled to support, oppose, or otherwise respond to the transferee’s application, either in person or by counsel, by submitting written comments to the court or by participating in the hearing. (YES.)

(I)    Notification of the time and place of the hearing and notification of the manner in which and the time by which written responses to the application must be filed, which may not be less than 15 days after service of the transferee's notice, in order to be considered by the court. (YES.)

(J)    If the payee entered into the structured settlement at issue within five years prior to the date of the transfer agreement, then the transferee shall provide notice to the payee’s attorney of record at the time the structured settlement was created, if the attorney is licensed to practice in California, at the attorney’s address on file with the State Bar of California. The notice shall be delivered by regular mail. (NA.)

(K) Proof of service showing compliance with the notification requirements of this section. (YES.)

 

The Court makes the following findings pursuant to Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivision (a):

 

            (1) The transfer is fair, reasonable, and in the best interest of Payee, taking into account the welfare and support of Payee’s dependents.

 

            (2) Payee has been advised in writing by the transferee to seek independent professional advice regarding the transfer and has either received that advice or knowingly waived that advice in writing. (Petition, Ex. E.)

 

            (3) Petitioner has provided Payee with a disclosure form that complies with Section 10136 and the transfer agreement complies with Sections 10136 and 10138. (Ex. C.)

 

            (4) The transfer does not contravene any applicable statute or the order of any court or other government authority.

 

            (5) Payee reasonably understands the terms of the transfer agreement, including the terms set forth in the disclosure statement required by Section 10136.

 

            (6) Payee reasonably understands and does not wish to exercise the payee's right to cancel the transfer agreement.

 

            (7)  Petitioner has provided a copy of the annuity contract and the qualified assignment agreement as required by Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivisions (f)(2)(E)–(F). (YES)

 

            Pursuant to Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivision (f)(2), the Petitioner has met the statutory requirements and served the required notice at least 20 days before the hearing. 

 

The Court should approve the transfer and retain continuing jurisdiction to interpret and monitor the implementation of the transfer agreement as justice requires, provided that Petitioner files the correct documents and Payee demonstrates that she understands the terms of the transaction. (Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (i).)

 

Conclusion:

 

            The Court grants the Petition and approves the transfer.
