Case Number: 24STCV05506    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 415

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

July 29, 2026

CASE NUMBER

24STCV05506

MOTION

Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Authorization

MOVING PARTY

Defendant Space Exploration Technologies Corporation

OPPOSING PARTIES

Plaintiff Michelle Dopak

 

MOTION

Defendant Space Exploration Technologies Corporation (“Defendant”) moves to compel Plaintiff Michelle Dopak (“Plaintiff”) to authorize the release of medical records.  Defendant Anthony Brooks joins the motion.  Plaintiff opposes the motion. 

ANALYSIS

Defendant cites no authority in the notice of motion under which the Court could grant the relief Defendant seeks. The Court cannot grant relief on grounds other than those set forth in the notice of motion.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1010 [“Notices must be in writing, and the notice of a motion, other than for a new trial, must state when, and the grounds upon which it will be made, and the papers, if any, upon which it is to be based”]; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1110(a) [“A notice of motion must state in the opening paragraph the nature of the order being sought and the grounds for issuance of the order”]; People v. American Surety Insurance Co. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 719, 726 [court concluded notice of motion made no reference to the fact of what the movant sought or the grounds for such relief, as required by rule 311(a) of the California Rules of Court, the predecessor to Rules of Court, rule 3.1110(a)].)  Defendant’s citation to  inapplicable statutes in the Civil Discovery Act is unavailing.

Consequently, the Court cannot grant Plaintiff’s motion, as it is defective.  

Moreover, no provision of the Civil Discovery Act permits one party to compel the other to sign an authorization for the release of records.  The Court does not have the power to create additional methods of discovery and will not do so here.  (See, e.g., San Diego Unified Port Dist. V. Douglas E. Barnhart, Inc. (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 1400, 1405 [court’s inherent power to exercise reasonable control over discovery matters did not authorize it to force a non-propounding party to pay for discovery it chose not to pursue].) 

Defendant’s reliance on Miranda v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 913 is misplaced.  The Court in that case did not consider the propriety of an order compelling a party to authorize the release of records.  (See id. at p. 918.)  “[A]n opinion is not authority for a proposition not therein considered.”  (Ginns v. Savage (1964) 61 Cal.2d 520, 524, fn. 2.)

The Court notes, however, were Defendant to serve requests for production of documents on Plaintiff for Plaintiff’s medical records, Plaintiff would be obligated to produce all documents in her “possession, custody, or control . . . .”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.220.)  As Plaintiff can obtain her own medical records, presumably Plaintiff would have to produce such documents in response to a request for production of documents from Defendant. 

Defendant is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling and file proof of service of same.

 


Case Number: 25STCV25586    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 415

           TENTATIVE RULING

 

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

July 29, 2026

CASE NUMBER

25STCV25586

MOTIONS

Demurrer to Complaint;
Motion to Strike Portions of Complaint

MOVING PARTY

Defendant S.E.S. International Express, Inc.

OPPOSING PARTY

Plaintiff Albatrans, Inc.

 

MOTION

Plaintiff Albatrans, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) brought the instant action against Defendant S.E.S. International Express, Inc. (“Defendant”), alleging Defendant received fraudulent payments from Plaintiff.  Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s complaint and moves to strike portions of it.  Plaintiff opposes the motions.

ANALYSIS

“It is black letter law that a demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.”  (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.)  In ruling on a demurrer, the court must “liberally construe[]” the allegations of the complaint.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)  “This rule of liberal construction means that the reviewing court draws inferences favorable to the plaintiff, not the defendant.”  (Perez v. Golden Empire Transit Dist. (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1238.)

Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading, may serve and file a motion to strike the whole pleading or any part thereof.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1322(b).)  On a motion to strike, the court may: (1) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the court.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (a)-(b); Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782.)

First and Second Causes of Action: Conversion and Civil Theft 

Money cannot be the subject of a cause of action for conversion unless Plaintiff alleges Defendant converted a specific, identifiable sum.  (PCO, Inc. v. Christensen, Miller, Fink, Jacobs, Glaser, Weil & Shapiro, LLP (2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 384, 395, 397.) 

In this regard, Plaintiff alleges Defendant received funds from Plaintiff through transfers from Plaintiff’s former employee, Defendant Richard Seng (“Seng”), who acted as  Defendant’s agent, and Defendant ratified and approved of Seng’s acts.  (Complaint, ¶¶ 5, 15, 20.)  Plaintiff also alleges Defendant converted a specific, identifiable sum.  (Id., ¶¶ 15, 22.)

The California Supreme Court has criticized generalized allegations as “egregious examples of generic boilerplate . . . .”  (Moore v. Regents of University of California (1990) 51 Cal.3d 120, 134, fn. 12.)  However, “such allegations may be necessary, especially at the outset of a lawsuit, before discovery.” (Barsegian v. Kessler & Kessler (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 446, 451, internal quotations and citations omitted.)  

Thus, the Court concludes in this instance the allegations are sufficient to thwart the challenge at this demurrer stage, as Defendant may obtain further information about Plaintiff’s claims through discovery.  (See, e.g., Lickiss v. Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 1125, 1135 [demurrers based on uncertainty are strictly construed “because ambiguities can reasonably be clarified under modern rules of discovery”]. ) 

The Court overrules the demurrer to the first and second causes of action.

Third and Fourth Causes of Action: Fraud and Deceit and Constructive Fraud 

Plaintiff must allege fraud with particularity.  “This means: (1) general pleading of the legal conclusion of fraud is insufficient; and (2) every element of the cause of action for fraud must be alleged in full, factually and specifically, and the policy of liberal construction of pleading will not usually be invoked to sustain a pleading that is defective in any material respect.”  (Wilhelm v. Pray, Price, Williams & Russell (1986) 186 Cal.App.3d 1324, 1331.)  “There are four circumstances in which nondisclosure or concealment may constitute actionable fraud: (1) when the defendant is in a fiduciary relationship with the plaintiff; (2) when the defendant had exclusive knowledge of material facts not known to the plaintiff; (3) when the defendant actively conceals a material fact from the plaintiff; and (4) when the defendant makes partial representations but also suppresses some material facts.”  (LiMandri v. Judkins (1997) 52 Cal.App.4th 326, 336, internal quotations and citations omitted.) 

In the instant case, Plaintiff alleges Seng transferred funds from Plaintiff to Defendant, while concealing from Plaintiff the funds were not for services Defendant provided.  (Complaint, ¶¶ 30-31.)  As Plaintiff alleges Seng was Plaintiff’s employee, Seng owed fiduciary duties to Plaintiff.  (Civ. Code, § 2322, subd. (c).)  Accordingly, Plaintiff adequately alleges Seng is liable for fraud.  As set forth above, Plaintiff also alleges Seng acted as Defendant’s agent and, thus, Defendant is liable for Seng’s fraud.  (Civ. Code, § 2338.)  Accordingly, the demurrer to the third and fourth causes of action is overruled.

Fifth Cause of Action: Money Had and Received 

To state a claim for money had and received, Plaintiff must allege Defendant is indebted to Plaintiff in a certain amount for money Defendant received for Plaintiff’s use.  (Farmers Ins. Exchange v. Zerin (1997) 53 Cal. App. 4th 445, 460.)  In this regard, Plaintiff alleges Defendant received money from Plaintiff through Seng, money which rightfully belongs to Plaintiff.  (Complaint, ¶ 45.)  Plaintiff has adequately alleged the cause of action for money had and received.  The demurrer as to this claim is overruled.

Eighth Cause of Action: Violation of Business and Professions Code Section 17200  

Plaintiff must allege a claim under Business and Professions Code section 17200 “with reasonable particularity . . . .”  (Khoury v. Maly's of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 619.)  Further, a claim under section 17200 must be “tethered to a[] constitutional, statutory or regulatory provision.”  (Bardin v. DaimlerChrysler Corp. (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 1255, 1273.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendant engaged in “unlawful business practices . . . .”  (Complaint, ¶ 59.)  However, Plaintiff does not allege facts to support this allegation with particularity.  Accordingly, the Court sustains the demurrer to the eighth cause of action with leave to amend.

Motion to Strike

Defendant moves to strike the punitive damages allegations.  

In ruling on a motion to strike punitive damages, “judges read allegations of a pleading subject to a motion to strike as a whole, all parts in their context, and assume their truth.”  (Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255.)  To state a prima facie claim for punitive damages, a plaintiff must allege the elements set forth in the punitive damages statute, Civil Code section 3294.  (College Hospital, Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 721 (College Hospital).)  Pursuant to Civil Code section 3294, subdivision (a), a plaintiff must allege the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud or malice.  “The mere allegation an intentional tort was committed is not sufficient to warrant an award of punitive damages. Not only must there be circumstances of oppression, fraud or malice, but facts must be alleged in the pleading to support such a claim.” (Grieves v. Superior Ct. (1984) 157 Cal.App.3d 159, 166, internal citations and footnotes omitted.)

As set forth above, Plaintiff adequately alleges Defendant, through its agent Seng, defrauded Plaintiff.  These allegations are of fraudulent conduct within the meaning of Civil Code section 3294.  Therefore, the Court concludes Plaintiff has alleged facts to support a claim for punitive damages, and the motion to strike the related allegations is denied.

Defendant also moves to strike the prayer for attorney fees.  As Defendant concedes, Plaintiff may base a claim for attorney fees on Plaintiff’s cause of action for civil theft.  (Motion to Strike, p. 3.)  Because Plaintiff adequately alleges that cause of action, its prayer for attorney fees suffices for pleading purposes.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant’s demurrer to the eighth cause of action is sustained with leave to amend.  Defendant’s demurrer is otherwise overruled.  Plaintiff is to file an amended complaint within 20 days of notice of this order.

Defendant’s motion to strike is denied.

Defendant is ordered to provide notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.

CASE NUMBER

25STCV36803B

MOTION 

Motion for Approval of Lis Pendens

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Jarett Cole

OPPOSING PARTY

None

 

MOTION

            Plaintiff Jarett Cole (“Plaintiff”) filed this motion for judicial approval of a lis pendens Plaintiff proposes to file. Defendant has not filed an opposition.

The Court hereby admonishes Plaintiff for the filing of overly long papers in this action. Pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1113(d), Plaintiff’s memorandum of points and authorities is limited to no more than 15 pages.  Yet, Plaintiff’s submission for this motion is more than two times the specified page limit.  Plaintiff did not seek dispensation from this Court, pursuant to Rules of Court, rule 3.1113(e), for filing a memorandum in excess of the page limit.  The Court advises Plaintiff if this persists, the Court will only consider submissions up to the page limit prescribed in the rules.

ANALYSIS

On request of a self-represented litigant, the Court may approve a lis pendens for filing.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.21.)  In determining whether to approve the lis pendens, the Court must “confirm that an action containing a real property claim . . . is pending,” and determine that the lis pendens is “proper in form . . .”  (Real Property Law Section Comments on Code Civ. Proc., § 405.21.)  For purposes of this statute, a “real property claim” is a cause of action in a pleading “which would, if meritorious, affect (a) title to, or the right to possession of specific real property, or (b) the use of an easement identified in the pleading ….”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.4.) 

Plaintiff’s complaint asserts causes of action for tenant harassment, retaliation, breach of the covenant of  quiet enjoyment, breach of the warranty of habitability, and intentional infliction of emotional distress.  If Plaintiff prevails on these claims, the title and possession of the property at issue will not change.  Instead, Plaintiff would obtain damages. 

Accordingly, the motion for judicial approval of the lis pendens is denied.

Plaintiff is to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.
