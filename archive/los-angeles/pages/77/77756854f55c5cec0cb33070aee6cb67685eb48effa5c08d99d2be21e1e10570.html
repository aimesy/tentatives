Case Number: 25NWCV01967    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: C

RUBIO vs FORD MOTOR COMPANY, et al.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV01967

HEARING:  8/4/26 @ 9:30 AM

#9

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Plaintiff Eric Rubio’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 16-18, 20-29, and 30 is DENIED in part as MOOT and GRANTED in part as set forth below.  No sanctions are imposed. 

Moving Party to give NOTICE.

 

Plaintiff Eric Rubio (Plaintiff) moves to compel further responses to Plaintiff’s request for production of documents, set one, Nos. 16-18, 20-29, and 30 from Defendant Ford Motor Company (Defendant) pursuant to CCP § 2030.310.

In Reply, Plaintiff indicates that Defendant's subsequent document production has rendered portions of the underlying motion moot. The remaining documents Plaintiff seeks are RAV Policies and Procedures and the CRC documents for the years 2020 through 2025 which were not included in Defendant's July 20, 2026 production. Additionally, Plaintiff requests that the Court award sanctions incurred in bringing the motion, notwithstanding Defendant's belated compliance.

Background

This is a lemon law case. On August 8, 2020, Plaintiff purchased a 2020 Ford Mustang (Vehicle). (Complaint, ¶ 16.) Plaintiff alleges during the warranty period, the Vehicle contained or developed defects and non-conformities which impaired the use of the Vehicle. (Complaint, ¶ 17.)  Plaintiff provided Defendant and its representatives in this state sufficient opportunity to service or repair the Vehicle. Defendant and its representatives in this state were unable and/or failed to service or repair the Vehicle within a reasonable number of attempts. (Complaint, ¶ 18-21.)   

Legal Standard

CCP § 2031.310 allows a party to file a motion compelling further answers to document requests if it finds that the response is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive, or an objection in the response is without merit or too general.  The motion shall be accompanied with a meet and confer declaration.  (CCP § 2031.310(b) (emphasis added).)

Discussion

As stated above, Defendant made a further production on July 20, 2026. However, Plaintiff still seeks RAV Policies and Procedures and the Customer Relationship Center (CRC) documents for the years 2020 through 2025.

In opposition, Defendant stated that the July 20, 2026 production in further response to Request Nos. 17 and 20-30, included policies and procedures for Ford’s Customer Relationship Center (“CRC”), which handles communications from customers to Ford regarding vehicle concerns applicable to 2020 to the present; and its RAV Policy & Procedure Manual, which contains policy or procedure information about vehicle refunds and replacements, applicable to 2020 to the present. (Opp. p. 2: 13-20 (emphasis added).)

In Reply, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s document production remains incomplete because Defendant only produced documents from 2025. Plaintiff additionally argues that Defendant’s responses are not code-compliant as to the attorney-client privilege objections because Defendant has failed to produce a privilege log or sufficient factual information to evaluate the merits of the claim.

The Court finds that, to the extent the July 20, 2026 production failed to include documents from 2020 to 2025, Defendant shall produce RAV Policies and Procedures and the Customer Relationship Center (CRC) documents for those years, as Defendant indicated it had done. (Opp. p. 2: 13-20 (emphasis added).)

As to the attorney-client objections, section 2031.240 (c)(1) provides that

If an objection is based on a claim of privilege or a claim that the information sought is protected work product, the response shall provide sufficient factual information for other parties to evaluate the merits of that claim, including, if necessary, a privilege log.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.240.)

Ford’s attorney-client privilege objection states: “Ford properly objected to Request Nos. 17 and 20-30 because these they seek “all” documents, training materials, or scripts and flow charts related to the subject of the Request. Given the breadth of these Requests, “all documents,” may include communications between Ford and its attorneys, which are presumed to have been made in confidence and are broadly protected from discovery. [Citations.]”  The Court finds that Plaintiff’s request for “all documents” is overbroad.  In this routine lemon law action, Defendant’s response shall be limited to non-privileged documents. 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED in part as MOOT and GRANTED in part as set forth above. 

Sanctions

The court shall impose a monetary sanction against the party who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further responses to interrogatories or demand for production of documents unless the party subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or the sanction would otherwise be unjust.

(CCP § 2031.310(h).)

Plaintiff argues that sanctions against Defendant are warranted despite Defendant’s supplemental response because “Defendant did not agree to proceed under the Los Angeles Superior Court's standard model protective order until after the motion had already been filed.” (Reply, p. 14-15.) This unsupported assertion is contradicted by Defendant’s sworn statement that it had offered to proceed under the model protective order on April 4, 2026, but Plaintiff did not sign it until June 30, 2026. (Eberle Decl., ¶¶ 9-10.) The instant motion was filed on April 28, 2026. 

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED. 

 


Case Number: 26NWCV00796    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: C

ESPINOZA, et al. vs GARCIA, et al.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV00796

HEARING:  8/4/26 @ 9:30 AM

#10

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants Victor Gandara Garcia and Raul Mauricio Gandara’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons is DENIED.

Plaintiffs to give NOTICE.

 

Defendants Victor Gandara Garcia (Garcia) and Raul Mauricio Gandara (Gandara) (Defendants) specially appear to quash service of summons for lack of personal jurisdiction.

Background

Plaintiffs ROSALINDA ESPINOZA and LINDA SIMMONS, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem, CARLY MEDINA (Plaintiffs) filed this action arising from a motor vehicle collision allegedly caused by Defendants that occurred in Santa Fe Springs, California. (Complaint, ¶ 10.) Based thereon Plaintiffs allege causes of action for:

1.    Negligence;

2.    Negligent Entrustment

 

Legal Standard

A defendant may move to quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her. (Code Civ. Proc. § 418.10, subd. (a)(1).) The court may dismiss without prejudice the complaint in whole, or as to that defendant, when dismissal is made pursuant to Section 418.10. (CCP § 581, subd. (h).)  

Discussion

Defendants argue that service by publication was improper. Plaintiffs served both Defendants in the same notice in the Los Angeles Daily Journal on March 12, 2025, March 19, 2025, March 26, 2025 and April 2, 2025. Defendants contend that neither has viewed the publication nor has access to the Los Angeles Daily Journal or could even be expected to know of that publication’s existence. (Rullo Decl., ¶5.) The Los Angeles Daily Journal promotes its mission of serving California’s legal profession by providing comprehensive reporting on trial and appellate court proceedings, legislative and regulatory matters and legal analysis for California attorneys and legal professionals. (Rullo Decl., ¶ 6.) Defendants are not attorneys or any other type of legal or paralegal professional who would normally be considered in the readership of the publication. (Rullo Decl., ¶ 7.) Garcia is a Spanish speaking permanent resident of Mexico, currently living in Tijuana in the Mexican state of Baja California. (Rullo Decl., ¶ 8.)

Additionally, Defendants dispute liability for the collision. While Garcia owns the vehicle driven during the collision, he was not involved or present at the collision. (Rullo Decl. ¶ 2.) Gandara neither owns the vehicle nor was present at the collision. (Rullo Decl. ¶ 4.)

In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that attempts to personally serve Defendants were unsuccessful. (Kibriya Decl., Exh. 1.) Despite Plaintiffs investigation and repeated service efforts, Plaintiffs did not locate another address for Gandara or any address for Garcia in Mexico before seeking service by publication. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 10.) The Whittier address remained the only potential address identified for Gandara, and neither the service attempts nor the neighborhood inquiries yielded another address at which he could be served. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 10.) On March 2, 2026, the Court found that Defendants could not, with reasonable diligence, be served by another authorized method and ordered the summons published once a week for four successive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation in Los Angeles County. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. 3.) Plaintiffs thereafter published the summons in the Los Angeles Daily Journal on March 12, March 19, March 26, and April 2, 2026. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. 4.)

Moreover, as to Gandara, Plaintiffs reasonably believe, subject to investigation, that the same and proper individual was served at the same address. (See Declaration of Maria Rullo – SambaSafety Record – Exhibit A – as to the Registered Owner Section; Kibriya Decl., ¶ 12, Exh. 6. True and correct copies of Lexis Report as to Defendant Raul Mauricio Gandara [along with known aliases at the same address].)

The Court finds that Plaintiffs have met their burden regarding proper service. “When a defendant challenges the court’s personal jurisdiction on the ground of improper service of process the burden is on the plaintiff to prove … the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Summers v. McClanahan (2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 403, 413.)

Here, Plaintiffs evidence to have reasonably believed Gandara’s service address was at 13808 Mulberry Drive, Whittier, California, given the vehicle that was part of the collision is registered at that address to Murillo Raul Gandara. Additionally, between December 6, 2025, and January 23, 2026, the process server made repeated attempts to serve Defendants at the Whittier address on different days and at varying times. Those efforts included two one-hour stakeouts and inquiries of neighbors. The process server documented those efforts in declarations of diligence submitted with Plaintiffs’ applications for service by publication. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 5, Exh. 1.) As to service by publication, this Court authorized service in the Los Angeles Daily Journal. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. 3.)

Therefore, Plaintiffs demonstrate a good faith effort at personal service was first attempted. i.e., there is a showing that summons “[could not] with reasonable diligence be personally delivered” to the individual defendants. (CCP § 415.20(b).) Defendants are thereafter required to establish availability for personal service in rebuttal, however, Defendants fail to do so here. (Evartt v. Sup.Ct. (1979) 89 Cal.App.3d 795, 801.) Indeed, Defendants provided Plaintiffs with a specific address for Garcia until July 15, 2026. (Kibriya Decl., ¶ 11.) Moreover, Defendants failed to submit a declaration regarding Gandara’s foreign address and whether Gandara owns the vehicle or is the same individual listed under the vehicle’s registration,  Finally, counsel’s assertions are based on information and belief.

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion to Quash is DENIED.

 

 


Case Number: 26NWCV01142    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: C

ORANGE COUNTY CUSTOM VINYL GRAPHICS & SIGNS, INC. vs RH LAS INC.

CASE NO.: 26NWCV01142 

HEARING:  8/4/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff ORANGE COUNTY CUSTOM VINYL GRAPHICS & SIGNS, INC.’s application for a writ of attachment against Defendant RH LAS, INC. in the amount of $112,046.00 is DENIED.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff ORANGE COUNTY CUSTOM VINYL GRAPHICS & SIGNS, INC. (Plaintiff) applies for a right to attach order and writ of attachment against Defendant RH LAS, INC. (Defendant).

 

Background

 

This is a commercial collection action.  In December 2024, Defendant requested an estimate from Plaintiff to remove decal wraps from approximately 90 vehicles which Defendant acquired from another company so they could be rebranded for Defendant’s Rooter Hero and Service Hero operations.   Plaintiff provided two estimates totaling $30,547.50.  The removal work proved more difficult and time-consuming than initially anticipated due to the condition of the wraps.  Plaintiff ultimately invoiced Defendant for labor, services, and materials in the sum of $l03,526.00.  Plaintiff contends that Defendant accepted the benefits of Plaintiff’s work, but Defendant has failed to pay.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Except as otherwise provided by statute, an attachment may be issued only in an action on a claim or claims for money, each of which is based upon a contract, express or implied, where the total amount of the claim or claims is a fixed or readily ascertainable amount not less than five hundred dollars ($500) exclusive of costs, interest, and attorney’s fees.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 483.010.)

 

“Upon the filing of the complaint or at any time thereafter, the plaintiff may apply pursuant to this article for a right to attach order and a writ of attachment by filing an application for the order and writ with the court in which the action is brought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.010.) The application shall be executed under oath and must include:

 

(a)  statement showing that the attachment is sought to secure the recovery on a claim upon which an attachment may be issued;

(b)  a statement of the amount to be secured by the attachment;

(c)  a statement that the attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on the claim upon which the attachment is based;

(d)  a statement that the applicant has no information or belief that the claim is discharged or that the prosecution of the action is stayed in a proceeding under the Bankruptcy Act (11 U.S.C. section 101 et seq.); and

(e)  a description of the property to be attached under the writ of attachment and a statement that the plaintiff is informed and believes that such property is subject to attachment.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § § 484.020.)

 

The Court shall consider the showing made by the parties, as well as the pleadings and other papers in the record. (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.090, subd. (a), (d).) The Court shall issue a right to attach order if it finds all of the following:

 

(1)  The claim upon which the attachment is based is one upon which an attachment may be issued.

(2)  The plaintiff has established the probable validity of the claim upon which the attachment is based.

(3)  The attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on the claim upon which the attachment is based.

(4)  The amount to be secured by the attachment is greater than zero.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 484.090, subd. (a)(1-4).)

 

At the times prescribed by Code of Civil Procedure section 1005, subd. (b), the defendant must be served with a copy of the summons and complaint, notice of application and hearing, and a copy of the application and supporting affidavits. (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.040.) “The Attachment Law statutes are subject to strict construction….” (Epstein v. Abrams (1997) 57 Cal.App.4th 1159, 1168.)

 

“In contested applications, the court must consider the relative merits of the positions of the respective parties and make a determination of the probable outcome of the litigation.” (Hobbs v. Weiss (1999) 73 Cal.App.4th 76, 80 [ellipses and quotation marks omitted].)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff applies for a right to attach order and writ of attachment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 483.010.

 

          Basis for the Attachment

 

Plaintiff argues that its claim is a proper claim for an attachment because it is on invoices and business records between the parties. Plaintiff claims $103,526.00 as the balance due. (Norton Decl., Exhs. A-E.) Additionally, Plaintiff seeks costs for Court & eFiling Fees of $435.00 and 85.00 for service of process. Plaintiff seeks $520.00 in total costs.

 

Scott Norton is the manager of Plaintiff Orange County Custom Vinyl Graphics & Signs, Inc.  Norton declares that in December 2024, Defendant requested that Plaintiff perform large-scale commercial wrap removal services for Defendant’s fleet vehicles located in both Southern California and Northern California. (Norton Decl., ¶ 11.) Prior to commencement of work, Plaintiff explained to Defendant that the project involved substantial uncertainty because the age, condition, adhesive integrity, and removability of the wraps could not be determined until physical removal work commenced. (Id., ¶ 13.) During performance, Plaintiff repeatedly communicated with Defendant regarding the difficult condition of the wraps and the unexpectedly labor-intensive nature of the removal work. (Id., ¶ 28.) Defendant was aware during the course of performance that the project required extensive labor and additional time beyond the initial best-case estimates. (Id., ¶ 30.) Defendant repeatedly requested status updates concerning completion timelines, remaining vehicle counts, labor progress, scheduling, speed of completion, and project costs. (Id., ¶ 31.) Upon completion of substantial portions of the work, Plaintiff issued invoices reflecting the labor, overtime, travel, lodging, materials, adhesive removers, staffing, and related services actually provided. (Id., ¶ 33.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that the amount to be attached is not readily ascertainable. Defendant contends that Plaintiff’s requested attachment amount is overstated, uncertain, unsupported, and disputed. Plaintiff has not demonstrated authorization for open-ended charges and excessive labor/travel. Defendant claims offset for defective work. In support of these contentions, Defendant provides various declarations from Defendant’s employees declaring that the project Plaintiff was hired to perform progressed slowly and/or was deficient. (Navarro Decl., ¶ 5; Hubbard Decl., ¶¶ 4-7; Donelan, ¶¶ 5-9; Lucio Decl. ¶¶ 4-23.) Fernando Lucio was Defendant’s primary point of contact regarding scheduling, approvals, project logistics, operational coordination, and payment communications.  Lucio declares that he was not provided with contemporaneous notice that Plaintiff intended to charge amounts substantially greater than the amount reflected in Plaintiff's original estimates. Because the invoices were first transmitted weeks or months after the invoice dates, he was not given the opportunity to evaluate the escalating charges as the work progressed or communicate any objection to those charges before Plaintiff continued performing additional work. (Lucio Decl., ¶ 18, Exh. A.)  Lucio further declares that at “no time during my communications with Scott Norton did I receive a written request seeking approval to increase the cost of the decal removal project from approximately $30,547.50 to more than $103,000, nor did I provide written approval for such an increase.” (Lucio Decl., ¶ 23.)

 

The Court is unable to conclude that the amount Plaintiff claims is fixed or readily ascertainable.  While it is undisputed that Plaintiff cautioned Defendant about increased labor costs, both before and during the project, it is unclear to the Court whether Defendant actually approved the cost increases before the additional work was performed.  Moreover, even if Defendant did give its approval, there appears to be a genuine dispute regarding the invoiced amount, which is more than three times the amount of the estimate.  On this record, Plaintiff has not sufficiently demonstrated the probable validity of its claims. 

 

Accordingly, the application is DENIED.
