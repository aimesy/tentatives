Case Number: 21STCV45868    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: H

Varela v. Sandoval, et al., Case No. 21STCV45868

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

  

Counsel for Plaintiff Juan Manuel Varela’s (i.e., Boyajyan Law Firm, APC) Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is GRANTED, contingent upon satisfactory resolution of the notice concern identified herein and effective upon the filing of the proof of service reflecting service of the signed order upon Client at Client’s last known address. 

 

Background

Plaintiff Juan Manuel Varela (“Plaintiff”) alleges that he sustained injuries and damages in a December 15, 2019 motor vehicle collision on Arrow Highway, near Bonnie Cove Avenue, in Glendora. 

On December 15, 2021, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting a cause of action for Motor Vehicle against Gabriela Teresa Sandoval (“Sandoval”), Christine Roche Cabrera (“Cabrera”) and Does 1-10. 

On June 6, 2025, an “Order of Dismissal” was entered, without prejudice, as to Cabrera. 

An Order to Show Cause Re: Why the Case Should Not be Dismissed for Failure to Prosecute and Abandonment is set for July 16, 2026. 

Pending before the Court is counsel for Plaintiff’s motion to be relieved as counsel.

 

Notice

The proof of service accompanying the instant motion reflects that the motion was electronically served on Sandoval’s counsel on June 18, 2026. There is no proof of service on the court docket reflecting that the client was served with the motion. California Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(c) provides that “[p]roof of service of the moving papers must be filed no later than five court days before the time appointed for the hearing;” accordingly, any proof of service reflecting Code of Civil Procedure section 1005, subdivision (b) compliant notice to Client should have been filed no later than July 9, 2026. Moving counsel is directed to provide the Court with satisfactory proof of service of the motion to Client at or before the time of the hearing.

 

Discussion

The following analysis is contingent upon satisfactory resolution of the notice concern identified above. 

Boyajyan Law Firm, APC seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for Plaintiff (“Client”). 

The court has discretion to allow an attorney to withdraw, and such a motion should be granted provided that there is no prejudice to the client and it does not disrupt the orderly process of justice. (See Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915; People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398.) 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure § 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under section 284(1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion, the declaration, and the proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) a proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-053)). The court may delay the effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (Cal. Rules Court, rule 3.1362(e).)

Attorney Marselin S.K. Boyajyan (“Boyajyan”) represents that “[t]here has been a complete and irreparable breakdown of the attorney-client relationship” and that he “can no longer represent the interests of [Client] as it relates to this action.”

Boyajyan states that he has served Client by mail at Client’s last known address with copies of the motion papers served with his declaration and that he confirmed within the past 30 days that the address is current, as follows: “[c]urrent address listed on Spokeo (a people search website).” 

The Court determines that the requirements of Rules of Court Rule 3.1362 enumerated above have been sufficiently met.

Accordingly, the motion is granted, effective upon the filing of the proof of service reflecting service of the signed order upon Client at Client’s last known address.     

Case Number: 23PSCV03689    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: H

Morales v. Cuevas, et al., Case No. 23PSCV03689 (Consolidated with Case No. 23PSCV03846)

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO SET ASIDE DISMISSAL

Plaintiff Kelsie Ramona Valenzuela’s Motion to Set Aside Dismissal is GRANTED. The Court orders Case No. 23PSCV03846 restored to its active case list.

 

Background 

Case No. 23PSCV03689

Plaintiff Andrew Morales (“Morales”) allegedly sustained injuries and damages in a December 16, 2021 motor vehicle collision on the 60 freeway.

On November 29, 2023, Morales filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against Roberto Cuevas (“Cuevas”), FBD Vanguard Construction, Inc. (“FBD”) and Does 1-25 for: (1) Motor Vehicle and (2) General Negligence.

On May 31, 2024, the Court related Case Nos. 23PSCV03689 and 23PSCV03846; Case No. 23PSCV03689 was designated as the lead case.

On February 10, 2025, the Court consolidated Case Nos. 23PSCV03689 and 23PSCV03846; Case No. 23PSCV03689 was designated as the lead case.

On March 18, 2025, Morales filed a dismissal of the “[e]ntire action of all parties and all causes of action,” with prejudice.

 

Case No. 23PSCV03846

Plaintiff Kelsie Ramona Valenzuela (“Valenzuela”) allegedly sustained injuries and damages in a December 16, 2021 motor vehicle collision on the 60 freeway.

On December 12, 2023, Valenzuela filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against Cuevas, FBD and Does 1-50 for: (1) Motor Vehicle and (2) General Negligence.

On May 31, 2024, the Court related Case Nos. 23PSCV03689 and 23PSCV03846; Case No. 23PSCV03689 was designated as the lead case.

On February 10, 2025, the Court consolidated Case Nos. 23PSCV03689 and 23PSCV03846; Case No. 23PSCV03689 was designated as the lead case.


Legal Standard

 “The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or the party’s legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against the party through the party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken. . . Notwithstanding any other requirements of this section, the court shall, whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney's sworn affidavit attesting to the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against the attorney’s client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against the attorney’s client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. The court shall, whenever relief is granted based on an attorney's affidavit of fault, direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Discussion 

Valenzuela moves the Court, per Code of Civil Procedure section 473 and the Court’s inherent equitable authority, for an order setting aside the dismissal of her complaint entered on March 18, 2025, on the basis that the dismissal of her complaint was wholly unauthorized because it was filed by Attorney Vick Petrosian (“Petrosian”), who had formally substituted out as her counsel before her complaint was filed and thus had no authority to act on her behalf. In the alternative, Valenzuela asserts that the dismissal is voidable under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) based on mistake, inadvertence and excusable neglect, on the basis that counsel’s failure to discover that the dismissal applied to her case was caused by the facial limitations of the Request for Dismissal, the mutual oversight of both counsel regarding a checkbox, the appearance of active litigation and extraordinary personal circumstances.

 

Procedural Deficiencies 

At the outset, the Court observes that Valenzuela’s memorandum of points and authorities is 17 pages long, which is in contravention of California Rules of Court rule 3.1113, subdivision (d) (i.e., “[e]xcept in a summary judgment or summary adjudication motion, no opening or responding memorandum may exceed 15 pages”). The court admonishes counsel for Valenzuela.

 

Merits 

Valenzuela and her counsel, Benjamin Arsenian (“Arsenian”) represent, and/or the accompanying exhibits reflect, inter alia, that Petrosian initially and briefly represented Valenzuela in connection with potential claims arising from the underlying motor vehicle accident (Valenzuela Decl., ¶ 18); that, prior to the filing of any complaint on Valenzuela’s behalf, Valenzuela retained the Law Offices of Benjamin Arsenian PC to replace Petrosian as her counsel (Id.); that this substitution of counsel occurred before either her complaint or the complaint by Morales was filed (Id.); that, from the date of substitution forward, Petrosian had no attorney-client relationship with Valenzuela and no authority to act on her behalf and she was represented exclusively by the Law Offices of Benjamin Arsenian PC (Id., ¶ 20); that Valenzuela’s complaint was filed by the Law Offices of Benjamin Arsenian PC on or about December 12, 2023 and assigned Case No. 23PSCV03846 (Id., ¶ 21; Arsenian Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. A); that Morales’s complaint was separately filed by Petrosian in connection with potential claims arising from the underlying motor vehicle accident (Valenzuela Decl., ¶ 22; Arsenian Decl., ¶ 7); that, while the Court consolidated Valenzuela’s and Petrosian’s cases for administrative efficiency and case management purposes, the cases remained separate actions with separate counsel and Valenzuela retained independent authority over the prosecution and disposition of her own claims (Valenzuela Decl., ¶¶ 23 and 24; Arsenian Decl., ¶¶ 9 and 10, Exh. B); that on or about March 18, 2025, Petrosian filed a Judicial Council Form CIV-110 Request for Dismissal, which identified Petrosian as acting “on behalf of Andrew Morales” on the signature line and listed Morales’s case number, not Valenzuela’s case number, but contained a small, checked box indicating dismissal of the “entire action of all parties and all causes of action” (Arsenian Decl., ¶¶ 11, 12 and 14, Exh. C) and that Valenzuela did not authorize or consent to any dismissal of her complaint and did not request dismissal of her case (Valenzuela Decl., ¶ 25).

Although Cuevas and FBD oppose the motion, they fail to provide the Court with any declarations or documentary evidence contradicting any of the representations set forth above.

Under the circumstances, then, it is clear that Petrosian had no authority to dismiss Valenzuela’s complaint on March 18, 2025, as he was not her counsel. As Valenzuela points out, there is no six-month time limitation applicable to unauthorized dismissals. “[D]ismissal of a cause of action by an attorney acting without any authority from his client is an act beyond the scope of his authority which, on proper proof, may be vacated at any time.” (Whittier Union High Sc. Dist. v. Superior Court (1977) 66 Cal.App.3d 504, 509.) In this case, there was no existing attorney-client relationship existing at the time of dismissal in the first instance.

The motion is granted. The Court orders Case No. 23PSCV03846 restored to its active case list.

Background 

Plaintiff Laura Garnica (“Plaintiff”) alleges that she sustained injuries and damages in a December 31, 2022 motor vehicle accident at the intersection of Mission Boulevard and Reservoir Street in Pomona. 

On December 12, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting a motor vehicle cause of action against Jerold Joseph Curry and Does 1-10. 

On May 20, 2025, Plaintiff filed an “Amendment to Complaint,” wherein Plaintiff corrected the name of “Jerold Joseph Curry” to “Jerold Joseph Curry aka Jerrold Joseph Curry” (“Defendant”). 

On August 21, 2025, Defendant’s default was entered. On January 22, 2026, an “Order on Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default” was entered. 

A Case Management Conference is set for July 16, 2026.

 

1. Motion to Compel Re: Form Interrogatories, Set One 

Legal Standard

A response to interrogatories is due within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).) “If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, . . . [t]he party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling response to the interrogatories.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).) 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).) 

Discussion 

Defendant moves the Court for an order compelling Plaintiff to provide responses, without objections, to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One. Defendant also seeks sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel in the amount of $1,111.65. 

Defendant’s counsel Eric Anvari (“Anvari”) represents, and/or the accompanying exhibits reflect, as follows: 

On January 27, 2026, the subject discovery was served. (Anvari Decl., ¶ 5, Exh. 1.) Responses were due on or before March 2, 2026. (Id., ¶ 6.) On March 6, 2026 at 12:05 p.m., Plaintiff’s counsel’s legal assistant Grace Park emailed Anvari and asked for an extension to March 27, 2026 to provide discovery responses. (Id., ¶ 7, Exh. 2.) Anvari granted this request at 1:35 p.m., but advised that responses should be free of objections on the basis that they were overdue. (Id.) Counsel thereafter exchanged a flurry of emails, during which time they disagreed over whether Anvari had retracted the extension. (Id., ¶¶ 8 and 9, Exhs. 3 and 4.)

No responses have been received from Plaintiff, as of the March 30, 2026 motion filing date. (Id., ¶ 10.) 

Plaintiff, in response, advises that he served objection-free responses to the subject discovery responses on or about June 18, 2026 and verifications on June 22, 2026, such that the motion is moot. (Feldman Decl., 4:12-14.) Plaintiff also advises that on June 22, 2026 he offered to pay $180.00 in filing fees as well. (Id.) 

Defendant, in reply, concedes that Plaintiff has provided verified responses and has offered $180.00 in filing fees. Defendant asserts, however, that the responses contain general and blanket objections incorporated into each response and are otherwise insufficient substantively in numerous ways. 

Defendant’s first assertion is well-taken. The Court has reviewed Plaintiff’s responses, which are attached as Exhibit 2 to Anvari’s reply declaration. Said responses contain a “Prefatory Statement,” which includes various objections which are “incorporate[d] by reference. . . into each and every response hereinafter.” This is improper. Defendant’s second assertion is the subject of a motion to compel further responses, not initial responses.

The motion is granted. Plaintiff is ordered to provide verified responses, without objections, to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling. 

Sanctions 

Defendant seeks sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $1,111.65 [calculated as follows: 2 hours preparing motion, plus 0.5 hours reviewing opposition, plus 1 hour preparing reply at $300.00/hour, plus $61.65 filing fee]. 

Utilizing a Lodestar approach, and in view of the totality of the circumstances, the Court finds that the total and reasonable amount of attorney’s fees and costs incurred for the work performed in connection with the pending motion is $510.00 (i.e., 1.5 hours attorney time at $300.00/hour, plus $60.00 filing fee). Sanctions are payable within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling.

3. Motion to Compel Re: Requests for Production of Documents, Set One 

Legal Standard 

A response to a request for production of documents is due within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) “If a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to serve a timely response to it, . . . [t]he party making the demand may move for an order compelling response to the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) 

“[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).) 

Discussion

Defendant moves the Court for an order compelling Plaintiff to provide responses, without objections, to Defendant’s Request for Production of Documents, Set One. Defendant also seeks sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel in the amount of $1,111.65. 

See synopsis of Motion #1.

Again, the Court has reviewed Plaintiff’s responses, which are attached as Exhibit 2 to Anvari’s reply declaration. Said responses contain a “Preliminary Statement,” which reads, in relevant part, as follows: “If any information within the scope of the attorney-client privilege or the attorney work-product doctrine are inadvertently disclosed in this response, Responding Party has not done so intentionally, and reserves the right to assert these privileges at any time in these proceedings. . . In addition, all evidentiary objections are reserved and no waiver of any objections is to be implied with this response.” This is improper. Defendant’s argument regarding the sufficiency of the responses is the subject of a motion to compel further responses, not initial responses.

The motion is granted. Plaintiff is ordered to provide verified responses, without objections, to Defendant’s Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling. 

Sanctions 

Defendant seeks sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $1,111.65 [calculated as follows: 2 hours preparing motion, plus 0.5 hours reviewing opposition, plus 1 hour preparing reply at $300.00/hour, plus $61.65 filing fee]. 

Utilizing a Lodestar approach, and in view of the totality of the circumstances, the Court finds that the total and reasonable amount of attorney’s fees and costs incurred for the work performed in connection with the pending motion is $510.00 (i.e., 1.5 hours attorney time at $300.00/hour, plus $60.00 filing fee). Sanctions are payable within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling.
