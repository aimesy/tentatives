ANALYSIS:

 

            Hibachi Kings Group, Inc. and Jose H. Moran failed to provide responses to Perrin Bernard Supowitz, LLC’s discovery requests.  Perrin moved to compel responses.  This Court granted the motion and ordered the payment of monetary sanctions.  Pursuant to that order, discovery responses were due, and sanctions were to be paid, within 30 days of notice of the Court’s ruling.  Perrin served notice of the ruling on February 2, 2026.  As of now, neither Hibachi Kings nor Moran has complied with the Court’s order.  Perrin moves for terminating sanctions.  The Court GRANTS the motion.

 

            A court has the power to impose monetary, evidence, issue, or terminating sanctions on a party who fails to respond to discovery or fails to obey a court order to provide discovery.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subds. (d), (g); id. § 2023.030, subds. (a)–(d).)  Terminating sanctions are often appropriate only as a last resort, either when a party fails to respond to escalating measures of coerced compliance or when its discovery misconduct is so bad that lesser sanctions would have the Court and the party seeking discovery running a fool’s errand.  (City of Los Angeles v. PricewaterhouseCoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 63; Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of N.Y., Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604.)

 

            In addition to failing to comply with the Court’s previous order, neither Hibachi Kings nor Moran bothered to oppose Perrin’s motion for terminating sanctions.  There is no reason to keep a case on the Court’s docket that a party has no interest in defending.  The Court STRIKES Hibachi Kings and Moran’s answer.  They are now in default.

  An Order to Show Cause Re: Entry of Default Judgment is set for AUGUST 13, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse.

 The 09/23/2026 Non-Jury Trial is advanced to this date and hereby taken off calendar/vacated.

CASE NUMBER:      25STLC07283                      

NOTICE:                  OK                                                                 

                                                                                                                       

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE; ALTERNATIVE REQUEST FOR LEAVE TO CONDUCT ADDITIONAL DISCOVERY

 

MOVING PARTY:   Defendant/Cross-Complainant Jason Racca

RESP. PARTY:         Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Jazmine Ly

 

MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS; REQUEST TO CONDUCT ADDITIONAL DISCOVERY

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 95, 2031.310)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

            The Court DENIES Jason Racca’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production of documents.

 

            The Court DENIES Racca’s request for leave to conduct additional discovery.

 

            Racca is to give notice.

 

SERVICE:

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK


OPPOSITION:          Filed June 29, 2026                                        [   ] Late                      [   ] None

REPLY:                     Filed July 2, 2026                                           [   ] Late                      [   ] None


ANALYSIS:

 

            Jazmine Ly and Jason Racca are litigating over ownership and possession of a dog.  Racca propounded discovery, including requests for production of documents, on Ly.  Ly responded; Racca now moves to compel further responses to several of his requests.  The Court DENIES the motion.

 

            The Court notes that the motion was not served and filed at least 16 court days, plus five calendar days—for service by mail—before this hearing date.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).)  The deadline to file and serve the motion was June 12, 2026.  It was not served until June 18 and not filed until June 22.  Regardless, Racca’s motion fails on its merits.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 94 constrains discovery in limited jurisdiction actions to any combination of 35 interrogatories with no subparts, demands to produce documents, and requests for admission with no subparts.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 94, subd. (a).)  Racca has propounded a total of 72 discovery requests on Ly:  40 requests for admission, 22 special interrogatories, and 10 requests for production.  The Court will not grant a motion to compel discovery that was propounded in violation of the Code of Civil Procedure.

 

Anticipating this problem, Racca asks the Court to authorize his additional discovery under to Code of Civil Procedure section 95.  That section authorizes a court, on noticed motion and subject to such terms and conditions as are just, to allow a party to conduct additional discovery—but only on a showing that the party will be unable to prosecute or defend the action effectively without the additional discovery.  (Id. § 95, subd. (a).)  In deciding whether to allow additional discovery, a court must whether the moving party has used all applicable discovery in good faith, and whether the party has attempted to secure the additional discovery by stipulation or by means other than formal discovery.  (Ibid.)  Racca has not sufficiently shown he is entitled to additional discovery.  For one, propounding discovery requests in excess of the limit, and then requesting relief under section 95, is not proper procedure.  Further, as Ly observes in opposition, there may be alternate ways for Racca to obtain the discovery he is seeking from Ly.

 

Racca is to give notice.
