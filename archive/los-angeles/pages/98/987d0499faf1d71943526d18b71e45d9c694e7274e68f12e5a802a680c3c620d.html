Tentative Ruling

Jane Doe v. Regents of the University of California, Case No. 25SMCV02901

Defendant’s Motion to Strike

Hearing Date: September 17, 2026

Background

Plaintiff Jane Doe (“Doe”) sues The Regents of the University of California (“The Regents”) and Does 2-25 for negligence and negligent hiring, retention, and supervision arising out of an alleged sexual assault in 2023 when she was approximately three years old. (First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), ¶ 33) Doe alleges that Christopher Rodriguez, a teacher at the University of California Los Angeles Early Care and Education Krieger Center, sexually abused and assaulted her multiple times over a period of several months while she was a student. (Id. at ¶ 33.)

On June 6, 2025, Doe filed the initial complaint.

On August 20, 2025, the Court granted Doe’s ex parte applications to seal, to appoint a guardian ad litem, and to proceed under fictitious names.

On December 10, 2025, The Regents filed a motion to strike.

On February 20, 2026, Doe filed an Amended Complaint, mooting the December 10 motion to strike.

On April 22, 2026, The Regents filed this motion to strike. On September 3, 2026, Doe filed opposition. On September 10, 2026, The Regents filed a reply.

Motion to Strike

Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof. (CCP, § 435(b)(1); CRC, rule 3.1322(b).) The court may, upon a motion or at any time in its discretion and upon terms it deems proper: (1) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the court. (CCP, § 436(a)-(b); Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782 [“Matter in a pleading which is not essential to the claim is surplusage; probative facts are surplusage and may be stricken out or disregarded”].)¿¿¿¿¿ 

“Where the defect raised by a motion to strike or by demurrer is reasonably capable of cure, leave to amend is routinely and liberally granted to give the plaintiff a chance to cure the defect in question.” (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1146.) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.)¿¿¿¿ 

Discussion

The Regents move to strike Plaintiff’s allegations that The Regents covered up abuse as irrelevant, arguing that the allegations only supported a claim for treble damages that Doe has since dismissed. (Motion to Strike, p. 4.) In opposition, Doe argues that the allegations are relevant to The Regents’ knowledge of the perpetrator’s history of abuse and failure to protect her from his abuse. (Opposition, p. 2.) In reply, The Regents argue that the term “cover up,” as used in the complaint is a specific term of art related to treble damages because it references the code section authorizing treble damages and should be stricken as immaterial. (Reply, p. 2.)  

The Regents may be correct that they are immune from treble damages based on an alleged coverup of abuse, but there is no law that The Regents cannot be accused of covering up sexual abuse. Plaintiff’s allegation may still be relevant to The Regents’ liability, especially to their knowledge of the abuse. Contrary to The Regents’ contention, Plaintiff’s reference to a code section defining a cover up for the purposes of treble damages does not establish that the allegation is improper, especially since the definition of “cover up” in that context is still relevant to the allegation that The Regents knew about abuse and failed to act. Where, as here, the challenged allegations add context to the plaintiff’s claims and are not themselves improper, the Court will not grant a motion to strike. (PH II, Inc. v. Superior Court (1995), 33 Cal.App.4th 1680, 1683 (“[U]se of the motion to strike should be cautious and sparing. We have no intention of creating a procedural ‘line-item veto’ for the civil defendant.”).)

Conclusion

The Regents’ Motion to Strike is DENIED.


Case Number: 25SMCV05873    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: P

Tentative Ruling

Helmsman Management Service v. Alexandra Latimer, et al., Case No. 25SMCV05873

Defendant’s Demurrer

Hearing Date: September 17, 2026

Background

This is a subrogation or contribution action arising from an underinsured or uninsured motorist claim. Plaintiff Helmsman Management Service, TPA for James River Insurance, (“Plaintiff”) sues Defendants Alexandra Latimer (“Latimer”) and American Family Insurance for equitable contribution. Plaintiff alleges that their insured’s employee, Greg Wilson, was injured when Latimer rear-ended him on November 15, 2019. (First Amended Complaint, ¶¶ 12-13.)

On November 7, 2025, Plaintiff filed the initial complaint.

On January 12, 2026, Latimer filed a demurrer.

On May 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative pleading, the First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), mooting Latimer’s demurrer.

On June 17, 2026, Latimer filed this demurrer. On September 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed opposition. On September 10, 2026, Latimer replied.

Latimer argues that Plaintiff’s FAC fails to state a claim for equitable contribution because it does not allege the terms of the subrogation or insurance agreement it purports to enforce. (Demurrer, p. 5.) Latimer also argues that the complaint is uncertain because it does not allege the existence of a subrogation agreement or the relevant underinsured or uninsured motorist provisions. (Id. at p. 6) Latimer next argues that Plaintiff cannot claim equitable contribution against alleged tortfeasors who settled with the injured party for payments made under underinsured motorist coverage in California under Home Insurance Co. v. Zurich Insurance Co., (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 17. (Id. at pp. 7-9.) Latimer also argues that Plaintiff is barred from seeking equitable contribution because it made a voluntary payment to Uber’s employee. (Id. at p. 10.) Next, Latimer argues that Plaintiff had no right to equitable contribution because Defendants’ insurance coverage did not cover the same risks at the same level. (Id. at p. 11.) Finally, Latimer argues that the statute of limitations for actions for property damage has run on Plaintiff’s claim. (Id. at p. 12.)

In opposition, Plaintiff asserts that the complaint states a claim for equitable subrogation and that the demurrer makes evidentiary arguments that are not proper on a demurrer. (Opposition, pp. 3-4.)

In reply, Latimer argues that Plaintiff’s late-filed opposition should not be considered and argues that Plaintiff’s silence concedes five of the six grounds for the demurrer, including the statute of limitations, the release, voluntary payment, no subrogation for underinsured motorist payments, and that equitable contribution requires that the insurance companies have the same insured. Latimer also argues that Plaintiff claims that it seeks equitable subrogation in opposition to this demurrer but plead equitable contribution. Latimer contends that the complaint does not state a claim under either theory. (Id. at pp. 304.)  

Demurrer

In a demurrer proceeding, the defects in the complaint must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Ibid.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245).

Before filing a demurrer, parties must meet and confer in person or by phone. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41.)

Discussion

Meet and Confer

Latimer’s attorney attempted to meet and confer with Plaintiff’s counsel before filing this demurrer, but Plaintiff’s counsel was unresponsive. (Slack Decl., ¶ 8.) Although counsel’s non-responsiveness is frustrating, the failure to meet and confer is not grounds for overruling a demurrer.

Equitable Subrogation

Plaintiff alleges that it issued Uber a policy that included uninsured motorist coverage. (FAC, ¶ 11.) Plaintiff alleges that Greg Wilson, Uber’s employee, sued Plaintiff and Plaintiff ultimately settled for $15,000, but that Wilson was damaged in the amount of $350,000. (Id. at ¶¶ 13-15.) Plaintiff alleges that after settling that lawsuit, it discovered that Latimer had insurance through Defendant American Family Insurance. (Id. at ¶ 11.) Plaintiff alleges that it paid a loss for which Defendants are primarily liable and thus seeks subrogation. (Id. at ¶ 20.)

In Home Ins. Co. v. Zurich Ins. Co., the Court of Appeal found that an uninsured motorist insurer could not seek subrogation against the uninsured or underinsured motorist’s insurance provider under Ins. Code § 11580.2. “As an underinsured motorist insurer, Home has no right of subrogation. (Hartford Fire Ins. Co. v. Macri (1992) 4 Cal.4th 318, 328, 14 Cal.Rptr.2d 813, 842 P.2d 112.) Home's interests are fully protected and limited to the right of reimbursement or credit for the amount the insured receives from the underinsured tortfeasor or his insurer. (Ins.Code, § 11580.2, subd. (p)(5).) Further, Home had no legal obligation to pay underinsured motorist benefits until Pinasco and Main received payment of the limits of Canfield's policy. (Ins.Code, § 11580.2, subd. (p)(3).” (Home Ins. Co. v. Zurich Ins. Co. (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 17, 27.)

This case is virtually identical. Plaintiff does not attempt to distinguish this case from Home in opposition to this motion. Latimer’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s subrogation claim is sustained.  

Equitable Contribution

It is not clear if Plaintiff seeks equitable subrogation or equitable contribution. Unlike equitable subrogation, equitable contribution does not flow from a contract. “In the insurance context, the right to contribution arises when several insurers are obligated to indemnify or defend the same loss or claim, and one insurer has paid more than its share of the loss or defended the action without any participation by the others. Where multiple insurance carriers insure the same insured and cover the same risk, each insurer has independent standing to assert a cause of action against its coinsurers for equitable contribution when it has undertaken the defense or indemnification of the common insured.” (Fireman’s Fund Ins. Co. v. Maryland Casualty Co. (1998) 65 Cal.App.4th 1279, 1293.) Here, Plaintiff alleges that it insured Uber and American Family Insurance insured Latimer. Therefore, Plaintiff does not state a claim for equitable contribution because it does not allege that the parties have the same insured. Latimer’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s equitable contribution claim is sustained.

Leave to Amend

Plaintiff requests leave to amend in passing but does not make any showing that there is a reasonable possibility that it can amend the complaint to state a claim. (See Opposition, p. 4.) It is Plaintiff’s burden to make such a showing. (Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) Plaintiff has not met its burden. Leave to amend is denied.  

Conclusion

Latimer’s Demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.


Case Number: 26SMCV03858    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: P

Tentative Ruling

Topanga Partners, LLC v. Roberta Koehl, Case No. 26SMCV03858

Defendant’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons

Hearing Date: September 17, 2026

Background

This is an unlawful detainer case. Plaintiff Topanga Partners, LLC seeks possession of property located at 23732 Zuniga Road, Topanga, California 90290. Plaintiff alleges that it purchased the subject property in Bankruptcy Case No. 1:24-bk MB, In re: Zuniga 23732, LLC, on April 21, 2026. (Complaint, ¶ 6.) Plaintiff alleges that Koehl was a tenant or subtenant of the prior owner. (Id. at ¶ 11.)

On July 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed the complaint.

On August 10, 2026, the Court granted an application and order to serve summons by posting for unlawful detainer.

On September 3, 2026, Defendant filed this Motion to Quash Service of Summons. On September 11, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s ex parte motion to advance the hearing. As of September 14, 2026, the Court has not received Plaintiff’s opposition to Defendants’ motion.

Defendant argues that the order authorizing service by positing was improperly obtained on an affidavit that did not establish that Defendant could not be served in any other manner specified in Article 3 and because Plaintiff only made three attempts at service, all at the same address. Next, Defendant argues that service was not complete until August 23, 2026. Next, Plaintiff argues that the complaint does not state a claim in unlawful detainer because the property was not sold by Defendant or by a debtor entity, but by a Chapter 7 trustee under bankruptcy law.

In opposition, Topanga argues that service was proper and it attempted personal service with sufficient diligence before obtaining an order authorizing service by posting. Topanga argues that Koehl’s challenge to Plaintiff’s title from the bankruptcy sale and her challenge to the three-day notice are improper for purposes of this motion.

Motion to Quash Service of Summons for Lack of Jurisdiction

Code of Civil Procedure, section 418.10 states the following: “(a) A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: (1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1).)¿ 

“[C]ompliance¿with the statutory procedures for service of process is essential to establish personal jurisdiction. [Citation.]” (Dill v. Berquist Construction Co.¿(1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1444.) “[T]he filing of a proof of service creates a rebuttable presumption that the service was proper” but only if it “complies with the statutory requirements regarding such proofs.” (Id.¿at pp. 1441-1442.) When a defendant moves to quash service of the summons and complaint, the plaintiff has “the burden of proving the facts that did give the court jurisdiction, that is the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Coulston v. Cooper¿(1966) 245 Cal.App.2d 866, 868.)¿¿ 

Discussion

Service

C.C.P. §418.10(a)(1) provides that “A defendant… may serve and file a notice of motion… [t]o quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her…” (CCP § 418.10(a).)

When a defendant moves to quash service of the summons and complaint, the plaintiff has, “the burden of proving the facts that did give the court jurisdiction, that is the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Coulston v. Cooper (1966) 245 Cal.App.2d 866, 868.)

C.C.P. §415.20(a) provides that, “In lieu of personal delivery of a copy of the summons and complaint to the person to be served…, a summons may be served by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint during usual office hours in his or her office…, with the person who is apparently in charge thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and complaint by first-class mail, postage prepaid to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left… Service of a summons in this manner is deemed complete on the 10th day after the mailing.”

C.C.P. §415.20(b) provides that, “If a copy of the summons and complaint cannot with reasonable diligence be personally delivered to the person to be served…, a summons may be served by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint at the person’s… usual place of business… in the presence of a competent member of the household or a person apparently in charge of his or her office [or] place of business…, at least 18 years of age, who shall be informed of the contents thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and of the complaint by first-class mail, postage prepaid to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left. Service of a summons in this manner is deemed complete on the 10th day after the mailing.”

Plaintiff’s showing of diligence on its proof of attempted service was sufficient for the Court to authorize service by posting. Three attempts at personal service at Koehl’s residence on three different days is generally sufficient to authorize service by alternate method, including posting. (Espinolda v. Nunez (1988) 199 Cal.App.3d 1389, 1392.)

Plaintiff is also correct that service by posting does not extend the notice period in unlawful detainer actions and that their typo in the proof of service—stating Robert Koehl instead of Roberta Koehl—is of no consequence because the notice itself is directed to Roberta Koehl.

Demurrer to Plaintiff’s Unlawful Detainer Claim

 

Koehl argues that in an unlawful detainer action, a Motion to Quash Service of Summons is a vehicle for challenging whether the complaint states a claim for unlawful detainer, citing Delta Imports, Inc. v. Municipal Court (1983) 146 Cal.App.3d 1033, 1035. Plaintiff contends that raising demurrer issues on a motion to quash is improper, citing Borsuk v. Appellate Division of Superior Court (2015) 242 Cal.App.4th 607. In Borsuk, the court found that Delta was misleading and held that:

Thus, the holding of Delta (despite the decision's all-encompassing language) is limited to the circumstances in Delta. Those circumstances were that the complaint failed to allege proper service of a notice to pay or quit. (Delta, supra, 146 Cal.App.3d at p. 1036, 194 Cal.Rptr. 685.) It was thus defective on its face because it contained “none of the required allegations regarding notice. It merely allege[d] that, ‘More than 3 days and more than 30 days have lapsed since the Defendants were given notice....’ There [was] no allegation that the notice was in writing, that it specified the alleged breaches of the lease or that it unequivocally demanded possession.” (Ibid.)

(Borsuk v. Appellate Division of Superior Court (2015) 242 Cal.App.4th 607, 616.)

Pursuant to the Court of Appeal’s reasoning in Borsuk, this Court will not consider Koehl’s challenge to the pleading on issues apart from any arguments Koehl raises regarding the complaint’s allegations of proper service of the three-day notice. Koehl contends that the proof of service, not the notice itself, improperly identifies her as “Robert Koehl,” that posting is only available where the tenant’s place of residence cannot be ascertained, and where the complaint was filed before the notice period.

First, the notice itself names Koehl correctly. On the proof of service filed with the Court contains the mis-spelling of the name. Koehl does not cite authority finding that this defect is sufficient to invalidate the three-day notice. Second, service by posting is an alternate method of service that does not require a showing that a tenant’s place of residence cannot be ascertained. (Code Civ. Proc., §415.45 (b).) Third, the notice period is not extended for service by posting in unlawful detainer actions. (See Losornio v. Motta (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 110, 115.)

Conclusion

Defendant’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons is DENIED.
