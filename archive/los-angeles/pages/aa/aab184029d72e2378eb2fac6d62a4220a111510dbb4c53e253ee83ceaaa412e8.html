SALAR HAZANY, et al.,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

SEMYON GINZBURG, et al.,

                        Defendants.

  Case No.:  24SMCV05764

  Hearing Date:  06/29/2026

  Trial Date:  08/30/2027

 [TENTATIVE] RULING RE:

DEFENDANT EMPRIE WEST HOMEOWNERS ASSOCIATION, INC.’S MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

Background

            This action arises out of a landlord-tenant dispute.

            On November 24, 2025, Salara Hazany and Tiffany Hazany (“Plaintiffs”) filed a Complaint against Semyon and William Ginzburg (collectively “the Ginzburgs”) and Empire West Homeowners’ Association, Inc. (“Empire West”) alleging causes of action for constructive eviction and negligence, among others, based on the claim of wrongful eviction. Empire West filed an answer on January 16, 2025.

            On May 26, 2026, Empire West filed the instant motion for leave to file a Cross-Complaint against the Ginzburgs.

            On June 15, 2026, the Ginzburgs filed an opposition.

            On June 22, 2026, Empire West filed a reply.  

 

Legal Standard

 

            “A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint. Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50(a)–(b).) A cross-complaint filed outside either of the aforementioned times requires leave of court, which may be granted “in the interest of justice at any time during the court of the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50(c).) 

            A party who fails to plead a cause of action subject to the requirements of this article, whether through oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause, may apply to the court for leave to amend his pleading, or to file a cross-complaint, to assert such cause at any time during the course of the action. The court, after notice to the adverse party, shall grant, upon such terms as may be just to the parties, leave to amend the pleading, or to file the cross-complaint, to assert such cause if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith. This subdivision shall be liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of causes of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.) Leave to file a cross-complaint may also be granted “in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 428.50, subd. (c).) 

 

Analysis

Empire West seeks leave to file a Cross-Complaint alleging causes of action for express and implied indemnity, declaratory relief, and breach of contract against Semyon Ginzburg and William Ginzburg.

            The claims asserted in the proposed cross-complaint are compulsory. Compulsory claims are those that are “related” to the suit in which the cross-complaint is brought, and a “related cause of action” is one that “arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause of action which the plaintiff alleges in his complaint.” (ZF Micro Devices, Inc. v. TAT Capital Partners, Ltd. (2016) 5 Cal.App.5th 69, 81.) Empire West’s proposed claims arise out of the same incident underlying the complaint. 

            Because Empire West’s claims are compulsory, leave to assert them in this action may not be denied absent a showing of bad faith. (See Silver Organizations Ltd. v. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 99 [“Factors such as oversight, inadvertence, neglect, mistake or other cause, are insufficient grounds to deny the motion unless accompanied by bad faith.”].) 

No bad faith is present here. The instant motion was prompted by the Ginzburgs’ refusal to engage with Empire West regarding the tender demand served on March 25, 2025. On April 21, 2026, Empire West circulated a Joint Stipulation to all parties and the Ginzburgs refused to stipulate, thus prompting this motion. Although significant time has passed since Empire West filed its Answer on January 16, 2025, the delay was not caused by Empire West and the Ginzburgs had notice that Empire West intended to file a motion for leave to file a cross-complaint back on May 12, 2025. (May 12, 2025 Case Management Statement, ¶ 2.) Thus, there is no showing of bad faith by Empire West.

The Ginzburgs argue that the Motion should be denied because the proposed Cross-Complaint fails to state a valid cause of action. The Court finds that since the Cross-Complaint’s causes of action are compulsory, the Court may not deny them absent a showing of bad faith. The Court has concluded there is no showing of bad faith, thus the Court will not determine whether the causes of actions stated in the Proposed Cross-Complaint fail to state causes of action. Those inquiries are more appropriately raised by demurrer, summary judgment, or other pretrial proceedings after the compulsory cross-complaint has been filed.  

            Therefore, the Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint is GRANTED.

Conclusion

            Defendant’s Empire West’s Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint is GRANTED.

NATALIE MELNIK, and DOES 1 through 20, inclusive,

  Trial Date:  N/A

 [TENTATIVE] RULING RE:

DEFENDANT KAINOA KILCHER’S MOTION TO SET ASIDE ORDER STRIKING ANSWER, TERMINATING SANCTION, AND ENTRY OF DEFAULT

 

Background

This a personal injury action.

 

On December 9, 2024, Plaintiff filed this action alleging causes of action for negligence, assault, battery, intentional infliction of emotional distress, negligent infliction of emotional distress, false imprisonment, and sexual abuse.

 

On August 8, 2025, Defendant filed an answer to the Complaint, but then failed to appear the Case Management Conference on November 7, 2025. Accordingly, the Court issued an Order to Show Cause why Sanctions should not be imposed for Defendant’s failure to appear and failure to submit a case management statement.  The Court set a hearing on the Order to Show Cause for January 8, 2026.

 

On January 8, 2026, Defendant failed to appear or other respond to the Order to Show Cause.  The Court struck Defendant’s answer and entered default.

 

On May 28, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to set aside default.

 

On June 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

On June 18, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

Legal Standard

      Code Civil Procedure section 473, subd. (b)¿provides for two distinct types of relief from a default -- commonly differentiated as “discretionary” and “mandatory.” “Under the¿discretionary relief provision, on a showing of ‘mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or¿excusable neglect,” the court has discretion to allow relief from default. Under the mandatory relief provision, on the other hand, upon a showing by attorney declaration of ‘mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect,’ the court must vacate any ‘resulting default judgment or dismissal entered.’” (Leader v. Health Industries of America, Inc.¿(2001) 89 Cal.App.4th 603, 615-616.)¿

The application for relief must be made no more than six months after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken. (Ibid.) And the application must be “accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein”. (Ibid.) 

 

      “It is settled that the law favors a trial on the merits. . . and therefore liberally construes section 473.” (Bonzer v. City of Huntington Park (1993) 20 Cal.App.4th 1474, 1477.) “Doubts in applying section 473 are resolved in favor of the party seeking relief from default. . . and if that party has moved promptly for default relief, only slight evidence will justify an order granting such relief.” (Id. at 1477-78.)  

     

      Applications seeking relief under the mandatory provision of §473 must be “accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., §473, subd. (b).)  The mandatory provision further adds that “whenever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault [the court shall] direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties.” (Ibid.)   

 

      Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d) addresses a motion to vacate a void judgment or order.  That section states, “The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) 

 

 

Analysis

      Defendant moves to set aside this Court’s January 8, 2026 Order which struck Defendant’s answer and entered default against Defendant. Defendant argues the January 8, 2026 Order must be set aside, pursuant to both Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivisions (b) and (d). The Court will first address the motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d).

 

Whether the Order is Void under CCP § 473(d)

      Here, Defendant argues the January 8, 2026 Order must be set aside because it is void. Specifically, Defendant argues he was not properly served with the Statement of Damages before Plaintiff sought default judgment. Defendant then argues Plaintiff’s subsequent service of the Statement of Damages by electronic service was invalid because Defendant, who is in pro per, never expressly consented to receive documents by electronic means. In opposition, Plaintiff argues Defendant was served via publication with the Statement of Damages on July 18, 2025.

 

      Here, the Court finds that the January 8, 2026 Order is not void. To begin with, the Court has not (as of the posting of this tentative ruling) issued a default judgment. The Court’s January 8, 2026 Order struck Defendant’s answer and entered default against him – not default judgment. The Court does note, however, that Defendant’s argument that the January 8, 2026 Order is void because he was not served with the Statement of Damages is without merit. Defendant was served with the Statement of Damages via publication on July 18, 2025, which Defendant includes as the last page to his Exhibit C.

 

      Therefore, the January 8, 2026, Minute Order is not void and Defendant’s motion is denied on these grounds.

 

Discretionary Relief under CCP § 473(b)

      Here, given Defendant is self-represented, he is only able to move for discretionary relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).

 

      Defendant has not shown the failure to attend the January 8, 2026 or otherwise substantively respond to the OSC re: Sanctions for Failure to Submit a Case Management Statement and failure to attend the November 7, 2025 Case Management Conference was due to mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. Defendant declares he failed to appear because he had no prior litigation experience and relied on a person hired through Upwork to assist with filings and docket monitoring. (Defendant Decl., ¶¶ 5-7.) Defendant seems to abandon his motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), as he states in reply, “Plaintiff misstates the motion: Defendant seeks relief from a void default under section 473(d), not merely discretionary relief for excusable neglect.” (Reply., p. 3:4-5.) The Court finds that the reasons provided by Defendant do not constitute excusable neglect under section 473, subdivision (b).

 

      Defendant had notice of the November 7, 2025, hearing because he was served via mail with the Court’s Order continuing the hearing to November 7, 2025. (See Clerk’s Certificate of Mailing Dated September 4, 2025.) Defendant did not attend the hearing on November 7, 2025 thus the Court set an OSC Re: Sanctions on Defendant for Failure to Submit a Case Management Statement; Failure to Appear on November 7, 2025.

 

      Although the Notice was served on Defendant via email – which Defendant did not consent to – Defendant does not argue he did not have notice of the OSC. (See Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6, subd. (a)(2)(A)(ii) (requiring express consent for electronic service).) As Plaintiff points out in opposition, Defendant has engaged in willful behavior which the Court had ample grounds to strike the Answer and enter default. (See Opp., p. 6:2-7.) The Court finds that this repeated pattern of behavior was not due to mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect.

 

      Therefore, the Motion to Set Aside is DENIED.

 

Conclusion

Defendant’s Motion to Set Aside the January 8, 2026 Minute Order entering default and striking his answer is DENIED.

MERCEDES-BENZ VEHICLE TRUST,  

 

Plaintiff, 

v. 

 

TIM SOUNAKHEN; ANTHONY JONES; and DOES 1 -10, inclusive,  

 

Defendants. 

 

  Case No.:  24SMCV06231 

  Hearing Date: June 29, 2026 

  [TENTATIVE] order RE: 

  DEFENDANT ANTHONY JONES’ motion for reconsideration 

 

 

Background

 

            This action arises from a breach of a lease agreement. Defendant Tim Sounakhen (“Defendant”) entered into a Lease Agreement with non-party WI Simonson. The five-year Lease Agreement was for a 2021 Mercedes-Benz G63W4, Vehicle Identification Number: W1NYC7HJ3MX394177 (“Vehicle”). In the same Lease Agreement, WI Simonson assigned its interest to Plaintiff Mercedes Benz Vehicle Trust (“Plaintiff”).  

            Under the lease agreement, Defendant agreed to pay Plaintiff and/or its successors a total sum of $231,083.01, consisting of $75,000 due at signing, followed by 59 months monthly payments of $2,635.39 per month, starting on May 20, 2021 and ending on May 20, 2026. But Defendant defaulted on his lease payments.  

            In the event Defendant fails to remit monthly payments, Plaintiff has the right to take possession of the Vehicle and to recover the outstanding residual of the lease plus costs, including attorneys’ fees and costs. As a result of Defendant’s failure to remit the required installment payments, Plaintiff has declared Defendant to be in default and declared the entire unpaid balance of the Agreement immediately due and payable, in the sum of $147,560.40. 

            Defendant relinquished possession of the Vehicle to Defendant Anthony Jones (“Jones”). Jones is in current possession of the Vehicle but refuses to surrender it to Plaintiff who is the legal owner of the vehicle.  

            This action ensued. Plaintiff sues Defendant for breach of contract, claim and delivery and conversion. Plaintiff later amended its Complaint to substitute Jones for one of the Doe Defendants. The Complaint seeks damages of $145,484.78, plus attorney’s fees and costs. It also seeks immediate possession of the Vehicle.  

Plaintiff served Jones with the summons and complaint on March 6, 2026, with a Notice and Acknowledgment of Receipt. Jones signed the Notice and Acknowledgment of Receipt on or around March 12, 2026. Jones did not respond. Plaintiff sought entry of default against Defendant and Jones, which was entered by the Clerk of the Court on April 14, 2026.

On May 19, 2026, the Court denied Jones’ Motion to Set Aside default judgment.

On June 2, 2026, Jones filed the instant motion for reconsideration of the May 19, 2026 Minute Order and renewed motion for relief from default. Jones also moves the Court to deem the proposed answer filed.

On June 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

On June 23, 2026, Jones filed a reply.

 

Legal Standard

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (a), a party may seek reconsideration of an order, from the same judge, within 10 days of service of that order. The party must include an affidavit alleging changed facts, circumstances or the law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (a).)       

As it relates to new or different facts, circumstances, or law under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (a), “the moving party must provide a satisfactory explanation for the failure to produce that evidence at an earlier time.” (Shiffer v. CBS Corp. (2015) 240 Cal.App.4th 246, 255.) Furthermore, “facts of which the party seeking reconsideration was aware at the time of the original ruling are not ‘“new or different.’” (In re Marriage of Herr (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1463, 1468.)      

 

Analysis

Here, Jones has not set forth new facts or law to support his motion for reconsideration. Jones was personally served with the Summons and Complaint on March 6, 2026. Jones signed the Notice and Acknowledgement of Receipt on March 12, 2026. Jones then failed to file a responsive pleading within the statutory timeframe and default was entered on April 14, 2026. The Court denied Jones’ Motion to Set Aside the Default on May 19, 2026.   The Proposed Answer submitted does not “cure the defect,” as Jones argues. (Mot., p. 4:13-24.)       

Although the May 19, 2026 Minute Order points out the procedural defect of failure to provide a proposed responsive pleading along with the Motion to Set Aside, that was not the only reason for the Court’s denial. The Court also found that Jones did not “point to any mistake, surprise, or excusable neglect.” (May 19, 2026 Minute Order, p. 3.) Moreover, Default has been entered against Jones and the Motion to Set Aside the Default was denied; thus any Proposed Answer is void. For purposes of the Motion for Reconsideration, a Proposed Answer does not constitute new facts that were not available to the movant at the time of filing the prior motion. 

Any alleged stipulation between the parties also does not constitute new or additional facts or law to support granting this Motion for Reconsideration. The stipulation entered into by the parties agreed to set aside Jones’ August 5, 2025 default. However, Default was then entered against Jones again on April 14, 2026. Thus, the Stipulation Order does not apply to the April 14, 2026 Default and does not constitute new or additional facts to support Jones’ Motion for Reconsideration, as this Stipulation Order was available to Jones at the time he filed his Motion to Set Aside.

Lastly, Jones’ arguments that Plaintiff will suffer no prejudice from this matter proceeding on the merits does not satisfy Jones’ burden of producing new or additional facts or law.

Jones failed to file a responsive pleading within the statutory timeframe and thus default was entered. The Motion to Set Aside Default was then denied by this Court and now Jones has failed to produce new facts or law to justify granting the instant Motion for Reconsideration.

Therefore, the Motion for Reconsideration is DENIED.

 

Conclusion

Defendant Anthony Jones’ Motion for Reconsideration is DENIED.
