Case Number: 23VECV01558    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Shaye v. Eskan Builders Corp, Case no. 23VECV01558

Hearing date July 22, 2026

D Eskan’s Motion to Compel Further Responses

Plaintiff Shaye, trustee of the eponymous property living trust, sues defendants Eskan Builders Corp., Saberi Construction Management and Ahmad Saberi for breach of contract, alleging defective construction work. Defendant Eskan moves to compel further responses to form and special interrogatories, set one. Plaintiff opposes.

Per Code Civ. Proc. §2031.310(a), “the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that . . . (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete. (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive...”

Eskan’s counsel substituted into this matter on 11/10/25, after which counsel identified alleged deficiencies in plaintiff’s prior discovery responses. Decl. Mashal, paras. 4-5. The parties met and conferred prior to an IDC on 4/17/26. Id. at paras. 7-11. Plaintiff served supplemental responses to special interrogatories on 5/11/26 and supplemental responses to form interrogatories on 5/12/26. Id. at para. 14, exh. 1, para. 16, exh. 2. Eskan asserts the further responses were deficient and provided only minimal clarifying information; counsel argues further responses are warranted. Id. at paras. 19-20, exhs. 3-4.

Plaintiff argues the motion constitutes a shift in the supplemental discovery initially sought. Eskan represented during meet and confer efforts that it only sought clarifying information on plaintiff’s initial responses and references to “defendants.” See Decl. Libo, exhs. A-B. Eskan’s counsel’s communications state the parties met and conferred regarding “…Responses to Interrogatories Plaintiff's responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One) and Special Interrogatories (Set One) vaguely reference 'defendants' without identifying any specific defendant…” Id. at exh. C. Meet-and-confer communications identify no other issues.

Eskan does not address plaintiff’s argument in reply, reasserting purported deficiencies. Counsels’ communications confirm the discovery was presented to plaintiff and the court as simply a request for clarification of discovery responses regarding the identity of defendants, not broader issues now identified in the separate statement. Sep. Statement, Decl. Libo, exhs. D-E.

The court continued the motion, held an IDC and ordered meet-and-confers, so the parties could informally identify outstanding issues with the responses and, hopefully, resolve the matter without the need for motion practice. Eskan’s failure to accurately identify the issues or to raise belated new issues frustrates the understanding of counsel and the court. Eskan’s motion is untimely as to initial responses served in December 2023. Any further discovery must be brought by motion within 45 days. See Code Civ. Proc. §2030.300(c). Further responses can only be ordered as to interrogatories to which plaintiff served further responses in May 2026.

Eskan is entitled to broad discovery, but there is no basis to award sanctions or deem objections waived; the unanticipated broadening of the discovery sought represents lack of diligence or gamesmanship. GRANTED in part; plaintiff to serve further, code-compliant responses as to discovery identified in this motion, previously supplemented in May 2026. No objections are deemed waived. No sanctions.


Case Number: 25VECV04823    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Nowaid v. Lopez, Case no. 25VECV04823

Hearing date July 22, 2026

Plaintiff Nowaid’s Motion for Leave to Serve via Publication - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Nowaid, in pro per, sues defendant Lopez for contractual fraud arising from a general partnership between plaintiff and defendant. Plaintiff filed a motion seeking leave to serve via publication on 1/26/26. The prior court, Hon. Harmon, denied the motion for failure to show diligence in attempting to serve defendant beyond making attempts at defendant’s home. 3/20/26 Min. Order. Plaintiff moves again for leave to serve via publication. The motion is unopposed.

“A summons may be served by publication if upon affidavit it appears to the satisfaction of the court in which the action is pending that the party to be served cannot with reasonable diligence be served in another manner specified in this article and that either: (1) A cause of action exists against the party upon whom service is to be made or he or she is a necessary or proper party to the action…” Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(a). An affidavit must be proffered which “must establish reasonable diligence by ‘probative facts’ based on personal knowledge.” Olvera v. Olvera (1991) 232 Cal.App.3d 32, 42.

The motion must be accompanied by an affidavit showing the party cannot, with reasonable diligence, be served in another manner. Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(a). Service was attempted via registered process server at defendant’s home address, 26881 Trestles Dr, Santa Clarita, CA 91351, seven times. Decl. Nowaid, paras. 6-7, exh. 1. Plaintiff conducted a public records search and has not identified alternative addresses for service. Id. at paras. 7-8, exh. 2. The records search identified two business entities associated with defendant; both use defendant’s home address, above, as the address for service. Id. As no other possible address for service has been identified, and as defendant is being sued in his individual capacity, plaintiff satisfied the diligence requirements of Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(a).

The motion must be accompanied by an affidavit establishing either “[a] cause of action exists against the party upon whom service is to be made or he or she is a necessary or proper party to the action” or “[t]he party to be served has or claims an interest in real or personal property in this state that is subject to the jurisdiction of the court or the relief demanded in the action consists wholly or in part in excluding the party from any interest in the property.” Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(a). This is a jurisdictional prerequisite. Olvera, supra, at 42, fn. 9.

The parties entered into an partnership on 9/25/21 whereby defendant was obligated to manage the partnership; plaintiff would front costs. Decl. Nowaid, paras. 3-4. Plaintiff asserts he met his obligations by providing $16,000, and defendant breached his obligations by operating without plaintiff’s approval and failing to obtain construction projects as envisioned by the partnership. Id. at para. 4. Plaintiff alleges defendant collected funds from plaintiff without intending to fulfill his obligations. Id. This is sufficient to satisfy Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(a).

Plaintiff seeks leave to publish in the Santa Clarita Valley Signal News, a local newspaper serving defendant’s address and surrounding municipality. Decl. Nowaid, para. 13. This is reasonably calculated to give defendant actual notice. GRANTED; plaintiff to publish in the SANTA CLARITA VALLEY SIGNAL NEWS as set forth under Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(b)-(c).
