DEPARTMENT 514 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV09134    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 514

Sandoval v. Azteca Import LLC
24STCV09134
Order to Show Cause re: Why Plaintiff’s Memorandum in Opposition to Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment Should not be Stricken
Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment

Tentative Ruling

On April 22, 2026, Defendant Uncle Phil LLC (“Defendant”) filed a motion for summary judgment or, in the alternative, for summary adjudication.  The hearing was initially set for July 17 and was continued to August 7.

On July 17, 2026, Plaintiff Judith Sandoval (“Plaintiff”) filed an opposition to the motion.  There is a problem with the page numbering of the opposition memorandum, which the Court assumes is advertent, but in any event a manual review reveals that the memorandum is 26 pages long.  That is improper.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1113(d) [“In a summary judgment or summary adjudication motion, no opening or responding memorandum may exceed 20 pages.”].)  Plaintiff did not seek permission to file an overly long memorandum. 

On July 24, 2026, the Court denied Defendant’s ex parte application to file an overly long reply memorandum.  The Court also set this Order to Show Cause re: Why the Opposition should not be Stricken.

Later on July 24, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel filed a declaration in response to the OSC.  On August 4, 2026, Defendant filed a response to the declaration.

The Court has considered the arguments and evidence presented by both sides.  Plaintiff has not shown good cause regarding why her opposition memorandum should not be stricken.  This is a straightforward slip-and-fall case, and good cause has not been shown regarding why the arguments in opposition to Defendant’s summary judgment motion cannot be made within 20 pages.

Accordingly, the Court ORDERS as follows:

The Court STRIKES the opposition memorandum (only) filed by Plaintiff on July 17, 2026.

The Court CONTINUES the hearing on Defendant’s motion for summary judgment to September 30, 2026 (or to a later date convenient for the Court and counsel).

The Court GRANTS LEAVE to Plaintiff to file a new memorandum in opposition to the motion for summary judgment that does not exceed 20 pages in length.  Plaintiff is NOT granted leave to file new evidence, a new request for judicial notice, or a new separate statement in opposition to the motion; the evidence and opposition separate statement filed on July 17, 2026, remain in the file and will be considered by the Court.  The new opposition memorandum must be filed and served no later than 20 days before the hearing.

The Court GRANTS LEAVE to Defendant to file a new (replacement) reply to Plaintiff’s opposition.  This is an optional filing.  Defendant may either (1) file a new reply or (2) stand on the reply filed on July 27, 2026.  If a new (replacement) reply is submitted, it must be filed and served no later than 11 days before the hearing.

Defendant is ordered to give notice.


Case Number: 26STCV02012    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 514

De La Barra v. Suh
26STCV02012
Defendant’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff to Provide Further Responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One)

Tentative Ruling

Defendant’s motion is denied.  Defendant’s request for sanctions is denied. 

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is granted in part.

Background

On January 20, 2026, William De La Barra (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against Andrew Dongin Suh, Joanne Oh, and Does 1 through 50, asserting a cause of action for negligence arising out of a motor vehicle accident on June 20, 2024 on Olympic Boulevard in Los Angeles.

On April 10, 2026, Andrew Dongin Suh and Joanne Oh filed an answer.

On July 7, 2026, Andrew Dongin Suh (“Defendant”) filed this motion to compel Plaintiff to provide further responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One).  Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 27, and Defendant filed a reply on July 31.

No trial date has been set.

Legal Standard

“On receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete. (2) An exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate. (3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a).)

Notice of a motion to compel further responses must be given “within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing.” (Id., subd. (c).)

A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a meet-and-confer declaration and a separate statement or, in the discretion of the Court, a “concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute.” (Id., subd. (b)(1) & (b)(2); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345.)

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a further response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (d).)

In Chapter 7 of the Civil Discovery Act, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (a) provides, in pertinent part, that the court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that any person “engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.” A “misuse of the discovery process” includes (among other things) failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery; making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to a discovery request; disobeying a court order to provide discovery; and making or opposing, unsuccessfully, a motion to compel without substantial justification. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subds. (d)-(h).)

Discussion

On April 10, 2026, Defendant served Plaintiff with Form Interrogatories (Set One). (Slack Decl., ¶ 4 & Exh. A.) On May 22, 2026, Plaintiff served his responses. (Id., ¶ 5 & Exh. B.)

Defendant moves to compel Plaintiff to provide further responses to Form Interrogatory 11.2.

Form Interrogatory 11.2 requires the responding party to provide certain information regarding any workers’ compensation claims in the past 10 years.  Plaintiff responded with information regarding only an injury occurring in March 2026 (after the accident at issue in this litigation).  (Slack Decl., Exh. B, at p. 17.)

Defendant argues that this response is materially incomplete, as it does not mention “prior workers’ compensation claim[s] noted in plaintiff’s responses to Form Interrogatories No. 10.1 which occurred from March 19, 1998 to July 15, 2013.”  (Mem., at 5:1-3.) 

Form Interrogatory 10.1 requires the responding party to provide certain information regarding prior “complaints or injuries that involved the same part of your body claimed to have been injured in the INCIDENT.”  In his response to Form Interrogatory 10.1, Plaintiff stated that he had experienced, and had sought medical care for, back and hand pain from March 1998 through July 2013 and neck and back pain in December 2023.  (Slack Decl., Exh. B, at p. 14.) 

Defendant argues that there is an inconsistency between Plaintiff’s responses to Form Interrogatories Nos. 10.1 and 11.2, but that does not appear to be the case – or, at the least, Defendant has not, on this record, shown that there is any inconsistency.  Nothing in the response to Form Interrogatory 10.1 identifies the injuries as related to a workers’ compensation claim.  And, of course, not every injury occurs in the workplace, and not every injury is covered by the workers’ compensation program.  The interrogatory responses indicate that Plaintiff experienced back and hand pain from March 1998 through July 2013 that had nothing to do with any injury covered by workers’ compensation.

Moreover, and independently, Form Interrogatory No. 11.2 asks only for information about workers’ compensation claims or demands made in the past 10 years.  In responding to Form Interrogatory 11.2 in May 2026, a responding party need not provide information regarding workers’ compensation claims made prior to May 2016.

Defendant has not shown that Plaintiff’s verified response to Form Interrogatory 11.2 is incomplete or otherwise deficient.  The motion to compel further responses is denied.

(The Court also notes that two arguments in Plaintiff’s opposition lack merit.  Defendant did file and serve a separate statement, and no Informal Discovery Conference was required by statute, court rule, or order.)

As Defendant’s motion to compel is denied, so is Defendant’s request for sanctions.

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is granted in part.  Defendant has unsuccessfully made a motion to compel a further response to an interrogatory.  Defendant has not acted with substantial justification, and there are no circumstances apparent in the record that would make the imposition of a sanction unjust.  The Court sets sanctions in the amount of $900, calculated based on three hours of attorney work multiplied by counsel’s reasonable billing rate of $300 per hour.  (Nemanpour Decl., ¶ 7.)

Conclusion

The Court DENIES the motion of Defendant Andrew Dongin Suh to compel Plaintiff William De La Barra to provide further responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One).

The Court DENIES Defendant’s request for sanctions.

The Court GRANTS IN PART Plaintiff’s request for sanctions.

The Court ORDERS Defendant Andrew Dongin Suh and his counsel of record, Slack & Associates, APC, jointly and severally, to pay monetary sanctions under the Civil Discovery Act in the amount of $900.00 to Plaintiff William De La Barra (through counsel) by September 8, 2026.

Counsel for Plaintiff is ORDERED to give notice.
