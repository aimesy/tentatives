Case Number: 23STLC06080    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Ashraf Mohammad Raoufi’s Motion to Set Aside Judgment Entered on February 19, 2026 is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On September 19, 2023, Plaintiff Jim & Stephanie Enterprises, Inc. dba Empire Plumbing Heating & Air Conditioning (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Ashraf Mohammad Raoufi (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer on February 13, 2024; at the time Defendant was represented by counsel. On February 4, 2025, Defendant filed a Substitution of Attorney changing to self-represented status.

 

Trial in this matter initially came for hearing on March 18, 2025, at which time the Court ordered Plaintiff to file a declaration showing its attempt to serve and call the number on the Substitution of Attorney five court days prior to trial. (Minute Order, 03/18/26.) Trial was continued to May 29, 2025. (Ibid.) Plaintiff’s counsel filed the declaration as ordered on May 21, 2025.

 

On May 29, 2025, the matter came for trial with no appearance by Defendant. (Minute Order, 05/29/25.) The Court took Plaintiff’s evidence and found in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant in the amount of $14,475.00. (Ibid.) Following multiple rejected proposed judgments, the Court entered judgment on February 19, 2026. (Judgment, 02/19/26.)

 

On June 2, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Judgment Entered on February 19, 2026 . Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 9, 2026, and Defendant replied on July 15, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves for relief from the judgment entered on February 19, 2026 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Under this statute, the Motion must be sought within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

 

The Motion is supported by a declaration in which Defendant attests that he listed the wrong address on the Substitution of Attorney form filed on February 4, 2025. (Motion, Raoufi Decl., ¶¶4-5 and Exh. A.) Defendant declares that as a result of this mistake, all the court filings were directed to “2141 Las Pasitas Court, Unit C, Livermore, California 94551” instead of the correct street name “Los Positas.” (Ibid.) The incorrect address was an old office that Defendant’s company used from approximately 2018-2019. (Id. at ¶5 and Exh. B.) Due to the papers being sent to this stale address, Defendant declares that he was unaware of the upcoming trial date. (Id. at ¶9.) Finally, Defendant declares that Defendant first learned of this action on May 26, 2026, when the company’s old office manager gave the papers to Defendant’s brother-in-law. (Id. at ¶10.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff disputes that this clerical error can be considered excusable, and that Defendant lacked notice. Regarding the second argument, Plaintiff’s counsel submits a declaration stating that he and Defendant spoke on May 21, 2025 for ten minutes regarding the trial, as ordered by the Court. (Opp., Ghaboosi Decl., ¶¶12-13.) This conversation is further corroborated by phone records from Plaintiff’s counsel’s office. (Id. at ¶¶14-5 and Exh. 2.) According to Plaintiff’s counsel, they discussed the error in the address Defendant listed, but Defendant declined to provide the correct address. (Id. at ¶13.)

 

Defendant’s reply does not sufficiently address the phone conversation between himself and Plaintiff’s counsel. It characterizes the conversation as occurring “seven months before trial documents were finalized and roughly ten months before entry of judgment.” (Reply, p. 1:27-28.) This ignores the fact that trial was held little more than a week after the conversation, on May 29, 2025. It is not credible that Defendant inadvertently ignored a conversation about such a pressing trial date. Nor does Defendant submit any further declaration in support of the reply to dispute Plaintiff’s account of the conversation. The Court finds the declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel credibly recounts a conversation that gave Defendant notice of the trial date, such that Defendant was aware of the need to appear on May 29, 2025. Defendant’s failure to appear for trial, therefore, cannot be attributed to mistake, inadvertance, surprise, or excusable neglect, as argued in the Motion.

 

Nor has Defendant shown that the timing of the instant Motion is reasonable. The evidence is that Defendant was aware of the trial date and the error in his address on file with the Court, but made no effort to check in regarding the proceedings or correct his address. It is not reasonable that Defendant willfully ignored the proceedings and then waited until June 2026 to seek relief. Defendant’s failure to appear, therefore, is not subject to relief pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Ashraf Mohammad Raoufi’s Motion to Set Aside Judgment Entered on February 19, 2026 is denied.

Case Number: 24STLC00272    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions, is granted in part and denied in part. Defendant Eduardo Rafael Garcia is to serve verified responses without objections to the Form Interrogatories, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is denied.

 

Plaintiff Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted; Request for Sanctions, is granted. Defendant Eduardo Rafael Garcia is ordered to pay sanctions of $285.00 to Plaintiff within 20 days’ notice of this ruling.

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) propounded Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Admission, Set One, on Defendant Eduardo Rafael Garcia (“Defendant”) on April 16, 2026. (Motions, Tapper Decl., ¶2 and Exh. A.) Defendant has not served responses to the discovery as of the June 12, 2026 filing of the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories; Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted; Request for Sanctions. (Id. at ¶3.)

 

Based on Defendant’s failure to serve initial responses to the discovery, the Motions to compel responses and to deem admissions admitted, are granted. There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses or deem admissions admitted can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2033.280.) Furthermore, the Motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide responses. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2033.280.) Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling Defendant to serve verified responses to the interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Plaintiff is also entitled to an order deeming the requests for admission admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.)

 

On a motion to deem requests for admission admitted, the Court is required to award sanctions under section 2033.280. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) While properly noticed, the sanctions sought are excessive for an unopposed motion to deem requests for admission admitted and for which Plaintiff’s counsel can appear telephonically. (Motion, Tapper Decl., ¶4.) Therefore, sanctions are granted against Plaintiff in the amount of $285.00 based on one hour of attorney time billed at $225.00 per hour, plus the $60.00 filing fee. (Id. at ¶¶5-6.)

 

Defendant requests sanctions for the Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, under which the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c), emphasis added.) Defendant did not make or oppose the instant Motion to Compel Responses. Therefore, sanctions are not available under section 2030.290.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions, is granted in part and denied in part. Defendant Eduardo Rafael Garcia is to serve verified responses without objections to the Form Interrogatories, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is denied.

 

Plaintiff Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted; Request for Sanctions, is granted. Defendant Eduardo Rafael Garcia is ordered to pay sanctions of $285.00 to Plaintiff within 20 days’ notice of this ruling.

Case Number: 25NWLC23115    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Enforce Settlement is continued to August 27, 2026.  By no later than July 29, 2026 Plaintiff is to file and serve a supplemental declaration addressing (1) the basis for the costs claimed in the sum $717.84, and (2) the calculation of the claimed interest in the sum of $2,347.68. 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 6, 2025, Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed this subrogation action against Defendant Paul Hutcherson aka Paul Henry Hutcherson dba Hutcherson Construction (“Defendant”). On February 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Mutual General Release and Settlement Agreement and the instant Motion to Set Aside Dismissal and Enter Judgment.

 

The Motion was served on the same day via first-class mail and electronic mail to Defendant. To date, no opposition has been filed.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion is brought under Code of Civil Procedure, section 664.6, which states in relevant part:

 

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (a).) “Parties” include “[a]n attorney who represents the party” and an insurer’s agent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (b).) The settlement must include the signatures of the parties seeking to enforce the agreement, and against whom enforcement is sought. (J.B.B. Investment Partners, Ltd. v. Fair (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 974, 985.) The settlement agreement complies with the statutory requirements set forth above because it was signed by both parties. (Motion, Aposhian Decl., Exh. 1, p. 3.)

 

The settlement provides that Defendant would pay Plaintiff $9,262.13 in three installment payments due on July 14, 2025, August 14, 2025, and September 14, 2025. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶1.) The settlement agreement also provides that in the event of Defendant’s default, Plaintiff may seek judgment in the full amount of liability ($20,550.63), plus interest, costs, and attorney’s fees as claimed in the Complaint. (Id. at Exh. 1, ¶2.) A total of $6,174.76 was paid towards the settlement, after which Defendant defaulted. (Id. at ¶3.)  Plaintiff seeks the principal amount plus interest in the sum of $2,347.68 and costs in the sum of $717.84, less payments in the sum of $6,174.76, for a total of $17,441.39.  (Id. at ¶¶4-6.)  The declarations do not speak to the calculation of the costs claimed, nor do they address the calculation of interest.  (See Motion, Aposhian, Richardson Decls.)  It appears from the Complaint that interest accrues on the amounts owed prior to treble damages, which would result in a lower amount than the amount claimed.  (See Compl., p. 6, ¶¶1-3.)  In any event, the Court will not award interest or costs without a declaration to substantiate the amounts.

 

In addition, Plaintiff is entitled to its attorney’s fees and costs incurred to enforce the Settlement Agreement. (Id., at Exh. 1, ¶10.) The supporting declaration attests to attorney’s fees in the amount of $1,750.00 and costs of $443.82, for a total amount of $2,193.82. (Motion, Aposhian Decl., ¶¶4-6.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Enforce Settlement is continued to August 27, 2026.  By no later than July 29, 2026 Plaintiff is to file and serve a supplemental declaration addressing (1) the basis for the costs claimed in the sum $717.84, and (2) the calculation of the claimed interest in the sum of $2,347.68.

Case Number: 25STCP02346    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE
RULING:

 

The Labor
Commissioner’s Motion to Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc to Correct Clerical Error
is granted. The Court signs the proposed order filed with this Motion. 

 

Analysis:

 

On June 24, 2025, the Labor
Commissioner filed a Request that Clerk Enter Judgment and Judgment on Final
Order, Decision or Award of the Labor Commissioner. The Court entered judgment
in favor of Plaintiff Michael Tlalpa and against Defendant Renee Avedissian on
June 25, 2025.

 

On May 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the
instant Motion to Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc to Correct Clerical Error. The
Motion was served on Defendant by first-class mail on the same day. No
opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves pursuant to Code of Civil
Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) to amend the judgment entered on June 24,
2025 on the grounds that the Labor Commissioner inadvertently attached the
wrong Order, Decision, or Award (“ODA”) to its Request that Clerk Enter
Judgment. The ODA attached pertained to Plaintiff Alejandro Tlalpa instead of the
correct party in this action, Plaintiff Michael Tlalpa. (Motion, Hernandez
Decl., ¶¶3-4.)

 

Courts have inherent powers to
correct judgments by a nunc pro tunc order where there has been clerical error
by clerk or by the judge himself, or where some provision of, or omission from,
order or judgment was due to inadvertence, or mistake of court. (Lane v.
Superior Court of Siskiyou County (1950) 98 Cal App 2d 165, 219; Code Civ.
Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) This includes clerical errors when made by an
attorney who drafts the judgment. (See In re Marriage of Kaufman (1980)
101 Cal.App.3d 147, 151.) However, while a trial court may correct clerical
errors and misprisions in a judgment, it cannot amend a judgment once entered,
if the error to be corrected is a judicial one, for instance if it embodies an
intentional action of the court even though legally erroneous. (Kamper v.
Mark Hopkins, Inc. (1947) 78 Cal App 2d 885.)

 

In light of the
Labor Commissioner’s clerical error attaching the wrong ODA to the Request for
Entry of Judgment, the Motion to amend the judgment by substituting the correct
ODA is granted. The Court deems substituted the ODA in State Case No.
WC-CM-812270 (Michael Tlalpa) for the ODA inadvertently attached from State
Case No. WC-CM-779891.  The correct ODA
is attached as Exhibit A to the Declaration of Jessenya Y. Herndandez, filed in
this matter on May 1, 2026. (See Mot., Hernandez Decl., Exh. A.)

 

Conclusion

 

The Labor
Commissioner’s Motion to Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc to Correct Clerical Error
is granted.

 

The Court signs
the proposed order filed with this Motion.

Case Number: 26STLC00627    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s Common Law Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Malwarebytes, Inc. (“Defendant”). Defendant filed its Answer on March 9, 2026. On March 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed (1) the Application for Leave to File Memorandum of Points and Authorities in Excess of Page Limit; (2) the Demurrer to Answer; and (3) the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings. On April 22, 2026, the Court sustained the Demurrer to the Answer with leave to amend as to the fifth through ninth and twenty-second affirmative defenses, and otherwise overruled the Demurrer. (Minute Order, 04/22/26.) Defendant filed its Amended Answer on May 12, 2026.

 

On May 14, 2026, Defendant filed a Motion to Require Plaintiff to Furnish Security. Defendant filed its opposition to the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings on June 5, 2026, to which Plaintiff filed an objection and Notice of Violation of Stay on June 8, 2026. At the initial hearing on the Motion, the Court continued the matter on account of the stay imposed by Code of Civil Procedure sections 391.1 and 391.6. (Minute Order, 06/22/26.) The Court denied Defendant’s Motion to Require Plaintiff to Furnish Security on July 7, 2026, without prejudice. (Minute Order, 07/07/26.)

 

Discussion

 

In light of the Court’s ruling denying Defendant’s Motion to Require Plaintiff to Furnish Security on July 7, 2026, the stay pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 391.6 is lifted.

 

Amended Answer filed on May 12, 2026

 

The first issue to address is whether Plaintiff’s Motion is moot on account of Defendant filing an Amended Answer after the Motion was filed. IN JKC3H8 v. Colton (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468, the Court of Appeals ruled that:

 

“ ‘ “[A]n amendatory pleading supersedes the original one, which ceases to perform any function as a pleading.” ’ ” (Foreman & Clark Corp. v. Fallon (1971) 3 Cal.3d 875, 884, 92 Cal.Rptr. 162, 479 P.2d 362.) “The amended complaint furnishes the sole basis for the cause of action, and the original complaint ceases to have any effect either as a pleading or as a basis for judgment. [Citation.] [¶] Because there is but one complaint in a civil action [citation], the filing of an amended complaint moots a motion directed to a prior complaint. [Citation.]” (State Compensation Ins. Fund v. Superior Court (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1124, 1130–1131, 109 Cal.Rptr.3d 88.)

 

(JKC3H8 v. Colton (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468, 477.) With the exception of substantively identical pleadings, the Court of Appeals holds that this rule renders demurrers to the original pleading moot. (Ibid; Delon Hampton & Associates, Chartered v. Superior Court (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 250, 253 n. 5.)

 

Here, Plaintiff moves for judgment on the pleadings on account of Defendant’s 30th and 31st affirmative defenses in the Answer, which are for Emotional Distress Caused by Other Factors and Emotional Distress Damages Unconstitutional, respectively. (Notice of Motion, p. 1:17-24.) Plaintiff also moves on the basis that Defendant is barred from raising any defense upon its own unlawful conduct, as demonstrated by the allegations in the Complaint. (Id. at p. 25-28.) The Amended Answer asserts the same affirmative defenses as the 29th and 30th affirmative defenses and is substantively identical to the Answer in this respect.

 

Given that the standard for ruling on a motion for judgment on the pleading is essentially the same as ruling on a demurrer, the Court finds the exception articulated in Delon Hampton applies and it will rule on the Motion as if directed to Defendant’s Amended Answer. (See Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.) 

 

The Court declines to strike Defendant’s opposition to the Motion, as Plaintiff asks. The opposition was filed during the stay of the action, however, hearing on the Motion was continued for sufficient time for Plaintiff to file a reply, should he so choose.  The minute order setting the continued hearing addressed the issue, stating, “Defendant has already filed its opposition. Plaintiff may file a reply in support of the Motion consistent with Code of Civil Procedure, §1005(b).”  (Minute Order, 06/24/26.)

 

Legal Standard

 

The standard for ruling on a motion for judgment on the pleadings is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer, that is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.  (Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.)  Where the moving party is the plaintiff, judgment on the pleadings may be granted on the basis that “that the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause or causes of action against the defendant and the answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense to the complaint.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(1)(A).) Matters which are subject to mandatory judicial notice may be treated as part of the complaint and may be considered without notice to the parties. Matters which are subject to permissive judicial notice must be specified in the notice of motion, the supporting points and authorities, or as the court otherwise permits. (Id.) The motion may not be supported by extrinsic evidence. (Barker v. Hull (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 221, 236.)

 

While a statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, et seq. must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration, there is no such requirement for a motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to the common law.

 

Doctrine of Confession-and-Avoidance

 

Plaintiff’s Motion begins with the erroneous premise that the Court must accept as true all the well-pleaded allegations of the Complaint and all the affirmative assertions made by Defendant in its Answer. (Motion, p. 8:10-11.) No authority is cited for this proposition regarding the allegations in the Complaint and it is contrary to California law:

 

The determination of the sufficiency of the answer requires an examination of the complaint because its adequacy is with reference to the complaint it purports to answer. (Chadbourn, Grossman, Van Alstyne, Cal. Pleading, § 1334, pp. 490, 491; Miller & Lux, Inc., v. San Joaquin Light & Power Corp., 120 Cal.App. 589, 600, 8 P.2d 560.) This requirement, however, does not mean that the allegations of the complaint, if denied, are to be taken as true, the rule being that the demurrer to the answer admits all issuable facts pleaded therein and eliminates all allegations of the complaint denied by the answer.

 

(South Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 733, emphasis added.) Plaintiff’s subsequent recitation of certain allegations in the Complaint as true for purposes of the instant Motion, therefore, is irrelevant.

 

Plaintiff argues that the language of the 30th and 31st affirmative defenses in the Answer (now the 29th and 30th affirmative defenses in the Amended Answer) confesses the truth of the allegations in the Complaint, namely, that Plaintiff suffered emotional distress. By the plain language of both defenses, however, Defendant has not admitted this fact. The 29th defense states in relevant part: “assuming arguendo that Plaintiff suffered any emotional distress (which Defendant denies), such emotional distress was proximately caused by factors other than Plaintiff’s work for Defendant, the actions of Defendant or anyone acting on its behalf or in its employ, or any of these.” (Amended Answer, filed 05/12/26, ¶29). Likewise, the next defense alleges “that Plaintiff is not entitled to recover emotional distress damages because California’s system of emotional distress damages is unconstitutional. (Id. at ¶30.) Neither defense “confesses” facts upon which the Court can find Defendant liable for Plaintiff’s injuries as they both “avoid” liability based on additional facts. In fact, Plaintiff’s confession-and-avoidance argument would always render a defendant liable on the alleged defense if it only looked to the “confession” portion of the defense.

 

Therefore, the 29th and 30th affirmative defenses are not grounds to grant Plaintiff judgment on the pleadings.

 

Doctrine of Unclean Hands

 

Plaintiff also moves for judgment on the pleadings by arguing that Defendant is barred from raising any defense based upon its own unlawful conduct, as alleged by the facts in the Complaint. As explained above, the Court does not take the allegations in the Complaint as true on the instant Motion. Instead, it eliminates all the facts alleged in the Complaint that were denied by the Amended Answer. The Amended Answer includes a general denial of each and every allegation contained in the Complaint. (Amended Answer, filed 05/12/26, p. 2:4-7.) The unclean hands doctrine, therefore, does not support a judgment on the pleadings against Defendant.

 

 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s Common Law Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC03015    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint filed by Defendants David Javdan, D.D.S. and David Javdan, D.D.S., Inc. dba Beverly Hills Prestige Dental Group is overruled. 

 

Defendants’ Answer due within 10 days from service of notice of this Order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Allie Ko (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the instant action for (1) professional negligence; and (2) intentional misrepresentation, in the alternative, negligent misrepresentation, against Defendants David Javdan, D.D.S. (erroneously sued herein as David Javdan, DDS) (“Defendant Javdan”) and David Javdan, D.D.S., Inc. dba Beverly Hills Prestige Dental Group (erroneously sued herein as Beverly Hills Prestige Dental Group) (“Defendant BHPDG”) on May 1, 2025. Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint on May 26, 2026.

 

Defendants filed the instant Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint on June 16, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 24, 2026 and Defendants replied on July 13, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Demurrer is accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. (Bridwell Decl., ¶¶3-4.) Defendants demur to the second cause of action – for intentional misrepresentation, in the alternative, negligent misrepresentation – for failure to allege sufficient facts and uncertainty. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subds. (e) and (f).) Because special demurrers are not permitted in the Limited Civil Court, the Court will not consider Defendants’ demurrer for uncertainty. (Code Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (c).)

 

In support of the second cause of action, the First Amended Complaint alleges that during Plaintiff’s visit to Defendants’ office on September 22 and 23, 2025, Defendant Javdan and Defendants’ staff misrepresented that: (1) Plaintiff had no cavities; (2) Plaintiff’s tooth #28 pain would be “fine in a couple of days,” with any remaining pain attributable to the deep cleaning; (3) Plaintiff’s gum disease had been cleared by the deep cleaning; and (4) Plaintiff’s clinical needs going forward were limited to cosmetic fillings and Invisalign orthodontic treatment. (FAC, ¶39.) Each of these representations was allegedly false at the time it was made, which Defendants knew or should have known in the exercise of reasonable care. (Id. at ¶¶40-41.) The decay on tooth #28 was visible on Plaintiff’s x-ray, 360-degree panoramic scan, and additional intraoral scan, which Defendants had at the time. (Id at ¶41.)

 

The elements of a cause of action for intentional misrepresentation are (1) a misrepresentation (including a false representation, concealment, or nondisclosure); (2) knowledge of falsity (scienter); (3) intent to induce reliance (i.e., intent to defraud); (4) actual and justifiable reliance on the misrepresentation; and (5) resulting damage. (Chapman v. Skype Inc., 220 Cal.App.4th 217, 230-231.) Fraud must be pleaded with particularity, which “necessitates pleading facts which show how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered.” (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 645.) The First Amended Complaint alleges that the misrepresentations were made by “Dr. Javdan and Defendants’ staff”. (FAC, ¶39.) The Demurrer cites no authority for its argument that these allegations are insufficient. (Demurrer, p. 7:4-5.)

 

Defendants also argue that their knowledge of the falsity of the statements is not sufficiently alleged because it relies on the contents of Plaintiff’s scans and x-rays to impute knowledge to Defendants. The First Amended Complaint, however, specifically alleges that Defendants reviewed the imaging and recognized the decay, but still made the misrepresentations in order to move on to selling Plaintiff other services. (FAC, ¶¶41-42.) These facts adequately allege Defendants’ knowledge of the true condition of Plaintiff’s teeth, and deliberate misrepresentation of the same.

 

Defendants contend that California law does not permit a fraud claim to rest solely on a medical opinion, citing Wang v. FMC Corp. (9th Cir. 1992) 975 F.2d 1412, 1414, overruled on other grounds by United States ex rel. Hartpence v. Kinetic Concepts, Inc., 792 F.3d 1121 (9th Cir. 2015) (en banc).   (Dem. at 7.)  The case concerned an appeal from an order granting defendant’s motion for summary judgment in a False Claims Act case.  The decision does not speak to pleading requirements nor address California law.  (See id. at 1420-21.)  It is not relevant to this Demurrer.

 

As indicated, Plaintiff pleads negligent representation in the alternative.  The elements of a cause of action for negligent misrepresentation are (1) a misrepresentation of a past or existing material fact; (2) without reasonable ground for believing it to be true; (3) with intent to induce another's reliance on the fact misrepresented; (4) justifiable reliance on the misrepresentation; and (5) resulting damage. (Chapman, supra, 220 Cal.App.4th at 231.) Defendants contend that Plaintiff “again has failed to allege any misrepresentation by Dr. Javdan versus his professional opinions.”  (Dem. at 7.)  Again, the Court finds the allegations sufficient to plead misrepresentation, without a reasonable basis.  (See FAC, ¶¶40-41.)   Defendants identify no other basis to find the facts pled insufficient.

 

Defendants argue that pleading in the two theories in the alternative is improper but cite no authority other than that it renders the cause of action uncertain.  (Dem. at 8.)  As indicated above, that is not a basis for demurrer in a Limited Civil court.

For all of the foregoing reasons, the Court overrules the Demurrer.

Conclusion

 

The Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint filed by Defendants David Javdan, D.D.S. and David Javdan, D.D.S., Inc. dba Beverly Hills Prestige Dental Group is overruled.
