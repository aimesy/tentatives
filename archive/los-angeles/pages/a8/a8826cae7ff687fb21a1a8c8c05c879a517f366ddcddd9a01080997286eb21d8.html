DEPARTMENT C LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24NWCV04942    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: C

DIAZ vs KACHIKWU, M.D., et al..

CASE NO.:  24NWCV04942

HEARING:  7/28/26 @ 9:30 AM

 

#9

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Joseph Avitabile, P.A.’s Unopposed Motion for Summary Judgment is GRANTED. 

 

Moving Party to give Notice.

 

 

Defendant Joseph Avitabile, P.A (Avitabile) moves for summary judgment pursuant to CCP § 437c on the grounds that he complied with the standard of care and did not cause the injuries complained of in this case.

 

On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff KARENA DIAZ (Plaintiff) filed a notice of non-opposition to Avitabile’s motion for summary judgment.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a medical malpractice case. Plaintiff alleges that on or about June 16, 2023, at PIH Health Hospital-Whittier, Defendants performed a surgical procedure described as debridement of an abdominal wall abscess, lavage, and wound vacuum-assisted dressing placement. (Complaint, ¶ 7.) Plaintiff alleges that Defendants failed to perform the procedure in a manner that met the standard of care. Defendants placed Adaptic dressings in the base of the wound adjacent to prosthetic mesh, and left the dressings there. The dressings were intended for use as external wound coverings to manage exudate and protect healing tissues from direct contact with secondary dressings. They were not designed for internal use. (Complaint, ¶ 8.)

 

The retained dressings acted as a nidus for bacteria and a focus of infection, and, following the June 16, 2023, surgery, plaintiff continued to suffer from complaints of abdominal pain and infection, and from foul discharges of fluids. She was required to seek medical attention on numerous occasions. (Complaint, ¶ 9.) Plaintiff did not discover, and despite her exercise of reasonable care could not have discovered, her injury and the cause of her injury until May 17, 2024, when another abdominal surgery was performed and the surgeon found three Adaptic dressings had remained in the body without any therapeutic or diagnostic purpose or effect. (Complaint, ¶ 10.)

 

Based thereon, Plaintiff asserts a cause of action for: 1) Professional Negligence.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party seeking summary judgment has the burden of producing evidentiary facts sufficient to entitle them to judgment as a matter of law. (CCP § 437c(c).) The moving party must make an affirmative showing that they are entitled to judgment irrespective of whether or not the opposing party files an opposition. (Villa v. McFerren (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 733, 742743.) Thus, “the initial burden is always on the moving party to make a prima facie showing that there are no triable issues of material fact.” (Scalf v. D. B. Log Homes, Inc. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1510, 1519 (citing Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850.)

When a plaintiff seeks summary judgment, they must produce admissible evidence on each element of each cause of action on which judgment is sought. (CCP § 437c(p)(1).) When a defendant seeks summary judgment, they have the “burden of showing that a cause of action has no merit if the party has shown that one or more elements of the cause of action, even if not separately pleaded, cannot be established, or that there is a complete defense to the cause of action.” (CCP § 437c(p)(2).)

The opposing party on a motion for summary judgment is under no evidentiary burden to produce rebuttal evidence until the moving party meets their initial movant’s burden. (Binder v. Aetna Life Insurance Company (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 832, 840.) Once the initial movant’s burden is met, then the burden shifts to the opposing party to show, with admissible evidence, that there is a triable issue requiring the weighing procedures of trial. (CCP § 437c(p).) The opposing party may not simply rely on their allegations to show a triable issue but must present evidentiary facts that are substantial in nature and rise beyond mere speculation. (Sangster v. Paetkau (1998) 68Cal.App.4th 151, 162.) Summary judgment must be granted “if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7Cal.App.4th1110, 1119.)

The duty owed by a medical provider is established by the standard of care followed by other medical providers in the same or similar community. “Expert evidence in a medical malpractice suit is conclusive as to the proof of the prevailing skill and learning in the locality and of the propriety of the particular conduct by the practitioner and in particular instances because such a standard and skill is not a matter of general knowledge and can only be supplied by expert testimony.” (Willard v. Hagemeister (1991) 121 Cal.App.3d 406, 412.) Where the moving party produces competent expert opinion declarations showing that there is no triable issue of fact on an essential element of the opposing party's claim, the opposing party's burden is to produce competent expert opinion declarations to the contrary. (Ochoa v. Pacific Gas & Elec. Co. (1998) 61 CA4th 1480, 1487.) 

DISCUSSION

 

“The elements of a cause of action for medical malpractice are: (1) a duty to use such skill, prudence, and diligence as other members of the profession commonly possess and exercise; (2) a breach of the duty; (3) a proximate causal connection between the negligent conduct and the injury; and (4) resulting loss or damage.” (Chakalis v. Elevator Solutions, Inc. (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 1557, 1571.)

 

“‘The standard of care against which the acts of a physician are to be measured is a matter peculiarly within the knowledge of experts; it presents the basic issue in a malpractice action and can only be proved by their testimony (citations), unless the conduct required by the particular circumstances is within the common knowledge of the layman.’” (Landeros v. Flood (1976) 17 Cal. 3d 399, 410.)

 

Avitabile moves for summary judgment on the grounds that no triable issue of material fact exists regarding whether the care and treatment provided by him met the standard of care. Additionally, no triable issue of material fact exists regarding whether an act or omission by him caused or contributed to Plaintiff’s injury. In support of the motion, Avitabile evidences that he assisted Dr. Kachikwu with surgery on June 16, 2023.

 

·       The records confirm that Dr. Kachikwu made a surgical decision to place an Adaptic dressing under Plaintiff's wound vac and over her mesh. This piece of Adaptic was not missed during the count of materials at the end of surgery because Dr. Kachikwu clearly noted in her operative report that she intentionally placed the Adaptic in the wound base. The note indicates she intended it to stay there. (Cole, M.D., Decl., ¶ 27.)

·       Avitabile was not responsible for the surgical decision to place the Adaptic. (Cole Decl. ¶ 28.)

·       Dr. Kachikwu wrote clear orders that the wound vac and dressings should be changed every 3 days by the home health provider, Miracle Home Health. She ordered that a piece of Adaptic should be placed over the mesh and under the wound vac at every dressing change until the mesh was no longer visible. The records from Miracle Home Health confirm that they were or should have been aware that Dr. Kachikwu placed Adaptic under the wound vac during her surgery and that they were to perform dressing changes every 3 days, including changing the Adaptic in Ms. Diaz's wound. (Cole Decl. ¶ 2.)

·       Avitabile did not see Ms. Diaz after surgery. He did not perform any dressing changes. He merely acted as Dr. Kachikwu's assistant during the June 16, 2023 surgery. (Cole Decl. ¶ 30.)

 

The Court finds that Avitabile has met his initial burden of showing that no triable issue of material fact exists regarding whether Avitabile met the standard of care in his treatment of Plaintiff. Plaintiff’s medical records demonstrate that Avitabile was there as an assistant to the surgeon, did not make any surgical decisions — including the one to place an Adaptic dressing, and was not responsible for removing the Adaptic dressing. (Cole Dec. ¶¶ 11-14, Exh. C.) Moreover, Dr. Cole has opined that Avitabile met the standard of care in his treatment of Plaintiff. (Cole Decl. ¶ 27.) Given the evidence demonstrating Avitabile met the standard of care in his treatment of Plaintiff, there is no negligent conduct that was a cause-in-fact of Plaintiff’s injury. (Bromme v. Pavitt (1992) 5 Cal.App.4th 1487, 1502, 7 Cal.Rptr.2d 608.)

 

The burden therefore shifts to Plaintiff to demonstrate a triable issue of material fact exists, however, Plaintiff has filed a notice of non-opposition.

 

Accordingly, Avitabile’s Motion for Summary Judgment is GRANTED.

 

 


Case Number: 25NWCV00393    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: C

AGUILAR vs MIKE TOPANGA, INC. DBA THE HOLIDAY, et al.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV00393

HEARING:  7/28/26 @ 9:30 AM

#10

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff ALEX PETER AGUILAR’s Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records is GRANTED. Plaintiff’s Motion for Monetary Sanctions is DENIED.  Defendant MIKE TOPANGA, INC. dba THE HOLIDAY GENTLEMEN’S CLUB’s Motion for Sanctions is GRANTED in part.  Sanctions in the amount of $1,175.00 are imposed upon Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel, jointly and severally, payable within 30 days. 

Defendant to give NOTICE.

 

Plaintiff ALEX PETER AGUILAR (Plaintiff) moves for an order quashing defendant MIKE TOPANGA, INC. dba THE HOLIDAY GENTLEMEN’S CLUB’s (Defendant) subpoena to Plaintiff’s prior employers pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1987.1; 2023.010.

Background

This is a premises liability case. Plaintiff alleges that on July 15, 2023, in the parking lot of The Holiday Gentlemen's Club, located at 10915 Norwalk Boulevard, Santa Fe Springs, California, Plaintiff was a lawfully present patron when he was attacked by five individuals who punched, kicked, and stomped on his head more than twenty times, rendering him unconscious. As a result of Defendant's failure to provide adequate security, Plaintiff sustained serious injuries. (First Amended Complaint, ¶¶ 8-18.)

Legal Standard

“If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court’s own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.” (CCP § 1987.1(a).)

A separate statement is required for a motion to quash. (California Rules of Court (CRC) Rule 3.1345(a)(5).) This separate statement must set forth the particular documents or demands at issue and the factual and legal reasons why production should not be compelled. (CRC Rule 3.1345(c).)

“A deposition subpoena that commands only the production of business records for copying shall designate the business records to be produced either by specifically describing each individual item or by reasonably particularizing each category of item . . . .” (CCP § 2020.410 (a).)

Current discovery standards hold that, “any party may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, that is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action . . . if the matter either is itself admissible in evidence or appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.” (Board of Registered Nursing v. Superior Court (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 1011, 1039.)

Discussion

On April 10, 2026, Defendant issued two Deposition Subpoenas for Production of Business Records directed to: (1) Allegis Group, Inc. (MCS #70541-31); and (2) Safran Aerosystem Services Americas, LLC (MCS #70541-32), both commanding production of records by May 11, 2026. Both subpoenas are identical in scope and demand, without any limitation as to time or subject matter, "[a]ny and all documents concerning the employment or the engagement as an Independent contractor" of Plaintiff, including but not limited to resumes, contracts, offer letters, job descriptions, discipline records, performance reviews, termination reports, absence records, incident reports, pre-employment examination records, W-2 and 1099 forms, compensation and benefits information, health plan records, and "all documents included in your files concerning the above listed subject." (Asryan Declaration, ¶ 2, Exh. 1.)

Plaintiff argues the subject deposition subpoena seeks private information protected by the California Constitution, such that a heightened standard for discovery applies. (Calif. Const. Art. 1, § 1; Britt v. Sup.Ct. (San Diego Unified Port Dist.) (1978) 20 Cal.3d 844, 855-856.)  Plaintiff contends the request is impermissibly overbroad because Defendant requests Plaintiff’s entirety of Plaintiffs employment records from two separate employers, Allegis Group, Inc. and Safran AerosystemServices Americas, LLC, without any restriction as to time period or subject matter. Defendant’s demand encompasses Plaintiffs private compensation, tax, benefits, and personnel records.

In opposition, Defendant argues that during the meet and confer process, Plaintiff’s counsel proposed limiting the records relating to the injuries at issue and/or workers’ compensation medical records. Defendant agrees that this scope of documents should be produced in response the subpoenas. Therefore, the parties agreed that wage and financial records need not be produced.  Defendant demands the following: (1) employment records showing any leaves of absence following the incident, and (2) any records showing any work restrictions after the incident. These records are directly relevant to the case because they would tend to show whether or not Plaintiff could and did continue working at his job following the incident.

In reply, Plaintiff contends that Defendant failed to withdraw the currently active and unlimited subpoenas. Defendant could have withdrawn these subpoenas and issued new narrowly tailored version that were limited to the scope that the parties agreed upon. Thus, Plaintiff’s motion remained necessary after the meet and confer efforts.

The Court finds Defendant’s subpoenas are overbroad and seek documents outside the scope of discovery because they are not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence. This right of privacy applies to insurance, medical and financial records. (John B. v. Sup.Ct. (2006) 38 Cal.4th 1177, 1198 (medical records); Moskowitz v. Superior Court (1982) 137 Cal.App.3d 313, 315 (financial records) ["Personal financial information comes within the zone of privacy protected by article I, section 1 of the California Constitution."].) Plaintiff’s right of privacy also extends to his confidential financial affairs, even when the information sought is admittedly relevant to the litigation. (Moskowitz v. Superior Court (1982) 137 Cal.App.3d 313, 315.)

Here, Plaintiff does not claim loss of earnings related to this civil action. Moreover, the documents requested by Defendant are overbroad because they seek without limitation as to time or subject matter "any and all documents concerning the employment or the engagement as an Independent contractor" of Plaintiff, including resumes, offer letters, compensation records, W-2 and 1099 forms, performance reviews, discipline records, absence records, health plan records, incident reports, and "all documents included in your files" relating to Plaintiff. (Asryan Declaration, ¶ 2, Exh. 1.) Defendant has agreed to limit the scope of the employment records to “leaves of absence following the incident, as well as any records showing any work restrictions after the incident.” Defendant argues that these types of documents would provide information relevant to Plaintiff’s injuries, and the impact of those injuries on his ability to work. (Opp. p. 3: 7-10.) Given the parties had reached an agreement, the demand shall be limited to (1) employment records showing any leaves of absence following the incident, and (2) any records showing any work restrictions after the incident.

Monetary Sanctions

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.2 authorizes the imposition of monetary sanctions in connection with the filing of a motion to quash if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2.)

Plaintiff argues that Defendant failed to withdraw the overbroad subpoenas despite Plaintiff’s meet and confer efforts, thus necessitating this motion. Plaintiff’s counsel requests compensation for five hours at the rate of $250.00 per hour, plus filing fees of $80.72, for a total of $1,330.72.

In opposition, defense counsel Justin Bubion argues that the declaration of plaintiff’s counsel, Gor Asyran, omits key facts regarding their telephone call on Mary 4, 2026.  Among them, Bubion told Asyran that they should continue discussing the issue and not proceed with any motions. (Bubion Decl., ¶ 3.) During the call, Bubion proposed that they engage in an informal discovery conference (IDC) with the court. Asyran was not interested. (Id., ¶ 4.) Bubion also proposed that if the parties could not resolve the dispute informally, and a motion was filed, that they should agree not to seek sanctions against each other.  Asyran was not interested. (Id., ¶ 5.) Defense counsel seeks compensation for six hours at the rate of $235.00 per hour for a total of $1410.00. 

The Court typically does not impose sanctions at the request of a party opposing a discovery motion, but in this case, sanctions are warranted against the moving party.  In reply, Plaintiff does not dispute any of the aforementioned details of the May 4 telephone call.  The parties are referred to the Courtroom Information for Dept. SE-C which states that informal discovery conferences are strongly encouraged in lieu of discovery motions.  Despite Defendant’s willingness to further meet and confer, Plaintiff filed the instant motion on May 6, 2026, a mere two days after their telephone call.  The Court finds that Plaintiff, in bringing this motion when he did, acted prematurely.  It can hardly be said that Defendant’s conduct necessitated the instant motion.  Thus, sanctions in the amount of $1,175.00 are imposed upon Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel, jointly and severally, payable within 30 days. 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Documents is GRANTED. Plaintiff’s Motion for Sanctions is DENIED.  Defendant’s Motion for Sanctions is GRANTED in part.
