I.       BACKGROUND  

On April 3, 2025, Plaintiff Dianna Sanchez filed a complaint against Defendants Keolis Transit Services, LLC, Keolis Transit America, Inc. and Eric Anthony Aguiar (“Defendants”).  On July 8, 2025, Plaintiffs Diana Sanchez and Rodrigo Sanchez filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants alleging (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.  Plaintiff was injured when a bus owned and operated by Defendants collided with her car. 

            On April 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of the entire action.  On June 22, 2026, the Court entered a dismissal of the entire action and retained jurisdiction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 to enforce the settlement. 

            On August 10, 2026, Plaintiff moved to enforce the settlement agreement.  On September 8, 2026, Defendants Keolis Transit Services, LLC, Keolis Transit America, Inc. and Eric Anthony Aguiar filed an opposition.  No reply has been filed as of September 16, 2026.

II.          LEGAL STANDARDS

“If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If requested by the parties, the court may retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.”  (Code Civ. Proc., §664.6.)

“Section 664.6 was enacted to provide a summary procedure for specifically enforcing a settlement contract without the need for a new lawsuit.”  (Weddington Productions, Inc. v. Flick (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 793, 809.)  “The statute recognizes that a settlement may be summarily enforced in either of two situations: where the settlement was made orally before the trial court or where it was made in writing outside the presence of the court.”  (Elyaoudayan v. Hoffman (2003) 104 Cal.App.4th 1421, 1428.)  Disputes regarding the terms of the settlement (or other disputed facts) may be adjudicated on a CCP § 664.6 motion, on the basis of declarations or other evidence.  (Malouf Bros. v. Dixon (1991) 230 Cal.App.3d 280, 284.)  “Although a judge hearing a section 664.6 motion may receive evidence, determine disputed facts, and enter the terms of a settlement agreement as a judgment, nothing in section 664.6 authorizes a judge to create the material terms of a settlement, as opposed to deciding what terms the parties themselves have previously agreed upon.”  (Osumi v. Sutton (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 1355, 1360.) 

    Once an action is dismissed, the court loses jurisdiction over the parties and the subject matter of the suit.  (Wackeen v. Malis (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 429, 437.)  However, pursuant to CCP §664.6, “[i]f requested by the parties, the court may retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.”  Thus, a court may retain jurisdiction under CCP 664.6, notwithstanding dismissal, but only if a proper request for retention of jurisdiction is made by the parties.  (Id. at 439-440 (unless parties presented trial court with a proper request to retain jurisdiction for purposes of section 664.6 motions, trial court did not have subject matter jurisdiction over action to enforce settlement post-dismissal).) In addition, the Court may dismiss without prejudice and retain jurisdiction to enforce a settlement agreement pursuant to CCP §664.6(e) if a Notice of Conditional Settlement is filed, as was done in this case.

III.       DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs move to enforce the parties’ April 14, 2026 settlement agreement.  Plaintiffs argue Defendants were obligated under the settlement agreement to pay Plaintiffs $200,000 in settlement plus $5,450 for Plaintiffs’ portion of the mediation costs.  Plaintiffs argue Defendants have not paid the $5,450 in mediation costs.  Plaintiffs argue Defendants attempted to pay with a $5,450 gift card on June 18, 2026, after Defendants stated on May 19, 2026 that they would follow up with the insurance carrier on the status of the check.  Plaintiffs’ counsel had no method of depositing the gift card as cash.  Plaintiffs’ counsel emailed defense counsel on July 9, 2026 to inform them that no check had been received and the mediation costs remained unpaid. 

Plaintiffs ask the Court to enter judgment in the amount of $5,450, plus statutory interest in the amount of 10% per annum from April 14, 2026, totaling $176.19 through August 10, 2026 and continuing to accrue until entry of judgment.  Plaintiffs also ask that the Court award them attorney’s fees in the amount of $3,000 for 3 hours of attorney time at $1,000/hr and $73.41 in filing fees.

In opposition, Defendants argue they are not in breach of the settlement agreement.  Defendants argue the settlement agreement does not impose any deadline on their payment of the $5,450 in mediation costs, nor did it specify a method of payment.  Defendants argue they sent a prepaid card in the amount of $5,450 on June 18, 2026.  Defendants argue Plaintiffs acknowledged receipt of the card but had no method of depositing it.  Defendants responded by immediately working with the insurance carrier to issue payment by check.  Defendants issued a check in the amount of $5,450 via overnight delivery to Plaintiffs’ counsel on August 11, 2026, the day after this motion to enforce settlement was filed.  Defendants argue the motion is moot. 

Plaintiffs fail to establish breach of the settlement agreement.  Plaintiffs admit that Defendants sent payment on June 18, 2026 by prepaid card.  The settlement agreement does not impose any deadline or specify a method of payment for the reimbursement of Plaintiffs’ $5,450 in mediation costs.  (Motion, Farrugia Dec., ¶3, Ex. A, ¶2.)  As such, performance must be within a reasonable time.  (The McCaffrey Group, Inc. v. Supr. Ct. (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1330, 1351 (“it is a well-established principle of contract law that if no time is specified for the performance of an act required to be performed, a reasonable time is allowed”); CACI No. 319.)

            Defendants attempted in good faith to pay the $5,450 in mediation costs by prepaid card on June 18, 2026 after the carrier’s internal system would not process the request to obtain a check.  (Motion, Farrugia Dec., Ex. C.)  Defendants contacted the carrier multiple times after Plaintiffs informed them that they had not received a check.  (Id.; Opposition, Fischer Dec., ¶¶6-9.)  Plaintiffs informed Defendants that the prepaid card was not acceptable on June 22, 2026 and July 9, 2026.  (Motion, Farrugia Dec., Ex. C; Opposition, Fischer Dec., ¶¶6-9.)  Defense counsel ultimately issued a check for $5,450 on August 11, 2026, the day after the motion was filed.  (Opposition, Fischer Dec., ¶¶6-9.) 

            Defendants attempted in good faith to pay the $5,450 on June 18, 2026 and paid by check on August 11, 2026.  Defendants made the bulk of the settlement payment of $200,000 on May 19, 2026.  (Id. at ¶5.)  In total, approximately two months passed from execution of the settlement agreement and attempted payment of the $5,450 and four months passed from execution of the settlement agreement and full payment of the settlement amount, including the $5,450 in mediation fees.  Payment in full was made within a reasonable amount of time from execution of the settlement agreement on April 14, 2026 and dismissal of the action on April 21, 2026. 

            Defendants also argue the motion must be denied because Defendants did not sign the settlement agreement.  “If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”  (Code Civ. Proc., §664.6.)  “We read the statute’s requirement of a writing ‘signed by the parties’ to require the signatures of the parties seeking to enforce the agreement under section 664.6 and against whom the agreement is sought to be enforced.”  (Harris v. Rudin, Richman & Appel (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 299, 305.)  The settlement agreement was only signed by Plaintiffs and Plaintiffs’ counsel.  However, as noted above, the Court in this case retains jurisdiction pursuant to subdivision (e) of section 664.6 as Notice of Conditional settlement was filed on 4/21/26.

            Plaintiffs’ Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement is denied.  Plaintiffs fail to establish a breach of the settlement agreement. 

IV.    CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement is denied. 

Moving party to give notice.

I.       BACKGROUND  

            Plaintiff alleges it entered into a settlement agreement with Defendant Jessican Mulhearn on August 23, 2022 (the “Settlement Agreement”) in connection with Mulhearn v. ADR Properties, et al., Case No. 22STCV08195 (the “Underlying Action”).  Plaintiff alleges Defendant breached the Settlement Agreement when she failed to vacate Apartment 315 at 5417 Zelzah Avenue, Encino, CA by August 31, 2024.  Plaintiff alleges it filed a motion to enforce the Settlement Agreement in the Underlying Action, which the court granted.  Pursuant to the court’s order enforcing the Settlement Agreement, judgment was entered as follows:  (1) possession of the unit was given to plaintiff; (2) Defendant was to vacate the unit within three days after entry of the order enforcing settlement; and (3) Plaintiff was entitled to the fair market rent of the unit after August 31, 2024 (the “Judgment”).  Plaintiff alleges Mulhearn did not vacate the unit until August 14, 2025 and has not paid any compensation for occupying the unit after August 31, 2024. 

            Plaintiff alleges that Defendant was required under the Settlement Agreement to leave the unit in the same condition she received it in, minus wear and tear.  Plaintiff alleges Defendant failed to do so.

            On September 18, 2025, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant alleging (1) breach of contract; and (2) unjust enrichment.

            On April 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for summary judgment or in the alternative, summary adjudication.  On July 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of non-opposition.  On July 9, 2026, Defendant filed a motion to continue the hearing on the motion.  On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to the motion to continue.  On July 20, 2026, the court granted the motion to continue the hearing on the motion for summary judgment to September 19, 2026. 

II.          LEGAL STANDARDS

            In reviewing a motion for summary judgment, the Court must apply a three-step analysis: (1) identify the issues framed by the pleadings; (2) determine whether the moving party has met his or her initial burden; and (3) determine whether the opposition has demonstrated the existence of a triable, material factual issue. (Hinesley v. Oakshade Town Center (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 289, 294.) “[T]he initial burden is always on the moving party to make a prima facie showing that there are no triable issues of material fact.” (Scalf v. D.B. Log Homes, Inc. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1510, 1519.) A defendant moving for summary judgment or summary adjudication has met his or her burden of showing that a cause of action has no merit if the party has shown that one or more elements of the cause of action . . . cannot be established, or that there is a complete defense to the cause of action.” (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 437c(p)(2).)

            A moving defendant may satisfy the initial burden to show that one or more elements cannot be established by “presenting evidence that conclusively negates an element of the plaintiff's cause of action or by relying on the plaintiff's factually devoid discovery responses to show that the plaintiff does not possess, and cannot reasonably obtain, evidence to establish that element.” (Schmidt v. Citibank, N.A. (2018) 28 Cal.App.5th 1109, 1119; Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 856 [it is insufficient for the defendant to merely point out the absence of evidence.]; Gaggero v. Yura (2003) 108 Cal.App.4th 884, 891.) “The defendant must present evidence, typically in the form of factually devoid discovery responses, showing that plaintiff does not have and cannot reasonably obtain evidence to support his or her claim.” (Id.)

Unless and until a defendant meets that burden, the plaintiff has no burden to present controverting evidence. (Consumer Cause, Inc. v. SmileCare (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 454, 468 [“There is no obligation on the opposing party . . . to establish anything by affidavit unless and until the moving party has by affidavit stated facts establishing every element . . . necessary to sustain a judgment in his favor.”].) If a defendant fails to meet that burden, summary judgment must be denied, even if the plaintiff fails to file an opposition and fails to proffer any evidence.

On the other hand, if the defendant “has met that burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff . . . to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to the cause of action or a defense thereto.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(2).) The plaintiff may not merely rely on allegations or denials in its pleadings to show that a triable issue of material fact exists, but instead “shall set forth the specific facts showing that a triable issue of material fact exists as to the cause of action.” (Id.) “If the plaintiff cannot do so, summary judgment should be granted.” (Avivi v. Centro Medico Urgente Medical Center (2008) 159 Cal.App.4th 463, 467.)

III.       DISCUSSION

            A.  Defendant did not file any opposition despite continuance

            The court continued the motion for summary judgment from July 20, 2026 to September 19, 2026 so that Defendant could prepare an opposition.  Defendant has not filed an opposition as of September 16, 2026.  The motion is unopposed.  However, Plaintiff may only obtain the requested relief if it satisfies its initial burden as moving party, regardless of whether the motion is unopposed.  (Consumer Cause, Inc., supra, 91 Cal.App.4th 454, 468.)

            B.  Plaintiff fails to satisfy its burden as moving party on the first cause of action for breach of contract

            Plaintiff fails to allege or establish breach of the Settlement Agreement based on Defendant’s failure to pay fair market rent from September 1, 2024 to August 14, 2025.  (Complaint, ¶¶11-12.)  Plaintiff alleges the obligation to pay fair market rent was based on the Judgment entered in the Underlying Action after the court granted its motion to enforce settlement agreement.  (Id.)  There is no allegation that the Settlement Agreement created any obligation to pay fair market rent for the period of September 1, 2024 to August 14, 2025. 

            Plaintiff also fails to present any evidence that the Settlement Agreement required Defendant to pay fair market rent from September 1, 2024 August 14, 2025.  (Plaintiff’s SSUMF Nos. 9-10; Ozer Dec., ¶¶11-13, Exs. 1 and 2.)  The Settlement Agreement contains no such provision.  (Ozer Dec., Ex. 1.)  The Settlement Agreement only states that Plaintiff would waive any rental payments between the date of the Settlement Agreement to August 31, 2024, “for which the reasonable fair rental value per month would have been [$3,095].”  (Id. at ¶4.3.)  However, the Judgment entered in the Underlying Action imposed such an obligation on Defendant.  (Plaintiff’s SSUMF Nos. 9-10, Ozer Dec., Ex. 2.)

            Plaintiff therefore fails to establish breach of the Settlement Agreement based on Defendant’s failure to pay fair market rent.  Plaintiff is in fact seeking to enforce the Judgment entered in the Underlying Action.  Plaintiff cannot do so by a complaint for breach of contract. 

            Plaintiff also alleges breach of the Settlement Agreement based on the failure to vacate the unit by August 31, 2024.  (Complaint, ¶¶9, 10,; Ozer Dec., Ex. 1, ¶4.1.)  However, Plaintiff already moved to enforce the settlement agreement based on Defendant’s breach of ¶4.1 and her obligation to vacate by August 31, 2024.  Plaintiff was entitled to either sue in a separate action for that breach or file a motion to enforce the settlement agreement, not both.  To the extent Plaintiff asserts otherwise, Plaintiff fails to cite any authority allowing it to sue for breach of the same contractual provision that was the basis of its motion to enforce the settlement agreement in the Underlying Action and the Judgment entered therein. 

            Plaintiff also alleges breach of ¶4.1 based on Defendant’s failure to leave the unit in the same condition as it was in when she received it.  (Complaint, ¶¶17, 18.)  Plaintiff alleges damages in the amount of $1,775 as a result of Defendant’s breach of ¶4.1.  However, summary adjudication can only be granted when it fully disposes of a cause of action.  (Code Civ. Proc., §437c, subd. (f)(1); Hindin v. Rust (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1247, 1259 (motion for summary adjudication “purporting to establish that some but not all of the multiple grounds for liability asserted in the prior action were brought with probable cause is improper for failure to completely dispose of an entire cause of action” as required under section 437c(f)(1); cf. Velocity Investments, LLC v. Nguyen (2026) 2026 WL 2542811, at *2 (“Regardless of how pled in the complaint, allegations may consist of two separate and distinct causes of action…In that case, a party may present a motion for summary adjudication challenging a separate and distinct wrongful act even though combined with other wrongful acts alleged in the same cause of action.”))  Defendant’s breach of ¶4.1’s obligation to leave the unit in the same condition is only one part of the first cause of action.  Even if Plaintiff satisfied its burden as to that specific breach, it would not fully dispose of the breach of contract cause of action.  In addition, the same issue arises as to whether Plaintiff is barred from suing on ¶4.1 of the Settlement Agreement when a judgment was already entered thereon in the Underlying Action pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. 

            Plaintiff fails to satisfy its burden as moving party on summary judgment or adjudication as to the first cause of action for breach of contract.  Plaintiff’s motion for summary judgment is denied.  Plaintiff’s motion for summary adjudication is also denied.

            B. Plaintiff fails to satisfy its burden on the second cause of action for unjust enrichment

            Whether unjust enrichment is a recognized as a cause of action is “largely academic because this district [First] has long taken the position that, even if unjust enrichment does not describe an actual cause of action, the term is synonymous with restitution, which can be a theory of recovery.”  (O’Grady v. Merchant Exchange Productions, Inc. (2019) 41 Cal.App.5th 771, 791.)  Unjust enrichment is synonymous with a claim for restitution.  (McBride v. Boughton (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 379, 387.) 

            “There are several potential bases for a cause of action seeking restitution. For example, restitution may be awarded in lieu of breach of contract damages when the parties had an express contract, but it was procured by fraud or is unenforceable or ineffective for some reason. Alternatively, restitution may be awarded where the defendant obtained a benefit from the plaintiff by fraud, duress, conversion, or similar conduct. In such cases, the plaintiff may choose not to sue in tort, but instead to seek restitution on a quasi-contract theory (an election referred to at common law as ‘waiving the tort and suing in assumpsit’).”  (McBride, supra, 123 Cal.App.4th  at 388.) 

            There is a split among California courts regarding whether unjust enrichment is an independent cause of action.  (O’Grady, supra, 41 Cal.App.5th at 791 (recognizing there are decisions holding that unjust enrichment is not a cause of action, “but this appellate district appears split on the issue).)  Although some California courts have suggested the existence of a separate cause of action for unjust enrichment, other courts have held that there is no cause of action in California for unjust enrichment.  (Peterson v. Cellco Partnership (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1583, 1593 (listing elements of unjust enrichment claim); Levine v. Blue Shield of Cal. (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 1117, 1138, quoting Durell v. Sharp Healthcare (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 1350, 1370 (no independent cause of action for unjust enrichment; unjust enrichment equivalent of restitution cause of action).)

            Plaintiff’s second cause of action seeks recovery of fair market rent for Defendant’s occupancy of unit 315 from September 1, 2024 to August 14, 2025.  Unjust enrichment is not a cause of action as stated by the Second Appellate District in Bank of New York Mellon v. Citibank, N.A. (2017) 8 Cal.App.5th 935, 955.  Even if construed as a cause of action for restitution, Plaintiff’s right to payment is not based on any contract but on the judgment entered in the Underlying Action, nor is there any allegation of fraud, duress, conversion or similar conduct.  (McBride, supra, 123 Cal.App.4th  at 388.)  Plaintiff’s evidence only establishes Defendant’s failure to satisfy the Judgment entered in the Underlying Action.

            Plaintiff fails to satisfy its burden as moving party on the motion for summary judgment or adjudication of the second cause of action for unjust enrichment.  Plaintiff’s motion for summary judgment, or in the alternative, summary adjudication is denied as to the Second Cause of Action. 

IV.    CONCLUSION

Plaintiff’s Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication is denied.
