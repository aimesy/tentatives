Case Number: 23VECV03731    Hearing Date: July 23, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Graham v. Global Customer Services, Case no. 23VECV03731

Hearing date July 23, 2026

Plaintiff Graham’s Motion for Preliminary Approval of Class Action and PAGA Settlement - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Graham sues defendants Global Customer Services, Inc. (“GCS”) and Arizona Pipeline Company (“APC”), alleging employment law violations, including failures to pay wages, meal periods, business reimbursements and others. Plaintiff also brought a Private Attorneys General Act of 2004 (“PAGA”) claim. The matter settled for $1,350,000 on behalf of approximately 820 class members. See Decl. Etemadi, exh. A. Plaintiff moves for preliminary approval of the class action and PAGA settlement. The motion is unopposed.

Plaintiff requests that the court issue an order: (1) “Preliminar[ily] approving the proposed Class Action and PAGA Settlement Agreement and Class Notice between Plaintiff and Defendants Global Customer Services, Inc. and Arizona Pipeline Company; (2) Conditionally certifying the Settlement Class for settlement purposes only; (3) Appointing Plaintiff as Class Representative; (4) Appointing David Alami, Daniel J. Hyun, and Shaheen Anthony Etemadi of the Torus LLP as Class Counsel; (5) Appointing CPT Group as the Settlement Administrator; (6) Directing that notice be mailed to the Settlement Class; and (7) Scheduling a hearing date for the Motion for Final Approval of Class Action Settlement.” Mtn., 2:5-15.

As a “fiduciary” of the absent class members, the trial court’s duty is to have information to determine if the settlement is fair, adequate and reasonable. 7-Eleven Owners for Fair Franchising v. The Southland Corp. (2000) 85 Cal.App.4th 1135, 1151 (citing Dunk v. Ford Motor Co. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 1794, 1801, 1802).

California Rules of Court, Rule 3.769 governs settlements of class actions. Any party to a settlement agreement may submit a written notice for preliminary approval of the settlement. The settlement agreement and proposed notice to class members must be filed with the motion, and the proposed order must be lodged with the motion. CRC rule 3.769(c).

Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 244-45 discusses factors the court should consider when considering reasonableness of the settlement. A presumption of fairness exists where: (1) the settlement is reached through arm’s length bargaining; (2) investigation and discovery are sufficient to allow counsel and the court to act intelligently; (3) counsel is experienced in similar litigation; and (4) the percentage of objectors is small. Wershba, supra, at 245, citing Dunk, supra, at 1802. The test is not the maximum amount plaintiff might have obtained at trial but whether the settlement is reasonable under all the circumstances. Wershba, supra, at 250.

The Los Angeles Superior Court provides a checklist for the preliminary approval of class action settlements. See LASC Checklist for Preliminary Approval of Class Action Settlement at www.lacourt.org/division/civil/pdf/PreliminaryApprovalofClassActionSettlement.pdf. The motion follows the LASC checklist. See Mtn. 4:17-15:28.

Defendant GCS provided debris removal, traffic control services, aerial imaging services and utility locating services throughout California. GCS is no longer in business. Defendant APC provides underground construction services throughout California. See Decl. Etemadi, para. 9. On 5/22/23 plaintiff submitted a PAGA notice to defendants and the California Labor and Workforce Development Agency (“LWDA”). Id. at para. 10. The LWDA did not respond. Id. Plaintiff filed the complaint on 1/30/26. Id. at para. 11.

Plaintiff alleges defendants violated provisions of the Labor Code and other applicable California law: “Plaintiff alleges Defendants violated several provisions of the Labor Code and other applicable California law by, inter alia, (1) Failure To Pay All Wages Owed (2) Failure To Pay Vacation Pay; (3) Failure To Reimburse For Necessary Business Expenses; (4) Failure To Provide Meal Periods; (5) Failure To Authorize And Permit Rest Breaks; (6) Failure To Provide Employees With Heat Recovery Periods; (7) Failure To Provide Notice Of And Pay Paid Sick Leave At The Correct Rate; (8) Failure To Maintain Accurate Timekeeping, Payroll And Employment Records; (9) Failure To Produce Employment Records; (10) Failure To Timely Pay Wages Owed During Employment And Upon Separation; (11) Failure To Provide Accurate Itemized Wage Statements; (12) Requiring Employees To Release Claims In Exchange For Payment; (13) Unfair Competition; and (14) Civil Penalties Under The Private Attorneys General Act Of 2004 For Violations Of Labor Code Sections And Applicable IWC Wage Orders.” Decl. Etemadi, paras. 11, 16-22.

Plaintiff is represented by David Alami, Daniel J. Hyun and Shaheen Anthony Etemadi of Torus LLP. See Decl. Etemadi, paras. 5-7; Decl. Alami, paras. 5-8. Class counsel is sufficiently experienced in wage and hour litigation and class representation. Id.

Counsel engaged in extensive informal discovery. See Decl. Etemadi, para. 12. This included informal production and review of timekeeping and payroll records during the class period for a sampling of 25% of the 820 class members; production of relevant employment policies during the class period, agreements governing the class members throughout the class period and other relevant documents ahead of mediation. Id. Plaintiff consulted with experts to analyze the timekeeping and payroll data. Id. The court finds plaintiff and defendants sufficiently investigated the claims and exchanged records and information regarding the claims prior to negotiating a settlement such that counsel and the court are able to act intelligently.

On 5/9/25 the parties mediated with Will Klatte, Esq., an experienced wage and hour class action mediator. See Decl. Etemadi, para. 13. All parties were represented by counsel at the mediation, and each party recognized the risk of an adverse result absent a settlement. Id. The parties exchanged and executed the long-form settlement agreement presented to the court on 12/29/25 following extensive negotiations. Id. at para. 13, exh. A. The court finds the parties have engaged in reasonable arms-length bargaining.

The net settlement amount (“NSA”) expected to be paid to the class members, after payment of class counsel fees of 35% of the gross settlement agreement (“GSA”) ($1,350,000) and costs of up to $25,000, a class representative service award of $15,000, settlement administration costs of up to $12,750, a total PAGA penalty payment of $100,000 (75% [$75,000] of which goes to the California Labor & Workforce Development Agency (“LWDA”) and 25% [$25,000] of which will be paid to aggrieved employees employed during the PAGA period), and excluding the employer’s share of payroll taxes, which shall not be paid from the GSA, is $749,750, with an average estimated individual class payment of approximately $914.33. The court finds this to be a reasonable amount under all circumstances.

A settlement does not have to provide 100% of the damages sought to be considered fair and reasonable. Rebney v. Wells Fargo Bank (1991) 220 Cal.App.3d 1117, 1139. Preliminary approval is warranted if the settlement falls within a “reasonable range.” North Cty. Contractor’s Ass’n., Inc. v. Touchstone Ins. Serv. (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 1085, 1089-90. Here, plaintiff alleges his claims are based on defendants’ common, class-wide policies and procedures, and liability could be determined on a class-wide basis without depending on individual assessments of liability. If plaintiff prevailed on the merits for all alleged claims, the reasonable estimated damages and penalties for all claims total approximately $2,588,850. Due to the risk of non-certification and losing on the merits, reaching a compromise settlement for $1,350,000.00 (approximately 52.15% of the estimated damages) is reasonable and will allow class members to obtain compensation for their claims without having to file and litigate their own cases. See Decl. Etemadi, paras. 16-23.

GRANTED; the court will sign the proposed order filed by plaintiff 3/3/26 and set a hearing for the motion for final approval.


Case Number: 25VECV06750    Hearing Date: July 23, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Chalikyan v. Volkswagen Group of America, Case no. 25VECV06750

Hearing date July 23, 2026

Plaintiff Chalikyan’s Motion to Compel Further Discovery

Plaintiff Chalikyan sues defendants Volkswagen Group of America (“VGA”) and Galpin Motors, Inc., for violations of the Song-Beverly Act. Plaintiff moves to compel further responses to requests for production of documents, set one, nos. 1-31. VGA opposes.

Code Civ. Proc. §2031.310(a) states “the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that . . . (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete. (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive...”

The discovery seeks documents relating to: (1) subject vehicle and repair attempts; (2) VGA’s warranty, replacement and repurchase polices; and (3) VGA’s knowledge of same or similar defects in vehicles of the same make, model and year. See Decl. Hozven, para. 11, exh. 2. VGA served boilerplate objection-only responses 2/6/26. Id. at para. 12, exh. 3. VGA served further responses on 2/10/26 which were comprised of boilerplate objections, without a privilege log, and only partially responsive. Id. at para. 13, exh. 4.

The parties exchanged meet and confer letters and spoke. Decl. Hozven, paras. 15, 19-20, exhs. 5-6. VGA’s counsel agreed to produce: (1) documents related to other similar incidents; (2) SAGA warranty claims; and (3) documents relating to VGA procedures, which were not produced. Id., para. 21.

VGA argues plaintiff failed to meet and confer in good faith. The parties exchanged letters and spoke. See Decl. Sharma, paras. 5-6, exhs. C-D. That the parties allegedly reached an agreement as to further production is evidence that meet and confer efforts were made, regardless of their ultimate effectiveness. There is no basis to find plaintiff failed to meet meet-and-confer obligations prior to filing this motion.

VGA argues the RFPs are overbroad and seek discovery beyond documents related to the subject vehicle. Documents related to similar incidents in vehicles of the same make, model and year are relevant and admissible. Donlen v. Ford Motor Co. (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 138, 154-55; Kwan v. Mercedes-Benz of N. Am., Inc. (1994) 23 Cal.App.4th 174, 185. Discovery in California is broad. Code Civ. Proc. §2017.010.

VGA’s opposition admits it “offered to produce SAGA and Other Similar Instances (“OSI”) documents… however, due to a technical issue at [d]efendant’s end, counsel for [d]efendant has been unable to get those documents but remains willing to provide them once received.” Opp. 3:23-26. This constitutes an admission that further responses are warranted. VGA argues it “already produced documents responsive to all of [p]laintiff’s RFPs with the exception to documents responsive to RFP Nos. 30 prior to [p]laintiffs(sic) filing this [m]otion. [VGA] has informed [p]laintiff that it will produce responsive documents to RFP Nos. 30 and 31.” Opp. 1:17-19. VGA “produced additional confidential documents pursuant to the stipulated protective order entered into by the [p]arties, including its policies and procedures and dealer manual.” Decl. Shama, para. 7. No further responses or supplemental filings have been received. See Reply Decl. Hozven, para. 28.

Plaintiff is entitled to full and complete production to RFPs, set one, nos. 1-31. To the extent documents are withheld based on privilege, a privilege log must be provided. No sanctions are sought. GRANTED; complete, code-compliant responses to be served within 30 days. The parties to update the court on the status of discovery at hearing.
