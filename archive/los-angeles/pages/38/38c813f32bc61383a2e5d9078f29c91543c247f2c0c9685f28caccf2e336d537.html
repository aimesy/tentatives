Case Number: 25CHCV00731    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 9/16/26

Case Name: Janeth Lizbeth Roldan; Juan Carlos Mendez vs. Elman Rene Chacon Venura; and Does 1-10

Case No. 25CHCV00731

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

SEPTEMBER 16, 2026

 

MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO INTERROGATORIES; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25CHCV00731

 

Motion filed: 4/8/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Elman Rene Chacon Ventura

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Janeth Lizbeth Roldan

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court compelling Plaintiff Janeth Lizbeth Roldan to respond to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Special Interrogatories, Set One; and awarding monetary sanctions in the amount of $835.00.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The motion is DENIED. The request for sanctions is GRANTED IN PART.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from injuries allegedly sustained by Plaintiffs Janeth Lizbeth Roldan (“Roldan”) and Juan Carlos Mendez (“Mendez”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) in a motor vehicle accident that occurred on March 16, 2023. On February 27, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the Complaint against Defendant Elman Rene Chacon Ventura (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 10, alleging motor vehicle and general negligence.

 

On June 20, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint.

 

On April 8, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to compel Roldan to respond to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Special Interrogatories, Set One (the “Motion”).

 

On September 8, 2026, Roldan filed an Opposition.

 

On September 10, 2026, Defendant filed a Reply.

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.    Preliminary Considerations

 

As a preliminary consideration, the Court notes that Defendant has combined two motions into one. The Court Reservation System requires a separate hearing reservation for each motion, and the Government Code requires a separate filing fee be paid for each motion. (Gov. Code, § 70617, subd. (f).)

 

The Discovery Act recognizes form interrogatories and special interrogatories as distinct discovery devices. A party seeking to compel form and special interrogatories must accordingly bring separate motions and pay separate filing fees for each.

 

Here, Defendant has moved to compel both form and special interrogatories in a single motion and has paid only one filing fee rather than two, circumventing statutory and Court requirements.

 

The Court accordingly orders Defendant to pay an additional $60.00 filing fee.

 

B.     Motion to Compel Responses to Form and Special Interrogatories

 

“A defendant may propound interrogatories to a party to the action without leave of court at any time.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.020, subd. (a).) “Within 30 days after service of interrogatories, the party to whom the interrogatories are propounded shall serve the original of the response to them on the propounding party.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.260, subd. (a).)

 

If a party fails to timely serve responses to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling response. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.290, subd. (b).)

 

A party who fails to timely respond to interrogatories waives “any objection to the interrogatories, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product under Chapter 4 (commencing with Section 2018.010).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).) The Court may relieve the party from that waiver if the party has subsequently served a response that substantially complies with the statute and the failure to serve a timely response was due to mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect. (Ibid.)

 

Defendant seeks to compel Roldan to respond to his Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Special Interrogatories, Set One. In Opposition, Roldan asserts that she served verified responses to the interrogatories on June 2, 2026. (Opp’n, at p. 2.) Plaintiffs’ counsel attests that the responses were electronically served after he re-established contact with Roldan in late May 2026. (Jimenez Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) Defendant does not dispute that responses were served. Instead, Defendant contends that the belated service does not excuse the failure to respond that necessitated the Motion and argues that sanctions are still warranted. (Reply, at pp. 2-3.)

 

Accordingly, the Court DENIES the Motion to compel responses as moot. The Court retains authority to consider the issue of sanctions.

 

C.    Monetary Sanctions

 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(c)).

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 authorizes the Court to impose monetary sanctions against any party, person, or attorney who engages in conduct that constitutes a misuse of the discovery process. Misuses of the discovery process include failing to respond to an authorized method of discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subd. (d).) Further, the Court may award sanctions “in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed[] … or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1348(a).)

 

Roldan does not offer justification for the untimely service of responses. The Court accordingly finds sanctions to be warranted.

 

In the motion papers, Defendant requests monetary sanctions against Roldan and her counsel of record in the amount of $835.00, calculated as 3.1 hours spent preparing the Motion at an hourly rate of $250.00, plus $60.00 in filing fees. (Nguyen Decl., ¶ 7.) However, in Reply, Defendant requests a total of $1,275.00, adding two hours spent reviewing the Opposition and preparing the reply papers.

 

The Court notes that the Notice of Motion and Motion do not indicate that Defendant is seeking fees beyond those incurred for preparation and filing of the motion papers and do not reserve the right to supplement or seek additional fees. The Court accordingly finds that there is inadequate notice for an award greater than the reasonable costs and fees connected with the moving papers.

 

Roldan asserts that the requested sanctions are unreasonable given the lack of complexity of the Motion. (Opp’n, at p. 2.) The Court agrees that the Motion was a straightforward motion directed to service of initial discovery responses. The Court therefore finds the total and reasonable fees and costs incurred in connection with preparing and filing the Motion to be $435.00, calculated as 1.5 hours spent preparing the Motion at a reasonable hourly rate of $250.00, plus the $60.00 filing fee.

 

Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is thus GRANTED IN PART.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court DENIES Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff Janeth Lizbeth Roldan’s responses to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Special Interrogatories, Set One.

 

Defendant’s request for sanctions is GRANTED IN PART. Plaintiff Janeth Lizbeth Roldan and her attorney of record are ordered to pay $435.00, jointly and severally, to Defendant within 30 days.

 

Defendant is ordered to pay an additional $60 filing fee to the clerk.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25CHCV04035    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 9/16/26

Case Name: Russell Sigler, Inc. v. Thermal Air Conditioning, Inc.; Robert Canizalez; Bledsoe XC, LLC; Xebec Building Company, Inc.; Sylmar Studios, LLC; and Does 1-50

Case No. 25CHCV04035

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

SEPTEMBER 16, 2026

 

MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25CHCV04035

 

Motion filed: 4/29/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Thermal Air Conditioning, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Russell Sigler, Inc.

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court setting aside the clerk’s entry of default against Defendant Thermal Air Conditioning, Inc.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The Motion is GRANTED.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from alleged nonpayment for equipment and services furnished by Plaintiff Russell Sigler, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) pursuant to a purchase order. On November 13, 2025, Plaintiff filed the Complaint against Defendants Thermal Air Conditioning, Inc. (“Thermal”), Robert Canizalez (“Canizalez”), Bledsoe XC, LLC, Xebec Building Company, Inc., Sylmar Studios, LLC, and Does 1-50, alleging breach of contract, common counts, account stated, breach of personal guaranty, and foreclosure of mechanic’s lien. Subsequently, on April 10, 2026, Bledsoe XC, LLC, Xebec Building Company, Inc., and Sylmar Studios, LLC were dismissed from the Complaint.

 

On January 26, 2026, the clerk entered default against Thermal.

 

On April 29, 2026, Thermal filed the instant motion to set aside the clerk’s entry of default (the “Motion”).

 

On September 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

 

On September 9, 2026, Thermal filed a Reply.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473 authorizes the Court to set aside default “taken against a party through the party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.” (§ 473, subd. (b).) This discretionary relief provision “is often applied liberally where the party in default moves promptly to seek relief, and the party opposing the motion will not suffer prejudice if relief is granted. [Citations.]” (Elston v. City of Turlock (1985) 38 Cal.3d 227, 233.) “[B]ecause the law strongly favors trial and disposition on the merits, any doubts in applying section 473 must be resolved in favor of the party seeking relief from default. [Citations.]” (Ibid.)

 

When an application for relief is made within six months after entry of judgment and is accompanied by “an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect,” the court must vacate any default entered by the clerk which will result in default judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) Relief from default is mandatory if based on attorney mistake, neglect, inadvertence, or surprise. (Ibid.)

 

Additionally, where the Court determines that service did not result in actual notice to the responding party in time to defend the action, and that the lack of notice “was not caused by the party’s avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect, it may set aside the default … on whatever terms as may be just and allow the party to defend the action.” (§ 473.5, subd. (c).)

 

A.    Motion to Set Aside Default

 

Thermal seeks to set aside the clerk’s entry of default against it on January 26, 2026, on the following grounds: (1) it did not receive actual notice of the action in time to defend, (2) the default resulted from mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect, (3) the Court’s inherent equitable authority supports relief, and (4) the default resulted from the neglect of Thermal’s attorney.

 

In its Reply, Thermal also asserts for the first time that the default is void. While a court may set aside a void order at any time on motion of a party, such relief requires proper notice and an opportunity to be heard. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) “Points raised for the first time in the reply brief are generally not considered, out of fairness to the respondent.” (Crowley Maritime Corp. v. Boston Old Colony Insurance Co. (2008) 158 Cal.App.4th 1061, 1072, citing Scott v. CIBA Vision Corp. (1995) 38 Cal.App.4th 307, 322.) The Court accordingly limits its analysis to the grounds asserted in the moving papers.

 

(1)     Lack of Actual Notice

 

Thermal first asserts that relief should be granted because it did not receive actual notice of the action.

 

Thermal explains that, at the time Plaintiff attempted initial service, Thermal’s designated agent for service listed with the Secretary of State was inaccurate. (Mot., at p. 3.) Thermal submits the declaration of its Responsible Managing Officer, Canizalez, in support. Canizalez attests that at the time Plaintiff attempted service, its designated agent for service of process had resigned. (Canizalez Decl., ¶ 7.) Canizalez further attests that Thermal had “unknowingly” not yet designated a replacement agent. (Ibid.) Canizalez states that Thermal did not receive any documents from the Secretary of State, and the first Canizalez personally learned of the action was when Thermal’s attorney notified him of the entry of default on January 26, 2026. (Ibid.)

 

The Court accepts that Thermal lacked actual notice of the action. Thermal’s position appears to be that the lack of actual notice resulted from incorrect information being filed with the Secretary of State. Other than Canizalez concluding that it was “unknowing,” however, Thermal does not explain how the failure to appoint a new registered agent and keep its business registration up to date was not the product of its own neglect.

 

The Corporations Code requires corporations to maintain a viable channel for service of process. (Corp. Code, §§ 1502-1504.) This includes appointing a registered agent, promptly replacing an agent who has resigned, and promptly filing a designation of a new agent. (Ibid.) As Plaintiff shows in its Opposition, Thermal’s actions and omissions undermine any assertion of inexcusable neglect. Canizalez attests that the registered agent resigned, but the Statement of Information filed with the Secretary of State indicates that Canizalez himself was the registered agent, chief executive officer, secretary, and chief financial officer of Thermal. (Clauss Decl., Exh. 1.) Under these circumstances, the Court does not credit the proposition that Canizalez’s resignation would lead to an “unknowing” vacancy. Moreover, Thermal affirmatively filed the Statement of Information within days before Plaintiff attempted service that indicates that the address for service was the address where Plaintiff in fact attempted service. (Clauss Decl., Exhs. 1, 2.) Thermal does not indicate who was responsible for the filing or present any facts indicating that the filing was inadvertent or mistaken.

 

The Court accordingly finds that Thermal has not met its burden of establishing that the lack of actual notice was not the result of avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect. Relief under section 473.5 is unavailable.

 

(2)     Discretionary Relief Under Section 473, Subdivision (b) and Relief Based on Extrinsic Mistake

 

Discretionary relief pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) also requires Thermal to show that the default resulted from excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) And relief based on the Court’s inherent equitable power requires a defendant to show a satisfactory excuse. (Kramer v. Traditional Escrow, Inc. (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 13, 29-30.)

 

Thermal’s argument for excusable neglect and equitable relief rests on the same facts as its argument for lack of actual notice. Specifically, Thermal contends that it did not respond to the action because it did not receive the Summons and Complaint due to inaccurate information being on file with the Secretary of State.

 

As discussed above, Thermal fails to establish that the failure to keep accurate information on file with the Secretary of State was excusable. Discretionary and equitable relief are accordingly likewise unavailable.

 

(3)     Mandatory Relief Under Section 473, Subdivision (b)

 

The mandatory provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) requires the court to vacate any resulting default entered by the clerk against an attorney’s client if relief is requested within six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by “an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” The Court must grant relief even in cases where the neglect was inexcusable, unless the Court finds that the default was “not in fact caused” by counsel’s error. (Martin Potts & Associates, Inc. v. Corsair (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 432, 439.) As long as the attorney attests to circumstances demonstrating that he or she was at fault, the mandatory provision of section 473 applies. (Carmel, Ltd. v. Tavoussi (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 393, 401.)

 

Thermal asserts that its attorney was aware of the instant action as early as December 30, 2025. (Mot., at p. 7.) Thermal contends that the failure of its attorney to investigate the action resulted in default and provides grounds for mandatory relief. (Ibid.) Thermal’s attorney, Jonelle Oldacre (“Oldacre”), submits a declaration in support of the Motion. Oldacre attests that on December 30, 2025, she was served with an electronic copy of an order denying Plaintiff’s request to deem the instant action and another action—filed by Thermal against Plaintiff—related. (Oldacre Decl., ¶ 8.) Oldacre further attests that she mistakenly did not realize that service had been effectuated in the instant action. (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff contends that Oldacre’s declaration identifies only error “in the abstract,” without establishing that the error was the cause of the default. The Court finds that the declaration states sufficient facts to demonstrate attorney fault. (See Carmel, Ltd., supra, 175 Cal.App.4th at p. 401 [“When an attorney ‘attests to’—that is, declares to be true—facts demonstrating he or she was at fault, application of section 473 is not negated by the attorney’s contrary opinion.”].) Oldacre admits that she had knowledge that this action was pending against Thermal but took no action on it. Given that Canizalez has declared that he had no knowledge of the action until after default, Oldacre was in a unique position to follow up with either Plaintiff’s counsel or Thermal and Canizalez regarding service, but failed to do so. Had Oldacre reached out to Plaintiff’s counsel, she would have known that the Complaint had been served before the default was taken on January 26, 2026.

 

Given the strong policy in favor of disposition of the merits, the Court finds Oldacre’s declaration sufficient to establish that failure to respond to the Complaint was due to attorney error.

 

The Court accordingly GRANTS the Motion.

 

B.     Monetary Sanctions

 

“The court shall, whenever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault, direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) The Court has granted relief pursuant to the mandatory provision of section 473, subdivision (b). Pursuant to that section, the Court accordingly orders Thermal’s counsel to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to Plaintiff’s counsel.  

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court GRANTS Defendant Thermal Air Conditioning, Inc.’s motion to set aside the January 26, 2026 entry of default. Thermal Air Conditioning, Inc.’s Answer, filed concurrently with the motion, is deemed served and filed as of this date.

 

Defendant’s counsel is ordered to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to Plaintiff’s counsel, in an amount to be determined upon further hearing.

Plaintiff’s counsel is ordered to file a supplemental declaration in support of attorney fees and costs at least ten days prior to the Case Management Conference. Defendant’s counsel may file any opposing declaration at least five days prior to the Case Management Conference.
