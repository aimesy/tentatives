I.       BACKGROUND  

This is a contractual fraud action arising out of the sale and purchase of the real property located at 20150-20152 Baltar St., Winnetka, CA 91306 (the “Subject Property”). On April 23, 2025, Plaintiffs Alireza Ghoorchian (“Ghoorchian”) and Ayda Nosrati (“Nosrati”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed a Complaint against Defendants Surrinder Kumar Malhi; Paramjit Kumar Malhi; Sansui Holdings LLC (“Sansui”); and DOES 1 through 20 (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Fraudulent Concealment; (3) Violation of California Civ. Code § 1102; (4) Violation of California Civ. Code § 896; and (5) Violation of Business and Profession Code § 7044.

On August 20, 2025, Defendants Surrinder Kumar Malhi and Paramjit Kumar Malhi (hereinafter, “Malhi Defendants”) filed their joint Answer to the Complaint.

On January 30, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Motion for Leave to File First Amended Complaint. The matter came for hearing on February 27, 2026; the motion was granted. On March 3, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants, alleging causes of action for: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Fraudulent Concealment; (3) Violation of California Civ. Code § 1102; (4) Violation of California Civ. Code § 896; (5) Violation of Business and Profession Code § 7044; (6) Fraudulent Conveyance; and (7) Constructive Fraud – Civ. Code §§ 3439.04(A)(2), 3439.05.

On March 4, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an Amendment to the Fac identifying SSW Holding LLC as Doe 1.

On April 1, 2026, SSW Holdings, LLC (“SSW”), erroneously sued as SSW Holding LLC, filed its Answer to the FAC. On April 3, 2026, the Malhi Defendants filed their Answer to the FAC.

On July 6, 2026, the Malhi Defendants filed the instant Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint. Any opposition would have been due on August 13, 2026. To date, no opposition has been filed.

II.          LEGAL STANDARDS

“A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint or cross-complaint may file a cross-complaint…against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him” before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint, or any time before the court has set a trial date. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (a); Code Civ. Proc. § 428.50, subd. (a) [same time as answer]; Code Civ. Proc. § 428.50, subd. (b) [any time before trial date].) A party may also  file a cross-complaint “against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (b).) A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint except one filed within the time specified in subdivision (a) or (b).” (Code Civ. Proc. § 428.50, subd. (c).)  

If the proposed cross-complaint is compulsory, then leave must be granted so long as the defendant is acting in good faith. (Code Civ. Proc. § 426.50.)¿If the proposed cross-complaint is permissive, leave of court may be granted “in the interest of justice” at any time during the course of the action. (Code Civ. Proc. § 428.50, subd. (c).) “Permission to file a permissive cross-complaint is solely within the trial court’s discretion.” (Crocker Nat. Bank v. Emerald (1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 852, 864.) “The purpose of allowing a cross–complaint is to enable all matters in dispute between the parties relating to or depending upon the contract, transaction or subject matter upon which the action is brought or affecting the property to which it relates, to be determined by a single action and by a single judgment. In other words, to avoid a multiplicity of suits and thereby save vexation and expense.” (Bracey v. Gray (1944) 65 Cal.App.2d 282, 285–286.) 

III.       DISCUSSION

The Malhi Defendants move for an order permitting them to file their proposed Cross-Complaint against Plaintiffs on the grounds that there are completely new facts post the filing of their Answer, which give rise to the proposed cross-claims for (1) Tortious Interference with Contractual Relationship; (2) Tortious Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage; (3) Defamation; and (4) Slander of Title. (Green Decl., ¶14, Ex. A.)

Specifically, the Malhi Defendants own a property located at 20151 Strathern Street, Winnetka, CA (the “Strathern Property”), which is adjacent to the Subject Property at issue in this instant case. (Green Decl., ¶7.) On June 14, 2026, Defendant Surrinder Malhi called his counsel of record, G. Richard Green, and told him that Ghoorchian has been approaching prospective buyers for the Strathern Property and making various false statements about the condition of the residence causing these prospective buyers to leave the open house showings. (Id., ¶8.) Furthermore, Ghoorchian has put up multiple signs of a home with fire and damages depicted in pictures and implying that it is the Strathern Property. (Id., ¶9.) Mr. Green later spoke with the Malhi Defendants real estate agent, Alex Galuz, who confirmed that Ghoorchian had been harassing, aggressive, and scary. (Id., ¶11.) Mr. Galuz also told Mr. Green that Ghoorchian was preventing people from coming into the Strathern Property for the open house, which led to Mr. Galuz canceling the open house and marketing of the Strathern Property for sale. (Id., ¶12.)

Essentially, Plaintiffs allege that the Malhi Defendants concealed defective conditions on the Subject property to induce them to purchase the Subject Property (FAC, ¶¶10-17) and the Malhi Defendants’ proposed cross-claims allege that Plaintiffs are preventing them from selling their adjacent property, the Strathern Property, by making false statements about the condition of the Strathern Property to prospective buyers. Thus, although this present action involves the purchase and sale of the Subject Property including the alleged concealment of the defective conditions on the Subject Property, the Malhi Defendants seek to assert claims of purported retaliatory actions by Plaintiffs in response to this instant lawsuit. Moreover, Plaintiffs have not opposed this instant motion, therefore, Plaintiffs concede on this issue. (See California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1113, subd. (a); see also Rule 3.1320(f); Herzberg v. County of Plumas (2005) 133 Cal.App.4th 1, 20 [the party who fails to argue an issue abandons that issue].)

Accordingly, the Malhi Defendants have shown that their proposed cross-claims concern matters of dispute against Plaintiffs, which should be heard in this present lawsuit to avoid multiplicity of suits and in the interests of justice and judicial economy.

IV.    CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ Surrinder Kumar Malhi and Paramjit Kumar Malhi Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint is GRANTED. The proposed Cross-Complaint to be filed and served separately within 5 days of this Order.  Responsive pleading to be filed and served 30 days thereafter. .

            Defendants Surrinder Kumar Malhi and Paramjit Kumar Malhi to give notice.

26VECV03542 MARIBEL RIVAS vs.15116 PARTHENIA STREET INC., et al.,

August 26, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

 

 

BACKGROUND 

This is a landlord-tenant action arising out of the collection of rent for a property subject to the Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Ordinance (“LARSO”). On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff Maribel Rivas (“Plaintiff”) filed the operative Complaint against Defendants 15116 Parthenia Street Inc. (“Parthenia”) and DOES 1 through 10 (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for: (1) Violation of Los Angeles Municipal Code (“LAMC”) §§ 151.04, 151.10 and (2) Declaratory Relief.

On July 8, 2026, Parthenia filed the instant Demurrer with Motion to Strike the Complaint. On August 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

 

I.             DISCUSSION

As a preliminary matter, Parthenia’s counsel of record, Alberto J. Campain’s declaration in support of the demurrer and motion to strike indicates, inter alia, that “On July 1, 2026, I transmitted a correspondence by email to Plaintiff’s counsel in a good faith attempt to meet and confer regarding objections that would be raised by a demurrer and a motion to strike portions of the complaint.” (Campain Decl., ¶2, Ex. A.)

Prior to filing a demurrer and motion to strike, the demurring/moving party is required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by videoconference with the party who filed the pleading for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer and motion to strike. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41; Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5.)¿

As shown above, the parties did not meet and confer in-person, by telephone, or by videoconference. Although Mr. Campain’s meet and confer email stated: “I am available to meet and confer telephonically if you desire. If we do not hear from you, we will assume that you do not intend to amend the Complaint and we will proceed with the filing of a motion to strike and demurrer,” Mr. Campain does not attest to any actual attempt to call Plaintiff’s counsel to meet and confer prior to filing the demurrer and motion to strike.  The Court finds this meet and confer effort fails to conform with the statute.

II.       CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court continues the hearing on Defendant 15116 Parthenia Street Inc.’s Demurrer and Motion to Strike to October 29,2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Dept. I at Van Nuys East Courthouse.

The parties are ordered to meet and confer either in person, by telephone, or by video conference within 10 days of the date of this order. If the parties are unable to informally resolve the pleading issues or if the parties are otherwise unable to meet and confer in good faith, Defendant 15116 Parthenia Street Inc. is to thereafter file and serve a declaration setting forth meet and confer efforts in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure Sections  430.41 and 435.5  within 5 court days of the continued hearing. 

     Defendant 15116 Parthenia Street Inc. to give notice.
