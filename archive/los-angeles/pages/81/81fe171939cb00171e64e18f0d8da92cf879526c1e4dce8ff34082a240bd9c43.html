            [TENTATIVE] ORDER: The Court VOIDS Moving Defendant’s motion to compel for failure to pay the filing fees.

 

Background

This action arises out a fraudulent transfer of title concerning the real property located at t 23018 Ventura Boulevard, Woodland Hills, California, 91364 (herein, Ventura Property). Plaintiffs are Raafat S. Iskander and Koharik T. Iskander, as Trustees of The Raafat S. Iskander and Koharik T. Iskander Revocable Living trust, Dated October 7, 2004. This trust has held title to the Ventura Property since 2005.

Plaintiffs allege that in November 2022 Moving Defendant German Simakovski and Defendant GE Holdings LLC carried out a scheme to transfer title to the subject property to GE Holdings LLC without the Plaintiffs’ consent or knowledge.

On February 7, 2023, Plaintiffs filed suit, alleging nine causes of action: 1) Declaratory Relief; 2) Quiet Title; 3) Cancellation of Written Instrument (Grant Deed); 4) Cancellation of Written Instrument (Deed of Trust); 5) Slander of Title; 6) Fraud; 7) Conspiracy to Commit Fraud; 8) Elder Abuse (Financial); and 9) Elder Abuse (Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress).

On April 6, 2023, defaults were entered against Moving Defendant Simakovski as well as Defendant GE Holdings LLC.

On November 7, 2023, Moving Defendant filed a motion to vacate and set aside the entry of default and default judgment on the grounds that Moving Defendant did not have sufficient actual notice of the action pending against it and that any default judgment entered against him is void for lack of proper service.

On December 5, 2023, this Court DENIED Moving Defendant’s motion to vacate, finding that Moving Defendant had failed to meet their burden of proof under CCP § 473.5 or CCP § 473(d). Thus, this case has effectively been resolved since 2023.

On June 1, 2026, Moving Defendant German Simakovski, in pro per, filed the instant Motion to Compel Statement of Amount Required to Satisfy Judgment Creditors. Moving Defendant also filed a request to waive court fees.

On June 26, 2026, this Court heard Moving Defendant’s ex parte fee waiver hearing. Moving Defendant did not attend the hearing. Thus, the Court denied this request.

Now, the Court hears Moving Defendant’s Motion to Compel Statement of Amount Required.

            Discussion

Moving Defendant moves to compel a statement of amount required to satisfy judgment creditors from Plaintiffs, pursuant to CCP § 724.050. (Mot. to Compel, p. 2:7-17.)

Under CCP § 724.050, “the judgment debtor, . . . may serve personally or by mail on the judgment creditor a demand in writing that the judgment creditor do one or both of the following: File an acknowledgment of satisfaction of judgment with the court . . . [or] Execute, acknowledge, and deliver an acknowledgment of satisfaction of judgment to the person who made the demand.” (CCP § 724.050(a)(1-2).)

Additionally, under CCP § 724.050(b), if the judgment creditor does not comply with this demand within 15 days, “the person making the demand may apply to the court on noticed motion for an order requiring the judgment creditor to comply with the demand.” (CCP § 724.050(d).)

Here, Moving Defendant asserts that they served Plaintiffs with a demand under CCP § 724.050, but that Plaintiffs did not respond within 15 days. (Mot. to Compel, Simakovski Decl. ¶¶ 6, 8.)

However, before reaching the merits of this motion, the Court must first address a crucial procedural issue. Namely, it appears that Moving Defendant has not paid the filing fee for this motion. Under GOV § 68634, “If an application is denied in whole or in part, the applicant shall pay the court fees and costs that ordinarily would be charged, or make the partial payment as ordered by the court, within 10 days after the clerk gives notice of the denial, unless within that time the applicant submits a new application or requests a hearing under subdivision (e). If the applicant does not pay on time, the clerk shall void the papers that were filed without payment of the court fees and costs.” (GOV § 68634(g) [emphasis added].)

Here, Moving Defendant filed a request to waive court fees on June 1, 2026. On June 26, 2026, this Court heard this request. (Minute Order, 6/26/26.) This Court denied Moving Defendant’s request, because Moving Defendant did not attend the hearing. (Ibid.) Notice of this denial was served to Moving Defendant by the clerk on the same day. (Certificate of Mailing, 6/26/26.) Thus, under GOV § 68634(g), Moving Defendant had until July 6, 2026, to pay the filing fee for this motion. As pointed out in the Opposition papers, Moving Defendant has yet to pay the filing fees. (Opp. pp. 5:4-24.) Thus, this Court is obligated to void this motion.

For this reason, the Court VOIDS Moving Defendant’s Motion to Compel for failure to pay filing fees.

Conclusion

The Court VOIDS Moving Defendant’s motion to compel for failure to pay the filing fees.

           

IT IS SO ORDERED, Plaintiffs TO GIVE NOTICE.

NO PROOF OF SERVICE

 

            [TENTATIVE] ORDER: The Court CONTINUES this motion to October 12, 2026, so that Moving Defendant can properly serve this motion.

 

Background

This is a dog bite personal injury case. On August 26, 2023, Plaintiff Patricio Fuentes was allegedly attacked by a dog named Koda while walking in Warner Park.

At the time of the incident, Koda was being walked by Moving Defendant Lovinjeet Singh. Ms. Singh is an employee of BAW Foundation Capone’s Coalition Inc., a dog rescue foundation. Ms. Singh was recruited as a volunteer dog walker by Defendant Giselle’s Legacy via a social media ad. Ms. Singh was allowed to check Koda out of Defendant Pawparazzi Dogs, LLC, and take him for a walk – which is when the alleged incident occurred.

On August 22, 2025, Plaintiff brought suit against defendants Sherrie Calderon, Giselle’s Legacy, Krista Castro, Pawparazzi Dogs, LLC, as well as Moving Defendant Lovinjeet Singh. Plaintiff alleges two causes of action against all defendants: 1) negligence and 2) strict liability under CIV § 3342.

On March 13, 2026, default was entered against Moving Defendant Lovinjeet Singh

Now, Moving Defendant moves to have this default judgment set aside.

            Discussion

i)                   Legal Merits

Moving Defendant moves this Court to set aside the March 13, 2026, default judgment, pursuant to CCP § 473.5 and § 473(d). (Mot. to Set Aside, pp. 1-2.) Moving Defendant asserts that she did not receive actual notice of this lawsuit. (Ibid.)

A default judgment can be set aside under CCP § 473.5 if: 1) the motion is timely – meaning it was filed and served within the earlier of two years after entry of a default judgment¿or six months after service of the default judgment – and 2) the court determines that the party's lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party's avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect. (CCP § 473.5(a),(c).)

a)      Timeliness

Here, Moving Defendant’s motion is timely. Default was entered on March 13, 2026. Moving Defendant brought this Motion to Set Aside on May 28, 2026, just over three months later. Thus, this motion is timely under either prong of CCP § 473.5(a).

b)      Lack of Notice

Additionally, Moving Defendant provides evidence that their failure to appear in this case was due to lack of notice. Specifically, Moving Defendant provides a sworn declaration, stating that, “I first became aware of this lawsuit on or about May 27, 2026, when I received notice regarding a request for entry of default and related communications.” (Singh Decl. ¶ 4.) Moving Defendant also states that, “the alleged service was made to a P.O. Box address and not by personal service my residence or place of business, and I did not sign any acknowledgment of service.” (Id. ¶ 6.) Finally, Moving Defendant states that, “I did not avoid service and was not attempting to evade notice of this lawsuit.” (Id. ¶ 7.) Notably, this Court is aware of a parallel case in this department based on these same facts. (See 24VECV04071.) Moving Defendant is also a defendant in that case, where she has both appeared and submitted filings. (See, 24VECV04071, Answer, 10/17/25.) Thus, The Court is convinced that Moving Defendant was not properly served, and is not evading service.

For these reasons, the Court finds that Moving Defendant’s lack of notice was not due to intentional evasion of service or inexcusable neglect.

ii)                 Lack of Proof of Service

However, despite the fact that Moving Defendant’s motion is meretricious, the Court must deny or continue this motion to a later date.

Under CCP § 1005(a), “written notice shall be given . . . for the following motions . . . Motion to Set Aside Default or Default Judgment and for Leave to Defend Actions pursuant to Section 473.5.” (CCP § 1005(a)(10).) Here, Moving Defendant submits no proof that this motion to set aside default was served upon Plaintiff. Thus, the motion is procedurally improper. For this reason, the Court CONTINUES this motion to October 12, 2026. Moving Defendant is ordered to serve Plaintiff, and to file proof of this service with the Court.

Conclusion

The Court CONTINUES this motion to October 12, 2026, so that Moving Defendant can properly serve this motion.

 

 

            IT IS SO ORDERED, Moving Defendant TO GIVE NOTICE.
