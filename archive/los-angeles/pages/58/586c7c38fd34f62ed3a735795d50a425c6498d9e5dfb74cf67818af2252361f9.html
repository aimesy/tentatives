21STCV30008

BOG SUN EO vs WON J. JANG, et al.

September 24, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR TERMINATING SANCTIONS TO STRIKE THE ANSWERS OF DEFENDANTS KENNETH HONG AND CHRISTINE OAM FOR THEIR FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH THE COURT ORDER TO RESPOND TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION AND REQUEST FOR ADDITIONAL SANCTIONS OF $1710.00 AGAINST DEFENDANTS.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the unopposed Motion.

The Court strikes the Answers of Defendants CHRISTINE YOUNG OAM and KENNETH HONG, and enters their defaults.

Plaintiff BOG SUN EO is awarded $1,710.00 in sanctions against Defendants CHRISTINE YOUNG OAM and KENNETH HONG, jointly and severally, due and payable within 20 days of this Order.

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

A trial of this case was held, and the Court issued the ruling on March 10, 2026, finding grounds for punitive damages against Defendants KENNETH HONG, CHRISTINE OAM, and KH & RH REALTY, INC. 

Previously, by Motions, filed on July 1 and 2, 2026, Plaintiff moved to compel production of financial documents related to the trial issue of punitive damages, from Defendant KENNETH HONG, and Defendant CHRISTINE YOUNG OAM.

On July 23, 2026, the Court granted that Motion to Compel, and vacated the Non-Jury Trial regarding Punitive Damages scheduled for August 4, 2026.

On August 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion for Terminating Sanctions against Defendants for disobeying the above-referenced Minute Order.

No Oppositions have been filed to these matters.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Where a party willfully disobeys a discovery order, courts have discretion to impose terminating, issue, evidence or monetary sanctions. (E.g., Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, subd. (g), 2030.290, subd. (c); R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486, 495.)

“A trial court has broad discretion to impose discovery sanctions, but two facts are generally prerequisite to the imposition of nonmonetary sanctions….: (1) absent unusual circumstances, there must be a failure to comply with a court order, and (2) the failure must be willful.” (Biles v. Exxon Mobil Corp. (2004) 124 Cal.App.4th 1315, 1327. But see Reedy v. Bussell (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1272, 1291 [“willfulness is no longer a requirement for the imposition of discovery sanctions.”].)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiffs move for an Order imposing monetary sanctions of $1710.00, jointly and severally, against Defendants KENNETH HONG, and CHRISTINE YOUNG OAM, based upon their willfully failing to comply with this Court’s July 23, 2026 Order,

Terminating sanctions are authorized where the evidence shows that less severe sanctions will not be successful in compelling discovery responses. (Mileikowsky v. Tenet Healthsystem (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 262, 279-280.)

To avoid sanctions, the burden of proving that a discovery violation was not willful is on the party on whom the discovery was served. (Cornwall v. Santa Monica Dairy Co. (1977) 66 Cal.App.3d 250, 252- 253.) “If the party seeking a monetary sanction meets its burden of proof, the burden shifts to the opposing party attempting to avoid a monetary sanction to show that it acted with ‘substantial justification.’” (Doe v. U.S. Swimming, Inc. (2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1424, 1435.)

Here, no Oppositions have been filed to show other than willful violations, or substantial justification.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

Default prove-up procedures must be followed for defaults entered as discovery sanctions. (Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 827-828; Matera v. McLeod (2006) 145 Cal.App.4th 44, 60; Reedy v. Bussell (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1272, 1294.)

Therefore, the Court grants the unopposed Motion, as prayed, and adds default entries.

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: MOTION OF KAPLAN KENEGOS & KADIN TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL-CIVIL.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the Motion.

The Court requests moving counsel to submit another proposed Form Order within five days, correcting the checkboxes that appear as the letter “D” on the filing received-stamped on August 18, 2026.

Kaplan Kenegos & Kadin to give notice.

 

ANALYSIS:

By the Motion, filed August 18, 2026, counsel moves to be relieved as attorney of record for Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant KAMRAN BENJAMIN, by declaring, in the form Declaration, at paragraph 2, that there has been an attorney-client-relationship breakdown, and attorneys’ fees payments are overdue.

Here, the form notice, declaration, proposed order, and proofs of service sufficiently comply with the requirements for a motion to be relieved as counsel. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)

Further, moving counsel’s declaration shows cognizable grounds for withdrawal:

  • A breakdown in the attorney-client relationship (or personality clash). (Estate of Falco v. Decker (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004, 1014.)
  • The client breaches an agreement or obligation to the member as to expenses or fees. (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16; Lempert v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1173.)

Although the case was stayed on April 24, 2026, after a Notice of Bankruptcy Stay filed April 23, 2026, attorney withdrawal carries no economic impact on a bankruptcy estate. The scope of a bankruptcy stay does not include an act or proceeding where property of the debtor's estate would be unaffected. (Grant v. Clampitt (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 586, 590-591.) “When a stay is vacated, is no longer in effect, or is modified, the party who filed the notice of the stay must immediately serve and file a notice of termination or modification of stay. If that party fails to do so, any other party in the action who has knowledge of the termination or modification of the stay must serve and file a notice of termination or modification of stay.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.650(d).)

Finally, no opposing document is filed in order to show any prejudice caused by attorney withdrawal. (See Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(d); Vann v. Shilleh (1975) 54 Cal.App.3d 192, 197.)

Therefore, the Court grants the Motion and will sign the proposed Order counsel submitted.

JUNI WAHAB vs COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, et al.

September 24, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS:  PETITIONER’S PETITION TO APPROVE COMPROMISE OF DISPUTED CLAIM

 

RULING:

The Court continues the Petition to October 9, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., for a Proof of Service.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On March 21, 2025, JUNI WAHAB (Plaintiff), a one-year-old minor, by and through guardian ad litem, Janis Ochoa, filed a Complaint against COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, DEPARTMENT OF CHILDREN AND FAMILY SERVICES; COUNTY OF ORANGE; SOCIAL SERVICES AGENCY; URBANSITTER, INC.; and TAMIKA MILLER (Defendants), identifying Causes of Action for:

1.      FAILURE TO PERFORM MANDATORY DUTY (GOV. CODE § 815.6);

2.      NEGLIGENT HIRING, SUPERVISION, OR RETENTION (GOV. CODE §§ 815.2 AND 820);

3.      NEGLIGENCE/NEGLIGENCE PER SE (GOV. CODE § 820);

4.      FAILURE TO PERFORM MANDATORY DUTY (GOV. CODE § 815.6);

5.      NEGLIGENT HIRING, SUPERVISION, OR RETENTION;

6.      NEGLIGENCE/NEGLIGENCE PER SE (GOV. CODE § 820);

7.      NEGLIGENCE AS TO DEFENDANT TAMIKA MILLER;

8.      VICARIOUS LIABILITY OF URBANSITTER, INC.;

9.      RESPONDEAT SUPERIOR AS TO URBANSITTER, INC.; and,

10.  NEGLIGENCE, NEGLIGENT HIRING, SUPERVISION, OR RETENTION AGAINST URBANSITTER, INC.

Plaintiff alleges that DEPARTMENT OF CHILDREN AND FAMILY SERVICES (DCFS) of the COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, and SOCIAL SERVICES AGENCY DIVISION of ORANGE COUNTY failed to abide by mandatory duties of investigating and providing an emergency response, after the parents sharing custody, engaged URBANSITTER, INC. to hire Defendant TAMIKA MILLER, inadequately screened, to babysit, and after receiving child endangerment reports from law enforcement, leading to Plaintiff’s presence in a home presenting a high risk of abuse and injury, involving babysitter negligence occurring while supervising Plaintiff, who accessed boiling water, which caused injuries and disfigurement to the back, neck, shoulders, arms, and chest.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Petitions for approval of minors’ compromises shall fully disclose all information that has any bearing on the compromises’ reasonableness. (Chui v. Chui (2022) 75 Cal.App.5th 873, 899 [citing Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950].)

Notice must be given to all appearing defendants in the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1014). Notice of a petition, including the time and place of hearing, generally, with several specified exceptions, is required at least 15 days before the day of the hearing.  (Cal. Prob. Code §1460, subds. (a)-(b).  See also generally, ibid., §§1042 [hearing shall be on notice unless statute dispenses with notice]; and 12260-1261 [regarding proof of required notice].) The Court can dispense with notice required to be given to a person under Section 1460.  (Cal. Prob. Code §1460, subd. (e)). Alternatively, as to an expedited petition: Unless a hearing is requested or ordered, an expedited petition must be determined without a hearing, not more than 35 days after filing, subject to extension for good cause.  (CRC Rule 7.950.5(b), (c)). “If a judge receives an unauthorized ex parte communication bearing upon the substance of a matter, the judge shall make provision promptly to notify the parties of the substance of the communication and provide the parties with an opportunity to respond.” (Code of Judicial Ethics, Canon 3B(7)(d).)

A petition for court approval of a compromise under Code of Civil Procedure section 372 must comply with California Rules of Court rules 7.950, 7.951, and 7.952. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1384(a).) Some of the Probate Code and the Code of Civil Procedure apply to compromises as to minors’ claims. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) In large part, the Local Rules incorporate by reference the California Rules of Court and statutory provisions. (See Super. Ct. LA County, Local Rules, rule 4.115 et seq.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

The Petitioner and Guardian Ad Litem petitions for Court approval of a settlement in the sum of $10,000.00, to go to Claimant, age 4.

 

Plaintiff would file a Request for Dismissal as to settling Defendant URBANSITTER, INC. upon

completion of settlement terms, and dismiss the remaining Defendants due to the lack of merit and the individual Defendant’s lack of funds for satisfying any judgment.

 

After treatment, including skin grafts, the Claimant has almost fully healed, but may have some residual discoloration that may heal in time.

 

Claimant’s net recovery of $8,103.52 would be placed into an annuity with guaranteed payments once Plaintiff reaches age 18. 

 

Total medical expenses were $551,645.90, but Medi-Cal agreed to accept a reduced amount of $1,896.48 as a lien out of the settlement.

 

There are no attorneys’ fees charged.

 

A proposed form Order has been lodged for Court signature and filing.

 

Most importantly, courts must decide whether to approve a compromise by determining if petitioners, such as guardians, are acting in the best interests of minors or incompetent persons. (E.g., CCP §372(a); Scruton v. Korean Air Lines Co. (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 1596, 1603-07.) “[T]he protective role the court generally assumes in cases involving minors, [is] a role to assure that whatever is done is in the minor's best interests….  [I]ts primary concern is whether the compromise is sufficient to provide for the minor's injuries, care and treatment.”  (Goldberg v. Sup. Ct. (1994) 23 Cal.App.4th 1378, 1382.) Judicial discretion in approving petitions for minors’ compromises, and ordering distributions, and local court policies, must be applied in the best interests of minors, in a case-by-case method, tailored to the circumstances.  (Christensen v. Sup. Ct. (1987) 193 Cal.App.3d 139, 142-44.)

 

Here, the Court defers consideration of the merits pending proof of notice to appearing Defendants.

 

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court continues the Petition, for notice to Defendants.

YOUNA KANG vs SUN INDUSTRIES CORPORATION, A CALIFORNIA CORPORATION, et al.

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery").  Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery"). Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery"). Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery"). Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery"). Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery"). Motion to Compel Discovery (not "Further Discovery").

 

RULING:

The Court grants all six unopposed Motions, as prayed.

The Court will complete, sign and file the proposed Orders received-stamped on August 21, 2026.

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

ANALYSIS:

By six Motions of YOUNA KANG (Plaintiff), filed on August 21, 2026, Plaintiff requests the following Order provisions:

1.      Compelling HANGWOO LEE, SEO YEON SON, and IRWIN SANGWOO LEE (Defendants), to serve responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories (General), Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, without objections.

2.      Requiring Defendants and counsel of record to pay a monetary sanction in the amount of $2,310.00 to Plaintiff, for each Motion.  

The Motions are pursuant to C.C.P. § 2030.290, on the grounds that Defendants failed to provide any responses to those specified discovery requests.

A motion to compel initial discovery responses need only show that discovery was properly served on the opposing party, the time to respond expired, and no response was served. (Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906; Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290 [compelling initial interrogatory responses], 2031.300 [compelling initial document requests].) A motion to compel initial discovery responses need not show good cause, meeting and conferring, or timely filing, and need not be accompanied by a separate statement. (Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pac. Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 404.)

The burden of justifying any failure to respond to discovery requests remains at all times with the responding party. (E.g., Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 541.)

Monetary discovery sanctions are mandatory as to parties losing discovery motions, unless courts find substantial justification or other injustice. (Deck v. Devs. Inv. Co. (2023) 89 Cal.App.5th 808, 830.)

Here, Defendants have filed no Opposition to show substantial justification for their failures to serve discovery responses to the duly served requests.

Therefore, the Court grants the unopposed Motions and will sign and complete the proposed Orders.

25STCV35907

EMMA SWEARENGIN, et al. vs PIPER MAVIS

September 24, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO INSPECTION DEMAND, SET NO. ONE [DEMAND NUMBERS 12, 14, AND 16] TO PIPER MAVIS.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the Motion.

Defendant PIPER MAVIS is ordered to provide responses without objection and produce responsive documents to Plaintiffs’ Inspection Demand, Set No. One, Demand Numbers 12, 14 and 16, within 20 days of the service of this Order.

Additionally, the Court issues a protective order requiring Plaintiffs’ counsel to restrict disclosure of the ordered discovery to counsel and to limit use of it for purposes of this lawsuit only.

The Court awards monetary discovery sanctions in the amount of $5,260.00 jointly and severally against Defendant PIPER MAVIS and counsel ENESTEIN PHAM GLASS GUTENPLAN & BULLING LLP.

Plaintiffs to give notice.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On September 3, 2026, EMMA SWEARENGIN and GIOVANNI BOSKOVICH (Plaintiffs) filed the Second Amended Complaint (SAC) against PIPER MAVIS (Defendant), for: TRESPASS, PRIVATE NUISANCE, NEGLIGENCE, BREACH OF HABITABILITY; STATUTORY BREACH OF HABITABILITY; BREACH OF QUIET ENJOYMENT; CONSTRUCTIVE EVICTION; FEHA DISCRIMINATION- CIVIL CODE SECTION 51 ET SEQ.; CIVIL CODE SECTION 1946.2; and CIVIL CODE SECTION 1950.5.

Plaintiffs allege that Defendant, as landlord, rented a single-family residence at 741 Montecito Drive, Los Angeles, where Plaintiffs, as tenants, experienced mold related injuries, due to needed plumbing repairs, and Defendant discriminated against Plaintiffs’ asthma conditions, based on evicting with the false excuse of inability to pay for remediation and a need to sell the house.

On August 27, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the Amended Motion to Compel Defendant’s Further Document Responses, to Request Numbers 12, 14 and 16, seeking financial records regarding Defendant’s asserted lack of finances to remediate. Plaintiffs also seek monetary sanctions.

Defendant Opposes mainly based upon privacy related objections, and requests  $5,245.00 in  sanctions against Plaintiffs.

The Reply, at page 2, emphasizes that there is more than just a text indicating inability to pay for remediation with intent to sell the house.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Where respondents object or respond inadequately to discovery requests, a motion lies to compel further responses, as to which respondent has the burden to justify the objections. (Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255; Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a) [motion to compel further responses lies “[o]n receipt of a response to an inspection demand….”].) A propounding party's remedy when it deems objections in discovery responses to be without merit is to move for an order compelling further responses. (Catalina Island Yacht Club v. Superior Court (2015) 242 Cal.App.4th 1116, 1127.)

Monetary discovery sanctions are mandatory as to parties losing discovery motions, unless courts find substantial justification or other injustice. (Deck v. Devs. Inv. Co. (2023) 89 Cal.App.5th 808, 830.) “‘[T]he phrase “substantial justification” has been understood to mean that a justification is clearly reasonable because it is well grounded in both law and fact.’” (Kwan Software Engineering, Inc. v. Hennings (2020) 58 Cal.App.5th 57, 75.) “If the party seeking a monetary sanction meets its burden of proof, the burden shifts to the opposing party attempting to avoid a monetary sanction to show that it acted with ‘substantial justification.’” (Doe v. U.S. Swimming, Inc. (2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1424, 1435.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiffs request an Order compelling Defendant to provide full and complete responses and production of documents to demand numbers 12, 14 and 16, without the financial privacy and other objections, and imposing sanctions against Defendant and ENENSTEIN PHAM GLASS GUTENPLAN & BOLLING LLP, in the amount of $5,260.00.

Defendant contends that the discovery of private finances is not relevant to the claims, and is not justified by a text message from May 2025 equivocally stating Defendant was “ ‘not financially prepared’ ” to undertake mold remediation at the rental property.

Courts must carefully balance a right of privacy against the interest in having just litigation. (Pioneer Electronics (USA), Inc. v. Superior Court (2007) 40 Cal.4th 360, 371; Valley Bank of Nevada v. Superior Court (1975) 15 Cal.3d 652, 657.)

Once good cause for production has been shown, the objecting party has the burden to file evidence to justify objections including those based upon privacy. (Kirkland v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 97-98.)

“[I]ndividuals have a legally recognized privacy interest in their personal financial information.” (International Federation of Professional & Technical Engineers, Local 21, AFL-CIO v. Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 319, 330.)

Whenever possible, judges should consider the possibility of a protective order limiting the intrusion or disclosure, for assuaging privacy concerns. (Alch v. Superior Court (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 1412, 1432.) Judges may issue protective orders restricting disclosure of information to counsel for parties requesting discovery, and limiting the use to lawsuit purposes. (Moskowitz v. Superior Court (1982) 137 Cal.App.3d 313, 318-319, disapproved on other grounds by Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 557.)

Restricting discovery as to defendants’ financial condition (Civ. Code § 3295(c)) is inapposite where finances are relevant as to a substantive claim.  (Rawnsley v. Superior Court (1986) 183 Cal.App.3d 86, 91).

Whether lessors have a right to evict tenants is a recognized issue as to claims for Wrongful Eviction. (See Cunningham v. Universal Underwriters (2002) 98 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1150 [a “wrongful eviction … occurs when the person recovering the property had no right to dispossess the other party from the property.”].) There is law to prevent landlords from evicting based on false pretenses. (E.g., Apartment Assn. of Los Angeles Cnty., Inc. v. City of Los Angeles (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 13, 23.) Further, good faith or pretext for evictions is generally determined from all relevant surrounding circumstances. (DeLisi v. Lam (2019) 39 Cal. App. 5th 663, 675. See also generally Wrongful eviction, Cal. Civ. Prac. Real Property Litigation § 30:18 [available on Westlaw].) Landlords have the obligation to provide habitable premises to tenants. (Fairchild v. Park (2001) 90 Cal. App. 4th 919, 928.) There have been laws to prevent landlords from going out of business unless residences are incapable of being made habitable in an economically feasible manner. (E.g., City of Santa Monica v. Yarmark (1988) 203 Cal.App.3d 153, 164.)

Here, the Court finds that Plaintiffs showed good cause for learning financial information from one-month periods of Defendant’s finances, towards evidencing whether Defendant’s alleged reason for eviction was pretextual to circumvent law. And Defendant has not demonstrated that financial privacy outweighs the interest in having just litigation, or any substantial justification.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the Court grants the Motion, for reasons stated.

Additionally, to mitigate privacy intrusion, the Court adds a protective order set forth above.

26STCV17030

KAKHABERI GVANTSELADZE vs LUV CAR WASH LLC   

September 24, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: DEFENDANT’S DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT.  DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO STRIKE.

 

 RULING:

The Court sustains the
Demurrer and grants the Motion to Strike, with leave to amend.

 Plaintiff may serve and file a First Amended
Complaint (FAC) on or before October 23, 2026.









Defendant to give notice.

I.                    BACKGROUND

On June 1, 2026, KAKHABER GVANTSELADZE (Plaintiff), a self-represented litigant, filed a Complaint against  LUV CARWASH, LLC (Defendant), apparently implying Causes of Action for Negligence and Unfair Business Practices.

The Complaint contains allegations that Plaintiff’s was severely damaged by malfunctioning equipment inside Defendant’s automated carwash, at 6651 Sunset Blvd, Los Angeles.

The Complaint improperly has a Motion for Summary Judgment attached as an exhibit.

On August 10, 2026, Defendant filed the Demurrer to the Complaint and Motion to Strike various allegations, on grounds including that the Complaint fails to allege claim elements beyond conclusions, and includes unauthorized damages such as emotional distress for property damage, such that leave to amend should be denied.

Plaintiff has not filed any Opposition to the Demurrer or Motion to Strike.
 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Demurrers are to be sustained where a pleading fails to plead adequately any essential element of the cause of action. (Cantu v. Resolution Trust Corp. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 857, 879-880.)

“It is both improper and insufficient for a plaintiff to simply plead the evidence by which [she or] he hopes to prove such ultimate facts.” (Careau & Co. v. Sec. Pac. Bus. Credit (1990) 222 Cal.App.3d 1371, 1390.) “A pleading is no place to quote, paraphrase, or even allude to the testimony of witnesses,” or to summarize deposition testimony and other evidence. (Blickman Turkus, LP v. MF Downtown Sunnyvale, LLC (2008) 162 Cal.App.4th 858, 868, fn.1.) A summary judgment “motion and supporting papers shall be served on all other parties to the action at least 81 days before the time appointed for hearing. If the notice is served by mail, the required 81-day period of notice shall be increased by 5 days if the place of address is within the State of California….” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (a)(2).)

Rarely should judges sustain demurrers to initial complaints without giving leave to amend. (Tarrar Enterprises, Inc. v. Associated Indemnity Corp. (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 685, 689.) “If the plaintiff has not had an opportunity to amend the complaint in response to the demurrer, leave to amend is liberally allowed as a matter of fairness, unless the complaint shows on its face that it is incapable of amendment.” (City of Stockton v. Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 730, 747.) Courts generally allow at least one time to amend a complaint, after sustaining a demurrer, even without any request for leave to amend. (McDonald v. Superior Court (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 297, 303.)

The failure to file a proper and timely opposition in trial court creates a waiver of the issues on any appeal. (Bell v. Am. Title Ins. Co. (1991) 226 Cal.App.3d 1589, 1602; Cabrini Villas Homeowners Assn. v. Haghverdian (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 683, 693.) “A failure to oppose a motion may be deemed a consent to the granting of the motion.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.54(c).)

A party appearing without a lawyer is not entitled to any greater privileges than an attorney following legal requirements. (Bianco v. California Highway Patrol (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1125-1126; Morgan v. Ransom (1979) 95 Cal.App.3d 664, 669; Burnete v. La Casa Dana Apartments (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1262, 1267.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Defendant demurs to the Complaint, because it fails to allege facts, such as what aspect of the carwash system failed, how Defendant controlled or operated it negligently, and what act or omission caused the alleged damage. Additionally, Defendant criticizes allegations of Unfair Business Practices, because Negligence and any other bases do not support it.  Also, Defendant objects to Strict Liability if intended as a claim, including because Defendant is not a manufacturer of products. Finally, Defendant argues uncertainty caused by the format that is not organized by any separated Causes of Action.

Additionally, Defendant moves to strike the following allegations, as being unauthorized damages:

1. Section III, 3 line, "bad faith evasion."

2. Section IV, line 4, "bad faith campaign of evasion."

3. Section IV, Request for Treble Damages.

4. Section IV lines 8-9, Treble Damage Multiplier (3x Penalty) $7,290.00x3=$21,870.

5. Section IV, line 13, "defendant is strictly liable."

6. Section IV "Documented Medical and Physical Collapse", paras. 13-16, including "physical injuries."

7. Section VII, No. 1 Treble Property Damage (Per UCL 17200): $21,870.

8. Section VII, No. 6 Emotional compensation for humiliation, discrimination, total ignorance and

complete plaintiff's and elderly people that he is supporting all his life health and life: $125,00000.

(Motion, p. 2.)

Demurrers are to be sustained where a pleading fails to plead adequately any essential element of the cause of action. (Cantu v. Resolution Trust Corp. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 857, 879-880.)

“Each separately stated cause of action, count, or defense must specifically state: [¶] (1) Its number (e.g., "first cause of action"); [¶] (2) Its nature (e.g., "for fraud")….” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.112.) The failure to identify claims and parties by labeling causes of action may render a complaint uncertain, except where the complaint contains allegations sufficiently apprising defendants of the issues, in which case a demurrer should be overruled. (Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139.) The ground of uncertainty relates to alleged facts, and does not lie as to the label, legal theory or effect of a cause of action. (County of Santa Clara v. Hayes Co. (1954) 43 Cal.2d 615, 619-620.)

“An ‘unlawful’ practice requires violation of another statute, and a business practice may be ‘unfair’ even if not otherwise proscribed by statute as long as the practice is not expressly authorized by law.” (People ex rel. Dept. of Motor Vehicles v. Cars 4 Causes (2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 1006, 1016.) See also Gutierrez v. Carmax Auto Superstores California (2018) 19 Cal.App.5th 1234, 1261 [“causes of action under the … UCL must be stated with reasonable particularity, which is a more lenient pleading standard than is applied to common law fraud claims.”]; Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title Guar. Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26, 46 [holding claims for unfair business practices need not be pled specifically, and impliedly disapproving Khoury v. Maly's of Cal. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 619 ("A plaintiff alleging unfair business practices under these statutes must state with reasonable particularity the facts supporting the statutory elements of the violation.")]; Gregory v. Albertson's, Inc. (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 845, 857 [“the demurrer was properly sustained because the complaint ‘identifies no particular section of the statutory scheme [of antitrust laws] which was violated and fails to describe with any reasonable particularity the facts supporting violation.’"]; Linear Technology Corp. v. Applied Materials, Inc. (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 115, 134-135 [“Whether a practice is deceptive, fraudulent, or unfair is generally a question of fact which requires ‘consideration and weighing of evidence from both sides’ and which usually cannot be made on demurrer.”]; Wolski v. Fremont Investment & Loan (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 347, 357 [a cause of action for violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200 fails where it is predicated on a violation of law that fails]; Bank of the West v. Superior Court (1992) 2 Cal.4th 1254, 1266 [“The Unfair Business Practices Act defines ‘unfair competition’ as any ‘unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business practice and unfair, deceptive, untrue or misleading advertising….’ (§ 17200.)].)

The law of negligence is declared in the Civil Code section 1714. (Simmons v. Stephens (1920) 48 Cal. App. 351, 354.) Civil Code section 3333 limits damages for negligence to detriment proximately caused by act or omission. (Barthels v. Santa Barbara Title Co. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 674, 679.  “Ordinarily, negligence may be pleaded in general terms and the plaintiff need not specify the precise act or omission alleged to constitute the breach of duty.” (Lopez v. S. Cal. Rapid Transit Dist. (1985) 40 Cal.3d 780, 795. Accord, Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747; Crouse v. Brobeck (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1509, 1532 [to allege negligence claims, complainants need only state what occurred, and generally that the acts were negligently done, but “need not state the specific act or omission constituting negligent conduct.”]. But see Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 527 [general allegations must set forth acts or omissions which are said to have been negligent].) “In order to state a cause of action for negligence, the complaint must allege facts sufficient to show a legal duty on the part of the defendant to use due care, a breach of such legal duty, and the breach as the proximate or legal cause of the resulting injury.” (Bellah v. Greenson (1978) 81 Cal.App.3d 614, 619.) As to negligence claims, causation may be alleged “succinctly and generally,” unless the pled facts “do not naturally give rise to an inference of causation….” (Bockrath v. Aldrich Chemical Co., Inc. (1999) 21 Cal.4th 71, 78.) “ ‘In an action to recover damages resulting from the negligence of the defendant, “a general allegation of negligence upon the part of defendant is sufficient.” In such a case, “the negligence is the ultimate fact to be pleaded, and is not a legal conclusion.’ ” (Kienlen v. Holt (1930) 106 Cal.App. 135, 139–140.)

Here, Plaintiff has failed to file any Opposition addressing law set forth above and in the Defendant’s filings. A judge in a civil case is not “‘obligated to seek out theories [a party] might have advanced, or to articulate … that which … [a party] has left unspoken.’” (Mesecher v. County of San Diego (1992) 9 Cal.App.4th 1677, 1686.)

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

Therefore, the Court sustains the Demurrer and grants the Motion, with leave to file an amended complaint, based upon the substantive Demurrer pages, and the law and procedures referenced herein.
