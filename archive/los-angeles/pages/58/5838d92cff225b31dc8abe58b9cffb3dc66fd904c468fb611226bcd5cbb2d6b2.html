Case Number: 23PSCV00325    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: H

Zou, et al. v. CC Express Trucking, et al., Case No. 23PSCV00325 (consolidated with Case No. 23STCV02756)


ORDER ON APPLICATION FOR DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

Plaintiffs Zhenni Zou and Xiaoye Cui’s Application for Default Judgment is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Background 

Case No. 23PSCV00325 

Plaintiffs Zhenni Zou (“Zou”) and Xiaoye Cui (“Cui”) (together, “Zou/Cui”) allege as follows:

          On July 30, 2023, Zou/Cui formed CC Express Trucking (“CC Express”). At that time, Zou/Cui each deposited $20,000.00 into the CC Express bank account to become equal shareholders. Zou was named as CC Express’ Director, CEO and CFO, while Cui was named as its Secretary. On August 29, 2020, Qian Yu (“Yu”) visited Zou/Cui’s house, during which time an oral agreement was entered into wherein Yu would become a 20% shareholder of CC Express for a $10,000.00 investment and Zou/Cui would remain equal, majority shareholders with 40% each of the remaining shares. On September 4, 2020, CC Express received Yu’s $10,000 investment.

On or around November 20, 2020, Zou/Cui and Yu orally agreed that Yu would make a down payment on a truck (i.e., #6) and purchase a container for CC Express’ benefit, with title in the name of CC Express; in return, Yu’s interest in CC Express would increase from 20% to 33 1/3%. Instead, Yu put title to truck #6 and the contained in her own name. Between October 2021 and December 2021, two of CC Express’ trucks, including truck #6, broke down out-of-state, resulting in expensive repairs and towing costs paid by CC Express. On or about November 26, 2021, Zou/Cui and Yu agreed to sell CC Express’ truck #10 for $100,000 to help pay for CC Express’ operating expenses. Yu convinced Zou/Cui to have the sale proceeds from truck #10 transferred to a separate account, to which Yu had sole access. After failing to get Zou/Cui to invest the sale proceeds from truck #10 into a different business venture, Yu transferred their share of the sale profits to them. Based on the agreement to sell truck #10, Zou/Cui had the understanding that the payouts would be reinvested back into CC Express. Yu used her share of the profits for her own benefit, while Zou/Cui deposited their shares into the CC Express account in order to continue paying operating expenses. Zou/Cui believe Yu has converted truck #6 and the container for her own use and benefit. Zou/Cui and Yu have had material disagreements which have resulted in the mismanagement of CC Express assets throughout the entirety of CC Express’ operational history. Yu has failed to cooperate with Zou/Cui’s efforts to wind down CC Express. 

On July 5, 2023, Case Nos. 23PSCV00325 and 23STCV02756 were related; Case No. 23PSCV00325 was designated as the lead case. 

On February 13, 2024, Zou/Cui filed a Derivative First Amended Complaint, asserting causes of action against Yu and Does 1-50 and Nominal Defendant CC Express for: (1) Conversion and (2) Dissolution of Corporation Pursuant to California Corporations Code § 1800 et seq. 

On April 9, 2024, Yu’s default was entered. 

On May 9, 2024, the Court consolidated Case Nos. 23PSCV00325 and 23STCV02756 for all purposes and designated Case No. 23PSCV00325 as the lead case. 

On February 19, 2026, the Court ordered Yu’s complaint filed February 8, 2023 in Case No. 23STCV02756 dismissed, without prejudice. 

An Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment is set for July 23, 2026.

Case No. 23STCV02756 

Yu alleges as follows: Yu, Zou and Cui are each 33.3% shareholders of CC Express. On or about September 14, 2021, Zou and Cui set up CSZ Logistics, Inc. (“CSZ”); since that time, Zou and Cui have transferred the entire business of CC Express to CSZ without Yu’s consent. 

On February 8, 2023, Yu filed a complaint, asserting ten causes of action against CC Express, CSZ Logistics, Inc., Zou, Cui and Does 1-50, including for breach of fiduciary duty, conversion, shareholder’s derivative action for damages, false promise, unfair competition, and unjust enrichment. 

On April 3, 2023, CC Express’ default was entered. 

On July 5, 2023, Case Nos. 23PSCV00325 and 23STCV02756 were related; Case No. 23PSCV00325 was designated as the lead case. 

On March 12, 2024, the Court ordered CSZ’s answer stricken and default entered. 

On May 9, 2024, the Court consolidated Case Nos. 23PSCV00325 and 23STCV02756 for all purposes and designated Case No. 23PSCV00325 as the lead case.

 

Discussion 

Zou/Cui’s application for default judgment is denied without prejudice. The following defects are noted:

1.       Zou/Cui seek $149,324.69 in compensatory damages. (Case Summary, 7:10-13.) The only specific monetary figures identified in the Derivative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), however, are Zou/Cui’s deposit of $20,000.00 each into CC Express’ bank account (FAC, ¶ 12), Yu’s $10,000.00 contribution to CC Express (Id., ¶¶ 13, 14 and 43), the parties’ agreement to sell CC Express’ truck #10 for $100,000.00 (Id., ¶¶ 19, 32 and 48), Yu’s retention of $33,333.33 from the sale of CC Express’ truck #10 for her own personal benefit (Id., ¶¶ 25, 35, 51, 57 and 58), and Yu’s transfer of $33,333.33 each to Zou and Cui following the sale of truck #10 and Zou’s and Cui’s subsequent deposit of these monies into CC Express’ account (Id., ¶¶ 33, 35 and 49). The prayer of the FAC seeks unspecified general, consequential and special damages. In actions for money damages a default judgment is limited to the amount demanded in the complaint. (See Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 824; Code Civ. Proc., § 580, subd. (a) [“The relief granted to the plaintiff, if there is no answer, cannot exceed that demanded in the complaint, in the statement required by Section 425.11, or in the statement provided for by Section 425.115. . .’].) The amount demanded in the complaint is determined both from the prayer and from the damage allegations in the complaint. (National Diversified Services, Inc. v. Bernstein (1985) 168 Cal.App.3d 410, 417-418.)

2.       Zou/Cui seek $300,000.00 in punitive damages. (Case Summary, 7:17-20.) It does not appear that a Code of Civil Procedure section 425.115 statement has been served. Additionally, Zou/Cui have not provided the Court with any evidence of Yu’s financial condition, which is required to support an award of punitive damages on default judgment. (See Cummings Medical Corp. v. Occupational Medical Corp. (1992) 10 Ca.App.4th 1291, 1299-1301.)

3.       The Court requests that Zou/Cui provide an itemized breakdown for their request for $648.09 in “e-filing and motion fees” and $1,185.00 in “court interpreter fees.”

4.       Zou/Cui reserve on the issue of attorney’s fees (Case Summary, 7:21-23); however, “a default judgment is intended to include all relief sought in the complaint and established by the plaintiff. Therefore, a plaintiff seeking an award of attorney fees from a defaulting defendant must request those fees at the time the plaintiff requests entry of default.” (Garcia v. Politis (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 1474, 1480.)

5.       The Court requests that Zou/Cui provide an interest calculation. 

Accordingly, the application is denied without prejudice, and Zou/Cui are directed to submit a revised and complete default judgment application that addresses the aforementioned issues. Any subsequently submitted default prove-up application must be full and complete, in and of itself. The Court will not review piecemeal submissions.    


Case Number: 25PSCV02303    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: H

Ramos v. Alliance Environmental, Case No. 25PSCV02303

 

ORDER ON MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND

 

Plaintiff Alexandria Monique Ramos’, Joseph Ramos’, James Ramos’ and Olive Ramos’ unopposed Motion for Leave to File Third Amended Complaint to Correct Clerical Error in Date of Incident is GRANTED. The Court directs Plaintiffs’ counsel to file a copy of Exhibit 1 attached to his accompanying declaration no later than 10 calendar days from the date of the hearing.

 

Background

Plaintiffs Alexandria Monique Ramos, Joseph Ramos, James Ramos and Olive Ramos (together, “Plaintiffs”) allege that they sustained injuries and damages in a June 26, 2023 motor vehicle accident on the 57 northbound freeway, south of Diamond Bar Boulevard. 

On March 9, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Second Amended Complaint, asserting a motor vehicle cause of action against Alliance Environmental Group, LLC, AEG Holdings, LLC (“AEG”), Jose Armando, Jorge Lopez De Los Santos, and Does 1-10. 

On June 9, 2026, Plaintiffs dismissed AEG, without prejudice.

A Case Management Conference is set for July 10, 2026.

The Final Status Conference is set for November 17, 2025. Trial is set for December 1, 2026.

 

Legal Standard 

“The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1); and see § 576 [“Any judge, at any time before or after commencement of trial, in the furtherance of justice, and upon such terms as may be proper, may allow the amendment of any pleading or pretrial conference order”].)

“[T]he trial court has wide discretion in allowing the amendment of any pleading.” (Bedolla v. Logan & Frazer (1975) 52 Cal.App.3d 118, 135.) “[I]t is irrelevant that new legal theories are introduced as long as the proposed amendments relate to the same general set of facts.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1048 [quotation marks and citation omitted].) “[E]ven if the proposed legal theory is a novel one, the preferable practice would be to permit the amendment and allow the parties to test its legal sufficiency by demurrer, motion for judgment on the pleadings or other appropriate proceedings.” (Id. [quotation marks and citation omitted].) With that said, “the failure of a proposed amendment to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action or defense may support an order denying a motion to amend.” (California Casualty Gen. Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1985) 173 Cal.App.3d 274, 280, disapproved of on other grounds in Kransco v. American Empire Surplus Lines Ins. Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 390) 

Courts must apply a policy of great liberality in permitting amendments to the complaint at any stage of the proceedings, up to and including trial, when no prejudice is shown to the adverse party. (Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2003) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 761.) However, “even if a good amendment is proposed in proper form, unwarranted delay in presenting it may—of itself—be a valid reason for denial. . . denial may rest upon the element of lack of diligence in offering the amendment after knowledge of the facts, or the effect of the delay on the adverse party.” (Roemer v. Retail Credit Co. (1975) 44 Cal.App.3d 926, 940.) 

“A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:
(1) Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;
(2) State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and
(3) State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.” (Cal. Rules Court, rule 3.1324(a).) 

Additionally, “[a] separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify:
(1) The effect of the amendment;
(2) Why the amendment is necessary and proper;
(3) When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and
(4) The reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.” (Cal. Rules Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

 

Discussion 

Plaintiffs move the Court for an order granting Plaintiffs leave to file a Third Amended Complaint (“SAC”) to 

The motion reflects compliance with California Rules of Court rule 3.1324, subdivision (a). Additionally, the Declaration of Rendell M. Guerrero (“Guerrero”) reflects compliance with subdivision (b).

The unopposed motion is granted.   

The Court directs counsel for Plaintiffs to file a copy of Exhibit 1 attached to the aforesaid declaration no later than 10 calendar days from the date of the hearing. 

Case Number: 25PSCV03282    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: H

J&P Equipment Rentals v. Gaby’s Tacos, Case No. 25PSCV03282

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

Counsel for Cross-Defendant BCS Technology and Graphics LLC’s (i.e., Jackman Law, P.C.) Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is GRANTED, effective upon the filing of the proof of service reflecting service of the signed order upon Client at Client’s last known address. The Court will set an Order to Show Cause Re: Representation of LLC for September 4, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

 

Background 

Plaintiff J&P Equipment Rentals (“Plaintiff”) alleges damages arising out of a February 22, 2024 motor vehicle accident. 

On September 10, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting a negligence cause of action against Gaby’s Tacos, LLC (“Gaby’s Tacos”) and Does 1-20. 

On October 30, 2025, Gaby’s Tacos filed a cross-complaint, asserting causes of action against BCS Technology and Graphics (“BCS”), David Lopez (“Lopez”) and Roes 1-20 for: (1) Equitable Indemnity, (2) Contribution, and (3) Declaratory Relief. 

On May 28, 2026, Lopez’s default was entered on the cross-complaint. 

A Case Management Conference is set for July 28, 2026. 

The Final Status Conference is set for October 22, 2026. Trial is set for November 4, 2026.

 

Discussion

Jackman Law, P.C. (“Firm”) seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for BCS (“Client”). 

The court has discretion to allow an attorney to withdraw, and such a motion should be granted provided that there is no prejudice to the client and it does not disrupt the orderly process of justice. (See Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915; People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398.) 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure § 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under section 284(1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion, the declaration, and the proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) a proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-053)). The court may delay the effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (Cal. Rules Court, rule 3.1362(e).)

Attorney Ryan E. Jackman (“Jackman”) represents that “[t]he attorney-client relationship has broken down to the extent that I am unable to continue representation.” He further advises that he must withdraw “for ethical reasons,” that the reason for withdrawal is “mandatory,” and that he is precluded by the principles of attorney-client privilege from disclosing the reasons for same. 

Jackman states that he has served Client by mail at Client’s last known address with copies of the motion papers served with his declaration and that he has confirmed within the past 30 days that the address is current by verifying the registered agent for service of process through Secretary of State records, which match the address provided by Client. 

The Court determines that the requirements of Rules of Court Rule 3.1362 enumerated above have been sufficiently met.

Accordingly, the motion is granted, effective upon the filing of the proof of service reflecting service of the signed order upon Client at Client’s last known address.

The Court will set an Order to Show Cause Re: Representation of LLC for September 4, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. The Court directs counsel to provide notice of the OSC to Client.

Background

1. Motion to Quash Re: First Credit Bank

Legal Standard

“If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), . . .may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).) “The following persons may make a motion pursuant to subdivision (a): (1) A party. (2) A witness . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (b).)

In making an order pursuant to section 1987.1, “the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2, subd. (a).)

Discussion

Zhang, CTC, RDD, SZ, U.S. Discount, Sierra, and Nominal Defendant C55 (collectively together, “Defendants”) move the Court, per Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, for an order quashing, or in the alternative modifying, the deposition subpoena issued by Plaintiffs on First Credit Bank.

Attorney Mark John Tundis (“Tundis”) represents that a copy of the subject subpoena has been attached as Exhibit 1 to his declaration; however, no exhibits have been attached. Accordingly, the Court will continue the hearing on the motion, to October 27, 2026, at 9:00 a.m.

Defendants’ counsel is directed to file and serve a supplemental declaration, limited to providing the Court with a copy of the subject subpoena, no later than 9 court days prior to the continued hearing date.
