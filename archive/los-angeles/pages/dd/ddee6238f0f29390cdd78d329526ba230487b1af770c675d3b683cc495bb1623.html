MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant General Motors LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Jeanette Carranza

Motion for Summary Judgment

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant General Motors LLC (“Defendant”) moves for summary judgment.

The court finds that Defendant’s separate statement does not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 437c and California Rules of Court rule 3.1350.  

A motion for summary judgment “shall include a separate statement setting forth plainly and concisely all material facts that the moving party contends are undisputed.  Each of the material facts stated shall be followed by a reference to the supporting evidence.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (b)(1).) “The requirement of a separate statement from the moving party and a responding statement from the party opposing summary judgment serves two functions: to give the parties notice of the material facts at issue in the motion and to permit the trial court to focus on whether those facts are truly undisputed.” (Parkview Villas Assn., Inc. v. State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. (2005) 133 Cal.App.4th 1197, 1210.) The separate statement must be presented in the two column format specified in the Rules of Court and must “separately” identify “[e]ach supporting material fact claimed to be without dispute with respect to the cause of action” –that is, each cause of action – “that is the subject of the motion.” (Cal Rules of Court, Rule 3.1350(d)(1)(B); subd. (h) [emphasis added].)

Defendant has not complied with Rule 3.1350 because Defendant’s separate statement does not separately identify each material fact claimed to be undisputed with respect to each cause of action that is the subject of the motion. Defendant instead set forth eight material facts without distinction as to each of the seven causes of action that are the subject of the motion. By failing to follow Rule 3.1350(d), the separate statement does not serve either of the essential functions of a separate statement. Rather than give fair notice to plaintiff and to the court of the facts which Defendant claims are material to each cause of action, the separate statement obfuscates that information in the highly fact-specific context of the summary judgment analysis.

Defendant bears the burden as the moving party to show that plaintiff’s causes of action have no merit. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(2).) It is therefore Defendant’s burden, not the court’s, to identify the evidence to support Defendant’s motion. (Collins v. Hertz Corp. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 64, 75 [“It is appellants’ duty to direct the court to evidence that supports their claims. It is not the court’s duty to rummage through the papers to construct or resuscitate their case”].)

The court therefore denies Defendant’s motion because Defendant’s separate statement does not comply with section 437c and California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (b)(1) [“The failure to comply with this requirement of a separate statement may in the court’s discretion constitute a sufficient ground for denying the motion”]; Beltran v. Hard Rock Hotel Licensing, Inc. (2023) 97 Cal.App.5th 865, 876 [“Trial courts should not hesitate to deny summary judgment motions when the moving party fails to draft a compliant separate statement . . . .”].) 

ORDER

The court DENIES defendant General Motors LLC’s motion for summary judgment.

The court orders plaintiff Jeanette Carranza to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Petition for approval of compromise of claim for minor claimant

 

MOVING PARTY:            Petitioner Jennifer Afari

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response

Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim for Minor Claimant 

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition papers were filed.

DISCUSSION

Petitioner Jennifer Afari seeks court approval of the settlement made on behalf of plaintiff minor claimant Julian David (“Minor Claimant”) in this action.

The compromise of a minor’s disputed claim for damages is valid only after it has been approved, upon the filing of a petition, by the court.¿ (Prob. Code, § 3500.)¿ The petition must be verified by the petitioner, must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise, and must be prepared on Judicial Council form MC-350.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

Defendant Barry J. Brock has agreed to settle this action for a total of $650,000.00, of which $500,000.00 is allocated to Minor Claimant.  (Pet., MC-350, ¶¶ 10-11.)  Of the 500,000.00 allocated to Minor Claimant, $157,571.38 will be paid to counsel for attorney’s fees and $16,011.01 will be paid for medical expenses, and $22,510.95 will be paid for costs.  (Pet., MC-350, ¶¶ 13, 16.)  The remaining $303,906.66 will be deposited into a single-premium deferred annuity, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the court.  (Pet., MC-350, ¶¶ 16, 18, subd. (b)(2).) 

The court has reviewed the petition and finds the settlement to be fair and reasonable, and in the best interests of Minor Claimant. The court further finds that the declaration of Robert Ounjian is sufficient to support the request for attorney’s fees in the amount $157,571.38  (representing 33 percent of the gross $500,000.00 settlement).  (Pet., Attachment 13a, Ounjian Decl., ¶14 .)   

          The court therefore grants the petition.

ORDER

The court GRANTS petitioner Jennifer Afari’s petition to approve the compromise of claim on behalf of minor claimant Julian David.

The court orders petitioner Jennifer Afari to file proof of purchase of annuity no later than October 16, 2026.   

The court sets for hearing an Order to Show Cause re filing proof of purchase of annuity on November 3, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Department 513.  

The court orders petitioner Jennifer Afari to give notice of this ruling. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
