DEPARTMENT 406 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV21584    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: 406

 

ART DECK, INC.,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

STRUCT 1 BUILDERS, INC., et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  25STCV21584 (Lead case)

                    25SMCV04920 (Related case)

  Hearing Date:  July 15, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

defendant design villas homeowners association’s demurrer to first amended complaint

 

 

I. BACKGROUND

            a. Lead Case (25STCV21584)

On July 22, 2025, Plaintiff Art Deck, Inc. (Art Deck) filed an action against Defendants Struct 1 Builders, Inc. (S1B), et al. for breach of contract and foreclosure of mechanics lien. Art Deck alleges that it was hired as a subcontractor of S1B to perform construction services on the subject property, which is allegedly owned by the other named defendants. Art Deck alleges that S1B breached the subcontract by failing to pay the amount owed. Art Deck sues S1B for breach of contract and seeks to foreclose on a mechanics lien as against the remaining owner defendants.

b. Related Case (25SMCV04920)

On September 19, 2025, S1B filed a complaint against Defendant Design Villas Homeowners Association, Inc. (HOA). S1B filed the operative First Amended Complaint on February 23, 2026, alleging (1) breach of contract, (2) foreclosure of mechanics lien, (3) quantum meruit, and (4) open book account.

S1B alleges that it contracted with HOA, which owns the subject property, to provide construction services. S1B alleges that HOA failed to pay the amount due under the contract. S1B seeks damages against HOA, including the amount that Art Deck seeks from S1B under the subcontract.  

The two cases have been related and consolidated, with 25STCV21584 as the lead case.

c. The Instant Motion

On May 29, 2026, HOA filed the instant demurrer to S1B’s FAC. S1B filed an opposition on July 1, 2026. HOA filed a reply on July 8, 2026.

II. LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. (Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228.) In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or by proper judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30(a).) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) “California law emphasizes ultimate fact pleading (with some exceptions, notably for fraud and related torts) ‘in ordinary and concise language,’ and the test for adequacy is not absolute but ‘whether the pleading as a whole apprises the adversary of the factual basis of the claim.’” (Lim v. The.TV Corp. Internat. (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 684, 690.)

III. MEET AND CONFER

Before filing a demurrer or a motion to strike, the demurring or moving party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to or the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.41, 435.5.)

Here, defense counsel’s declaration only indicates meet and confer regarding the original complaint. (See Zumstein Decl. ¶¶ 6-7.) However, “[i]f an amended complaint . . . is filed, the responding party shall meet and confer again with the party who filed the amended pleading before filing a demurrer to the amended pleading.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41(a).) The instant demurrer pertains to the FAC, but counsel’s declaration contains no evidence of a meet and confer pertaining to the FAC. Therefore, the Court finds that Defendant failed to adequately meet and confer.

Nonetheless, “[a] determination by the court that the meet and confer process was insufficient shall not be grounds to overrule or sustain a demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41(a)(4).) The issues raised in the demurrer are relatively straightforward, and the parties’ positions are clearly set forth in their respective briefs. Therefore, the Court proceeds on the merits.

IV. DISCUSSION

            a. Breach of Contract

            A demurrer for uncertainty is disfavored and is only granted “if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695.) A complaint does not need to be a “model of clarity” to survive a demurrer because most ambiguities can be clarified through discovery. (Ibid.)

            HOA argues that the first cause of action is uncertain because S1B alleges amounts owed under three separate contracts but claims one omnibus outstanding amount. The alleged outstanding amount is also less than the sum of the three contracts, indicating that approximately $43k has been paid towards the balance. HOA argues that the FAC “never provides what construction work was paid for, or relatedly what work has not been paid for” and that “the correct amount of balance owed has not been identified.” (Dem. 3:10-16.)

            However, these are ambiguities that can be clarified through discovery. The complaint sufficiently “apprises [HOA] of the factual basis of the claim” by alleging that HOA failed to pay the amount owed under the specified contracts. (See Lim, supra, 99 Cal.App.4th at p. 690.) This places HOA on sufficient notice to respond. The failure to specify the precise amount due does not render the complaint “so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” (See A.J. Fistes Corp., supra, 38 Cal.App.5th at p. 695.) 

            b. Foreclosure of Mechanics Lien

            HOA’s argument regarding the second cause of action is largely derivative of the above. Namely, HOA argues that the claim is uncertain because the alleged lien amount of $263k is inconsistent with the apparent payment of $43k. HOA argues that the FAC “does not specify the value of the work performed or who owes Plaintiff what amount of money for what work.” (Dem. 4:3-4.) Again, these are ambiguities that can be clarified through discovery. They are not pleading defects that render the complaint incomprehensible.

            c. Quantum Meruit

            HOA again argues that the claim is ambiguous because “the FAC sets forth no information as to what the ‘reasonable value of the services’ Plaintiff is trying to recover is or was conveyed on Defendant.” (Dem. 5:1-5.) HOA contends that the FAC “does not specify which of the three contracts, or portions thereof, were paid, or not paid.” (Ibid.) These are ambiguities that can be clarified through discovery.

For pleading purposes, the FAC has sufficiently pled a benefit conferred to HOA (construction services) for which full compensation has not been provided, resulting in a balance owed. This sets forth a quantum meruit claim with sufficient clarity for HOA to respond.

 

V. CONCLUSION

            Defendant Design Villas Homeowners Association, Inc.’s demurrer to First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

 


Case Number: 26STCV06903    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: 406

 

DAVID PORTILLO,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

FOX NORMANDIE APARTMENTS, L.P., et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  26STCV06903

  Hearing Date:  July 15, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

defendants’ demurrer and motion to strike

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On March 3, 2026, Plaintiff David Portillo filed this action against Defendants Fox Normandie Apartments, LP and Community of Friends. The complaint asserts (1) breach of contract/covenant of quiet enjoyment/warranty of habitability, (2) tortious breach of the warranty of habitability, (3) negligence, (4) violation of Civil Code section 1942.4, (5) violation of the UCL, and (6) tenant harassment.

            On June 16, 2026, Defendants filed the instant demurrer and motion to strike. Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 1, 2026. Defendants filed a reply on July 8, 2026.

LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. (Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228.) In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or by proper judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30(a).) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) “California law emphasizes ultimate fact pleading (with some exceptions, notably for fraud and related torts) ‘in ordinary and concise language,’ and the test for adequacy is not absolute but ‘whether the pleading as a whole apprises the adversary of the factual basis of the claim.’” (Lim v. The.TV Corp. Internat. (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 684, 690.)

MEET AND CONFER

Before filing a demurrer or a motion to strike, the demurring or moving party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to or the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.41, 435.5.) The Court finds that Defendants have satisfied the meet and confer requirement. (See Cary Decl.)

DISCUSSION

I. Demurrer

            a. Uncertainty

            A demurrer for uncertainty is disfavored and is only granted “if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695.) A complaint does not need to be a “model of clarity” to survive a demurrer because most ambiguities can be clarified through discovery. (Ibid.)

            Defendants argue that “[t]he Complaint is devoid of any facts regarding when Plaintiff’s tenancy began, when he experienced alleged conditions, and when Defendants were allegedly given notice of the alleged defects affecting Plaintiff. The extent of the defects are also not pled, and therefore whether these were material defective conditions is not sufficiently stated.” (Dem. 3:20-24.)

However, these are ambiguities that may be clarified in discovery. They are not factual deficiencies that detract from the adequacy of the pleading. For pleading purposes, the complaint sufficiently puts Defendants on notice of the nature of the claims. Therefore, the complaint is not uncertain.

b. Combined Causes of Action

Plaintiff’s first “cause of action” combines three causes of action: breach of contract, quiet enjoyment, and warranty of habitability. These are independent claims with distinct elements. Plaintiff argues that the covenant of quiet enjoyment and warranty of habitability are the contract terms which were breached. However, in that case, the breach of contract claim is duplicative of the other two claims. (See Award Metals, Inc. v. Superior Court (1991) 228 Cal.App.3d 1128, 1135 [a claim is duplicative if it “adds nothing to the complaint by way of fact or theory”].) Therefore, the demurrer is sustained as to breach of contract.

c. Statutory Claims

Defendants argue that the statutory claims (fourth and fifth causes of action) have not been pled with particularity. (See City of Los Angeles v. Superior Court (2021) 62 Cal.App.5th 129, 138.)

            1. Civil Code Section 1942.4

Civil Code section 1942.4 prohibits a landlord from collecting rent under the following conditions: (1) the dwelling is uninhabitable under section 1941.1; (2) the landlord has been notified by a public officer or employee responsible for enforcement of housing law; (3) the conditions are not abated for 35 days after the notice without good cause; and (4) the conditions were not caused by the tenant.

The complaint specifically pleads all of these elements. (Compl. ¶¶ 60-65.) Defendants argue that “[t]he only facts alleged by Plaintiff in the 4th cause of action is that there were leaks in Plaintiff’s unit.” (Dem. 4:1-3.) However, the complaint alleges that the conditions included inadequate weather protection, inadequate plumbing, mold, poor sanitation, lack of heating and ventilation, vermin infestation, structural hazards, inadequate mechanical equipment, lack of safety, and harassment. (Compl. ¶ 60.) This is adequate for pleading purposes.

            2. UCL

Business and Professions Code section 17200 prohibits unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent business acts or practices. Each of the three prongs is an independent basis for relief. (Smith v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 700, 718.) Unlawful conduct is defined as any practice forbidden by law. (Farmers Ins. Exchange v. Superior Court (1992) 2 Cal.4th 377, 383.) UCL actions alleging unlawful conduct “borrow” from other statutes or common law causes of action outside Section 17200. (Klein v. Chevron U.S.A., Inc. (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1342, 1383.)

Because Plaintiff has sufficiently pled a violation of Civil Code section 1942.4, breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment, and breach of the warranty of habitability, those violations serve as a predicate for the UCL claim under the “unlawful” prong. Because the same facts underly each claim, the UCL claim has been pled with the requisite specificity.

d. Tenant Harassment

Los Angeles Municipal Code section 45.33 prohibits a landlord from engaging in “bad faith conduct directed at a specific tenant or tenants that causes the latter detriment or harm.” (LAMC § 45.33.) “‘Bad faith’ refers to willful, reckless, or grossly negligent conduct.” (Ibid.) The code specifically identifies examples of tenant harassment, such as reducing housing services, failing to perform timely repairs, interfering with a tenant’s right to enjoy the premises, engaging in any act that renders the premises unfit for habitation, or any other repeated act that substantially interferes with a tenant’s comfort or peace. (Ibid.)

The complaint alleges sufficient facts to constitute tenant harassment. Whether Defendants’ alleged conduct was in bad faith or directed at Plaintiff are questions of fact. The complaint alleges that the conduct was directed at Plaintiff, which must be assumed true. (See Compl. ¶ 80.) Therefore, the tenant harassment claim is adequately pled.

II. Motion to Strike

            a. Punitive Damages

            “In an action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract, where it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice, the plaintiff, in addition to the actual damages, may recover damages for the sake of example and by way of punishing the defendant.” (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).) “‘Malice’ means conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.” (Id., subd. (c)(1).) “‘Oppression’ means despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights.” (Id., subd. (c)(2).) Fraud is “intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.” (Id., subd. (c)(3).)

            Here, the complaint alleges a knowing failure to abate uninhabitable conditions despite repeated notice. This sufficiently establishes “willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others” or “subject[ing] a person to cruel and unjust hardship.” (See Civ. Code, § 3294(c).) Therefore, the complaint properly prays for punitive damages.

           

 

b. Attorney’s Fees

“Except as attorney’s fees are specifically provided for by statute, the measure and mode of compensation of attorneys and counselors at law is left to the agreement, express or implied, of the parties . . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1021.) Thus, “[a]ttorney fees are not recoverable unless a fee award is expressly authorized by either statute or the parties’ contract.” (Ilshin Investment Co., Ltd. v. Buena Vista Home Entertainment, Inc. (2011) 195 Cal.App.4th 612, 627.)

Plaintiff alleges that he is entitled to attorney’s fees under the terms of the lease. (Compl. ¶ 52.) This is sufficient for pleading purposes. Whether and to what extent the alleged lease provides for attorney’s fees is a question of fact.

CONCLUSION

            Defendants’ demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to breach of contract and OVERRULED in all other respects. The motion to strike is DENIED.

 


Case Number: 26STCV08045    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: 406

 

JUAN ANTONIO MORAN,  

 

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

FCA US LLC; et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  26STCV08045

 

  Hearing Date:  July 15, 2026  

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

defendant’s demurrer to complaint and motion to strike

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On April 16, 2026, December 22, 2021, Plaintiff filed this “lemon law” action against Defendant FCA US LLC (FCA). Plaintiff’s operative Complaint asserts causes of action for violations of the Song-Beverly Act and fraudulent concealment.

            On June 17, 2025, Defendant filed the instant demurrer to the First Amended Complaint (FAC). Plaintiff filed opposition on July 1, 2026.  Defendant filed a reply on July 8, 2026.   

LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. (Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228.) In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or by proper judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a).) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (Ibid.) The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action. (Hahn, supra, 147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)

MEET AND CONFER

Before filing a demurrer or a motion to strike, the demurring or moving party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to or the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.41, 435.5.) The Court finds that Defendant has satisfied the meet and confer requirement.

DISCUSSION

I.                Fraudulent Concealment

Economic loss consists of “damages for inadequate value, costs of repair and replacement of the defective product or consequent loss of profits—without any claim of personal injury or damages to other property.” (Robinson Helicopter Co., Inc. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979, 988.) The economic loss rule requires a purchaser to recover in contract for purely economic loss due to disappointed expectations, unless the purchaser “can demonstrate harm above and beyond a broken contractual promise,” such as some form of personal injury or damage to property other than the defective product itself. (Ibid.) The economic loss rule exists to prevent “the law of contract and the law of tort from dissolving one into the other.” (Ibid.) “An omission to perform a contract obligation is never a tort, unless that omission is also an omission of a legal duty.” (Erlich v. Menezes (1999) 21 Cal.4th 543, 551.)

            “A plaintiff may assert a fraudulent concealment cause of action based on conduct occurring in the course of a contractual relationship if the elements of the claim can be established independently of the parties' contractual rights and obligations, and the tortious conduct exposes the plaintiff to a risk of harm beyond the reasonable contemplation of the parties when they entered into the contract.” (Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 13.)

In this case, Plaintiff has not alleged that he suffered any physical injury or that there was any damage to property other than to the subject vehicle. Plaintiff has not alleged a breach of duty beyond contractual obligations. The fraud claim is premised on Defendant’s failure to provide a vehicle free of defects, which is a breach of warranty. Plaintiff has alleged only economic loss. Therefore, the fraudulent concealment claim is barred by the economic loss rule.

Moreover, a duty to disclose only arises in “direct dealings between the plaintiff and the defendant; it cannot arise between the defendant and the public at large.” (Bjoin v. J-M Manufacturing Co., Inc. (2025) 113 Cal.App.5th 884, 902.) Here, there were no direct dealings between Plaintiff and FCA. Plaintiff purchased the vehicle from a dealership. “[Plaintiff] did not transact with [FCA] in any way.” (Id. at p. 903.) The fraudulent concealment claim fails for this independent reason.  

CONCLUSION

            Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to the fraud cause of action.
