Smith v. ESL Technologies, Inc., et al.

Case Number: 22STCV17408

TENTATIVE RULING

 

The Court hereby GRANTS final approval and awards the following: (1) $138,866.67 (1/3) for attorney fees to Class Counsel, Moon Law Group, PC; (2) $15,330.27 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) an enhancement payment of $7,500.00 to the class representative, Vivian Grace Smith; (4) $31,245.00 (75% of $41,660 PAGA penalty) to the LWDA; and (5) $12,000 for settlement administration costs to Phoenix Class Action Administration Solutions.

 

BACKGROUND

This is a wage and hour class action. Defendant ESL Technologies, Inc. is in the business of maintaining and repairing computers and computer peripheral devices.

On May 26, 2022, Vivian Grace Smith (“Plaintiff” or “Smith”) commenced this Action by filing a Complaint alleging causes of action against Defendants ESL Technologies, Inc. (“ESL”), Stay Safe Staffing, Inc. (“Stay Safe”), Valutech Outsourcing, Inc. (“Valutech”), Teleplan Holding USA, Inc. (“Teleplan Holding”), Teleplan Service Logistics, Inc. (“Teleplan Logistics”), and Does 1 through 10 (collectively, “Defendants”) for: failure to pay minimum wages, failure to pay overtime compensation, failure to provide meal periods, failure to authorize and provide rest breaks, failure to indemnify for necessary business expenses, failure to timely pay final wages at termination, failure to provide accurate itemized wage statements, and Unfair Business Practices.

On August 15, 2022, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleging an additional cause of action for violation of the Private Attorneys General Act (“PAGA”). 

On December 8, 2022, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Application to Dismiss All Class and Individual Claims against Defendants Valutech, Teleplan Holding, and Teleplan Service Logistics and dismissed these Defendants from the FAC without prejudice.

Counsel represents that the parties conducted informal and formal discovery and investigation, including the exchange of data and discoverable information in preparation for the mediation session.  The data was drawn from the entire Class and included: 1) time clock, pay and other data pertaining to Plaintiff and the Class during the relevant Settlement Period, including but not limited to the numbers of former and current members of the Class, average workweeks and pay periods, and average rate of hourly pay and 2) data pertaining to Plaintiff and the Class during the relevant Class Period, including but not limited to documents reflecting wage and hour policies and practices during the Class Period and information regarding the total number of current and former employees in the Class.  After reviewing this information, Class Counsel was able to evaluate the probability of class certification, success on the merits, and the reasonably obtainable maximum monetary exposure for all claims, prepare a damage analysis, and investigate applicable law regarding claims and defenses asserted in litigation.  

On December 19, 2024, the Parties participated in an all-day mediation with Marc Feder, Esq., which ultimately led to a proposed settlement.  On December 15, 2025, counsel filed a long-form settlement agreement executed by Plaintiff and Defendant ESL, attached as Exhibit 1 to the Declaration of Scott Leviant (“Leviant Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval.

On February 13, 2026, the Court granted preliminary approval contingent on counsel making certain revisions. In response, on March 13, 2026, counsel filed a fully executed Amended Settlement Agreement attached to the Supplemental Declaration of Scott Leviant (“Leviant Supp. Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval as Exhibit 3.

On March 20, 2026 the Court entered a Preliminary Approval Order without conditions. Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered. (See Declaration of Lluvia Islas (“Islas Decl.”).)  

Now before the Court is the motion for final approval of the settlement agreement.

C.      Attorney Fees and Costs

Class Counsel request $138,866.67 (33%) in fees and litigation costs and expenses in the amount of $15,330.27 to Class Counsel. (Moon Decl. ISO Final Approval, ¶¶64, 72.) The Settlement provides for attorney's fees up to $138,866.67 (33%) and costs of $18,000 (Settlement Agreement, ¶3.2.2.); the class was provided notice of the requested awards and none objected. (Islas Decl., ¶10 and Exhibit A thereto.)

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba at 254.)  Here, class counsel requests attorney fees using the percentage method. (Motion ISO Final, pgs. 16-21.)

The fee request represents 33% of the GSA, which is the average generally awarded in class actions.  See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 (“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”).

D.     Incentive Award to Class Representative

The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $10,000 for a Service Payment to the Named Plaintiff. (Settlement Agreement, ¶3.2.1.)

 Plaintiff Smith represents that her contributions to this litigation include, but are not limited to spending over 40 hours on the following: obtaining counsel, gathering documents, reviewing documents, answering counsel’s questions, identifying witnesses, preparing for and assisting with mediation, and reviewing the settlement. (Declaration of Plaintiff Smith, ¶¶6-7, 15-20.)

The Court notes that such efforts are commendable, yet not expectational. Based on the above, the recommendation is to award an enhancement award in the amount of $7,500.

 

E.      Claims Administration Costs

The claims administrator requests $12,000 for the costs of administering the settlement. (Islas Decl., ¶20.) This is less than the $30,000 maximum amount disclosed in the notice to class members, to which there were no objections. (Islas Decl., ¶10 and Exhibit A thereto.)

Based on all the work performed by the Claims Administrator, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $12,000.

Case Number: BC576604    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 1

TENTATIVE RULING

1. Who falls within the scope of tenants identified in the class definition? Are children and other occupants not named on the lease or having paid rent or otherwise acted as a tenant under the legal definition members of the class?

 

The parties agree that a class member must be a tenant or lessee who signed a written lease establishing a HOME tenancy.  Distribution of funds, if any, among multiple lessees will be addressed at a later stage in the case.

 

2. What specific kinds of monetary relief and/or other equitable relief are being sought by Plaintiffs? Are the kinds of monetary relief sought properly classified as restitution or restitutionary disgorgement of profits, or are they damages or other relief not available under the Unfair Competition Law, Business & Professions Code § 17200, et seq.?

 

The loss recognized by the Court of Appeal does not constitute expectation damages arising from the breach of a contract.  The Court of Appeal found Plaintiffs suffered a property loss, which it described as the loss of Plaintiffs’ rights to exclusive possession, full use and enjoyment of the leased property and faced the immediate legal peril of being subject to eviction whey they were improperly served with a 3-day notice terminating their tenancies. (Campbell v. FPI Management, Inc. (2024) 98 Cal.App.5th 1151, 1163-1167.)  The Court of Appeal ruled Plaintiffs are entitled to seek “a restitution award based on the monetary equivalent of the loss of property rights they suffered as a result of FPI’s unlawful termination of their tenancies, and to disgorge profits that FPI earned from its unlawful management practices.”  (Id., at 1172.)  The Court of Appeal noted that questions about “what specific harms [Plaintiffs] suffered as a result of FPI’s actions, how such harms can be given a monetary value for purposes of establishing a restitution award, or how FPI profited from its unlawful management practices” remain to be “decided at a later stage of the case.” (Ibid.)

 

Based on these appellate rulings, the Court finds that FPI’s failure to provide the 30-day notice that is required under the HOME program gave rise to a loss of at least 27 days of protected tenancy that may be compensable by way of a restitution award.  This is a measure of the lost property interest, not expectation damages under the lease agreement.  While the proposed measure offered by Plaintiffs may offer a model for expectation damages in another context, the Court finds it provides a reasonable predicate for restitution here given that the property loss is the protection of a below-market rate leasehold.  The Court will hear argument about whether the lost property interest at issue extends to the entire lease term but is not inclined to extend the property lost to potential subsequent least terms.  Later leases are necessarily dependent on annual income recertifications, questions about the communities in which each Plaintiff seeks to reside, and a host of other factors that cannot be said to give rise to an established property right that existed at the time of the failed notice. 

 

With respect to the 27-day loss of tenancy and the potential loss associated with the premature termination of the full lease term, the Court grants Plaintiffs’ request for a schedule (1) allowing targeted discovery addressing whether  FPI’s management compensation, contract-retention interests, or employee incentive and evaluation criteria are tied to physical or economic occupancy, gross potential rent, rent collection, delinquency, vacancy duration, or turnover at the class properties; (2) directing the parties to meet and confer promptly after responses are served and, if necessary, request an informal discovery conference to resolve any disputes; and (3) providing for supplemental briefs, with supporting evidence, and a continued hearing on whether Plaintiffs  have a colorable theory of restitution based on a prima facie showing that that the monetary recovery sought as restitution reflects not only some value lost by Plaintiffs but also some amount acquired by FPI.  The Court will discuss the specific schedule at the September 14, 2026 hearing.
