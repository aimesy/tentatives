DEPARTMENT SED LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24NWCV02722    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: SED

ESTATE OF CHRISTOPHER FARIAS ORTIZ, ET AL. V. NEWPORT DIVERSIFIED, INC.

CASE NO.:  24NWCV02722

HEARING:  06/30/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Newport Diversified, Inc’s motion to compel Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set Two is GRANTED.

 

The Court GRANTS Defendant Newport Diversified’s request for sanctions in the reduced amount of $1,375.00 against Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz and her attorneys, jointly and severally.

 

Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz is ordered to submit verified responses, without objections, within 30 days of this order.

 

Sanctions are to be paid within 30 days of notice of this order.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Newport Diversified (Newport Diversified) moves for an order compelling Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz’s (Ortiz) response to Newport Diversified’s Request for Form Interrogatories, Set Two.

 

Newport Diversified also seeks monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,090.00.

 

Background

 

On July 31, 2025, Plaintiffs Estate of Christopher Farias Ortiz, by and through his successors in interest, Andrea Ortiz and Ramon Farias, Andrea Ortiz, and Ramon Farias, an incompetent adult, by and through his Guardian Ad Litem, Estrella Soto, (collectively, Plaintiffs), filed the operative Second Amended Complaint against Defendants Newport Diversified, Inc., MIB-Shield LLC, Rudy Zazueta, Security Guard Reynoso, and Does 1 through 10, inclusive, asserting six causes of action: (1) Wrongful Death; (2) Negligence; (3) Negligent Hiring, Training, Retention & Supervision; (4) Assault & Battery; (5) Violation of Civil Code Section 52.1 (Tom Bane Civil Rights Act); and (6) Premise Liability.

 

On May 26, 2026, Newport Diversified filed the instant motion to compel.

 

On June 23, 2026, Newport Diversified filed a notice of non-opposition to its motion to compel.  

 

Ortiz filed an opposition on June 24, 2026.  Ortiz’s opposition was due on or before June 22, 2026.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b) [“All papers opposing a motion so noticed shall be filed with the court and a copy served on each party at least nine court days”].)  Accordingly, the opposition is untimely and the Court declines to consider it in its tentative ruling.

 

On June 26, 2026, Newport Diversified filed a reply.

 

Legal Standard

 

Interrogatories

 

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses and for a monetary sanction.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).)  The statute contains no time limit for a motion to compel where no responses or no verified responses have been served.  All that need be shown in the moving papers is that a set of interrogatories was properly served on the opposing party, that the time to respond has expired, and that no response of any kind has been served.  (See¿Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)

 

Sanctions

 

If the motion to compel is granted and the moving party properly asks for monetary sanctions, the court “shall” order the party to whom the discovery was directed to pay the propounding party's reasonable expenses, including attorney fees, in enforcing discovery “unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (a).) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Meeting and conferring is not required for a motion to compel initial responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411.)  However, the Court notes that Newport Diversified attempted to meet and confer with Ortiz prior to bringing this motion.  (Reid Declaration, ¶¶ 34-40.) 

 

Analysis

 

On February 24, 2026, Newport Diversified served Ortiz with Form Interrogatories—General, Set Two.  (Reid Decl., ¶¶ 27-28, Ex. J.)  Ortiz's responses were initially due on March 20, 2026.  (Id. at ¶ 29.)  On that date, Ortiz requested a three-week extension.  (Ibid.)  Newport Diversified agreed to a one-week extension, making the new response deadline April 3, 2026.  (Id. at ¶ 31.)

 

On April 2, 2026, Newport Diversified received unverified and otherwise non-code-compliant responses.  (Id. at ¶¶ 32-33, Ex. N.)  On April 23, 2026, Newport Diversified sent a meet-and-confer letter identifying the deficiencies and requested that Ortiz advise by May 1, 2026, whether verified, code-compliant supplemental responses would be served; otherwise, Newport Diversified would proceed with filing the instant motion.  (Id. at ¶¶ 34-35, Exs. O, P.)  On April 25, 2026, Ortiz represented that supplemental responses would be served by May 8, 2026.  (Id. at ¶¶ 37-38, Ex. Q.)  No supplemental responses were received.  (Id. at ¶ 39.)  On May 12, 2026, Ortiz again represented that supplemental responses would be served no later than May 15, 2026.  (Id. at ¶¶ 40-41, Ex. R.)  As of the filing of this motion, Ortiz had not served verified responses.  (Id. at ¶ 42.)

 

Newport Diversified has demonstrated that it propounded Form Interrogatories—General, Set Two, and that Ortiz has failed to serve verified responses.  An unverified interrogatory response is ineffective and is the equivalent of no response at all.  (Appleton v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 632, 636.)  Therefore, Ortiz's unverified responses do not satisfy its obligations under the Discovery Act.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Newport Diversified’s Motion to Compel Ortiz’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set Two.

 

          Sanctions

 

Newport Diversified also requests monetary sanctions against Ortiz and its counsel in the amount of $2,090.00, based on 4.4 hours preparing this motion, 2 hours preparing a reply brief, and 1 hour appearing at the hearing, at an hourly rate of $275.  (Reid Decl., ¶ 58.) 

 

Here, as discussed above, the Court grants Newport Diversified's motion to compel; therefore, monetary sanctions are warranted.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).)  Although Ortiz's opposition was untimely and the Court declines to consider it, Newport Diversified nevertheless prepared and filed a reply addressing the opposition before learning the Court would not consider it.  Accordingly, the Court finds it reasonable to award compensation for preparing a reply.  However, the Court finds that 4.4 hours to prepare this straightforward motion is excessive and reduces that time to 3.0 hours.  The Court further finds that 2.0 hours to prepare the reply is excessive under the circumstances and reduces that time to 1.0 hour.  Thus, the Court finds that 5.0 hours of attorney time for preparing the motion, preparing the reply, and appearing at the hearing, at an hourly rate of $275.00, is reasonable.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS monetary sanctions in the reduced amount of $1,375.00.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Newport Diversified, Inc’s motion to compel Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set Two is GRANTED.

 

The Court GRANTS Defendant Newport Diversified’s request for sanctions in the reduced amount of $1,375.00 against Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz and her attorneys, jointly and severally.

 

Plaintiff Andrea Ortiz is ordered to submit verified responses, without objections, within 30 days of this order.

 

Sanctions are to be paid within 30 days of notice of this order.


Case Number: 25NWCV01273     Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: SED

BLAKE A. COSTA V. HYUNDAI MOTOR AMERICA, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV01273

HEARING:  07/06/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#12

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants Hyundai Motor America and Genesis Motor America LLC’s motion to set aside defaults is DENIED.

 

Moving parties to give notice.

 

Defendants Hyundai Motor America and Genesis Motor America LLC (collectively, Defendants) move for an order to set aside the defaults against them on June 10, 2025.

 

Background

 

On April 10, 2025, Plaintiff Blake A. Costa (Plaintiff) filed the operative complaint against Defendants and Does 1 through 20, inclusive, asserting two causes of action: (1)-(2) Breach of Implied Warranty of Merchantability and Express Warranty under the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

On April 11, 2025, Plaintiff served Defendants by personal service.

 

On June 10, 2025, Plaintiff requested entry for default.  The clerk entered the default on the same day.

 

On May 14, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion.

 

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

No reply has been filed.

 

Analysis

 

          Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b)

 

The court has broad discretion to vacate the entry of default, default judgment, or a dismissal where the moving party timely establishes a proper ground for relief.  (Cruz v.¿Fagor¿America, Inc.¿(2007) 146 Cal.App.4th 488, 495.)¿  Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) contains two distinct provisions for relief from default.¿  The first provision is discretionary and broad in scope, providing: “The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.¿  (Code Civ. Proc., §¿473, subd. (b).)  Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.”  (Ibid.)

 

The general underlying purpose of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) is to promote the determination of actions on their merits.  (Even Zohar Const. & Remodeling, Inc. v. Bellaire Townhouses, LLC¿(2015) 61 Cal.4th 830, 838-839.) ¿

 

Here, Defendants seek relief under the discretionary provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), on the grounds of mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect by a party.  (Code of Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

 

Relief under the discretionary provision of section 473, subdivision (b), however, must be sought "within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months" after entry of the default.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

The Clerk entered Defendants' defaults on June 10, 2025.  Defendants did not file the instant motion until May 14, 2026, nearly eleven months after entry of default.  Accordingly, the motion is untimely under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), and the Court lacks authority to grant discretionary relief under that statute.

 

Accordingly, Defendants are not entitled to relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). 

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendants Hyundai Motor America and Genesis Motor America LLC’s motion to set aside defaults is DENIED.
