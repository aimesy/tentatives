DEPARTMENT 735 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV04929    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 735

Bond. v. Stepping Stones Healthcare Services, LLC, et al.

Defendant ABC Unified School District’s Demurrer to Complaint and Motion to Strike

 

BACKGROUND 

On February 20, 2025, Plaintiff Rebecca Bond filed a complaint against Defendants Stepping Services, LLC, The Stepping Stones Autism Services Group, LLC, The Stepping Stones Group, LLC and ABC Unified School District, asserting causes of action for (1) age discrimination; (2) harassment based on age; (3) disability discrimination; (4) harassment based on disability; (5) failure to provide accommodation; (6) failure to engage in the interactive process; (7) failure to prevent discrimination and harassment; (8) whistleblower retaliation; (9) retaliation in violation of Labor Code § 6310; and (10) wrongful termination.

Plaintiff alleges she was discriminated against based on her age and disablity and terminated in retaliation for complaints about working conditions and for filing a claim for industrial injuries.

DISCUSSION

Demurrer

Defendant ABC Unified School District demurs to the complaint on the ground that it was not Plaintiff’s employer. Defendant asserts that the complaint shows Plaintiff was employed by Defendant Stepping Stones and was assigned to work at one of the school district’s school sites through this company.

The complaint alleges that “[a]ll defendants employed or co-employed Plaintiff directly or indirectly.” The Court must treat the allegations in the complaint as true on demurrer. Accordingly, Plaintiff has sufficiently alleged that moving Defendant was her employer. The Court overrules Defendant’s demurrer.

Motion to Strike

Defendant moves to strike the claims for punitive damages on the ground that Plaintiff cannot recover punitive damages from a public entity Defendant. Government Code section 818 provides, “notwithstanding any other provision of law, a public entity is not liable for damages awarded under section 3294 of the Civil Code or other damages imposed primarily for the sake of example and by way of punishing the defendant.” In opposition, Plaintiff concedes that punitive damages cannot be stated against a public entity and notes that the complaint limits the request for punitive damages to “nongovernment entity defendants.”

As Plaintiff’s complaint already states that it is seeking punitive damages against only nongovernment entity defendants the Court need not strike the claims for punitive damages. The Court denies the motion to strike.

CONCLUSION 

The Court overrules Defendant’s demurrer.

The Court denies Defendant’s motion to strike.

Defendant shall file and serve its answer within ten days.

Defendant shall give notice.


Case Number: 26STCP01163    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 735

Schild v. Floyd, et al.

Plaintiff’s Motion for Preliminary Injunction

 

BACKGROUND 

On March 23, 2026, Plaintiff Walter Schild filed a complaint against Defendants David Ryan Floyd and RW2, LLC, asserting causes of action for (1) breach of fiduciary duty; (2) constructive fraud; (3) fraud and concealment; (4) breach of contract; (5) breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing; (6) accounting; (7) conversion; (8) money had and received; (9) declaratory relief; and (10) UCL violations.

Plaintiff, an assignee of entity Dilson & Walter, LLC (“D&W”), alleges D&W is a 40% shareholder and Defendant Floyd is a 60% shareholder of Defendant RW2, LLC. RW2 operates a restaurant and bar called 33 Taps Silverlake. Plaintiff alleges that Floyd wrongfully removed D&W as a 40% shareholder on tax documents beginning in 2023 and failed to pay D&W distributions thereafter. Plaintiff also alleges that Floyd has made distributions of funds from RW2 for personal use, wrongfully paid himself and entities he controls, and engaged in misconduct relating to loans.

On May 13, 2026, Floyd filed a cross-complaint against Dilson & Walter, LLC, Walter Schild, 33 Taps, LLC, Meco Properties, LLC, Culinary Lab Partners, LLC and Soprano Hollywood, LLC, asserting causes of action for (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of fiduciary duty; (3) aiding and abetting breach of fiduciary duty; (4) conversion; (5) theft; (6) quantum meruit; (7) constructive trust; and (8) accounting.

LEGAL STANDARD

In deciding whether to issue a preliminary injunction, the court looks to two factors: (1) the likelihood that the petitioner will prevail on the merits, and (2) the relative balance of harms that is likely to result from the granting or denial of interim injunctive relief.  The factors are interrelated, with a greater showing on one permitting a lesser showing on the other.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves for a preliminary injunction restraining Floyd and RW2 from making insider distributions, unauthorized member loans, non-distribution payments (including guaranteed payments and member tax payments), and non-arm’s-length related-party transfers pending trial, while expressly preserving RW2’s ordinary business operations. The injunction also seeks to declare D&W a 40% member of RW2. Plaintiff argues preliminary injunctive relief is necessary to preserve the status quo of RW2’s business operations. Plaintiff asserts monetary damages are an inadequate remedy for the ongoing harm Floyd is inflicting on D&W’s interest in RW2.

In opposition, Defendant Floyd argues that this preliminary injunction would essentially require 33 Taps to cease operations. Defendant asserts that the restriction on transfers of funds “to any member or manager, including Floyd, or to any entity owned, controlled, or managed by any member or manager of RW2, other than arm’s-length payments in the ordinary course of RW2’s own business for goods or services actually received by and for the benefit of RW2” will prevent the restaurant from paying its administrative services company, Eat Drink Hospitality, LLC (“EDH”). Defendant Floyd is a member of EDH and EDH manages RW2’s operations by staffing the restaurant; ordering food, liquor and supplies; restaurant advertising; supervising RW2 day-to-day expenditures; managing the restaurant’s operating account; maintaining RW2’s financial books and records; managing the restaurant’s compliance with applicable health and safety; and procuring and maintaining RW2’s licenses and permits.

Defendant also argues that the preliminary injunction would function as an adjudication of disputed facts in this action and give Plaintiff control over RW2’s operations. The injunction seeks to prevent Defendant from altering, modifying or making “any internal company record, tax filing, or ownership-related filing that is inconsistent with RW2’s 2016–2022 treatment of D&W as a 40% member, pending final adjudication of this action or further order of the Court.” Defendant disputes that D&W is a member of RW2 and treating it as a member would give it power of RW2’s operations.

Lastly, Defendant asserts Plaintiff has not met his evidentiary burden to show that D&W is a member of RW2 or that Plaintiff has received an assignment of rights from D&W because Plaintiff does not properly authenticate RW2’s bank records, tax records or the assignment. (All other evidentiary objections are preserved.)

The Court declines to issue a preliminary injunction. The proposed injunction would require a preliminary adjudication of the main issue of this action (D&W’s membership interest) and could potentially halt operations of the restaurant by interfering with distributions to its management services company. Plaintiff asserts that the restriction on distributions to Floyd-related entities would not preclude Floyd from making distributions to EDH that are made in the ordinary course of business while at the same time arguing that the historical payments to EDH have been inflated and wrongful. Placing restrictions on distributions of funds will alter the status quo, not maintain it. Similarly, Plaintiff concedes that RW2 has not listed D&W as a member on tax and bank documents since 2022 but requests the Court declare D&W a member. This again would alter the status quo, not maintain it. Lastly, the alleged injuries suffered are financial and capable of redress via monetary damages.

CONCLUSION 

The Court denies Plaintiff’s motion for preliminary injunction.

Plaintiff shall give notice.
