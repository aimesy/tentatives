A settlement was reached pursuant to which Defendant agreed to pay a total of $1,500,000.00.  (Decl. of Shakhnis ¶4, Exh. II.                Legal Standard

Compromises of disputed claims brought by minors are governed in part by C.C.P. §372.  The statute allows a guardian ad litem to appear in court on behalf of a minor claimant and gives the guardian ad litem the power to compromise the minor’s claim “with the approval of the court in which the action or proceeding is pending.”  A petition for court approval of a compromise must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise or covenant.  (CRC Rule 7.950.)  CRC Rule 7.952(a) requires the attendance of the petitioner and claimant at the hearing on the compromise of the claim unless the court for good causes dispenses with their personal appearance.

“Neither section 372 nor the California Rules of Court (rules 7.950 & 7.952) contemplates a noticed motion and adversary hearing when court approval of a minor’s compromise is sought. Although we need not decide the question, it would appear that a petition to approve or disapprove a minor’s compromise may be decided by the superior court, ex parte, in chambers.”  (Pearson v. Superior Court (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1333, 1337.) 

CRC Rule 7.955(a) requires the Court to use “a reasonable fee standard” when approving and allowing the amount of attorneys’ fees payable from money to be paid for the benefit of a person with a disability and requires that the Court “give consideration to the terms of any representation agreement made between the attorney and the representative of the minor . . . and evaluate the agreement based on the facts and circumstances existing at the time the agreement was made, except where the attorney and the representative of the minor . . . contemplated that the attorney’s fee would be affected by later events.” 

CRC Rule 7.955(b) sets forth fourteen nonexclusive factors the Court may consider in determining a reasonable attorney’s fee.  CRC Rule 7.955(c) requires that a petition requesting Court approval and allowance of an attorney’s fee under 7.955(a) must include a declaration from the attorney that addresses the factors listed in 7.955(b) that are applicable to the matter before the Court.

 

III.              Analysis

Petitioners C. Sanchez, M. Macias, S. Guerra, and C. Gonzalez seek court approval for a settlement under which minor claimants would each receive $45,000.00-$50,000.00.  The amounts reflect payment amounts to each minor claimant after deduction of attorneys’ fees representing 35% of each minor claimant’s settlement and after deduction of costs in amounts specific to each minor claimant.  (See Petitions ¶16(f).) 

This is a habitability suit, in which minor claimants lived in uninhabitable conditions, including but not limited to living with cockroach and rodent infestations, property with widespread water leaks which led to chronic mold growth, and physically defective units, which caused injuries including headaches, earaches, stuffy and runny nose, itchy/watery eyes, and shortness of breath.  (Petitions ¶¶5-6; Complaint ¶13.)  The minor claimants have recovered completely from these injuries with no permanent injuries.  (Petitions ¶8.) 

Petitioners’ counsel, Philip Shakhnis, declares that the attorney fees requested are based on a contingency basis and attorneys’ fees are contingent on a settlement or judgment.  (Decl. of Shakhnis ¶13.)  Shakhnis declares in the course of this litigation, his firm conducted numerous client meetings, site inspections, preparing for depositions, mediation sessions, and made court appearances.  (Decl. of Shakhnis ¶28.)  Shakhnis declares his firm propounded and responded to written discovery relative to each plaintiff client including the minor claimants, and his firm propounded written discovery on the defendants on behalf of his clients.  (Decl. of Shakhnis ¶28.)  Shakhnis declares his firm spent over 300 hours in this case since early 2024.  (Decl. of Shakhnis ¶28.)

Petitions include a copy of the retainer agreements between Petitioners and their counsel that are included with English translations.  (Decl. of Shakhnis ¶4, Exh. E.)  The retainer agreement identifies the names of the individuals who signed the submitted retainer agreements on behalf of the minor clients. As such, the submitted copies of the written attorney fee arrangement agreements with minor claimants are sufficient and support the attorneys’ fees request.

Petitioners request this Court order the disposition of the minor claimants’ balance of the settlements be invested in a single-premium deferred annuity, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the Court.  (Petitions ¶18(b)(3).)  Petitioners select an annuity from Pacific Life Insurance Company, and the check to fund the minors’ annuities are payable to Pacific Life & Annuity Services, Inc.  (Petitions at Attachs. 18(b)(3).)

Given the age of the minor claimants, the Court will only require attendance at the hearing by the adult Petitioners to grant the petition.  (CRC Rule 7.952.)

 

IV.            Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the petitions to approve the minors’ compromise are granted for Leonardo, Johnny, Hailey, Jonathan Daniel, Jordan, Damian, and Ashley.  The Court orders the funds invested in a single-premium deferred annuity, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the Court.
