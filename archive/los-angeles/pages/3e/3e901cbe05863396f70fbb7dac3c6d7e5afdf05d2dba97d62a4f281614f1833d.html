RULING: The motion is granted.    

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & RELEVANT PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of a dog attack that occurred on 4/25/24 and ensuing confrontations and/or altercations between Plaintiff/former Cross-Defendant Ralph Sasson (Plaintiff) and Defendants Quentin (Quentin) Collins, Bernard Collins (Bernard) and Armene Voskanyan (Armene) (collectively, Defendants) occurring on 4/25/24, 4/26/24, 4/27/24 and 7/10/24.

 

On 9/6/24, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendants for: (1) Strict Liability (Bernard & Armene), (2) Negligence Per Se (Bernard & Armene), (3) Negligence (Bernard & Armene), (4) Trespass to Chattels (Bernard & Armene), (5) Negligent Supervision (Bernard & Armene), (6) Assault I (Quentin), (7) Assault II (Quentin), (8) Battery (Quentin), (9) Trespass to Land (Quentin), (10) Assault (Bernard), (11) Battery (Bernard), (12) Trespass to Land I (Bernard), (13) Trespass to Land II (Bernard), (14) Slander Per Se (Bernard) and (15) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress (Bernard & Quentin). 

 

On 10/25/24, Defendants, representing themselves, answered the complaint.  On 2/13/25, represented by counsel, each Defendant filed an Amended Answer to the complaint.   

 

On 3/27/26, Plaintiff filed and served the instant motion for summary adjudication against Quentin and Bernard as to the following issues:

 

Issue No. 1: Defendants’ Third Affirmative Defense for apportionment has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 2: Defendants’ Fourth Affirmative Defense for assumption of risk has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 3: Defendants’ Sixth Affirmative Defense for comparative fault has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 4: Defendants’ Eighth Affirmative Defense for intervening and superseding cause has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 5: Defendants’ Ninth Affirmative Defense for justification and/or excuse has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 6: Defendants’ Tenth Affirmative Defense relating to lack of malice and/or punitive damages has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 7: Defendants’ Thirteenth Affirmative Defense relating to proximate cause, including cause of Plaintiff and/or cause of others, has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 8: Defendants’ Fourteenth Affirmative Defense relating to proximate cause of the Plaintiff has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 9: Defendants’ Fifteenth Affirmative Defense relating to lack of malice and/or punitive damages has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 10: Defendants’ Seventeenth Affirmative Defense for unclean hands has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 11: Bernard’s Eighteenth Affirmative Defense for immunity has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 12: Bernard’s Nineteenth Affirmative Defense for truth has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 13: Bernard’s Twentieth Affirmative Defense for privilege has no merit and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that defense.

 

Issue No. 14: Bernard owed Plaintiff a duty of care, as a matter of law, with respect to Plaintiff’s causes of action for negligence per se, negligence, and negligent supervision, and there is no triable issue of material fact as to that issue.

 

On 5/26/26, Plaintiff filed and served a Notice of Errata Re: Plaintiff’s Separate Statement in support of the motion and a corrected Separate Statement.

 

No opposition to the motion has been filed. 

 

On 9/8/26, the Court denied Defendants’ ex parte application (filed on 9/3/26) to continue the hearing date on Plaintiff’s motion for summary adjudication and conform the briefing schedule accordingly on the ground that it was untimely pursuant to CCP 437c(h).  (See 9/8/26 Minute Order).

 

ANALYSIS

 

CCP 437c(f)(1) provides:

 

“A party may move for summary adjudication as to one or more causes of action within an action, one or more affirmative defenses, one or more claims for damages, or one or more issues of duty, if the party contends that the cause of action has no merit, that there is no affirmative defense to the cause of action, that there is no merit to an affirmative defense as to any cause of action, that there is no merit to a claim for damages, as specified in Section 3294 of the Civil Code, or that one or more defendants either owed or did not owe a duty to the plaintiff or plaintiffs. A motion for summary adjudication shall be granted only if it completely disposes of a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for damages, or an issue of duty.” (emphasis added).

 

9th Affirmative Defense: Justification/Excuse

 

The doctrine of collateral estoppel/issue preclusion precludes the relitigation of issues argued and decided in a prior proceeding.  See Hernandez (2009) 46 C4th 501, 511.  In order for the doctrine  to apply, the following requirements must be met: (1) the issue sought to be precluded from relitigation must be identical to that decided in a former proceeding; (2) this issue must have been actually litigated in the former proceeding; (3) this issue must have been necessarily decided in the former proceeding;(4) the decision in the former proceeding must be final and on the merits; and (5) the party against whom preclusion is sought must be the same as, or in privity with, the party to the former proceeding.  Id.; Lucido (1990) 51 C3d 335, 341.

 

Issue preclusion depends on what issues were adjudicated, not the label of the proceeding or form of relief; therefore, findings in an equitable action such as a Civil Harassment Restraining Order (CHRO) proceeding may preclude relitigation in a later civil action for damages.  See Lumpkin (1996) 49 CA4th 1223, 1231; Bennett (2019) 35 CA5th 908, 919.

 

Here, the issues in the proceeding regarding Plaintiff’s and Defendants’ competing CHRO petitions are identical as they address identical factual allegations.  See Hernandez, supra at 511-512.  Bernard’s CHRO petition against Plaintiff alleged that on April 27, Plaintiff “spit on” Quentin and was “threatening [Quentin] with physical violence.”  (Separate Statement (SS) 67).  Quentin also claimed Plaintiff spat on him and “swung first.”  (SS 70-71).  In Plaintiff’s CHRO petitions,  Plaintiff alleged that Quentin stormed across his property, trespassed onto Plaintiff’s lawn, and punched him first, after which Bernard joined the beating. (SS 65-66). 

 

The issues of whether Plaintiff was the initial aggressor, whether Plaintiff spat on Quentin, whether Plaintiff threatened physical violence, whether Quention and Bernard were responding defensively, whether the encounter was mutual, and whether Bernard and Quentin were lawfully on Plaintiff’s property which underlie Quentin’s and Bernard’s defense of justification/excuse are the same issues which were decided against them in the trial on the CHRO petitions. 

 

The CHRO court found Bernard came onto Plaintiff’s property without permission and stayed after being told to leave.  (SS 10-11, 15-25, 75).  The CHRO court also found that Quentin made threats of violence on April 25.  (SS 12-18, 20, 76).  Additionally, the CHRO court found that on April 27 Plaintiff’s alleged words were “not threats but hurtful words,” while Quentin was the one “making threats that he’s going to beat Mr. Sasson,” and that Quentin’s recorded run across two houses showed he was “actually ready to fight.”  (SS 26-50, 77-78).  The Appellate Court confirmed the same underlying facts (e.g. that Plaintiff committed no unlawful violence or made a threat of violence, and footage showing Plaintiff yelling from his property while Defendants responded with threats and Quentin crossed onto Plaintiff’s front lawn).  (SS 96-97, 100-104). 

 

The foregoing issues were actually litigated by the CHRO court.  The evidence shows that Bernard’s CHRO petition alleged Plaintiff spit and physical threats by Plaintiff.  (SS 67).   Quentin testified to Plaintiff spitting and Plaintiff allegedly swinging first.  (SS 70-71).   Bernard testified to Plaintiff spitting.  (SS 69).  Plaintiff pleaded and testified the opposite: threats, trespass, and first punch by Quentin, joined by Bernard.  (SS 65-66).  The video was introduced into evidence and argued.  (SS 26-29, 73).  The CHRO court heard and saw all of it. The spitting issue, the aggressor issue, the threat issue, the trespass issue, and the defensive force issue were at the center of the hearing/trial on the CHRO petitions.  Under California law, an issue may be “actually litigated” even where the prior determination reflects a failure of proof rather than an express finding saying “I reject X.”  See Younan (1996) 51 CA4th 401, 407.  Therefore, when the CHRO court heard the spitting allegation, the “swing first” allegation, and  found Quentin’s running onto Plaintiff’s property was a violent attack and Bernard’s entry onto Plaintiff’s property was unauthorized harassment, while finding Plaintiff did not engage in any unlawful threats or violence, the issues were decided against Quentin and Bernard and in favor of Plaintiff.  (SS 69-80, 99-104).

 

The foregoing issues were necessarily decided in the proceeding on the CHRO petitions; otherwise, a CHRO could not have been issued against Bernard and Quentin.  (See 10-11, 15-50, 53-54, 67-86, 89-90, 94-95, 100-102).

 

The decision of the CHRO court is final as Defendants never appealed the orders.  (SS 98).  Additionally, Plaintiff’s request for appellate review of the CHRO imposed against him resulted in a reversal.  (SS 64-80, 98-104). 

 

The trial of the CHRO petitions involved the same parties: Plaintiff, Quentin and Bernard.

 

4th Affirmative Defense: Assumption of Risk  

 

Assumption of risk only provides a defense for negligence actions and does not apply to actions based on intentional or reckless conduct.  Ordway (1988) 198 CA3d 98, 108.  Where the doctrine of assumption of risk does apply, it “requires knowledge and appreciation of the risk, and a voluntary choice to encounter it.”  Gallegos (1955) 137 CA2d 14, 19.  The unrefuted evidence establishes that Plaintiff had no knowledge that Bernard posted on the neighborhood What’s App page that he was letting his dogs run off leash on the night of the dog attack.  (SS 3, 8).  There is also no evidence to support a finding that Plaintiff assumed the risk of any conduct for which he claims injury and/or damages in this action.

 

3rd Affirmative Defense: Apportionment of Fault & 6th Affirmative Defense: Comparative Fault

 

A contributory negligence defense which bars all recovery by a Plaintiff found to be partially at fault no longer exists under California law.  Li (1975) 13 C3d 804, 829.

 

The elements of comparative fault are: (1) relative responsibility of various parties for an injury; and (2) equitable apportionment of loss in proportion to the parties’ comparative responsibilities. See Knight (1992) 3 C4th 296, 314.  However, the defense of apportionment of fault does not apply where the defendant has committed an intentional tort and the injured plaintiff was merely negligent.  See Heiner (2000) 84 CA4th 335, 350.  As noted above in relation to the 9th cause of action, the CHRO proceeding already decided the issues against Quentin and Bernard with regard to their intentional conduct. 

 

Even if comparative/apportionment of fault could apply, Quentin and Bernard would have to produce evidence of actual negligence on the part of Plaintiff that caused injury.  CACI 405.  Quentin and Bernard have failed to do so.  The unrefuted evidence does not establish negligence on the part of Plaintiff that contributed to his claimed injuries/damages in this case.   

 

8th Affirmative Defense: Intervening and Superseding Cause; 13th Affirmative Defense: Proximate Cause – Other Person & 14th Affirmative Defense: Proximate Cause – Plaintiff

 

Conduct cannot be considered as a substantial factor in causing harm if the same harm would have occurred without that conduct.  Yanez (2013) 221 CA4th 180, 187.  Here, the unrefuted evidence shows that Bernard’s conduct was the direct, substantial factor in causing the dog attack/trespass to chattels.  (SS 1-6).  The substantial factor causing harm was Bernard’s unleashed dogs, not Plaintiff standing on his own property.  Similarly, the unrefuted evidences establishes that Plaintiff did not cause the later threats, trespasses, and assault by Quentin and Bernard.  Defendants who refuse to leave private property and a defendant who refuses to control his son cannot blame the victim for objecting.  (SS 14-25, 31-47).  There is also no evidence of any superseding cause or other person that caused Plaintiff’s claimed injuries/damages in this case. 

 

17th Affirmative Defense: Unclean Hands

 

The doctrine of unclean hands “cannot be distorted into a proceeding to try the general morals of the parties.”  Fibreboard Paper Products Corp. (1964) 227 CA2d 675, 729.  Nor may equitable defenses be used to nullify claims grounded in legislatively declared public policy.  Salas (2014) 59 C4th 407, 432.  With regard to the dog attack claims, the alleged wrongdoing by Bernard occurred before any purported misconduct by Plaintiff and was wholly independent of it. Bernard admitted Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s dogs were lawfully on Plaintiff’s property, that Bernard’s dogs charged onto that property, and that he knew his dogs were “aggressive,” foresaw the danger, and still let them roam off leash.  (SS 1-6).  With regard to the garage confrontation and April 27 attack, Plaintiff’s conduct cannot be deemed inequitable as it has already been determined in the CHRO proceedings that Quentin and Bernard acted improperly and Plaintiff was lawfully on his property.  Additionally, Plaintiff’s wife has submitted a declaration indicating that she never invited Quentin or Bernard onto Plaintiff’s property.  (SS 14, 19-22).  Even if Plaintiff used profanity towards Quentin and/or Bernard, such conduct does not amount to the sort of inequitable conduct that bars relief.  As noted above, the CHRO court found Plaintiff never threatened anyone, and the Appellate Court emphasized there was no finding that Plaintiff committed unlawful violence or made threats of violence.  (SS 31, 77-79, 100-104).  As such, the evidence does not support a finding that unclean hands of Plaintiff bar recovery. 

 

10th Affirmative Defense: No Malicious Intent & 15th Affirmative Defense: Punitive Damages Barred

 

As noted above with regard to collateral estoppel/issue preclusion in relation to the 9th Affirmative Defense, Defendants are precluded from relitigating the issue that Plaintiff was purportedly the aggressor.  The evidence establishes that on April 25, Quentin threatened Plaintiff in the garage and on the driveway, while Bernard allowed him to remain on Plaintiff’s property even though Plaintiff’s wife did not consent to their presence, and Plaintiff repeatedly ordered them to leave and called 911.  (SS 10-25).  The evidence also establishes that on April 27, Quentin again threatened Plaintiff, Bernard joined in with his own threats, Quentin changed his shoes, sprinted onto Plaintiff’s lawn to attack, and Bernard admitted he believed Quentin was running to physically attack Plaintiff.  (SS 26-50, 94-95).  Bernard then joined the attack, came onto Plaintiff’s property, and choked Plaintiff, later admitting the chokehold could have killed him and expressly tying the “consequences” to Plaintiff’s speech while changing their spitting story in their own pleadings.  (SS 51-63, 85-86).  The foregoing unrefuted evidence does not support the defenses of no malicious intent or a finding that punitive damages are barred.   

 

As to Bernard Only - 18th Affirmative Defense: Statutory Immunity; 19th Affirmative Defense: Truth - As to Slander Claims & 20th Affirmative Defense: Privilege – As to Slander Claims

 

The defense of truth fails because Defendants’ spitting accusation was litigated and rejected as set forth above under the 9th cause of action regarding collateral estoppel/issue preclusion.  Additionally, Plaintiff alleged Bernard was falsely telling neighbors that Plaintiff spat on Quentin, and Bernard refused to deny those factual allegations in his verified answer. (SS 87-88).  A party cannot dodge a pleaded fact in a verified pleading and then later pretend the opposite is established truth.  Walker (1966) 240 CA2d 118, 120; Travelers Ins. Co. (1932) 123 Cal.App. 473, 482 (if a defendant does not deny a material allegation, the fact “is not in issue, and no evidence need be offered to prove [it].”); Hennefer (1986) 182 CA3d 492, 504 (“[t]otal failure to deny a material allegation necessarily results in an admission.”). 

 

The  defense of privilege fails because the litigation privilege does not extend to extrajudicial statements to outsiders who have no connection to the proceeding and whose involvement does nothing to achieve its objects.  Mallard (2010) 188 CA4th 531, 543.  Here, the alleged statements were made to neighbors, not to participants in litigation, and Bernard refused to deny that he made those false statements to them.  (SS 87-88).

 

The defense of immunity fails because the First Amendment does not protect defamatory falsehoods.  ZL Technologies, Inc. (2017) 13 CA5th 603, 611.  Therefore, once the purported  factual predicate for Bernard’s speech defense collapses, so does the immunity theory it is built on.  (SS 81-90, 100-104).

 

Bernard’s Duty to Plaintiff

 

With regard to the negligence and negligence per se causes of action, Bernard admitted he was walking his three large German Shepherds off leash through a residential neighborhood, knew they were “aggressive,” foresaw the risk of harm, and disregarded that risk anyway.  (SS 2, 4-6). On the other hand, Plaintiff was lawfully on his own front lawn with his two small dogs.  (SS 1, 4).  Based on the foregoing, Bernard clearly owed Plaintiff a duty to exercise ordinary care in controlling his dogs.  

 

Bernard also owed the duty imposed by Civil Code 3342, regarding dog bites and the liability of owners, because he admitted he owned the dogs that charged onto Plaintiff’s property and attacked.  (SS 4-5).   

 

With regard to negligent supervision, Bernard owed Plaintiff a duty to exercise reasonable care to control Quentin, Bernard’s minor child, once Quentin’s threatening conduct became apparent. The evidence establishes that on April 25, Quentin threatened Plaintiff in Bernard’s presence on Plaintiff’s property.  (SS 12-18].  Bernard admitted Quentin was a minor, Bernard was responsible for him, Quentin listens to him, and that Quentin has a penchant to be “triggered.” (SS 17-18, 39, 103).  Despite the foregoing, Bernard did not remove or restrain Quentin on April 25, and he admitted he did not tell Quentin to go inside or restrain him before Quentin sprinted onto Plaintiff’s lawn on April 27.  (SS 18, 23-24, 40-45). 

 

Based on the foregoing, Bernard owed Plaintiff a duty of care. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted. 

 

 

 


Case Number: 24CHCV03432    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 9/17/26                                                                TRIAL DATE: 5/3/27

Case #24CHCV03432

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES

(Form Interrogatories – Employment Law, Set 1)

 

Motion filed on 3/2/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Krystal Huber

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Saugus Union School District

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Defendant Saugus Union School District to serve further responses to Plaintiff Krystal Huber’s Form Interrogatories – Employment Law, Set 1, Nos. 204.5, 204.6, 209.2, 214.1 and 216.1 served on 4/3/25.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests an order imposing sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, McCune & Harber, and in favor of Salusky Law Group in the amount of $9,860.00.

 

RULING: The motion is granted as set forth below. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of the termination of Plaintiff Krystal Huber’s (Plaintiff) employment as a teacher with Saugus Union School District (Defendant).  On 9/23/24, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant for: (1) Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (2) Retaliation in Violation of FEHA; (3) Failure to Engage in an  Interactive Process; (4) Failure to Reasonably Accommodate; (5) Failure to Prevent Discrimination and Retaliation in Violation of FEHA and (6) Retaliation in Violation of Labor Code 6310.  On  1/21/25, Defendant answered the complaint. 

 

On 4/3/25, Plaintiff served Defendant with Form Interrogatories – Employment Law, Set 1.  (Aliazis Decl. ¶2, Ex.1).  After being granted an extension to respond, on 5/30/25, Defendant served unverified responses to the interrogatories.  (Id. ¶3, Ex.2).  On 6/26/25, Plaintiff’s counsel emailed Defendant’s counsel regarding the responses Plaintiff found to be deficient.  (Id. ¶4, Ex.3).  On 8/9/25, Defendant emailed further responses with purported verifications which Plaintiff’s counsel deemed to be invalid.  (Aliazis Decl. ¶5).  On 8/11/25, Plaintiff’s counsel spoke with defense counsel, Asheesh Mohindru, regarding the responses and agreed that there was no deadline to file a motion to compel further responses because a proper verification had not been served.  (Id. ¶6, Ex.4).  On 10/10/25, Defendant served verifications for the responses to interrogatories.  (Id. ¶¶7-8, Ex.5-6).  On 2/25/26, Defendant served further responses to some of the interrogatories, but not to those at issue in this motion.  (Id. ¶9, Ex.7).

 

The parties agreed multiple times to extend the deadline to file a motion to compel further responses with the last extension expiring on 3/20/26.  (Aliazis Decl. ¶10, Ex.8).

 

On 3/2/26, Plaintiff filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling Defendant  to serve further responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories – Employment Law, Set 1, Nos. 204.5, 204.6, 209.2, 214.1 and 216.1 served on 4/3/25.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests an order imposing sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, McCune & Harber, and in favor of Salusky Law Group in the amount of $9,860.00.  Defendant has opposed the motion and Plaintiff has filed a reply to the opposition. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

CCP 2030.300 provides in relevant part:

 

“(a) On receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply:

(1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete.

(2) An exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate.

(3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.

(b)(1) A motion under subdivision (a) shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.

. . .

 

(c) Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response to the interrogatories.

(d) The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a further response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”

 

 If a timely motion to compel further responses is filed, the responding party has the burden of justifying any objection.  Williams (2017) 3 C5th 531, 541-542; Fairmont Insurance Co. (2000) 22 C4th 245, 255; Coy (1962) 58 C2d 210, 220-221.

 

Defendant’s claim that the instant motion is untimely is without merit.  Despite the fact that the motion includes evidence that Defendant initially served unverified responses to the interrogatories on 5/30/25 (which amount to no response See Appleton (1988) 206 CA3d 632, 636); that Plaintiff sent a meet and confer letter less than a month later on 6/26/26; that proper verifications for the responses were not served until 10/10/25; and that defense counsel agreed to extend the deadline to file a motion to compel further responses until 3/20/26, the opposition disingenuously argues that the motion is untimely when, in fact it was filed and served on 3/2/26, 18 days before the last agreed upon extension to file the motion.  (See; Aliazis Decl. ¶¶3-8, 10, Ex.2-6, 8; Opposition, p.1:24-p.2:3).

 

Defendant’s claim that Plaintiff failed to adequately meet and confer before filing the motion is also without merit.  The evidence establishes that Plaintiff’s counsel sent a meet and confer letter regarding the unverified responses less than a month after they were served; defense counsel agreed to provide further responses and Defendant never provided further responses to the interrogatories at issue in this motion.  (See Aliazis Decl., Ex.3-4; Aliazis Reply Decl., Ex.4-6, 14-17, 20).  Contrary to Defendant’s assertion, Plaintiff’s counsel’s meet and confer letter and the instant motion put Defendant on notice of the issues with the subject discovery responses. 

 

As noted above, once the instant motion was filed, the burden shifted to Defendant to justify its objections to the subject form interrogatories which have already been approved by the Judicial Counsel.  Defendant has failed to meet its burden.  Additionally, any of the substantive responses provided, without waiving the unmeritorious objections, are incomplete and evasive  (e.g. responses to Form Interrogatories 204.6, 214.1).

 

Plaintiff is entitled to an award of sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, McCune & Harber, for their failure to comply with their discovery obligations.  CCP 2030.300(d).  However, the Court finds that the $9,860.00 requested is excessive.  The Court finds that sanctions in the amount of $4,460.00 sanctions are warranted (5 hours to prepare the motion + 2 hours to review the opposition and prepare the reply + 1 hour to prepare for and appear at the hearing multiplied by $550/hour = $4,400.00 + $60.00 filing fee = $4,460.00).

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.  Defendant  Saugus Union School District is ordered to serve further verified responses to Plaintiff Krystal Huber’s Form Interrogatories – Employment Law, Set 1, Nos. 204.5, 204.6, 209.2, 214.1 and 216.1 within 20 days.  served on 4/3/25.  Additionally, Defendant and its counsel, McCune & Harber, are ordered to pay sanctions in favor of Salusky Law Group in the amount of $4,460.00, payable within 20 days.

 


Case Number: 25CHCV04348    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Case #25CHCV04348

 

MOTION FOR DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

Motion filed on 5/12/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff State National Companies, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Alejandro Lezama Macias dba Macias Kustom Autobody

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: Entry of Default Judgment against Defendant Alejandro Lezama Macias dba Macias Kustom Autobody.

 

RULING: The motion is denied without prejudice. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of Plaintiff State National Companies, Inc. (Plaintiff) claim that Defendant Alejandro Lezama Macias dba Macias Kustom Autobody (Defendant) has been unlawfully withholding possession of a 2018 Chevrolet Camaro (the Vehicle), with an approximate value of $51,000.00, from Plaintiff, the legal owner and lienholder of the Vehicle.  (Complaint ¶¶1, 8-9; Iqbal Decl. ¶2-4; Cleff Decl. ¶¶2-4) 

 

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant came into possession of the Vehicle sometime on or before 5/13/25 when Defendant sent a written demand to Plaintiff seeking payment of $32,530.00 for towing and storage, with charges accruing at $250.00 per day.  (Complaint ¶10; Iqbal Decl. ¶4; Cleff Decl. ¶5). 

 

On 9/26/25, Plaintiff personally served Defendant with a letter (the Letter) which demanded, within 10 days: (a) an itemized breakdown of all charges; (b) a list of all repairs performed with dates and costs; and (c) evidence of compliance with California Vehicle Code  10652.5.  (Complaint ¶¶12-14; Iqbal Decl. ¶5).  Defendant never responded.  (Complaint ¶15; Iqbal Decl. ¶7).

 

On 10/10/25,  the California Department of Motor Vehicles issued a Lien Sale Notice (the Notice) to Plaintiff reflecting Defendant’s claimed repair costs of $2,500.00, a daily storage rate of $250.00, and a current storage balance of $30,000.00.  (Complaint ¶¶16-17; Iqbal Decl. ¶¶ 6, 8).  The Notice showed that Defendant claimed services were completed on 4/29/25, and the Vehicle was towed on 5/13/25.  (Complaint ¶ 17; Iqbal Decl. ¶¶6-7). Defendant never provided any itemized invoice, work order, or owner authorization to Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶ 18; Iqbal Decl. ¶9).

 

On 12/5/25, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant alleging causes of action for: (1) Claim and Delivery; (2) Violation of Civil Code 3068; (3) Violation of California Vehicle Code 10652.5; (4) Violation of Civil Code 3071; (5) Conversion and (6) Declaratory Relief.  On 12/15/25, Defendant was personally served with the Summons and Complaint.  (See Proof of Service filed 1/21/26). 

 

On 2/9/26, default was entered against Defendant.  On 5/12/26, Plaintiff filed and served the instant motion for entry of default judgment against Defendant. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A plaintiff may seek default judgment if: (1) a defendant fails to answer a complaint despite being properly served; and (2) default has been entered against the defendant.  CCP 585(b).  In order to obtain a default judgment, a plaintiff need only establish a prima facie case.  Johnson (1999) 72 CA4th 357, 361.  The court “shall hear the evidence offered by the plaintiff, and shall render judgment in plaintiff’s favor for that relief, not exceeding the amount stated in the complaint, . . . as appears by the evidence to be just.”  CCP 585(b). The court may permit the use of affidavits, in lieu of personal testimony, as to all or any part of the evidence heard at the default judgment hearing.  CCP 585(d). 

 

In determining whether to enter default judgment, the court may accept all of the allegations of the Complaint as true.  Bristol Convalescent Hospital (1968) 258 CA2d 848, 859; Los Angeles v. (1907) 150 Cal. 647, 649.   

 

Plaintiff addresses its claims under the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th and 6th causes of action and requests that the Court enter default judgment as follows:

 

1.  Ordering Defendant Alejandro Lezama Macias, DBA Macias Kustom Autobody, to immediately release the Vehicle (2018 Chevrolet Camaro ZL1, VIN 1G1FJ1R63J0167631, License Plate No. 8XIN236) to Plaintiff;

 

2. Declaring that Defendant’s purported lien on the Vehicle is void and unenforceable under California Civil Code §§ 3068, 3071, and Vehicle Code § 10652.5;

 

3. Declaring that Plaintiff owes Defendant no storage charges, towing charges, or other fees in excess of any amounts lawfully permitted under California Civil Code § 3068 and Vehicle Code § 10652.5; and

 

4. For such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

 

Plaintiff has failed to establish that Defendant still has possession of the Vehicle so as to be able to immediately release the Vehicle to Plaintiff.  The complaint and Plaintiff’s evidence acknowledge that on 10/10/25, Plaintiff received a Lien Sale Notice from the California Department of Motor Vehicles which reflected that Defendant’s claimed repair costs of $2,500.00, a daily storage rate of $250.00, and a current storage balance of $30,000.00.  (See Complaint ¶¶16, 41, Ex.6; Cleff Decl. ¶7; Iqbal Decl. ¶6).  The Lien Sale Notice provides that the Department of Motor Vehicles is required by law to issue the application to hold a lien sale of the Vehicle unless Plaintiff wishes to exercise its right to a court hearing to contest such action.  (See Complaint, Ex.6).  To exercise such right, Plaintiff was required to complete and sign the Declaration of Opposition included with the Lien Sale Notice and return it to the DMV within 10 days of the date of the notice.  Id.  There is no indication that Plaintiff returned the Declaration of Opposition.  As such, it is possible that Defendant sold the Vehicle to a third party and cannot release the Vehicle to Plaintiff.

 

Also, if Defendant sold the Vehicle pursuant to Lien Sale, the issue of Defendant’s lien on the Vehicle would be moot. 

 

Additionally, the motion fails to address Plaintiff’s claim for conversion and the request for monetary damages, including actual damages, punitive damages and attorneys’ fees as permitted by law requested in the complaint.  (See Complaint,  Prayer ¶5). 

 

Further, Plaintiff has not submitted a proposed Default Judgment.  Only, a proposed order on the motion has been submitted.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is denied without prejudice.
