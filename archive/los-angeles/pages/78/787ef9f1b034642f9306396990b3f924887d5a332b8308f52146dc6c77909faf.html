ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On May 1, 2024, Plaintiffs Ana Bertha Morales (“Morales”) and Patricia Trujillo Camarena (“Camarena”) (“Plaintiffs”) filed the instant action against Defendant Nancy Carina Becerra (“Defendant”) and Does 1-50, alleging causes of action for Motor Vehicle and General Negligence. (Compl., pp. 1, 3.)

 

On October 7, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer.

 

On May 5, 2026, Morales filed the instant Motion to Compel Defendant to Provide Further Responses to Plaintiff’s First Set of Requests for Admission (“RFAs”) and request for sanctions.

 

On May 13, 2026, Camarena filed the instant Motion to Compel Defendant to Provide Further Responses to Plaintiff’s RFAs and request for sanctions.

 

On May 20, 2026, the Court, in pertinent part, consolidated the Motions’ hearings to the same date, and issued an order assigning the case to Resolve Law LA Virtual MSC Program. (5/20/26 Order.)

 

On June 9, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a supplemental brief regarding the Motions.

 

On June 11, 2026, the Court called an Informal Discovery Conference (“IDC”) for hearing; however, “[t]here being no appearance this date by the plaintiff the matter [was] placed off calendar.” (6/11/26 Order.)

 

On July 2, 2026, Defendant filed Oppositions to the Motions.

 

On July 10, 2026, Plaintiffs filed Replies to the Motions.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

Notice of a motion to compel further discovery must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.290, subd. (c).)

 

The motion must also be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.290, subd. (b).)

 

In California, discovery statutes “must be construed liberally in favor of disclosure unless the request is clearly improper.” (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 541.) “Under the discovery statutes, information is discoverable if it is unprivileged and is either relevant to the subject matter of the action or reasonably calculated to reveal admissible evidence.” (John B. v. Superior Court (2006) 38 Cal.4th 1177, 1186.) “Discovery may relate to the claim or defense of the party seeking discovery or of any other party to the action.” (Ibid.) “When discovery requests are grossly overbroad on their face, and hence do not appear reasonably related to a legitimate discovery need, a reasonable inference can be drawn of an intent to harass and improperly burden.” (Obregon v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 424, 431.)

 

III.            Discussion

 

A.    Requests for Admission

 

Plaintiffs move for further responses to Defendant’s supplemental responses for RFAs Nos. 2, 5, 9, 19, and 20. (Motions, Sep. Statements.)

 

In the Oppositions, Defendant argues the Motions should be denied on the grounds that Plaintiffs failed to appear at the IDC, and Defendant’s responses to both sets of RFAs are sufficient and comply with the Code of Civil Procedure.

 

In the Replies, Plaintiffs state Plaintiffs’ counsel did not intentionally fail to appear at the IDC and, moreover, the lack of appearance at an IDC should not be grounds to deny the Motions; the Motions should be granted as Defendant’s responses are inadequate; and the Motions should be granted as the Oppositions demonstrate Defendant has sufficient information to admit or deny the RFAs pertaining to negligence.

 

Plaintiff Morales’ Motion

 

As an initial matter, the Court finds that the Motion was not brought less than 45 days from service of the responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.290, subd. (c).) Indeed, the Motion presents evidence that the last supplemental responses were served on March 19, 2026; however, the Motion was not filed until May 5, 2026, which is 46 days after service of the responses. (Motion, Exh. E.)

 

Further, no sufficient meet and confer declaration accompanies the Motion in violation of Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.290, subdivision (b). While the declaration accompanying the Motion states a couple meet and confer letters were sent regarding the issues raised in the Motion, letters alone are insufficient to satisfy the meet and confer requirement. (Motion, Lopez Decl.) To the extent the declaration accompanying the Reply broadly references meet and confer attempts between the parties prior to filing the Motions, the Court does not find this declaration satisfies the statutory meet and confer requirement either. 

 

Finally, the Court finds the responses to all subject RFAs are sufficient under the Code of Civil Procedure. (See Motion, Sep. Statement.) Each supplemental response to the RFAs either: admitted the material; denied the material; or stated the responding party lacked information or knowledge to admit or deny, and responding party made a reasonable inquiry into the matter to no avail. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.220, subds. (a)-(c) [“(a) Each answer in a response to requests for admission shall be as complete and straightforward as the information reasonably available to the responding party permits. (b) Each answer shall: (1) Admit so much of the matter involved in the request as is true, either as expressed in the request itself or as reasonably and clearly qualified by the responding party. (2) Deny so much of the matter involved in the request as is untrue. (3) Specify so much of the matter involved in the request as to the truth of which the responding party lacks sufficient information or knowledge. (c) If a responding party gives lack of information or knowledge as a reason for a failure to admit all or part of a request for admission, that party shall state in the answer that a reasonable inquiry concerning the matter in the particular request has been made, and that the information known or readily obtainable is insufficient to enable that party to admit the matter”].)

 

To the extent any supplemental response contained objections and the response of admit, deny, or lack of information to admit or deny and a reasonable inquiry was made, the Court finds the objections were well-founded given the phrasing of the request. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.230.) For instance, RFA No. 2 seeks for Defendant to “[a]dmit that on December 18, 2022, YOU were negligent in the operation of the vehicle YOU drove with regard to the INCIDENT,” and RFA No. 5 seeks for Defendant to “[a]dmit that YOUR negligence with regard to the INCIDENT was a substantial factor in bringing about harm to Plaintiffs.” (Motion, Sep. Statement.) Negligence is a legal term of art with multiple components, and thus Defendant’s objections paired with admissions as to portions of the matter involved were permissible responses given the phrasing of the questions.  

 

Furthermore, the supplemental responses provided by the responding party include the language requested by the moving party that: “This responding party lacks sufficient information or knowledge to either admit or deny. A reasonable inquiry concerning this particular request has been made and the information known is insufficient to enable this party to admit to the matter.”

 

To the extent that the moving party wishes Request for Admissions to incorporate expert opinions, the parties are reminded that the parties may conduct expert discovery at the appropriate juncture of the case.

 

            Thus, Morales’ Motion is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff Camarena’s Motion

 

As an initial matter, no sufficient meet and confer declaration accompanies the Motion in violation of Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.290, subdivision (b). While the declaration accompanying the Motion states a couple meet and confer letters were sent regarding the issues raised in the Motion, letters alone are insufficient to satisfy the meet and confer requirement. (Motion, Lopez Decl.) To the extent the declaration accompanying the Reply broadly references meet and confer attempts between the parties prior to filing the Motions, the Court does not find this declaration satisfies the statutory meet and confer requirement either. 

 

Finally, the Court finds the responses to all subject RFAs are sufficient under the Code of Civil Procedure. (See Motion, Sep. Statement.) Each supplemental response to the RFAs either: admitted the material; denied the material; or stated the responding party lacked information or knowledge to admit or deny, and responding party made a reasonable inquiry into the matter to no avail. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.220, subds. (a)-(c) [“(a) Each answer in a response to requests for admission shall be as complete and straightforward as the information reasonably available to the responding party permits. (b) Each answer shall: (1) Admit so much of the matter involved in the request as is true, either as expressed in the request itself or as reasonably and clearly qualified by the responding party. (2) Deny so much of the matter involved in the request as is untrue. (3) Specify so much of the matter involved in the request as to the truth of which the responding party lacks sufficient information or knowledge. (c) If a responding party gives lack of information or knowledge as a reason for a failure to admit all or part of a request for admission, that party shall state in the answer that a reasonable inquiry concerning the matter in the particular request has been made, and that the information known or readily obtainable is insufficient to enable that party to admit the matter”].)

 

To the extent any supplemental response contained objections and the response of admit, deny, or lack of information to admit or deny and a reasonable inquiry was made, the Court finds the objections were well-founded given the phrasing of the request. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.230.) For instance, RFA No. 2 seeks for Defendant to “[a]dmit that on December 18, 2022, YOU were negligent in the operation of the vehicle YOU drove with regard to the INCIDENT,” and RFA No. 5 seeks for Defendant to “[a]dmit that YOUR negligence with regard to the INCIDENT was a substantial factor in bringing about harm to Plaintiffs.” (Motion, Sep. Statement.) Negligence is a legal term of art with multiple components, and thus Defendant’s objections paired with admissions as to portions of the matter involved were permissible responses given the phrasing of the questions. 

 

Thus, Camarena’s Motion is DENIED.

 

For all these reasons, the Motions are DENIED.

 

B.    Sanctions

 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.290, subd. (d).)

 

Here, the Motions were unsuccessful. However, Defendant neither requests sanctions nor provides evidence outlining a reasonable sanctions award in the Oppositions. (See Opps.)

 

As such, the Court does not award sanctions at this time.

 

IV.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiffs Ana Bertha Morales and Patricia Trujillo Camarena’s Motions to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions and requests for sanctions are DENIED.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On June 30, 2024, Plaintiff Dermot Givens (“Plaintiff”) filed an action against Defendants City of Los Angeles (“City of LA”), Los Angeles Police Dept., LAPD Officer Emmanuel Figueroa, LAPD Officer Gregory Papik, LAPD Officer L. Williams, LAPD Officer L. Zaragoza, LAPD Commander (Retired) Cory Palka, and Does 1 through 10, alleging causes of action for (1) Cal. Const. Art. I, section 3, (2) Cal. Const. Art. I, section 13, (3) Cal. Civil Code section 52.1, (4) false imprisonment, (5) Cal. Civil Code section 51.7, (6) assault, (7) negligence, and (8) IIED. (Complaint, p. 1.) On January 26, 2026, Plaintiff identified Doe 1 as Antonio Gomez and Doe 2 as Rocky Martinez. On April 6, 2026, Plaintiff identified Doe 3 as Roger Klohr and Doe 4 as Amar Pal. On April 15, 2026, Plaintiff identified Doe 5 as Antonio Gomez again. On May 26, 2026, Plaintiff identified Doe 6 as Pela Aceves.

 

On January 20, 2026, the Court granted summary judgment in favor of Defendants City of LA, Emmanual Figueroa, Gregory Papik, Christian Zaragoza, and Christian Williams. On February 5, 2026, Judgment was entered in favor of Defendants City of LA, Emmanual Figueroa, Gregory Papik, Cristian Zaragoza, and Christian Williams.

 

On February 10, 2026, Palka filed an Answer.

 

On May 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories (“SROGs”) Set One (“SROGs Motion”).

 

On June 1, 2026, Defendant Cory Palka (“Defendant”) filed an Opposition to the SROGs Motion.

 

On June 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Discovery Responses to Request for Admissions (“RFAs”) Set 2 from Defendant (the “RFAs Motion”).

 

On June 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply to the SROGs Motion.

 

On June 11, 2026, the Court continued the SROGs Motion and ordered the parties to meet and confer twice by telephonic conference and to file a joint statement report prior to the continued hearing. (6/11/26 Order.) The Court also admonished Plaintiff to follow the Stipulated Protective Order, and the Court continued the RFAs Motion to the same hearing date as the SROGs Motion. (6/11/26 Order.)

 

On July 6, 2026, Defendant filed an Opposition to the RFAs Motion.

 

On July 7, 2026, Defendant filed a Status Report Re: Remaining Issues on the SROGs Motion.

 

On July 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply regarding the RFAs Motion.

 

On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Status Report Re: the SROGs Motion.

 

 

 

II.              Legal Standard & Discussion

 

A.    SROGs Motion

 

Parties’ Arguments

 

Plaintiff seeks evidence of how LAPD officers acting under Defendant’s leadership are involved in targeting Plaintiff. (SROGs Motion, p. 3.) Plaintiff had a phone conversation with Defendant’s counsel, but Defendant refused to amend his responses. (SROGs Motion, p. 4.)

 

In Opposition, Defendant argues that the SROGs Motion was filed and served untimely and lacks the required separate statement. Further, Defendant argues Plaintiff fails to cite or rely on any legal authority for compelling further responses. (Opp., p. 1.)

 

In Reply, Plaintiff reiterates arguments raised in the SROGs Motion.  

 

Analysis

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.300, subdivision (a), provides that “[o]n receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) [a]n answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete [;] (2) [a]n exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate [; or] (3) [a]n objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.”

 

The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300(b)(1).) The motion must be filed within 45 days from responses, supplemental responses, or a specific later date agreed to in writing. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300(c).)

 

California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345 requires a separate statement to be filed in conjunction with a motion to compel further responses to interrogatories.

 

Here, the Court finds Defendant’s responses to the SROGs are solely unverified objections. Thus, the Court does not find the SROGs Motion untimely. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300.)

 

However, the Court finds there is no separate statement filed in support of the Motion. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345, subd. (a).) Thus, Plaintiff’s rationale for why a further response is needed is unclear. 

 

That said, upon review of Defendant’s responses provided to Plaintiff’s SROGs, the Court finds the responses to the SROGs are unverified and constitute boilerplate objections. (See SROGs Motion, Doc. 15, Exh. 5; Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a)(3); see also Korea Data Systems Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 51 Cal.App.4th 1513, 1515.) Defendant fails to provide the specific ground for each objection and/or to provide a response to the extent possible if only part of the SROG is objectionable. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.220, 2030.240.)

 

Further, there is no copy in the Court’s record of Defendant’s SROGs responses with an attached verification as required by the Code of Civil Procedure. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300.)

 

For these reasons, the SROGs Motion is GRANTED.

 

B.    RFAs Motion

 

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant’s responses to RFAs Set Two served on April 30, 2026, and May 16, 2026. (Motion.)

 

In Opposition, Defendant states the RFAs provided in conjunction with the RFAs Motion are directed to City of LA, not Defendant, and thus Defendant is not obligated to respond to the RFAs. Further, Defendant states Plaintiff failed to meet and confer prior to filing the RFAs Motion, and Defendant requests monetary or terminating sanctions against Plaintiff for making the RFAs Motion.

 

In Reply, Plaintiff makes various ambiguous arguments and then asserts the parties met and conferred regarding the issues raised in the RFAs Motion. Plaintiff also asserts that Defendant provided verified responses to the RFAs and reiterates arguments made in the RFAs Motion.

 

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).) “If a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response…(a) [that party] waives any objection to the requests, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (a).) “The requesting party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction under Chapter 7.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) 

 

            Here, Plaintiff provides a copy of the RFAs in conjunction with the Motion (RFAs Motion, Exh. 6); however, there is no proof of service accompanying the RFAs and, moreover, the RFAs are directed towards City of LA, who is a different defendant in this matter than Defendant Palka.

 

            The Court may not compel Defendant to respond to discovery requests made to a different defendant and absent proof of proper service.

 

            Thus, the RFAs Motion is DENIED.

 

            Defendant seeks sanctions under the Code of Civil Procedure section for compelling a further response to discovery; however, the RFAs Motion appears to be brought as a motion seeking initial responses, and thus Defendant’s request is inapt.

 

The Court does not award sanctions in relation to the RFAs Motion.

 

III.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff Dermot Givens’ Motion to Compel Further Discovery Responses to Special Interrogatories-Set One is GRANTED. Defendant is to provide further and verified responses to Plaintiff’s SROGs within 20 days of this Court’s order.

 

The Court admonishes Plaintiff and reminds Plaintiff to follow the Stipulated Protective Order requiring parties to file protected material under seal. (See 11/22/24 Order.)

 

Plaintiff Dermot Givens’ Motion to Compel Initial Responses to Requests for Admissions, Set Two is DENIED.

I.                Background

 

On August 22, 2025, Plaintiff Choice Foods, Inc. filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants Shoreline Foods Inc., Amusement Foods Inc., Freeway Foods, Inc., Duquesne Properties, LLC, 730 Broadway, Inc., Waffle Plaza Properties, Inc., Diego Plate Properties, LLC, Gower Properties, LLC, La Breafo, Inc., La Brea Food, Inc., Roscoe’s Cool, Inc., Heirbel Management, Inc., Heirbella Drink Co, LLC, Roscoe’s  Intellectual Property, LLC, Herbert Hudson, and Sam White (“Defendants”) and Does 1-30, alleging Breach of Contract, Open Book Account, Account Stated, Intentional Misrepresentation, Negligent Misrepresentation, Promissory Estoppel, Quantum Meruit, and Unjust Enrichment causes of action.

 

On September 22, 2025, Shoreline Foods, Amusement Foods, Freeway Foods, LA Breafo, Duquesne Properties, Waffle Plaza Properties, Diego Plate Properties, LA Brea Foods, Heirbel Management, Heirbella Drink, and Roscoe’s Intellectual Properties LLC filed an Answer to the FAC. No Opposition has been filed.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 284 states that “the attorney in an action…may be changed at any time before or after judgment or final determination, as follows: (1) Upon the consent of both client and attorney…; (2) Upon the order of the court, upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 284; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)  “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133); see Lempert v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1173.)

 

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Counsel Forms MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion), MC-052 (Declaration), and MC-053 (Proposed Order). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) 

 

In addition, California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 subsection (d) requires that the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case by personal service, electronic service, or mail. If the notice is served by electronic service, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating that the electronic service address is the client’s current electronic service address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(2).) As used in this rule, “current” means:

 

“[T]he address was confirmed within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved.

Merely demonstrating that the notice was sent to the client’s last known address and was not returned or no electronic delivery failure message was received is not, by itself, sufficient to demonstrate that the address is current.”

 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d).)

 

III.            Discussion

 

On June 22, 2026, Counsel moved the Court to be relieved as attorney of record for Amusement Foods. (MC-051.) Counsel filed MC-051, MC-052, and MC-053 Forms. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) 

 

Counsel seeks to be relieved on the following grounds:

 

Complete breakdown of communication between moving counsel and client. Last telephonic and text exchanges with client’s sole owner and owner’s third-party financial advisor concerning issues in this case were on November 27, 2025, at which point moving counsel was informed that new counsel would be substituting into this case, but no substitution of attorney form has been circulated, served, or filed. Client has not responded to requests to meet concerning this (and other) cases. Client has not paid outstanding invoices. Client’s owner and financial advisor have not responded to moving counsel's inquiries regarding discovery since November, 2025. Client apparently has engaged other counsel to ‘speak’ for it in dealing with plaintiff s counsel (without substituting into the case in place of moving counsel.

 

(MC-052, ¶ 2.)

 

The Court is satisfied with Counsel’s reasons for seeking to be relieved. (Cal. Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16.) The Court also finds the Motion satisfactorily complies with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s Motion is GRANTED.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On April 29, 2025, Plaintiff Silvia Polanco (“Plaintiff”) filed a Complaint for Damages against Defendants Sawhorse Productions, LLC, Takeone Network Corp. dba Wrapbook, Victor Sun, and Jimmy Dower (“Defendants”) and Does 1-100, alleging 16 causes of action. (Compl., pp. 1-2.)

 

On March 3, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel, in pertinent part, informed the Court that the case has been resolved only as to the corporate defendants. (3/3/26 Order.)

 

On March 24, 2026, Sawhorse Productions and Takeone Network Corp. were dismissed with prejudice as to the Complaint.

 

On April 6, 2026, Cross-Complainants Victor Sun and Jimmy Dower filed a Cross-Complaint for Contractual Indemnity, Equitable Indemnity, Contribution, Breach of Contract, Negligence, and Declaratory Relief against Cross-Defendants Sawhorse Productions and Cast Locations, LLC, and Roes 1-10. (Cross-Compl.)

 

On June 15, 2026, Cross-Defendant Sawhorse Productions (“Sawhorse Productions”) filed the instant Motion for an Order Continuing the Final Status Conference and Trial and All Trial Related Dates (the “Motion”).

 

That same day, Sawhorse Productions filed an Answer to the Cross-Complaint.

 

On July 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

 

That same day, Cross-Complainants filed a Joinder to the Motion.

 

On July 10, 2026, Sawhorse Productions filed a Reply to the Motion.

 

II.              Legal Standard & Discussion

 

Sawhorse Productions requests a continuance of trial and all related dates by 120 days “due to the fact that Cross-Defendant recently entered into this case on June 15, 2026 and requires additional time to conduct discovery and prepare for trial.” (Motion, p. 2, Su Decl.)

 

 In Opposition, Plaintiff states the Motion should be denied as Plaintiff and Sawhorse Productions have exchanged multiple rounds of discovery in the instant action; Sawhorse Productions has full knowledge of this case through its prior participation in the action involving the Complaint; Cross-Complainants’ Joinder to the Motion is not grounds to grant the Motion; Plaintiff would suffer prejudice if trial were continued; and the requested continuance length is excessive. (Opp., pp. 5-9, Minnick Decl.)

 

In Reply, Sawhorse Productions states that any discovery undertaken regarding the Complaint is irrelevant as to the action involving the Cross-Complaint, and the Opposition fails to establish how a four-month continuance is excessive. (Reply, pp. 2-4.)

 

California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subdivision (c) states: “Although continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The Court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance.” Circumstances that may indicate good cause include: the excused unavailability of a party, trial counsel, or witness; the substitution of trial counsel if the substitution is required in the interests of justice; the addition of a new party in certain circumstances; a party’s excused inability to obtain essential testimony, documents, or other material evidence despite diligent efforts; or a significant, unanticipated change in the status of the case. (Cal. Rules of Court, subd. (c)(1)-(7).)

 

The Court may also look to the following factors in determining whether a trial continuance is warranted: (1) proximity of the trial date; (2) whether there was any previous continuance, extension, or delay of trial due to any party; (3) the length of the continuance requested; (4) the availability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion; (5) the prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; (6) if the case is entitled to preferential trial setting; (7) the court’s calendar; (8) whether trial counsel is engaged in another trial; (9) whether all parties have stipulated to the continuance; (10) whether the interests of justice are best served by the continuance; and (11) any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application.  (See generally Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1332, subd. (d)(1)-(11).) 

 

Here, the Court first notes that the Motion and supporting declaration are very sparse and fail to fully flesh out the reasons supporting good cause for continuance of trial. Sawhorse Productions is cautioned to ensure any filing with the Court contains a full account and analysis of all pertinent details and requirements.

 

That said, the Court finds the Motion contains enough to make an affirmative showing that good cause exists for a 120-day trial continuance in this matter. The Motion and evidence before the Court demonstrate Sawhorse Productions was dismissed from the action regarding the Complaint and only appeared in the action on the Cross-Complaint on June 15, 2026. (Opp., p. 3, Su Decl., ¶ 3.) Sawhorse Productions’ counsel declares that no discovery has been done on the action involving the Cross-Complaint, including that Plaintiff’s deposition has not been taken. (Opp., Su Decl., ¶ 3.) Thus, the Court finds that a 120-day trial continuance would give the parties time to complete discovery and adequately prepare for trial on the Cross-Complaint.

 

Further, the Court notes that this is the first continuance in this matter, the continuance request is limited, the interests of justice are best served by a continuance, and Plaintiff will not suffer prejudice from a brief trial continuance.

 

Finally, the Court notes that Cross-Complainants Victor Sun and Jimmy Dower also submitted a Joinder to the Motion.

 

For these reasons, the Motion is GRANTED.
