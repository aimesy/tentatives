LEGAL STANDARD:

 

In unlawful detainer actions, Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) § 1170.7 provides that “A motion for summary judgment may be made at any time after the answer is filed upon giving five days notice. Summary judgment shall be granted or denied on the same basis as a motion under Section 437c.”   

 

Under CCP section 437c(p)(1), a plaintiff or cross-complainant has the initial burden on summary judgment or adjudication of showing that there is no defense to a cause of action if that party has proved each element of the cause of action entitling the party to judgment on that cause of action. Once that burden is met, the burden shifts to the defendant or cross-defendant to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action or a defense thereto. 

  

On summary judgment, the issues are defined by the pleadings. (See Physicians Comm. For Responsible Medicine v. McDonald’s Corp. (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 554, 568 [“the issues framed by the pleadings are the only issues a motion for summary judgment must address.”]; FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima (1991) 231 Cal. App. 3d 367, 382-383; Jordan-Lyon Prods., ITD., v. Cineplex Odeon Corp. (1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 1459, 1472; Bostrom v. County of San Bernardino (1995) 35 Cal. App. 4th 1654, 1663; 580 Folsom Assocs. v. Prometheus Dev. Co. (1990) 223 Cal. App. 3d 1, 14, 18 [burden to address theories “reasonably contemplated by the opponent’s pleading”]; Melican v. Regents of Univ. of Cal. (2007) 151 Cal. App. 4th 168, 182 [not requiring party to negate an unstated claim].) 

 

Courts cannot decide summary judgment or adjudication motions based upon credibility. (AARTS Prods., Inc. v. Crocker Nat’l Bank (1986) 179 Cal.App.3d 1061, 1064 [“[T]he role of the court in summary judgment proceedings is not to weigh the evidence, but to determine whether there exists a triable issue of material fact.”]; Habitat Trust for Wildlife, Inc. v. City of Rancho Cucamonga (2009) 175 Cal. App. 4th 1306, 1342.) That said, the affidavits and declarations of the moving party are strictly construed and those of the opponent are liberally constructed. (Howe v. Pioneer Mfg Co. (1968) 262 Cal. App. 2d 330, 336-37.)  

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Plaintiff contends summary judgment is appropriate because he is the property owner, provided 60-day notice to quit to Defendant, Defendant is an occupant and not a tenant, and it is inappropriate to litigate title issues in this action. Plaintiff also contends that Defendant has no viable defense because he is not a tenant.

 

Civ. Code § 789 provides: “A  tenancy or other estate at will, however created, may be by the landlord's giving notice in writing to the tenant, in the manner prescribed by Section  of the Code of Civil Procedure, to remove from the premises within a period of not less than 30 days, to be specified in the notice.”

 

Here, Plaintiff established he is entitled to a Judgement for Possession. First, he provided evidence that he owns the property. (Declaration of Robert Vasquez (Vasquez Decl.) ¶¶ 3, 4, 5, 6, 11; Exhibits 1 [trust excerpt], 2 [recorded change of trustee], 3 [recorded deed].) Second, he provided evidence that Plaintiff is living at the property. (Id. at ¶¶ 7, 13.) Third, he provided evidence that Plaintiff is an occupant, not a tenant. (Id. at ¶ 11.) Fourth, he provided evidence of serving a notice to quit on Defendant. (Id. at ¶ 12; Declaration of Michael J. Klosk ¶¶ 4, 5, 6; Exhibit 1 [60-day notice to quit], 3 [letter to Defendant].)

 

Defendant principally contends that Plaintiff’s ownership is disputed and that this unlawful detainer action should be stayed pending resolution of the parties’ related action, Case No. 25STCV33409 (Quiet Title Action).[2] The court did trail this matter until conclusion of that trial on the merits. Defendant did not prevail. As such, now, no issues of title remain. Thus, for purposes of this action, Plaintiff has presented evidence establishing his present right to possession, and Defendant’s competing claims concerning title do not create a triable issue material to that determination.

 

Defendant also contends that his payment of property taxes, utilities, and expenses created a tenancy and that Plaintiff subsequently waived termination of that tenancy by accepting such payments. The opposition, however, presents no competent evidence of an agreement between Plaintiff and Defendant that Defendant would pay those expenses as consideration for the right to occupy the property. Defendant’s assertion in his memorandum that Plaintiff “required” him to pay the property taxes is not supported by a declaration establishing such an agreement. Evidence that Defendant paid expenses associated with the property, without evidence that Plaintiff agreed to accept those payments as rent or other consideration for continued occupancy, does not create a triable issue as to the existence of a tenancy or waiver.

 

Defendant’s opposition does not create a triable issue of material fact. Defendant’s challenge to Plaintiff’s title is not properly adjudicated in this unlawful detainer action and remains for determination, as appropriate, in the related action. Nor has Defendant presented competent evidence establishing a tenancy or other agreement permitting his continued occupancy. Accordingly, Defendant has failed to demonstrate a triable issue concerning Plaintiff’s present right to possession.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for summary judgment is GRANTED.

Moving party is to give notice.
