Case Number: 21STCV26403    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 734




            Plaintiff alleges that he was terminated for complaining about being accused of being hungover at work after New Year’s Day, although he was feeling sick not hungover, and being deprived of wages thereby.

            The Court ordered the matter to arbitration. Plaintiff Jahad Muhammad moves to confirm the arbitration award.

TENTATIVE RULING          

            The hearing on Plaintiff’s petition to confirm arbitration award is CONTINUED to October 16, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. Plaintiff is to serve Defendant with the notice of continuance by mail.

ANALYSIS

Petition To Confirm Arbitration Award

Discussion

            Plaintiff seeks confirmation of the arbitration award resulting from contractual arbitration.

The petition to confirm must be served and filed no later than four years after the date of service of a signed copy of the award on the petitioner (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288) but may not be served and filed until at least 10 days after service of the signed copy of the award upon the petitioner. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288.4.)

The Award was served on all parties on July 7, 2026. (Petition, Exh. D.) The Petition was filed on July 23, 2026 and served on July 22, 2026. The petition is timely.

Although a petition to confirm arbitration award may be filed after 10 days of the service of the award (Civ. Proc. Code, § 1288.4), the hearing must be at least 100 days after the service of the award (Civ. Proc. Code, § 1288.2), which is the period of time in which the respondent may either file a file a petition to vacate the award, even if that time is beyond the 10 days in which the respondent may file a response to the petition. (Civ. Proc. Code, §§ 1285.2, 1290.6.) As articulated by the California Supreme Court, it appears that a response to a petition to confirm an arbitration award, which response seeks to vacate or reduce the award, may be served within 100 days of the service of the award.(Law Fin. Grp., LLC v. Key (2023) 14 Cal.5th 932, 946-47 [bold emphasis and underlining added].)

Here, following the July 7, 2026 service of the Award, the 100-day period would expire on October 15, 2026. As such, the hearing date is premature.  The petition was originally set for a November 6, 2026 hearing and was served upon Defendant’s counsel of record by electronic service on July 23, 2026. However, Defendant’s counsel was relieved, effective August 5, 2026.  Subsequently, Plaintiff filed an Amended Notice regarding the Petition To Confirm which re-set the hearing date for September 17, 2026, which is inside the 100 day period.   

As such the hearing on the petition to confirm arbitration award is CONTINUED to October 16, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. Plaintiff is to serve Defendant, which is unrepresented by counsel in this matter, with the notice of continuance by mail.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1010.6(f)(2); CRC 2.251(c)(3)(B).)

            This is a Lemon Law action based on alleged defects in Plaintiff’s vehicle.

Defendant David Wilson Villa Ford of Orange County moves for judgment on the pleadings as to the First Amended Complaint.

 TENTATIVE RULING

            Defendant David Wilson Villa Ford of Orange County’s motion for judgment on the pleadings as to the First Amended Complaint is GRANTED without leave to amend as to the fifth cause of action.

ANALYSIS

Motion For Judgment on the Pleadings

Meet and Confer

            The Declaration of Nicole M. Peterson reflects that Plaintiff’s counsel did not respond to meet and confer efforts. This satisfies Code Civ. Proc. § 439(a)(3)(B).

Discussion          

The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

On a motion for judgment on the pleadings, the same rules applicable on demurrer apply. (County of Orange v. Association of Orange County Deputy Sheriffs (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 21, 32-33.)

Defendant David Wilson Villa Ford of Orange County moves for judgment on the pleadings as to the First Amended Complaint.

1.         Fifth Cause of Action (Negligent Repair).

            Defendants argues that this cause of action is barred by the economic loss rule.

            The fifth cause of action is based upon the dealer Defendant allegedly performing negligent repairs when Plaintiff bought the vehicle in for repairs. (1AC, ¶¶ 72 – 75.)

            Because a repair contract would necessarily exist between Plaintiff when he brought the vehicle for repairs (1AC, ¶ 72), and because Plaintiff does not allege injury to her person or property as a result of the allegedly negligent repairs, it appears that the economic loss rule bars this cause of action as pled:

We begin with a review of the contours of the economic loss rule. The rule itself is deceptively easy to state: In general, there is no recovery in tort for negligently inflicted “purely economic losses,” meaning financial harm unaccompanied by physical or property damage. (Citations omitted.)

. . .

In another recurring set of circumstances, the rule functions to bar claims in negligence for pure economic losses in deference to a contract between litigating parties. . . [*923]  . . .

. . . Not all tort claims for monetary losses between contractual parties are barred by the economic loss rule. But such claims are barred when they arise from—or are not independent of—the parties’ underlying contracts. (Citations omitted.) Plaintiff’s claim here arises from, and is not independent of, the mortgage contract.

(Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905, 922-23.)

In contrast, we have never done what plaintiff now asks us to do: rely on Biakanja to impose a tort duty on a contracting party to avoid negligently causing monetary harm to another party to that contract. (Citation omitted.)

(Sheen, supra, 12 Cal.5th at  938.)

 

“ ‘[A]ctual damage’  [*1662]  in the sense of “harm” is necessary to a cause of action in negligence; nominal damages are not awarded. (Citations omitted.) This is reflected in the California statutes. Compensatory damages are allowed to one “who suffers detriment.” ( Civ. Code, § 3281; see also id., § 3333.) “Detriment is a loss or harm suffered in person or property.” ( Civ. Code, § 3282.)”

(Duarte v. Zachariah (1994) 22 Cal.App.4th 1652, 1661-62 [bold emphasis added].)

            While this Court has previously cited case law which held that the economic loss rule does not apply to contracts for services (North American Chemical Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 764, 781), upon further review, the Court notes that this principle has been limited to the context of insurance policies and contracts for professional services, such as legal services. (Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905, 929, 932, 933.) As such, the Court finds that the economic loss rule applies to this garden-variety contract for car repair services.

            The motion for judgment on the pleadings as to the fifth cause of action is GRANTED without leave to amend.

Case Number: BC651071    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 734

The following constitutes the Court's ruling on the ex parte application discussed herein, and is posted for the convenience of the parties.  The application will not be called for hearing.   

Cross-Defendants Herbert Marshak and Champion Medical Group apply ex parte for an order specially setting a hearing on their motion to dismiss the cross-complaint.  The Court has reviewed the ex parte application and the opposition papers filed by Cross-Complainant Sahibzada Aasim Akhtar (“Akhtar”), and finds the matter suitable for decision in chambers without argument.

 

Cross-Defendants contend that an order advancing the hearing on their motion to dismiss is appropriate, because “the Court's irregularly short setting of the trial on 8/6/26” leaves inadequate time for the motion to be heard before trial.

 

A partial review of the procedural history of this case is relevant to the ex parte application.

 

This case was previously assigned to the Honorable Holly J. Fujie in Department 56.  On November 21, 2025, Judge Fujie held a hearing on two motions filed by Defendant/Cross-Complainant Akhtar:  (1) a motion for reconsideration, and (2) a motion to set case for jury trial.  Judge Fujie denied the motion for reconsideration and granted the motion to set the case for jury trial.  The minutes of the November 21, 2025 hearing indicate that the parties present waived notice, and memorialize the following information about trial setting: 

 

Final Status Conference is scheduled for 10/06/2026 at 08:30 AM in Department 56 at Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

 

The Court orders counsel to prepare a joint exhibit list (with stipulations or objections noted as to each exhibit), a joint witness list (with time estimates for each side and totals), a joint verdict form, a joint set of final jury instructions, and a joint statement of the case. Motions in limine must be fully briefed. Counsel are ordered to comply with Department 56 Local Rules.

 

All counsel are to personally appear for the Final Status Conference hearing.

 

Trial and exhibits binders are to be submitted directly in the courtroom by October 5, 2026.

 

Jury Trial /Court Trial is scheduled for 10/19/2026 at 09:30 AM in Department 56 at Stanley Mosk Courthouse.  

 

 (Nov. 21, 2025 minute order at 5.)  The Court’s review of the file in this matter does not appear to indicate any order issued in Department 56 modifying the trial schedule.

 

On January 6, 2026, the Clerk of the Court issued a notice of reassignment, indicating the case was reassigned from Judge Fujie to the Honorable Steven A. Ellis, with the case remaining in Department 56. 

 

On February 2, 2026, Akhtar filed a peremptory challenge directed at Judge Ellis pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 170.6.  Also on February 2, 2026, the Clerk sent notice to the parties that the case was reassigned to Department 76 (the “Reassignment Order”).  Nothing in the Reassignment Order indicated that any dates previously scheduled in Department 56 were vacated, but the Clerk in Department 56 served a separate order vacating the hearing on Akhtar’s motion for reconsideration that had been set in Department 56. 

 

On March 16, 2026, this Court issued a notice of case management conference, indicating that a case management conference was scheduled for August 6, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Department 76.

 

On August 6, 2026, none of the parties appeared for the case management conference.  None of the parties had filed a case management statement in advance of the case management conference as required by Rule of Court 3.725.  The Court therefore could only guess at the status of the case and the parties’ intentions, and was unable to ask the parties any questions concerning the trial readiness of this very aged case.  The Court’s obligation to manage its docket and to ensure timely completion of cases requires that the Court actively manage its trial calendar and avoid the rescheduling of hearings.  Since this case had preexisting dates for the final status conference and trial that had been set in Department 56, the Court attempted to match those dates in its own trial calendar.  The Court ordered the Clerk to give notice of an October 6, 2026 final status conference date, and an October 29, 2026 trial date.

 

In light of this procedural history, Cross-Defendants’ assertion that the Court “irregularly” set a trial date on short notice is puzzling.  The October 19, 2026 trial date was set ten months ago in Department 56.  Any party that was unable to prepare for that date had the ability to request a continuance in Department 56 before the case was transferred, or to request a continuance from this Court.  None of the parties even appeared for the August 6, 2026 case management conference to inform the Court of any developments requiring the Court’s attention.

 

Based on Cross-Defendants’ representation that there is a good faith basis for a dispositive motion, the Court on its own motion VACATES the October 6, 2026 final status conference and October 29, 2026 trial dates.  A trial setting conference is set for December 15, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.  The ex parte application is DENIED; Cross-Defendants may reserve hearing on a motion to dismiss via CRS.
