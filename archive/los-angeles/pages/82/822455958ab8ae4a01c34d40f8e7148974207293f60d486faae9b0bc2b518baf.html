Case Number: 25VECV00115    Hearing Date: June 29, 2026    Dept: I

25VECV00115 MIRELLA HERNANDEZ vs VOLKSWAGEN GROUP OF AMERICA, INC., A NEW JERSEY CORPORATION, et al.

June 29, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling – Motion for Sanctions

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On January 9, 2025, Plaintiff Mirella Hernandez (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (“Volkswagen”), Sherman Oaks-A, Inc. dba Audi Van Nuys (“Audi Van Nuys”), and Does 1 to 10 alleging four causes of action: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of express warranty; (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of implied warranty; (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act Section 1793.2; and (4) negligent repair.

 

On May 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to enforce compliance with the Court’s March 24, 2026, Discovery Order. Plaintiff also requests sanctions.

 

On June 15, 2026, Volkswagen filed its opposition.

 

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed her reply.

 

II.     Legal Standard

 

Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 2023.030 permits the Court, in its discretion, to impose the following types of terminating sanctions against anyone engaging in conduct that is a “misuse of the discovery process”:

 

(1) An order striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings of any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process.

(2) An order staying further proceedings by that party until an order for discovery is obeyed.

(3) An order dismissing the action, or any part of the action, of that party.

(4) An order rendering a judgment by default against that party.

 

(CCP § 2023.030(d).)

 

Conduct that is a “misuse of the discovery process” includes failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery and disobeying a court order to provide discovery. (CCP § 2023.010(d) and (g).) “Imposition of sanctions for misuse of discovery lies within the trial court's discretion, and is reviewed only for abuse.” (Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 967, 991.) “The trial court should consider both the conduct being sanctioned and its effect on the party seeking discovery[.]” (Id. at 992.) Sanctions should be tailored to the harm created by the withheld discovery and should not be used as punishment. (Ibid.) The court should also consider whether the discovery misconduct was “willful” and whether the requesting party was prejudiced by the other’s failure to comply. (Do It Urself Moving & Storage, Inc. v. Brown, Leifer, Slatkin & Berns (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 27, 37.) Courts have held that terminating sanctions are warranted when a party repeatedly fails to propound adequate discovery responses, even after the court imposed monetary sanctions. (Liberty Mutual Fire Ins. Co. v. LcL Administrations, Inc. (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 1093.) For example, in Mileikowsky v. Tenet Healthsystem (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 262, the Court of Appeal held that five motions to compel created a record “replete with evidence of [appellant’s] failures to answer discovery requests.” (Id. at 279–280.)

 

Additionally, CCP § 2023.030(a) authorizes monetary sanctions for anyone engaging in misuse of the discovery process, including disobeying a court order to provide discovery. (See CCP § 2023.030(a).)

 

III.  Discussion

 

On March 24, 2026, the Court ordered Defendant to serve Plaintiff with substantive, verified, code-compliant responses, without objections, to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, Requests for Admissions, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, within 45 days. (See March 24, 2026, Order.)

 

On May 8, 2026, Defendant served responses that included objections, thereby failing to comply with the Court’s March 24, 2026, Order. (Hovzen Decl. ¶ 10; Exhs. 2a, 2b, and 2c [responses].) As of the date of this motion, Defendant still had not complied with the Court’s March 24, 2026, Discovery Order. (Id. at ¶ 13.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that it has “already produced all confidential and non-confidential documents in its possession, including confidential policy / procedure and OSI documents, and further supplemental responses to Plaintiff’s [discovery requests] . . .” (Opposition at 2:3-10.) Defendant also argues that it “realized that [Defendant] inadvertently left objections in [its] May 8, 2026 Supplemental Responses . . . On May 26, 2026, [Defendant] served Further Supplemental Responses to Plaintiff’s [discovery requests] without any objections in compliance with the Court’s order . . .” (Id. at 18-26 [citing Fletcher Decl. at ¶ 4; Exh. B [responses].)

 

The Court finds that Defendant has substantially complied with its March 24, 2026, Order. Any further deficiencies identified by Plaintiff may be addressed through a motion to compel further responses.

 

Notwithstanding the foregoing, Defendant only served its objection-free responses after this motion was filed. Accordingly, for the cost incurred with bringing this motion, the Court awards Plaintiff sanctions in the amount of $500.

 

IV.  Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion is GRANTED in part and DENIED in part.

 

The Court finds that Defendant has substantially complied with its March 24, 2026, Discovery Order. Therefore, Plaintiff’s request for terminating sanctions is denied.

 

However, for the cost incurred in bringing this motion, Defendant and its counsel, jointly and severally, are to pay Plaintiff’s counsel sanctions in the amount of $500 within 20 days of this order.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25VECV04359    Hearing Date: June 29, 2026    Dept: I

25VECV04359 DENISE MORATAYA-PACQUING vs RALPHS GROCERY COMPANY, et al.

June 29, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling – Motion to Dismiss

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On August 4, 2025, Plaintiff Denise Morataya-Pacquing (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants Ralphs Grocery Company (“Ralphs”), the Kroger Co. (“Kroger”), Doe Employee; and Does 1 to 25 alleging two causes of action: (1) premises liability; and (2) general negligence.

 

On April 30, 2026, Ralphs filed the instant motion to dismiss.

 

On May 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed her opposition.

 

To date, Ralphs has not filed a reply.

 

On May 27, 2026, the Court continued the hearing on Ralph’s motion to allow for compliance with the subject discovery requests.  There have been no new filings since the 5/27 hearing.

 

II.     Legal Standard

 

Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 2023.030 permits the Court, in its discretion, to impose the following types of terminating sanctions against anyone engaging in conduct that is a “misuse of

the discovery process”:

 

(1) An order striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings of any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process.

(2) An order staying further proceedings by that party until an order for discovery is obeyed.

(3) An order dismissing the action, or any part of the action, of that party.

(4) An order rendering a judgment by default against that party.

(CCP § 2023.030(d).)

 

Conduct that is a “misuse of the discovery process” includes failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery and disobeying a court order to provide discovery. (CCP § 2023.010(d) and (g).) “Imposition of sanctions for misuse of discovery lies within the trial court's discretion, and is reviewed only for abuse.” (Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 967, 991.) “The trial court should consider both the conduct being sanctioned and its effect on the party seeking discovery[.]” (Id. at 992.) Sanctions should be tailored to the harm created by the withheld discovery and should not be used as punishment. (Ibid.) The court should also consider whether the discovery misconduct was “willful” and whether the requesting party was prejudiced by the other’s failure to comply. (Do It Urself Moving & Storage, Inc. v. Brown, Leifer, Slatkin & Berns (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 27, 37.) Courts have held that terminating sanctions are warranted when a party repeatedly fails to propound adequate discovery responses, even after the court imposed monetary sanctions. (Liberty Mutual Fire Ins. Co. v. LcL

Administrations, Inc. (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 1093.) For example, in Mileikowsky v. Tenet Healthsystem (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 262, the Court of Appeal held that five motions to compel created a record “replete with evidence of [appellant’s] failures to answer discovery requests.” (Id. at 279–280.)

 

III.  Discussion

 

Ralphs argues for terminating sanctions on the grounds that Plaintiff failed to comply with the Court’s March 30, April 7, and April 9, 2026, discovery orders. (See Motion at 2:8-14.) On these dates, the Court ordered Plaintiff to provide responses to Ralphs’s various discovery requests. (See March 30, April 7, and April 9, 2026, Orders.) Ralphs argues that, at the date of filing this motion, Plaintiff had failed to comply with these orders. (Motion at 5:15-18.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that her failure to serve further responses “was the result of a culmination of unexpected life events in Plaintiff’s life over the last 5 months including, but not limited to, Plaintiff’s full-time responsibility for caring for her special needs child combined with Plaintiff’s own medical issues that have arisen (not related to this case) that required a recent surgery. Plaintiff’s health has improved and Code-compliant verified responses are being prepared and will be served forthwith prior to the hearing on this Motion.” (Opposition at 2:3-12.)

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the Court’s orders does not yet create a record “replete with evidence of [Plaintiff]’s failure to answer discovery requests.” (See Mileikowsky, supra, 128 Cal.App.4th at 279-280.) The Court finds that Plaintiff’s failure to comply appears from good cause and Plaintiff is making effort to comply with the Court orders. (See Opposition at 2:3-12.) “A decision to order terminating sanctions should not be made lightly. But where a violation is willful, preceded by a history of abuse, and the evidence shows that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with the discovery rules, the trial court is justified in imposing [terminating sanctions].” (Mileikowksy, supra, 128 Cal.App.4th at 279-280.) Ralphs does not show that less severe sanctions would fail to produce Plaintiff’s compliance.

 

Nonetheless, considering the possible prejudice to Ralphs, the Court reminds Plaintiff of her obligation to comply with the discovery statutes and previous court orders. The Court will hear from the parties as to any further responses that may have been served since the previous hearing before determining the appropriate sanction and/or Order to Show Cause.

 

IV.  Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Ralphs’s motion to dismiss and request for sanctions are DENIED without prejudice.

Case Number: 25VECV06080    Hearing Date: June 29, 2026    Dept: I

25VECV06080 ALEXANDRA MERCEDES DUFFEY vs VOLKSWAGEN GROUP OF AMERICA, INC., A NEW JERSEY CORPORATION

June 29, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling – Motion to Compel Further Discovery

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On October 23, 2025, Plaintiff Alexandra Mercedes Duffey (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 10 alleging three causes of action: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of express warranty; (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of implied warranty; and (3) violation of Song-Beverly Act Section 1793.2.

 

On May 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel Defendant’s further responses to her Requests for Production of Documents, Set One.

 

On June 15, 2026, Defendant filed its opposition.

 

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed her reply.

 

II.     Motion to Compel Further Discovery

 

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete. (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive. (3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 2031.310(a).)

 

Analysis

 

A motion to compel further responses shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (CCP § 2031.310(b).) On February 18, 2026, the parties’ counsel met and conferred telephonically and Defendant agreed to serve further responses by March 18, 2026. (Hozven Decl. at ¶ 23.) On March 18, 2026, Defendant requested a further extension until April 17, 2026, which Plaintiff granted. (Id. at ¶ 24.) As of the date of this motion, Defendant still had not served substantive responses to Plaintiff’s discovery request. (Ibid.) Accordingly, Plaintiff satisfies the meet and confer requirement of CCP § 2031.310(b).

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant’s further responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 1-31. (See Separate Statement.) These requests pertain to, inter alia, Defendant’s warranties, policies, and procedures regarding the Subject Vehicle and repurchase or repair of the Subject Vehicle pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act. (Ibid.) Defendant largely provided boilerplate objections. (Ibid.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that it is “presently finalizing supplemental responses and producing responsive, non-privileged documents and anticipates serving those materials prior to the hearing . . . Accordingly, the Motion will either be rendered moot or substantially narrowed.” (Motion at 2:24-3:1.) Defendant also argues that it “should not be required to waive objections that remain applicable, including objections based upon attorney-client privilege, attorney work product, privacy rights, overbreadth, proportionality, undue burden, and other legally recognized protections.” (Id. at 6:22-26.)

 

In reply, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s June 18, 2026, further responses still fail to cure the applicable defects. (Reply at 2:13-23.) “[Defendant] continues to rely on improper objections, vague limiting language, and unsupported privilege assertions without providing a proper privilege log or sufficient factual information to allow Plaintiff to evaluate whether responsive documents are being withheld. Worse, Defendant still has not produced core documents directly relevant to Plaintiff’s claims . . .” (Ibid.)(emphasis removed.)

 

The Court reviews Defendant’s supplemental responses and agrees with Plaintiff. Plaintiff demonstrates good cause for her requests because this evidence is relevant to show that Defendant was aware, prior to Plaintiff’s purchase, of defects in a similar make and vehicle model and was either “unwilling or unable” to fix such defects. (Santana v. FCA US, LLC (2020) 56 Cal.App.4th 334, 346.) Additionally, the Court notes that, on June 24, 2026, the parties filed a joint stipulation and protective order pertaining to some of the allegedly privileged information. The Court has signed the stipulation and protective order.

 

III.  Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses is GRANTED.

 

Defendant is to produce substantive, code-compliant responses, without objections, to Plaintiff’s Requests for Production of Documents, Set One within 30 days of this Order.

 

Moving party to give notice.
