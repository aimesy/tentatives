Case Number: 24NWCV03545    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: L

Defendant Bellflower Unified School District’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

Defendant’s Motion to Strike is GRANTED.

 

Defendant is ordered to file an answer within 10 days of this order.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for sexual assault of a minor. On October 9, 2024, Plaintiff Jane Doe, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem, P.C., (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Bellflower Unified School District (“BUSD”) and Does 1 to 40.

 

On November 5, 2025, the Court sustained BUSD’s Demurrer to the Complaint with 30 days leave to amend. (11/5/25 Order.)

 

On December 5, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC). The FAC alleges that Plaintiff was a student at Thomas Jefferson Elementary School in 2023. Plaintiff and two other female students entered a girls’ bathroom and were “confronted by a person who is believed to be an adult male, who, with his pants down, assaulted, harassed, annoyed, and intimidated [Plaintiff] and the other students.” (FAC, ¶ 11.) Plaintiff immediately reported the incident to school staff, and a staff member escorted the perpetrator, Defendant Doe 1, off campus.

 

The FAC alleges the following causes of action:

 

1.    Negligence (against BUSD and Does 2 to 40);

2.    Sexual Battery (against Doe 1);

3.    Battery (against Doe 1);

4.    Assault (against Doe 1);

5.    False Imprisonment (against Doe 1); and

6.    Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress (against Doe 1).

 

BUSD now demurs to the entire FAC or as to each cause of action individually. BUSD also moves to strike portions of the FAC.


Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, subdivision (a) and section 435.5 require that the parties meet and confer “in person,¿by telephone, or by video conference.” 

 

On December 30, 2025, Defense counsel emailed Plaintiff’s counsel to schedule a meet and confer telephone call, but Plaintiff’s counsel failed to respond. (Roche Decl., ¶¶ 2-3, Exh. A.) On January 2, 2026, Defense counsel filed a Declaration of Automatic Extension stating that the parties were unable to meet and confer at least 5 days prior to the demurrer deadline of January 8, 2026. (Roche Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.) The Demurrer was filed on February 9, 2026.

 

Despite the parties’ failure to meet and confer, the Court will address the merits of the Demurrer in the interests of judicial efficiency.

 

Discussion

 

BUSD demurs to all causes of action on grounds of uncertainty. BUSD also demurs on grounds that the first cause of action fails to state a claim against it. The Court will address each issue in turn.

 

i.             Uncertainty as to All Causes of Action

 

Firstly, BUSD argues that the FAC is uncertain as to whether all causes of action are alleged against it. Specifically, the FAC alleges the following: “Wherever reference is made in this complaint to any act by a DEFENDANT or DEFENDANTS, such allegation and reference shall also be deemed to mean the acts and failures to act of each DEFENDANT, including DOES, acting individually, jointly, and severally.” (FAC, ¶ 8.) Paragraph 8 to the SAC is incorporated by reference in each cause of action.

 

In the second through sixth causes of action the FAC alleges the following:

 

“As a direct and proximate result of the acts and omissions of DEFENDANT, as alleged herein, PLAINTIFF suffered injuries and damages including, but not limited to, physical and/or mental pain and suffering, emotional distress, past and future costs of medical and/or psychological care and treatment, and other economic and non-economic damages, in an amount not yet ascertained but which exceed the minimum jurisdictional limits of this Court.”

 

(FAC, ¶¶ 32, 35, 38, 42, 46.)

 

Reading the allegations together, the FAC alleges that “BUSD proximately caused the intentional harm for which Plaintiff seeks compensation against DOE 1 under her Second through Sixth COAs […].” (Dem., 7:17-18.) In other words, even though only the first cause of action is alleged against BUSD, it is unclear whether the other causes of action are also alleged against BUSD.

 

Second, BUSD argues that the FAC is uncertain because it contradicts itself regarding Doe 1’s status. Paragraph 4 of the FAC alleges that Doe 1's identity is unknown. However, paragraph 7 of the FAC alleges that Doe 1 was at all times an agent and employee of Defendants, including BUSD. (See FAC, ¶ 7 [“[E]ach of the DEFENDANTS sued herein as DOES 1 through 40, inclusive, was the agent and employee of each of the remaining DEFENDANTS […]”.])

 

Further, the first cause of action is only alleged against BUSD and Does 2 to 40. (See FAC, ¶ 18.) But the first cause of action alleges that “DEFENDANTS, and each of them, failed to uphold duties imposed on them by law, through applicable standards of care, and through written policies and procedures applicable to DEFENDANTS […].” (FAC, ¶ 19.) When read together with paragraph 8, the term “Defendants” includes Doe 1. Thus, it is unclear whether Doe 1 is included in the first cause of action. To the extent that the FAC alleges that BUSD is vicariously liable for the intentional torts committed by Doe 1, such claims are barred because sexual misconduct is not within the scope of school district employment.

 

Overall, it is uncertain whether the FAC alleges that BUSD is vicariously liable for the intentional acts of Doe 1, as alleged in the second through sixth causes of action.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the FAC is not so uncertain that BUSD cannot determine what is being alleged against it. Plaintiff clarifies that the first cause of action is alleged against BUSD and Does 2 to 40 only. The second through sixth causes of action are alleged against Doe 1 only. The FAC’s headings are clear as to which causes of action are alleged against which Defendants.

 

Further, the FAC is not unclear as to Doe 1’s status. The FAC alleges that Doe 1’s identity is unknown. Plaintiff is unaware at this time if Doe 1 is the agent/employee of BUSD. However, the first cause of action is clear that it is only alleged against BUSD and Does 2 to 40. In other words, the first cause of action is not based on any vicarious liability for Doe 1’s actions as an employee or agent.

 

In reply, BUSD argues that the text of the FAC explicitly defines the global term “DEFENDANT” to include BUSD in paragraphs 8 and 9. Such paragraphs are incorporated by reference into all causes of action. Thus, when considering the incorporated allegations, the FAC is uncertain because the second through sixth causes of action are also alleged against BUSD. Thus, BUSD cannot determine which causes of action are alleged against it. Such uncertainty prevents BUSD from accurately evaluating its affirmative defenses.

 

“A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly's of California, Inc. (“Khoury”) (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.) A demurrer for uncertainty should be overruled when the facts as to which the complaint is uncertain are presumptively within the defendant's knowledge. (Chen v. Berenjian (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 811, 822.)

 

The Court finds that the FAC is not so uncertain that BUSD is not on notice of what is being alleged against it. The causes of action are clearly labeled as to which cause of action is being alleged against which defendant. The Court finds that “Defendants” as used in the first cause of action, refers to BUSD and Does 2 to 40 only. The Court finds that “Defendant” as used in the second to sixth causes of action refers to Doe 1.

 

Further, the Court finds that any ambiguity as to the identity and employee status of Doe 1 can be determined through discovery procedures. (See Khoury, supra, 14 Cal.App.4th at p. 616.) Regardless, the employee status of Doe 1 is not relevant for this Demurrer. The first cause of action is not based on the theory that BUSD is vicariously liable for conduct of Doe 1 as an agent or employee. (See FAC, ¶ 18 [“DEFENDANT BUSD, through its employees, staff, and agents including those identified in this Complaint as DOES 2 through 20 and Principal Angelica Montelongo, owed a duty of care to PLAINTIFF by way of the special relationship that exists between a school district and its students.”].)

 

ii.            Failure to State a Claim as to First Cause of Action

 

BUSD argues that, because the first cause of action is uncertain, the first cause of action fails to meet the pleading standards required for claims against public entities.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that all elements of negligence are adequately alleged against BUSD.

 

For the reasons stated previously, the Court finds that the first cause of action is not uncertain. The FAC adequately states a claim against BUSD. Further, on BUSD’s prior Demurrer to the Complaint, the Court found that the Complaint sufficiently alleged “all elements of negligence” against BUSD. (See 11/5/25 Order.)

 

Accordingly, the Demurrer to the FAC is OVERRULED.

 

Motion to Strike

 

Code of Civil Procedure, section 436 permits trial courts to strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in a pleading:

 

“The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:

 (a)¿Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.

 (b)¿Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 436.)

 

BUSD moves to strike the following portion of the FAC, under the section entitled “Facts Applicable to All Causes of Action”:

 

“. . .However, Plaintiff is informed and believes, and thereon alleges, that no staff member associated with the elementary school or Defendant BUSD including, but not limited to, Angelica Montelongo, immediately contacted law enforcement or any reporting agency. Instead, Plaintiff is informed and believes, and thereon alleges, that a staff member of the elementary school merely escorted the perpetrator, Defendant DOE 1, off campus. Plaintiff is informed and believes, and thereon alleges, that it was not until the media received knowledge of the incident and contacted the school staff, that a staff member reported the incident to law enforcement. Thus, by the time the school day was over and despite PLAINTIFF and other girls reporting the assault, Plaintiff is informed and believes that neither law enforcement, nor PLAINTIFF's guardian, had been contacted by BUSD of the incident."

 

(FAC, ¶ 12.)

 

BUSD argues that such allegations are irrelevant to the first cause of action for negligence, the sole cause of action alleged against it. Specifically, the negligence claim is based on BUSD’s duty to use reasonable care to supervise and protect students, to properly train teachers, to supervise staff, to protect students, etc. (FAC, ¶ 19.) Any allegations that BUSD and its agents failed to make a report to law enforcement on the date of incident are irrelevant to proving any such negligence. Such allegations are an improper attempt to allege a violation of the Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting Act (CANRA), which provides no private right of action against a public entity or its employees for failure to report.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that such allegations are relevant to the negligence cause of action. Specifically, such allegations support Plaintiff’s claims that “BUSD’s employees failed to act promptly to protect students, failed to implement and enforce appropriate safety procedures, failed to adequately train and supervise its employees, and failed to act reasonably after learning of the assault.” (Opp., 5:18-21.)

 

In reply, BUSD argues that the FAC fails to allege with particularity that BUSD failed to train its employees on CANRA reporting policies, or that BUSD mandated reporters have a pattern or practice of violating CANRA. Further, the FAC fails to allege how any failure to report caused the alleged October 26, 2023 incident.

 

The Court finds that such allegations are irrelevant to support Plaintiff’s negligence claim. The negligence claim essentially alleges that, as a result of BUSD’s and/or its agents’ breach of their duty to properly supervise students and to train its employees, Plaintiff was injured by Doe 1’s assault. (FAC, ¶¶ 17-27.) Allegations that BUSD failed to report such assault to law enforcement or Plaintiff’s guardian do not support Plaintiff’s damages alleged in the negligence cause of action. As explained in the Court’s ruling on BUSD’s prior Demurrer to the Complaint, the FAC similarly does not allege how BUSD’s alleged failure to timely report the incident proximately caused injuries to Plaintiff separate from those associated with the assault itself. (See 11/5/25 Order.) The Court thus finds that the allegations regarding BUSD’s employees’ failure to report are improper.

 

Accordingly, the Motion to Strike is GRANTED.


Case Number: 26NWCV000032    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: L

Defendant Rosa Patterson’s Demurrer to the Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

Defendant is ordered to file an answer within 10 days of this order.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for partition regarding real property located at 4165 Sebren Ave, Lakewood, CA 90713-3014 (“Subject Property”). On January 5, 2026, Plaintiff Bobby Patterson (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Rosa Patterson, Nationstar Mortgage LLC, Mortgage Electronic Registration Systems, Inc., and Does 1 to 10. The Complaint alleges that the parties acquired their interest acquired their interests in the Subject Property as joint tenants through a Grant Deed dated April 21, 2005, and recorded on May 27, 2005. (Compl., Exh. B.)

 

On March 2, 2026, Defendant Rocket Mortgage, LLC was named as Doe 1.

 

Defendant Rosa Patterson now demurs to the Complaint.

 

Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 29, 2026. No reply has been filed as of July 13, 2026.

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

Defendant’s Requests for Judicial Notice of the following documents are granted:

 

-      Exhibit A – January 5, 2026, Summon and Partition Action filed in LASC Case No. 26NWCV00032 caption Bobby Patterson v. Rosa Patterson.

-      Exhibit B – October 19, 2022, Petition for Dissolution of marriage filed in LASC Case No. 22WHFL01798.

-      Exhibit C – December 21, 2022, Request to Enter Default filed LASC Case No. 22WHFL01798.

-      Exhibit D – February 1, 2023, Judgment of Dissolution filed in LASC Case No. 22WHFL01798.

-      Exhibit E – April 21, 2005, Grant Deed.

-      Exhibit F – December 1, 2025, Request for Emergency Orders filed in LASC Case No. 22WHFL01798.

-      Exhibit G – December 4, 2025, Request for Emergency Orders filed LASC Case No. 22WHFL01798.


Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that the parties meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or by video conference.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

Counsel met and conferred via telephone and written communication regarding the issues in this Demurrer but were unable to come to a resolution. (Lewis Decl., ¶¶ 4-5; Gold Decl., ¶¶ 6-7, Exh. 7.) The Court finds that the meet and confer requirement has been met.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant provides the following context regarding this action: On April 21, 2005, the parties purchased the Subject Property during their marriage as joint tenants. (RJN, Exh. E.) On October 19, 2022, Plaintiff filed a petition for dissolution of marriage in Los Angeles County Superior Court under Case No. 22WHFL01798 (hereinafter, “Dissolution Matter”). (RJN, Exh. B.) On February 1, 2023, the family court entered the Judgment of Dissolution (RJN, Exh. D.) On December 1, 2025, Plaintiff filed a request for temporary emergency orders in the Dissolution Matter seeking an order shortening time to divide the Subject Property. The Court denied the request, ruling that Plaintiff has not “articulated reason why the request could not be made through noticed motion.” (RJN, Exh. F.) On December 4, 2025, Plaintiff filed the same request, and it was denied again. (RJN, Exh. G.) Plaintiff filed this partition action on January 5, 2026.

 

Defendant makes three primary arguments in this Demurrer: 1) this action is barred by Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b), 2) the family court that adjudicated the Dissolution Matter retains exclusive jurisdiction to determine the division of omitted community property pursuant to Family Code section 2556, and 3) this action is an improper motion for reconsideration.

 

The Court will address each argument in turn.

 

i.             Whether this Action is Barred by CCP § 872.210

 

Defendant argues that this action is barred by Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b), which states the following: “[A]n action between spouses or putative spouses for partition of their community or quasi-community property or their quasi-marital interest in property may not be commenced or maintained under this title.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.210, subd. (b).)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that this action is not an attempt to adjudicate the parties’ marital interests in the Subject Property outside of family court. Rather, Plaintiff seeks to adjudicate the parties’ interest in the property as joint tenants after the conclusion of their divorce. Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b) does not apply here because the parties are no longer spouses.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff. Because the parties are no longer spouses, this action is not an “action between spouses” for purposes of Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b). Thus, this action is not barred by that section.

 

ii.            Jurisdiction

 

Defendant also asserts that the family court that adjudicated the Dissolution Matter retains exclusive jurisdiction to determine the division of unadjudicated community property, citing Family Code section 2556. In other words, this civil court has no jurisdiction to adjudicate division of the Subject Property.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that, while Family Code section 2556 states that a family court retains “continuing jurisdiction” to adjudicate division of community property after judgment, such jurisdiction is not exclusive. Regardless of whether the family court retained continuing jurisdiction here, under the exclusive jurisdiction rule, when two courts have concurrent jurisdiction over the over the subject matter and parties, the first court to obtain jurisdiction has exclusive and continuing jurisdiction. Here, Plaintiff filed his partition action on January 5, 2026, giving this Court jurisdiction over the Subject Property, and served Defendant on January 10, 2026, giving this Court jurisdiction over Defendant. (Gold Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.) As of the date of this opposition, Defendant has not filed and served a postjudgment motion in the family court action pursuant to Family Code section 2556. (Gold Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, Plaintiff argues that this Court has exclusive jurisdiction over this matter.

 

The Court provides context for Family Code section 2556. Prior to the enactment of section 2556, community estate assets not before the court in a dissolution or otherwise left unadjudicated by the judgment, or “omitted assets”, were subject to later litigation between the parties. (See Henn v. Henn (“Henn”) (1980) 26 Cal.3d 323, 330 [“[U]nder settled principles of California community property law, ‘property which is not mentioned in the pleadings as community property is left unadjudicated by decree of divorce, and is subject to future litigation, the parties being tenants in common meanwhile.’”].) Such independent civil actions—known as “Henn proceedings”—were the only avenue for postjudgment property claims. Acting on criticism that Henn proceedings were expensive and burdensome on judicial resources, the Legislature enacted Family Code section 2556 in 1990 to authorize postjudgment adjudication of “Henn-type” claims in the underlying family law action. (See In re Marriage of Hixson (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 1116, 1121.)

 

Family Code section 2556 states the following:

 

“In a proceeding for dissolution of marriage, for nullity of marriage, or for legal separation of the parties, the court has continuing jurisdiction to award community estate assets or community estate liabilities to the parties that have not been previously adjudicated by a judgment in the proceeding. A party may file a postjudgment motion or order to show cause in the proceeding in order to obtain adjudication of any community estate asset or liability omitted or not adjudicated by the judgment. In these cases, the court shall equally divide the omitted or unadjudicated community estate asset or liability, unless the court finds upon good cause shown that the interests of justice require an unequal division of the asset or liability.”

 

(Fam. Code, § 2556, emphasis added.)

 

Considering the history and language of Family Code section 2556, the Court agrees with Plaintiff’s interpretation. While a family court has continuing jurisdiction to adjudicate division of community property after judgment upon a party’s filing of a postjudgment motion, such jurisdiction is not exclusive. Family Code section 2556 states that a party “may” file such postjudgment motion, not that a party must do so. Family Code section 2556 only authorizes “an alternative procedure” to accomplish the object of an action authorized under Henn. (11 Witkin, Summary 11th Com. Prop § 258 (2026). Thus, the Court finds that the family court’s continuing jurisdiction under Family Code section 2556 does not necessarily bar this Henn-type action.

 

Moreover, under the rule of exclusive concurrent jurisdiction, “when two [California] superior courts have concurrent jurisdiction over the subject matter and all parties involved in litigation, the first to assume jurisdiction has exclusive and continuing jurisdiction over the subject matter and all parties involved until such time as all necessarily related matters have been resolved.” (People ex rel. Garamendi v. American Autoplan, Inc. (1993) 20 Cal.App.4th 760, 769-70.) Here, there is no pending postjudgment motion in the family court. (Gold Decl., ¶ 9.) Plaintiff filed this action and served Defendant, through which the Court obtained jurisdiction over the Subject Property and Defendant. (Gold Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.) Thus, this Court has exclusive jurisdiction over this matter.

 

Thus, the Demurrer cannot be sustained on these grounds.

 

iii.           Improper Motion for Reconsideration

 

Defendant argues that this action is an improper motion for reconsideration pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008. Specifically, Defendant asserts that Plaintiff’s filing this partition action effectively seeks improper reconsideration of the divorce court’s denial of Plaintiff’s December 1, 2025, and December 4, 2025, emergency requests for orders shortening time for a hearing to divide the subject property. Defendant requests sanctions in the amount of $8,000.00 under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (d).

 

In opposition, Plaintiff asserts that this action is not a motion for reconsideration. Plaintiff’s previous ex partes in the family court sought an order shortening time to hold a hearing to divide the Subject Property, and the family court denied the ex partes on procedural grounds. There was no adjudication on the merits of any postjudgment motion. In other words, there is no order for this Court to reconsider. Further, motions for reconsideration must be heard in the same court in front of the same judge.

 

Relatedly, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s request for sanctions is procedurally and substantively deficient. The request is procedurally deficient because Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 sanctions, which are authorized by Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (d), must be filed in a separate motion. The request is substantively deficient because Plaintiff has committed no violation of Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, as explained previously.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff. Plaintiff has filed no improper motion for reconsideration that warrants sanctions. As discussed in the prior section, Plaintiff’s partition action is valid. No postjudgment motion under Family Code section 2556 is pending or has been ruled upon in the family court.

 

Thus, the Court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (d).

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the Demurrer is overruled.
