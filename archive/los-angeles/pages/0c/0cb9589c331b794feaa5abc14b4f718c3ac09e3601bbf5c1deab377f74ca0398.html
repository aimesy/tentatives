            This is a Lemon Law action based on an alleged Engine Defect.

            Defendant General Motors, LLC moves to compel Plaintiff’s compliance with Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1)(deposition) and requests the imposition of sanctions.

TENTATIVE RULING

            Defendant General Motors LLC’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s deposition pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1) and request for sanctions is DENIED.

ANALYSIS

Motion To Compel Deposition

            The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

            Defendant General Motors, LLC moves to compel Plaintiff’s deposition pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1), which provides: 

(c) Within 120 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties have the right to conduct initial depositions, each not to exceed two hours, of the following deponents:

 

(1) The plaintiff.


     (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1).)

            Defendant moves for monetary sanctions in the sum of $1,500 pursuant to Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(j)(2).

            On July 18, 2025, Defendant filed a demurrer to the Complaint. As such, Defendant had the right to conduct Plaintiff’s deposition by November 17, 2025. Of note, Defendant had initially noticed Plaintiff’s deposition for August 5, 2025. (Declaration of Nathan Rodriguez, ¶ 5; Exh. B.) On August 5, 2025,  Plaintiff’s counsel objected to the deposition date as having been unilaterally set and indicated meet and confer efforts would occur to schedule a mutually convenient date. (Id., ¶ 6; Exh. C.)

On December 22, 2025, counsel for the Plaintiff offered January 28, 2026 as an available date for Plaintiff’s deposition. GM replied and confirmed the proposed date and time for deposition. (Rodriguez Decl., ¶ 7; Exh. D.) Notably, Defendant does not present efforts to take the deposition prior to November 17, 2025, instead waiting until December 22, 2025 for Plaintiff’s counsel to offer January 28, 2026 as a deposition date. (Id.)

Indeed, while Defendant’s counsel does not mention this, Plaintiff’s counsel states as follows:

5. On or about November 7, 2025, Plaintiff sent an email with a deposition offer date of December 29, 2026 [sic]. On or about November 20, 2026, Defendant responded by rejecting the offered date (attached hereto as [sic] Exhibit 3).

     (Declaration of Jana Perks, ¶ 5 [bold emphasis added].)

            Thus, Defendant rejected the December 29, 2025 date on November 20, 2025—which was after the November 17, 2025 deadline for Defendant to take Plaintiff’s deposition pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1). Defendant did not file and serve the instant motion until February 4, 2026.

            Under the foregoing circumstances, the Court finds that Defendant waived the right to compel Plaintiff to sit for his deposition pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1) by failing to diligently attempt to procure a deposition date before November 17, 2025. Defendant may resort to the general civil procedure rules governing depositions to obtain Plaintiff’s deposition.

            Defendant General Motors LLC’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s deposition pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(c)(1) and request for sanctions is DENIED.
