Case Number: 23STLC04408    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Thurs., July 16, 2026                                      JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           Aspire General Insurance Company v. Ross 

COMP. FILED:         07-12-23

CASE NUMBER:      23STLC04408                       

NOTICE:                   OK                                                                 

                                               

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION TO SET ASIDE AND VACATE JUDGMENT

 

MOVING PARTY:   Defendant Okeem Smith Ross

RESP. PARTY:         Plaintiff Aspire General Insurance Company

 

MOTION TO SET ASIDE AND VACATE DEFAULT AND DEFAULT JUDGMENT

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 473(d), 473.5)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court DENIES Okeem Smith Ross’s motion to set aside the default and default judgment entered against him.

 

The Judicial Assistant will give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          Filed July 2, 2026                                           [   ] Late                      [  ] None

REPLY:                     Filed July 7, 2026                                           [   ] Late                      [  ] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Aspire General Insurance Company obtained a default judgment against Okeem Smith Ross.  Ross now moves to have the judgment and underlying default set aside under either Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d)—because the service was improper—or Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5—because proper service did not result in actual notice.

 

            Start with Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d).  After Aspire made diligent attempts to serve Ross by other methods, this Court granted an application for publication.  Aspire then filed proof of publication in the Southwest Wave, demonstrating that publication was made at least once a week for four successive weeks in February and March 2025.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 415.50 [service can be done in this way when it appears to the court that the defendant cannot with reasonable diligence be served in another manner].) 

 

Nowhere does Ross’s motion dispute the validity of the publication.  He argues only that “[t]he [p]roof of [s]ervice appears inaccurate and contains irregularities, including alleged service by mail at an address where [Ross] does not receive mail.”  Ross never explains what the purported irregularities are, or why an “alleged service by mail” is relevant to Aspire’s service by publication.  Without any real explanation of what was supposedly wrong with the service in this case, Ross cannot successfully attack the judgment as void under section 473, subdivision (d).

 

            To prevail under Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, Ross must demonstrate that, despite proper service, he did not receive actual notice in time to defend the action—and that his failure to answer was not the result of avoiding service or of his inexcusable neglect.  (Id. § 473.5, subds. (a), (c); see Rios v. Singh (2021) 65 Cal.App.5th 871, 885 [burden of proof is on the party invoking section 473.5].)  There is no evidence going to either of those points. 

 

Aspire, on the other hand, presented evidence that it attempted to serve Ross personally at 912 W. 66th Street, Los Angeles, California 90044 before it obtained an order to serve by publication.  That is the same address that Ross has placed on his moving papers, but when Aspire attempted to serve him personally at that address, an occupant told its process server that no one with Ross’s name lived there.

 

And all of that to one side, Ross fails to attach a proposed answer as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, subdivision (b). 

 

So section 473.5 also offers Ross no relief from the default judgment in this case.

 

            Ross advances several other arguments in his motion, but if anything, they go to the merits of the underlying dispute.  They do not go to whether a default and default judgment were entered appropriately.

 

            The Court DENIES Ross’s motion to set aside the default and default judgment entered against him.

 

            The Judicial Assistant will give notice.

MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

(CCP § 2031.300)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

            The Court DENIES John Jenkins’s motion to compel responses to demand for inspection and request for sanctions.

 

            Jenkins is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 


[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK


OPPOSITION:          None filed as of July 13, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

REPLY:                     None filed as of July 13, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

 


ANALYSIS:

 

            John Jenkins sued Mary C. Castillo.  In April 2025, he served Castillo with a subpoena duces tecum.  Castillo failed to respond, and Jenkins now moves to compel responses pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300.

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300 governs the enforcement of discovery requests for the production of documents.  It does not govern the enforcement of a subpoena duces tecum.  Jenkins does not provide authority for the Court to compel responses to the subpoena served on Castillo.  Additionally, Jenkins provided no copy of the subpoena for the Court to review. 

 

            The Court DENIES Jenkin’s motion and request for sanctions.

 

            Jenkins is to give notice.

PETITION TO REMOVE MECHANIC’S LIEN

(Civ. Code § 8460, et seq.)

            The Court GRANTS Wanda Scott’s petition to release the lien.

REPLY:                     None filed as of July 13, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Wanda Scott petitions this Court to release property located at 8365 Fullbright Avenue, Winnetka, California 91306 from a mechanics lien that Voge, Inc. recorded in February 2025.

 

After a mechanics lien has been recorded, “[t]he owner of property or the owner of any interest in property subject to a claim of lien may petition the court for an order to release the property from the claim of lien if the claimant has not commenced an action to enforce the lien within the time provided in Section 8460.”  (Civ. Code, § 8480, subd. (a).)  A claimant must commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days of recording it, otherwise the claim of the lien expires and is unenforceable. (Civ. Code, § 8460, subd. (a).) 

 

Scott has properly complied with the service requirements, petition allegation requirements, and demand requirements.  (Civ. Code, §§ 8108, 8482, 8484, 8486.)  Voge has failed to file an opposition to find otherwise.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Scott’s petition to release the lien.

 

Scott is to give notice.
