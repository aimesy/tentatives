[Tentative] Order RE: MOTION TO ENFORCE THE STIPULATED JUDGMENT BY ENTERING JUDGMENT FOR MONEY

 

 

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                Plaintiff Mark Hathaway

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       None.

The Court considered the moving papers filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

            On December 26, 2025, Plaintiff Mark Hathaway (“Plaintiff”) filed this unlawful detainer action against Defendant Charles White (“Defendant”).

            On April 28, 2026, the parties lodged notice of a joint stipulation and judgment with the Court.

            On June 25, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to enforce the stipulated judgment.

            As of July 29, 2026, Defendant has not filed an opposition.

LEGAL STANDARD

            “If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (a).)¿

A 664.6 motion to enforce a settlement involves (1) examining if there is an enforceable settlement, (2) interpreting the settlement terms, and (3)¿determining¿the rights of the parties. (Estate of Jones¿(2022) 82 Cal.App.5th 948, 952.) In a motion to enforce a settlement agreement, the Court may interpret the settlement terms and conditions, but it cannot impose terms to which the parties did not agree.¿(Ibid.)¿Strict compliance with the statutory requirements is necessary before a court can enforce a settlement agreement under this statute. (Sully-Miller Contracting Co. v.¿Gledson/Cashman Construction, Inc.¿(2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 30, 37.) Thus, to enforce a written settlement agreement under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, the following three elements must be met: (1) the parties must have come to a meeting of the minds on all material points; (2) there must be a writing that contains the material terms of the agreement; and (3) the writing must be signed by the parties. (Weddington¿Productions, Inc. v. Flick¿(1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 793, 797-98.) “Although a judge hearing a¿section 664.6¿motion may receive evidence, determine disputed¿facts, and enter the terms of a settlement agreement as a judgment, nothing in¿section 664.6¿authorizes a judge to¿create¿the material terms of a settlement, as opposed to deciding what terms¿the parties themselves¿have¿previously agreed upon.”¿ (Id. at p. 810 [internal citations omitted, emphasis in original].)¿ 

DISCUSSION

            Plaintiff requests entry of judgment against Defendant in the amount of $57,439.34. Under¿Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, subdivision (a), the Court may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. 

            Here, the Unlawful Detainer Stipulated Judgment (the “Agreement”) included the following terms:

(1)   Defendant agreed to vacate the property by May 16, 2026;

(2)   Defendant agreed to remove all personal property and all persons;

(3)   Defendant agreed to leave the premises without any malicious damage;

(4)   Defendant agreed to dismiss LASC Case No. 24NNCV0424 with prejudice within 7 days of the Stipulated Judgment agreement, no later than May 5, 2026;

(5)   Plaintiff agreed to pay Defendant $20,000.00 in relocation assistance, by cashier’s check payable to Defendant, to be sent by certified mail to the Defendant’s attorney no later than May 5, 2026.

(Hathaway Decl., ¶ 4, Exh. 1.)

             The Agreement is signed by both Plaintiff and Defendant. (Hathaway Decl., Exh. 1.) Plaintiff sent a cashier’s check for $20,000.00 to Defendant’s counsel on April 30, 2026, for the relocation assistance. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 5, Exhs. 2-3.) However, Defendant did not dismiss the lawsuit until May 18, 2026. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. 4.) Defendant vacated the property without notice before May 16, 2026, without removing all personal property. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 6.) Further, Defendant damaged the property significantly. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 7.) Defendant’s damage included smearing the walls, windows, and floors with rotten food and stains, smearing peanut butter into stair railings, banisters, and an electrical breaker panel, gluing shut the freezer and sliding doors, ripping open custom cushions, and removing the built-in kitchen television. (Hathaway Decl., ¶¶ 8-10, 13-18, Exh. 5.) Defendant also blocked the property’s roof drains, causing water leaks into the property. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 19.) Plaintiff spent $19,840.43 to remediate the property, exclusive of repairs and mold remediation caused by the blocked roof drains. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 20.)

            The Court finds that the statutory requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 are met. Plaintiff introduces the Agreement, signed by both parties, demonstrating the parties’ meeting of the minds. Defendant has not filed an opposition arguing otherwise. “A failure to oppose a motion may be deemed a consent to the granting of a motion.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.54(c).) Further, Plaintiff demonstrates that he paid the relocation assistance required of him pursuant to the Agreement, while Defendant did not timely dismiss this action and remove all personal property.  Notably, Defendant left considerable, malicious damages.

            Plaintiff requests the Court enter a monetary judgment in the amount of $57,439.34. The total cost to remediate Defendant’s extensive damage was $19,840.43. (Hathaway Decl., ¶ 20.) Plaintiff attaches invoices, receipts, and Chase banking statements reflecting the individual costs for junk removal, professional cleaning services, sliding door repair, and carpet and wood floor replacements. (Hathaway Decl., Exhs. 6-9.) Plaintiff also requests the Court award past-due rent in the amount of $6,794.91 and holdover damages in the amount of $30,804.00.

The Agreement expressly states, “Defendant agrees to leave the premises without any malicious damages.” (Hathaway Decl., Exh. 1.) The Agreement also states that Plaintiff and Defendant have “notice and opportunity to be heard on the issue of any… alleged violation of the conditions of staying the enforcement of the judgment. We give up these rights and freely agree that judgment may be entered against us in accordance with this stipulation.” (Hathaway Decl., Exh. 1.) The Court finds that Defendant’s malicious damages are a violation of the Agreement, warranting monetary sanctions and entry of judgment. However, the Agreement expressly provides that past due rent and holdover damages are not included. (See Hathaway Decl., Exh. 1.) Thus, the Court will not award the past-due rent in the amount of $6,794.91 and holdover damages in the amount of $30,804.00.

Therefore, the Court grants Plaintiff’s motion to enforce settlement and enters a monetary judgment in the reduced amount of $19,840.43 for cleanup, removal, and property damage.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff Mark Hathaway’s motion to enforce the stipulated judgment.

The Court enters a monetary judgment against Defendant in the reduced amount of $19,840.43, payable to Plaintiff’s counsel of record within twenty (20) days of this order.

Moving party is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

The Court considered the moving papers filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

On March 29, 2024, Plaintiffs Norik Harutyunyan (“Harutyunyan”) and Garik Aroutiounian (“Aroutiounian”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) brought this action against Defendant Khachik Tarakchyan (“Tarakchyan”), alleging causes of action for (1) motor vehicle negligence and (2) general negligence. The causes of action arise out of a motor vehicle accident occurring on the 134 Freeway near the San Fernando Exit in Glendale, California, wherein Tarakchyan allegedly rear-ended Plaintiffs.

On October 1, 2024, the Court found this action related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a), to case No. 24NNCV02368 and designated the instant matter as the lead case.

On April 8, 2026, Tarakchyan filed the instant motions to compel further responses to Harutyunyan’s Requests for Production of Documents (“RFPs”), Form Interrogatories, Set One (“Form Rogs.”), and Special Interrogatories, Set One (“SROGs”).

Harutyunyan did not file an opposition.

LEGAL STANDARD

A.    Requests for Production

Any party may obtain any discovery of information, documents, land,¿property¿or electronically stored information so long as the discoverable matter is not privileged¿and¿is relevant to the subject matter and can lead one to admissible evidence. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.010.) A party can demand the production of documents that is in the “possession, custody, or control of the party¿on¿whom the demand is made.” (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2031.010, subd. (c).) If a court¿determines¿the scope of discovery imposes a burden, causes great expense or is intrusive, and such outweighs the likelihood that the discoverable information¿sought¿will garner admissible evidence, the court shall limit the scope of discovery. (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2017.020.)¿¿

When responses to demands for production of documents¿are deficient, the propounding party may file a motion to compel further responses. (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2031.310, subd. (a).) The motion to compel further responses may be brought when the responses contain: (1) “a statement of compliance when the demand is incomplete;” (2) “answers that are inadequate, incomplete or evasive;” or (3) “an objection to a particular request is without merit or too general.”¿(Ibid.)¿

A motion to compel further responses must set forth specific facts showing¿good cause justifying the discovery¿sought¿by the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd.¿(b)(1).) “To establish good cause, a discovery proponent must identify a disputed fact that is of consequence in the action and explain how the discovery sought will tend in reason to prove or disprove that fact or lead to other evidence that will tend to prove or disprove the fact.  (Digital Music News LLC v. Superior Court (2014) 226 Cal. App. 4th 216, 224).  A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a meet-and-confer declaration in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(2).)

B.     Interrogatories

Within 30 days after service of interrogatories, the responding party must serve responses unless otherwise stipulated or ordered. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).)

Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.300 provides that a propounding party may move for an order compelling further responses if:

(1) an answer is evasive or incomplete;

(2) the exercise of option to produce documents is unwarranted or inadequate; or

(3) an objection is without merit or too general.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a).)

If a party fails to timely respond, objections are waived unless the court relieves the waiver upon a showing of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect and a subsequently served compliant response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)

A motion to compel further responses must be brought within 45 days of service of verified responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (c); Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1409; Vidal Sassoon, Inc. v. Superior Court (1983) 147 Cal.App.3d 681, 685.)

DISCUSSION

A.    Timeliness

On December 12, 2025, Tarakchyan served the RFPs, SROGS, and Form Rogs. on Harutyunyan. On February 20, 2026, Harutyunyan served responses. Tarakchyan had 45 days, plus two additional days for electronic service (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (a)(3)(B)), to file motions to compel further responses. Tarakchyan timely filed on April 8, 2026.

B.     Separate Statement

Tarakchyan filed separate statements in compliance with rule 3.1345(a).

C.     Meet and Confer

A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration showing a reasonable and good faith effort to resolve the dispute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040; § 2030.300, subd. (b)(2).)

Counsel met and conferred via email on March 2, 2026 and sent a detailed deficiency letter on March 12, 2026. No supplemental responses were provided.

Accordingly, the Court finds the meet and confer requirement satisfied.  

D.    Requests for Production

Tarakchyan seeks further responses to RFPs Nos. 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 15, 21, 23, 27, and 28.

RFP No. 1

RFP No. 1 seeks documents from health care providers who examined, consulted with, or treated Harutyunyan within the five years preceding the subject incident concerning the same body parts Harutyunyan claims were injured in the accident. 

The Court finds the request relevant to Harutyunyan’s claimed injuries and damages. By placing his physical condition at issue, Harutyunyan has waived his privacy rights to the extent necessary to permit discovery of medical information directly relevant to the injuries alleged. The request is appropriately limited in time and subject matter and does not seek Harutyunyan's entire medical history.

Motion granted.

RFP No. 2

            RFP No. 2 seeks billing records and statements for treatment relating to the same body parts allegedly injured in the accident during the preceding five-year period. 

For the same reasons discussed with respect to RFP No. 1, the Court finds the request reasonably tailored and relevant to issues of causation, preexisting condition, and damages. 

Motion granted.

            RFP No. 6

            RFP No. 6 seeks photographs depicting injuries allegedly sustained in the subject accident. 

Harutyunyan responded that he is unable to comply because responsive documents are not within his possession, custody, or control. However, the response does not satisfy the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.230 because it fails to specify whether the documents never existed, have been lost or destroyed, or identify any person or entity believed to possess them. 

Motion granted.

            RFP No. 7

            RFP No. 7 seeks documents pertaining to loss of earnings resulting from the subject accident.

            Harutyunyan responded that the request seeks premature disclosure of expert opinion and attorney work product. The Court finds the request relevant to Harutyunyan’s claimed damages and does not require expert opinion or disclosure of attorney work product.  

            Motion granted.

RFP No. 8

            RFP No. 8 seeks documents pertaining to the claim for loss of earning capacity resulting from the incident.

            Harutyunyan responded that the request seeks premature disclosure of expert opinion and attorney work product. The Court finds the request relevant to Harutyunyan’s claimed damages and does not require expert opinion or disclosure of attorney work product.

            Motion granted.

            RFP No. 9

            RFP No. 9 seeks all documents supporting any missed time from Harutyunyan's employment.

The Court agrees that evidence of lost time from work is relevant to damages. However, the request contains no time limit and is therefore overbroad on its face. Defendant has not demonstrated good cause for production without a reasonable limitation in scope.

Motion denied.

RFP Nos. 12 and 13

RFP Nos. 12 and 13 seek CT scan and MRI reports relating to the same body parts allegedly injured in the accident during the five years preceding the incident. 

The Court finds these requests directly relevant to the issues of causation, preexisting condition, and damages. The requests are narrowly tailored and do not improperly seek unrelated medical information. 

Motion granted.

RFP No. 15

            RFP No. 15 seeks social media postings concerning the subject incident from August 2, 2023, to the present. 

            The Court finds the request reasonably tailored to obtain information concerning Harutyunyan’s allegations, claimed injuries, and the circumstances of the accident. While Harutyunyan maintains a privacy interest in social media content, that interest is limited where the request is narrowly directed to postings concerning the incident itself. Tarakchyan has demonstrated the relevance of such materials, and the request is not unduly burdensome.

            Motion granted.

            RFP No. 21

            RFP No. 21 seeks documents relating to liens asserted by medical providers or governmental entities arising from treatment allegedly necessitated by the accident. 

The Court finds the requested materials relevant to the nature and amount of Harutyunyan’s claimed medical damages and any reimbursement obligations associated with those damages. 

Motion granted.

            RFP No. 23

            RFP No. 23 seeks documents relating to claims submitted to insurance carriers arising from the subject incident. 

The Court finds such materials relevant because they concern the same occurrence at issue in this action and may contain statements, representations, or information concerning Harutyunyan's alleged injuries and damages. 

Motion granted.

            RFP No. 27

            RFP No. 27 seeks documents concerning prior lawsuits, insurance claims, and workers’ compensation claims during the preceding ten years. 

The Court finds the request relevant insofar as prior claims may bear on Harutyunyan's medical history, prior injuries, damages, and credibility. The request is limited to a ten-year period and is not rendered objectionable merely because responsive materials may include information concerning prior injuries. 

Motion granted.

            RFP No. 28

            RFP No. 28 seeks Harutyunyan's W-2 forms and Forms 1099 from 2016 to the present. 

California recognizes a qualified privilege protecting tax-return information. Tarakchyan has not demonstrated that an exception to that privilege applies or that the information cannot be obtained through less intrusive means. The Court therefore finds Harutyunyan's objection well taken. 

Motion denied.

G.    Sanctions

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 authorizes monetary sanctions for misuse of the discovery process.

Sanctions are warranted given the failure to provide proper responses and failure to oppose the motion.

Counsel requests $1,490.00 per motion for the RFPs motion and Form. Rogs. motion and $1,357.50 for the SROGs motion, based on an hourly rate of $220.00 and estimated time of three hours per motion. The Court notes that the $1,357.50 requested for the SROGs motion appears to be a miscalculation. The Court finds the hourly rate reasonable but reduces the time claimed due to overlap between motions and absence of oppositions.

The Court awards sanctions as follows: 3 hours drafting (one hour per motion), 1 hour appearance, and a filing fee of $60.00 for each motion for a total of $1,060.00. Sanctions are imposed jointly and severally against Harutyunyan and counsel of record.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Tarakchyan’s motion to compel further responses to Requests for Production of Documents Nos. 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 12, 13, 15, 21, 23, and 27.

The Court DENIES Tarakchyan’s motion to compel further responses to Requests for Production of Documents Nos. 9 and 28.

The Court GRANTS Tarakchyan’s motion to compel further responses to Special Interrogatories Nos. 2, 6, 7, 10, 14, 15, 18, 21, 22, 23, 24, and 25.

The Court DENIES Tarakchyan’s motion to compel further responses to Special Interrogatories Nos. 3, 5, 8, 9, 11 and 12.

The Court GRANTS Tarakchyan’s motion to compel further responses to Form Interrogatories Nos. 1.1, 2.6, 6.3, 6.6, 6.7, 7.1, 7.3, 8.1, 10.1, 10.3, 11.1, 11.2, 14.1, 20.2, 20.6, 20.9, 20.10, and 20.11.

Harutyunyan shall provide verified further responses without objection, except where specifically sustained, within 20 days of notice of this order. Total monetary sanctions, for all three motions, are imposed against Harutyunyan and his attorney of record, jointly and severally, in the amount of $1,060.00, payable to counsel for Tarakchyan within 20 days of notice of this order.

Moving party is ordered to give notice of this ruling.
