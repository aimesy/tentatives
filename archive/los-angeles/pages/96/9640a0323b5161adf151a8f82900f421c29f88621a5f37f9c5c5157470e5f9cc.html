Case Number: 23AVCV01252    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: A14

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES – NORTH DISTRICT

 

 

ALEXIS GARATE, a minor by and through his Guardian Ad Litem, DIANA PEREZ,

 

Plaintiff, 

v. 

 

MAGUA MAYES II; DATRON KENDRIX JR.; PALMDALE SCHOOL DISTRICT; and DOES 1 through 100, inclusive,

 

Defendants. 

 

  Case Number 23AVCV01252

 

  [TENTATIVE] 

  RULING ON MOTION 

 

 

  Date of Hearing: September 17, 2026

  Dept. A-14 

  Judge William H. Forman 

 

 

      I.          Background

 

Plaintiff’s counsel Angelo F. Campano of Campano Law Group, APC (together “Counsel”) moves the Court to be relieved as counsel.

 

On October 30, 2023, Plaintiff Alexis Garate (“Plaintiff”), a minor by and through his Guardian Ad Litem Diana Perez (“Perez”) filed a complaint against Defendants Magua Mayes II (“Mayes”), Datron Kendrix, Jr. (“Kendrix”), and Palmdale School District (“PSD”) (collectively “Defendants”), asserting eight causes of action for (1) Negligence, (2) Motor Vehicle Negligence, (3) Negligence Per Se (Cal. Veh. Code, § 22350), (4) Negligence Per Se (Cal. Veh. Code, § 27318(c)), (5) Negligence Per Se (Cal. Veh. Code, § 22109), (6) Common Carrier Liability (Civ. Code, § 2100), (7) Negligent Entrustment, and (8) Negligent Hiring, Retention, Supervision, Training.

 

Plaintiff alleges that on September 28, 2022, while a passenger on a school bus owned, controlled, and managed by PSD and operated by Kendrix, a vehicle operated by Mayes struck the school bus while it was at a stop, causing Plaintiff to suffer injury. (Compl., ¶¶ 17-23.)

 

On January 17, 2025, Plaintiff’s Counsel orally informed the Court that the case has settled at the FSC.

 

On January 28, 2026, Mayes filed his Answer and demanded a jury trial.

 

On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case and the Court set an OSC hearing re dismissal and order of dismissal for April 2, 2026. The OSC was subsequently continued and set for September 17, 2026.

 

On August 18, 2026, Counsel filed the present motion to be relieved. No opposition has been filed.

 

-----

 

    II.          Legal Standard

 

Standard for Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – The Court has discretion to permit an attorney to withdraw from representation, and such a motion should be granted if there is no undue prejudice to the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 prescribes the following requirements of a movant seeking to be relieved as counsel: (1) The notice of motion and motion must be directed to the client using form MC-051 (“Notice of Motion and Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”); (2) The motion must be accompanied by a declaration using form MC-052 (“Declaration in Support of Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”) and “must state in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1).”; (3) A proposed order must be lodged with the court along with the moving papers and must use form MC-053 (“Order Granting Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”) and “must specify all hearing dates scheduled in the action or proceeding, including the date of trial, if known. If no hearing date is presently scheduled, the court may set one and specify the date in the order.”; (4) “The notice of motion and motion, the declaration, and the proposed order must be served on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case” either by personal service, electronic service, or by mail.

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a) – (e).)  

 

“If the notice is served on the client by mail under Code of Civil Procedure section 1013, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating facts showing that either: (A) the service address is the current residence or business address of the client; or (B) The service address is the last known residence or business address of the client and the attorney has been unable to locate a more current address after making reasonable efforts to do so within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d)(1).) “‘[C]urrent’ means that the address was confirmed within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved. Merely demonstrating that the notice was sent to the client’s last known address and was not returned…is not, by itself, sufficient to demonstrate that the address is current.” (Id at rule 3.1362(d).)

 

-----

 

 III.          Discussion

 

Application – Counsel moves to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff. Counsel has substantially complied with the statutory requirements of Rule 3.1362 and has filed an MC-051 that contains all required information, is directed to Plaintiff, and was served via standard and certified mailing. Counsel has also provided an MC-052 form, declaring that there has been a breakdown in the attorney-client relationship such that Counsel can no longer provide effective and adequate legal counsel to Plaintiff, that Plaintiff has failed to maintain meaningful and reliable contact with Counsel despite Counsel’s requests for information and assistance in finalizing the matter, and as a result, Plaintiff has caused Counsel to needlessly expend significant time and incur needless expenses and resources. Attached to the MC-052 is a Declaration of Counsel Angelo F. Campano, who asserts that between January 27, 2026 and August 17, 2026, Counsel has not received any meaningful response, or any response at all, from Plaintiff through Perez, and details multiple dates of attempted communications and the results of those attempts. Campano attaches appropriately redacted evidence of some of their communication efforts, and states that despite these efforts, Counsel is unable to prepare for trial, which would result in prejudice to Plaintiff. The MC-052 also states Counsel has confirmed Plaintiff’s last known address is current within the previous 30 days by mail, return receipt requested.

 

Finally, Counsel has provided an MC-053 Order form, though the form does not include Plaintiff’s current address and telephone number, or the information required regarding upcoming hearings and trial information. Nonetheless, most of this information has been adequately provided on the MC-052 form, and the Court finds Counsel’s substantial compliance sufficient for granting the motion.

 

The Court also finds minimal prejudice to Plaintiff in granting this motion. It appears the case has settled as to at least some Defendants, with dismissal pending due to the involvement of a minor’s compromise that may require special arrangements be made for any minor trust to be opened, and because Perez has not responded to Counsel’s efforts to obtain medical records to assist in the determination of what, if any, account would be required for Plaintiff Garate. (See Campano Decl., 06/26/26.) It seems, therefore, that finalization of the settlement is contingent on information in Perez’s possession and control, and Plaintiff therefore has the opportunity and ability to move forward in finalization of the settlement without counsel. Moreover, Perez has the opportunity to retain subsequent counsel if she so chooses to finalize settlement. As to Defendant Mayes, no trial date has been set, no hearings are on calendar, Plaintiff may proceed in propria persona or retain subsequent counsel, and Plaintiff will not be prejudiced in the granting of this motion. Finally, the only hearing on calendar, aside from the present motion, is an OSC re Dismissal (Settlement).

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s motion to be relieved is GRANTED. Counsel shall provide Plaintiff’s contact information, if known, in accordance with the Court’s final ruling within 48 hours of this Order.

 

-----

 

 IV.          Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s counsel Angelo F. Campano of Campano Law Group, APC’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel is GRANTED. 

 

Angelo F. Campano and Campano Law Group, APC shall given notice to Plaintiff and all other parties. Further, Campano shall provide Plaintiff’s last known telephone number(s), if any is known, to the Court and Defendants’ counsel within 48 hours of this hearing to be included on the final MC-053 Order. If no telephone number for Plaintiff is known, Campano shall provide Plaintiff’s electronic mailing address to the same within 48 hours of this Order. Otherwise, Campano shall confirm with the Court and Defendants’ counsel that no telephone number, electronic mailing address, or other method of contact for Plaintiff is known within 48 hours of this Order. 


Case Number: 25AVCV01343    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: A14

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES – NORTH DISTRICT

 

 

DORENE MENAHERNANDEZ,

 

Plaintiff, 

v. 

 

BAGGETT TRANSPORTATION COMPANY, a Corporation; LOCKHEED MARTIN CORPORATION; and DOES 1 through 30, inclusive,

 

Defendants. 

 

  Case Number 25AVCV01343

 

  [TENTATIVE] 

  RULING ON MOTION 

 

 

  Date of Hearing: September 17, 2026

  Dept. A-14 

  Judge William H. Forman 

 

 

      I.          Background

 

Defendant Baggett Transportation Company (“Baggett”) moves the Court for leave to file a Cross-Complaint.

 

On October 10, 2025, Plaintiff Dorene MenaHernandez (“Plaintiff”) filed her form Complaint against Defendants Baggett and Lockheed Martin Corporation (“Lockheed Martin”) (together “Defendants”), asserting one cause of action for General Negligence. Plaintiff alleges that she was lawfully on the premises of Lockheed Martin, located at 1011 Lockheed Way, Palmdale, CA 93599 (the “Facility”) on October 16, 2023, when she was exposed to harmful chemicals through negligent packaging, disclosure, warnings, and practices while performing her duties as a security guard, causing Plaintiff to sustain injuries.

 

On November 12, 2025, Lockheed Martin filed its Answer.

 

On December 15, 2025, Bagget filed its Answer.

 

On August 18, 2026, Baggett filed its Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint. Both Lockheed Martin and Plaintiff have filed separate Statements of Non-Opposition to the motion.

 

-----

 

    II.          Legal Standard

 

Standard for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint – A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint may file a cross-complaint setting forth any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him, or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the case brought against him. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (b).)

 

“A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (a).) “Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (b).) A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint not filed before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or not filed at any time before the Court has set a date for trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (c).) “A party who fails to plead a cause of action subject to the requirements of this article, whether through oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause, may apply to the court for leave to…file a cross-complaint, to assert such cause at any time during the course of the action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.)

 

Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (c).) Where the proposed cross-complaint arises out of the same transaction as the plaintiff’s claim, the court must grant leave to file the cross-complaint so long as the defendant is acting in good faith and all other statutory requirements have been satisfied, and leave shall be liberally granted to avoid forfeiture of causes of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50; see also Silver Orgs. V. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 99 [“motion to file a cross-complaint at any time during the course of the action must be granted unless bad faith of the moving party is demonstrated where forfeiture would otherwise result,” and “[f]actors such as oversight, inadvertence, neglect, mistake or other cause, are insufficient grounds to deny the motion unless accompanied by bad faith”].)

 

-----

 

 III.          Discussion

 

Application – Baggett moves for leave to file a Cross-Complaint against Haas Group International LLC (“Haas”) and Wesco Aircraft Hardware Corp., dba Incora (“Incora”) pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50, subdivision (c) on the grounds that 1) the proposed Cross-Complaint arises out of the same transaction or occurrence as does Plaintiff’s Complaint against Defendants and 2) allowing such filing is in the interests of justice and will promote the efficient resolution of claims between the parties. (Motion, p. 1:26-2:3.) This action is set for trial on February 26, 2027.

 

Baggett asserts that it never received notice from Lockheed Martin indicating the subject shipment sent by Baggett was damaged or compromised in any way, that photographs taken on October 16, 2023 contain no indication that the shipment was damaged or compromised, and that Baggett was unaware of Plaintiff’s claim for injuries and damages arising from the incident until the filing of the complaint. (Motion, p. 3:9-19; Schuler Decl., ¶¶ 4-6.) Since Baggett became aware of Plaintiff’s action, the parties have exchanged written discovery responses and documents, which indicate the shipment sent by Baggett did not appear damaged or to have spilled, leaked, seeped, or otherwise escaped their packaging to be able to cause exposure. (Motion, p. 3:20-27; Schuler Decl., ¶¶ 8-9.)

 

Baggett asserts Plaintiff produced photographs of the contents of Baggett’s trailer that show the company name “HAAS” on one of the containers, and on March 17, 2026, Baggett identified and produced documents showing Incora was the shipper of the contents that Baggett delivered to Lockheed Martin on October 16, 2023. (Motion, p. 3:27-4:4; Schuler Decl., ¶¶ 9-10.) Since this discovery exchange, Baggett has continued fact investigation and discovery to discovery theories on how the alleged exposure could have occurred and who many be responsible, and based on its findings, Baggett is informed and believes that Haas is the manufacturer of certain contents in Baggett’s trailer on October 16, 2023, and Incora was the shipper of such contents to the Facility, thereby identifying both entities as potentially responsible parties. (Motion, p. 4:4-12.)

 

Baggett seeks leave to file a Cross-Complaint to assert causes of action for (1) Equitable Indemnity, (2) Contribution, and (3) Declaratory Relief against Haas and Wesco based on their potential liability for the alleged exposure. (Schuler Decl., Ex. A.) Baggett’s counsel asserts he spoke with counsel for Plaintiff and Co-Defendant Lockheed Martin, that both counsel advised they would not oppose the motion, and both have since filed statements of non-opposition. (Schuler Decl., ¶ 14.)

 

Baggett’s proposed Cross-Complaint arises from the same transaction or occurrence as Plaintiff’s underlying Complaint, being her alleged exposure to harmful chemicals. The Court also finds that Baggett is acting in good faith, requesting leave to file following exchange of discovery leading to the information necessary for discovering Haas and Incora’s respective potential liability for the incident underlying the Cross-Complaint. Plaintiff does not oppose the filing of the Cross-Complaint, and Baggett appears to have moved in a non-dilatory and reasonable manner in moving for leave.

 

Accordingly, Baggett’s motion is GRANTED.

 

The Court notes that page 4 of the proposed Cross-Complaint appears to have inadvertently been attached out of order as the last page of the Cross-Complaint. Baggett shall ensure the Cross-Complaint is properly formatted and file the proposed Cross-Complaint within five (5) days of this Order.

 

-----

 

 IV.          Conclusion

 

Defendant Baggett Transportation Company’s Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint is GRANTED. Defendant Baggett Transportation Company shall file the Proposed Cross-Complaint within five (5) days of this Order.


Case Number: 26AVCV00127    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: A14

SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES – NORTH DISTRICT

 

 

SHAREEMAH TIPLER, an individual and as Guardian Ad Litem for TIBERIUS RAY, a minor,

 

Plaintiffs, 

v. 

 

ROLL LANCASTER LLC, a California limited liability company; GATELY INDUSTRIES, INC., a California Corporation; EARL GATELY, an individual; and DOES 1 through 10, inclusive,

 

Defendants. 

 

  Case Number 26AVCV00127

 

  [TENTATIVE] 

  RULING ON MOTION 

 

 

  Date of Hearing: September 17, 2026

  Dept. A-14 

  Judge William H. Forman 

 

 

      I.          Background

 

Plaintiff Shareemah Tipler (“Plaintiff”) moves for leave to file a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”).

 

On January 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed her Complaint as an individual and as Guardian Ad Litem for minor Tiberius Ray (“Tiberius”) against Defendants Roll Lancaster LLC (“Roll”), Gately Industries, Inc. (“Gately Industries”), and Earl Gately (“Gately”) (collectively “Defendants”), asserting seven causes of action for (1) Collection of Rent on Substandard Dwelling (Civ. Code, § 1942.5), (2) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Habitability, (3) Breach of the Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment, (4) Negligence, (5) Violation of Los Angeles Municipal Code § 45.33, (6) Negligence Per Se, and (7) Retaliatory Eviction.

 

The Complaint alleges that Plaintiff has been a bona fide tenant residing at the real property located at 841 West Avenue J-9, Lancaster, CA 93534 (the “Property”), which is owned, leased, and/or managed by Defendants. (Compl., ¶ 2.) Plaintiff alleges a number of substandard living conditions existed on the Property, including exposure to raw sewage, sewage vapors, plumbing failures, and unsanitary conditions exposing Plaintiff and her minor children to health and safety risks, resulted in medical symptoms, and required medical evaluation and treatment. (Compl., ¶ 3-4.) Plaintiff also alleges that her minor child, two year old Ray, was also diagnosed with Rhino Virus multiple times after exposure to said conditions on the Property, and Plaintiff and her children had to reside in hotels elsewhere after medical providers advised the environmental exposure and unsanitary living conditions posed heightened risks to Plaintiff’s condition and to Ray’s respiratory health. (Compl., ¶¶ 4-5.)

 

On March 23, 2026, Gately and Gately Industries filed their Answer.

 

On May 29, 2026, following a CMC, the Court referred the parties to mediation, to be completed by December 4, 2026.

 

On August 6 and 13, 2026, the Court granted Orders for Appointment of Plaintiff as Guardian Ad Litem for minor children Tyson Ray (“Tyson”), Tyrone Butterfield (“Tyrone”), and Tobias Ray (“Tobias”).

 

On August 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed her Motion for Leave to File an FAC. The motion is unopposed.

 

-----

 

    II.          Legal Standard

Standard for Leave to Amend Complaint – Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a)(1) provides, in relevant part: “The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by adding or striking out the name of any party, or by correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect; and may, upon like terms, enlarge the time for answer or demurrer. The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” This discretion should be exercised liberally in favor of amendments, for “judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters in the same lawsuit.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047.)

“The trial court has discretion to permit or deny the amendment of the complaint, but instances justifying the court's denial of leave to amend are rare.” (Armenta ex rel. City of Burbank v. Mueller Co.¿(2006) 142¿Cal.App.4th¿636, 642.)¿ However, “[a]lthough courts are bound to apply a policy of great liberality in permitting amendments to the complaint at any stage of the proceedings, up to and including trial [citation], this policy should be applied only ‘[w]here no prejudice is shown to the adverse party . . .’ [Citation.] A different result is indicated ‘[w]here¿inexcusable delay and probable prejudice to the opposing party’ is shown. [Citation.]” (Magpali¿v. Farmers Group, Inc.¿(1996) 48¿Cal.App.4th¿471, 487.) “If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend.” (Morgan v. Superior Court of Los Angeles County (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.) Prejudice includes “delay in trial, loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation.” (Solit¿v. Tokai Bank, Ltd. New York Branch¿(1999) 68 Cal.App.4th 1435, 1448.) 

Under rule 3.1324, subdivision (a) of the California Rules of Court, a motion to amend a pleading shall (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) state what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a).)

Under subdivision (b), a separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Id., subd. (b).)

 

-----

 

 III.          Discussion

 

Application – Plaintiff moves for leave to file an FAC pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 473, subdivision (a)(1) and 576 on the grounds that Plaintiff seeks to effect the following amendments to the Complaint: (1) add three additional minor Plaintiffs, Tyson, Tobias, and Tyrone, each already identified in the Complaint as members of the household residing at the Property and whose claims arise from the same course of conduct already alleged, (2) correct the properly registered legal name of Defendant Role Lancaster LLC, which was inadvertently misspelled in the Complaint’s caption, (3) correct a typographical error in the First Cause of Action as arising under Civil Code section 1942.5 when it in fact arises under Civil Code section 1942.4, (4) remove the Fifth Cause of Action for Violation of the Los Angeles Municipal Code section 45.33, which does not extend to real property located in the City of Lancaster, and (5) consolidate the stand-alone Sixth Cause of Action for Negligence in recognition that negligence per se operates as an evidentiary presumption under Evidence Code section 669 rather than an independent cause of action. (Motion, p. 2:11-25.)

 

Plaintiff has substantially complied with California Rules of Court rule 3.1324, subdivision (a). Plaintiff has provided a copy of the proposed FAC, which is serially numbered as the “First Amended Complaint,” and states what allegations and contents in the Complaint are to be modified, added, and deleted. (Grigoryan Decl., Ex. 1.) Plaintiff does not provide a redlined copy of the proposed FAC or identify exactly by page, paragraph, and line number the allegations to be amended, deleted or added. However, the motion and the Declaration of Chris Grigoryan, Esq. make clear to the Court and Defendants exactly what changes are proposed, and generally where such changes are located in the Complaint. The Grigoryan Declaration also puts forth all required information under Rule 3.1324, subdivision (b), specifying the effect of the amendment (Grigoryan Decl., ¶ 7), why it is necessary and proper (Grigoryan Decl., ¶¶ 8-12), when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered (Grigoryan Decl., ¶ 13), and the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier (Grigoryan Decl., ¶ 14).

 

Finally, as Plaintiff notes, Defendant Role Lancaster has not yet filed a responsive pleading, discovery is in its early stages and no cutoff has been set, no trial date has been set, no dispositive motions have been filed, and the case is in its very early stages. These amendments seek only to add named Plaintiffs already identified in the operative initial Complaint, causes of action on behalf of those Plaintiffs arising from the same course of conduct and facts underlying the Complaint, correct typographical errors to clarify existing causes of action, and to remove or consolidate existing causes of action asserted against Defendants. The Court also notes the motion is unopposed. Based on these circumstances and the amendments sought, there is no cognizable prejudice to any Defendant that would result from granting leave to amend.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is GRANTED. Plaintiff to file the proposed FAC within five (5) days of this Order.

 

-----

 

 IV.          Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Shareemah Tipler, individually and as Guardian Ad Litem for Tiberius Ray’s Motion for Leave to File a First Amended Complaint  is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff shall file the proposed First Amended Complaint within five (5) days of this Order.

      I.          Background

 

Plaintiff’s counsel Mindy S. Bish of Bish Law, APC (together “Counsel”) moves the Court to be relieved as counsel.

 

On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff Youlander Yvette Pressey (“Plaintiff”) filed her form complaint against Defendants LA Darts Properties, LLC, Darts Properties Inc., and Sunrise Lancaster Apartments (collectively “Defendants”), asserting one cause of action for Premises Liability. Plaintiff alleges that she was residing in her apartment located at 1243 Meadow View Lane, Apartment 7, Lancaster, CA 93534, where she had experienced ongoing water leak and plumbing issues since 2022, when her ceiling caved in, causing Plaintiff to slip and fall on water debris, slammed her head, and sustained injuries.

 

On May 1, 2026, Defendants filed an answer.

 

On July 22, 2026, the Court held a CMC and referred the parties to mediation, to be completed by March 22, 2027.

 

On August 12, 2026, Counsel moved to be relieved as counsel of record for Plaintiff. No opposition has been filed.

 

-----

 

    II.          Legal Standard

 

Standard for Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – The Court has discretion to permit an attorney to withdraw from representation, and such a motion should be granted if there is no undue prejudice to the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 prescribes the following requirements of a movant seeking to be relieved as counsel: (1) The notice of motion and motion must be directed to the client using form MC-051 (“Notice of Motion and Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”); (2) The motion must be accompanied by a declaration using form MC-052 (“Declaration in Support of Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”) and “must state in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1).”; (3) A proposed order must be lodged with the court along with the moving papers and must use form MC-053 (“Order Granting Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil”) and “must specify all hearing dates scheduled in the action or proceeding, including the date of trial, if known. If no hearing date is presently scheduled, the court may set one and specify the date in the order.”; (4) “The notice of motion and motion, the declaration, and the proposed order must be served on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case” either by personal service, electronic service, or by mail.

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a) – (e).)  

 

“If the notice is served on the client by mail under Code of Civil Procedure section 1013, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating facts showing that either: (A) the service address is the current residence or business address of the client; or (B) The service address is the last known residence or business address of the client and the attorney has been unable to locate a more current address after making reasonable efforts to do so within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d)(1).) “‘[C]urrent’ means that the address was confirmed within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved. Merely demonstrating that the notice was sent to the client’s last known address and was not returned…is not, by itself, sufficient to demonstrate that the address is current.” (Id at rule 3.1362(d).)

 

-----

 

 III.          Discussion

 

Application – Counsel moves to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff. Counsel has substantially complied with the statutory requirements of Rule 3.1362 and has filed an MC-051 that contains all required information, is directed to Plaintiff, and was served via standard mailing. Counsel has also provided an MC-052 form, declaring that Counsel has to the best of their ability represented Plaintiff, but that Plaintiff has been unresponsive and refused to answer Counsel’s attempts at communication in a timely manner throughout the pendency of the action. Counsel also asserts this lack of communication hinders their ability to resolve the action, renders them unable to fulfill their duties as Plaintiff’s counsel, and has resulted in a deterioration of the attorney-client relationship. The MC-052 form also provides that Counsel has been unable to confirm that Plaintiff’s last known address or locate a mote current address for Plaintiff after mailing the motion papers to Plaintiff’s last known address with return receipt requested, calling Plaintiff’s last known telephone number(s), and conducting a Skip Trace search. Finally, Counsel has provided an MC-053 Order form with all required information except for Plaintiff’s last known telephone number, which has not been provided.

 

The Court finds minimal prejudice to Plaintiff in granting this motion. While the case has been referred for mediation, with an OSC re Completion of Mediation and Trial Setting Conference set for hearing on March 22, 2027, Plaintiff may choose to proceed in propria persona, and there is sufficient time for Plaintiff to retain counsel to represent her in these hearings and in mediation, if she so chooses. The Court also notes that Plaintiff’s subsequent counsel, if she chooses to so retain, has the option of brief continuance of the OSC and/or TSC if retained without sufficient time to prepare or to otherwise engage in mediation efforts. However, the case is in its beginning stages, very little has transpired on the record, and at the time of hearing on this motion, over six months remain for the parties to attend and complete mediation.

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s motion to be relieved is GRANTED. Counsel shall provide Plaintiff’s contact information, if known, in accordance with the Court’s final ruling within 48 hours of this Order.
