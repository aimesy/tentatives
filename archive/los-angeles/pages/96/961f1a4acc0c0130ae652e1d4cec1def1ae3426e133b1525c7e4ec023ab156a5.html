Case Number: 24STCV23171    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 11 – Judi Wise, et al. v. BVCA Homeowners Association, et al.

Cross-Defendant SCDS Enterprises’s Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement

Cross-Defendant
SCDS Enterprises dba Servpro of Westlake (“Servpro”) moves for an order
determining the good faith of its settlement with Plaintiffs Judi and Warren
Wise under Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6, subdivision (a).

When
only some of multiple defendants alleged to be jointly liable settle a case,
the settling parties may apply to the court for a determination of good faith
settlement.  (Code Civ. Proc., §
877.6(a).) A determination of good faith “shall bar any other joint tortfeasor
or co-obligor from any further claims against the settling tortfeasor or
co-obligor for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative
indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault.” (Id.,
subd. (c).)     

Any opposing party bears “the
burden below to show the settlement amount was ‘so far “out of the ballpark” in
relation to’ the [various] factors that the settlement was inconsistent with
the equitable objectives of section 877.6.” (Cahill v. San Diego Gas &
Electric Co. (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 939, 967; accord Code Civ. Proc., §
877.6(d).)

After
considering all relevant factors, the Court finds the settlement between Cross-Defendant
Servpro and Plaintiffs Judi and Warren Wise was made in good faith. In making such a determination, the Court must
consider various factors stated in Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde &
Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499-500. “ ‘Good faith may be found
only if there has been no collusion between the settling parties and where the
settlement amount appears to be within the “reasonable range” of the settling
party’s proportionate share of comparative liability for a plaintiff’s
injuries.’ ” (Long Beach Memorial Medical Center v. Superior Court
(2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 865, 872.) 

Servpro settled with Plaintiffs for $20,000. (McNamara
Decl., ¶ 18.) While Plaintiffs did not bring any claims directly against
Servpro, counsel for Servpro states that Plaintiff requests “at least $200k in
damages against several Defendants.” (Id., ¶ 14.) Counsel states that
the settlement was “the result of arm's-length negotiations between counsel for
Plaintiffs and counsel for Servpro. The settlement involves no collusion among
any parties.” (Id., ¶¶ 19-20.) The Court finds Servpro presents
sufficient evidence showing this settlement is within the reasonable range of
its potential share of liability.    

No other party has asserted a lack of good
faith or presented any evidence on that issue. “The party asserting the lack of
good faith shall have the burden of proof on that issue.” (CCP § 877.6(d).)

Disposition















           Cross-Defendant SCDS Enterprises dba
Servpro of Westlake’s motion for determination of good faith settlement is granted.
Under Code of Civil Procedure
section 877.6, the Court hereby finds the settlement between Cross-Defendant
Servpro and Plaintiffs Judi and Warren Wise was made in good faith.


Case Number: 24STCV27362    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 10 – Marisol Jimenez v. Dignity Health, et al.

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Disqualify Defense Counsel Law & Brandmeyer LLP, To Take the Motion for Summary Judgment Off-Calendar, and to Continue Trial

Plaintiffs Marisol Jimenez and John Ramirez move to
disqualify Law + Brandmeyer LLP as counsel for Defendants Kendra Duvall and
Dignity Health.[1]
Plaintiffs also move to take the November 5, 2026 hearing on Defendants’ motion
for summary judgment off-calendar, and to continue the trial date.

“A
complaining party who files a motion to disqualify is required to have
standing.” (Blue Water Sunset, LLC v. Markowitz (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th
477, 485.)  Standing generally requires
either “a present or past attorney-client relationship with the attorney who is
the target of the motion” or that “the lawyer owed a duty of confidentiality to
the complaining party and breached it.” (Ibid.)

Plaintiffs
argue they have standing because they fall into a narrow exception to this
general rule—the “manifest-violation” exception. “[I]n California where the
ethical breach is manifest and glaring and so infects the litigation in which
disqualification is sought that it impacts the moving party’s interest in a
just and lawful determination of [his or] her claims, a nonclient might meet
the standing requirements to bring a motion to disqualify based upon a third
party conflict of interest or other ethical violation.” (Kennedy v. Eldridge
(2011) 201 Cal.App.4th 1197, 1204 [cleaned up].)

Plaintiffs
argue that the conflict here is “manifest and glaring” on three separate
grounds.

First, they argue that Defendants Mohaned Abdelzaher
and Kendra Duvall have inherently adverse interests because the complaint
alleges they conspired with one another to manufacture false harassment claims
against Plaintiff Ramirez. The Court is not convinced. Plaintiffs’ mere
allegations of conspiracy between these Defendants in a pleading do not transform
Defense counsel’s representation of them into a manifest ethical violation.
Allegations are not evidentiary facts of adverse interests. Even if they were, the
parties could have waived, and presumably did waive, any potential conflict.

Second, Plaintiffs argue that the individual
Defendants and Defendant Dignity Health have materially adverse interests
because the complaint alleges Dignity Health is vicariously liable for their
conduct. Again, allegations of vicarious liability are not evidence of an
actual breach of professional duties. There is no evidence that such breach
occurred here, nor that any such breach if it did exist undermines the integrity of the judicial system.

Third, Plaintiffs claim that no informed written
consent has been obtained from any Defendant waiving any conflict. For support,
Plaintiffs cite their counsels’ declaration that states “Based on the
correspondence described above and on my personal knowledge of this matter, I
am informed and believe that Law + Brandmeyer LLP has not obtained, and does
not intend to seek, informed written conflict waivers from any defendant.”
(Khan Decl., ¶ 9.) This is pure speculation by Plaintiffs' counsel. Defense counsel is not obliged to advise Plaintiff’s
counsel of whether it received conflict waivers from its own clients; such
communications between attorney and client are privileged and confidential.

In
reply, Plaintiffs argue that Abdelzaher’s recent substitution of new counsel
does not cure the defects identified in their motion because new counsel is
allegedly coordinating litigation strategy with Law + Brandmeyer LLP. For support, Plaintiffs claim that Abdelzaher’s new
counsel changed its position on proposed deposition dates after speaking with
Law + Brandmeyer LLP. This argument is also speculative and certainly does not
constitute evidence of a manifest and glaring conflict of interest.

Plaintiffs thus do not present evidence that they have the requisite
standing to bring this motion. There is no evidence that Defendants’ interests are
adverse to each other, nor evidence that actual, nonspeculative harm has arisen
from any such conflict. Rather, the record indicates a
possibility that Plaintiffs made this motion for purely tactical reasons. Law +
Brandmeyer LLP have been counsel of record for all three Defendants since the
case was filed in 2024. The original complaint contained the same allegations
of conspiracy and vicarious liability. Bringing this motion for
disqualification at this late juncture supports the conclusion that the motion
is strategic. 

Further, the Court declines to consider Plaintiffs’
additional requests to take Defendants’ motion for summary judgment off
calendar and continue the trial date. Including these requests in a motion for disqualification
constitutes an improper omnibus motion. Plaintiffs must raise these requests by
way of properly noticed motions. Nor is there any factual or legal basis presented that warrants such relief.

Disposition

           Plaintiffs
Marisol Jimenez and John Ramirez’s motion to disqualify defense counsel, to
take the motion for summary judgment off-calendar, and to continue trial, is denied.




































[1] At the time this motion was filed,
Law + Brandmeyer LLP also represented
Defendant Mohaned Abdelzaher. However, as of June 9, 2026, Defendant Abdelzaher
is represented by Tina Lee at Reback McAndrews & Blessey LLP.



 





Case Number: 25STCV34806    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 – Javier Alexander Mejia Sevilla v. Guardian International Solutions, et al.

Defendants Guardian International Solutions, Inc. and Super Center Concepts, Inc.’s Motion to Quash Service of Summonses

Defendants
Super Center Concepts, Inc. and Guardian International Solutions Inc. moves to
quash service of summons by Plaintiff Javier Alexander Mejia Sevilla. “[O]nce a
defendant files a motion to quash the burden is on the plaintiff to prove by a
preponderance of the evidence the validity of the service and the court’s
jurisdiction over the defendant.” (Bolkiah v. Superior Court (1999)
74 Cal.App.4th 984, 991.)

Plaintiff does not meet his burden to show
valid service. For a corporation, substituted service on the person to be
served may be done “by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint during usual
office hours in his or her office or, if no physical address is known, at his
or her usual mailing address… with the person who is apparently in charge
thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and complaint.” (CCP §
415.20(a).)

Plaintiff’s proof of service shows service via
mail to the registered agent of each Defendant. This service is improper. If
Plaintiff intended to serve the summons via substitute service, he should have
left a copy of the summons and complaint at the business’s address “with the
person who is apparently in charge thereof” and subsequently mailed a copy to
the same address. If Plaintiff intended to personally serve Defendants, he
should have delivered the summons and complaint to the registered agent as
provided in CCP § 416.10(a).

Disposition









Defendants
Super Center Concepts, Inc. and Guardian International Solutions Inc.’s motion
to quash service of summons is granted. The Court hereby quashes
service of summons on Defendants Super Center Concepts, Inc. and Guardian
International Solutions Inc. as reflected in Plaintiff’s proofs of service
filed on April 13, 2026. Plaintiff is afforded 60 days to effect proper service on the two defendants.
