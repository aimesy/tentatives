Case Number: 23NWCV00014    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: L

Defendant Amazon Logistics, Inc.’s Motion to Bifurcate Trial is GRANTED.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for wrongful death regarding an accident that occurred on January 2, 2020. On January 3, 2023, Plaintiffs Mariano Altamirano and Graciela Altamirano, individually and as successors-in-interest to Grisel Altamirano (“Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendants Kempt Transport Inc., Gurjit Singh, Supreme Transportation Inc., nominal Defendant Valerii Sytnyk, and Does 1 to 10. The Complaint alleges that Grisel Altamirano (“Decedent”) was seated in the right front passenger seat of a vehicle owned/operated by non-party Alejandro Garcia. The Complaint alleges that Defendant Kempt Transport Inc.’s agent, Defendant Singh, was acting in the scope of his employment when the accident occurred. Specifically, Defendant Singh, while driving on the freeway in a truck owned by Defendant Supreme Transportation Inc., negligently collided with the vehicle Decedent was in. The Complaint alleges two cause of action for 1) Negligence, and 2) Wrongful Death.

 

On April 25, 2023, Defendants Kempt Transport Inc. and Gurjit Singh filed the Cross-Complaint. The Cross-Complaint was later dismissed without prejudice.

 

On August 1, 2023, Plaintiffs amended the complaint to name Defendants Sukhvinder Singh and Amazon Logistics, Inc. as Does 1 and 2, respectively.

 

On October 17, 2023, Plaintiffs requested dismissal of the Complaint without prejudice as to Defendants Supreme Transportation Inc. and Sukhvinder Singh.

 

Defendant Amazon Logistics, Inc. (“Amazon”) now moves to bifurcate trial into a liability phase and a damages phase.


Legal Standard

 

“The court, in furtherance of convenience or to avoid prejudice, or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy, may order a separate trial of any cause of action, including a cause of action asserted in a cross-complaint, or of any separate issues or of any number of causes of action or issues, preserving the right of trial by jury required by the Constitution or a statute of this state or of the United States.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (b).)

 

The court may order that “the trial of any issue or any part thereof shall precede the trial of any other issue or any part thereof in the case” when the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economy and efficiency of handling the litigation would be promoted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 598.)

 

Discussion

 

Amazon argues that trial should be bifurcated into a damages phase and a liability phase. Amazon argues that it is likely to prevail on the issue of liability for two reasons. Firstly, Defendant Singh was not acting in the course or scope of employment with Amazon, and thus, Amazon is not vicariously liable. Second, the “sudden emergency” doctrine bars Plaintiffs’ claims against Amazon.

 

Amazon argues that bifurcation will promote judicial efficiency, convenience of the parties, and the ends of justice. Amazon asserts that defense verdict on the issue of liability will prevent the need for resources to be spent on the issue of damages. There will be minimal overlap in liability and damages evidence—for example, the issue of damages involves much more witnesses than the issue of liability. Further, separating the issues of liability and damages will minimize the possibility that a jury will overlook the issues of liability in order to compensate Plaintiff for damages out of sympathy. Lastly, bifurcation will streamline trial and encourage settlement.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Amazon cannot show a high probability of success on the issue of liability. Plaintiff asserts that the Amazon’s sudden emergency defense is hotly contested by the evidence, as evidenced by the Court’s denying of Amazon’s Motion for Summary Judgment based on the defense. (See 5/12/26 Minute Order.) Further, the issue of whether Defendant Singh was an independent contractor employee of Amazon is highly fact-dependent. Thus, Amazon cannot demonstrate a high probability of prevailing on the issue of liability.

 

Plaintiff further argues that Amazon will suffer no prejudice if trial is not bifurcated. Any potential sympathy effect is mitigated by appropriate jury instructions and voir dire. Only Plaintiffs will be prejudiced by bifurcation. Further, Plaintiff argues that interests in judicial economy are favored where the issues liability and damages arise from one event and overlap in proof. Specifically, facts relating to liability are also relevant to proving the extent and pain suffering Decedent experienced for purposes of damages. Plaintiff notes that the newly amended Code of Civil Procedure section 377.34 (amended by SB 447) allows “a decedent’s personal representative or successor in interest to recover damages for a decedent’s pain, suffering, or disfigurement in a survival action if it was filed on or after January 1, 2022, and before January 1, 2026.” (Opp., 6:28, 7:1-2.) Lastly, Plaintiff argues that Amazon will have no incentive to settle the case before the liability phase.

 

Amazon reiterates its arguments in the reply.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 598 was enacted by the legislature to avoid “the waste of time and money caused by the unnecessary trial of damage questions in cases where the liability issue is resolved against the plaintiff.” (Horton v. Jones (1972) 26 Cal.App.3d 952, 955.) Even in cases where there is some evidentiary overlap between what is needed to prove liability and what is needed to prove damages, so long as such repetition is minimal, bifurcation remains proper. (See Kaiser Steel Corp. v. Westinghouse Elec. Corp. (1976) (“Kaiser”) 55 Cal.App.3d 737, 746 [“[W]hile some evidence relating to damages would also be necessary on the issue of liability, only a small fraction of the evidence would be repeated so that the ends of justice were served by bifurcation.”], superseded by statute on other grounds as stated in Billings v. Edwards (1981) 120 Cal.App.3d 238.)

 

The Court finds that bifurcation is warranted under the circumstances. The Court is persuaded that the issue of liability and the issue of damages largely involve separate evidence. The issue of liability will involve a total of five witnesses (two percipient witness and three expert witness). (Grosch Decl., ¶¶ 7-8, Exhs. D-E.) The trial on damages will involve many more witnesses. For example, Plaintiff has designated eleven non-retained witnesses to testify on post-accident treatment. (Grosch Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. E.) Further, in light of the highly emotional nature of the case, the Court finds that bifurcation will help to avoid sympathetic jurors deciding Defendant is liable out of a desire to award damages to Plaintiffs. 

 

The Court acknowledges that certain evidence relevant to the issue of liability (e.g., the circumstances surrounding the crash) will also be presented as to the issue of damages (e.g., the Decedent’s emotional and physical suffering). However, the Court finds such overlap in evidence is minimal and does not outweigh factors in favor of bifurcation. (See Kaiser, supra, 55 Cal.App.3d at p. 746.)

 

 

The Court clarifies that it does not weigh on the probability that Amazon will succeed on the merits on the issue of liability. The Court only finds that the interests of judicial economy weigh in favor of a bifurcated trial.

 

Accordingly, Amazon’s Motion to Bifurcate Trial is granted.

Case Number: 23NWCV03507    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: L

Plaintiffs Fortino Santana and Maria Santana’s Motion for Relief from Mistake, Inadvertence, Surprise, or Excusable Neglect, or Alternatively, Motion For Reconsideration is GRANTED, in part.

 

The Court-ordered dismissal entered on January 22, 2026 is VACATED.

 

Plaintiffs are ordered to file the proposed First Amended Complaint within 5 days of this order.

 

A Case Management Conference is set for October 8, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Dept. L.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a lemon law action. On October 31, 2023, Plaintiffs Fortino Santana and Maria Santana (“Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendants Ford Motor Company (“Ford”), Cerritos Ford, Inc., doing business as Norm Reeves Ford Superstore (“Norm Reeves”), (collectively, “Defendants”) and Does 1 to 10. The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Violation of Subdivision (D) of Civil Code Section 1793.2, 2) Violation of Subdivision (B) of Civil Code Section 1793.2, 3) Violation of Subdivision (A)(3) of Civil Code Section 1793.2, 4) Breach of Implied Warranty of Merchantability, 5) Repair Fraudulent Inducement – Concealment, and 6) Negligent.

 

On December 1, 2023, Ford filed its answer.

 

On December 26, 2023, Norm Reeves filed its answer.

 

On December 11, 2025, the Court granted Defendants’ Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings with 30 days’ leave to amend. (12/11/2 Minute Order.)

 

On January 15, 2026, Defendants filed a Motion and Enter Judgment in Favor of Defendants CCP Section 438(h)(4)(C) and (i)(1)(B) (“Motion to Enter Judgment”).

 

On January 22, 2026, the Court granted Defendants’ Ex Parte Application to Advance the Hearing Date on Motion to Enter Judgment Following Plaintiffs' Failure to Amend their Complaint. (1/22/26 Minute Order.) The Court advanced the Motion to Enter Judgment to that date and entered judgment in favor of Defendants. (1/22/26 Order.)

 

Plaintiffs now move for Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) relief from the dismissal, or alternatively, for reconsideration under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008.

 


Legal Standard

 

i.     CCP § 473(b) Relief

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) provides for discretionary and mandatory relief for mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.

 

Discretionary relief is available to “relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Mandatory relief is available “whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney's sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect…unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

ii.            CCP §1008 Reconsideration

 

A motion for reconsideration under Section 1008 requires that the moving party present new or different facts that were not previously considered by the Court. (New York Times Co. v. Superior Court (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 206, 212-13.) However, the burden under Section 1008 “is comparable to that of a party seeking a new trial on the ground of newly discovered evidence: the information must be such that the moving party could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered or produced it at the trial.” (Id.; Even Zohar Construction & Remodeling, Inc. v. Bellaire Townhouses, LLC (2015) 61 Cal.4th 830, 833 [finding that Section 1008 imposes the special requirement of having to not only show new or different facts, circumstances, or law, but also to “show diligence with a satisfactory explanation for not presenting the new or different information earlier…”].)

 

Discussion

 

i.     CCP § 473(b) Relief

 

Plaintiffs argue that Plaintiffs’ failure to file an amended complaint following the sustaining of Defendants’ Demurrer was due to counsel’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Specifically, due to the volume cases and/or the format of the calendaring application, Plaintiffs’ counsel failed to see the calendared deadline to file the amended complaint for this case. (Lister Decl., ¶¶ 3-7, Exh. A.) Plaintiffs’ counsel was only made aware of the missed deadline when Defendants filed the Ex Parte Application requesting dismissal on January 21, 2026. (Lister Decl., ¶ 8.) Plaintiffs’ counsel promptly prepared an amended complaint. (Lister Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. B.) Plaintiffs seek relief from the dismissal based on the discretionary and/or mandatory relief provisions of Code of Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Plaintiffs request that the Court allow the proposed First Amended Complaint to be filed. (Lister Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. B.)

 

In opposition, Defendants argue that neither the discretionary and/or mandatory relief provisions of Code of Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) applies. Firstly, the mandatory relief provision does not apply because the dismissal here was not procedurally equivalent to a default. Second, the discretionary relief provision does not apply because Plaintiffs’ counsel’s failure to read his assignment calendar is inexcusable neglect. Further, Plaintiffs’ counsel lacked diligence in seeking relief. Specifically, Plaintiffs took no action when Defendants filed the Motion to Enter Judgment on January 15, 2026 and waited until January 30 to seek the instant relief.

 

In reply, Plaintiffs argue that the mandatory relief provision applies because the January 22, 2026 dismissal was the equivalent of a default. Plaintiffs argue that there was no merits determination on that date—it was determined only that Plaintiffs failed to timely amend the complaint, and dismissal was entered. Plaintiffs cite to Younessi v. Woolf (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 1137, 1148-49 to argue that a dismissal resulting from an ex parte application after plaintiff had not amended the complaint was equivalent to a default judgment and the mandatory relief provision did not apply. Alternatively, discretionary relief is warranted under totality of the circumstances.

 

The Court finds that the mandatory relief provision applies here.

 

Firstly, the Court acknowledges that mandatory relief provision of Code of Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) applies to “dismissals which are the procedural equivalent of defaults […].” (Leader v. Health Industries of America, Inc. (2001) 89 Cal.App.4th 603, 618, emphasis in the original, citing Bernasconi Commercial Real Estate v. St. Joseph's Regional Healthcare System (1997) 57 Cal.App.4th 1078, 1082.)

 

The Court finds that two cases shed light on whether the dismissal in this case is the equivalent of a default judgment such that the mandatory relief provision applies: Leader v. Health Industries of America, Inc. (“Leader”) (2001) 89 Cal.App.4th 603 and Younessi v. Woolf (“Younessi”) (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 1137.

 

In the Leader case, the defendants' demurrers were sustained with leave to amend, the plaintiffs failed to file an amended complaint within the time permitted by the trial court. (Leader, supra, 89 Cal.App.4th at p. 607.) The plaintiffs moved for relief pursuant to the mandatory relief provision of Code of Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). (Id. at pp. 608-609.) The defendants moved to dismiss the action for failure to timely amend the complaint. (Id. at p. 610.) After a hearing on both motions, the trial court denied the plaintiffs’ motion for relief and granted the defendant’s motion to dismiss. (Id. at pp. 610-611.) The Court of Appeal affirmed, concluding the mandatory relief provision does not apply to “discretionary dismissals based on the failure to file an amended complaint after a demurrer has been sustained with leave to amend, [where] the dismissal was entered after a hearing on noticed motions which required the court to evaluate the reasons for delay in determining how to exercise its discretion.” (Id. at p. 620.) The Court of Appeals reasoned that the plaintiffs had the opportunity to oppose the motion to dismiss, which was granted on a noticed hearing. (Id. at p. 621.) Because the plaintiffs had their “day in court” the dismissal was not the equivalent to a default judgment, and the mandatory relief provision did not apply. (Ibid.)

 

In contrast, in Younessi case, the trial court dismissed the action following the plaintiff’s failure to timely amend the complaint via an ex parte application for entry of dismissal filed by a defendant. (Younessi, supra, 244 Cal.App.4th at p. 1141.) The plaintiffs moved to set aside the dismissal pursuant to the discretionary and mandatory relief provisions of Code of Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). (Id. at p. 1142.) The trial court granted the motion for relief. (Ibid.) The Court of Appeal affirmed, reasoning that, unlike in Leader, the “dismissal resulted from an order granting [the defendant’s] ex parte application for entry of a dismissal, without any opposition from plaintiffs that would allow the trial court to evaluate why they had failed to timely file an amended complaint.” (Id. at p. 1148.) The Court of Appeals found that the dismissal was the equivalent of a default judgment, and the mandatory relief provision applied. (Id. at pp. 1148-1149.)

 

The Court finds that the instant matter is more similar to Younessi than Leader. Here, Defendants filed a Motion for Entry of Judgment (to dismiss the case), and subsequently brought an ex parte application to advance that Motion, or alternatively, to rule on the Motion on the date of the ex parte hearing. The Court advanced the Motion for Entry of Judgment to that date and granted it, entering judgment in favor of Defendants. (1/22/26 Order.) Similar to Yousessi, Plaintiffs here did not have a proper opportunity to oppose the request to dismiss the action. The Court acknowledges that Plaintiffs’ counsel was present at the ex parte hearing. However, without formal opposition papers, the Court was not able “to evaluate why [plaintiffs] had failed to timely file an amended complaint.” (See Younessi, supra, 244 Cal.App.4th at p. 1148.) Based on all circumstances, the Court finds that the mandatory relief provision of Code of Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) applies because the dismissal was the functional equivalent of a default.

 

The Court finds that Plaintiffs’ counsel’s failure to timely file the amended complaint was due to inexcusable neglect. However, the Court is satisfied that the Declaration of Jacob Lister qualifies as an attorney affidavit of fault to support mandatory relief. The Court finds that Plaintiffs acted diligently in seeking relief—the instant Motion was only filed 15 days after Defendants’ Motion for Entry of Judgment was filed and 9 days after Defendants’ Ex Parte Application was filed. The Court finds minimal prejudice to Defendants will result if relief is granted. Further, public policy weighs in favor of granting relief and allowing the case to proceed on the merits. (See Elston v. City of Turlock (1985) 38 Cal.3d 227, 233 [“[B]ecause the law strongly favors trial and disposition on the merits, any doubts in applying section 473 must be resolved in favor of the party seeking relief from default.”], superseded on other grounds by Wilcox v. Birtwhistle (1999) 21 Cal.4th 973.)

 

Thus, Plaintiffs are entitled to relief from the January 22, 2026 entry of dismissal.

 

ii.            CCP §1008 Reconsideration

 

The Court need not discuss whether the order can be reconsidered where the Court finds that mandatory relief applies.

 

The Court notes that judge loses jurisdiction to rule on a motion for reconsideration after entry of judgment, as is the case here. (See Marshall v. Webster (2020) 54 Cal.App.5th 275, 281 [“It is settled law that a motion for reconsideration is ineffectual if it is filed after entry of judgment.”].)

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ Motion for Relief from Mistake, Inadvertence, Surprise, or Excusable Neglect, or Alternatively, Motion For Reconsideration is GRANTED, in part.

 

The Court-ordered dismissal entered on January 22, 2026 is VACATED.

 

Plaintiffs are ordered to file the proposed First Amended Complaint within 5 days of this order.

 

A Case Management Conference is set for October 8, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Dept. L.


Case Number: 25NWCV00282    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: L

 

I.             Defendants’ Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records of First Foundation Bank is DENIED.

II.            Defendants’ to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records of D/Aq Corporation d.b.a. DAUM Commercial Real Estate Services is GRANTED.

 

Defendants to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for breach of contract. On January 23, 2025, Plaintiff Brent J. Brunner (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Brad Ridenour, Malinda Ridenour, Jason Decker, (collectively, “Defendants”) and Does 1 to 50.

 

On October 25, 2025, the Court entered the parties’ Stipulation and Protective Order.

 

On February 11, 2026, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC). The FAC alleges that Plaintiff was an equal member of the limited liability company, KOI CBD, LLC (hereinafter, “KOI”), alongside Defendants. The FAC alleges the following: “Plaintiff developed a substance dependency issue, and the members of the LLC asked Plaintiff to temporarily sell his membership interest to Defendants, with an option to repurchase his interest at a later date after Plaintiff completed his medical treatment.” (FAC, ¶ 10.) On September 12, 2022, the parties entered into a written agreement (“Agreement”) that provided for a transfer of Plaintiff’s twenty-five percent (25%) membership interest to Defendants. Plaintiff has since attempted to exercise his buy-back option, but Defendants have refused to honor the agreement. The FAC alleges the following causes of action: 1) Fraud, 2) Breach of Written Contract, and 3) Breach of Fiduciary Duty.

 

There are two matters on calendar:

 

-      Defendants’ Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records of First Foundation Bank (“First Foundation”)

-      Defendants’ to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records of D/Aq Corporation d.b.a. DAUM Commercial Real Estate Services (“D/Aq”)

 

Plaintiff has submitted a joint opposition to both Motions. Defendants have submitted separate replies.

 

The Court will address each Motion in turn.


Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1 states that “[i]f a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically

stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court’s own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).) “In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.” (Ibid.) Subdivision (b) provides that a party may make a motion pursuant to subdivision (a). (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (b).)

 

Discussion

 

i.             First Foundation Subpoena

 

On December 15, 2025, Plaintiff served a Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records directed to the Custodian of Records for First Foundation Bank (“First Foundation Subpoena”). (Kirollos Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.) On December 18 to 29, 2025, the parties exchanged meet and confer letters regarding the Subpoena, but were unable to come to a resolution. (Kirollos Decl., ¶¶ 4-7, Exhs. B-E.)

 

The First Foundation Subpoena seeks the following documents:

 

1.    Any and all DOCUMENTS that CONCERNS or RELATE TO payments made by wire transfer or electronically from the SUBJECT ACCOUNTS, during the period between January 2016 to present.

2.    Any and all checks made payable from the SUBJECT ACCOUNTS during the period between January 2016 to present.

3.    Any and all bank statements between during the period between January 2016 to present that was RELATE TO the SUBJECT ACCOUNTS.

4.    Any and all DOCUMENTS that CONCERN or RELATE TO the identity of the owners and holders of the SUBJECT ACCOUNTS.

5.    Any and all DOCUMENTS that shows, relates to or evidences the person or persons authorized as a signor on the account to sign checks, make deposits, withdrawals, included but not limited to signature cards, Power of Attorney, Corporate Resolution.

 

(Kirollos Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.)

 

“Subject Accounts” is defined as “any and all accounts held in the name of KOI CBD, LLC, individually and/or jointly, including but not limited to account number 1011020789, all checking and/or savings accounts, investments accounts, mutual funds, money market, certificate of deposits, IRA accounts, SEP IRA accounts, retirement accounts, retirement brokerage accounts from their inception to present unless otherwise stated herein.” (Ibid.)

 

Defendants now move to quash the First Foundation Subpoena on grounds that it seeks confidential financial information belonging to KOI and Defendants. Defendants assert that the Subpoena seeks KOI’s “confidential financial transactions and account information—materials that directly implicate the company’s business operations, payroll, vendor relationships, employee information, and financial structure.” (Mot., 10-12.) Defendants are the only authorized signatories on the KOI account, and are entitled to invoke privacy rights on their behalf and on the behalf of KOI and its employees.

 

Defendants argue that the potential invasion of privacy is serious because the First Foundation Subpoena demands banking records from 2016 to present. Additionally, the Subpoena’s categories of requested documents are not reasonably particularized such that they are overbroad and unduly burdensome. Further, Plaintiff has not exhausted methods of obtaining the information from Defendants before turning to a First Foundation, a non-party.

 

Lastly, Defendants assert that Plaintiff has not sought leave to conduct pretrial discovery of Defendants’ financial condition pursuant to Civil Code section 3295, subdivision (c).

 

In opposition, Plaintiff asserts that the First Foundation Subpoena seeks evidence relevant to Plaintiff’s claims—specifically, the Subpoena will shed light on “who controlled KOI funds, how KOI funds were used, whether KOI funds were used for transactions or benefits relevant to restitution and disgorgement, whether Defendants’ fiduciary conduct was consistent with their claimed position, and how Plaintiff’s twenty five percent interest should be valued.” (Opp., 9:15-18.) Plaintiff argues that there is no temporal disconnect. Requested records from before the September 2022 agreement may bear on the value of Plaintiff’s membership interest and KOI’s assets before the disputed transfer Requested records from after the 2022 agreement and denial of Plaintiff’s alleged right to buy back his ownership in KOI may bear on the benefits Defendants retained due to the denial.

 

Plaintiff argues that the need for discovery outweighs any asserted privacy concerns. Plaintiff asserts that any privacy concerns can be addressed via the existing protective order, redaction of account numbers, personal identifiers, vendor information, etc.

 

Lastly, Plaintiff asserts that to Civil Code section 3295, subdivision (c) does not bar the First Foundation Subpoena where it is not seeking Defendants’ financial condition for the proving punitive damages. Rather, the Subpoena is seeking KOI account records to prove ownership, control, signatory authority, valuation, restitution, disgorgement, damages, and fiduciary conduct.

 

In reply, Defendants reiterate that the First Foundation Subpoena is overly broad to the extent it seeks information regarding KOI’s valuation.

 

The Court finds that the First Foundation Subpoena is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence regarding Plaintiff’s claims. Specifically, the Subpoena may shed light on Defendants’ motivations behind their alleged breach of the 2022 buyback agreement and KOI’s valuation, which is relevant to liability and damages.

 

As to Defendants’ right of privacy, the California Supreme Court has recognized a privacy right for financial information, although it is not absolute. (See Valley Bank of Nevada v. Superior Court (1975) 15 Cal.3d 652, 656-658 [finding that the following factors should be considered by the trial court in balancing the interests between the right of civil litigants to discover relevant facts and the right of bank customers to maintain reasonable privacy regarding their financial affairs: “. . . the purpose of the information sought, the effect that disclosure will have on the parties and on the trial, the nature of the objections urged by the party resisting disclosure, and ability of the court to make an alternative order which may grant partial disclosure, disclosure in another form, or disclosure only in the event that the party seeking the information undertakes certain specified burdens which appear just under the circumstances”].)

 

The Court finds that Defendants’ privacy interests are outweighed by Plaintiff’s need for discovery. Plaintiff’s claims and damages are closely intertwined with KOI’s financial condition and transactions. The Court agrees with Plaintiff that privacy concerns can be mitigated via protective measures such as the Protective Order in place and redactions of confidential information and personal identifiers.

 

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records of First Foundation Bank is DENIED.

 

ii.            D/Aq Subpoena

 

On December 10, 2025, Plaintiff served a Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records directed to the Custodian of Records for D/Aq (“D/Aq Subpoena”). (Kirollos Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. B.) On December 18 to 29, 2025, the parties exchanged meet and confer letters regarding the Subpoena, but were unable to come to a resolution. (Kirollos Decl., ¶¶ 4-7, Exhs. C-F.)

 

The D/Aq Subpoena seeks the following documents:

 

1.    All DOCUMENTS, CORRESPONDENCE, and records in the YOUR possession, custody, or control relating to the property commonly known as 14632 Best Avenue, Norwalk, CA 90650 (“SUBJECT PROPERTY”).

2.    All escrow instructions (initial, supplemental, amendments, modifications, addenda) executed or proposed by any party to this lawsuit that relate to the SUBJECT PROPERTY.

3.    All loan DOCUMENTS provided to or handled by the title/escrow company, including but not limited to: loan applications (if in file), closing disclosures, loan estimates, notes, deeds of trust, riders, addenda, and any equity cash-out related documentation for any property to which Defendants Brad Ridenour, Malinda Ridenour and/or Jason Decker is a party.

4.    All closing statements, settlement statements, HUD-1s, CDs (Closing Disclosures), disbursement ledgers, wire confirmations, checks, deposit records, invoices, receipts, and any record reflecting the source, allocation, or disbursement of funds, including any cash-out proceeds that relate to the SUBJECT PROPERTY.

5.    All CORRESPONDENCE, DOCUMENTS, and notes (including but not limited to emails, letters, text messages, internal notes, memos, and recorded telephone message notes) between YOU and any party to the subject lawsuit including to the borrowers, lenders, mortgage brokers, real estate agents, and any third parties related to SUBJECT PROPERTY.

 

(Kirollos Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. B.)

 

Defendants now move to quash the D/Aq Subpoena on grounds that it seeks irrelevant escrow, loan, and property transaction records related to 146311 Best Avenue, Norwalk, California 90650 (“Subject property”), which is KOI’s place of business. Defendants explain that D/Aq is a commercial real estate company which assisted in the purchase of the Subject Property. (Ridenour Decl., ¶ 6.) The Subject Property was acquired by another entity, and Plaintiff was not involved in the purchase. (Ridenour Decl., ¶ 6.) Defendants further assert that the Subpoena seeks broad categories of documents without any time or subject matter limitation, placing undue burden on D/Aq.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that KOI’s real property, financing, disbursements, and related communications may bear on Plaintiff’s requested remedies of restoration of his membership interest, damages, restitution, and disgorgement of benefits. Specifically, the D/Aq Subpoena will shed light on “whether KOI assets or funds were used, whether Defendants received or retained benefits connected to KOI assets, how the property was financed, how proceeds were allocated, and what information Defendants or their agents communicated regarding the transaction.” (Opp., 8:14-17.)

 

In reply, Defendants reiterate that 1) KOI is not the owner of the Subject Property and was not involved in its purchase, and 2) the D/Aq Subpoena fails the reasonable particularity requirement.

 

The Court is not persuaded that the documents sought by the D/Aq Subpoena are reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence. The Subpoena seeks real estate transaction documents regarding the Subject Property. While KOI uses the Subject Property as its place of business, Defendants represent that KOI did not purchase the Subject Property. Considering KOI’s lack of involvement in the purchase, the Court does not see how the requested documents will shed light on Plaintiff’s damages and claims in the instant action.

 

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records of D/Aq Corporation d.b.a. DAUM Commercial Real Estate Services is GRANTED.

 

Sanctions

 

“[I]n making an order pursuant to motion made under subdivision (c) of Section 1987 or under Section 1987.1, the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney's fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2, subd. (a).)

 

Defendants do not seek sanctions in relation to these Motions.

 

In the opposition, Plaintiff seeks sanctions in the amount of $3,150.00 to compensate Plaintiff for opposing both Motions.

 

Based on the mixed ruling, the Court respectfully denies Plaintiff’s request for sanctions.

Background

 

This is an action for breach of contract. On February 17, 2026, Plaintiff Tae Kyoung Hwang (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Wonnie Won-Suk Kim (“Kim”), Erica Eun Ah Kim (“Erica”), Bella Assets, LLC (“Bella”), 13330 Bloomfield MGMT, LLC (‘Bloomfield”), Won Suk Kim Real Estate, Inc. (“WSKRE”), (collectively, “Defendants”) and Does 1 to 20. The Complaint alleges that Defendants “obtained monies from various individual lenders and/or investors to pay their other lenders and/or investors with promise unfounded guarantee of return of an annual interest income.” (Compl., ¶ 18.) The Complaint alleges that, on November 21, 2025, Plaintiff loaned Bella $300,000.00, as a conduit for Kim. The loan was evidenced by a promissory note (“Note”). (Compl., ¶ 19, Exh. A.) The loan was secured by a Second Deed of Trust on real property located at 15917 Prairie Ave., Lawndale, CA 90260. The Note stated that Bella agreed to pay Plaintiff an interest payment of $3,500.00 per month. On December 12, 2026, Plaintiff demanded return of his money when he found out that Kim and Bella were having financial issues and would not be able to pay the interest payments. Defendants thereafter failed to pay back all interest payments and the unpaid balance when due (January 5, 2026).

 

The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Breach Of Contract (Promissory Note), 2) Account Stated, 3) Open Book Account, 4) Fraud - Promissory Fraud, 5) Fraud – Concealment, 6) Civil Conspiracy Fraud, 7) Alter Ego, and 8) Constructive Trust.

 

On April 2 and 3, 2026, default was entered as to all Defendants.

 

Plaintiff now applies for three Writs of Attachment as to all the property of Defendants Bella, Bloomfield, and WSKRE. The amount to be secured by the attachment is $324,500.00.

 

No oppositions have been filed as of June 22, 2026.

 

As the three Applications for Writ of Attachment are identical, the Court will address them concurrently.


Legal Standard

 

“Upon the filing of the complaint or at any time thereafter, the plaintiff may apply pursuant to this article for a right to attach order and a writ of attachment by filing an application for the order and writ with the court in which the action is brought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.010.) 

 

The application shall be executed under oath and must include: 

 

1.    a statement showing that the attachment is sought to secure the recovery on a claim upon which an attachment may be issued; 

2.    a statement of the amount to be secured by the attachment; 

3.    a statement that the attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on the claim upon which the attachment is based; 

4.    a statement that the applicant has no information or belief that the claim is discharged or that the prosecution of the action is stayed in a proceeding under the Bankruptcy Act (11 U.S.C. section 101 et seq.); and

5.    a description of the property to be attached under the writ of attachment and a statement that the plaintiff is informed and believes that such property is subject to attachment. 

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 484.020.) 

 

“The application [for a writ of attachment] shall be supported by an affidavit showing that the plaintiff on the facts presented would be entitled to a judgment on the claim upon which the attachment is based.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.030.) Statutory attachment procedures are purely creations of the legislature and as such “are subject to strict construction.” (Hobbs v. Weiss (1999) 73 Cal.App.4th 76, 79 (internal quotations omitted).) A judge does not have authority to order any attachment that is not provided for by the attachment statutes. (Jordan-Lyon Productions, Ltd. v. Cineplex Odeon Corp. (1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 1459, 1466.) “In contested applications, the court must consider the relative merits of the positions of the respective parties and make a determination of¿the probable outcome of the litigation.” (Id. at p. 80 (ellipses and internal quotations omitted).) 

 

The Court shall issue a right to attach order if the Court finds all of the following: 

 

1.    The claim upon which the attachment is based is one upon which an attachment may be issued. 

2.    The plaintiff has established the probable validity of the claim upon which the attachment is based. 

3.    The attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on the claim upon which the attachment is based. 

4.    The amount to be secured by the attachment is greater than zero. 

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 484.090.)

 

Discussion

 

     i.        Basis of Attachment and Amount

 

The Court shall issue a right to attach order if the claim upon which the attachment is based is one upon which an attachment may be issued. (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.090.) “[A]n attachment may be issued only in an action on a claim or claims for money, each of which is based upon a contract, express or implied, where the total amount of the claim or claims is a fixed or readily ascertainable amount not less than five hundred dollars ($500) exclusive of costs, interest, and attorney’s fees.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 483.010, subd. (a).)

 

Plaintiff states that the attachment may be properly issued in this action on a claim for money, based on a contract, where the total amount exceeds $500.00. (Applications, ¶ 3.) Specifically, Plaintiff seeks to a writ of attachment as to the principal payment due under the Note ($300,000.00), plus contractual interest through the hearing date of August 4, 2026 ($24,500.00), totaling $324,500.00. (Applications, ¶ 8; Hwang Decls., ¶ 14.)

 

The Court notes that the hearing date was advanced to today’s date, and thus, Plaintiff must submit amended Right to Attach Orders reflecting the corrected amount to be attached with the proper interest calculation.

 

The Court finds that the writ of attachment is based on a breach of contract claim for a readily ascertainable amount of money exceeding $500.00. (See Compl.) Thus, there is a basis for attachment.

 

    ii.        Probable Validity of Plaintiff’s Claims

 

“A claim has ‘probable validity’ where it is more likely than not that the plaintiff will obtain a judgment against the defendant on that claim.” (Code of Civ. Proc., §¿481.190.) “If the defendant opposes the application, ‘the court must then consider the relative merits of the positions of the respective parties and make a determination of the probable outcome of the litigation.’ [Citations.]” (Pech v. Morgan (2021) 61 Cal.App.5th 841, 855.)

 

To state a cause of action for breach of contract, Plaintiff must be able to establish “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) the resulting damages to the plaintiff.” (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.) 

 

Plaintiff offers the following evidence in arguing that Plaintiff’s claim has probable validity:

 

-         On November 21, 2025, Plaintiff loaned Bella $300,000.00, as evidenced by the Note. (Hwang Decls., ¶ 4, Exh. 2.) Under the Note’s terms, Bella agreed to pay Plaintiff an interest payment of $3,500.00 per month and repay the principal sum of $300,000 one year from date of deposit. (Hwang Decls., ¶ 5.)

-         The loan was secured by a Second Deed of Trust on real property located at 15917 Prairie Avenue, Lawndale, CA 90260. (Hwang Decls., ¶ 4, Exh..) However, no Second Deed of Trust has been recorded to secure Plaintiff’s loan. (Hwang Decls., ¶ 6.)

-         Defendants thereafter failed to pay any interest payments or the principal amount on the loan, despite Plaintiff’s demand. (Hwang Decls., ¶¶ 9-11, Exh. 3.)

-         Bella, Bloomfield, WSRE, Kim, and Eric are alter egos of one another such that their assets were commingled and exchanged without consideration. (Hwang Decls., ¶ 12.)

 

No opposition has been filed.

 

The Court finds that Plaintiff has established probable validity of the breach of contract claim.

 

   iii.        Purpose and Bankruptcy Status

 

In compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 484.020, subdivisions (c) and (d), Plaintiff states that 1) attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on a claim upon which the attachment is based, 2) Plaintiff is not seeking attachment of Defendants’ property for any purpose other than recovery for my claim, and 3) Plaintiff has no information or believe that my claim is discharged or has been stayed by any bankruptcy proceeding. (Applications, ¶¶ 3-5; Hwang Decls., ¶ 3.)

    v.        Undertaking

 

“Before issuance of a writ of attachment. . .the plaintiff shall file an undertaking to pay the defendant any amount the defendant may recover for any wrongful attachment by the plaintiff in the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 489.210.)

 

“Except as provided in subdivision (b), , the amount of an undertaking filed pursuant to this article shall be ten thousand dollars ($10,000).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 489.220, subd. (a).) “If, upon objection to the undertaking, the court determines that the probable recovery for wrongful attachment exceeds the amount of the undertaking, it shall order the amount of the undertaking increased to the amount it determines to be the probable recovery for wrongful attachment if it is ultimately determined that the attachment was wrongful.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 489.220, subd. (b).)

 

Plaintiff has not yet filed an undertaking. (Applications, ¶ 15.) Defendants do not object to the amount of the undertaking. Plaintiff is thus required to pay a $10,000.00 undertaking for each Application.
