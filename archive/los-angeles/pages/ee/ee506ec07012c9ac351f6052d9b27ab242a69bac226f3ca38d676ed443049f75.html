DEPARTMENT D LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 20STCV43963    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: D

CARLOS BARRERAS, ET AL. V. VICKIE WILLIAMS

CASE NO.:  20STCV43963

HEARING:  08/14/2026 @ 8:30 a.m.

 

#1

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Petitioner Karla Ramos’s petition for expedited approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment for minor Claimant Elijah Barreras is DENIED; Petitioner may submit an amended Expedited Petition correcting all deficiencies.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Petitioner Karla Ramos (Petitioner) brings this petition for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment as the Guardian ad litem and parent of the minor Claimant Elijah Barreras.  (Claimant).

 

Legal Standard

 

Compromises of disputed claims brought by minors are governed in part by Code of Civil Procedure section 372.  The statute allows a guardian ad litem to appear in court on behalf of a minor claimant and gives the guardian ad litem the power to compromise the minor’s claim “with the approval of the court in which the action or proceeding is pending.”  A petition for court approval of a compromise must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise or covenant.  (California Rules of Court (CRC) Rule 7.950.)

 

CRC Rule 7.952 subdivision (a) requires the attendance of the petitioner and claimant at the hearing on the compromise of the claim unless the court for good cause dispenses with their personal appearance.

 

CRC Rule 7.955(a) requires courts to use “a reasonable fee standard” when approving and allowing the amount of attorneys’ fees payable from money to be paid for the benefit of a minor and requires that courts “give consideration to the terms of any representation agreement made between the attorney and the representative of the minor . . . . and evaluate the agreement based on the facts and circumstances existing at the time the agreement was made, except where the attorney and the representative of the minor . . . . contemplated that the attorneys’ fee would be affected by later events.”  CRC Rule 7.955(b) sets forth fourteen nonexclusive factors the Court may consider in determining a reasonable attorney’s fee.  CRC Rule 7.955(c) requires that a petition requesting Court approval and allowance of an attorney’s fee under 7.955(a) must include a declaration from the attorney that addresses the factors listed in 7.955(b) that are applicable to the matter before the Court.

 

Analysis

 

The Court has reviewed the expedited petition filed on June 18, 2026 by Petitioner on behalf of Claimant. 

 

Petitioner has submitted a completed Judicial Council form MC-350 on behalf of the minor Claimant, signed by Petitioner, together with a proposed Order Approving Compromise (form MC-351).

 

Petitioner seeks court approval of a settlement under which Claimant will receive a gross settlement of $9,500.00.  Petitioner also requests attorney’s fees in the amount of $2,375.00, representing 25% of the gross settlement.  The fee request is supported by the declaration of Jack Bazerkanian, Esq., and the parties’ contingency fee agreement, submitted as Attachment 14(a).  After payment of the requested attorney’s fees, $666.33 in medical expenses, and $310.77 in costs, the petition reflects that Claimant’s net recovery is $6,147.90, to be deposited into a blocked account at Chase Bank.

 

However, the Court DENIES the petition without prejudice for the following reasons:

 

-        No proof of service on the other parties has been filed.

-        Petitioner should clarify her representative capacity.  Although the current petition identifies Karla Ramos solely as Claimant's parent, prior filings and counsel's March 20, 2026, declaration identify Ramos as Claimant's guardian ad litem.

-        Item 13(a)(5) is incomplete and should reflect the $666.33 Medi-Cal lien.

-        Item 13(f)(1) appears inconsistent with Item 13(c)(2)(b) and the DHCS documentation because it states there are no statutory or contractual liens.

-        Attorney's Fees: Although counsel submitted a declaration and the contingency fee agreement as Attachment 14(a), the declaration does not sufficiently explain the basis for the requested fee, including a discussion of the applicable factors set forth in California Rules of Court, rule 7.955(b).

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Petitioner Karla Ramos’s petition for expedited approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment for minor Claimant Elijah Barreras DENIED; Petitioner may submit an amended Expedited Petition correcting all deficiencies.


Case Number: 26NWCV00315    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: D

ARGONAUT INSURANCE COMPANY V. GREEN CONTRACTOR STUDIO, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV00315

HEARING:  08/14/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#20

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Argonaut Insurance Company’s unopposed motion for leave to amend its First Amended Complaint is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Argonaut Insurance Company to separately file their proposed Second Amended Complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Argonaut Insurance Company (Plaintiff) moves for an order granting leave to amend its Second Amended Complaint (SAC).

 

Background

 

On April 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (FAC) against Defendants Green Contractor Studio, Inc., Herald Kim, Shannon Wang, Legacy 21, LLC, Legass Investments, Inc., and Does 1 through 35, inclusive, asserting four causes of action: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Statutory Reimbursement; (3) Quia Timet; and (4) Violation of California’s Uniform Voidable Transactions Act.

 

On May 13, 2026, Plaintiff requested dismissal as to Defendant Legass Investments, Inc.  The Clerk entered the dismissal that same day.

 

On June 5, 2026, Plaintiff requested entering default as to Defendants Green Contractor Studio, Inc., Shannon Wang, and Herald Kim.  The Clerk entered the defaults that same day.

 

On July 16, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s ex parte application seeking an order granting Plaintiff leave to file a SAC finding no affirmative factual showing of irreparable harm or immediate danger.

 

On July 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion.

 

No opposition or reply has been filed.

 

Legal Standard

 

The court may grant leave to file a cross-complaint if the failure to plead a cause of action was the result of oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.)  Where the proposed cross-complaint arises out of the same transaction as plaintiff’s claim, the court must grant leave to file the cross-complaint as long as defendant is acting in good faith.  (Ibid.; see Silver Organizations Ltd. v. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 99 (Silver Organizations) [the Court may not “exercise discretion” in the denial of a motion to file a compulsory cross-complaint].)  Judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters between the parties in the same lawsuit and discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendment of the pleadings.  (See Nestle v. Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 929.)¿

 

“‘Bad faith,’ is defined as ‘[t]he opposite of “good faith,” generally implying or involving actual or constructive fraud, or a design to mislead or deceive another, or a neglect or refusal to fulfill some duty or some contractual obligation, not prompted by an honest mistake ..., but by some interested or sinister motive[,] ... not simply bad judgment or negligence, but rather ... the conscious doing of a wrong because of dishonest purpose or moral obliquity; ... it contemplates a state of mind affirmatively operating with furtive design or ill will.  [Citation.]’  [Citations.]”  (Silver Organizations, supra, 217 Cal.App.3d at 100.) 

 

A party may obtain leave of court to file a cross-complaint at any time during the course of a lawsuit, and leave may be granted in the interest of justice.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 426.50, 428.50; see also Sidney v. Superior Court (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 710, 718.)  “To the extent practicable, all supporting memorandums and declarations must be attached to the notice of motion.”  (Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1113(j).)¿

 

Analysis

 

Plaintiff moves for an order granting leave to file a SAC on the grounds that (1) Defendant Shannon Whang’s name was misspelled in the FAC, and (2) Plaintiff recently discovered grounds to add a new defendant, Jong Ja Jun, in its fourth cause of action for Violation of California’s Uniform Voidable Transactions Act.

 

Pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court 3.1324(a), a motion to amend must: (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered; and (2) state what allegations are proposed to be deleted from or added to the previous pleading and where such allegations are located.  Cal. Rules of Court 3.1324(b) requires a separate declaration that accompanies the motion, stating: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reason why the request for amendment was not made earlier.¿

 

Here, Plaintiff has satisfied the procedural requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324.  Plaintiff attached a proposed Amendment to the FAC correcting the misspelling of Defendant Shannon Whang’s name (incorrectly spelled Shannon Wang), added the new defendant, Jong Ja Jun, in the fourth cause of action, identified the proposed amendment and its effect, and submitted a supporting declaration explaining that the amendment is necessary based on a recent review of property records that show Defendants Herald Kim and Shannon Whang transferred an interest in their property to new Defendant Jong Ja Ju.  (Hook Decl., ¶¶ 2-3, Exs. 1 and 2.) 

-

Moreover, leave to amend should be liberally granted in the furtherance of justice, and the record does not demonstrate any prejudice that would result from permitting the amendment.  Trial has not been scheduled, and the motion is unopposed.  Under these circumstances, the Court finds Plaintiff has established good cause for leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Argonaut Insurance Company’s unopposed motion for leave to amend its First Amended Complaint is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Argonaut Insurance Company to separately file their proposed Second Amended Complaint within 7 days of this ruling.
