DEPARTMENT J LAW AND MOTION RULINGS



Case Number: 25SMCV03679    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: J

The application of Franklin D. Munoz to appear as counsel pro hac vice for defendants LBA Inc., LBA Realty LLC, and One Culver Owner LP is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

 

The supporting memorandum’s signature block identifies the clients as “Petaluma People Services Center and Kassidy Meiko Ford,” who are not parties to this action. The application is otherwise brought on behalf of defendants LBA Inc., LBA Realty LLC, and One Culver Owner LP. Because the papers are internally inconsistent as to whom counsel represents, the court cannot confirm that the application is made on behalf of the defendants in this case. The papers also state local counsel Michael W. Melendez’s State Bar number inconsistently, as both 125895 and 125985.

 

In all other respects the application satisfies the requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 9.40. The application is unopposed, and proof of service and proof of payment are attached. Counsel may correct the signature block and the local-counsel State Bar number, and refile. If corrected, the court will consider the application in chambers and counsel need not appear.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

 

 


Case Number: 25SMCV06047    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: J

TENTATIVE RULING:

            motion to be relieved as counsel;

            The court having read the papers rules as follows:

The motion is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

An attorney may move to be relieved as counsel of record under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2). The motion must comply with the procedural requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.

Counsel has shown good cause for withdrawal. The sole defendant has filed for bankruptcy, which subjects any recovery to the automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. section 362 and to a creditor priority scheme. Continued representation of the plaintiff on a contingency basis is therefore not reasonably practicable. Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(10).

The motion is procedurally deficient in two respects.

First, counsel served the moving papers on the client by mail but did not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d)(1). When the client is served by mail, the supporting declaration must state facts showing either that the service address is the client’s current address, or that it is the client’s last known address and counsel was unable to locate a more current address after reasonable efforts made within 30 days before filing the motion. The declaration makes neither showing. It lists only the client’s last known address. Sending papers to a last known address, without more, does not establish that the address is current.

Second, the proposed order does not specify all hearing dates as required by California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e). The lodged order lists only the May 19, 2026 Case Management Conference. That conference has been continued to June 30, 2026, and the court has set an Order to Show Cause re monetary sanctions and an Order to Show Cause re bankruptcy status for the same date. The order must reflect all currently scheduled hearings so that the client is informed of them.

Counsel is ordered to correct these deficiencies and to file corrected papers before the next hearing. If the deficiencies are corrected before the hearing, the motion will be granted and the parties need not appear.

Counsel is to give notice. Service may be made electronically pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).
