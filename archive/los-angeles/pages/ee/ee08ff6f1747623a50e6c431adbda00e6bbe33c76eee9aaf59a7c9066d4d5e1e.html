Legal Standard

“Any party to an action in which it is alleged that two or more parties are joint tortfeasors or co-obligors on a contract debt shall be entitled to a hearing on the issue of the good faith of a settlement entered into by the plaintiff or other claimant and one or more alleged tortfeasors or co-obligators, upon giving notice in the manner provided in subdivision (b) of Section 1005 . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (a)(1).)

“A determination by the court that the settlement was made in good faith shall bar any other joint tortfeasor or co-obligor from any further claims against the settling tortfeasor or co-obligor for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (c).)

“[T]he intent and policies underlying section 877.6 require that a number of factors be taken into account including a rough approximation of plaintiffs’ total recovery and the settlor’s proportionate liability, the amount paid in settlement, the allocation of settlement proceeds among plaintiffs, and a recognition that a settlor should pay less in settlement than he would if he were found liable after a trial.

Other relevant considerations include the financial conditions and insurance policy limits of settling defendants, as well as the existence of collusion, fraud, or tortious conduct aimed to injure the interests of nonsettling defendants. Finally, practical considerations obviously require that the evaluation be made on the basis of information available at the time of settlement.” (Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499 [emphasis added].)

Additionally, “the trial court’s good faith determination must take into account the settling tortfeasor’s potential liability for indemnity to a cotortfeasor, as well as the settling tortfeasor’s potential liability to the plaintiff.” (Far West Financial Corp. v. D&S Co. (1988) 46 Cal.3d 796, 816, fn. 16.) “If section 877.6 is to serve the ends of justice, it must prevent a party from purchasing protection from its indemnification obligation at bargain-basement prices.” (Long Beach Memorial Medical Center v. Superior Court (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 865, 876.)

The moving party’s initial evidentiary burden depends on whether the good faith of the settlement is being contested. If the nonsettling defendants do not oppose the motion on the good faith issue, a “barebones” motion which sets forth the grounds of good faith, accompanied by a declaration which sets forth a brief background of the case, is sufficient. (City of Grand Terrace v. Superior Court (1987) 192 Cal.App.3d 1251, 1261.)

When a motion for determination of good faith settlement is contested, however, the moving party must make a more specific showing under the Tech-Bilt factors. (Id. at 1261-62.) Such a showing may be made either in the original moving papers or in counter-declarations filed after the nonsettling defendants have filed an opposition challenging good faith of the settlement. (Id. at 1262.) Where good faith is contested, the showing requires competent evidence in support of “good faith.” (Greshko v. County of Los Angeles (1987) 194 Cal.App.3d 822, 834.)

“Once there is a showing made by the settlor of the settlement, the burden of proof on the issue of good faith shifts to the non-settlor who asserts that the settlement was not made in good faith.” (City of Grand Terrace, supra, 192 Cal.App.3d at 1262; Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (d).)

 

Discussion

PRSE and Rohrer move the Court for an order, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 877 and 877.6, determining that their settlement reached with Plaintiffs was entered into in good faith.

 

Procedural Defects

At the outset, the Court observes that PRSE and Rohrer failed to comply with California Rules of Court rule 3.1382 (i.e., “[a] motion or application for determination of good faith settlement may include a request to dismiss a pleading or a portion of a pleading. The notice of motion or application for determination of good faith settlement must list each party and pleading or portion of pleading affected by the settlement and the date on which the affected pleading was filed” [Emphasis added].)

PRSE and Rohrer make only a generic reference to a “pending cross-complaint for equitable indemnity, contribution and declaratory relief.” The procedural history reflects that PSRE and Rohrer are defendants in Plaintiff’s SAC filed September 4, 2025 and cross-defendants in Stile Construction/Design Incorporated’s cross-complaint filed April 22, 2026.

 

Merits 

The motion is essentially unopposed [FN]; in this situation, it is enough for PRSE and Rohrer to make a “barebones motion which sets forth the ground of good faith, accompanied by a declaration which sets forth a brief background of the case…” (City of Grand Terrace, supra, 192 Cal.App.3d at 1261.)

PRSE and Rohrer’s motion meets this lower threshold. The motion provides the requisite “brief background of the case.” This construction defect action arises out of a renovation of the Property, which involved, inter alia, the removal of numerous interior and exterior bearing and shear walls and construction of an addition at the first and second floors. (Davidson Decl., ¶ 4.) On or about April 5, 2020, Plaintiffs entered into a construction contract with L. Delgado dba L&R Construction (“L&R Construction”). (Id., ¶ 5.) L&R Construction performed construction work at the Property from approximately April 2020 through November 2022, at which point it was terminated by Plaintiffs. (Id.) 

On February 7, 2023, Plaintiffs filed their original complaint. (Id., ¶ 6.) On August 11, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a Doe amendment, naming both PRSE and Rohrer as defendants. (Id.) On September 4, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a SAC against PRSE and Rohrer for breach of contract, professional negligence and declaratory relief. (Id., ¶¶ 6 and 10, Exh. 2.) 

PRSE’s and Rohrer’s counsel, David Davidson (“Davidson”) represents, and/or the accompanying exhibits reflect, as follows: 

          PRSE and Rohrer have agreed to settle all claims asserted against them by Plaintiffs in exchange for $30,000.00 and a $3,410.00 waiver of PRSE’s outstanding fee receivable from Plaintiffs. (Id., ¶¶ 40 and 55.) Davidson estimates that Plaintiffs are attempting to seek $2,643,778 in damages but asserts that “[b]ased on the claims being made by Plaintiff[s], none of the items of damages are associated with the services provided by PRSE but are solely related to work performed by the contractor, subcontractors, other consultants of the owner and replacement contractors as well as others some of which are in default. (Id., ¶¶ 66 and 67.) 

          PRSE is a California structural engineering firm and Rohrer is the licensed structural engineer and same. (Id., ¶ 19.) PRSE’s engagement arose from a written Agreement for Structural Service dated March 26, 2020 (“Agreement”), initially entered into between PRSE and DesignDLaux Inc. (“DesignDLaux”)/Pete Laux (“Laux”), with PRSE Job No. 20-017. (Id., ¶¶ 11 and 20, Exh. 3.) The contract was subsequently executed by Plaintiffs on April 2, 2020. (Id.) PRSE’s scope of work on the Property was limited to: (a) structural engineering design, calculations, and drawings; (b) responding to building department comments; (c) assisting the contractor with interpretation of the structural design; and (d) periodic construction observation site visits, performed only upon request, at $400 per visit. (Id., ¶ 19.)

The Agreement expressly provides, in relevant part, as follows: 

          “Construction Observation (Billed on a Time & Materials Basis)

          -Site visits by the Structural Engineer to observe the construction for general conformance with the approved structural plans may be required by the Building Official. Site visits by the structural engineer will be performed upon request only and will be billed at $400.00 per visit. Please allow (2) working days notice for scheduling. . . 

          TERMS AND CONDITIONS. . . 

          2. Engineer will in no way be responsible for how the contractor’s work is performed. Job safety, methods of performance, and timeliness are the sole responsibility of the contractor. The Client will agree to Hold Harmless and defend the engineer against any and all acts, errors, or omissions on the part of the Client, the Contractor, and/or any of the subcontractors. . . 

          13. Limitation of Liability - The Client recognizes the inherent risks connected with construction, and agrees to limit any and all liability, claim for damages, cost of defense, or expenses to be levied against Engineer on account of a design defect, error, omission, or professional negligence to a sum not to exceed $250,000.00.” 

(Id., ¶ 11, Exh. 3.) 

Laux, DesignDLaux’s principal, who served as Plaintiffs’ designer and the de facto agent on the Property, testified at deposition that prior to the termination of L&R Construction's involvement on the project, he was Plaintiffs’ agent responsible for coordinating PRSE's services. (Id., ¶ 16, Exh. 8, 132:6-10.) Laux testified that PRSE would only come to the construction site when called to do so (Id., 134:18-23 and 136:16-18) and that he, not Plaintiffs or L&R Construction, was the person responsible for calling PRSE out to the site to perform construction observations (Id., 134:18-23 and 136:16-18). PRSE made 2 construction observation site visits to the Property during the period that L&R Construction was the general contractor, on April 15, 2021 and September 16, 2021. (Id., ¶ 29, Exhs. 4 and 5; ¶ 16, Exh. 8, 142:12-15.) 

While Plaintiffs’ professional negligence claims against PRSE included allegations that PRSE failed to conduct adequate structural observations during construction and failed to observe and verify proper installation of critical structural elements (SAC, ¶ 52), PRSE was not called to observe the framing prior to the installation of drywall. (Id., ¶ 16, Exh. 8, 138:16-18; ¶ 32.) The final pre-drywall walk-through occurred on approximately November 8, 2021 and Laux confirmed that framing was finalized around that time. (Id., ¶ 16, Exh. 8, 137:23-138:9.) Further, Laux testified that he did not have any criticisms of PRSE on this project and, when asked in his opinion why PRSE was named in this lawsuit, answered, “Because he was there.” (Id., ¶ 16, Exh. 8, 149:9-14; ¶ 36.) Montesinos responded affirmatively when asked if she would hire PRSE today for structural engineering work. (Id., ¶ 17, Exh. 9, 213:21; ¶ 37.)

Additionally, PRSE and Rohrer have proffered a declaration from a licensed civil and structural engineer, Peter S. Curry, SE (“Curry”). Curry sets forth his qualifications and states that he has reviewed Plaintiff’s SAC, Stile Construction/Design Incorporated’s cross-complaint filed April 22, 2026, PRSE’s and Rohrer’s cross-complaint, L&R Construction’s cross-complaint and other documents, including discovery responses, document production and some deposition transcripts in this case (Id., ¶¶ 9 and 22); that he has inspected the Property (Id., ¶ 21) that he reviewed the structural plans and calculations prepared by PRSE for the Property (Id., ¶¶ 24 and 25); and that he personally met with and discussed the Property with Rohrer at the site and discussed observations and allegations being made by Plaintiffs against PRSE in this case (Id., ¶ 26). 

Curry opines that the structural plans and calculations prepared by PRSE met the standard of care for a structural engineer for the Property (Id., ¶¶ 24 and 25); that PRSE: (1) conducted a thorough footing inspection on April 15, 2021, finding the work acceptable; (2) returned on September 16, 2021, identified structural deficiencies in the framing, issued a written observation report, and provided a corrective engineering sketch (SK-1) to guide remediation; (3) operated in full conformance with its contract, which required site visits only upon request; (4) was never called to observe the framing before drywall installation by Laux, the one person who bore the responsibility for making that request; (5) received no criticism whatsoever from either Laux or Plaintiffs regarding the quality or completeness of its professional services; and (6) continued to assist the project even after L&R Construction was terminated (Id., ¶ 34); that “any problems with the framing at the Project are directly caused by the failure of the contractors and subcontractors to perform their work in compliance with the permitted set of structural drawings and industry standards” (Id., ¶ 32); and that the settlement “is not only in the ballpark but represents more than PRSE’s proportionate share of liability in this case as PRSE bears zero liability in this case” (Id., ¶ 36).

PRSE and Rohrer argue that Business & Professions Code section 6735, subdivision (b) (i.e., “[A] licensed civil engineer who signs civil engineering documents shall not be responsible for damage caused by subsequent changes to or uses of those documents, if the subsequent changes or uses, including changes or uses made by state or local governmental agencies, are not authorized or approved by the licensed civil engineer who originally signed the documents, provided that the engineering service rendered by the civil engineer who signed the documents was not also a proximate cause of the damage”) also provides them with a complete statutory defense, inasmuch as the evidence establishes that L&R Construction and subcontractors deviated from PRSE’s approved structural drawings, that PRSE never approved the final framing condition before it was concealed by drywall installation and that PRSE’s services were not a proximate cause of Plaintiffs’ damages.

Davidson asserts that PRSE “would have also sought its attorneys fees and costs against Plaintiff based on the Crawford v. Weathershield (2008) 44 Cal.4th 541 and UDC-Universal Development, L.P. v. CH2M Hill (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 10 arising from Plaintiffs individual duty to defend, indemnify and hold PRSE harmless.” (Davidson Decl., ¶ 70.)

Davidson represents that PRSE is a small structural engineering firm with limited financial resources to fund a settlement (Id., ¶ 48); that PRSE carries professional liability insurance with applicable policy limits (Id., ¶ 15, Exh. 7, Resp. to No. 304.1); that the settlement negotiations were arms-length and adversarial and without collusion, fraud or tortious conduct (Id., ¶¶ 48-55). 

L&R Construction, in response, asserts that the entire value of the settlement, should the motion be granted, should apply as a credit to the non-settling defendants. PRSE and Rohrer did not file a reply brief addressing L&R Construction’s position. 

The Court is inclined to grant the motion, but will hear further from the parties with respect to the offset issue raised by L&R Construction at the time of the hearing.

He v. Wang, et al., Case No. 24PSCV04081

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO STRIKE

 

Defendants Michael Wang aka Xiaojing Wang, Yueh Ho, Eva Wang, Sinola Restaurant, LLC, and Crossgates Property LLC’s Motion to Strike Plaintiff’s Notice of Errata in First Amended Complaint is GRANTED.


Background 

Plaintiff Shan He (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows:

Michael Wang a/k/a Xiaojing Wang (“M. Wang”) and Yueh Ho (“Ho”) are married and both trustees and beneficiaries of the H&W Family Trust dated September 25, 2023 (“Trust”); Eva Wang (“E. Wang”) and Patrick Wang (“P. Wang”) are M. Wang’s adult children and Trust beneficiaries. M. Wang is the owner, president, chief executive officer and sole or majority shareholder of Sinola Restaurant, LLC (“Sinola”) and the owner, president, chief executive officer and sole or majority shareholder of Crossgates Property LLC (“Crossgates”).

Between December 2016 and January 2017, Plaintiff loaned $1,000,000.00 to M. Wang, who agreed to assist Plaintiff with the application of a green card. On/about February 11, 2017, M. Wang executed a “Payment Guaranty Agreement” (“February 11, 2017 Agreement”), wherein M. Wang agreed to repay Plaintiff $1,000,000.00 within 6 months of one of the following events occurring: (1) Plaintiff obtaining her official green card; (2) Plaintiff withdrawing her application for green card or (3) Plaintiff’s green card application being denied “for reasons of the company (Sinola Restaurant LLC) or the U.S. government.” The Agreement further provided that M. Wang guaranteed the repayment with his personal property and Sinola’s assets and that he would pay interest of $20,000.00/year to an account designated by Plaintiff by December 31 of each year.

In October 2018, M. Wang paid Plaintiff interest for the years of 2017 and 2018, in the amount of $40,000.00. Plaintiff subsequently decided to withdraw her green card application and formally notified M. Wang of same on November 9, 2018. Plaintiff and M. Wang reached an agreement on/about November 15, 2018, wherein M. Wang confirmed that the green card application was withdrawn as of November 15, 2018. M. Wang agreed that, per the February 11, 2017 Agreement, he should repay the $1,000,000.00 within 6 months from November 15, 2018, but claimed to need more time, and so confirmed in writing that he would provide a written refund schedule with a specific timeline and dates.

M. Wang thereafter acknowledged his obligation to repay the monies (i.e., on/about December 18, 2018, May 5, 2019, May 11, 2020, and December 5, 2020). On/about January 20, 2021, Plaintiff discovered that M. Wang had blocked Plaintiff and her parents on all communication platforms, preventing her from locating and contacting him. Plaintiff’s ability to act was constrained by COVID-19 restrictions between February 13, 2020-December 13, 2022 and by her mother’s severe health issues from 2017-2024. M. Wang and Ho set up Trust to hide their assets from Plaintiff. In or about March 2024, Trust purchased real properties located at 5387 and 5395 Crossgates Avenue in Las Vegas; said properties were then transferred to Crossgates for zero value. Additionally, Sinola made transfers of money or property to M. Wang, Ho, E. Wang, and P. Wang to avoid paying Plaintiff.

On April 9, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), asserting causes of action against M. Wang and Ho, individually and as Trustees of Trust, E. Wang, P. Wang, Sinola, Crossgates, and Does 1-25 for: (1) Breach of Written Contract (against M. Wang and Sinola); (2) Fraud (against all Defendants); (3) Breach of Personal Guarantee (against M. Wang and Sinola); (4) Unlawful Distributions (against M. Wang and Sinola); (5) Actual Fraudulent Transfers (against all Defendants); and (6) Constructive Fraudulent Transfers (against all Defendants).

On August 7, 2025, the Court sustained M. Wang, Ho, E. Wang, Sinola, and Crossgates’ demurrer to the second cause of action in Plaintiff’s FAC, without leave to amend. 

On March 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a “Notice of Errata Re: First Amended Complaint.” 

A Case Management Conference and an Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service on FAC are set for November 5, 2025. 

A Post-Mediation Status Conference/Case Management Conference is set for September 17, 2026.

 

Legal Standard 

“(a) As used in this section: . . .
(2) The term ‘pleading’ means a demurrer, answer, complaint, or cross-complaint.
(b)(1) Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435). 

“The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:
(a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.
(b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 436).

 

Discussion 

M. Wang, Ho, E. Wang, Sinola and Crossgates (hereinafter, “Defendants”) move the Court, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 435 and 436 and California Rules of Court Rule 3.1322, for an order striking Plaintiff’s “Notice of Errata Regarding First Amended Complaint” (“Notice of Errata”). 

A Notice of Errata may be used to correct typographical or clerical errors/omissions, not to alter substantive allegations in a pleading. (See, e.g., Flores v. Nature’s Best Distribution, LLC (2016) 7 Cal.App.5th 1, 6 [notice of errata filed as to a missing exhibit]; Falahati v. Kondo (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 823, 833-834 [notice of errata filed adding defendant to caption of Third Amended Complaint where there was no mention of him in body of complaint was improper].) 

Plaintiff asserts that the 36-page Notice of Errata filed March 4, 2026 merely seeks to correct a typographical error. The Court disagrees. The Notice of Errata purports to change dates in Paragraphs 31 and 32 of the First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) from “December 5, 2020” and “January 20, 2021” to “December 5, 2021” and “January 20, 2024,” respectively, rewrites Paragraph 31 to allege that the December 5, 2021 communication was an “unconditional acknowledgment of the debt” that “restart[ed] the applicable statute of limitations period as a matter of law,” and adds Exhibit A. 

Inasmuch as the Notice of Errata purports to amend the FAC, the Court construes same as a “pleading” within the context of Code of Civil Procedure sections 435 and 436. The motion is granted. Plaintiff’s request for leave to amend the FAC must be made via a separately-noticed motion.

Case Number: 25PSCV02589    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: H

Abdoush v. Dandana Café and Banquet, Inc., et al., Case No. 25PSCV02589

 

ORDER ON DEMURRERS AND MOTIONS TO STRIKE

 

1.       Defendant Rami Wardeh’s Demurrer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

2.       Defendant Rami Wardeh’s Motion to Strike First Amended Complaint is DENIED.

3.       Defendant Dandana Café and Banquet Inc.’s Demurrer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED. 

4.       Defendant Dandana Café and Banquet Inc.’s Motion to Strike First Amended Complaint is DENIED.


Background 

Plaintiff Philip Abdoush (“Abdoush”) alleges as follows:

Hani C. Karam (“Karam”), Rami A. Wardeh (“Wardeh”) and Mazen K. Manjah (“Manjah”) wanted to purchase the “Dandana” restaurant located at 765 W. Route 66 in Glendora (“Restaurant”) from Deeb I. Deeb (“Deeb”). An escrow had already been opened by Deeb in his purchase of the Restaurant from DCB Food, Inc. (“DCB”), but prior to closing, Deeb sought to sell his ownership interest therein to Karam and Wardeh. Karam and Wardeh claimed to be short of funds to purchase the Restaurant and represented to Abdoush and Manjah that if they provided them with $250,000.00, they would be able to purchase the Restaurant from Deeb, with Abdoush and Manjah each receiving a 25% ownership in the Restaurant. They assured Abdoush that they would handle the corporate structure and purchase of the Restaurant, that ownership of the Restaurant would be through Dandana Cafe and Banquet, Inc. (“Dandana”) and that Abdoush would own 25% of the Restaurant through his ownership of 25% of Dandana. Abdoush agreed and provided the $250,000.00, which covered his and Manjah’s payment; this was paid to Deeb. Almost immediately after payment, Karam and Wardeh ceased their efforts to purchase the Restaurant from Deeb, Deeb ceased his efforts to purchase the Restaurant from DCB and Ghazi Fedail (“Fedail”) and escrow was cancelled. Prior to the cancellation of escrow, Abdoush provided Karam with $75,000.00, which Karam represented was for his work to renovate the Restaurant. Abdoush has not received any ownership interest in the Restaurant or a refund. 

On April 2, 2026, Abdoush filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), alleging causes of action against Dandana, DCB, Wardeh, Karam, Estate of Fedail, Doe 1, Deeb, Karam and Wardeh, as Co-Trustees of the Manjah/Karam/Wardeh Trust and Does 2-25 for: (1) Fraud, (2) Conversion, (3) Breach of Fiduciary Duty, and (4) Money Had and Received.

1. Wardeh’s Demurrer to FAC

Legal Standard 

A demurrer may be made on the grounds that, inter alia, the pleading does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) 

“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.” (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905 [citations omitted].) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) “[A] demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction placed on an instrument pleaded therein, or facts impossible in law, or allegations contrary to facts of which a court may take judicial knowledge.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732 [citations omitted].) 

Discussion 

Wardeh demurs to the entirety of the FAC, on the basis that it was untimely filed without leave of court. Wardeh additionally demurs to the second cause of action (i.e., for Conversion), on the basis that it fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. 

At the outset, the Court observes that Wardeh’s first argument is not a recognized basis for demurrer. The grounds for a demurrer are identified in Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10 and limited to the following:
“(a) The court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the pleading.
(b) The person who filed the pleading does not have the legal capacity to sue.
(c) There is another action pending between the same parties on the same cause of action.
(d) There is a defect or misjoinder of parties.
(e) The pleading does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.
(f) The pleading is uncertain. As used in this subdivision, “uncertain” includes ambiguous and unintelligible.
(g) In an action founded upon a contract, it cannot be ascertained from the pleading whether the contract is written, is oral, or is implied by conduct.
(h) No certificate was filed as required by Section 411.35.”  

The Court summarily overrules Wardeh’s demurrer to the entirety of Plaintiff’s FAC on this basis. [FN] 

Next, “[c]onversion is the wrongful exercise or dominion over the property of another. The elements of a conversion claim are: (1) the plaintiff’s ownership or right to possession of the property; (2) the defendant’s conversion by a wrongful act or disposition of property rights; and (3) damages.” (Burlesci v. Petersen (1991) 68 Cal.App.4th 1062, 1066.) 

Plaintiff’s second cause of action identifies two sums: the $250,000 Dandana Payment and the $75,000.00 Improvement Funds. (FAC, ¶ 50.) Wardeh’s demurrer is directed solely to the $75,000.00 Improvement Funds and fails to address the $250,000 Dandana Payment whatsoever. Additionally, a review of Paragraphs 51 and 52 reflects that the conversion cause of action as directed against Wardeh pertains to the $250,000 Dandana Payment payment only. 

Wardeh’s demurrer to the second cause of action is overruled.

 

2. Wardeh’s Motion to Strike FAC 

Legal Standard 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 436, “the court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.” The grounds for a motion to strike must “appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437.) 

Discussion 

Wardeh moves the Court for an order striking out Plaintiff’s FAC on the basis that it was untimely filed without leave of court. Wardeh moves to strike out the second cause of action for conversion on the grounds that it was not sufficiently pled. 

Wardeh’s motion to strike is summarily denied. As stated above, Wardeh’s request to strike out the FAC reads as an improper motion for reconsideration. Wardeh’s second argument, regarding the sufficiency of the conversion cause of action, is a ground for demurrer, which was raised and overruled by the Court above.
