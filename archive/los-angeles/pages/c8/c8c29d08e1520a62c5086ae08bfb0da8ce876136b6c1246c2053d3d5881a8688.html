Motion to Compel Further Response to Requests for Production; Request for Sanctions

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 24NNCV02870

CASE NAME: Yesayan v. AVB Studio City III-A, LP

Moving Party: Plaintiff Silva Yesayan

Responding Party: Defendant AVB Studio City III-A, LP

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: DENY.

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is an action arising from habitability concerns, conditions, water intrusion, and eviction. On July 12, 2024, plaintiff Silva Yesayan (Plaintiff) filed the initial Complaint against defendant AVB Studio City III-A, LP (Defendant). On October 7, 2024, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint alleging breach of implied warranty of habitability, breach of covenant of quiet enjoyment, negligence, private nuisance, fraudulent/bad faith eviction, wrongful eviction, breach of contract, intentional infliction of emotional distress, abuse of legal process, and declaratory relief.

On August 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion seeking to compel further responses to Requests for Production (RPDs) served on Defendant. On August 12, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. On August 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

            “Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” the trial court lacks jurisdiction to order further responses to interrogatories and/or requests for production of documents.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300 (c), 2031.310 (c); see Vidal Sassoon v. Superior Court (1983) 147 Cal.App.3d 681, 685 (45-day deadline to file motion to compel further is mandatory and jurisdictional).)

Compel Further Production of Documents

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that … (1) [a] statement of compliance with the demand is¿incomplete[;] … (2) [a] representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or¿evasive[; or] (3) [a]n objection in the response is without merit or too general.”¿¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(a).)

“A motion under subdivision (a) shall: (1) set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand; (2) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration” and (3) contain a separate statement or a concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(b).)

If a court¿determines¿the scope of discovery imposes a burden, causes great expense or is intrusive, and such outweighs the likelihood that the discoverable information¿sought¿will garner admissible evidence, the court shall limit the scope of discovery. (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2017.020.)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Timeliness

            Here, verified responses to request for production were provided by Defendant on July 22, 2026. (Mot., Bakhdanyan Decl., Exh. C.) The instant motion was filed by Plaintiff on August 5, 2026, 13 days after the issuance of responses. Thus, the motion is timely and conforms with the 45-day statutory deadline.  

Separate Statement

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345 requires a separate statement for motions to compel further responses. Plaintiff filed a separate statement in compliance with rule 3.1345(a) for the noticed motion. Accordingly, this requirement is satisfied

Meet and Confer

            A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a¿meet¿and confer declaration “showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a); § 2030.300, subd. (b)(2).)

            Here, Plaintiff declares that they have attempted to engage in meet and confer efforts by sending two detailed meet and confer letters but Defendant responded only in writing declining to provide amended responses and did not meet and confer. (Bakhdanyan Decl., ¶¶ 3, 5-6.) Defendant does not acknowledge having engaged in meet and confer efforts.

Neither party confirms whether meet and confer efforts were conducted in good faith if at all. The parties are admonished to comply with meet and confer requirements in the future; however, the Court will address the motions on its merits.

Compel Further Production of Documents 

            Plaintiff moves the Court to compel Defendant to produce further responses to RPD items 21-24 and 28-38 arguing that Defendant’s responses are incomplete. (Mot., pp. 2-3.) Items #21-24 and #28-31 seek incident, maintenance, complaint, insurance-fact, inspection, repair, photograph, and relocation records at the subject complex and Items #32-38 seek applicable policies and records identifying similar habitability related claims or tenancy actions.

Defendant responded to RPDs #21-24, 28, 30-31, and 33-38 by asserting objections only. (Mot., p. 3; Separate Statement (SS), pp. 1-4, 7-14.) Plaintiff argues that Defendant must provide further substantive responses to the relevant portions and log any actually withheld privileged material. (Ibid.)  

Plaintiff responded to RPD #29 stating they would provide the documents at the time of deposition and responded to RPD #32 similarly adding that the documents would be produced under stipulated protective order. (Mot., p. 3; SS, pp. 7-9.) Defendant argues that the documents were not produced, Defendant did not provide a definite production date, and the responses were not verified. (Mot., p. 3.) Plaintiff argues that Defendant should be compelled to provide further responses. (SS, pp. 7-9.)

Defendant argues that this is a perfect example of a “fishing expedition” and argues that Plaintiff goes beyond proper scope of discovery in seeking documents pertaining to any type of water intrusion, water discharge event, habitability complaint, lawsuit, termination of tenancy, or unlawful detainer, spanning hundreds of residents, properties, and units over five years. (Opp., pp. 1, 4.) Further, Defendant argues that Plaintiff has sought identical information via at least two other recent methods of discovery, both of which are the subject of pending motions (Opp., p. 1.) As a result, Defendant argues that this inquiry is overburdensome and a tremendous undertaking. (Ibid.)

In reply, Plaintiff argues that Defendant has only confirmed non-compliance with the RPDs. (Reply, pp. 2, 7-8.) Plaintiff reiterates that the information sought is relevant and tied to the alleged conduct. (Reply, pp. 3-4.) Plaintiff argues that they cannot obtain the information sought through alternative channels because Defendant controls the records being requested. (Reply, p. 5.) Lastly, Plaintiff argues that Defendant describes ordinary electronic discovery tasks, not undue burden supporting denial of the instant motion. (Reply, p. 6.)

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) Here, the RPDs were served on Defendant on June 20, 2026, and due July 20, 2026. (Bakhdanyan Decl., ¶ 3.) However, responses were not provided until July 22, 2026. (RPD SS, Exh. B.) This is beyond the 30-day statutory deadline, and any objections are waived pursuant to Code Civil Procedure section 2031.290.

Here, Plaintiff seeks evidence concerning maintenance, complaints, insurance-fact, inspections, repairs, photographs, relocation records, and habitability and tenancy actions at the complex. (Bakhdanyan Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.) However, the FAC alleges habitability issues due to water intrusion from sewage pipes in Plaintiff’s single unit. (FAC, ¶¶ 16-18.) “[E]vidence is "relevant" if it might reasonably assist a party in evaluating its case, preparing for trial, or facilitating a settlement.” (Glenfed Dev. Corp. v. Superior Court (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1117.) Plaintiff has not established that the evidence of property wide complaints, tenancy actions, and records of any condition made during the past five years is relevant to a Complaint based on a single unit, and perhaps even adjacent units. Thus, the Court finds that the scope of RPD Items #21-24 and 28-38 are overbroad, burdensome, and Plaintiff has not demonstrated good cause for compelling further response.

Accordingly, the motion to compel further is DENIED.

Sanctions

The Court shall impose sanction on the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to compel responses to interrogatories or RPD. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300(c), 2030.290(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a) [the court can award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party seeking to compel discovery even though no opposition was filed, the opposition was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed].)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

Plaintiff request imposition of sanctions in the amount of $2,685 against Defendant consisting of $2,625 in attorney’s fees reflecting 7.5 hours meeting and conferring, reviewing Defendant’s objections, preparing the instant motion, declaration, and proposed order at a rate of $325 per hour, plus a $60 filing fee. (Bakhdanyan Decl., ¶¶ 10-12.)

Here, the Court will not award sanctions as the motion to compel further is denied.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff Silva Yesayan’s Motion to Compel Further Response to Requests for Production is DENIED.

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

 

Motion to Quash Subpoena

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 24NNCV02870

CASE NAME: Yesayan v. AVB Studio City III-A, LP

Moving Party: Defendant AVB Studio City III-A, LP

Responding Party: Plaintiff Silva Yesayan

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT.

BACKGROUND

This is an action arising from habitability concerns, conditions, water intrusion, and eviction. On July 12, 2024, plaintiff Silva Yesayan (Plaintiff) filed the initial Complaint against defendant AVB Studio City III-A, LP (Defendant). On October 7, 2024, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint alleging breach of implied warranty of habitability, breach of covenant of quiet enjoyment, negligence, private nuisance, fraudulent/bad faith eviction, wrongful eviction, breach of contract, intentional infliction of emotional distress, abuse of legal process, and declaratory relief.

On July 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to quash subpoenas, or limit the scope, for deposition of (1) the Custodian of Records for the Los Angeles Housing Department (the City), and (2) the Custodian of Records for the County of Los Angeles Department of Public Health (the County).

            On July 29, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition to the motion. No reply has been filed as of August 18, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

            If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion … or upon the court's own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).)

            A motion to quash production of documents pursuant to a deposition subpoena must be accompanied by a separate statement setting forth the inspection demand at issue and the factual and legal reasons why production should not be compelled. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345 subd. (a)(5).)¿ 

A party seeking discovery from a person who is not a party to the action may obtain discovery by oral deposition, written deposition, or deposition for production of business records.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.010.)¿

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Defendant moves the court for an order quashing two subpoenas served to the City and the County and an imposition of sanctions in the amount of $3,300 to compensate Defendant for needing to file this motion. (Mot., p. 1.)

Motion to Quash Subpoenas

Defendant argues that the scope of Plaintiff’s discovery inquiries are overbroad and seek to uncover property wide complaints of any condition made during the past five years whereas the instant action focuses only on one unit. (Mot., pp. 3-4.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff seeks irrelevant information that does not have any tendency to prove or disprove any disputed fact that is of consequence to the action. (Mot., p. 5.) Moreover, Defendant argues that Plaintiff does not make any allegations about habitability issues in the complex or common areas yet seeks information about other residential units and common areas. (Mot., p. 7.) Defendant argues that the scope of discovery is therefore intrusive, burdensome, and outweighs the likelihood that the information will lead to admissible evidence. (Ibid.) Lastly, Defendant argues that sanctions are warranted in the amount of $3,300. (Mot., p. 9.)

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the First Amended Complaint (FAC) alleges that Defendant failed to maintain the property in a habitable condition, breached the covenant of quiet enjoyment, acted negligently, maintained a nuisance, and wrongfully and fraudulently evicted Plaintiff after she asserted her rights. (Opp., pp. 2, 5.) Plaintiff argues that evidence of prior governmental complaints, inspections, or notices of violations are directly relevant to these claims. (Ibid.) Plaintiff argues that the records requested through subpoena are maintained by government agencies in the ordinary course of business and may establish Defendant’s knowledge of violations and recurring habitability concerns. (Opp., pp. 2, 5-6.) Further, Plaintiff argues that discovery is meant to give parties the fullest possible knowledge of the issues, and discovery statutes must be construed broadly in favor of disclosure. (Opp., p. 3.) Plaintiff argues that discoverability does not equate to admissibility, and Defendant’s argument regarding inadmissible evidence is misplaced at this stage. (Opp., p. 4.) Further, Plaintiff argues that Defendant does not explain why the 5-year temporal scope of records requested is excessive. (Opp., pp. 7, 9.) Thus, Plaintiff argues that Defendant has not carried their burden in establishing that the information sought is overbroad or burdensome, and request imposition of sanctions against Defendant in the amount of $4,200. (Opp., pp. 12-13.)

Here, Defendant seeks quashing of subpoenas to the City and the County. (Yamabe Decl., ¶¶ 13-15.) Alternatively, Defendant asks the Court to reduce the scope and eliminate Requests for Production (RFAs) numbers 39 and 40 issued to the City, and numbers 41, 42, and 43 issued to the County. (Separate Statement pp. 1-2.) Plaintiff seeks evidence from government agencies concerning Defendant’s knowledge of prior habitability complaints, conditions, inspections, and maintenance practices. (Bakhdanyan Decl., ¶¶ 4, 8.) However, the FAC alleges habitability issues due to water intrusion from sewage pipes in Plaintiff’s unit. (FAC, ¶¶ 16-18.) “[E]vidence is "relevant" if it might reasonably assist a party in evaluating its case, preparing for trial, or facilitating a settlement.” (Glenfed Dev. Corp. v. Superior Court (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1117.) Plaintiff has not established that the evidence of property wide complaints of any condition made during the past five years is relevant to a Complaint based on a single unit, and perhaps even adjacent units. Thus, the Court finds that the scope of the subpoenas issued is overbroad and burdensome.

Accordingly, the motion to quash subpoena is GRANTED.

Sanctions

When granting a motion to quash, “the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making…the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made…without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2(a).)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

Defendant requests imposition of sanctions in the amount of $3,300 against Plaintiff reflecting 12 hours meeting and conferring, preparing the instant motion, declaration, and anticipated time spent reviewing an opposition and drafting a reply at a rate of $275 per hour. (Yamabe Decl., ¶¶ 19-23.)

Here, the Court in its discretion will reduce the fee to $550 in attorney’s fees reflecting 2.0 hours preparing the instant motion at a rate of $275 per hour.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant AVB Studio City III-A, LP’s motion to quash subpoena is GRANTED.

Defendant’s request for sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $550 to be paid by Plaintiff within 15 days from this ruling.

MOVING PARTY for this motion shall submit a proposed order consist with these rulings within five days and provide notice.

Motion to Strike Answer

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 25NNCV01521

CASE NAME: Simounian v. Gevorgyan

Moving Party: Plaintiff Simon Simounian

Responding Party: Defendant Arayik Gevorgyan

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

On March 6, 2025, Plaintiff Simon Simounian (Plaintiff) filed this action against Defendant Arayik Gevorgyan (Defendant) arising out of a motor vehicle accident which occurred on October 29, 2023.

On July 7, 2025, the clerk entered default against Defendant.

On September 10, 2025, the Court set an Order to Show Cause (OSC) re Failure to Proceed with Default Judgment for November 19, 2025. Plaintiff was ordered to submit either a default judgment package or a declaration at least five calendar days before the hearing, and the Court warned that failure to do so could result in dismissal. On November 19, 2025, Plaintiff did not file a default judgment package, did not file a declaration, and did not appear at the OSC hearing. The Court therefore dismissed the action.

On March 27, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s motion to vacate dismissal due to counsel’s inadvertent clerical error.

On March 30, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer to the original Complaint.

On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion seeking to strike Defendant’s amended answer on the grounds that default has been entered, and Defendant is prohibited from making affirmative steps in litigation. On August 10, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

No reply has been filed as on August 17, 2026.  

 

LEGAL STANDARD

            The grounds for a motion to strike shall appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437(a).) Where a paragraph contains multiple factual allegations or distinct propositions, a responding party may not simply admit or deny the paragraph as a whole if doing so would obscure which parts are true and which are disputed. Instead, the party must specifically admit or deny the separate branches of the allegation. If the paragraph embraces several clauses or propositions, each material subpart must be addressed separately and disjunctively, or the denial is evasive and insufficient. (More v. Del Valle (1865) 28 Cal. 170, 172.)

“Before filing a motion to strike pursuant to this chapter, the moving party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purpose of determining if an agreement can be reached that resolves the objections to be raised in the motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5(a).) “The parties shall meet and confer at least 5 days before the date a motion to strike must be filed.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5(a)(2).)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s answer must be stricken because default was entered by the court clerk, immediately terminating Defendant’s right to participate in litigation. (Mot., p. 4.) Plaintiff argues that Defendant remains in default and must take steps to set aside the default before filing an Answer. (Mot., p. 5.)  

In opposition, Defendant argues that striking this answer would just cause Defendant to seek the same relief due to its mistaken reliance on the Court’s prior dismissal, accordingly Defendant argues this motion is a waste of the Court’s time. (Opp., p. 1.) Further, Defendant argues that the motion is untimely, the underlying motion to vacate dismissal was untimely, and Plaintiff has failed to meet and confer prior to filing this motion to strike. (Ibid.)

Meet and Confer

Here, Plaintiff makes no reference to meet and confer efforts, rather Plaintiff mentions orally moving to strike Defendant’s answer and being instructed to file a noticed motion. (Zargaryan Decl., ¶ 15.) Nonetheless, “determination by the court that the meet and confer process was insufficient shall not be grounds to grant or deny the motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5(a)(4).) The parties are admonished to comply with meet and confer requirements in the future.

Motion to Strike

            Here, in the March 27, 2026, Order vacating dismissal, the Court described Plaintiff’s failure to submit a default judgment packet due to mistake or inadvertence but did not set aside entry of default on Defendant. (See 3/27/26 Minute Order, p. 3.) “The entry of a default terminates a defendant's rights to take any further affirmative steps in the litigation until either its default is set aside or a default judgment is entered.” (Devlin v. Kearny Mesa Amc/Jeep/Renault (1984) 155 Cal.App.3d 381, 385.) Accordingly, Defendant is prohibited from making the affirmative step of filing a responsive pleading until default is set aside. (Parish v. Peters (1991) 1 Cal.App.4th 202, 209, n.8.) To date Defendant has not successfully moved to set aside entry of default.

Accordingly, the motion to strike Defendant’s answer is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff Simon Simounian’s motion to strike Defendant’s Answer is GRANTED.

MOVING PARTY shall provide notice and provide a proposed order consistent with this ruling within 15 days from this date.

MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL RESPOSES; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS AGAINST DEFENDANT ROYAL

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 25NNCV02550

CASE NAME: Rose Gloria by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez, et al. vs Royal Palms Post Acute, LLC, et al.    

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Rose Gloria, by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT.

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is an elder abuse action. Decedent Rose Gloria, by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez (Plaintiff), and her children, Yolanda Gonzalez, Jose Gonzalez, Lisa White, Edward Gloria, Cynthia Castro, and Michael Gloria have brought this action against defendants Royal Palms Post Acute, LLC, (Defendant Royal) Palms Licensee 3, LLC, Beecan Health, LLC, and Steeve Henry, the owners and operators of the skilled nursing facility, Royal Palms Post Acute (Facility), where Ms. Gloria was a resident patient. On April 14, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against all defendants alleging Wrongful Death.

On July 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motions to compel initial responses to Form Interrogatories (Res ID: 1269), Special Interrogatories (Res ID: 2683), and Requests for Production of Documents (RPDs) (Res ID: 6650) served on Defendant Royal.

No oppositions have been filed as of August 14, 2026. 

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom requests for production of documents are directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) The party also waives the right to make any objections, including one based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to production of documents other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.020, subd. (a), 2031.300.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300; Sinaiko, supra, 148 Cal.App.4th at 411.)

A party must respond to interrogatories within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom interrogatories are directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).) The party also waives the right to make any objections, including one based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to interrogatories other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2024.020, subd. (a), 2030.290.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411.)

 

DISCUSSION           

Motion to Compel Initial Responses

Plaintiff contends that Defendant Royal failed to timely respond to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and RPDs on or by the statutory deadline. (Mot., p. 4.)

Here, Plaintiff’s counsel served Defendant Royal with the Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and RPDs by electronic service on March 13, 2026. (Mot. Compel Form Interrog., Horii Decl., ¶ 2; Mot. Compel Special Interrog., Horii Decl., ¶ 2; Mot., Compel RPDs, Horii Decl., ¶ 2.) Plaintiff contends that initial responses were due April 14, 2026. (Horii Decl., ¶ 3.) On April 15, Plaintiff’s counsel provided Defendant Royal with a two-week extension via email, but Defendant Royal again failed to respond. (Horii Decl., ¶ 4.) On April 22, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel left a voicemail and on April 23, 2026, spoke with Defendant Royal’s counsel via telephone and gave a subsequent extension making responses due May 15, 2026. (Horii Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) Defendant Royal again failed to respond. (Ibid.) As of July 1, 2026, Plaintiff declares that Defendant Royal has not provided verified responses. (Horii Decl., ¶ 7.)

Since Defendant Royal was properly served and failed to respond to discovery requests by the statutory deadline, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling Defendant Royal to provide responses without objections to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and RPDs.

Accordingly, the motion to compel initial responses is GRANTED.

Sanctions

The Court shall impose sanction on the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to compel responses to interrogatories or RPD. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300(c), 2030.290(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a) [the court can award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party seeking to compel discovery even though no opposition was filed, the opposition was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed].)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

Defendant Royal’s failure to comply with discovery requests necessitated the motion, and the conduct is subject to sanctions. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a).) Moreover, The Court finds Plaintiff’s Notice of Motion complies with Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040.

Plaintiff’s counsel requests sanctions in the amount of $633.54 for each motion. (Mot., p. 6.) Plaintiff has filed a total of 12 motions to compel initial responses from four total defendants, six of which are being heard on August 21, 2026, and the remaining 6 being heard on August 28, 2026. For all 12 motions, Plaintiff seeks attorney’s fees of $6,882.50 plus $720 ($60.00 x 12) in filing costs, totaling $7,602.50. (Horii Decl., ¶¶ 10-11.) The $6,882.50 in fees reflects 12.0 hours spent drafting the motions, 3.0 hours anticipated drafting replies, and 1 hour spent attending the hearings at a rate of $395.00 per hour; plus, 0.75 hours spent by Partner Kathryn Cassano, Esq. reviewing the motions at a rate of $750.00 per hour. LEGAL STANDARD

            “Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” the trial court lacks jurisdiction to order further responses to interrogatories and/or requests for production of documents.  (Code Civ. Compel Further Production of Documents

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that … (1) [a] statement of compliance with the demand is¿incomplete[;] … (2) [a] representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or¿evasive[; or] (3) [a]n objection in the response is without merit or too general.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(a).)

“A motion under subdivision (a) shall: (1) set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand; (2) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration” and (3) contain a separate statement or a concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(b).)

If a court¿determines¿the scope of discovery imposes a burden, causes great expense or is intrusive, and such outweighs the likelihood that the discoverable information¿sought¿will garner admissible evidence, the court shall limit the scope of discovery. (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2017.020.)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Timeliness

            Here, Plaintiff purports that verified responses to request for production and interrogatories were provided by Semerdjian on February 24, 2026. (Mot., p. 4.) Thus, the motion is timely and conforms with the 45-day statutory deadline.

Separate Statement

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345 requires a separate statement for motions to compel further responses. Plaintiff filed separate statements in compliance with rule 3.1345(a) for each of the instant motions. Accordingly, this requirement is satisfied

Meet and Confer

            A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a¿meet¿and confer declaration “showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a); § 2030.300, subd. (b)(2).)

            Here, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have spoken with defense counsel and requested further responses, but defense counsel has not provided such responses even though they agreed to provide information. (Fry Decl., ¶ 4.) Notably, these efforts lack any attempts in person, by telephone, or by video conference to meet and confer. Therefore, the Court finds counsel’s meet and confer efforts insufficient and noncompliant with Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040. 

However, the Court will address the motions on its merits.

On November 5, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against Semerdjian and Green Dragon (collectively, Defendants) alleging strict liability under the dog bite statute, negligence, and conscious disregard of the rights and safety of Plaintiff.

On April 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel further response to Form Interrogatories (Res ID: 4728) served on Green Dragon.

On the same day Plaintiff filed separate motions to compel further response to Requests for Production (RPDs) (Res ID: 5402) served on Green Dragon and RPDs (Res ID: 1264) and Form Interrogatories (Res ID: 7931) served on Defendant Semerdjian.

On July 24, 2026, Green Dragon served a supplemental response to the Form Interrogatories at issue. On July 27, 2026, both Defendants filed a consolidated opposition to all motions.

On July 31, 2026, Plaintiff filed Second Separate Statement in response to Green Dragon’s supplemental response. On August 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a consolidated reply.

This ruling addresses the Motion to Compel Further Response to Form Interrogatories served on Green Dragon. (Res ID: 4728)

 

LEGAL STANDARD

            “Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” the trial court lacks jurisdiction to order further responses to interrogatories and/or requests for production of documents.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300 (c), 2031.310 (c); see Vidal Sassoon v. Superior Court (1983) 147 Cal.App.3d 681, 685 (45-day deadline to file motion to compel further is mandatory and jurisdictional).)

Compel Further Responses to Interrogatories

            “On receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that… (1) [a]n answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete; (2) [a]n exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or…(3) [a]n objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300(a).)

“A motion under subdivision (a) shall: (1) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration” and (2) contain a separate statement or a concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300(b).)

            If a party fails to timely respond, objections are waived unless the court relieves the waiver upon a showing of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect and a subsequently served compliant response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) 

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Timeliness

            Here, Plaintiff purports that verified responses to interrogatories were provided by Green Dragon on February 24, 2026. (Mot. to Compel Further Response (MTCF) to Form Interrogatories, p. 3.) Green Dragon’s counsel confirms that verified responses were served on February 24, 2026. (Saboorian Decl., ¶ 3.) The instant motion was filed on April 6, 2026, 41 days from issuance of responses. Thus, the motion is timely and conforms with the 45-day statutory deadline.

Separate Statement

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345 requires a separate statement for motions to compel further responses. Plaintiff filed a separate statement in compliance with rule 3.1345(a) for the instant motion. Accordingly, this requirement is satisfied

Meet and Confer

            A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a¿meet¿and confer declaration “showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a); § 2030.300, subd. (b)(2).)

            Here, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have spoken with defense counsel and requested further responses, but defense counsel has not provided such responses even though they agreed to provide information. (Fry Decl., ¶ 4.) Notably, these efforts lack any attempts in person, by telephone, or by video conference to meet and confer. Therefore, the Court finds counsel’s meet and confer efforts insufficient and noncompliant with Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040. 

However, the Court will address the motions on its merits.

Compel Further Response to Interrogatories

Plaintiff moves the Court to compel Green Dragon to produce further responses to Form Interrogatories Items #4.1, 4.2, 12.1, 14.1, 15.1, and 17.1. (Mot., p. 2.) The Court notes that because Green Dragon provided responses to the RPDs after the deadline, objections are waived absent relief by the Court. (Fry Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) In their Second Separate statement, filed July 31, 2026, regarding Form Interrogatories served on Green Dragon, Plaintiff reduces further responses sought to Items #12.1, and 15.1.

Plaintiff argues that Items #12.1, and 15.1 request information on witnesses, contentions of whether a violation of statute caused the incident, and documents supporting affirmative defenses and denials. (Mot., pp. 4-6.) Regarding Item #12.1, Plaintiff argues that there was another employee who witnessed the event and it is unclear whether that employee is Semerdjian or another individual. (Ibid.) Regarding Item #15.1, Plaintiff argues that Green Dragon was asked to identify each denial of a material allegation and each special or affirmative defense but only addressed the affirmative defenses, making his response incomplete. (Mot., p. 6.)

In opposition, Green Dragon argues that the only remaining interrogatories at issue are #2.2, 2.3, 2.5-2.8. (Opp., p. 3.) Green Dragon argues that this dispute concerns a dog bite and his general residential, educational, and personal background is not at issue. Green Dragon argues that his identity is known, and unrestricted disclosure of his personal information is unnecessarily intrusive and disproportionate to the needs of the action. (Opp., pp. 3-4.) Alternatively, Green Dragon argues that the information sought should be limited in scope and permit redaction of personal identifiers. (Opp., p. 4.)

In reply, Plaintiff argues that Defendants have failed to adequately identify witnesses. (Opp., p. 3.)

The Court finds that Plaintiff does not demonstrate good cause for compelling further response to Form Interrogatories #12.1 and 15.1. because Green Dragon has provided a supplemental response with substantive answers. (Saboorian Decl., Exh. A, ¶ 12.1, 15.1.) While Plaintiff maintains that there was another employee who witnessed the attack, Plaintiff does not show where Defendants claimed there were three witnesses to the event. (Reply, p. 3.) In their second separate statement, Plaintiff makes similar unsupported argument. (Second SS, p. 3.)

Accordingly, the motion to compel further response to Form Interrogatories as to Items #12.1 and 15.1 is DENIED.

Sanctions

The Court shall impose sanction on the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to compel responses to interrogatories or RPD. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300(c), 2030.290(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a) [the court can award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party seeking to compel discovery even though no opposition was filed, the opposition was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed].)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

Plaintiff request imposition of sanctions in the amount of $1,110 against Plaintiff. (Mot., p. 9.) Defendant’s requests $1,110 in attorney’s fees reflecting 3 hours preparing the instant motion, at a rate of $350 per hour, plus a $60 filing fee. (Fry Decl., ¶ 12.)

Here, sanctions will not be awarded because the motion to compel further response has been denied.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff Julio Bello’s Motion to Compel Further Response to Form Interrogatories is DENIED as to Items #12.1 and 15.1.

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED.  

MOVING PARTY shall provide notice and provide a proposed order consistent with this ruling within 15 days from this date.

(Motorsport); California Department of Motor Vehicles; and DOES 1-10 inclusive asserting causes of action for (1) claim and delivery of personal property; (2) conversion; (3) quiet title; and (4) declaratory relief. LEGAL STANDARD

“If no provision is made in this chapter or other law for the service of summons, or if a plaintiff, despite exercising reasonable diligence, has been unable to effect service of the summons by any of the methods authorized under this chapter, the court in which the action is pending may, upon motion, direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the party to be served, including by electronic mail or other electronic technology, and that proof of such service be made as prescribed by the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(1).) This requires a plaintiff to “set forth facts detailing all attempts to serve the defendant by each of the methods prescribed by statute.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(2).)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Plaintiff argues that they have exercised reasonable diligence to attempt service through authorized methods but have not been able to personally serve Motorsport at its principal place of business or the residential address of its owner. (Mot., p. 2.) For this reason, Plaintiff moves the court to authorize service via electronic mail. (Ibid.)

First, Plaintiff attempted to serve Motorsport at its business address, but the premises was vacant. (Mot., p. 3.) Per an onsite manager, Plaintiff argues that Motorsport moved off the premises three years ago. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. D; Mot., p. 3.) Subsequently, Plaintiff attempted to serve Motorsport’s sole owner and agent at their residential address but was unsuccessful. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. E; Mot., p. 3.) Plaintiff seeks to serve Motorsport using a business email found on an invoice issued by Motorsport prior to the commencement of this action. (Mot., p. 4.) Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have communicated with Motorsport’s sole owner via email before and received an immediate response. (Krugliak Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that this is the most likely method for providing notice of the summons, complaint, and other related documents. (Ibid.)

In the supplemental declaration, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that the mailing address on record for Defendant is outdated or unreliable and in light of unsuccessful attempts at that address, there is no reason to believe Defendant would receive notice at that address. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 7.) Plaintiff’s counsel also declares that the address on file with the Secretary of State as Defendant’s principal place of business is similarly outdated and unreliable. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 8.) Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have had success communicating with Defendant through a confirmed email address in a previous action and as recent as August 10, 2026, when sharing a courtesy copy of the notice of continuance. (Supp. Decl., ¶¶ 11-15.) Further, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that the court in its previous lawsuit against Defendant (Case No. 26VECV01113) granted its motion to serve by electronic mail based on these facts. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 11.)

Here, reasonable efforts to personally serve Motorsport have been exhausted. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. E.) Motorsport’s mailing address and principal place of business address appear to be outdated, and Plaintiff has attempted service at the residential address of its owner or agent. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 5.)

Under section 413.30 the court is authorized “to direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice”, this doesn’t necessarily mean electronic service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30(a)(1).) Plaintiff has shown, through supplemental declaration, that service through electronic mail is reasonably calculated to give actual notice. Defendant has responded to Plaintiff’s communications through the email address provided and the court, in a prior lawsuit between the same parties, has authorized electronic service based on these facts.

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff VW Credit Inc.’s Motion to Authorize Electronic Service is GRANTED.

ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

BACKGROUND

This is a lemon law action. In 2025, Plaintiffs Lewis Ojong Asu and Mirabel Bahbit Sama (Plaintiffs) purchased a used 2022 vehicle. On April 2, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Complaint against defendants Mercedez-Benz USA, LLC (Defendant), Los Angeles M, Inc., and DBA Keyes European alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Act and negligent repair.

On May 22, 2026, Defendant filed the instant demurrer as to the first cause of action for violation of the Song-Beverly Act, the only cause of action asserted against Defendant.

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Judicial notice may not be taken of any matter, unless authorized or required by law.  (Evid. Code, § 450.) Under Evidence Code section 452, the Court may take judicial notice of the private and official acts of the legislative, executive, and judicial departments of the United States and of any state of the United States; and the records of any court of the United States and of any state of the United States; and it must take judicial notice of those acts and records if a party so requests and provides sufficient notice to the adverse party. (Evid. Code, §§ 452, 453.)  “In determining the sufficiency of a complaint against demurrer a court will consider matters that may be judicially noticed.” (Joslin v. H.A.S. Ins. Brokerage (1986) 184 Cal.App.3d 369, 374.) 

Here, Defendant requests judicial notice of Exhibit (3): The Contract, signed by Plaintiff on March 27, 2025, pursuant to Evidence Code section 452(h). (RJN, p. 2.) The request is denied. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (h).)

LEGAL STANDARD

            “[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

Further, the court may, upon motion, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper, strike any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (a).) The court may also strike all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (b).) The grounds for a motion to strike are that the pleading has irrelevant, false, or improper matter, or has not been drawn or filed in conformity with laws. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437.)

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

DISCUSSION

Meet and Confer

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that before the filing of a demurrer the moving party “shall meet and confer in person or by telephone” with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(a).)  The parties are to meet and confer at least five days before the date the responsive pleading is due. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(a)(2).)  Thereafter, the moving party shall file and serve a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(a)(3).) 

Defendant’s counsel declares they sent a meet and confer letter to Plaintiff’s counsel on June 6, 2026, asking Plaintiff to dismiss the action. (Karapetyan Decl., ¶ 3.) Defendant’s counsel declares that they followed up with Plaintiff’s counsel via telephone on June 8, 2026, and Plaintiff refused to dismiss the FAC. (Ibid; see also Decl. of Demurring or Moving Party Regarding Meet and Confer.) Plaintiff does not address meet and confer efforts.

Although one letter and phone call asking opposing party to dismiss the action outright does not sufficiently exhaust meet and confer efforts, the Court cannot overrule a demurrer based on an insufficient meet and confer.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41(a)(3).). The Court will review the demurrer on its merits but admonishes Defendant to make more of an effort to meet and confer in the future.

Demurrer       

Defendant demurs to the first cause of action for violations of the Song-Beverly Act on the grounds that the subject vehicle is used, and Plaintiffs lack standing to bring Song-Beverly Act claims. (Mot., p. 4.)

Violation of the Song-Beverly Act

Every manufacturer of consumer goods sold in this state and for which the manufacturer has made an express warranty shall, “if unable to service or repair a new motor vehicle…to conform to the applicable express warranties after a reasonable number of attempts, the manufacturer shall either promptly replace the new motor vehicle in accordance with subparagraph (A) or promptly make restitution to the buyer in accordance with subparagraph (B).” (Civ. Code, § 1793.2(d)(2).)

            A “new” motor vehicle is defined by statute as a “motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer's new car warranty.” (Civ. Code, § 1793.22(e)(2).) The California Supreme Court held that a used motor vehicle purchased with an unexpired manufacturer’s new car warranty does not qualify as a “motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer’s new car warranty” under section 1793.22, subdivision (e)(2)’s definition of “new motor vehicle” unless the new car warranty was issued with the sale. (Rodriguez v. FCA US LLC (2024) 17 Cal.5th 189, 197.)

Defendant argues that the subject vehicle is a certified pre-owned vehicle (CPO) which does not qualify as a new vehicle under the Act. (Mot., p. 6.) Defendant argues that a CPO is not sold with a new car warranty as part of the sale and instead is sold with the remaining warranty on the pre-owned vehicle. (Mot., pp. 6-7.) Further, Defendant points out that the vehicle was a 2022 model not purchased by Plaintiff until 2025 with over 14,000 miles on the odometer. (Mot., p. 10.) Defendant argues that this shows the vehicle was not delivered to Plaintiffs with the manufacturer’s new car warranty, and Plaintiffs’ first cause of action must fail. (Ibid.)

In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that nothing in the Complaint suggests the vehicle was used. (Opp., p. 4.) However, Plaintiff’s also argue that Defendant provided Plaintiffs with a certified pre-owned vehicle warranty which, they argue, is subject to the Song-Beverly Act. (Opp., p. 5.) Plaintiffs request that if the Court sustains Plaintiffs’ demurrer, leave to amend be granted so they can plead facts with more specificity. (Opp., 9.)

In reply, Defendant points out that Plaintiffs do not dispute that the vehicle was pre-owned. (Reply, p. 1.) Defendant argues that the Complaint does not allege issuance of a new car warranty, but describes a different warranty issued specifically with a CPO vehicle. (Ibid.) Further, Defendant argues that leave to amend must be denied because Plaintiffs cannot cure the facts with amendment. (Reply, p. 5.)

Here, in the Complaint, Plaintiffs allege that the vehicle is pre-owned. (Comp., ¶ 8.) The Court in Rodriguez v. FCA US, LLC (2024) discussed how the sale of a vehicle to its first retail buyer is what gives rise to a new car warranty. (Rodriguez, supra, 17 Cal.5th 189, 199.) Pre-Owned cars, which have been sold to a prior retail purchaser, are not sold with a manufacturer's new car warranty.” (Ibid.) Further, a “vehicle purchased with an unexpired manufacturer’s new car warranty does not qualify as a ‘motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer’s new car warranty’ under section 1793.22, subdivision (e)(2)’s definition of ‘new motor vehicle’ unless the new car warranty was issued with the sale.” (Id., at 196.) Therefore, when the vehicle was sold to Plaintiff, as a CPO vehicle, it did not carry a new car warranty.

However, the Court acknowledges that Plaintiff alleges Defendant issued an additional used car warranty upon purchase. (Comp., ¶ 8.) In Kiluk v. Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC found that “although the Song-Beverly Act generally binds only distributors and retail sellers in the sale of used goods…[the Vehicle manufacturer] stepped into that role by issuing an express warranty on the sale of a used vehicle” and the defect manifested during the coverage period of the used vehicle warranty. (Kiluk v. Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC (2019) 43 Cal.App.5th 334, 337.) Here, Plaintiff’s lawsuit similarly derives from a CPO vehicle, but they have not alleged how Defendant has stepped into the role of a distributor or retail seller, or which express warranty the defect manifested under. (See Complaint.) Given the liberal stand on leave to amend, and the reasonable possibility for Plaintiff to cure defects, leave to amend is granted. (Goodman, supra, 18 Cal.3d at 349.)

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the first cause of action is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant Mercedez-Benz USA, LLC’s demurrer as to the first cause of action for Violation of the Song-Beverly Act is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

Plaintiff has 20 days from the date of this order to file an amended pleading.

LEGAL STANDARD

The purpose of a preliminary injunction is to preserve the¿status quo¿and prevent irreparable harm pending a trial on the merits.¿(White v. Davis¿(2003) 30 Cal.4th 528, 554.) To obtain a¿preliminary injunction, a moving party must show: (1) she would likely suffer an irreparable injury for which damages would be an inadequate remedy if the injunction¿were¿denied; and (2) there exists a reasonable probability that she will prevail on the merits of her claims.¿(Huong¿Que, Inc. v. Luu¿(2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 400, 408.)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Plaintiffs base their argument for equitable relief on the risk of facing imminent transfer and enforcement of title and loss of possession of their primary residence. (Ex Parte Mot., p. 9.) Plaintiffs argue that the foreclosure sale is void because it was conducted in violation of an automatic stay and bankruptcy filings. (Ex Parte Mot., pp. 8-9.) Further, Plaintiffs argue they are likely to prevail on the merits because the foreclosure sale is voidable, Defendants failed to provide notice of the foreclosure sale, and Defendants were not transparent about their roles. (Ex Parte Mot., p. 8.)

In opposition, Defendants argue that Plaintiffs’ request for preliminary injunction rests on an alleged automatic stay, which has already been adjudicated in bankruptcy court. (Opp., p. 3.) Moreover, Defendants argue that the foreclosure sale at issue took place January 20, 2026, and was promptly recorded in May, well before Plaintiffs filed their Complaint. (Opp., p. 4.)  Thus, no irreparable injury is at risk of occurring. (Ibid.) Further, Defendants argue that Plaintiffs have not established probability of recovering title from defendant Gala, who currently holds title to the Subject Property. (Opp., p. 6.)

Here, Plaintiffs filed for bankruptcy one day before the trustee’s sale was scheduled to take place. (RJN, Exh. C.) Although Plaintiffs bankruptcy attorney requested the sale be stopped the morning of, the sale took place. (Ibid.) This is Plaintiffs’ basis for voidability. However, on April 23, 2026, the bankruptcy court entered an order granting Defendants relief under title 11 United States Code section 362(d)(1) and (d)(4). (Ibid.) The court annulled the automatic stay retroactively to the petition date and determined that Defendants did not violate the stay. (Ibid.) Moreover, Plaintiffs’ bankruptcy proceedings were adjudicated on April 16, 2026, prior to the filing of this action. (RJN, Exh. D.) Further, the Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale concerning the Subject Property had been recorded after adjudication of Plaintiffs’ bankruptcy proceedings and prior to the filing of Plaintiffs’ Complaint. (RJN, Exh. A.)

The Court acknowledges that the irreparable injury Plaintiffs are trying to prevent is the loss of possession of the property through eviction. However, Plaintiffs are trying to prevent conduct that occurred months prior to the filing of the Complaint. Thus, considering the bankruptcy court adjudication and documents submitted by Defendant, Plaintiffs have not demonstrated a reasonable probability of success.

Accordingly, the motion is DENIED

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiffs Leticia Prado and Jack Eliakim’s Motion for Preliminary Injunction is DENIED.
