DEPARTMENT 529 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV02725    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 529

SAMHO TOUR, INC., vs A-JU TOURS, INC.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL MUTUAL TRANS, INC.’S COMPLIANCE WITH DEPOSITION SUBPOENA FOR PRODUCITON OF BUSINESS RECORDS  

 

Date of Hearing:        June 24, 2026                         Trial Date:       November 23, 2026

Department:              529                                         Case No.:        25STCV02725

 

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.    

Responding Party:     Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc.  

Meet and Confer:      Yes. Seror Decl.    

 

BACKGROUND

 

On January 31, 2025, Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. filed a complaint against Defendants A-Ju Tours, Inc. for unfair competition.

 

On April 7, 2025, A-Ju Tours, Inc. filed a cross-complaint against Samho Tour, Inc. for unfair business practices pursuant to Business and Professions Code section 17200 and violation of California’s False Advertising Law Business and Professions Code section 17500, et seq.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Mutual Trans, Inc.’s Compliance with Deposition Subpoena for Production of Records is GRANTED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Samho Tour, Inc. move this court for an order compelling third-party Mutual Trans, Inc. (“Mutual Trans”) to comply with Plaintiff’s Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records and produce all responsive documents within 20 days of this court’s order on this motion. Samho Tour makes the motion on the grounds that Mutual Trans was duly-served with the Subpoena requiring production of business records on April 9, 2026, yet produced no documents and served untimely objections on April 30, 2026.

 

“If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court’s own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders.” (CCP § 1987.1(a).) A party to an action may bring a motion pursuant to CCP § 1987.1(a). (CCP § 1987.1(b)(1).)  

 

Plaintiff argues Mutual Trans failed to properly respond to the Subpoena and instead served untimely objections after the date of production set forth in the Subpoena. Additionally, Mutual Trans produced no documents. Plaintiff contends good cause exists as Defendant’s and Mutual Trans’ entities are inextricably linked. Public filings with the California Secretary of State show that each entities’ sole officers and/or directors share the same surname of “Bahk” and have identical addresses. (Seror Decl., ¶¶ 4-5, Exs. B-C.) Moreover, Mutual Trans’ general business and financial relationship with Defendant is highly relevant to assessing Plaintiff’s unfair competition allegations and reasonably calculated to understanding the scope of Defendant’s unfair business practices. Plaintiff also notes the entered Protective Order is sufficient to address Mutual Trans’ confidentiality concerns.

 

In opposition, A-Ju Tours, Inc. opposes the motion on the grounds Samho has requested various documents from Mutual Trans without the requisite need nor proof of any relevant to the issues in this case. Moreover, A-Ju has provided everything that the Subpoena Deposition requests. Mutual Trans has also provided an opposition arguing Samho seeks to compel a non-party to open its books in a lawsuit that does not concern it. Mutual Trans contends it is a transportation company; it does not advertise A-Ju's tours, set A-Ju's prices, or employ A-Ju's tour guides. Accordingly, the records are not relevant. Whether a non-party transportation company pays its own drivers lawfully is not an issue in this case.  Moreover, production would disclose competitively sensitive records and the parties’ protective order does not protect a non-party.

 

The court finds Plaintiff has set forth good cause for an order compelling Mutual Trans to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records, Nos. 1-24.  First, a party is not required to specifically plead a legal theory before obtaining discovery related to that theory. “Discovery necessarily serves the function of ‘testing the pleadings,’ i.e., enabling a party to determine what his opponent's contentions are and what facts he relies upon to support his contentions.” (Burke v. Superior Court of Sacramento County (1969) 71 Cal.2d 276, 281.) Plaintiff has shown the allegations are not limited to tour guides, but workers as well and workers could include bus drivers. Moreover, the Protective Order allows a non-party to designate information as “confidential” or “highly confidential,” and “any such designation by a non-Party shall have the same force and effect, and create the same duties and obligations, as if made by one of the undersigned Parties hereto.” (Protective Order, Feb. 4, 2026.) Plaintiff also asserts, under penalty of perjury, that A-Ju has refused to produce documents in response to Plaintiff’s requests regarding Mutual Trans. (Supp. Seror Decl. ¶3.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion to compel Mutual Trans, Inc.’s Compliance with Deposition Subpoena for Production of Records is GRANTED.

 

Sanctions

 

Mutual Tarns requests expenses in the amount of $6,771.22, against Samho Tour, Inc. and its counsel of record Matthew Seror, Esq. and his firm Buchalter LLP, jointly and severally pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.2.

 

The court finds the motion was not made in bad faith and denies the request.


Case Number: 25STCV33103    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 529

ARTURO TOPETE vs GENERAL MOTORS, LLC

 

motion for compliance and sanctions

 

Date of Hearing:        June 24, 2026                                     Trial Date:       None set.

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        25STCV33103

 

Moving Party:            Defendant General Motors LLC

Responding Party:     Plaintiff Arturo Topete

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a lemon law action. On November 12, 2025, Plaintiff Arturo Topete filed a complaint against Defendant General Motors LLC for various violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Defendant General Motors LLC’s Motion for Compliance and Sanctions is granted, in part.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant General Motors LLC moves this court for an order for compliance requiring Plaintiff Arturo Topete to schedule and conduct the deposition mandated under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(c) on the grounds Plaintiff’s deposition has not yet been conducted. GM also moves for the imposition of sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel in the amount of $1,500.00.

 

Effective January 1, 2025, in an action seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle pursuant to Section 871.20 (above), the parties shall: “Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, … without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h) [of Section 871.26].” (CCP § 871.26(b).)¿ “Within 120 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties have the right to conduct initial depositions, each not to exceed two hours.” (CCP 871.26(c).)

 

GM contends it filed its answer to Plaintiff’s complaint on January 2, 2026, which put Plaintiff’s deposition due on May 4, 2026. (Kay Decl. ¶4.) On January 2, 2026, GM also sent a noticed deposition for April 2, 2026 as well as a meet-and-confer letter asking that Plaintiff provide alternative deposition dates within 14 days if Plaintiff was unavailable on the noticed date. (Kay Decl. ¶¶5-6, Exh. A.) On the date of the deposition, Plaintiff asked for the remote link for Plaintiff’s deposition. (Kay Decl. ¶7.) GM did not intend to proceed with the deposition remotely and mistakenly neglected to insert a deadline to confirm whether Plaintiff had objected or would be appearing for the noticed deposition on April 2, 2026. (Kay Decl. ¶5.) As GM’s Counsel was unaware of the erroneously served deposition notice, GM’s Counsel withdrew the deposition notice five minutes prior to the time at which the deposition was noticed to proceed, alerting Plaintiff’s Counsel to an amended notice that would be forthcoming. (Kay Decl. ¶7.) Plaintiff’s Counsel then stated that they would not be producing their client for a “second” deposition unless GM demonstrated “good cause”. (Kay Decl. ¶7.) Upon apologizing for any inconvenience, GM’s counsel followed up with Plaintiff’s counsel regarding the scheduling of Plaintiff’s deposition, noting that GM had not received Plaintiff’s alternative dates for deposition. (Kay Decl. ¶¶7-9.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues this motion is without merit. Plaintiff has been willing to submit to a deposition. Plaintiff has simply asked Defendant to compensate Plaintiff and his counsel for the time and fees incurred in preparing for Plaintiff’s deposition that was cancelled five minutes prior to the start time. Defendant refused. Plaintiff also contends Defendant made no effort to meet and confer with Plaintiff.

 

While both parties are supposedly amenable to taking the deposition of Plaintiff, the deposition has not yet occurred. This motion could have been avoided if the parties were truly open to complying with Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26.  The court orders the deposition to go forward within the next 45 days.  

 

Sanctions

 

Both parties seek sanctions. GM seeks sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(j)(2). However, those are only mandatory unless Plaintiff has shown good cause. The court finds Plaintiff has shown good cause.

 

Plaintiff also seeks sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.480 on the basis GM did not act with substantial justification. The court denies the request for sanctions. While GM worked on rescheduling the deposition notice, it is the one who canceled 5 minutes before the set deposition.
