Case Number: 26VECP00275    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

State Farm Insurance v. Bondy, Case no. 26VECP00275

Hearing date July 28, 2026

Petitioner State Farm’s Motion to Compel Arbitration – JOINED BY RESPONDENT

Petitioner State Farm Insurance filed a complaint against respondent Bondy to have the court appoint a neutral arbitrator on this uninsured/underinsured motorist claim. See Compl., paras. 3-4. Petitioner moved to compel arbitration, seeking a court order appointing a neutral arbitrator per to Code Civ. Proc. §1281.6. Respondent joins.

This claim arises from a 3/6/23 motor vehicle collision. See Decl. Krueger, para. 4. Ins. Code §11580.2(f) requires UM/UIM arbitration to proceed before a single neutral arbitrator. The policy between the parties incorporates section 11580.2 but fails to provide a mechanism for selecting a neutral arbitrator. Id. at para. 5, exh. A. The parties exchanged proposed arbitrator lists but have been unable to agree on a single neutral arbitrator. Id. at paras. 20-26, exh. I.

Petitioner requests the court either appoint a neutral arbitrator or, in the alternative, nominate a list of five potential neutral arbitrators, with the court retaining authority to appoint one of the five if the parties are unable to select one themselves within five days. Respondent joins in the alternative request.

GRANTED; the court will discuss with the parties how to create the list of five potential arbitrators. The court encourages the parties to meet and confer in advance of the hearing.


Case Number: 26VECV02151    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Schrage v. Fierro, Case no. 26VECV02151

Hearing date July 28, 2026

Defendants’ Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings - CONTINUED

Plaintiff Schrage sues defendants Fierro for unlawful detainer. Defendants move for judgment on the pleadings, alleging defective 3-day notice. The court heard arguments and continued the hearing. See 7/8/26 Min. Order.

The court previously granted summary judgment for defendants in Schrage v. Fierro, Case no. 25VECV05223, as the court found plaintiff’s 3-day notice failed to include either the date served or expiration date. See 3/26/26 Min. Order in Case no. 25VECV05223. The 3-day notice in this matter contains the date served, 3/29/26 (See Compl., exh. 2), but does not state an expiration date. Id. The 3-day notice states the notice period expires 3 court days after service. Id.

Plaintiff argues controlling law, Eshagian v. Cepeda (2025) 112 Cal.App.5th 433, 457, requires a 3-day notice to contain the date of service, number of days until expiration of the notice period and what days are excluded from calculations. Plaintiff argues the 3-day notice at issue is distinguishable from that in Eshagian, supra, which did not identify the date of service, the event that commenced the statutory period and did not state that weekends and judicial holidays were excluded. Id. at 459.

Per Eshagian, supra, a notice is defective where it fails to provide information without which “an ordinary tenant would not have reasonably understood the deadline… to pay the rent due to avoid forfeiture of the premises.” Id. at 459. While the 3-day notice here did not state the date of expiration, no such requirement is stated in Eshagian, supra, or Code Civ. Proc. §1161(2). The 3-day notice contained the date of service, the length of the notice period and noted all days excluded from calculations. See Compl., exh. 2. This is sufficient to give an ordinary tenant notice. The 3-day notice is not defective for failing to state the expiration date.

Defendants separately argue the 3-day notice was served prior to entry of judgment in the prior UD matter, constituting invalid service. Judgment was entered in the prior action on 3/30/26; the 3-day notice at issue was served on 3/29/26. Defendants argue two separate actions cannot be pending between the same parties which arise from the same cause of action. See Code Civ. Proc. §430.10.

The complaint in this action was filed on 4/10/26, after judgment was entered in the prior action. Defendants provide no authority that posting a 3-day notice constitutes commencement of an action. An action is commenced via filing a complaint or petition; service of a notice to pay rent or quit does not constitute initiation of a lawsuit. The 3-day notice is neither defective nor prematurely served. DENIED.
