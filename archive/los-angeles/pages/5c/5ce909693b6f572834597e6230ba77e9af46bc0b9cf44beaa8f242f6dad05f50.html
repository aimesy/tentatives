DEPARTMENT 734 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV04971    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 734









 

            This is a Lemon Law action based on a Transmission Defect.

 

Plaintiffs Lucy Moreno and Lorenzo Moreno move to compel Defendant to provide the complete production of documents and written disclosure of information required under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h).

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

The hearing on Plaintiffs Lucy Moreno and Lorenzo Moreno’s motion to compel complete production pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h) is CONTINUED to November 16, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. The parties’ joint separate statement is due November 5, 2026.

 

The Court will reserve the issue of sanctions for a future hearing. If Plaintiff fails to meet and confer such that the parties are able to submit a joint separate statement, the hearing will be placed off-calendar.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Motion To Compel Initial Disclosures

 

            The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

 

Plaintiffs Lucy Moreno and Lorenzo Moreno move to compel Defendant to provide the complete production of documents and written disclosure of information required under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h). 

 

The Court notes that the instant motion shows that rather than reducing the proliferation of discovery motions in Song-Beverly litigation, the 2024 amendments to the Song-Beverly Act simply created new fields to fight old battles.  The legislative history for the bill amending the Song-Beverly Act ambitiously stated:

 

The expedited discovery provisions of this measure will reduce protracted discovery disputes. Discovery disputes, most notably arguments over what must be turned over to opposing parties in litigation, [are] one of the largest drivers in delays in almost every type of civil litigation. Lemon law litigation is no different. Presently, parties squabble over what maintenance records are to be disclosed, what warranty documents must be disclosed, and how depositions can be conducted. Every time one of these disputes arises, the court is forced to calendar a motion hearing and delay a potential trial. Given that lemon law cases are typically very document-heavy matters and involve a limited universe of documents, many of these discovery disputes represent little more than unnecessary litigation tactics that only serve to delay justice.

 

(Assem. Com. on Judiciary, analysis on A.B. 1755 (2023-2024 Reg. Sess.) as amended August 26, 2024, p. 14.)  In the Court’s experience, the expedited discovery provisions of A.B. 1755 have not decreased the frequency of discovery motions.  The instant motion is the fifth motion pursuant to section 871.26 calendared in the past seven weeks.

 

Defendant filed its demurrer on May 12, 2025, which makes the disclosures due on July 1, 2025.  (Civ. Proc. Code, § 871.26(b), (h).) Plaintiffs argue that FCA remains in willful noncompliance as of March 17, 2026, which is 249 days past the statutory deadline.

 

Plaintiffs argue that Defendant’s production of documents was incomplete as follows: The documents FCA refuses to produce include: (1) the Warranty Policies and Procedure Manual (WPPM); (2) Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI) records showing what defects FCA knew about the vehicle’s condition at the time of sale; (3) the entire warranty transaction history with missing diagnostic trouble code (DTC) results and software version information; (4) field action and recall documentation; (5) applicable technical service bulletins (TSBs) and information service bulletins (ISBs); and (6) service manuals for systems the dealer diagnosed and repaired.

 

Code Civ. Proc. 871.26, subdivision (h)(12) requires FCA to produce “[w]arranty policies and procedure manuals.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h)(12).)

 

Plaintiffs demand full compliance with section 871.26, subdivision (h)(12) through production of the complete WPPM, including:

Plaintiffs argue that FCA has produced incomplete records that omit critical information about customer concerns, diagnostic procedures, and repair attempts.

Plaintiffs seek mandatory $2,500 sanctions against FCA for failing to produce required documents that undeniably exist.

 

Defendant FCA argues that it complied with the statute. Before the statutory deadline, FCA served an initial disclosure production consisting of several hundred pages of materials, including repair records, warranty claims records, the repair order detail report, sales documents, the vehicle information detail report, glove-box documents, the Monroney label, Customer Assistance Inquiry records, service contract reports, Technical Service Bulletins, and recall-related materials applicable to the Subject Vehicle. FCA also produced, subject to the governing protective order, the Warranty Administration Manual, Dealer Policy Manual, Service Manual, and customer-assistance policies and procedures.

 

Defendant argues that it served its initial disclosure production on July 11, 2025 (Lopez Decl., ¶ 3) and  has produced several hundred pages of documents, including:

• A copy of the quick reference guide for the Subject Vehicle

• A copy of the Warranty Information Booklets

• A copy of the policies and procedures of its Customer Assistance Center regarding handling of customer complaints and requests for refund or replacement of their vehicle

 

(Lopez Decl., ¶ 4.)

 

In fact, Defendant admits that due to an inadvertence in Defendant counsel’s office, on July 23, 2025, FCA US served its initial disclosure production. (Claudia Gavrilescu Decl., ¶ 11. ) It is factual that FCA US was slightly late in serving its production, but Defendant contends that Plaintiffs have suffered no prejudice. Defendant contends that Plaintiff has been provided with all mandatory initial disclosure production.

 

Defendant argus that nothing in section 871.26 requires a manufacturer to serve written responses, verifications, sworn statements, or a certification of search completeness. Defendant argues that Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 establishes a limited early disclosure procedure for Song-Beverly cases.

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s motion should be denied because FCA already produced the documents required by section 871.26. FCA’s production included the vehicle specific materials necessary to evaluate Plaintiff’s claim, including repair records, warranty claims records, the repair order detail report, vehicle information records, sales documents, service contract reports, Customer Assistance Inquiry records, the Monroney label, TSBs, and recall-related materials, among other documents.

 

Defendant argues that the statute identifies specific categories of information to be exchanged early in the case. It does not require a manufacturer to produce every internal, administrative, financial, technical, dealer-facing, parts-return, preauthorization, or customer-retention record associated with a VIN. Plaintiff’s requested order would expand section 871.26 far beyond its text and purpose. If Plaintiff wants broader internal database discovery, Plaintiff must use the Civil Discovery Act, where FCA may assert appropriate objections and confidentiality protections. Plaintiff cannot use a motion to compel initial disclosures to obtain a database-wide production that the statute does not require. In fact, section 871.26 does not contain an enforcement mechanism and only permits sanctions. So even if Plaintiff could properly demonstrate that documents required by section 871.26 were outstanding, Plaintiff’s remedy is an order for sanctions alone—not an order compelling production. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j)(1)-(4).)

 

The Court notes that the statute contemplates that the manufacturer’s compliance may fail to fully comply with Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(b), (c) or (d),

 

(4) For a manufacturer’s or defendant’s repeated noncompliance with subdivision (b)[1], (c), or (d), a court shall order that evidentiary sanctions attach precluding the manufacturer or defendant from introducing evidence at trial regarding whether the motor vehicle had a nonconformity that substantially impaired the use, value, or safety of the motor vehicle, or whether the motor vehicle was repaired to match the written warranty after a reasonable number of opportunities to do so.


(Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(j)(4)[bold emphasis added].)

 

The possibility that evidentiary sanctions may be imposed pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(j)(4) suggests that the parties are given an opportunity to request further disclosures and production pursuant to § 871.26(h) if the initial production is not satisfactory.

 

However, as Defendant points out, Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26 does not require verified responses, and characterizes the production as an “initial disclosure,” suggesting that standard discovery responses are not required.

 

            Because there is no case law interpreting Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26, the Court must adopt a practical approach. The Court orders the parties to meet and confer and submit a joint separate statement as to each category of documents which Plaintiffs contend should have been produced. If Defendants claim that the documents are subject to being withheld based on privilege or trade secret, or that disclosure should be pursuant to the terms of a protective order, the Court will set a hearing on a motion for protective order as to those categories of documents.

 

            The Court will reserve the issue of sanctions for a future hearing.

 

            The Court previously continued the hearing on this motion for submission of a separate statement. On September 11, 2026, Defendant’s counsel Armando Lopez submitted a declaration indicating that Plaintiff’s counsel did not respond to meet and confer efforts toward preparation of a joint separate statement. As such, the hearing on the motion to compel complete production pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h) is CONTINUED to November 16, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. If Plaintiff fails to meet and confer such that the parties are able to submit a joint separate statement, the hearing will be placed off-calendar.

 

The parties’ joint separate statement is due November 5, 2026.

Case Number: 25STCV22798    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 734


            Plaintiff alleges that Defendants failed to properly remediate a water intrusion incident that resulted in mold contamination in Plaintiff’s apartment unit. Defendants also deactivated the fire sprinkler system without notice. Defendants further gave Plaintiff insufficient notice to vacate the apartment for repairs. Defendants failed to file a Tenant Habitability Plan for primary renovation work, and threatened to force an entry without legal authorization.

Plaintiff Kamran Rahimov moves for an order deeming admitted requests for admission, and an order compelling initial discovery responses, both as to Defendant OP Property Management L.P.. 

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Kamran Rahimov’s motion to compel initial discovery responses to special interrogatories, set one and requests for production of documents, set one is GRANTED. Verified responses, without objections (which have been waived) are due within 20 days. 

Plaintiff Kamran Rahimov’s motion to compel for an order deeming admitted requests for admission, set one is GRANTED. Defendant’s objections have been deemed waived due to the untimely responses. 

Even the untimely responses served by Defendant OP Property Management, L.P. on August 31, 2026 are verified by Dan Hooks, the Vice President and Assistant General Counsel for Air Communities and officer of Apartment Income REIT LLC--not OP Property Management, L.P.  The Court is aware that the identity and capacity of the parties and their declarants is an ongoing issue in this case.  The issue becomes multiplied with regard to discovery, since a declarant who does not state a factual basis for personal knowledge and authorization to speak on behalf of an entity named as a party cannot bind that party to the responses.  OP Property Management, L.P. must find someone who can answer on its behalf, even if the responses are that Defendant lacks information to answer because it was not the management company for the subject property. On the other hand, if AIR Property Management TRS, LLC contends that it is the proper defendant, it may bring a motion to intervene. Otherwise, its responses are of no effect.
