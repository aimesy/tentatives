Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 16, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Rafael Raz, et al. v. Beith David Educational Center

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV08540

 

MOTION TO MODIFY PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiffs Rafael Raz and Shlomo Sharaf and Cross-Defendant Hooman M. Gideon

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendant/Cross-Complainant Beith David Educational Center

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order modifying the May 20, 2024 preliminary injunction ordering Rabbi Farzan Levihim to comply with the Demand Letter requesting financial disclosure, remove Rabbi Levihim’s authority over the operations of BDEC, discovlose if BDEC has any other financial account at any financial institutions, order compliance with laws, and order compliance with BDEC’s bylaws.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion to modify preliminary injunction is DENIED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On April 4, 2024, Plaintiffs Rafael Raz, Fariba Yamini, Khosro Yamini, Orit Nazarian, Shlomo Sherf, Javad S. Rad, Mike Noor, Ken Pezeshki, David Dardashti, and Mitra Pezeshki (Plaintiffs) filed a verified petition for an order voiding purported election of non-profit corporation and ordering a new properly supervised election and verified complaint for injunctive relief.

 

On April 22, 2024, Defendants/Cross-Complainants Fahim Farivar and Beith David Educational Center (Beith David) filed a cross-complaint against Rafael Raz, Hooman Moshe Gideon a/k/a Moshe Hooman Gideon, Shlomo Sherf, and Ken Pezeshki for: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing; (3) breach of fiduciary duty; (4) fraud; (5) conspiracy to commit a fraud; (6) conversion; (7) removal of director from non-profit corporation for malfeasance pursuant to Corps. Code § 7223(a); (8) open book account; (9) books and records; (10) aiding and abetting; (11) express indemnity; (12) implied indemnity; (13) specific performance; (14) intentional interference with prospective economic interests; (15) intentional interference with current business advantage; (16) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (17) civil harassment; (18) defamation; (19) trade libel; (20) ejectment; and (21) declaratory relief.

 

On May 15, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a motion to disqualify counsel which the court DENIED.

 

On May 16, 2025, Beith David filed an amended First Amended Cross-Complaint (FACC) which omitted Mr. Farivar as a cross-complainant. The FACC also omitted the causes of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress, civil harassment, defamation, and trade libel.

 

On May 16, 2024, Cross-Defendants filed a special motion to strike (anti-SLAPP) on the FACC which the court DENIED.

 

On May 20, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a request for dismissal of the second cause of action only and Ken Pezeshki filed a request for dismissal of his claims.

 

On May 20, 2024, the court issued a preliminary injunction.

 

On May 23, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a request for dismissal as to Fahim Farivar only.

 

On May 29, 2024, Beith David filed an answer to the complaint.

 

On May 31, 2024, Plaintiff Mitra Pezeshki filed a request for dismissal as to her claims only.

 

On June 6, 2024, Beith David filed a motion for judgment on the pleadings which was GRANTED in part.

 

On June 7, 2024, the court entered a preliminary injunction.

 

On June 28, 2024, Plaintiff filed a motion to tax costs which the court GRANTED.

 

On August 2, 2024, Beith David filed a motion to disqualify counsel which the court DENIED.

 

On October 7, 2024, Plaintiffs/Cross-Defendants filed a notice of appeal.

 

On December 18, 2024, Beith David filed a motion to bifurcate the cross-complaint from the complaint which the court DENIED.

 

On May 29, 2025, Plaintiff Orit Nazarian filed a request for dismissal of her claims only.

 

On July 8, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a motion to tax costs which the court GRANTED in part.

 

On December 1, 2025, Beith David filed a motion for attorney’s fees which the court DENIED.

 

On June 2, 2026, Cross-Defendants filed the instant motion for order to modify the preliminary injunction. On September 2, 2026, Beith David filed an opposition. Replies were due on or before September 9, 2026. As of September 11, 2026, the court has not received any reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

In any action, the court may on notice modify or dissolve an injunction or temporary restraining order upon a showing that there has been a material change in the facts upon which the injunction or temporary restraining order was granted, that the law upon which the injunction or temporary restraining order was granted has changed, or that the ends of justice would be served by the modification or dissolution of the injunction or temporary restraining order. 

 

(Code Civ. Proc. (CCP) § 533.) Modification of a preliminary injunction “rests in the sound discretion of the trial court upon a consideration of all the particular circumstances of each individual case.” (Union Interchange, Inc. v. Savage (1959) 52 Cal.2d 601, 606.) The party seeking modification has the burden of proving that the request is justified. (See Loeffler v. Medina (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1495, 1504 (Loeffler).)  

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The court GRANTS Beith David’s request for judicial notice.

 

Modify Preliminary Injunction

 

Moving Parties seek an order to modify the preliminary injunction issued on May 20, 2024 due to changed facts concerning Rabbi Levihim’s neutrality and the state of Beith David.

 

Beith David argues Moving Parties did not meet their burden to modify the injunction, they seek to relitigate issues already decided at the injunction hearing, the court cannot modify as requested due to the First Amendment, Moving Parties apply statutes for relief they cannot seek, and an evidentiary showing would not support modification at any rate.

 

As of September 11, 2026, the court has not received a reply.

 

There are three independent bases to modify an injunction: (1) change in the facts, (2) change in the law, or (3) ends of justice. (Loeffler, supra, 174 Cal.App.4th at p. 1504 [citing Luckett v. Panos (2008) 161 Cal.App.4th 77, 85.)])

 

Here, Moving Parties move under the first grounds for change of facts. (Mot. P. 2 [“The facts that have unfolded since the injunction demonstrate the opposite.”]) Thus, the court limits its discussion to this factor.

 

In Loeffler, the trial court applied the “new or different facts [or] circumstances” standard articulated in CCP § 1008(a) when ruling on a motion to dissolve an injunction. (Loeffler, supra, 174 Cal.App.4th at p. 1505-1506.) The Court of Appeal apparently adopted this standard in reviewing the trial court’s ruling for an abuse of discretion. (Id. at p. 1506.) Thus, the court will apply this standard, too.

 

Moving Parties have not met their burden to demonstrate a change in facts warranting modification. They point to loss of members and donations, failing to produce documents pertaining to a new caterer contract, decline in events and facility use, members avoiding life-cycle events at Beith David, public health violations, the rabbi’s lack of neutrality, inability to attract new members or donations, hostile conduct toward dissenting community members, and Beith David was originally founded without a rabbi. They support these claims with declarations. But there is more.

 

Based on these “new facts” Moving Parties ask the court to remove Rabbi Levihim’s authority over Beith David. (Mot. P. 7, Request for Relief No. 1.)

 

To avoid conflict with the establishment clause: (1) the challenged government activity must have a secular legislative purpose, (2) the principal or primary effect of the activity must be one that neither advances nor inhibits religion, and (3) the activity must not foster an excessive government entanglement with religion. (Singh v. Singh (2004) 114 Cal.App.4th 1264, 1286.)

 

These declarations supporting the motion demonstrate disagreement and unhappiness with Rabbi Levihim’s management style. (See, e.g., Declaration of Shlomo Sherf ¶ 7.) But the court may not resolve disputes concerning a religious entity’s governance or administration. (New v. Kroeger (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 800, 815.) Removing Rabbi Levihim would serve no apparent secular purpose and would improperly entangle the court in religious governance.[1]

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES the motion to modify the preliminary injunction.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion to modify preliminary injunction is DENIED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV09082    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 16, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Carmen E. Rivas, et al. v. Specialized Loan Servicing, LLC, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                25STCV09082

 

MOTION FOR TERMINATING SANCTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Newrez LLC dba Shellpoint Mortgage Servicing fka Specialized Loan Servicing, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): None

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order for terminating sanctions against Plaintiffs in the form of dismissal of the complaint with prejudice for willful failure to comply with their discovery obligations.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for terminating sanctions is GRANTED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On March 27, 2025, Plaintiffs Carmen E. Rivas and Yecica P. Rivas (Plaintiffs) filed a Verified Complaint against Defendants Specialized Loan Servicing, LLC and Newrez, LLC dba Shellpoint Mortgage Servicing (fka Specialized Loan Servicing, LLC) (Defendants) with six causes of action for: (1) Wrongful Foreclosure; (2) Violation of Homeowner Bill of Rights; (3) Quiet Title; (4) Constructive Fraud; (5) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress; and (6) Declaratory Relief. 

 

According to the Complaint, Plaintiffs owned real property located at 7116 11th Ave., Los Angeles, CA 90043 (the Property) secured by mortgage with Bank of America, N.A. Plaintiffs allege that Bank of America, N.A. transferred the mortgage to Defendants without telling Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs thereafter received documents from Defendants indicating Plaintiffs owed $30,000 in arrears. Plaintiffs further allege that Defendants proceeded with foreclosure instead of working with Plaintiffs towards resolution.  

 

On April 16, 2025, Plaintiff filed a notice of lis pendens. 

 

On April 22, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Fictitious Name Amendment naming BIV Capital, LLC as DOE 1. 

 

On May 22, 2025, Defendant BIV Capital, LLC filed an Answer. That same date, BIV Capital, LLC also filed a Cross-Complaint against Cross-Defendants Specialized Loan Servicing, LLC and Newrez, LLC (Cross-Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of Fiduciary Duty; (3) Negligence; (4) Negligent Interference with Prospective Economic Relations; (5) Implied and Equitable Indemnity; (6) Contribution; and (7) Declaratory Relief. 

 

On June 20, 2025, Defendant/Cross-Defendant Newrez, LLC (Newrez) filed a demurrer to the complaint and demurrer to the cross-complaint which the court OVERRULED.

 

On December 16, 2025, Newrez filed answers to the complaint and cross-complaint.

 

On March 12, 2026, Newrez filed a motion to compel Plaintiffs to sit for deposition and for monetary sanctions which the court GRANTED.

 

On June 24, 2026, Counsel for Plaintiffs filed a motion to be relieved as counsel which the court GRANTED.

 

On June 30, 2026, Newrez filed a motion for terminating sanctions. Oppositions were due on or before September 2, 2026. As of September 9, 2026, the court has not received any opposition.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Where a party willfully disobeys a discovery order, courts have discretion to impose¿terminating, issue, evidence, or monetary sanctions.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010(d), (g), 2023.030; R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486, 495.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure § 2030.040 requires that “[a] request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought.”¿ Furthermore, the notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction¿sought.¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.040.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

Monetary sanctions may be imposed “ordering that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct…unless [the Court] finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.030(a).)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

Issue sanctions may be imposed “ordering that¿designated¿facts shall be taken as¿established¿in the action¿in accordance with¿the claim of the party adversely affected by the misuse of the discovery process. The court may also impose an issue sanction by an order prohibiting any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process from supporting or opposing designated claims or defenses.”¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.030(b).)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

Evidence sanctions may be imposed “by an order prohibiting any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process from introducing designated matters in evidence.”¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.030(c).)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

In more extreme cases, the¿court may also impose¿terminating¿sanctions by “striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings,” “staying further proceedings,” “dismissing the action, or any part of the action,” or “rending a judgment by default” against the party misusing the discovery process.¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 2030.030(d).)¿ The court should look¿to¿the totality of the circumstances in¿determining¿whether terminating sanctions are¿appropriate.¿ (Lang v. Hochman¿(2000) 77¿Cal.App.4th 1225, 1246.)¿ Ultimate discovery sanctions are justified where there is a willful discovery order violation, a history of abuse, and evidence showing that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with discovery rules.¿ (Van Sickle v. Gilbert¿(2011) 196¿Cal.App.4th 1495, 1516.)¿ “[A] penalty as severe as dismissal or default is not authorized where noncompliance with discovery is caused by an inability to comply rather than willfulness or bad faith.”¿ (Brown v. Sup. Ct.¿(1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 701, 707.)¿ “Although in extreme cases a court has the authority to order a terminating sanction as a first measure [citations], a terminating sanction should generally not be imposed until the court has attempted less severe alternatives and found them to be unsuccessful and/or the record clearly shows lesser sanctions would be ineffective.”¿ (Lopez v. Watchtower Bible and Tract Society of New York, Inc.¿(2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604-605.)¿¿¿ 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Newrez contends terminating sanctions are appropriate because Plaintiffs have apparently abandoned this case by failing to communicate with their counsel and failing to schedule, let alone sit for, court-ordered depositions. They add that imposing a lesser sanction, which the court has already done via monetary sanction on the motion to compel deposition, would continue to prove ineffective.

 

Plaintiffs did not file an opposition.

 

Here, terminating sanctions are warranted. First, Newrez provided evidence showing a pattern of Plaintiffs’ nonresponsiveness. (Declaration of Neil J. Cooper ¶¶ 4, 7, 8.; See also Exhibit 5, Exhibit 6.) Second, Plaintiffs did not oppose Newrez’s motion to compel depositions and the court granted the motions. (Id. at ¶ 6.) Third, Plaintiffs did not comply with the court’s order. (Id. at ¶ 9.) This is clearly a discovery abuse unabated by the imposition of lesser sanctions. As such, the only appropriate remedy is to issue terminating sanctions.

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Newrez’s motion for terminating sanctions.[1]

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for terminating sanctions is GRANTED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 26STCV16288    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 16, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Kendra Lerner v. Alfred H. Pickens

 

CASE NO.:                26STCV16288

 

MOTION FOR STAY PENDING APPEAL

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Alfred H. Pickens

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Kendra Lerner, as Successor Trustee of the Eleanor L. Smith 2020 Revocable Living Trust, dated June 10, 2020

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order issuing of stay of proceedings pending final resolution of the appeal in Court of Appeal Case No. B353568.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for stay pending appeal is GRANTED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On May 20, 2026, Plaintiff Kendra Lerner, as Successor Trustee of the Eleanor L. Smith 2020 Revocable Living Trust, dated June 10, 2020 (Plaintiff) filed a verified complaint against Defendant Alfred H. Pickens (Defendant) with causes of action for: (1) partition of real property by sale; and (2) accounting between cotenants.

 

According to the complaint, this case involves real property located at 6249 S. Cimarron St., Los Angeles, California 90047 (the Property). Plaintiff alleges that title in the Property was vested in Eleanor L. Smith and Defendants as joint tenants, that Ms. Smith transferred her interest into the Trust, and that Ms. Smith died on May 24, 2023. Plaintiff alleges that the Probate Court has already decreed Ms. Smith’s interest in the Property as a Trust asset and that Plaintiff holds a 1/2 interest in the Property as a result. Plaintiff seeks a sale of the Property due to Defendant’s refusal to do a voluntary partition or buyout of Plaintiff’s interest.

 

On June 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of pendency of action.

 

On June 30, 2026, Defendant filed an answer.

 

On June 30, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for stay of proceedings pending appeal.

 

On August 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of related case with: (1) Case No. 23STCV25940 Pickens v. Smith, et al. pending in Los Angeles County Superior Court Dept. 36 filed October 23, 2023; and (2) Case No. 25STPB07082 In re: the Eleanor L. Smith 2020 Revocable Living Trust, dated June 10, 2020 disposed of by judgment filed June 20, 2025.

 

On September 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to the motion to stay pending appeal. On September 9, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Code of Civil Procedure § 916(a) places a stay on certain proceedings in a trial court while an appeal is perfected. The code stays proceedings “upon the judgment or order appealed from or upon the matters embraced therein or affected thereby, including enforcement of the judgment or order, but the trial court may proceed upon any other matter embraced in the action and not affected by the judgment or order.” (CCP § 916(a).) In other words, for a matter to be stayed, it must be embraced by the order appealed from. “The purpose of the automatic stay provision of section 916, subdivision (a) ‘is to protect the appellate court's jurisdiction by preserving the status quo until the appeal is decided.’” (Varian Medical Systems, Inc. v. Delfino¿(2005) 35 Cal.4th 180, 189.) A proceeding “affects the effectiveness of an appeal if the possible outcomes on appeal and the actual or possible results of the proceeding are irreconcilable.” (Varian Medical Systems, Inc.,¿supra, 35 Cal.4th at 190.)¿¿ 

 

A court ordinarily has inherent power, in its discretion, to stay proceedings when such a stay will accommodate the¿ends¿of justice. (OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho¿(2019) 8 Cal.5th 111, 141.) The power to stay proceedings is incidental to the power inherent in every court to control the disposition of the cause on its docket with the economy of time and effort for itself, for counsel, and for litigants. (Id.) Trial courts¿generally have¿inherent power to stay proceedings in the interest of justice and to promote judicial efficiency. (Freiberg v. City of Mission Viejo¿(1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1484, 1489.)¿¿ 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The court DENIES Plaintiff’s request for judicial notice as immaterial to this ruling.

 

Stay

 

Defendant contends a stay is appropriate because the pending appeal concerns (and depends on) the very Probate Order on which Plaintiff relies to establish her cotenancy rights. He adds that allowing this action to proceed risks inconsistent factual and legal determinations on the same issue already decided by two different courts. Finally, Defendant contends he is not required to post an undertaking since the underlying ruling does not concern a money judgment or sale of real property – only a determination that a real property interest is a confirmed asset of a trust.[1]

 

Plaintiff argues there is no automatic stay, a discretionary stay is not warranted, and if the court is inclined to grant a stay it should be limited to enforcement of any interlocutory judgment of partition.

 

Defendant replies that a stay is appropriate because Plaintiff is seeking to enforce the appealed order, a stay will avoid inconsistent judicial determinations, preserving the status quo is not prejudicial, and a limited stay would not prevent inconsistent rulings.

 

Here, a stay is appropriate. First, this case directly seeks to enforce the appealed order.[2] (Varian, supra, 35 Cal.4th at p. 189.) Plaintiff specifically alleges her cotenancy based on Ms. Smith’s transfer of the interest into the Trust and the December 30, 2025 Probate Order confirming such. (Compl. ¶¶ 13-16.) The parties do not dispute that this is the order subject to appeal. (See Declaration of Mark L. Armstrong ¶¶ 3, 4, 6.) Second, the “proceeding itself is inherently inconsistent with the possible outcome on appeal.” (Varian, supra, 35 Cal.4th at p. 190.) In fact, if the Court of Appeal reverses the Probate Order, then Plaintiff no longer has standing to bring this partition claim since she would not be a cotenant. Therefore, a stay is appropriate.[3]

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Defendant’s motion for a stay of proceedings.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for stay pending appeal is GRANTED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
