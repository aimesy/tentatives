ANALYSIS:

 

            Pensco Trust Company LLC sued Felix Bobritsky, Yelena Bobritsky, Cooper Dietz, Daniala Bobritsky, and Shoshanah Bobritsky.  Shoshanah was dismissed;a  default judgment was entered against Felix, Yelena, Daniala, and Dietz; and this action was reclassified as a civil limited jurisdiction matter.

 

            On May 2, 2024, this Court granted Pensco’s motion to compel Felix and Yelena to respond to interrogatories to enforce the judgment and imposed monetary sanctions against them both in the amount of $1,823.91.  Felix and Yelena failed to obey the Court’s order.  Pensco then propounded interrogatories to enforce the May 2, 2024 order, Felix and Yelena failed to respond, so Pensco now moves to compel responses to those interrogatories.

 

            A monetary sanctions order has the force and effect of a money judgment and is enforceable under the Enforcement of Judgments Law.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 680.230, 680.270; Newland v. Superior Court (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 608, 615; see Lucky United Properties Investment, Inc. v. Lee (2010) 185 Cal.App.4th 125, 144.)  A judgment creditor may propound written interrogatories to the judgment debtor, in the manner prescribed in Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.010 et seq., requesting information to aid in enforcement of a money judgment.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.020, subd. (a).)  The judgment debtor must answer within the time prescribed in Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.010 et seq.  (Id., § 708.020, subd. (a).) 

 

A party must respond to interrogatories within 30 days after service of such interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).)  If a party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the interrogatories.  (Id., § 2030.290, subd. (a), (b).) 

 

Pensco served Felix and Yelena with the interrogatories in March 2026.  To date, no responses have been served.  Felix and Yelena have not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Pensco’s motion.  Felix and Yelena are ordered to serve verified responses without objections to the interrogatories within 30 days of this order. 

 

Felix and Yelena must also pay sanctions for causing Pensco to compel responses to the interrogatories.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); see id., § 708.020, subd. (c).)  Pensco requests $2,623.41 from Felix and Yelena, reflecting 3 hours of counsel’s work at $850.00 per hour plus $73.41 in filing fees.  The Court finds this request reasonable.  Felix and Luna must pay Pensco $2,623.41, jointly and severally, within 30 days of this order.

 

Pensco is to give notice.

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Jaafar’s petition.

(Civ. Code § 8460, et seq.)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

ANALYSIS:

 

            Palo Verde petitions this Court to release property located at 30048 Knoll View Drive, Palos Verdes, California 90275 from a mechanics lien recorded in 2023 by Soo Yon Kim.       

 

After a mechanic’s lien has been recorded, “[t]he owner of property or the owner of any interest in property subject to a claim of lien may petition the court for an order to release the property from the claim of lien if the claimant has not commenced an action to enforce the lien within the time provided in Section 8460.”  (Civ. Code, § 8480, subd. (a).)  A claimant must commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days of recording it, otherwise the claim of the lien expires and is unenforceable.  (Civ. Code, § 8460, subd. (a).) 

 

Palo Verde has now properly complied with the service requirements, petition allegation requirements, and demand requirements.  (Civ. Code, §§ 8108, 8482, 8484, 8486.)  Kim did not file an opposition to the petition.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Palo Verde’s petition to release the mechanics lien.

 

            As the prevailing party on the petition, Palo Verde is entitled to reasonable attorney fees and costs.  (Civ. Code, § 8488, subd. (c); Code Civ. Proc., § 1032.)  Palo Verde requests $4,200.00 in fees, reflecting its attorney’s five hours for preparing the petition, one hour for preparing a reply, and one hour for attending this hearing, at a rate of $600.00 per hour.  Both the rate and number of hours expended on preparing the petition are reasonable.  However, given the lack of need for a reply, the Court awards attorney fees in the reduced amount of $3,000, reflecting five total hours of attorney work.  Palo Verde also requests $370.00 for the petition’s filing fee.  That’s reasonable.  Accordingly, the Court awards Palo Verde a reduced total of $3,370.00, reflecting $3,000.00 in attorney fees and $370.00 in costs.

  The Petitioner is directed to serve and electronically
submit a proposed form of Order, consistent with this Court’s Ruling – within
10 days.

            Palo Verde is to give notice.
