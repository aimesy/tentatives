PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSE TO FORM INTERROGATORY NO. 4.1

 

Motion filed on May 7, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Armen Beglaryan (Plaintiff)

RESPONDING PARTY: Tyler Miller (Defendant)

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Order compelling Defendant to serve a further verified response to Form Interrogatory No. 4.1 and awarding $1,827.97 in monetary sanctions against Defendant and Robert E. Schroth Jr., Esq. (Defense Counsel).

RULING: Granted. Sanctions granted in the reduced amount of $500.00.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On June 10, 2025, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant and others.

 

Plaintiff served Form Interrogatories, Set One, on September 22, 2025. (Kravchenko Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. A.) Defendant served verified responses on March 6, 2026. (Kravchenko Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. B.)

 

Plaintiff sent a meet and confer letter on April 17, 2026. (Kravchenko Decl. ¶ 4, Ex. C.) Counsel thereafter met and conferred by telephone. On April 20, 2026, Plaintiff confirmed in writing that Defendant’s deadline to serve amended responses was extended to May 4, 2026, and Plaintiff’s deadline to file a motion to compel was extended to May 18, 2026. (Kravchenko Decl. ¶ 5, Ex. D.)

 

Plaintiff filed this motion, the supporting declaration of Niko Kravchenko, Esq., and a separate statement on May 7, 2026. No timely opposition was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Further Response to Form Interrogatory No. 4.1

 

A party responding to an interrogatory must provide an answer containing the information sought, exercise the option to produce writings under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.230, or assert an objection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.210, subd. (a).) An answer must be as complete and straightforward as the information reasonably available to the responding party permits. If the interrogatory cannot be answered completely, it must be answered to the extent possible. If the responding party lacks sufficient personal knowledge to respond fully, the party must so state and make a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain the information by inquiry to other natural persons or organizations, unless the information is equally available to the propounding party. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.220, subds. (a)-(c).) A further response may be compelled when an answer is evasive or incomplete. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a)(1).)

 

Here, Defendant answered Form Interrogatory No. 4.1 by stating that automobile liability coverage existed through the rental vehicle and identifying Dirt Cheap Car Rental as the named insured and custodian of the policy. As to the policy number, limits of coverage, and whether any reservation of rights or coverage dispute exists, Defendant responded “unknown.” (Kravchenko Decl., Ex. B at 4-5.) Although those answers state that Defendant does not presently know the requested information, they do not indicate that Defendant made a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain it by inquiry to Dirt Cheap Car Rental or another natural person or organization, as Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.220, subdivision (c), requires. The responses are therefore incomplete.

 

Defendant must serve a further verified response to subparts (d), (e), and (f) after making the reasonable and good faith inquiry required by section 2030.220, subdivision (c). If any information remains unknown after that inquiry, Defendant must answer to the extent possible and state that he is unable to provide the remaining information after making the required inquiry.

 

Sanctions

 

The Court shall impose a monetary sanction against a party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a further response to interrogatories unless the Court finds substantial justification or other circumstances making sanctions unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (d).)

 

Here, Plaintiff requests $1,827.97 based on 3.5 hours at $500 per hour and $77.97 in costs. (Kravchenko Decl. ¶¶ 6-7.) Sanctions are warranted but reduced. The motion concerns one interrogatory, no opposition was filed, and no reply was required. One hour at counsel’s stated rate of $500 is reasonable. Sanctions are awarded in the amount of $500.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Plaintiff Armen Beglaryan’s Motion to Compel Further Responses is granted.

 

Defendant Tyler Miller is ordered to serve a further verified answer to Form Interrogatory No. 4.1 within 20 days of notice of this order. The answer must comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.210, subdivision (a)(1), and 2030.220, subdivisions (a) through (c).

 

Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is granted in the reduced amount of $500, jointly and severally against Defendant Tyler Miller and his counsel of record, payable within 30 days of notice of this order.

DEFENDANT TY INVESTMENT LLC’S MOTION TO COMPEL ARBITRATION AND TO STAY PROCEEDINGS

 

Motion filed on April 30, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant TY Investment LLC

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiffs Jose Gracia-Medrano and Georgina Gracia-Medrano

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Order compelling arbitration and staying or dismissing this action.

RULING: Continued.

 

ORDER

The Court has reviewed the moving, opposition, and reply papers. The motion presents an issue requiring further briefing before the Court rules on the request to compel arbitration.

 

Plaintiffs argue the Arbitration Agreement is substantively unconscionable because it excludes from arbitration “[a]ctions by the lender to judicially or non-judicially foreclose on the note and deed of trust . . . to recover possession, ejectment or relief from the automatic stay in bankruptcy, or to obtain relief through governmental agencies.” (Hu Decl., Ex. 7.) Plaintiffs contend these carveouts preserve Defendant TY’s judicial remedies while requiring Plaintiffs to arbitrate any challenge to the foreclosure. The Court notes Defendant TY has filed a separate unlawful detainer action involving possession of the subject property.

 

The Court requires supplemental briefing on whether these lender-only carveouts render the Arbitration Agreement substantively unconscionable under the circumstances of this case, including whether Flores v. Transamerica HomeFirst, Inc. (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 846 and/or Sanchez v. Valencia Holding Co., LLC (2015) 61 Cal.4th 899 applies to, or is distinguishable from, the carveouts at issue here. The parties shall also address whether, if any carveout is found unconscionable, the proper remedy is severance, limitation of the carveout, or denial of arbitration.

 

Accordingly, the hearing on Defendant TY Investment LLC’s Motion to Compel Arbitration and to Stay Proceedings is continued to August 11, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department F-46.

 

The parties shall file simultaneous supplemental briefs, not exceeding five pages, no later than July 28, 2026. The supplemental briefs shall be limited to the issue identified above. No reply briefs shall be filed unless ordered by the Court.

 

The Court does not request further briefing on the existence or scope of the Arbitration Agreement, the consumer/business-purpose loan issue, arbitration costs, or fraud in the inducement.
