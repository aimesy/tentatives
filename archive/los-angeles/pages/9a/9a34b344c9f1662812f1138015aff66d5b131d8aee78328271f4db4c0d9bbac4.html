DEPARTMENT I LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

If the parties wish to submit on the tentative ruling and avoid a court appearance on the matter, the moving party must contact the opposing party and all other parties who have appeared in the action and confirm that each will submit on the tentative ruling. Please call the court no later than 4:30 p.m. on the court day before the hearing, leave a message with the court clerk at (310) 260-3629 advising her that all parties will submit on the tentative ruling and waive hearing, and finally, serve notice of the Court's ruling on all parties entitled to receive service. If any party declines to submit on the tentative ruling, then no telephone call is necessary, and all parties should appear at the hearing.

Case Number: 22SMCV00916    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: I

The court has before it a motion for sanctions brought by plaintiff.  Plaintiff seeks evidentiary, issue, or terminating sanctions.  That aspect of the motion is DENIED.  The court cannot grant such sanctions as a matter of discovery absent violation of a court order, and none is alleged here.  Defendant is right about that.

 

However, the court is troubled by defendant’s tactics.  The responses to written discovery are all objection only responses.  The court has looked at the objections and it is plain that they are in bad faith.  Accordingly, The court agrees that it will consider the alternative relief sought: an order compelling further responses and to force a second day of deposition.  In that respect, the motion is GRANTED.  Defendant is to provide verified responses without objection to the discovery at issue, including the actual production of documents.  Attorney/client or work product objections can be asserted, but any documents not produced for that reason will be logged at the same time.  If defendant cannot fully comply, then a statement compliant with section 2031.230 will be provided.  That means that if documents exist but defendant cannot provide them, or that documents once existed but no longer exist, the declaration is required.  That will be completed within 5 court days.  In addition, Jones will sit for a second day of deposition.  The date will be set by plaintiff on any business day but on not less than 72 hours notice.  If Jones cannot sit on that day, Jones may, within 24 hours of the notice, so object and plaintiff will pick another date, also on not less than 72 hours’ notice, to which Jones may not object.  The deposition will not last longer than an additional 7 hours, exclusive of colloquy and time off the record, without leave of court.

 

Jones had no right to walk out of the deposition at 2 pm.  That was bad faith.  As to the discovery responses, the MSJ was denied based on the theory that lots of these expenses were legitimate.  That may or may not be true, but Jones cannot give a partial or inadequate response to the very discovery to test that theory. 

 

The court warns Jones that if this order is violated, other sanctions may be imposed.

 

The court is aware that its order means that there will be issues regarding the trial date.  To that extent, the court allows the discovery to go forward notwithstanding the discovery cut off, but the cut off is in no other way extended at this time.  If plaintiff needs to continue the trial date, plaintiff may bring an application to do so.  While normally the court would not so allow, there has been a history of conduct in this case that might lead to a continuance.

 


Case Number: 22SMCV02709    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: I

The motion for reconsideration is denied.  Plaintiff seeks reconsideration of the court’s prior order granting defendant’s motion for summary judgment.  The motion was denied in part for the failure to file a separate statement, but also on the merits.  Plaintiff presents no fact or evidence that was unavailable to him at the time of the motion, nor has there been a change in the law.  Even were there some new evidence, the court seen no justification for the failure to have obtained it earlier.  The court was also concerned that plaintiff, who was employed in a foreign country by defendant and lived and worked there, cannot sue under California law.  California employment law is generally not extra-territorial.  All of the actions here occurred outside of California—indeed, outside the United States.  In reply, plaintiff seeks redress under section 473, claiming excusable neglect.  The court does not believe it has been established.

 

The motion is DENIED. 

 

The motion to vacate the judgment is also DENIED.  Plaintiff does not object to the form of the judgment—which was the only thing the court gave him the right to do—he objects to the substance.  The substance was set when the court granted the summary judgment motion.  Because there is no basis to recall or modify the judgment, there is no basis for the motion and plaintiff was not denied due process.

Case Number: 26SMCV03898    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: I

The court is inclined to GRANT the application.  The court has seen the opposition noting that there does not seem to be an emergency here.  And the court agrees that there does not seem to be an emergency.  The problem is that the UD timeline is deliberately short by legislative directive.  Here, the demurrer was filed on August 5 but the hearing is now set for November 2—almost three months.  Defendant states that this is the earliest date available.  The court is not sure that is correct—the court has looked at its docket and there seem to be a number of open dates before then.  But the fact remains that the Legislature has deemed that the matter will be heard within 7 court days and right now it is closer to 60.  Therefore, the court will set the matter for either Monday October 12 or Wednesday October 15.  The court would set it earlier, but the court cannot hear it this week and the court will be dark from then until October 7, which means that its calendars upon return are remarkably bleak.  Needless to say, this ought not be taken as any indication as to how the court will rule on the merits.  (Ever notice how whenever one begins a sentence with “needless to say” the speaker then goes ahead and says it?)
