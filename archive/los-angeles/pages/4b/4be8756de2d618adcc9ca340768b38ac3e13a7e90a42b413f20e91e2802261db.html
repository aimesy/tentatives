Hon. Victor Avila, Dept B 

Motion to Strike Answer

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 25NNCV01521

CASE NAME: Simounian v. Gevorgyan

Moving Party: Plaintiff Simon Simounian

Responding Party: Defendant Arayik Gevorgyan

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

On March 6, 2025, Plaintiff Simon Simounian (Plaintiff) filed this action against Defendant Arayik Gevorgyan (Defendant) arising out of a motor vehicle accident which occurred on October 29, 2023.

On July 7, 2025, the clerk entered default against Defendant.

On September 10, 2025, the Court set an Order to Show Cause (OSC) re Failure to Proceed with Default Judgment for November 19, 2025. Plaintiff was ordered to submit either a default judgment package or a declaration at least five calendar days before the hearing, and the Court warned that failure to do so could result in dismissal. On November 19, 2025, Plaintiff did not file a default judgment package, did not file a declaration, and did not appear at the OSC hearing. The Court therefore dismissed the action.

On March 27, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s motion to vacate dismissal due to counsel’s inadvertent clerical error.

On March 30, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer to the original Complaint.

On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion seeking to strike Defendant’s amended answer on the grounds that default has been entered, and Defendant is prohibited from making affirmative steps in litigation. On August 10, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

No reply has been filed as on August 17, 2026.  

 

LEGAL STANDARD

            The grounds for a motion to strike shall appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437(a).) Where a paragraph contains multiple factual allegations or distinct propositions, a responding party may not simply admit or deny the paragraph as a whole if doing so would obscure which parts are true and which are disputed. Instead, the party must specifically admit or deny the separate branches of the allegation. If the paragraph embraces several clauses or propositions, each material subpart must be addressed separately and disjunctively, or the denial is evasive and insufficient. (More v. Del Valle (1865) 28 Cal. 170, 172.)

“Before filing a motion to strike pursuant to this chapter, the moving party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purpose of determining if an agreement can be reached that resolves the objections to be raised in the motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5(a).) “The parties shall meet and confer at least 5 days before the date a motion to strike must be filed.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5(a)(2).)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s answer must be stricken because default was entered by the court clerk, immediately terminating Defendant’s right to participate in litigation. (Mot., p. 4.) Plaintiff argues that Defendant remains in default and must take steps to set aside the default before filing an Answer. (Mot., p. 5.)  

In opposition, Defendant argues that striking this answer would just cause Defendant to seek the same relief due to its mistaken reliance on the Court’s prior dismissal, accordingly Defendant argues this motion is a waste of the Court’s time. (Opp., p. 1.) Further, Defendant argues that the motion is untimely, the underlying motion to vacate dismissal was untimely, and Plaintiff has failed to meet and confer prior to filing this motion to strike. (Ibid.)

Meet and Confer

Here, Plaintiff makes no reference to meet and confer efforts, rather Plaintiff mentions orally moving to strike Defendant’s answer and being instructed to file a noticed motion. (Zargaryan Decl., ¶ 15.) Nonetheless, “determination by the court that the meet and confer process was insufficient shall not be grounds to grant or deny the motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5(a)(4).) The parties are admonished to comply with meet and confer requirements in the future.

Motion to Strike

            Here, in the March 27, 2026, Order vacating dismissal, the Court described Plaintiff’s failure to submit a default judgment packet due to mistake or inadvertence but did not set aside entry of default on Defendant. (See 3/27/26 Minute Order, p. 3.) “The entry of a default terminates a defendant's rights to take any further affirmative steps in the litigation until either its default is set aside or a default judgment is entered.” (Devlin v. Kearny Mesa Amc/Jeep/Renault (1984) 155 Cal.App.3d 381, 385.) Accordingly, Defendant is prohibited from making the affirmative step of filing a responsive pleading until default is set aside. (Parish v. Peters (1991) 1 Cal.App.4th 202, 209, n.8.) To date Defendant has not successfully moved to set aside entry of default.

Accordingly, the motion to strike Defendant’s answer is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff Simon Simounian’s motion to strike Defendant’s Answer is GRANTED.

MOVING PARTY shall provide notice and provide a proposed order consistent with this ruling within 15 days from this date.


Case Number: 25NNCV02550    Hearing Date: August 21, 2026    Dept: B

 

Hon. Victor Avila, Dept B 

MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL RESPOSES; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS AGAINST DEFENDANT ROYAL

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 25NNCV02550

CASE NAME: Rose Gloria by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez, et al. vs Royal Palms Post Acute, LLC, et al.    

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Rose Gloria, by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT.

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is an elder abuse action. Decedent Rose Gloria, by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez (Plaintiff), and her children, Yolanda Gonzalez, Jose Gonzalez, Lisa White, Edward Gloria, Cynthia Castro, and Michael Gloria have brought this action against defendants Royal Palms Post Acute, LLC, (Defendant Royal) Palms Licensee 3, LLC, Beecan Health, LLC, and Steeve Henry, the owners and operators of the skilled nursing facility, Royal Palms Post Acute (Facility), where Ms. Gloria was a resident patient. On April 14, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against all defendants alleging Wrongful Death.

On July 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motions to compel initial responses to Form Interrogatories (Res ID: 1269), Special Interrogatories (Res ID: 2683), and Requests for Production of Documents (RPDs) (Res ID: 6650) served on Defendant Royal.

No oppositions have been filed as of August 14, 2026. 

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom requests for production of documents are directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) The party also waives the right to make any objections, including one based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to production of documents other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.020, subd. (a), 2031.300.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300; Sinaiko, supra, 148 Cal.App.4th at 411.)

A party must respond to interrogatories within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom interrogatories are directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).) The party also waives the right to make any objections, including one based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to interrogatories other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2024.020, subd. (a), 2030.290.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411.)

 

DISCUSSION           

Motion to Compel Initial Responses

Plaintiff contends that Defendant Royal failed to timely respond to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and RPDs on or by the statutory deadline. (Mot., p. 4.)

Here, Plaintiff’s counsel served Defendant Royal with the Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and RPDs by electronic service on March 13, 2026. (Mot. Compel Form Interrog., Horii Decl., ¶ 2; Mot. Compel Special Interrog., Horii Decl., ¶ 2; Mot., Compel RPDs, Horii Decl., ¶ 2.) Plaintiff contends that initial responses were due April 14, 2026. (Horii Decl., ¶ 3.) On April 15, Plaintiff’s counsel provided Defendant Royal with a two-week extension via email, but Defendant Royal again failed to respond. (Horii Decl., ¶ 4.) On April 22, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel left a voicemail and on April 23, 2026, spoke with Defendant Royal’s counsel via telephone and gave a subsequent extension making responses due May 15, 2026. (Horii Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) Defendant Royal again failed to respond. (Ibid.) As of July 1, 2026, Plaintiff declares that Defendant Royal has not provided verified responses. (Horii Decl., ¶ 7.)

Since Defendant Royal was properly served and failed to respond to discovery requests by the statutory deadline, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling Defendant Royal to provide responses without objections to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and RPDs.

Accordingly, the motion to compel initial responses is GRANTED.

Sanctions

The Court shall impose sanction on the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to compel responses to interrogatories or RPD. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300(c), 2030.290(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a) [the court can award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party seeking to compel discovery even though no opposition was filed, the opposition was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed].)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

Defendant Royal’s failure to comply with discovery requests necessitated the motion, and the conduct is subject to sanctions. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a).) Moreover, The Court finds Plaintiff’s Notice of Motion complies with Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040.

Plaintiff’s counsel requests sanctions in the amount of $633.54 for each motion. (Mot., p. 6.) Plaintiff has filed a total of 12 motions to compel initial responses from four total defendants, six of which are being heard on August 21, 2026, and the remaining 6 being heard on August 28, 2026. For all 12 motions, Plaintiff seeks attorney’s fees of $6,882.50 plus $720 ($60.00 x 12) in filing costs, totaling $7,602.50. (Horii Decl., ¶¶ 10-11.) The $6,882.50 in fees reflects 12.0 hours spent drafting the motions, 3.0 hours anticipated drafting replies, and 1 hour spent attending the hearings at a rate of $395.00 per hour; plus, 0.75 hours spent by Partner Kathryn Cassano, Esq. reviewing the motions at a rate of $750.00 per hour. (Horii Decl., ¶ 11.) Plaintiff divides the total fees and costs requested, $7,602.00, by 12 motions to reflect $633.54 per motion. (Ibid.)

The Court, in its discretion, will only award $575 to reflect one hour at a rate of $395 for the 3 motions filed against Defendant Royal plus $60 filing fees for each motion. All motions are substantially similar and straight forward.

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s request for imposition of sanctions against Defendants Royal in the amount of $575.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff Rose Gloria, by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez’s motion to deem RFAs admitted is GRANTED.

Plaintiff Rose Gloria, by and through her Successor-in-Interest, Yolanda Gonzalez’s request for sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $575, to be paid by Defendant Royal to Plaintiff 30 days from this ruling.

MOVING PARTY to submit a proposed consistent with this ruling and provide notice of this order.

MOTION TO DEEM REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION ADMITTED

Hearing Date: 08/21/26

CASE NO.: 25NNCV06250

CASE NAME: Jordyn Green-Brown v. Brady Trucking, Inc., et al.

Moving Party: Defendant Brady Trucking, Inc.

Responding Party: Plaintiff Jordyn Green-Brown

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT.

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is a personal injury case arising from a motor vehicle accident that occurred on November 3, 2023. On September 5, 2025, Plaintiff Jordyn Green-Brown (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against defendants Brady Trucking, Inc. (Defendant), John Doe, and DOES 1 to 50, inclusive alleging personal injury, property damage, and general negligence. On December 29, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

On June 3, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to compel requests for admission (RFAs) admitted. On July 10, 2026, the Court granted Vanessa Marie Fantasia’s motion to be relieved as Plaintiff’s counsel. On July 20, 2026, Plaintiff’s former counsel filed a response out of abundance of caution, due to having not yet received the signed July 10th order. On July 24, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

On July 31, 2026, the Court delivered a signed order reliving Plaintiff’s former counsel. On July 31, 2026, the matter was called for hearing, and the Court granted Defendant’s request for sanctions but continued the motion to deem RFAs admitted to afford Plaintiff the opportunity to retain new counsel or appear pro per.

 As of August 13, 2026, Plaintiff has not filed an opposition.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)  “If a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response…(a) [that party] waives any objection to the requests, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (a).) “The requesting party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction under Chapter 7.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) 

 

DISCUSSION           

Defendant contends that Plaintiff failed to timely respond to the RFAs on or by the statutory deadline. (Mot., p. 1.)

Deem Request for Admission Admitted

Here, Defendant’s counsel served Plaintiff with the RFAs by electronic service on December 29, 2025. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 2.) Defendant granted three extensions at the request of Plaintiff, bringing the due date to April 7, 2026. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 3.) On April 1, 2026, Plaintiff served Defendant responses to the RFAs without verifications. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 4.) On April 27, 2026, Defendant requested verifications from Plaintiff and provided additional time to provide the signed verifications. (Ibid.) Defendant argues that because Plaintiff has failed to serve verified responses, Plaintiff has not properly responded to the RFAs. (Mot., p. 2.)

Plaintiff’s former counsel did not substantively oppose the motion but instead requested continuance, in the interest of justice, due to the status of Plaintiff’s representation. (Resp., p. 2.) On July 31, 2026, Plaintiff’s former counsel received a signed order relieving them from representation. (See 7/31/26 Court Order.) To date Plaintiff has not filed an opposition.

Here, Plaintiff has failed to provide verified responses to Defendant’s discovery request, thus Plaintiff has not effectively responded to Defendant’s discovery requests by the statutory deadline, or deadline set after several extensions. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 5.) The Court continued this hearing in the interests of justice, and Plaintiff is yet to oppose the motion or respond to the subject discovery requests.

Accordingly, the motion to deem RFAs admitted is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant Brady Trucking, Inc.’s motion to deem RFAs admitted is GRANTED.

Motion to Authorize Electronic Service

Hearing Date: 8/21/26

CASE NO.: 26NNCV00100

CASE NAME: VW Credit Inc. v. GG Motorsport, Inc., et al.

Moving Party: Plaintiff VW Credit Inc. dba VW Credit Leasing Ltd

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: GRANTED

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and ANY PARTY SEEKING ARGUMENT SHOULD NOTIFY ALL OTHER PARTIES AND THE COURT BY 4:00 P.M. ON THE COURT DAY BEFORE THE HEARING of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is a personal property dispute action. On January 7, 2026, Plaintiff VW Credit Inc. dba VW Credit Leasing Ltd (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against defendants GG Motorsport, Inc. (Motorsport); California Department of Motor Vehicles; and DOES 1-10 inclusive asserting causes of action for (1) claim and delivery of personal property; (2) conversion; (3) quiet title; and (4) declaratory relief. On May 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion seeking an order authorizing service of summons to Motorsport via electronic mail.

On July 31, 2026, the matter was called for hearing, and Plaintiff’s counsel requested a continuance to file a supplemental declaration to provide the Court with more information on which to base its decision. The Court granted Plaintiff’s request.

On August 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed a supplemental declaration in support of the motion to authorize service of summons by electronic mail.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“If no provision is made in this chapter or other law for the service of summons, or if a plaintiff, despite exercising reasonable diligence, has been unable to effect service of the summons by any of the methods authorized under this chapter, the court in which the action is pending may, upon motion, direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the party to be served, including by electronic mail or other electronic technology, and that proof of such service be made as prescribed by the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(1).) This requires a plaintiff to “set forth facts detailing all attempts to serve the defendant by each of the methods prescribed by statute.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(2).)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Plaintiff argues that they have exercised reasonable diligence to attempt service through authorized methods but have not been able to personally serve Motorsport at its principal place of business or the residential address of its owner. (Mot., p. 2.) For this reason, Plaintiff moves the court to authorize service via electronic mail. (Ibid.)

First, Plaintiff attempted to serve Motorsport at its business address, but the premises was vacant. (Mot., p. 3.) Per an onsite manager, Plaintiff argues that Motorsport moved off the premises three years ago. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. D; Mot., p. 3.) Subsequently, Plaintiff attempted to serve Motorsport’s sole owner and agent at their residential address but was unsuccessful. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. E; Mot., p. 3.) Plaintiff seeks to serve Motorsport using a business email found on an invoice issued by Motorsport prior to the commencement of this action. (Mot., p. 4.) Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have communicated with Motorsport’s sole owner via email before and received an immediate response. (Krugliak Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that this is the most likely method for providing notice of the summons, complaint, and other related documents. (Ibid.)

In the supplemental declaration, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that the mailing address on record for Defendant is outdated or unreliable and in light of unsuccessful attempts at that address, there is no reason to believe Defendant would receive notice at that address. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 7.) Plaintiff’s counsel also declares that the address on file with the Secretary of State as Defendant’s principal place of business is similarly outdated and unreliable. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 8.) Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have had success communicating with Defendant through a confirmed email address in a previous action and as recent as August 10, 2026, when sharing a courtesy copy of the notice of continuance. (Supp. Decl., ¶¶ 11-15.) Further, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that the court in its previous lawsuit against Defendant (Case No. 26VECV01113) granted its motion to serve by electronic mail based on these facts. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 11.)

Here, reasonable efforts to personally serve Motorsport have been exhausted. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. E.) Motorsport’s mailing address and principal place of business address appear to be outdated, and Plaintiff has attempted service at the residential address of its owner or agent. (Supp. Decl., ¶ 5.)

Under section 413.30 the court is authorized “to direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice”, this doesn’t necessarily mean electronic service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30(a)(1).) Plaintiff has shown, through supplemental declaration, that service through electronic mail is reasonably calculated to give actual notice. Defendant has responded to Plaintiff’s communications through the email address provided and the court, in a prior lawsuit between the same parties, has authorized electronic service based on these facts.

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff VW Credit Inc.’s Motion to Authorize Electronic Service is GRANTED.
