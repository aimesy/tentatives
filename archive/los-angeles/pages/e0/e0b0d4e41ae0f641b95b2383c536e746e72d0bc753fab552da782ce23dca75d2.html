Case Number: 25STCV08308    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 - Quan Ding, et al. v. FCA US, LLC, et al.

    Plaintiffs Quan Ding and Soozee Kang's Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures

          Plaintiffs Quan Ding and Soozee Kang move to compel Defendant FCA US, LLC to serve initial disclosures pursuant Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(h). Plaintiffs also move for $2,500 in sanctions against Defense counsel pursuant section 871.26(j)(1). 

Initial Disclosures

          Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(b) provides, “Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).”

Here, Plaintiffs argue that Defendant has not provided written responses accompanied by verifications of defense counsel as required by Civil Code of Procedure section 2016.090(a)(5). In addition, Plaintiffs argue that Defendant has not produced all documents required under statute, such as the complete DealerCONNECT report, unverified complaint claims, AutoPay claims, all Technical Service Bulletins, Informational Service Bulletins, and service manuals for all systems reasonably related to the alleged nonconformities.

Section 871.26 does not require a manufacturer to serve written responses with verifications. Instead, it states: “Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).” (Civ. Proc. Code, § 871.26(b).) In their motion, Plaintiffs incorrectly conflate section 871.26 disclosures with initial disclosures under section 2016.090(a)(5). Thus, Plaintiffs’ argument that Defendant has failed to provide verified disclosures is inapplicable. 

Moreover, Plaintiffs do not contend that Defendant failed to produce its initial disclosures in their entirety. Instead, Plaintiffs argue that the initial disclosures are incomplete. Defendant claims it has served its complete initial disclosures which included over 1,000 pages of “repair records, warranty claims records, sales brochures, owners manual, Quick Start Guide, Warranty Booklet, Emissions Warranty, vehicle information detail report, glove box documents, the Monroney label, Technical Service Bulletins (“TSBs”), and recalls applicable to the Subject Vehicle.” (Kita Decl., ¶ 7; see also Ex. A.) As section 871.26 does not require verification, Defendant was not required to have verified that all records have been produced. Thus, the Court finds that Defendant complied with its initial disclosure obligations for these categories. To the extent that Plaintiffs seek additional information, they should pursue the information through interrogatories or requests for production. 

Sanctions

          Plaintiffs request sanctions for $2,500. Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (j) provides: “Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: [¶] (1) . . . [T]wo-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney … paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).

Because the Court finds Defendant complied with section 871.26 initial disclosure requirements, the Court denies Plaintiffs’ request for sanctions. 

Disposition

          Plaintiffs Quan Ding and Soozee Kang’s motion to compel initial disclosures is denied.


Case Number: 25STCV10411    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 3 – Intellectual Property v. Hyera An, et al.

          Plaintiff Intellectual Property’s Request for Default Judgment

Plaintiff Intellectual Property requests court judgment by default against Defendants Hyera An and Anthony An. Plaintiff’s application for default judgment has several defects.

Amount of Damages

Plaintiff presents insufficient evidence showing he is entitled to $37,800 under the parties’ residential listing agreement. “Even when the allegations of a complaint do support the judgment a plaintiff seeks, he is not automatically entitled to entry of that judgment by the court, simply because the defendant defaulted. Instead, it is incumbent upon the plaintiff to prove up his damages, with actual evidence.” (Kim v. Westmoore Partners, Inc. (2011) 201 Cal.App.4th 267, 272.) “When determining if the evidence at the [default] prove-up hearing supports the requested damages, the court’s role is serious, substantive, and often complicated.” (LCPFV, LLC v. Somatdary Inc. (2024) 106 Cal.App.5th 743, 754.)

Here, the listing price of the property according to the contract is $575,000. The agreement provides that Plaintiff, as Defendants’ broker, is due commission based on the following structure: “Commission compensation to the broker shall be 3% of the purchase price if the property is sold ‘AS IS’ to the tenant, or 5% if the Broker represents a buyer and the seller, or 6% if the Broker represents only the seller and there is a cooperating broker.” (Ex. 1, at p. 1.) Plaintiff does not specify which percentage he is due. Even assuming Plaintiff is due the highest 6% commission, this only amounts to $34,500, not $37,800. Plaintiff should clarify this in any renewed request for default judgment. If a different amount of damages is sought, the pre-judgment interest will need to be re-calculated.

Excessive Attorney Fees

Plaintiff also seeks excessive attorney fees. Attorney fees for a default judgment are generally limited to a specified schedule based on the amount of damages. (Local Rules 3.207(a), 3.214(a).) A plaintiff may recover “a fee greater than listed in the [default] schedule because of extraordinary services.” (Rule 3.214(d).) Plaintiff’s proposed judgment includes a request for $35,000 in attorney fees. That is far greater than the schedule, which provides for attorney fees of “$690 plus 3% of the excess over $10,000” for judgments between $10,000.01 to $50,000. (Rule 3.214(a).) In addition, while Plaintiff claims counsel submitted a declaration supporting this fee award, no such declaration was submitted to the Court. Plaintiff does not show extraordinary services justifying a greater fee.

Disposition

Plaintiff’s request for default judgment is denied without prejudice.

No. 12 - Angela Perry, et al. v. Edwin Redway

Defendant Edwin Redway’s Demurrer and Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Complaint

Defendant Edwin Redway demurs to Plaintiffs Angela Perry, trustee of the Perry Trust Agreement dated June 1, 2002, and Andrea Perry’s complaint. Defendant also moves to strike portions of Plaintiffs’ complaint referencing punitive damages. Plaintiffs did not file an opposition.

The Court finds there was no sufficient meet and confer between the parties.

California Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41(a) states that, before filing a demurrer, “the demurring party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (CCP § 430.41(a).) A declaration setting forth such meet and confer efforts must accompany the demurrer. (CCP § 430.41(a)(3).) A motion to strike has identical meet and confer requirements. (See CCP § 435.5(a).)

The Court notes that there is no declaration filed in support of the demurrer or motion to strike, detailing meet and confer efforts. There is also no description of meet and confer efforts in the memorandum of points and authorities

The hearing on the demurrer and motion to strike will be continued to allow parties time to meet and confer. Should the parties be unable to reach an agreement, Defendant should submit the required meet and confer declaration at least 16 court days before the next hearing as required under the Code.

Disposition

Defendant Edwin Redway’s demurrer and motion to strike as to Plaintiffs Angela Perry, trustee of the Perry Trust Agreement dated June 1, 2002, and Andrea Perry’s complaint are continued to September 8, 2026, at 8:30 am.


Case Number: 26STCP01416    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 11- Infinity Insurance Company v. Adrian Uriarte Rios

Petitioner Infinity Insurance Company’s Motion to Obtain Cellular Phone Records from T-Mobile, or Alternatively, To Enforce Deposition Subpoena

Petitioner Infinity Insurance Company moves the Court to issue an order requiring T-Mobile to produce cell phone records. In the alternative, Petitioner moves to enforce the deposition subpoena for production of business records.

The Court cannot grant the relief Petitioner seeks.

The Court acknowledges that Petitioner filed a “petition for superior court case number for the purpose of issuing subpoenas to witnesses for documents” on April 9, 2026. Petitioner was assigned a case number and served a deposition subpoena on T-Mobile on May 4, 2026. (Colman Decl., Ex. B.) T-Mobile has since objected to the subpoena, stating that it requires a court order to release the desired phone records, even with the authorized deposition subpoena and written consent from Respondent.

The Court is unaware of any legal authority permitting an insurance company to issue and enforce deposition subpoenas for its use in its claim investigations. Importantly, Code of Civil Procedure section 1985(c) provides, in relevant part, “An attorney at law who is the attorney of record in an action or proceeding, may sign and issue a subpoena duces tecum to require production of the matters or things described in the subpoena.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1985(c) [emphasis added]; see also Code Civ. Proc. § 1985.3(a)(3) [“‘Subpoenaing party’ means the person or persons causing a subpoena duces tecum to be issued or served in connection with any civil action or proceeding pursuant to this code.”].) The Civil Discovery Act states that “ ‘Action’ includes a civil action and a special proceeding of a civil nature.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2016.020(a).) A civil case is “a case prosecuted by one party against another for the declaration, enforcement, or protection of a right or the redress or prevention of a wrong.” (California Rules of Court, rule 1.6.)

Here, the Petition does not seek any declaration, enforcement, or protection of any right, nor does it seek to prevent or redress a wrong. It does not appear that Petitioner has any intention to file a lawsuit, and is seeking the business records only in its administrative, investigative capacity. Moreover, the Court is not aware of any authority under the Insurance Code that allows an insurance company to invoke subpoena power for this limited purpose. Petitioner does not identify any such authority in its Petition. Since there is no action or proceeding, Petitioner could not issue a valid subpoena, nor can it compel enforcement of one.

Disposition

Petitioner Infinity Insurance Company’s motion to obtain cellular phone records from T-Mobile, or alternatively, to enforce deposition subpoena is denied. Because no further relief is requested, the Court dismisses the Petition.
