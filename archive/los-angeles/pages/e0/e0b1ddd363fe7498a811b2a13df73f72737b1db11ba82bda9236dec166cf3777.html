Case Number: 22STCV34806    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 2 - Randy Rangel v. City of Los Angeles

OSC re: Enforceability of Lien

          The instant dispute concerns whether former counsel for Plaintiff, Abel Nair, is entitled to enforce a lien he holds against Plaintiff’s final judgment award of $5,171,403.39. Nair filed his notice of attorney charging lien with the Court on February 4, 2026, wherein he requests 50% of Plaintiff’s gross recovery per his retainer agreement with Plaintiff.

On July 24, 2026, the Court set an order to show cause regarding the enforceability of Nair’s lien on Plaintiff’s judgment against the City. The Court ordered briefing from Plaintiff and Nair to be filed per code. (See 7/27/26 Nunc Pro Tunc Minute Order.) The Court has received Rangel’s opening brief, reply brief, and supplemental reply brief. While Rangel’s supplemental reply brief references Nair’s opposition brief, which was apparently untimely served on Rangel on September 11, 2026, Nair did not file this brief with the Court.

On September 17, the day before the hearing, Nair emailed a “courtesy copy” of an opposition brief and declaration to the Department, along with confirming that the documents were submitted for efiling on September 15, 2026. As of noon on September 17, the Court does not have record that either document was filed. Even if the documents are filed prior to the hearing, they are long overdue. The Court exercises its discretion to disregard the brief and declaration as untimely filings. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d)).

The Court finds that Nair’s lien is unenforceable. The Court already determined the reasonable value of Nair’s services. On September 16, 2025, in connection with an attorney fee motion, this Court reviewed evidence submitted by Nair, including a supporting declaration and time records. From this evidence, the Court determined that Nair was entitled to recover $320,417.50, which accounted for 492.95 hours spent on the case at a rate of $650 per hour. (See 9/16/25 Minute Order.) Nair did not perform any legal services on this matter subsequent to October 2024, so all of his time was included in the prior fee award.

As mentioned above, Nair has not timely submitted any briefing or evidence for the Court’s consideration of the amount requested in the lien. There is no evidence that Nair should be entitled to recover any more than the fees already awarded to him in September 2025.

Disposition  

The Court hereby deems the charging lien filed by Nair on February 4, 2026 unenforceable. Nair’s fee award is already provided for in the Court’s September 11, 2026 ruling. That amount, $320,417.50, is to be paid by Plaintiff to Nair from the attorney fees portion of the judgment to be paid by Defendant.


Case Number: 23STCV06561    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 14 - Anthony Garcia v. Express Services, Inc., et al.

          Defendant Hutchinson Aerospace & Industry, Inc.’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award

Defendant Hutchinson Aerospace & Industry, Inc. (“Hutchinson”) petitions to confirm an arbitration award against Plaintiff Anthony Garcia. This is Hutchinson’s second petition to confirm the award. The Court again cannot confirm the arbitration award.

The Court denied Hutchinson’s first petition to confirm the arbitration award on March 27, 2026, for failure to attach the arbitrator’s final award to the petition. Instead, Hutchinson attached a copy of the arbitrator’s order granting its motion for summary judgment.

Here, in Hutchinson’s second petition to confirm the arbitration award, it claims to attach the arbitrator’s ruling on Hutchinson’s motion for summary judgment as Exhibit 1, the arbitrator’s judgment in favor of Hutchinson as Exhibit 2, and Hutchinson’s notice of entry of judgment as Exhibit 3. (See Petition, ¶¶ 6, 8-9.) However, upon review of the exhibits, all identified rulings and orders refer to Defendant Express Services, Inc., not Defendant Hutchinson Aerospace & Industry, Inc. Therefore, Hutchinson still does not submit evidence of a final award adjudicating all issues in its favor.

Disposition

Defendant Hutchinson Aerospace & Industry, Inc.’s petition to confirm arbitration award is denied.


Case Number: 25STCV03226    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 12 - Sara Iveth Bonilla Hernandez, et al. v. American Honda Motor Company, Inc.

    Plaintiffs' Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production

Plaintiffs Sara Iveth Bonilla Hernandez and Walter V. Reyes move to compel Defendant American Honda Motor Company, Inc. to provide further responses to requests for production nos. 7, 10, 16-24.

Further Responses

A requesting party may move to compel further responses if “[a] statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete,” “[a] representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive,” or “[a]n objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(a).)

Defendant makes an incomplete statement of compliance in response to request no. 7. After objecting to this request, Defendant responds:

Subject to and without waiving these objections, the requested production will be allowed in part and all documents in the demanded category that are in the possession, custody, or control of AHM will be included in the production. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.280, the production will include a copy of Sections 3 and 5 of its 2024-2025 Dealer Operations Manual directed to warranty repair, which contain information that is confidential, commercially sensitive, and/or proprietary, pursuant to entry of an appropriate protective order.

Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.220 provides, “A statement that the party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling has been directed will comply with the particular demand shall state that the production, inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, and related activity demanded, will be allowed either in whole or in part, and that all documents or things in the demanded category that are in the possession, custody, or control of that party and to which no objection is being made will be included in the production.”

This response does not clearly specify Defendant would produce “all” responsive documents in its possession, custody, or control. A statement of compliance in whole must use the word “all” because it states under oath not only that the responding party will produce some documents, but also that the responding party is not withholding any responsive documents. To the extent Defendant intended to make only partial statements of compliance or is withholding documents, the responses are evasive and do not identify any documents withheld subject to objections as required. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.240, subd. (b)(1).)

For remaining requests nos. 10 and 16-24, Defendant provided a code-compliant inability to comply statement. For example, in response to request no. 10 seeking a Workshop Manual for Plaintiff’s vehicle, Defendant objects and then responds, “Subject to and without waiving these objections, AHM is unable to comply with this request. Pursuant to a diligent search and reasonable inquiry, there is no ‘Workshop Manual’ specific to 2024 Honda CR-V Hybrid vehicles and no such document has ever existed.”

“A representation of inability to comply with the particular demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling shall affirm that a diligent search and a reasonable inquiry has been made in an effort to comply with that demand. This statement shall also specify whether the inability to comply is because the particular item or category has never existed, has been destroyed, has been lost, misplaced, or stolen, or has never been, or is no longer, in the possession, custody, or control of the responding party.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.230.) Defendant’s inability to comply statements fulfill these requirements.

Plaintiffs argue that Defendant’s inability to comply statements are incompatible with the objections preceding them. The Court disagrees—a party can object to the materials sought while also stating inability to comply with the request.

Compel Compliance

          Plaintiffs also request the Court compel Defendant to produce documents responsive to their requests. The Court declines to do this. By its nature, a motion to compel compliance is inherently contrary to a motion to compel a further response based on an inadequate initial response. Plaintiffs make no effort to address this inconsistency.

Disposition

Plaintiffs Sara Iveth Bonilla Hernandez and Walter V. Reyes’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production is granted in part. Defendant is ordered to serve a further verified response to no. 7 within 21 days. 


Case Number: 25STCV10909    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 15 - Sajma Wittmann v. Michael Finley, et al.

Defendant Michael Finley’s Motion for Imposition of Issue, Terminating, and Monetary Sanctions

Defendant Michael Finley moves for issue, terminating, and monetary sanctions against Plaintiff Sajma Wittmann for failure to comply with discovery orders.

CCP § 2023.030(d) permits courts to impose terminating sanctions against a party for misuse of the discovery process. Misuse of the process includes “[f]ailing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery” (CCP § 2023.010(d)) and “[d]isobeying a court order to provide discovery” (CCP § 2023.010(g)). Courts “may impose a terminating sanction” by “striking out” pleadings. (CCP § 2023.030(d)(1).) Discovery sanctions should be imposed incrementally, “starting with monetary sanctions and ending with the ultimate sanction of termination.” (Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604.)

On June 2, 2026, the Court heard Defendant’s motions to compel initial discovery responses to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production. The Court granted Defendant’s motions, ordered Plaintiff to serve verified responses without objections within 10 days, and issued monetary sanctions. In the instant motion, Defendant declares that Plaintiff has still not served her written discovery responses. (Finley Decl., ¶ 5.)

In opposition, Plaintiff does not address whether she has served the discovery responses, nor attached any responses to show compliance with the Court’s order. Instead, she incorporates an excerpt from her prior brief opposing Defendant’s discovery motions. She also argues that this Court is not permitted to decide the instant motion because she filed a statement of disqualification.

As an initial matter, the Court notes that the issue of disqualification has already been decided. The Court reviewed Plaintiff’s statement of disqualification and struck it on September 3, 2026. Thus, the Court has the authority and ethical duty to decide this motion, as Plaintiff did not disclose any valid legal grounds for disqualification.

Regarding sanctions, the Court has reviewed the file and declines to award terminating sanctions. While the Court acknowledges that Plaintiff has seemingly not served initial discovery responses for a case she initiated in April 2025, terminating sanctions is too drastic a remedy considering the Court has only imposed monetary sanctions once. “[A] terminating sanction should generally not be imposed until the court has attempted less severe alternatives and found them to be unsuccessful and/or the record clearly shows lesser sanctions would be ineffective.” (Ibid.) Terminating sanctions are “ ‘ordinarily a drastic measure which should be employed with caution.’ ” (McArthur v. Bockman (1989) 208 Cal.App.3d 1076, 1080.)

The Court finds the appropriate sanction will be to give CACI jury instruction No. 204, which may be modified if warranted by the evidence introduced at trial. CACI No. 204, based on Evidence Code section 413, states, “You may consider whether one party intentionally concealed or destroyed evidence. If you decide that a party did so, you may decide that the evidence would have been unfavorable to that party.”

The Court will make a final determination of whether to give CACI No. 204 as drafted or modified once both parties have rested at trial. “Giving these instructions [CACI No. 204] [is] proper” when there is “evidence from which it could reasonably be concluded that” a party “failed to produce stronger evidence or willfully suppressed evidence.” (Sprague v. Equifax, Inc. (1985) 166 Cal.App.3d 1012, 1051.) The Court will give the instruction if the evidence at trial permits such a conclusion.

The Court exercises its discretion not to award Defendant monetary sanctions for having to bring this motion. The Court cautions Plaintiff, though, that further failure to comply with discovery orders could result in additional sanctions, including monetary, issue or terminating sanctions.

Disposition

          Defendant Michael Finley’s motion for imposition of issue, terminating, and monetary sanctions is granted in part. The Court will consider giving CACI No. 204 as a jury instruction, either as drafted or as to be revised. The Court will reserve its ruling on the instruction until after the evidence is presented at trial.


Case Number: 26STCV13792    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 13 - Gabriel Escobar v. Sai Long Beach B, Inc., et al.

Plaintiff Gabriel Escobar's Motion for Leave to File First Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Gabriel Escobar moves for leave to file his first amended complaint to add a request for damages under his Consumer Legal Remedies Act claim and add Wells Fargo Bank National Association as a Defendant.

Code of Civil Procedure section 473(a)(1) provides, “The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.” Courts exercise their discretion “liberally to permit amendment,” and “[t]he policy favoring amendment is so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified.” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428.) A motion for leave to amend will normally be granted unless (a) the party seeking to amend has delayed bringing the proposed amendment; and (b) the delay in seeking leave to amend will cause prejudice to an opposing party. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.)

Plaintiff filed his initial complaint in February 2026. The parties are still at the pleading stage and trial has not been set. Defendants filed no opposition identifying any delay in amendment or resulting prejudice. The Court will permit Plaintiff to file the first amended complaint.

Disposition

Plaintiff Gabriel Escobar’s motion for leave to file a first amended complaint is granted. Plaintiff is ordered to file the proposed first amended complaint included as Exhibit 2 in Plaintiff’s Compendium of Exhibits.
