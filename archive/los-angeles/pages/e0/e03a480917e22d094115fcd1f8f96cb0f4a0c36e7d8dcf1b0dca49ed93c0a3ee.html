UNOPPOSED

___________________________________________________________________

 

Defendants Big Sav Discount Store’s and Brand LLC’s Motions to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories from Plaintiff Edna Isayan ___________________________________________________________________

 

Facts: This is an action for negligence and premises liability. The Complaint alleges as follows. Plaintiff Edna Isayan (“Plaintiff”) was injured in a slip and fall in a puddle of liquid negligently permitted to remain on the premises of Defendants Big Sav Discount Store and Brand LLC. (Complaint at pp. 5–6.) 

 

Procedural History: Plaintiff filed the Complaint on January 28, 2025, alleging two causes of action:

 

1.      Negligence

2.      Premises Liability

 

Defendants Big Sav Discount Store and Brand LLC filed the present motions to compel on May 21, 2026. No opposition has been filed.

 

Analysis:

I.                   MOTIONS TO COMPEL

A propounding party may demand a responding party to produce documents that are in their possession, custody or control. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010.) A party may likewise conduct discovery by propounding interrogatories to another party to be answered under oath. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.010, subd. (a).) The responding party must respond to the production demand either by complying, by representing that the party lacks the ability to comply, or by objecting to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210.) The responding party must respond to the interrogatories by answering or objecting. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.210, subd. (a).) If the responding party fails to serve timely responses, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the production demand and interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.)

 

A party who fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories or a demand for inspection waives any objection to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.)

Defendants Big Sav Discount Store and Brand, LLC (“Defendants”) bring motions to compel responses from Plaintiff Edna Isayan (“Plaintiff”) to Special Interrogatories. Defendants served the interrogatories upon Plaintiff on January 5, 2026, with an agreed upon extended deadline for response of April 13, 2026. (Brockenbrow Decl. ¶¶ 2–6.) Plaintiff has not provided responses despite Defendants’ attempts to meet and confer. (Brockenbrow Decl. ¶¶ 7–11.)

Defendants have demonstrated that Plaintiff has failed to serve responses, timely or untimely, to the discovery at issue, and are therefore entitled to responses. Plaintiff has filed no opposition to the motions.

The motions are therefore GRANTED. Responses shall be served, without objections, within 20 days of this order.

1.      Sanctions

The prevailing party on a motion to compel is generally entitled to monetary sanctions, unless the court “finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Sanctions are also mandatory against a party whose failure to serve responses to requests for admission makes the motion necessary. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

 

Defendants seek $390 per motion, representing two hours of attorney work at $165 per hour on each motion, plus a $60 filing fee. (Brockenbrow Decl. ¶ 12.)

 

Sanctions are awarded against Plaintiff and counsel in the amount of $780.00.

Case Number: 25STCV08698    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: 732


Jennifer Troia, Director of California Department of Social Services v. The Food Bank of Southern California., et al.

 

Wednesday, June 23, 2026

 

 

 

CASE NUMBER: 25STCV08698

 

OPPOSED

___________________________________________________________________

 

Plaintiff Jennifer Troia, as Director of California’s Department of Social Services’ Motion to Compel Further Responses and Production for Requests for Production from Defendant Gregg Bynum

 ___________________________________________________________________

 

Facts: This is an action for breach of contract, fraud, and conversion, brought by Plaintiff Jennifer Troia in her capacity as Director of the California Department of Social Services (“DSS”). The Complaint alleges as follows. DSS had at least two contracts with Defendant The Food Bank of Southern California (“Food Bank”) through which DSS agreed to reimburse Food Bank for certain disbursements and expenses related to its mission to provide food to those in need, pursuant to various California and federal programs. (Complaint ¶¶ 26–55.)

In response to a whistleblower complaint in June 2024, DSS investigated and uncovered various acts of misappropriation of the funds it provided to Food Bank. (Complaint ¶¶ 61–64.) Food Bank obtained reimbursement from DSS for expenditures to third party companies owned or associated with one of its officers, Defendant Giuseppe Briguglio and his relative, Defendant Abele Cosmo Briguglio, which were not lawfully compensable and in many cases were for goods and services never delivered.  (Complaint ¶¶ 67–116.) Similar apportionments were made to companies associated with Food Bank officer Defendant Dion Rambo, (Complaint ¶¶ 117–123.) Food Bank received funds for certain programs which failed to meet program requirements. (Complaint ¶¶ 124–152.)

Food Bank received reimbursement for CEO travel, donor management, and other non-compensable items. (Complaint ¶¶ 153– 179.) The officers and directors violated the Food Bank’s bylaws, failed to maintain corporate formalities, and Defendants Jeanne Cooper, Lamarr Ramsey, Brian Weaver, Egerton Forster, Alice Harris, Rambo diverted funds for private uses.  (Complaint ¶¶ 180–229.)

Procedural History: Plaintiff filed the Complaint on March 25, 2025, alleging nine causes of action:

1.      Breach of Contract

2.      Petition for Writ of Possession

3.      Breach of Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing

4.      Money Had and Received

5.      Equitable Accounting

6.      Fraud – Concealment

7.      Fraud – Intentional Misrepresentation

8.      Conversion

9.      Declaratory Relief

This court on January 12, 2026, sustained in part the demurrer of Defendants Abele Cosmo Briguglio, Joe Briguglio, ANB Consulting Services, Inc., Creative Foods, Inc., Heritage Fine Foods, Inc., JCO Holdings, and River Point Foods, Inc., to the seventh cause of action for fraudulent concealment with leave to amend, and as to the eighth cause of action for fraudulent misrepresentation with leave to amend against Defendants G. Briguglio and A. Briguglio. The court granted these defendants’’ motion to strike allegations in paragraphs 324 and 325 of the Complaint alleging conversion of real property.

Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) on February 11, 2026, alleging the same causes of action as in the original Complaint.

On April 28, 2026, this court denied Defendants Foodbank and Brian Weaver’s motions to quash subpoenas, but granted their motion in part as directed to Quigley & Myron.

Plaintiff filed the present motion to compel further on May 14, 2026.

Defendant Bynum filed an opposition on June 10, 2026.

Plaintiff filed a reply on June 15, 2026.

Analysis

I.                   MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER

“A party may demand that any other party produce . . . a document that is in the possession, custody, or control of the party on whom the demand is made.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010(b).) The demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that (1) a statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete, (2) a representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive, or (3) an objection in the response is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(a).) “The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand,” and “[t]he motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(b).)

 

A motion to compel a further response to an inspection demand must set forth specific facts showing “good cause” justifying the discovery sought by the inspection demand. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(b)(1); Kirkland v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 98.) Unless there is a legitimate privilege issue or claim of attorney work product, that burden is met simply by a fact-specific showing of relevance. (TBG Ins. Services Corp. v. Superior Court (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 443, 444.) Once the moving party demonstrates good cause for the discovery, the burden is on the responding party to justify any objection or failure to fully respond to the inspection demand.  (Coy v Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 210, 220.)

 

“If a party filing a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling under Sections 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, 2031.240, and 2031.280 thereafter fails to permit the inspection, copying, testing, or sampling in accordance with that party's statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.320, subd. (a).)

 

Plaintiff Jennifer Troia, in her capacity as Director of the California Department of Social Services (“Plaintiff”) seeks further responses and to compel production of records from Defendant Gregg Bynum (“Bynum”) to Requests for Production No. 2, 3, 13, 18,

19, 31, 32, and 38. Bynum was a member of the board of Defendant The Food Bank of Southern California (“Foodbank”) from 2009 on, and was its director in 2022 and 2023. (Matsushima Decl. ¶ 15.)

 

The requests are as follows. Requests No. 2, 3, and 13, seek communications with Foodbank, its accountants, and its board of directors from January 1, 2016 to the present — the “relevant time period.” (Separate Statement at pp. 3–7.) Requests No. 18, 19, and 38 seek documents related to Bynum’s time on the board of directors, all board director meeting minutes, and all documents related to his involvement with the Foodbank during the relevant period. (Separate Statement at pp. 8–11, 14–15.) And Requests No. 31 and 32 seek documents relating to Foodbank’s tax filings and finances during the same period. (Separate Statement at pp. 11–12.)

 

Defendant provided statements of compliance for Requests No. 2, 18, 19, 32, and 38, stating he would produce all responsive non-privileged documents in his possession. As to Request No. 3, relating to communications with accountants, he responded with a statement of inability to comply because such documents “never existed.” (Separate Statement at p. 5.) And he provided a less-specific statement of inability to comply for Request No. 31, relating to tax filings, stating that he is “not currently in possession” of responsive documents. (Separate Statement at pp. 11–12.)

 

Plaintiffs in the present motion argue that Bynum’s promised documentary production is insufficient, consisting of only 890 pages, comprising 62 documents. (Matsushima Decl. ¶ 19.) Plaintiff argues that Bynum was a board member of Foodbank from 2009 on, and that per the documents produced in this case the Foodbank’s board members used their personal emails to correspond regarding Foodbank business. (Matsushima Decl. ¶ 16d.) And as secretary and treasurer, Bynum would have been responsible at various times for maintaining minutes of board meetings and keeping its financial records. (Matsushima Decl. Exhs. 6, 7.) But Bynum has produced only four documents relating to board meeting minutes and only nine documents related to Foodbank finances. (Separate Statement at pp. 11, 13–14; Matsushima Decl. ¶ 19.)

 

Plaintiff also argues that despite claiming that no correspondence between himself and Foodbank accountants ever existed, concurrent production from another source reveals at least one such correspondence. (Separate Statement at pp. 5–6.) Plaintiff also argues that Bynum’s statement of inability to comply related to tax filings is not code-compliant. (Separate Statement at pp. 11–12.)

 

Finally, Plaintiff argues that the requests sought native files of the documents sought, or image files of the documents, combined with relevant metadata. (Separate statement at p. 17..) But Defendant produced only PDF files in its production. (Separate Statement at pp. 17–18.)

 

Defendant Bynum in opposition argues that Foodbank’s President since 2014, Jeane Cooper, exercised exclusive control over Foodbank’s finances and that he himself lacked access ordinarily expected to a treasurer during the relevant period. (Opposition at p. 3.) Bynum argues that most relevant correspondence regarding Foodbank’s financial situation would have been verbal, rather than through email. (Ibid.) Bynum argues that the failure to produce responsive documents in native format was the product of inadvertence. (Opposition at p. 4.)

 

Plaintiff has shown good cause for the present motion.  Bynum is a longstanding board member of the Foodbank and held official positions as secretary and treasurer during the relevant period, calling into question the relative scarcity of the production. Although he proffers explanations for the paucity of his production, he offers no declaration or other evidence in support of his justifications, whether for the domineering of Jeane Cooper or the verbal nature of relevant correspondence.

 

Indeed, Bynum in opposing the motion suggests that the requests have been construed in a manner to limit the universe of responsive production. Request No. 2 seeks correspondence between Bynum and Foodbank, and Bynum argues that this request seeks communications with “the Food Bank as an entity,” which during the relevant period would have been its president Jeane Cooper. (Opposing Separate Statement at p. 4.) But as noted in Plaintiff’s motion, the request for correspondence with Foodbank seeks communications with  “its principals, operating divisions, present or former owners, employees, servants, officers, directors, agents, representatives, and any other PERSONS” acting on its behalf. (Separate Statement at pp. 2–3.) Bynum’s opposition thus suggests that additional responsive documents exist, when the requests are properly interpreted.

 

Bynum also does not address the defective character of his statements of inability to comply offered in response to Requests No. 3 and 31. Bynum does not address the contention that additional correspondence between himself and Foodbank accountants has been found, contradicting his statement that such correspondence “never existed.” And his response to Request No. 31, related to tax filings, does not comply with Code of Civil Procedure § 2031.230:

 

A representation of inability to comply with the particular demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling shall affirm that a diligent search and a reasonable inquiry has been made in an effort to comply with that demand. This statement shall also specify whether the inability to comply is because the particular item or category has never existed, has been destroyed, has been lost, misplaced, or stolen, or has never been, or is no longer, in the possession, custody, or control of the responding party. The statement shall set forth the name and address of any natural person or organization known or believed by that party to have possession, custody, or control of that item or category of item.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.230.) Bynum’s response offers no explanation as to his inability to comply or who may have responsive documents, and the explanation that he offers in opposition to the present motion is not verified or supported by evidence.

 

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED. Defendant Bynum shall produce all nonprivileged documents responsive to Requests for Production No. 2, 3, 13, 18, 19, 31, 32, and 38 within 30 days of this order. Simultaneously, he shall serve further discovery responses to reflect any supplemental productions. ESI shall be produced in native format.

 

1.      Sanctions

 Statute provides that the court shall impose sanctions upon a party who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to interrogatories, requests for production of documents, or requests for admission, absent substantial justification otherwise. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.300, subd. (d); 2031.310, subd. (h); 2033.290, subd. (d).)

 

Plaintiff seeks $13,812.50 in sanctions against Bynum, representing 21.25 hours of attorney work meeting and conferring on the discovery at issue and for bringing the present motion, at a rate of $650 per hour.  (Matsushima Decl. ¶¶ 28–29.)   This includes 3.5 hours meeting and conferring, which included document requests not at issue and yielded supplemental responses thereto, which hours are properly deducted from the total. (Matsushima Decl. Exhs. 3–5.) And given the relative simplicity of Bynum’s opposition materials, the seven hours anticipated to prepare a reply are properly reduced to 3.5 hours. (Matsushima Decl. ¶ 28.) Accordingly, sanctions are awarded against Bynum in the reduced amount of $9,262.50.

Case Number: 25STCV16733    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: 732

Estate of Deborah Shipp, et al. v. CJ Spa Group, Inc., et al. 

 

Tuesday, June 23, 2026 

 

 

 

CASE NUMBER: 25STCV15733 

 

OPPOSED 

___________________________________________________________________ 

 

Defendant CJ Spa Group Inc.’s Motions to Compel Further Responses to Form and Special Interrogatories from Plaintiff Joe Shipp 

___________________________________________________________________ 

 

Facts: This is an action for negligence. The Complaint alleges as follows. Plaintiff Deborah Shipp (“Decedent”) died following a medical emergency suffered on the premises of a spa operated by Defendant CJ Spa Group Inc. (“Defendant”), arising from their negligent supervision and maintenance of a sauna. (Complaint ¶¶ 8–14.) The present action is brought by Decedent’s spouse and children as successors in interest, Joe Shipp, Joseph Shipp, Jerren Shipp, and Brittney Shipp. (Complaint ¶ 1.)  

 

Procedural History: Plaintiffs filed the Complaint on May 30, 2025, alleging three causes of action: 

 

  1. Negligence 

  1. Premises Liability 

  1. Negligent Hiring, Supervision, & Retention 

 

On June 4, 2026, this court denied Defendant’s motion to compel further responses to requests for admission from Plaintiff Brittney Shipp. 

 

On June 9, 2026, this court granted in part Defendant’s motion to compel further responses to form and special interrogatories from Plaintiff Brittney Shipp, and granted Defendant’s motion to deem matters admitted against Plaintiffs Joe and Jarren Shipp. 

 

On June 11, 2026, this court granted in part Defendant’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production from Plaintiff Brittney Shipp.  

 

Defendant filed the present motions to compel further responses to form and special interrogatories from Plaintiff Joe Shipp on May 1, 2026. 

 

Plaintiffs filed an opposition on June 9, 2026. 

 

Defendant filed a reply on June 15, 2026.  

  

  

Analysis: 

  1. MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER 

 

“Any party may obtain discovery . . . by propounding to any other party to the action written interrogatories to be answered under oath.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.010(a).) If a propounding party is not satisfied with the response served by a responding party, the former may move the court to compel further interrogatory responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 403.)  The propounding party must demonstrate that the responses were incomplete, inadequate or evasive, or that the responding party asserted objections that are either without merit or too general.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300(a)(1)–(3); Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc., supra, 148 Cal.App.4th at 403.) 

Defendant CJ Spa Group, Inc. (“Defendant”) moves to compel further responses to Form Interrogatories No. 2.3, 2.4, 10.2, and 12.1, and Special Interrogatories No. 7, 118–123, 125, 126, 129–134, 140, 141, 144–153, 160, 163, 166, 169 and 172, from Plaintiff Joe Shipp.  

The present motion conforms substantially to a motion previously brought by Defendant for further responses against Plaintiff Brittney Shipp. Though some interrogatories at issue in the prior motion are not at issue here, all of the interrogatories at issue in the present motion were at issue in the prior motion, save Form Interrogatory No. 10.2.  

In its order on the prior motion to compel further responses to the same interrogatories, the court found the motions untimely except as to Form Interrogatory No. 12.1 and Special Interrogatories No. 49, 50, 66-75, and 82-86. The court ruled that further responses were required as to Form Interrogatory No. 12.1 and Special Interrogatories No. 66–74, and 82–86, limited in scope to illnesses or injuries of Decedent that occurred on or after June 1, 2024.  

The same logic applies here. The declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel denies that any agreement was reached concerning interrogatories other than Form Interrogatory No.12.1 and Special Interrogatories No. 49, 50, 66-75, and 82-86. (Ganji Decl. ¶ 7.) As set forth in the prior order, such testimony bears heavily on the motion as Defendant’s basis for claiming the motion is timely consists of written memorializations of  oral conferences which do not describe the discovery at issue and which are not subscribed by Plaintiff’s counsel.  Plaintiff in reply acknowledges the June 9 order, and seeks an order compelling further responses in conformity with the logic set forth there. (Reply at p. 3.) As Special Interrogatories No. 66-75 and 82-86 are not at issue in the present motion, this means that Plaintiff has narrowed the scope of the motions to seek  a further response to Form Interrogatory No. 12.1 alone. (Ibid.) 

Form Interrogatory No. 12.1 asked Plaintiff to provide contact information for individuals who witnessed the “incident,” or heard or made statements on the scene of the incident, or any person Plaintiff claims has knowledge of the incident. (Separate Statement at pp. 4–5.) “Incident” is defined in the interrogatories as  “the circumstances and events surrounding the alleged accident, injury, or other occurrence or breach of contract giving rise to this action or proceeding.” (O’Brien Decl. Exh. 12.)  

Plaintiff responded as follows: 

This request is objectionable to the extent it seeks information that is protected by privilege, privacy or attorney work product. Subject to the foregoing: After a reasonable and diligent search, unknown. This information is more available to Defendant as  the patrons came to Defendant to report that Mrs. Shipp was in distress. 

(Separate Statement at p. 5.)  

Plaintiff’s response is internally inconsistent, and a further response is required. Plaintiff at once states that he has no knowledge of any percipient witnesses, and then states that “the patrons came to Defendant to report that Mrs. Shipp was in distress.” (Ibid.) Plaintiff thus purports to rely on knowledge of the incident, presumably derived in some manner from witnesses on the scene, in order to explain why he cannot provide information concerning witnesses.  

Plaintiff may state that he lacks knowledge sufficient to answer, if such is in fact the case following “a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain the information by inquiry to other natural persons or organizations.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.220, subd. (c).) But “[i]f only partial answers can be supplied, the answers should reveal all information then available to the party. If a person cannot furnish details, he should set forth the efforts made to secure the information. He cannot plead ignorance to information which can be obtained from sources under his control.” (Deyo v. Kilbourne (1978) 84 Cal.App.3d 771, 782.) Here, Plaintiff’s response itself discloses some knowledge of the scene, but not how Plaintiff derives such knowledge, if not from the very people whom this interrogatory seeks to reveal. Thus a further response is required to this interrogatory. 

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED as to Form Interrogatory No. 12.1. The motion is otherwise DENIED as to the remaining Form and Special Interrogatories. 1 

  1. Sanctions 

 

Statute provides that the court shall impose sanctions upon a party who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to interrogatories, requests for production of documents, or requests for admission, absent substantial justification otherwise. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.300, subd. (d); 2031.310, subd. (h); 2033.290, subd. (d).) 

 

Defendant seeks $424.01 per motion, representing a split of the total hours incurred on Defendant’s multiple discovery motions, plus two $82.01 filing fees. (O’Brien Decl. ¶ 20.) Plaintiff, meanwhile, seeks $3,500.00, representing five hours of attorney work at $500 per hour. (Ganji Decl. ¶ 9.) No sanctions are awarded, as the parties have obtained only partial relief.
