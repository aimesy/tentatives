[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING MOTION TO COMPEL DISCOVERY RESPONSES

 

Dept. 506

8:30 a.m.

September 24, 2026

 

Plaintiff served Requests for Production (Two) on Defendant, and Defendant did not serve responses.

On August 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel responses.

Where a party fails to serve timely responses to discovery requests, the court may make an order compelling responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290 [interrogatories], 2031.300 [documents]; Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 403.)   A party that fails to serve timely responses waives any objections to the request, including ones based on privilege or the protection of attorney work product.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (a) [interrogatories], 2031.300, subd. (a) [documents].)

Defendant contends that the motion is moot because it served late responses on September 3, 2026.  However, Defendant’s responses consist of objections, which have been waived.  The Court will not grant Defendant affirmative relief from waiver or from alleged overbreadth based solely on arguments raised in opposition.  (See Opposition at pp. 5-9.)  If Defendant believes it has meritorious arguments on those points, Defendant must file the appropriate motions.

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.

Defendant is ordered to provide verified responses, without objections, to Requests for Production (Set Two) within 30 days.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER OVERRULING DEMURRERS

 

Dept. 506

8:30 a.m.

September 24, 2026

 

On August 15, 2024, Plaintiff Lake Lindero Homeowners Association filed this legal malpractice action, and on April 2, 2025, it filed a first amended complaint (“FAC”).

On May 16, 2025, Defendants Patrick Kevin O’Brien and O’Brien Law P.C. (collectively, “O’Brien”) filed a demurrer.

On August 25, 2025, Defendants Myers, Widders, Gibson, Jones & Feingold, LLP (“MWGJF”) and Jill Lisa Friedman filed a demurrer.

LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.  (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.  (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)  “Because a demurrer challenges defects on the face of the complaint, it can only refer to matters outside the pleading that are subject to judicial notice.”  (Arce ex rel. Arce v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 556.)

DEMURRER OF O’BRIEN

Defendants’ request for judicial notice is granted.  (See Day v. Sharp (1975) 50 Cal.App.3d 904, 914 [“‘A court may take judicial notice of the existence of each document in a court file, but can only take judicial notice of the truth of facts asserted in documents such as orders, findings of fact and conclusions of law, and judgments.’”].)

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s claims are barred by its own unclean hands.  Defendants cite the Final Statement of Decision in the underlying action, where “the underlying court in fact reprimands Plaintiff itself at great length for its ‘improper pre-trial behavior,’ ‘wrongful conduct,’ ‘violation of privacy rights,’ and ‘unlawful acts’ throughout the underlying action.”  (Demurrer at p. 9; see RJN, Ex. C.)  There is a general rule that application of the doctrine of unclean hands is a question of fact.  (CrossTalk Productions, Inc. v. Jacobson (1998) 65 Cal.App.4th 631, 639.)  The Court cannot conclude at the pleading stage that it was solely Plaintiff’s conduct that led to its damages.

Defendants also argue that Plaintiff’s claims are time-barred.  To sustain a demurrer on the statute of limitations, the running of the statute must appear clearly and affirmatively on the face of the complaint.  (Geneva Towers Ltd. Partnership v. City of San Francisco (2003) 29 Cal.4th 769, 781.)  “‘[I]t is not enough that the complaint might be time-barred.  [Citation.]’”  (Ibid.)  An action against an attorney for a wrongful act or omission arising from professional services must be brought within the earlier of (1) one year after the plaintiff discovers (or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered) the fact constituting the wrongful act or omission, or (2) four years after the date of the wrongful act or omission.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 340.6, subd. (a).)  The time shall not exceed four years, but it is tolled when the plaintiff has not sustained actual injury.  (Ibid.)  “The test for actual injury under section 340.6 . . . is whether the plaintiff has sustained any damages compensable in an action, other than one for actual fraud, against an attorney for a wrongful act or omission arising in the performance of professional services.”  (Jordache Enterprises, Inc. v. Brobeck, Phleger & Harrison (1998) 18 Cal.4th 739, 751.)

Defendants argue that Plaintiff knew of its injury more than one year before filing the FAC on April 2, 2025, making it time barred.  (Demurrer at p. 12.)  But the correct timeline is based on the filing date of the original Complaint (which also named the O’Brien Defendants).  Thus, the calculation must be based on the August 15, 2024 filing.  Defendants unwittingly undermine their own argument and demonstrate why this action is timely:  “Undoubtedly, Plaintiff knew on August 18, 2023, of the alleged wrongful acts or omissions of Defendants and certainly should have undertaken an investigation at that time, particularly with new counsel, to discover any additional facts giving rise to Plaintiff’s claims against Defendants.”  (Demurrer at p. 13.)  Additionally, “[b]y August 18, 2023, when the underlying court issued its Statement of Decision, Plaintiff had already expended some certain sum of legal fees and costs; and was then subject to various sanctions and negative results in litigation,” constituting actual injury.  (Id. at p. 14.)  Plaintiff timely filed this action on August 15, 2024, less than one year after Plaintiff’s August 18, 2023 knowledge of its injury.

The demurrer is OVERRULED.

Defendants are ordered to file their answers within 10 days.

DEMURRER OF MWGJF AND JILL LISA FRIEDMAN

Defendants’ request for judicial notice is granted.  (See Day v. Sharp (1975) 50 Cal.App.3d 904, 914 [“‘A court may take judicial notice of the existence of each document in a court file, but can only take judicial notice of the truth of facts asserted in documents such as orders, findings of fact and conclusions of law, and judgments.’”].)

Defendants argue that the underlying legal malpractice was caused by the prior counsel’s conduct that led to terminating sanctions.  Defendants cite the Final Statement of Decision in the underlying action, which stated that the prior counsel delivered “a ‘nuclear blow’ to the attorney-client privilege and fatally taints this case.”  (RJN, Ex. B at p. 15; Demurrer at p. 4.)  According to Defendants, “[t]he clearly unlawful conduct all happened before Myers became involved and Myers had no opportunity to counsel LLHOA to refrain from engaging in the offensive conduct that had already occurred.”  (Demurrer at p. 7.)

Yet, the same Final Statement of Decision also states, “MWGJF tries to isolate itself from the wrongdoings of prior counsel for LLHOA.  It cannot.”  (RJN, Ex. B at p. 94.)  Thus, on the face of the FAC and judicially noticeable documents, it is not clear that “[t]here is no way to cure the issue of LLHOA’s unclean hands as a defense in a further amended pleading.”  (See Demurrer at p. 8.)

The demurrer is OVERRULED.

Defendants are ordered to file their answers within 10 days.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR STAY

 

Dept. 506

8:30 a.m.

September 24, 2026

 

On August 24, 2026, Plaintiff Eric Green filed a motion to stay this action against Defendants County of Los Angeles (erroneously sued as Los Angeles County Sheriff), California Dept. of Insurance, Andrew Gulcher, Erin Hall, and Myra Svoboda.

Plaintiff asks to stay this action pending the final resolution of the “related Petition for Writ of Administrative Mandamus” currently pending in Case No. 26STCP03091.  According to Plaintiff, “[t]he resolution of that administrative writ will determine whether the underlying agency action was valid or abused it’s [sic] discretion.”  Plaintiff contends that “[p]roceeding without a stay would inflict severe hardship on Petitioner by forcing the expenditure of time and significant financial resources litigating matters that may be fundamentally structured pause designed to bring clarity to the dispute.”

The Court already determined that this case and Case No. 26STCP03091 are not related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a).  Plaintiff provides no relevant legal authority or argument.  “[P]arties are required to include argument and citation to authority in their briefs, and the absence of these necessary elements allows this court to treat [an] issue as waived.”  (Interinsurance Exchange v. Collins (1994) 30 Cal.App.4th 1445, 1448.)

Accordingly, the motion for stay is DENIED.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER SUSTAINING DEFENDANT’S DEMURRER

 

Dept. 506

8:30 a.m.

September 24, 2026

 

On November 14, 2025, Plaintiff Mekonnen Lemlem filed this action against Defendant Metro Bus Operations.

On May 4, 2026, Defendant filed a demurrer.

DISCUSSION

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.  (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.  (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)  A special demurrer for uncertainty under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (f) is disfavored and will only be sustained where the pleading is so bad that defendant or plaintiff cannot reasonably respond—i.e., cannot reasonably determine what issues must be admitted or denied, or what counts or claims are directed against him or her.  (Khoury v. Maly’s of Calif., Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.)

The complaint uses a Judicial Council form for an action based in personal injury.  The Court agrees that “Plaintiff has merely filed a Judicial Council form face page without attaching any necessary cause of action pages to allege duty, breach, causation, or damages.  The total absence of any cause of action pages makes it impossible for Defendant to ascertain the nature of the claims asserted or the specific acts or omissions for which Defendant is allegedly liable.”  (Demurrer at p. 3.)

Item 10 on page 3 of the form complaint states, “The following causes of action are attached and the statements above apply to each (each complaint must have one or more causes of action attached).”  Plaintiff checked the box for General Negligence.  At Item 11, Plaintiff selected that she suffered “hospital and medical expenses” and “general damage.”  There are no additional attached pages.

“The Judicial Council pleading forms have simplified the art of pleading, and have made the task of drafting much easier.  Nevertheless, in some cases more is required than merely placing an ‘X’ in a box.  [Citation.]”  (People ex rel. Dept. of Transportation v. Superior Court (1992) 5 Cal.App.4th 1480, 1484.)

The Complaint is so uncertain that Defendant cannot reasonably respond to it.

CONCLUSION

The demurrer is SUSTAINED with 30 days’ leave to amend.
