Case Number: 21STLC01780    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 731

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: CROSS-DEFENDANT’S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT Dept. 731 9:30 a.m. July 14, 2026

Moving Party: Cross-Defendant, Robert Bare dba BARE LAW.

Opposing Party: Cross-Complainant, Michellei Cephus.

Notice: OK.

1. Background Facts

Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Robert Bare d/b/a BARE LAW (“Cross-Defendant”) sues Defendant/Cross-Complainant Michellei Cephus (“Cross-Complainant”) pursuant to a February 4, 2021, Complaint alleging a single cause of action for breach of contract arising from allegations that Cross-Complainant failed to pay for legal services rendered and owes Cross-Defendant $3,475 in fees.

In turn, Cross-Defendant sues Cross-Complainant pursuant to a November 3, 2023, First Amended Cross-Complaint (“FAXC”) alleging claims of legal malpractice, breach of contract, and breach of fiduciary duty related to the services alleged to have been rendered in Cross-Defendant’s Complaint.

Before the Court is Cross-Defendant’s April 20, 2026, motion for summary judgment against the claims alleged in the FAXC. Cross-Complainant filed an opposition on June 22, 2026, and Cross-Defendant filed a reply on July 7, 2026.

2. Motion for Summary Judgment: DENIED

a. Legal Standard

i. Summary Judgment – Generally

A motion for summary judgment shall be granted if all the papers submitted show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact for trial or that the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (c).)

ii. Summary Judgment – Timeliness

Notice of the motion and supporting papers shall be served on all other parties to the action at least 81 days before the time appointed for hearing. If the notice is served by mail, the required 81-day period of notice shall be increased by 5 days if the place of address is within the State of California, 10 days if the place of address is outside the State of California but within the United States, and 20 days if the place of address is outside the United States. If the notice is served by facsimile transmission, express mail, or another method of delivery providing for overnight delivery, the required 81-day period of notice shall be increased by two court days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (a)(2).)

iii. In Pro Per Consent to Electronic Service

A person appearing in propria persona (self-represented) in California can voluntarily waive the protection afforded by the consent requirement in California Rules of Court, rule 2.251(b) only by

affirmatively consenting to electronic service through the specific mechanisms prescribed by the rule and its enabling statute.

Specifically, a person appearing in propria persona must either: (A) serve a notice on all parties and other persons that the party accepts electronic service and file the notice with the court, which must include the electronic service address at which the party agrees to accept service; or (B) manifest affirmative consent through electronic means with the court or the court's electronic filing service provider (EFSP), and concurrently provide the party's electronic service address for the purpose of receiving electronic service, which may be manifested by serving notice of consent to all parties and either agreeing to the terms of service with an EFSP that clearly states that agreement constitutes consent to receive electronic service, or by filing Judicial Council Form EFS-005-CV (Consent to Electronic Service and Notice of Electronic Service Address). (California Rules of Court, rules 2.251(b)-(b)(1)(B)(iii), 2.251(c)(3)(A)-(B) & Advisory Committee Comment, subd. (b)(1)(B).)

b. Discussion

The Court DENIES Cross-Defendant’s motion for failure to comply with the requirements imposed on Cross-Defendant by the nexus of Code of Civil Procedure section 437c, subdivision (a)(2), and California Rules of Court, rule 2.251.

Consistent with Plaintiff’s opposition arguments, here, there exists no record before the Court to show that Plaintiff has expressly consented to electronic service as contemplated in California Rules of Court, rule 2.251.

Cross-Defendant’s reply implicitly recognizes this fact by arguing that the ‘electronic consent’ requirement in rule 2.251 should effectively be waived because Cross-Complainant (1) acknowledged notice of this motion in her opposition, (2) still filed an opposition on the merits, and (3) the parties have historically served documents electronically. (Reply, pp. 2-3.)

As far as waiver, the Court disagrees that waiver of rule 2.251 can occur. The rule is clear: among others, “self-represented parties” “are to be served by non-electronic methods unless they

affirmatively consent to electronic service,” and part of that affirmative consent must take the form of a court filing by the self-represented litigation confirming such consent (California Rules of Court, rules 2.251(b)-(b)(1)(B)(iii), 2.251(c)(3)(A)-(B)), where no such filing appears in the record for this action.

Consequently, the motion before the Court had to be served by Cross-Defendant on Cross-Complainant by other authorized means, consistent with California Rules of Court, rule (c)(3)(B). Yet, the proof of service for Cross-Defendant’s motion shows only service via email. (4/24/26 Proof of Service.) Consequently, no service in conformity with Code of Civil Procedure section 473(c), subdivision (a)(2), has taken place, and Cross-Defendant’s motion must accordingly fail.

c. Conclusion

Cross-Defendant Robert Bare dba BARE LAW’s motion for summary judgment is DENIED.

Cross-Defendant Robert Bare dba BARE LAW is ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION TO STRIKE OR TAX DEFENDANT STATE FARM GENERAL INSURANCE COMPANY'S MEMORANDUM OF COSTS Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 14, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiffs, Gevork Tashchyan and Aida Tashchyan

Opposing Party: Defendant, State Farm General Insurance Company

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

Plaintiffs Gevork Tashchyan and Aida Tashchyan (“Plaintiffs”) brought this suit alleging claims of breach of contract and breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing against Defendant State Farm General Insurance Company (“Defendant”) on the grounds that, despite insuring Plaintiffs, Defendant failed to cover a claim for burglarized items wrongfully taken from Plaintiff’s’ home.

A jury verdict was issued on January 30, 2026, with judgment entered on February 27, 2026, finding for Plaintiffs in the amount of $97,486.63 for Defendant’s breach of contract but finding for Defendant on the count of breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

Plaintiffs served a notice of judgment on March 4, 2026, via electronic service, with Defendant filing a memorandum of costs on March 13, 2026, and Plaintiffs filing a memorandum of costs on March 18, 2026.

On April 6, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a motion to strike or tax costs in Defendant’s March 13, 2026, memorandum of costs. Defendant filed a June 30, 2026, opposition, and Plaintiffs filed a July 7, 2026, reply. (Defendant separately challenged Plaintiffs’ memorandum of costs by way of a motion to strike or tax costs, which the Court denied as untimely on June 9, 2026, without prejudice.)

Plaintiffs’ motion is now before the Court.

2. Motion to Strike or Tax Costs

After review, the Court GRANTS Plaintiffs’ motion, as Defendant is not entitled to cost either pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1032 or Code of Civil Procedure section 998.

In general, the “prevailing party” is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs for suit in any action or proceeding. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (b); Santisas v. Goodin (1998) 17 Cal.4th 599, 606; Scott Co. Of Calif. v. Blount, Inc. (1999) 20 Cal.4th 1103, 1108.) Here, judgment vis-à-vis the first cause of action for breach of contract was entered in favor of Plaintiff in the amount of $97,486.63, for which reason Defendant cannot be a prevailing party pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1032, subdivision (a)(4), which defines a prevailing party as (1) the party with a net monetary recovery, (2) a defendant who is dismissed from the action, (3) a defendant where neither plaintiff nor defendant recovers anything, and (4) a defendant as against those plaintiffs who do not recover any relief against that defendant. (See Lincoln v. Schurgin (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 100, 105 [If the party does not fall within one of these four express categories, the court may exercise its discretion to award or deny costs].)

The Court also finds insufficient merit to costs based on Code of Civil Procedure section 998 offers made by Defendant during the litigation of this action. (See Opposition, pp. 4-6 [§ 998 offers of $115,000.00 and $300,000.00].) As pointed out in Plaintiffs’ reply, Plaintiffs’ collective judgment and pre-offer costs—$97,486.63 in judgment, $34,686.13 in pre-offer costs, for a total of $132,172.76—exceeds the first offer of $115,000.00. (Reply, p. 3 & Reply, Townsend Decl., ¶ 7; see Hersey v. Vopava (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 792, 798-799 [more favorable judgment calculated from adding judgment and pre-offer

costs].) Separately, our Supreme Court has held that where multiple 998 offers are made, California courts need not apply a doctrine by which the last 998 offer extinguishes a preceding offer for purposes of 998 analysis. (See Martinez v. Brownco Construction Co. (2013) 56 Cal.App.4th 1014, 1021-1024.)

Plaintiffs’ motion is thus GRANTED.

The Court STRIKES Defendant’s memorandum of costs.

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT JAJ2, LLC’S NOTICE OF MOTION AND SPECIAL MOTION TO STRIKE THE COMPLAINT OF KATHERINE D. KELLER FOR MALICIOUS PROSECUTION AND VIOLATION OF THE BANE ACT PURSUANT TO CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 425.16 Dept. 731 9:30 a.m. July 14, 2024

Moving Party: Defendant JAJ2, LLC

Opposing Party: Plaintiff, Katherine Keller

Notice: OK

Before the Court is Defendant Doe 2 JAJ2, LLC,’s anti-SLAPP motion against the Complaint’s two causes of action, which are alleged against it by way of alter ego. Plaintiff filed an opposition with a request for judicial notice, and Defendant filed a reply and evidentiary objections.

After review, and though not directly raised in the opposition, the Court DENIES Defendant’s motion for lack of proper notice and identification of claims at issue in this anti-SLAPP motion.

To meet its burden under the anti-SLAPP statute, a defendant must identify all allegations of protected activity and all claims for relief that arise from the defendant’s protected acts. (Baral v. Schnitt (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376, 384.) In a recent court of appeal opinion, the court held that a Defendant did not

meet its identification burden when the motion listed page and line numbers to strike, but those numbers simply corresponded to prayer for relief and all of complaint’s causes of action in their entirety. (Littlefield v. Littlefield (Nov. 15, 2024) 106 Cal.App.5th 815, 826.)

Here, Defendant’s notice of motion and memorandum go defectively further, neither identifying page numbers nor lines to challenge on anti-SLAPP. This is fatal because, on an anti-SLAPP motion, each claim must be identified, regardless of whether all or only some of the claims in a count—often and confusingly for anti-SLAPP purposes referred to by counsel as “cause of action,” e.g., first, second, third cause of action—are being challenged. (See Bonni v. St. Joseph Health Sys. (2021) 11 Cal.5th 995, 1010-1011 (Bonni).) Stated otherwise, following Bonni, it is clear that in ruling on an anti-SLAPP motion, the Court is analyzing each separate claim for relief within challenged counts (usually misnamed “causes of action” one, two, three, etc. in pleadings) to determine whether the alleged acts in each count are protected, not whether the essence or gravamen of the sum of the allegations in the count leans towards protected activity. (Ibid.)

Defendant JAJ2, LLC’s anti-SLAPP motion is accordingly DENIED.

Based on the dispositive result above, the Court does not address other requests or arguments before the Court, e.g., opposition request for judicial notice, reply objection to opposition memorandums’ length, first- and second-prong anti-SLAPP arguments.

Defendant JAJ2, LLC, is ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PETITIONS FOR APPROVAL OF COMPROMISE OF CLAIM FOR MINORS



  1. Background Facts

Plaintiffs sue Defendant Manual Regalado pursuant to an April 10, 2026, Complaint alleging unhabitable rental conditions from Defendant, Plaintiff’s landlord, resulting in injuries to Plaintiff, supporting seven causes of action.

On March 3, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a notice of settlement, noting a conditional settlement with an expected dismissal of June 1, 2026.

On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff Jose Luis Galindo Vargas, as parent and guardian ad litem, filed petitions for approval of the settlement as relates to minor Plaintiffs, Brianna Iris Galindo Benitez, Kimberly Jasmine Galindo Benitez, and Ashley Galindo (for ease of reference hereafter referred to by first names; no disrespect is intended). The petitions were set for hearing on May 21, 2026.

On May 21, 2026, the Court denied the petitions without prejudice based on an errata filing by Plaintiffs on May 19, 2026.

That same day, Plaintiff Jose Luis Galindo Vargas filed amended petitions for Brianna and Kimberly Galindo Benitez—though the record fails to reflect the same for Ashley Galindo.

The two amended minor Plaintiffs’ petitions are now before the Court.

 

  1. Petitions to Approve Minors’ Compromise

a.     Legal Standard on Petitions to Approve Minors’ Compromise

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 372, any compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment made for a minor or adult with a disability must be approved by the Court. (See also Prob. Code § 3600, subd. (b) [a compromise or covenant for a disputed claim or damages, money, or other property of a minor or person who lacks legal capacity is valid only after it has been approved by the superior court].) A petition for court approval of a compromise of a minor or disabled adult’s compromise or judgment of a pending action or proceeding to which this person is a party must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise, covenant, settlement, or disposition. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950; see also Cal. Rules of Court, rules 7.950.5-7.955.) The petition is generally submitted on a completed Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Minor or Person with a Disability (Judicial Council form MC-350). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) If the Court is satisfied that the compromise, covenant, settlement, or disposition is in the best interest of the person, then the Court should approve the same. (See Pearson v. Superior Court (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1333, 1338.)

b.     Discussion

A review of the petition shows that it meets all the requirements in California Rules of Court, rules 7.950 to 7.955. The record reflects that the amended petitions filed for Brianna and Kimberly:

(1) Are filed on verified Civil Form MC-350EX and contain a full disclosure of all information that bears upon the reasonableness of the compromise, covenant, settlement, or disposition (see, e.g., Petitions at §§ 10, 11, 15, 16, 17, attachs. 11b.(5), 17a, 17e);

(2) Show none of the circumstances prohibiting expedited approval of these petitions (cf. Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.5, subd. (a));

(3) Show the name, state bar number, law firm, and business address of the attorney (§ 17.b.), to-date compensation (§ 17.c.), insurer involvement (§ 17.d.), representation of other parties in this action (§ 17.e., attach. 17.e.), expected fees (§ 17.f., attach 11b.(5)), and representation terms (§ 17.a.(2), attach 17a.);

(4) Need not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 7.952 as good cause exists to dispense with the need for their personal appearance or the personal appearance of their parent/guardian ad litem;

(5) Need not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 7.953 as the Court has not ordered deposit of funds;

(6) Need not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 7.954 as no withdrawal of funds are at issue; and

(7) Show reasonable fees charged to the minors here (§ 16 [roughly 24%]) (§ 16, attach 11b.(5)).

Moreover, nothing before the Court indicates that the settlement is not in the children’s best interests, where the parents in the best position to use the remaining settlement proceeds to further the economic welfare and wellbeing of these minors. (Attach. 11b.(5).)

c.     Conclusion

Plaintiffs’ petitions are GRANTED.

Petitioner Jose Luis Galindo Vargas is ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION AND

MOTION FOR COMPLIANCE WITH

DISCOVERY PROCESS AND THE

COURT’S MARCH 24, 2026 ORDER

COMPELLING PLAINTIFF’S DEPOSITION

WITH SANCTIONS




  1. Background Facts

Plaintiffs Ricardo Campos Sanchez and Marlene Romero sue Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. (“Defendant”), and Does 1 through 10 pursuant to a March 14, 2025, Complaint alleging lemon law claims related to Plaintiffs’ purchase of a 2020 Honda Insight manufactured and/or distributed by Defendant.

Before the Court is Defendant’s un-opposed motion to compel compliance with this Court’s March 24, 2026, order imposing monetary sanctions on Plaintiff Ricardo Sanchez Campos, which Defendant argues have not been paid to date. Defendant has filed a notice of non-opposition noting the absence of opposition from Plaintiffs.

///

///

  1. Motion to Compel Compliance with Court Order

The Court finds in favor of Defendant in part and in favor of Plaintiff Ricardo Sanchez Campos in part.

The Court finds merit in Defendant’s request for an order directing Plaintiff Ricardo Sanchez Campos to comply with the Court’s March 24, 2026, order, which ruled that several discovery motions directed at this plaintiff were moot but nevertheless imposed monetary sanctions on him in the amount of $2,000.00, to be paid directly to Defendant’s counsel within 30 days. (Motion, pp. 4-5; see 3/24/26 Minutes, p. 1.) Every California court has the inherent and statutory authority to provide for the orderly conduct of proceedings before it and to control its process and orders so as to make them conform to law and justice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128, subds. (a)(3), (a)(8).) Here, conforming the facts before the Court to justice necessitates ordering Plaintiff Richard Sanchez Campos to comply with this Court’s March 24th order. Defendant’s motion is thus GRANTED as to this requested relief.

However, Defendant’s motion is DENIED as to the imposition of either further monetary sanctions or terminating sanctions. As for monetary sanctions, such sanctions orders are enforceable as money judgments unless the court orders otherwise; instead, the remedy to enforce payment of monetary sanctions is to obtain and levy a writ of execution on assets of the debtor. (Newland v. Superior Court (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 608, 615.) Sanctions cannot be imposed for failure to pay previously ordered sanctions. (Ibid.) As for terminating sanctions, such remedy is disproportionate to the violation here, which is a mere failure to pay money rather than a violation substantively affecting Defendant’s ability to develop its denials and defenses.

a.     Conclusion

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s motion is thus:

(1) GRANTED as to ordering Plaintiff to remit sanctions previously ordered by this Court, which the Court ORDERS Plaintiff to remit within fourteen (14) days of service this order; and

(2) DENIED as to monetary and terminating sanctions.

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. is ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO COMPEL SANCTIONS PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 871.26

  1. Background Facts

Plaintiff, who brings this action seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle, brought this motion seeking an order that compels Defendants to comply with initial disclosure obligations and granting sanctions against Defendant pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26.

Defendant has filed an opposition, which argues that this motion has been mooted by since-made disclosures, and that sanctions should be denied because, according to Defendant, the parties were working on settlement and put a pause on initial disclosures when Plaintiff filed this motion.

Because no reply appears in the record, there are no papers from Plaintiff to dispute Defendant’s mootness and good cause arguments.

///

///

///

  1. Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures; Request for Sanctions

Plaintiff’s motion seeks disclosures and sanctions from Defendant for failure to comply with its initial disclosure requirements under section 871.26.

After review, the Court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion in full.

Initial-disclosure procedures exist for specific causes of action, including procedures described in Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivisions (b) and (f) to (h) for civil actions seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.20, et seq.

Regarding disclosures, Defendant has provided evidence of disclosures, the extent and nature of which are not addressed here. (See Opposition, Sweeney Decl., ¶¶ 4-13, Exs. A-B.) Separately, Plaintiff has failed to file a reply, for which reason the Court can accept that the issue of whether Defendant has complied with its initial disclosure obligations is moot.

As for sanctions, though Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (j), mandates that this Court impose sanctions for a party’s failure to make initial disclosures, those sanctions can be withheld where the non-producing party shows good cause. Here, Defendant argues that the parties were engaged in settlement negotiations, for which reason Defendant was under the impression that disclosures were paused, and once Defendant received notice of motion, it made disclosures and thus brought itself into compliance with the Code. (Opposition, Sweeney Decl., ¶¶ 4-13, Exs. A-B.) Again, no reply exists to dispute this position. Sanctions consequently fail under a showing of good cause.

Plaintiffs’ motion is accordingly DENIED.

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO DISMISS PLAINTIFF’S COMPLAINT, OR IN THE ALTERNATIVE STAY THE ACTION BASED ON FORUM NON CONVENIENS Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 14, 2026

Moving Party: Defendant Taleo Capital, LP

Opposing Party: Plaintiff, Andry Holmes

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

Plaintiff Andry Holmes sues Defendants Teleo Capital, LP (“Teleo Capital”), Teesnap LLC (“Teesnap”), David Casares, and Does 1 to 50 pursuant to a July 31, 2025, Complaint alleging various Fair Employment and Housing Act, Unfair Competition, and common law claims arising from allegations of Plaintiff’s employment with Defendants, which began remotely in California in April 2024 but later required relocation to Nevada in October 2024, where Defendants unlawfully terminated employment in January 2025 following incidents of sexual harassment against Plaintiff that she reported to Defendants.

Before the Court is Defendant Teleo Capital’s opposed motion to dismiss or stay this action based on the doctrine of forum non conveniens.

///

///

2. Motion to Dismiss or Stay Based on Forum Non Conveniens

a. Forum Non Conveniens Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 410.30, subdivision (a) provides that “[w]hen a court upon motion of a party or its own motion finds that in the interest of substantial justice an action should be heard in a forum outside this state, the court shall stay or dismiss the action in whole or in part on any conditions that may be just.” “In applying the traditional forum non conveniens analysis, the trial court must engage in a two-step process, on which the defendant bears the burden of proof. [Citation.] In the first step, the court must determine whether a suitable alternative forum exists. [Citation.] If the court finds that a suitable alternative forum exists, it must then balance the private interests of the litigants and the interests of the public in retaining the action in California. [Citation.]” (Animal Film, LLC v. D.E.J. Prods., Inc. (2011) 193 Cal.App.4th 466, 471, 472 (“Animal Film”); see National Football League v. Fireman’s Fund Ins. Co. (2013) 216 Cal.App.4th 902, 926 (National Football League) [The party seeking a dismissal due to an inconvenient forum bears the burden of proof].)

An action brought by a California resident specifically may not be dismissed on grounds of forum non conveniens except in extraordinary circumstances. (Century Indemnity Co. v. Bank of America (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 408, 411 (Century Indemnity).) In those circumstances, a trial court has more discretion to grant a stay because California would retain jurisdiction. (Id. at p. 412.)

Courts disagree on what level of deference should be attributed to the plaintiff’s choice of forum where the plaintiff is a resident of another state. Most courts have held that when a plaintiff is from another state, the plaintiff’s choice of forum should be given substantial deference as with a California resident and should not be disturbed unless the balance of private and public factors strongly favors the defendant. (See, e.g., Morris v. AGFA Corp. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1452, 1465 & fn. 6 (Morris); Ford Motor Co. v. Insurance Co. of N. Am. (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 604, 610-611; cf. Lueck v. Sundstrand Corp. (9th Cir. 2001) 236 F.3d 1137, 1143 [in interpreting forum non conveniens under federal common law, determining that when the plaintiff is a U.S. citizen, the defendant must satisfy heavy burden of proof].)

One court, however, has held that when a plaintiff is from another state, the plaintiff’s choice of forum should be given due deference but not a strong presumption. (National Football League, supra, 216, Cal.App.4th at p. 929.)

The decision to grant a forum non conveniens motion is discretionary and is accorded substantial deference on appeal. (Century Indemnity, supra, 58 Cal.App.4th at p. 411.)

b. Discussion

The Court finds in favor of Plaintiff.

i. Suitable Alternative Forum

Here, an alternative forum exists in Nevada for reasons advanced by Defendant’s moving papers, i.e., Teesnap being headquartered and operating in Nevada, alleged conduct occurring in Nevada, Defendants being amenable to jurisdiction in Nevada, Nevada’s recognition of the harms and claims alleged in this suit, and no applicable statute of limitations. (Motion, p. 2; see Guimei v. General Electric Co. (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 689, 694 [defendants subject to jurisdiction in alternative forum, and no limitations bar]; Animal Film, supra, 193 Cal.App.4th at p. 472 [suitable alternative forum where action can be brought even if not necessarily won].)

ii. Balancing of Interests

However, a balancing of interests supports keeping litigation in California.

“The private interest facts are those that make trial and the enforceability of the ensuring judgment expeditious and relatively inexpensive, such as the ease of access to sources of proof, the cost of obtaining attendance of witnesses, and the availability of compulsory process for attendance of unwilling witnesses. The public interest facts include avoidance of overburdening local courts with congested calendars, protecting the interest of potential jurors so that they are not called upon to decide cases in which the local community has little concern, and the weighing of competing interests of California and the alternative jurisdiction in the litigation.” (Morris v. AGFA Corp. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1452, 1463-1464.) The court also considers whether the rights of the parties can best be determined by

the court of the other jurisdiction because of the nature of the subject matter and the stage to which the proceedings in the other court have already advanced. (Century Indemnity, supra, 58 Cal.App.4th at p. 412.)

Strictly for purposes of ruling on this motion alone, the Court rejects the argument that the parties’ alleged arbitration agreement requires arbitration of any dispute in Nevada. This is a disputed issue before the Court, with a motion to compel arbitration set for hearing on September 29, 2026, and with Plaintiff’s opposition papers arguing that the arbitration agreement is not enforceable as a matter of law. (See, e.g., Opposition, pp. 4-6.)

Moving to the balancing of factors, private interests—ease of access to sources of proof, the cost of obtaining attendance of witnesses, and the availability of compulsory process for attendance of unwilling witnesses—support keeping the litigation in California. The parties dispute whether material witnesses mostly reside in California or Nevada. However, a motion to quash by Defendants was denied by the Court on January 20, 2026, for which reason Defendant Teleo Capital is presently under jurisdiction of this Court and party-affiliated witnesses would need to be produced and could be produced remotely via videoconferencing. (Compare Motion, pp. 4-5, with Opposition, pp.8-10.) Moreover, in the online context, costs related to depositions should not appreciably differ. Separately, the Court finds merit in the opposition’s argument that if the evidence at issue here is electronic, the electronic nature of discovery will minimize the burden on the parties, a position which the reply does not dispute, merely arguing that the position is neutral because it cuts both ways. (Opposition, p. 10; Reply, p. 5.)

As far as public factors—avoidance of overburdening local courts, protecting potential jurors from cases in which the local community has little concern, and the weighing of competing interests between possible forums—those factors also support keeping this action in California. The Complaint alleges that, among other things, a six-month employee was relocated to Nevada where she was subjected to sexual harassment for three months before being terminated following complaints relating to that harassment. (Complaint, ¶¶ 12, 14.) In turn the opposition clarifies that Plaintiff was a remote, California-based

employee for six months (April to October 2025), commuted from California to Nevada for 1.5 months (October to November 2024), and moved to Nevada in December 2024, where she lived when she was terminated in January 2025. (Opposition, Holmes Decl., ¶¶ 4-12.) This timeline is not disputed in the moving papers or reply and leads to the conclusion that, whatever competing interests California and Nevada may have in the litigation (see Motion, pp. 7-8), Californianians have a strong interest in this action as a result of the inducement of employment of a California resident, who was for the most part employed remotely in California throughout her employment, and was forced to relocate to another state, where she was allegedly sexually harassed by Defendants.

Finally, because there is no active sister action in Nevada, there is no basis to determine that Nevada can at this time better resolve the present dispute because, for example, it is at a more advanced stage of proceedings, as contemplated in Century Indemnity.

Defendant’s motion accordingly fails. (Century Indemnity, supra, 58 Cal.App.4th at p. 411 [An action brought by a California resident specifically may not be dismissed on grounds of forum non conveniens except in extraordinary circumstances].)

c. Conclusion

Defendant Teleo Capital, LLC’s motion is DENIED.

Defendant Teleo Capital, LLC is ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT’S MOTION FOR SANCTIONS UNDER CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE SECTIONS 128.5 AND 128.7 Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 14, 2026

Moving Party: Defendant, Kay Properties & Investments LLC

Opposing Party: Plaintiff, Brent Wilson

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

Plaintiff Brent Wilson sues Defendant Kay Properties & Investments LLC and Does 1 to 10 pursuant to a November 17, 2025, Complaint, alleging statutory and common law claims relating to Plaintiff’s since-terminated employment with Defendant. Before the Court is Defendant’s opposed motion for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5 and 128.7.

2. Motion for Sanctions – Code Civ. Proc., §§ 128.5, 128.7

a. Safe Harbor Period

Unlike most motions, motions for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 128.5 and 128.7 include a safe-harbor period between the time the motion is served and the time it is filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 128.5, subd. (f)(1)(B), (C), 128.7, subd. (c)(1), (2).) Here, Defendant presents a sufficient

basis for the Court to conclude that this motion was served 21 or more days prior to being filed with the Court. (Motion, Scheithauer Decl., ¶¶ 2-5, 18, Ex. A.)

b. Legal Standard on Motion for Sanctions

California law permits a party to file (1) motions based on actions or tactics that are made in bad faith and are frivolous or solely intended to cause unnecessary delay (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5) and (2) motions based on the presentation of frivolous papers or papers brought for an improper purpose (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7). California courts have broad discretion to impose these sanctions even where statutory predicates are met, keeping in mind that the sanctions statute must not be construed so as to conflict with the primary duty of an attorney to represent his or her client zealously, where forceful representation often requires that an attorney attempt to read a case or an agreement in an innovative though sensible way. (Peake v. Underwood (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 428, 441 (Peake) [in § 128.7 context].)

c. Discussion

The Court finds in favor of Plaintiff but denies monetary sanctions for either party.

Here, Defendant’s argument under both statutory sections stems from a uniform idea: Plaintiff violated sections 128.5 and 128.7 by bringing this action after having signed a release at termination covering the claims alleged in this action, and by falsely alleging in his Complaint at paragraph 25 that he “refused” Kay Properties’ severance offer, as contradicted by Defendant’s evidence. (Motion, p. 11; see Motion, Scheithauer Decl., ¶ 2, Ex. A [safe harbor letter] & Sub-Ex. A [release].)

However, the Court finds that neither bad faith, frivolous papers, improper purpose, nor an intent to cause unnecessary delay appears here. Simply, the parties’ papers show a colorable dispute relating to whether the November 30, 2023, release is enforceable as to bar Plaintiff’s prosecution of claims in this action, with Plaintiff arguing that the release is not enforceable because it is unlawful on its terms and lacks consideration for contract formation purposes. (Opposition, pp. 9-12.) A simple amendment to the operative pleadings should be sufficient to update the allegations to confirm with Plaintiff’s position here.

Moreover, and specifically relating to bad faith under section 128.5, in 2017, the Legislature amended section 128.5 to make clear that bad faith is measured subjectively. (See Sen. Rules Com., Off. of Sen. Floor Analyses, 3d reading of Assem. Bill No. 984 (2017–2018 Reg. Sess.) as amended July 12, 2017, p. 1.) Here, the Opposition shows a lack of subjective bad faith in the filing of the Complaint in this action.

And even if Defendant had met the elements for a motion for sanctions, the Court would be disinclined to grant sanctions here. The purpose of sanctions is to deter improper conduct and to encourage compliance with court orders and rules. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 128.5, subd. (f)(2) [sanctions limited to what is sufficient to deter repetition of action or tactic], 128.7, subd. (d) [limited to what is sufficient to deter repetition of conduct].) Here, to impose sanctions on a party making a colorable argument for voiding of a release would accomplish less to deter improper conduct and encourage compliance with court orders or rules than to chill Plaintiff’s right to file suit and interfere with zealous representation as contemplated in Peake.

Last, monetary sanctions as requested by Defendant and Plaintiff in their papers are DENIED. Defendant’s motion was denied, and fees in favor of Plaintiff would not be appropriate in light of the good faith dispute presented by the parties’ papers, which negates bad faith, improper purpose, and an intent to create unnecessary delay by way of Defendant having filed its motion.

d. Conclusion

Defendant Kay Properties & Investments LLC’s motion for sanctions is DENIED.

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED.
