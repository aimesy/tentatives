1. Contact the opposing party and all other parties who have appeared in the action and confirm that each will submit on the tentative ruling.

3. Serve notice of the Court's ruling on all parties entitled to receive service.

If this procedure is followed, when the case is called the Court will enter its ruling on the motion in accordance with its tentative ruling. If any party declines to submit on the tentative ruling, then no telephone call is necessary, and all parties should appear at the hearing. If there is neither a telephone call nor an appearance, then the matter may either be taken off calendar or ruled on.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO ENFORCE SETTLEMENT

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: June 30, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 06/03/2022

DISPOSED: 06/06/2025

 

MOVING PARTY:              Plaintiff Lin Wang

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant Chunming Li

1.   Background

This is an action for failure to pay wages. Plaintiff Lin Wang (“Plaintiff”) alleged that she was employed by defendant Chunming Li (“Defendant”). Plaintiff alleged that Defendant required Plaintiff to work in excess of eight hours a day and forty hours a week but failed to pay wages for excess hours worked. Plaintiff also alleged that Defendant failed to pay Plaintiff a commission included in her compensation for her last month of work. Plaintiff alleged that Defendant unlawfully terminated Plaintiff for using a sick day.

On June 5, 2025, the action was dismissed without prejudice and with the court retaining jurisdiction to enforce the parties’ settlement agreement pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6.

 

2.   Discussion

Plaintiff now moves to enforce the settlement agreement. Plaintiff contends that the parties settled for $40,000, but that Defendant has only made payments in the total amount of $13,200, leaving a remaining amount due of $26,800, and has defaulted on the agreement. In opposition, Defendant contends that he has made payments totaling $20,800. Defendant does not otherwise oppose the motion.

 

Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6 provides: “If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If requested by the parties, the court may retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.”

 

Strict compliance with the statutory requirements is necessary before a court can enforce a settlement agreement under this statute.¿ (Sully-Miller Contracting Co. v. Gledson/Cashman Construction, Inc. (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 30, 37.)¿ To enforce a written settlement agreement under Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6, the following three elements must be met: (1) the parties must have come to a meeting of the minds on all material points; (2) there must be a writing that contains the material terms of the agreement; and (3) the writing must be signed by the parties, an attorney who represents the party, or an agent authorized by an insurer to sign on the party’s behalf.¿ (Weddington Productions, Inc. v. Flick (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 793, 797-98; Code Civ. Proc. § 664.6.) On a motion to enforce a settlement agreement, the court must determine whether the settlement agreement is valid and binding and assess whether the material terms of the settlement were reasonably well defined and certain and whether the parties expressly acknowledged that they understood and agreed to be bound by those terms. (Estate of Jones (2022) 82 Cal.App.5th 948, 952.)

 

Plaintiff has provided a copy of the written accepted offer to compromise between Plaintiff and Defendant, signed by both parties. (Hsu Decl., Ex. A.) There is no indication that the parties did not come to a meeting of the minds on all material points. This is sufficient to satisfy the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6.

 

The settlement agreement states in pertinent part:

“2. Settlement Payment. In exchange for Plaintiffs execution and performance of this Agreement, Defendants will pay Plaintiff the total sum of $40,000.00 as a full and final settlement between the Parties (the "Settlement Payment"), which shall be subject to the issuance of 1099 MISC Form(s).

…

2.1.1. First check in the amount of $7,000.00 will be delivered within thirty (30) days of the completion of both of the following events: (1) receipt of the fully executed original Agreement by Defendants' counsel, James Howard, Esq.; and (2) the expiration of the Effective Date as set forth below. 2.1.2. Followed by subsequent second, third, fourth, fifth, and sixth checks in the amount of $6,600.00 delivered by the 1st calendar day of each month, beginning on July 1, 2025 through November 1, 2025.”

(Hsu Decl., Ex. A.)

 

The settlement agreement requires Defendant to pay Plaintiff a total of $40,000 in monthly installments between July 1, 2025 and November 1, 2025. Attorney Ray Hsu states that his office received two payments of $6,600 on October 16, 2025 and December 2025, and that he has not received any other settlement payments from Defendant. (Hsu Decl. ¶¶ 10-12.)

 

Defendant contends that he has made payments totaling $20,800. However, in support of this contention, Defendant provides only three unauthenticated images of receipts for wire transfers and withdrawals of money. A writing must be authenticated to be admitted into evidence. (Evid. Code § 1401, subd. (a).) Defendant has therefore provided no admissible evidence as to total sum of money that he contends he has paid.

 

As Defendant is obligated to pay a total of $40,000 in monthly installments under the terms of the settlement agreement, which he has not done as of the filing of the motion, and the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6 have been satisfied, the court grants Plaintiff’s motion to enforce the settlement agreement.

 

3.   Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court GRANTS Plaintiff’s motion to enforce the settlement agreement against Defendant. The court will enter judgment in favor of Plaintiff in the amount of $26,800.00. Plaintiff is ordered to submit a proposed judgment on form JUD-100 within ten (10) days of the date of entry of this order.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Hon. Elizabeth L. Bradley

Judge of the Superior Court

[PROPOSED] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT AIR DISTRIBUTION USA, INC’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH DEPOSITION SUBPOENA

 

[PROPOSED] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF ROSE ML INC.’S MOTION FOR LEAVE TO CONDUCT ANTI-SLAPP DISCOVERY

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: June 30, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 08/30/2023

4th AMENDED COMPLAINT FILED: 04/20/2026

CROSS-COMPLAINT FILED: 09/19/2023

2nd AMENDED CROSS-COMPLAINT FILED: 04/08/2026

1.     Background

This is an action arising from an alleged breach of contract. The complaint alleges that defendants Air Distribution USA, Inc. and Robert Maneson (collectively, “Defendants”) entered into an agreement with plaintiffs Rose ML, Inc. (“Rose”) and Shisha Aroma, Inc. (“Shisha Aroma”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) not to sell certain products except to certain persons or under certain circumstances, and that Defendants breached this agreement.

Defendant Air Distribution USA, Inc. (“Air Distribution”) filed a cross-complaint against Rose and cross-defendants Sal Haddadin; Alfa Distribution, Inc. (“Alfa”); and Corona Wholesale, Inc. (“Corona”) (collectively, “Cross-Defendants”). In the cross-complaint, Air Distribution alleges that Alfa and Corona are alter egos of Haddadin. Air Distribution alleges that Rose and Haddadin failed to pay for $3,158,685.65 worth of tobacco products delivered to them by Air Distribution.

A. Motion to Compel Compliance with Discovery Subpoena

2.     Discussion

Air Distribution moves to compel third party T & M Automotive, Inc.’s (“T&M”) compliance with a deposition subpoena. Air Distribution contends that it noticed T&M’s Person Most Knowledgeable (“PMK”) deposition for April 15, 2026 but that T&M neither objected nor produced its PMK for deposition. In opposition, Cross-Defendants do not contend that T&M’s PMK’s anticipated testimony is relevant to the claims and defenses asserted in Plaintiff’s current operative pleading which is the subject of the pending anti-SLAPP motion. 

Instead, Plaintiff contends that the motion should be denied because pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16, subd. (g), all discovery proceedings in the action shall be stayed, including with respect to the operative cross-complaint and including T&M’s PMK deposition, and that it would be unjust to require Plaintiff to seek leave to conduct discovery by way of noticed motion, but to permit Cross-Complainant leave to freely conduct discovery in connection with the claims asserted in the operative cross-complaint.  Plaintiff further asserts that Code of Civ. Proc. § 425.16 is to be construed broadly, including subdivision (g), and that no published case provides that the stay on discovery does not extend to a cross-complaint where the anti-SLAPP motion is directed to Plaintiff’s complaint.

After an anti-SLAPP motion has been filed in an action, discovery is automatically stayed as to that action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (g).) However, a cross-complaint is considered to be a separate action from the complaint. (Trinity Risk Management, LLC v. Simplified Labor Staffing Solutions, Inc. (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 995, 1004; Ohio Casualty Insurance Group v. Superior Court (1995) 30 Cal.App.4th 444, 448-449.)

The Legislature enacted section 425.16 to prevent and deter “lawsuits [referred to as SLAPP's] brought primarily to chill the valid exercise of the constitutional rights of freedom of speech and petition for the redress of grievances.” (Varian Med. Sys., Inc. v. Delfino (2005) 35 Cal. 4th 180, 192 (“Varian”), citing § 425.16, subd. (a).) Because these meritless lawsuits seek to deplete “the defendant's energy” and drain “his or her resources” the Legislature sought “ ‘to prevent SLAPPs by ending them early and without great cost to the SLAPP target’ ” (Id., citing Simmons v. Allstate Ins. Co. (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 1068, 1074, Equilon Enterprises v. Consumer Cause, Inc. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 53, 65). Section 425.16 therefore establishes a procedure where the trial court evaluates the merits of the lawsuit using a summary judgment-like procedure at an early stage of the litigation. In doing so, section 425.16 seeks to limit the costs of defending against such a lawsuit. (Id., citing Equilon Enterprises, at p. 65, [noting that the “short time frame for anti-SLAPP filings and hearings” and the “stay of discovery” pending resolution of the motion evidences the Legislature's intent to minimize the litigation costs of SLAPP targets].)

Plaintiff contends the stay on appeal of an order made under § 425.16 supports its position.  The court disagrees.  Under Code of Civil Procedure § 916, “the trial court is divested of” subject matter jurisdiction over any matter embraced in or affected by the appeal during the pendency of that appeal.  (Varian, supra, p. 196-197, emphasis added.)  While it is true that § 916 stays all further proceedings on the merits during the pendency of an appeal from the denial of an anti-SLAPP motion, such an appeal does not stay proceedings relating to causes of action not affected by the motion.  (Varian, supra, 35 Cal. 4th at 194, fn. 8, emphasis added.)  In Varian, defendants’ anti-SLAPP motions encompassed all of plaintiffs’ causes of action.  As such, all of the matters on trial were embraced in and affected by defendants’ appeal from the denial of the motion, and the trial court lacked subject matter jurisdiction “over these matters.”  (Id., at 88-189, 200.)

Plaintiff’s reliance on Britts v. Superior Court (2006) is misplaced. At issue there was whether the stay on “all discovery proceedings” under § 425.16, subd. (g) extends to discovery motions generally, and specifically to discovery motions filed prior to and pending when the anti-SLAPP motion was filed.  The court concluded the stay on all discovery proceedings includes discovery motions generally, and also includes discovery motions pending when the anti-SLAPP motion was filed.  There was no cross-complaint at issue in Britts, as is at issue here.

Our Supreme Court’s holding in Varian is consistent with this court’s interpretation of the stay on discovery required by § 425.16 prior to appeal. Air Distribution’s notice of deposition, provided as Exhibit 2 to the declaration of attorney Kina Wong, indicates that the deposition was sought in connection with the allegations in the Second Amended Cross-Complaint (“SACC”)), as the deposition topics concern transfers of money or property between Cross-Defendants and T&M. Attorney Wong declares that this issue arose because of discovery into Haddadin’s bank records, which is at issue in the SACC but not in the Fourth Amended Complaint. (Wong Decl. ¶¶ 2-3.)

Plaintiff does not contend that T&M’s PMK deposition relates to allegations in or defenses to matters alleged in the Fourth Amended Complaint which is the subject of the anti-SLAPP motion.  Instead, Plaintiff merely objects on procedural grounds, contending that the court issued a stay on discovery on May 19, 2026, or determined that there was a “blanket” stay on discovery in effect as a result of the filing of the anti-SLAPP motion.  However, the May 19, 2026 minute order on Motion to Deem Request for Admissions Admitted and on Ex Parte Application for order continuing hearing on pending discovery-related matters contains no such order either imposing a “blanket” stay or determining that a “blanket” stay is in effect precluding discovery on issues limited in scope to the claims asserted in the SACC.  The court’s order made no findings of fact or conclusions of law with respect to the scope of the automatic stay imposed pursuant to § 425.16 in the May 19, 2026 order.  Instead, the order merely granted Rose ML’s request to continue the hearing on the anti-SLAPP motion as well as the hearings on the then-pending discovery motions to dates after the new hearing on the anti-SLAPP motion. 

This court’s subsequent order denying Air Distribution’s June 12, 2026 Ex Parte Application for an Order Advancing the Hearing Date on Air Distribution and Maneson’s Motion for Clarification Regarding Anti-SLAPP Discovery Stay itself indicates that the court had not decided the scope of any stay.  Though the court denied the application for lack of any emergency warranting ex parte relief, the denial was clearly not on the merits as the court indicated that arguments regarding the scope of any stay could be advanced in connection with the pending noticed discovery motions on calendar such as the instant motion.

Based on the foregoing, the court finds that the discovery at issue in this motion is not automatically stayed by the filing of an anti-SLAPP motion as to the Fourth Amended Complaint, because it does not pertain to the Fourth Amended Complaint and instead relates only to the SACC.

Code of Civil Procedure §1987.1 provides that “[i]f a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court's own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare.”

On March 10, 2026, Air Distribution noticed T&M’s PMK deposition for April 15, 2026. (Wong Decl. ¶¶ 4-5.) T&M did not object to this deposition. (Wong Decl. ¶ 6.) T&M failed to produce its PMK for this deposition. (Wong Decl. ¶ 10.) The motion is reasonably made. Air Distribution is therefore entitled to an order compelling T&M’s compliance with Air Distribution’s deposition subpoena, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure §1987.1, subd. (a).

Although Cross-Defendants opposed the instant motion, they did not oppose the motion on substantive grounds. As the court has determined that the motion is procedurally proper, the court will grant Air Distribution’s motion to compel T&M’s compliance with Air Distribution’s deposition subpoena.

3.     Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court GRANTS Cross-Complainant Air Distribution USA, Inc.’s motion to compel third party T & M Automotive, Inc’s compliance with Air Distribution’s deposition subpoena.  The deposition shall take place on ________, 2026, at ___________ at the location specified in the notice of deposition.  The deponent shall appear in person. 

To the extent this order resolves all issues raised in the pending Motion for Clarification of Anti-SLAPP Discovery Stay scheduled to be heard on July 17, 2026, the court once again urges moving party to consider withdrawal of that motion.

B. Motion for Leave to Conduct Anti-SLAPP Discovery

2.     Discussion

Plaintiffs move for leave to conduct discovery irrespective of the anti-SLAPP stay on discovery on the complaint. Plaintiffs contend that, in order to adequately oppose Defendants’ anti-SLAPP motion, they require discovery into the letter that Defendants sent to third-party customers, including which customers received the letter, other communications with those customers, and internal communications regarding drafting of the letter. In opposition, Defendants contend that Plaintiffs have failed to establish good cause for the requested discovery and that Plaintiffs have already had a substantial opportunity to obtain the requested documents through ordinary discovery procedures.

“All discovery proceedings in the action shall be stayed upon the filing of a notice of [an anti-SLAPP motion]. The stay of discovery shall remain in effect until notice of entry of the order ruling on the motion. The court, on noticed motion and for good cause shown, may order that specified discovery be conducted notwithstanding this subdivision.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (g).)

A plaintiff must show “good cause” before taking any discovery after the filing of an anti-SLAPP motion. (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (g).) “Good cause” requires a showing that the requested discovery is necessary for the plaintiff to oppose the anti-SLAPP motion and is narrowly tailored to that end. (Balla v. Hall (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 652, 692.) Discovery that is not relevant to a legal defense being asserted by the defendant in the anti-SLAPP motion is not permitted. (Blanchard v. DIRECTV, Inc. (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 903, 922.) A plaintiff must demonstrate the facts they expect to uncover in their discovery that would enable them to oppose the anti-SLAPP motion. (Ibid.)¿

 Plaintiffs request discovery into the letter that Defendants sent to Plaintiffs’ customers; the identity of the customers who received the letter; all communications and related documents with those customers; internal communications about the letter and drafts of the letter; depositions of individuals with knowledge of the letter; and depositions of customers. Plaintiffs cite to Lafayette Morehouse, Inc. v. Chronicle Publishing Co. (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 855 for the proposition that “[i]f the plaintiff makes a timely and proper showing in response to the motion to strike, that a defendant or witness possesses evidence needed by plaintiff to establish a prima facie case, the plaintiff must be given the reasonable opportunity to obtain that evidence through discovery before the motion to strike is adjudicated.” (Id. at p. 868.)

Plaintiffs do not state what facts they anticipate obtaining from the requested discovery. Furthermore, Plaintiffs appear to already be in possession of the letter, as in their reply, they make arguments that the statements in the letter are not privileged while quoting the letter itself. Therefore, discovery into the contents of the letter does not appear to be at issue. Plaintiffs do not establish what good cause there is for the remaining requested discovery, or what facts Plaintiffs seek to discover that would permit them to oppose the anti-SLAPP motion.

Moreover, even if Plaintiffs had established good cause, Plaintiffs have already had substantial opportunity to conduct discovery on this matter. This action has been ongoing for nearly three years. On August 28, 2025, Rose filed a motion to compel further discovery from Air Distribution as to its Requests for Production Set One, which included requests for all communications mentioning either Rose or Shisha Aroma between Air Distribution and any other person, as well as all communications sent from Air Distribution to any of Rose or Shisha Aroma’s customers. On September 29, 2025, the court granted this motion. Air Distribution provided supplemental responses on October 29, 2025. (Higgins Decl. ¶ 4.)

Rose subsequently did not file a motion to compel further responses on Air Distribution’s supplemental responses. (Higgins Decl. ¶ 4.) Notice of such a motion must be filed within 45 days of service of the verified discovery response or supplemental discovery response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).) A party may not avoid this deadline by filing a second set of duplicative discovery responses and moving to compel on the second set. (Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1408-1409, citing Professional Career Colleges, Magna Institute, Inc. v. Superior Court (1989) 207 Cal.App.3d 490.) Therefore, Plaintiffs have already had a reasonable opportunity to conduct discovery on this matter and have, seemingly, failed to obtain the necessary discovery.

The court will therefore deny Plaintiffs’ motion for leave to conduct discovery pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16, subd. (g).

3.     Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court DENIES Plaintiffs’ motion for leave to conduct discovery pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16, subd. (g).

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 24CMCV00281    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: A

Defendant Avalon Villa Healthcare, LLC moves to compel Plaintiff’s claims to arbitration. Defendant contends that Plaintiff executed a voluntary arbitration agreement upon admission to Defendant’s medical facility. Plaintiff does not oppose this motion.

 

Defendant has attached a copy of the arbitration agreement between Plaintiff and Defendant, signed by Plaintiff and by a facility representative. The agreement states that “any dispute between Resident… and [Defendant], and their its owners, investors, operators, officers, directors, administrators, staff, employees, agents, and any management and administrative services company and all related entities and individuals… including any action for injury or death arising from negligence, torts, intentional tort and/or statutory causes of action (including all California Welfare and Institutions Code sections, all California Business and Professions Code sections, Health and Safety Code section 1430), and all other statutory claims under any applicable California code, will be determined by submission to binding arbitration and not by lawsuit or resort to court process except as California law provides for judicial review of arbitration proceedings… By signing this agreement, the parties waive their right to commence and/or be a party to any class or collective action in any court against the other party relating in any manner whatsoever to the Resident's residency at the Facility.” (Chevez Decl., Ex. A.) Based on the court’s review of the complaint, it is apparent that the instant action arises out of or relates to Plaintiff’s residency at the facility. Therefore, the arbitration provision applies to all parties in this action.

 

Under Gamboa and Iyere, attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement to the motion constitutes setting forth the agreement’s provisions in the motion, which is sufficient to fulfill Defendant’s initial burden of proving the existence of an arbitration agreement between the parties. The burden now shifts to Plaintiff to establish any fact necessary to her opposition. As Plaintiff has not opposed this motion, Plaintiff has failed to carry her burden.

 

The court will therefore grant Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s claims to arbitration.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANTS LANDON WHITNEY AND KIMBERLY WHITNEY’S MOTION FOR DETERMINATION OF GOOD FAITH SETTLEMENT

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: June 30, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 07/17/2024

1st AMENDED COMPLAINT FILED: 02/13/2026

TRIAL: 11/16/2026

 

MOVING PARTY:              Defendants Landon Whitney and Kimberly Whitney

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant Sami Khatib; Urban Worldwide LA; and Radii 360, Inc.

1.   Background

This is a motor vehicle collision action. Plaintiff Stacy Dillow (“Plaintiff”) alleges that on July 19, 2022, she was a passenger in a vehicle being driven by defendant Sami Khatib. Plaintiff alleges that defendant Landon Whitney was driving behind Defendant Khatib’s vehicle and caused a rear-end collision with Defendant Khatib’s vehicle. Plaintiff alleges that defendants Landon Whitney; Kimberly Whitney; Sami Khatib; Urban Worldwide LA; and Radii 360, Inc. dba Opulux Car Service negligently operated, serviced, repaired, maintained, entrusted and/or inspected their vehicles so as to cause Plaintiff personal injury.

2.   Discussion

Defendants Landon Whitney and Kimberly Whitney (collectively, “Settling Defendants”) move for determination of a good faith settlement between Settling Defendants and Plaintiff. Settling Defendants contend that they have entered into a settlement agreement with Plaintiff wherein Settling Defendants will pay Plaintiff $100,000 in exchange for dismissal of Plaintiff’s claims against Settling Defendants. In opposition, defendants Sami Khatib; Urban Worldwide LA; and Radii 360, Inc. dba Opulux Car Service (collectively, “Respondents”) contend that the settlement amount is not roughly proportionate with Settling Defendants’ liability, particularly considering the existing cross-complaints against Settling Defendants.

A plaintiff may settle with one of several joint tortfeasors or co-obligors on a contract without releasing the others. Provided it is in “good faith,” the settlement discharges the settling defendant from liability to the other defendants for equitable contribution or comparative indemnity but¿not¿for contractual indemnity. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 877, 877.6.)

“Good faith” does not have a precise definition, but may be determined by taking into account a number of factors, including “a rough approximation of plaintiffs' total recovery and the settlor's proportionate liability, the amount paid in settlement, the allocation of settlement proceeds among plaintiffs, and a recognition that a settlor should pay less in settlement than he would if he were found liable after a trial. Other relevant considerations include the financial conditions and insurance policy limits of settling defendants, as well as the existence of collusion, fraud, or tortious conduct aimed to injure the interests of nonsettling defendants.” (Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Assocs. (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499 (Tech-Bilt).) Another key factor is the settling tortfeasor's¿potential liability for indemnity¿to joint tortfeasors. (Far West Fin'l Corp. v. D & S Co.¿(1988)¿46 Cal.3d 796, 816, fn. 16.) The party asserting the lack of good faith has the burden of proof on that issue. (Code. Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (d).)

Settling Defendants state that the $100,000 settlement amount is roughly proportionate to their liability in this action because Plaintiff came to this agreement with Settling Defendants and therefore the amount is reflective of Plaintiff’s assessment of Settling Defendants’ potential liability. However, Settling Defendants provide no authority for this proposition. The court finds that this reading of the law is inconsistent with Tech-Bilt. If the settlement amount inherently reflected a rough approximation of Settling Defendants’ liability, this factor would be irrelevant to the determination of a good faith settlement because it would be fulfilled in all settlement offers. The court declines to read Tech-Bilt in such a manner as to render a portion of the court’s opinion in that case superfluous.

Settling Defendants do not provide any other information tending to show that the $100,000 settlement amount is roughly proportionate to their liability in this action. Settling Defendants concede that their liability is disputed. Respondents provide the statement of damages served by Plaintiff, which shows claimed damages totaling $6,350,000. Based on the allegations in the complaint and disputed liability, the court finds that the $100,000 is not roughly proportionate to Settling Defendants’ liability in this action. The court finds that, even considering disputed liability, the insurance policy limit of Settling Defendants of $100,000, and the recognition that Settling Defendants should pay less at settlement than if they were found liable after trial, the settlement agreement was not made in good faith.

The court will therefore deny Settling Defendants’ motion for determination of good faith settlement.

3.   Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court DENIES Defendants Landon Whitney and Kimberly Whitney’s motion for determination of good faith settlement.

Settling Defendants are ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOVING PARTY:              Plaintiff Albert Anthony Miramontes

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant General Motors LLC

1.   Background

This is an action under the Song-Beverly Act. Plaintiff Albert Anthony Miramontes (“Plaintiff”) alleged that on December 8, 2020, he purchased a 2021 Chevrolet Silverado (the “Vehicle”), manufactured and/or distributed by defendant General Motors LLC (“Defendant”). Plaintiff alleged that, in connection with the purchase of the Vehicle, he received an express written warranty from Defendant. Plaintiff alleged that during the applicable express warranty period, the Vehicle developed defects and nonconformities to warranty that substantially impaired its use, safety, and/or value.

The action was dismissed without prejudice on March 23, 2026 following a conditional settlement, with the court retaining jurisdiction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6.

2.   Discussion

Plaintiff moves for attorney’s fees in the amount of $25,676 as the prevailing party in this action. In support of this motion, Plaintiff provides the declaration of attorney Jordan G. Cohen. Attorney Tate declares the hourly rates of himself and attorneys Nancy Zhang, Alessandro Manno, Diana Rivero, and Bobby Walker, as well as paralegals David Gomez, Robert Aguilar, Wendy Martinez, Ana Mora, and Maria Chavarin.

Defendant did not file an opposition to this motion. Although, based on statements in Plaintiff’s reply, Defendant apparently served an opposition, no opposition was filed for the court to consider. Defendant filed a sur-reply; however, a sur-reply may only be filed with leave of the court, which the court has not provided here. The court will therefore not consider Defendant’s sur-reply filed on June 22, 2026.

“[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate. . . .¿ The reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work.¿ The lodestar figure may then be adjusted, based on consideration of factors specific to the case, in order to fix the fee at the fair market value for the legal services provided.”¿ (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095 (internal citations omitted); Reck v. FCA US LLC (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 682, 691 [“To determine a reasonable attorney fee award, the trial court applies the lodestar method”].) 

The prevailing party is entitled to “’compensation for all the hours reasonably spent” in litigating the action to a successful conclusion. (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc.¿(2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 821.) ‘Reasonably spent’ means that time spent ‘in the form of inefficient or duplicative efforts is not subject to compensation.’ (Horsford v. Board of Trustees of California State University (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 394.) A plaintiff who receives monetary recovery as part of a settlement agreement is considered to be the prevailing party. (DeSaulles v. Community Hospital of Monterey Peninsula (2016) 62 Cal.4th 1140, 1156.)

“[T]he verified time statements of the attorneys, as officers of the court, are entitled to credence in the absence of a clear indication the records are erroneous.”¿ (Horsford v. Board of Trustees of California State Univ. (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 396.)  However, the court may rely on his or her own experience and is given broad discretion in calculating reasonable attorney’s fees. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 ["The experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court...”].)

The lodestar may be augmented or diminished “by taking various relevant factors into account including (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved and the skill displayed in presenting them; (2) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys; and (3) the contingent nature of the¿¿fee award, based on the uncertainty of prevailing on the merits and of establishing eligibility for the award.” (Ibid.) The multiplier is a risk enhancement based on the probability of loss. (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc.¿(2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 821 (Robertson).)

Plaintiff requests $25,676 in attorney’s fees, based on $22,876 in past fees and $2,800 in anticipated fees in connection with the instant motion.

Plaintiff provides a copy of the settlement agreement as Exhibit 2 to Attorney Cohen’s declaration. The settlement agreement states that Plaintiff is the prevailing party in this action. (Cohen Decl., Ex. 2.) Plaintiff is therefore entitled to recover from Defendant attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses that were reasonably incurred in connection with the commencement and prosecution of this action. (Civ. Code § 1794, subd. (d).)

Upon the court’s review of counsel’s declarations, the court finds that the following hourly rates are reasonable: $560 per hour for attorney Jordan G. Cohen; $540 per hour for attorney Nancy Zhang; $440 per hour for attorney Bobby C. Walker; $440 for attorney Alessandro Manno; and $525 per hour for attorney Diana Rivero. The court finds that $215 per hour for paralegal work is unreasonable, and therefore reduces paralegals David Gomez, Robert Aguilar, Wendy Martinez, Ana Mora, and Maria Chavarin’ hourly rates to $150.

The court has reviewed each entry on the billing statements submitted by Plaintiff’s counsel. Based on the court’s review of the billing statement, the court finds that the requested hours were reasonably incurred. Additionally, Attorney Cohen’s supplemental declaration states that he spent 5.4 hours preparing the reply brief in connection with this action at an hourly rate of $560, totaling more than the requested $2,800 in anticipatory fees. The court therefore finds that Plaintiff has established, as to the attorney’s fees incurred to commence and prosecute this action and to prepare the pending motion for attorney’s fees, a lodestar amount of $24,330.50, after reducing paralegal rates as discussed above.

 

3.   Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court GRANTS Plaintiff’s motion for attorney’s fees. The court orders that Plaintiff shall recover from Defendant a total of $24,330.50 in attorney’s fees, pursuant to Civil Code § 1794, subdiv. (d).

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Plaintiff has, without explanation, failed to comply with the court’s March 20, 2026 order compelling deposition attendance. However, no notice of ruling was filed with respect to the March 20, 2026 order, although the court ordered Defendants to give notice at that time. Because there is no admissible evidence that Williams was ever given notice of the court’s March 20, 2026 order, any failure by Williams to comply with this order cannot be presumed to have been willful. The court therefore finds that terminating sanctions are inappropriate under the circumstances and denies the motion.

1.   Background

This is an action brought under the Song-Beverly Act. Plaintiff Sharon Evet Collins (“Plaintiff”) alleges that on November 23, 2019, Plaintiff entered into a warranty contract with defendant Nissan North America, Inc. (“Defendant”) regarding a 2019 Nissan Kicks (the “Vehicle”). Plaintiffs allege that defects and nonconformities to warranty manifested themselves in the Vehicle within the applicable express warranty period, substantially impairing the use, value, or safety of the Vehicle.

 

2.   Request for Judicial Notice

Defendant requests that the court take judicial notice of minute orders in various other cases.

Factual findings in prior judicial orders, opinions, or decisions are not a proper subject of judicial notice. (Kilroy v. State of Calif. (2004) 119 Cal.App.4th 140, 148.) Nor does the court take judicial notice of matters not relevant to the proceeding. (People ex rel. Lockyer v. Shamrock Foods Co. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 415, 422, fn. 2.) However, pursuant to California Evidence Code 452, subd. (c), judicial notice may be taken of “[o]fficial acts of the legislative, executive, and judicial departments of the United States and of any state of the United States.”

The court declines to take judicial notice of the requested documents as not relevant to the proceeding.

3.   Discussion

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant’s initial discovery disclosures, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 871.26. Plaintiff contends that Defendant failed to timely provide these initial disclosures. In opposition, Defendant contends that the disclosures have been provided since the filing of the instant motion and that sanctions are unwarranted because the parties agreed to pause their disclosure obligations while engaged in active settlement negotiations.

Under Code of Civil Procedure § 871.26, subdivision (b), “[w]ithin 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (b).) Subdivisions (f), (g), and (h) set out the specific disclosures and documents required to be disclosed.

Defendant concedes that the requested initial disclosures were not timely served. However, Defendant contends that the motion is moot because disclosures were served on April 14, 2026. (Sweeney Declaration, ¶ 10.) Although Plaintiff sets forth in her motion specific discovery that she seeks within Defendant’s initial disclosures, and Defendant disputes the scope of the requested discovery, Plaintiff has not filed a reply contending that Defendant’s initial disclosures are inadequate or not code-compliant.

Defendant has served its initial disclosures. The instant motion is therefore moot to the extent that it seeks an order compelling disclosures. It is not moot with respect to the issue of sanctions.

Plaintiff requests monetary sanctions against Defendant “and/or its counsel of record” in the amount of $2,500, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 871.26, subd. (j)(1).

Code of Civil Procedure § 871.26, subd. (j)(1) provides that, unless the offending party makes a showing of good cause, the court shall impose a $2,500 sanction upon a defense attorney who fails to comply with the provisions of Section 871.26.

By requesting a generic award of monetary sanctions against Defendant’s “counsel of record,” Plaintiff has failed to clearly identify “every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought,” as is required by Code of Civil Procedure § 2023.040. The court will therefore deny Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant’s “counsel of record.”

Code of Civil Procedure § 871.26 only provides for monetary sanctions against counsel, not against a party directly. Therefore, the court will deny Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant.

4.   Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion to compel initial disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 871.26. The court DENIES Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

The court will therefore strike Plaintiff’s request for punitive damages from the complaint, without leave to amend.

Defendant moves to compel Plaintiff’s claims to arbitration. Defendant contends that Plaintiff executed a voluntary arbitration agreement upon admission to Defendant’s medical facility. Plaintiff does not oppose this motion.

 

Defendant has attached a copy of the arbitration agreement between Plaintiff and Defendant, signed by Plaintiff and by a facility representative. The agreement states that “any dispute between Resident… and [Defendant], and their its owners, investors, operators, officers, directors, administrators, staff, employees, agents, and any management and administrative services company and all related entities and individuals… including any action for injury or death arising from negligence, torts, intentional tort and/or statutory causes of action (including all California Welfare and Institutions Code sections, all California Business and Professions Code sections, Health and Safety Code section 1430), and all other statutory claims under any applicable California code, will be determined by submission to binding arbitration and not by lawsuit or resort to court process except as California law provides for judicial review of arbitration proceedings… By signing this agreement, the parties waive their right to commence and/or be a party to any class or collective action in any court against the other party relating in any manner whatsoever to the Resident's residency at the Facility.” (Chevez Decl., Ex. A.) Based on the court’s review of the complaint, it is apparent that the instant action arises out of or relates to Plaintiff’s residency at the facility. Therefore, the arbitration provision applies to all parties in this action.

 

Under Gamboa and Iyere, attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement to the motion constitutes setting forth the agreement’s provisions in the motion, which is sufficient to fulfill Defendant’s initial burden of proving the existence of an arbitration agreement between the parties. The burden now shifts to Plaintiff to establish any fact necessary to her opposition. As Plaintiff has not opposed this motion, Plaintiff has failed to carry her burden.

 

The court will therefore grant Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s claims to arbitration.  The case management conference is vacated.  The order to show cause re failure to file proof of service is discharged. The court sets a status conference re arbitration for March 30, 2027, 8:30 a.m. in Department A. 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.
