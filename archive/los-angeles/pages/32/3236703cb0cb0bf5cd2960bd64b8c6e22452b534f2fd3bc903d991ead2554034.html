Case Number: 21STLC00813    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Lisa Leann Sunshine Madrigal’s Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is denied.

                                                                                                                               

 

Analysis:

 

On January 29, 2021, Plaintiff Covina Pines Homeowners Association (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Starr Lynn Cano aka Starr L. Cano (“Defendant Cano”) and Lisa Leann Sunshine Madrigal (“Defendant Madrigal”). Following the lack of any responsive pleading by Defendants, the Court entered their default and default judgment on March 29, 2021 and May 6, 2021, respectively.

 

Defendant Cano, in propria persona, filed a Motion for Order Vacating and Setting Aside Entry of Default and Default Judgment, which the Court denied on January 8, 2026. (Minute Order, 01/08/26.) On January 14, 2026, Defendant Madrigal, also in propria persona, filed a Motion to Set Aside Default and/or Default Judgment. The Court granted Defendant Madrigal’s first Motion to Set Aside on March 5, 2026 and set an Order to Show Cause Re: Dismissal of Action for Failure to Serve Defendant within Three Years of Filing of the Complaint. (Minute Order, 03/05/26.) Prior to the Order to Show Cause, Plaintiff filed Proof of Substitute Service on April 3, 2026.

 

Following Defendant Madrigal’s failure to file a response to the action again, the Court entered her default on April 30, 2026. The Order to Show Cause was discharged on May 7, 2026 and Defendant filed an Answer on May 12, 2027. The Court entered default judgment against Defendant Madrigal on July 9, 2026.

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Default and/or Default Judgment on August 17, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on September 2, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant Madrigal moves to vacate the default and default judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Under that section, an application for relief must be made within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s or counsel’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) The six-month deadline is jurisdictional and not subject to tolling. (Manson, Iver & York v. Black (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 36, 42.)

 

The Court’s March 5, 2026 order vacating the initial default set an Order to Show Cause re Proof of Service for May 7, 2026.  Plaintiff initiated service on Defendant Madrigal by substitute service within a week of that order, delivering the papers to her husband on March 12, 2026, and mailing them to her residence the next day.  (Proof of Substitute Service, 04.03.2026.)  Plaintiff did not seek to have default entered until April 29, 2026, which the Clerk entered the same day.  (Default, 04/30/26.)  Defendant Madrigal appeared at the May 7, 2026 show-cause hearing and was advised that she was again in default.  She nevertheless filed an Answer to the Complaint on May 12, 2026, and did not move to vacate default or default judgment until filing this Motion on August 17, 2026.   Her declaration states that she did not begin to draft the motion until she received notice that Judgment had been entered on July 9, 2026.  (Motion, Madrigal Decl., ¶12.)  The three-and-a-half month delay between learning of the default and filing this Motion is unreasonable.  Defendant Madrigal had previously been in default and moved to vacate.  It is not clear to the Court why there was any misunderstanding about the implications of that status that would make the delay reasonable. 

 

Setting aside the issue of untimeliness, the Motion is supported by a declaration in which Defendant Madrigal attests that she was confused about the procedural posture of the case after the Court granted her first Motion to Set Aside, stating that she “was confused about what I was required to do next after the March 5 ruling and the new service,” and that she “mistakenly believed that the already-pending court proceedings would address what [she] needed to do next and [she] did not understand that [she] was required to file another responsive pleading within the applicable response period.” (Motion, Madrigal Decl., ¶4.) Defendant Madrigal does not dispute being served with the Summons and Complaint in March 2026. (Id. at ¶¶3-4.) Defendant Madrigal filed an Answer on May 12, 2026, after her default had been entered. (Id. at ¶7.)

 

Defendant Madrigal’s misunderstanding of procedural requirements is not a cognizable “mistake” under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). “Mistake is not a ground for relief under section 473, subdivision (b), when it ‘is simply the result of professional incompetence, general ignorance of the law, or unjustifiable negligence in discovering the law....’ (8 Witkin, Cal. Procedure (5th ed. 2008) Attack on Judgment in Trial Court, § 155, p. 749.)” (Hearn v. Howard (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1193, 1206.) There are no exceptions to this principle for pro se litigants.  (See Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal. 4th 975, 979 (“Procedural law cannot cast a sympathetic eye on the unprepared, or it will soon fragment into a kaleidoscope of shifting rules.”)  Rather, the Court has to be able to find that a litigant’s neglect was “such as might have been the act of a reasonably prudent person under the same circumstances.”  (Hearn, 177 Cal.App.4th at 1206.)  The Court cannot make that finding here. The Summons clearly states that a response to the action must be made within 30 days of service. Defendant’s Motion does not address this language. Nor does Defendant Madrigal point to anything communicated to her by a clerk or Plaintiff to suggest that she had no need to timely file a responsive pleading after being served.  (See Rappleyea, 8 Cal. 4th at 979 (reliance on misinformation from clerk and plaintiff rendered mistake excusable.)  Defendant Madrigal’s assumption that she could neglect to respond until she was told what to do – especially after her default had been previously entered – and disregard of the clear mandate of the Summons, amounts to general ignorance of the law and unjustifiable negligence in discovering the law. This kind of error is not grounds to vacate the entry of default or default judgment.

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Defendant Lisa Leann Sunshine Madrigal’s Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC04788    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff American Contractors Indemnity Company’s Motion to be Deemed Prevailing and for Attorneys’ Fees and Costs is granted in the amount of $4,562.50 attorney’s fees and $1,445.15 costs.

 

 

Analysis:

           

Plaintiff American Contractors Indemnity Company (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Umar Ali (erroneously sued as Umar Ali aka Ali Umar) (“Defendant Ali”) and R U Trucking (“Defendant R U Trucking”) on June 20, 2025. Defendant Ali filed an Answer to the Complaint on August 18, 2025. Following Defendant R U Trucking’s failure to file a response, the Court entered its default on August 21, 2025.

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions Admitted, and Request for Monetary Sanctions was granted against Defendant Ali on January 27, 2026. (Minute Order, 01/27/26.) On March 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Motion for Summary Judgment. The Court granted the Motion for Summary Judgment on June 2, 2026 and entered judgment and default judgment against Defendants Ali and R U Trucking, respectively, on July 6, 2026.

 

Plaintiff filed and served the instant Motion to Be Deemed Prevailing Party and for Award of Attorneys’ Fees on June 16, 2026. Plaintiff filed a Notice of No Opposition on September 10, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an award of attorney’s fees in the amount of $5,562.50 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1032 and 1033.5, and Civil Code section 1717. Plaintiff also seeks costs of $1,445.15, as set forth in its memorandum of costs, also filed on June 16, 2026. A prevailing party is entitled to recover costs, including attorney’s fees when authorized by contract. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4); § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10)(A).) Civil Code section 1717 provides that attorneys’ fees and costs shall be awarded to the prevailing party in an action on a contract, where the contract specifically provides for attorney’s fees and costs. (Civ. Code, § 1717, subd. (a).)

 

A motion for attorney’s fees must be filed and served within the time for filing a notice of appeal under Cal. Rules of Court Rule 8.822. (Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1702(a).) Cal. Rules of Court Rule 8.822 states that an appeal must be filed within either (1) 30 days after service of the notice of entry of the judgment; or (2) 90 days after entry of the judgment. (Cal. Rules of Court 8.822(1).) The instant Motion was timely filed after the hearing on the Motion for Summary Judgment, but before entry of judgment.

 

It is undisputed that Plaintiff is the prevailing party in this action, as the party in whose favor summary judgment was granted and judgment has been entered. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4).) Accordingly, it is entitled to recover its costs under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, subdivision (a). It is also entitled to recover its attorney’s fees if such recovery is provided for in the contracts at issue. The parties’ contracts require Defendants “[t]o indemnify Surety against all losses, liabilities, costs, damages, attorneys’ and consultants’ fees and expenses the Surety may incur or has incurred due to the execution and issuance of the bond (Motion, Batoon Decl., Exhs. 1, 2, 3, at ¶2.) Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to recover its attorney’s fees as the prevailing party.

 

Plaintiff’s counsel declares that its attorneys billed their work between $300.00 and $375.00 per hour, and the paralegals billed their time at $175.00 per hour. (Id. at ¶¶12-13 and Exh. 5.) These are reasonable billing rates for attorneys in Southern California with years of experience. (See id. at ¶12.) However, a review of its attached billing records shows that the amounts billed for the first five attorney entries do not match the billing rate and time spent, e.g. 1 hour at $175.00 is not $409.36, as the second entry reads. (Id. at Exh. 5, p. 1.) The Court, therefore, declines to award the $1,000.00 claimed in those entries. The Court is satisfied with the accuracy of the remaining attorney entries, which mathematically reflect the billing rate and time spent. (Id. at Exh. 5, pp. 1-2.) The Court will not award the additional $450 requested for reviewing and responding to an opposition, given that one was not filed. (See id., ¶14.)  Plaintiff is awarded attorney’s fees of $4,562.50. 

 

Finally, Plaintiff seeks the costs of $1,445.15 set forth in its Memorandum of Costs filed on June 16, 2026. As no motion to tax or strike costs has been filed by the 15-day deadline, the costs are also granted. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(1).)

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff American Contractors Indemnity Company’s Motion to be Deemed Prevailing and for Attorneys’ Fees and Costs is granted in the amount of $4,562.50 attorney’s fees and $1,445.15 costs.

Case Number: 26STCP01850    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Jacqueline Denham’s Petition to Vacate Arbitration Award is dismissed.

 

Analysis:

 

On May 14, 2026, Petitioner Jacqueline Denham (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Vacate Arbitration Award against Respondent Vision ASU, LLC (“Respondent”). The Court set the Petition for hearing on September 17, 2026 and ordered Petitioner to give notice of the hearing date to Respondent. (Notice of Hearing, 05/14/26.) No proof of service of the Petition or Notice of Hearing has been filed to date. No response to the Petition has been filed to date.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party to an arbitration in which an award has been made may petition the court to confirm, correct or vacate the award.  The petition shall name as respondent all parties to the arbitration and may name as respondents any other persons bound by the arbitration award.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.) “If a petition or response under this chapter is duly served and filed, the court shall confirm the award as made, whether rendered in this state or another state, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.)

 

A petition to vacate or correct an arbitration award, or a response to a petition to confirm arbitration award that seeks to vacate or correct the award must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent, or within ten days of service of the petition to confirm, whichever is sooner.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288, 1288.2, 1290.6; Valencia v. Mendoza (2024)103 Cal. App. 5th 427, 445.) The court must confirm the award as made, unless it corrects or vacates the award, or dismisses the proceeding.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286; Valsan Partners Limited Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility, Inc. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.)

 

Discussion

 

Filing Requirements of a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award (CCP § 1285.4)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1285.4 states: “A petition under this chapter shall:

 

(a)   Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

(b)   Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

(c) Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4 (emphasis added).) The Petition neither sets forth the substance of the arbitration agreement, nor attaches a copy of the arbitration agreement or award. (Pet., ¶4.) In fact, the Petition includes no attachments. The Petition only provides the name of the arbitrator, Eric Blum. (Pet., ¶6.) Therefore, the Petition does not satisfy the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1285.4.

 

Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing (CCP § 1290.4)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4 states in pertinent part:

 

“(a) A copy of the petition and a written notice of the time and place of the hearing thereof and any other papers upon which the petition is based shall be served in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice.

 

(b) If the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made and the person upon whom service is to be made has not previously appeared in the proceeding and has not previously been served in accordance with this subdivision: ¶ (1) Service within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4.) Without a copy of the arbitration award attached to the Petition, the Court must require that service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing comports with the requirements for service of a summons. As noted above, no proof of service of the Petition or Notice of Hearing has been filed to date. The statutory service requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4, therefore, are not satisfied.

 

Service of the Arbitration Award (CCP § 1283.6)

         

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 provides that: “The neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.”  (Emphasis added.) This requirement may be satisfied by service by the arbitrator, or upon proper service of the award with the Petition. (See Murry v. Civil Service Employees Ins. Co. (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 796, 799-800.)

 

The Petition states that the award was served on the parties on May 4, 2026 but attaches no proof of this service. Nor was the award served with the Petition. Therefore, the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 are also not met.

 

Timing of Service of Petition (CCP § 1288)

           

A response to a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award that seeks to vacate or correct the award must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent, or within ten days of service of the Petition to Confirm, whichever is sooner.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288.2, 1290.6; Valencia v. Mendoza (2024)103 Cal. App. 5th 427, 445.)  Petitioner has not demonstrated service of the arbitration award and service of the instant Petition, thus, the Court cannot find that the Petition is timely under Code of Civil Procedure section 1288.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the lack of compliance with any of the mandatory requirements for service and presentation of the Petition to Vacate Arbitration Award, Petitioner Jacqueline Denham’s Petition to Vacate Arbitration Award is dismissed.

Case Number: 26STLC02686    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Jerome Edgar’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One; (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; and (3) Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted, are denied without prejudice.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 18, 2026, Plaintiff Jerome Edgar (“Plaintiff”) served Special Interrogatories, Set One, Request for Production of Documents, Set One, and Requests for Admission, Set One, on Defendant Talisha Hamilton (“Defendant”). (Motions, Edgar Decl., Exh. A.) Plaintiff filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One; (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; and (3) Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted, on August 11, 2026. As of the filing of this Motion, Defendant has not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶¶4-5.) Defendant filed an opposition to the Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted on August 26, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The discovery requests served on Defendant do not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 94, which allows only 35 written discovery requests in the Limited Civil Court unless otherwise authorized by court order or by stipulation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 94, subd. (a); § 95.) Plaintiff served Defendant with 25 requests for admission, 10 requests for production, and 13 special interrogatories, for a total of 47 written discovery requests. (Motions, Edgar Decl., Exh. A.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the instant discovery Motions are denied without prejudice. The Court will not grant motions to compel responses, or to deem admissions admitted, with respect to discovery requests propounded in violation of the Code of Civil Procedure.  

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Jerome Edgar’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One; (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; and (3) Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted, are denied without prejudice.
