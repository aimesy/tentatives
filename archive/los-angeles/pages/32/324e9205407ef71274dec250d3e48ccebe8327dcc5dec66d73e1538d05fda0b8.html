LASC Case No. 24LBCV00194
Hearing Date: September 11, 2024
Request for Default Judgment by Court
 
 
In preparation for the September 11, 2024 default judgment hearing, the court has identified several concerns with Plaintiff’s request: 
 

  1. Request for Entry of Default was entered on May 9, 2024 which is less than 30 days after service of the summons and complaint to Defendant Danielle Canissa and CDW Logistics, LLC. (C.C.P. §587);
 
  1. Plaintiff checked the wrong box and should have checked “Court Judgment” in the “Request For” section on the 1st page. (See 7/29/2024 Request for Entry of Default/Judgment);
 
  1. No DOEs dismissal filed;
 
  1. No attached/adequate and/or authenticated exhibits provided with Plaintiff’s Declaration. Exhibits should demonstrate, e.g. costs for title and insurance for two vehicles, power of attorney, agreement entered into with Defendants to purchase trucks and provide logistic and shipping services to clients;
 
  1. No contractual or statutory basis provided by Plaintiff as to recovery of attorney fees. Also, attorney fees requested is more than what is allowed in a contract for recovery of reasonable attorney fees under Local Rule of the Court, rule 3.214(a);  
 
  1. Prayer for complaint does not specify damages claimed;
 
  1. No notice of punitive damages as required under Code Civ. Proc., § 425.11, subd. (c).  Appears Plaintiff may be waiving punitive damages based upon proposed judgment;
 
  1. Proposed Judgment (UD-100) incorrect calculations as to attorney fees and prejudgment interest calculations.

Motion:  Proposed Intervenor Long Beach Public Transportation Company’s Motion for Leave to File Complaint-in-Intervention  

Moving Party:  Plaintiff-in-Intervention Long Beach Public Transportation Company (“LBPTC”) 

Responding Party: None  

 

 

Tentative Ruling 

 

LBPTC moves the Court for an order permitting intervention as a party plaintiff.   

 

LBPTC states that Plaintiff sustained personal injuries in the subject incident while acting in the course and scope of his employment with LBPTC.  (Bustos Decl., ¶¶ 2-4.)  LBPTC asserts that it has “become obligated to pay said EMPLOYEE certain sums of  

money for which INTERVENOR now has a subrogation claim pursuant to Labor Code § 3852.”  (Bustos Decl., ¶ 5.)  No opposition has been filed.   

 

LBPTC has demonstrated it is entitled to intervene in the instant action because it has paid workers’ compensation benefits to and on behalf of Plaintiff for the injuries he suffered in the subject incident.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subds. (c) and (d)(1)(A); Lab. Code, § 3852.)  LBPTC has also submitted a copy of its proposed complaint-in-intervention pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 387, subdivision (c). 

 

LBPTC’s motion for leave to intervene is GRANTED.  LBPTC is to file its complaint-in-intervention within 20 days.  

Case Number: 26LBCV00037    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Defendant Los Angeles Police Department’s Motion for Order Requiring Plaintiff to Post a Security 

Moving Party:  Defendant Los Angeles Police Department (“Defendant LA”)    

Responding Party: Plaintiff Neel Karia (“Plaintiff”)   

 

Background 

On January 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Christian Outreach in Action, Long Beach Rescue Mission, Luther Social Services, Beacon Light Rescue Mission, Long Beach Police Department, Los Angeles Police Department and Department of Mental Health, alleging a civil rights violation cause of action.  On March 20, 2026, Defendant LA filed the instant motion for order requiring Plaintiff to post a security.  That same day, Defendant LA also filed a demurrer.  On April 16, 2026, Defendant City of Long Beach filed an answer.  That same day, Defendant City of Long Beach filed a joinder to the instant motion.  On April 24, 2026, Defendant Long Beach Rescue Mission filed a demurrer to and motion to strike portions of the complaint.  On May 26, 2026, Defendant Lutheran Services of Southern California filed a demurrer to and motion to strike portions of the complaint.  On May 27, 2026, Defendant Christian Outreach in Action filed an answer.  That same day, Defendant Lutheran Services of Southern California filed a joinder to the instant motion.   

 

As of July 13, 2026, no opposition has been received.  

 

Relevant Facts 

On January 6, 2026, this Court deemed Plaintiff to be a vexatious litigant in Neel Karia v. Dignity Health St. Mary Medical Center, Case No. 25LBCV01194.  (RJN Ex. 3.)  In the Court’s ruling on the motion to deem Plaintiff a vexatious litigant, the Court stated that any defendant “may seek Code of Civil Procedure sections 391.1 - 391.4 relief, requiring a meritless or frivolous case be dismissed unless (Plaintiff) furnishes ordered security.”  (Id. at p. 8.)   

 

Request for Judicial Notice 

Defendant LA’s request for Judicial Notice is granted in its entirety.     

 

Taking judicial notice of a document is not the same as accepting the truth of its contents or accepting a particular interpretation of its meaning.  (Herrera v. Deutsche Bank National Trust Co. (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1366, 1375.)  When judicial notice is taken of a document, the truthfulness and proper interpretation of the document are disputable.  (Ibid.)  Judicial notice of a Court record is limited to the existence of the documents and is not the same as taking notice of the truth of any matters or facts stated therein.  (Ibid.) 

 

Legal Standard 

“A ‘vexatious litigant’ [includes] a person who … [i]n any litigation while acting in propria persona, repeatedly files unmeritorious motions, pleadings, or other papers, conducts unnecessary discovery, or engages in other tactics that are frivolous or solely intended to cause unnecessary delay.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 391, subd. (b)(3).)  "Litigation," for purposes of determining vexatious litigant status, means "any civil action or proceeding, commenced, maintained or pending in any state or federal court."  (Code Civ. Proc., § 391, subd. (a).)   

¿ 

A¿particular litigation is finally determined when avenues for direct review (appeal) have been exhausted or the time for appeal has expired.  (Childs v. PaineWebber Incorporated¿(1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 982, 993-994.)  Any actions the pro per plaintiff has filed and dismissed counts for the purpose of the vexatious litigant statute, even if the dismissal is voluntary and without prejudice, as they still burden the judicial system and the target of the litigation.  (Tokerud v. Capitol Bank Sacramento (1995) 38 Cal.App.4th 775, 779.)¿ 

 

Under the vexatious litigant statutes, (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 391-391.7), a defendant may move the court for an order requiring plaintiff to furnish security on the ground that plaintiff is a vexatious litigant and the litigation lacks merit.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 391.1, 391.2.)¿ If the trial court determines that the plaintiff is a vexatious litigant and that there is no reasonable probability that the plaintiff will prevail against the moving defendant, the court must order the plaintiff to furnish security for the benefit of the moving party.¿ (Code of Civ. Proc., § 391.3.)¿ 

¿ 

Under vexatious litigant statute, the entirety of the litigation is stayed once the defendant files a motion to determine that the plaintiff is a vexatious litigant who should be required to furnish security in that action.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.6.)  The stay shall remain effective until 10 days after the motion is denied or 10 days after the security is furnished.  (Ibid.) 

  

Parties’ Arguments 

Defendant LA argues that Plaintiff has already been declared a vexatious litigant by this Court.  (RJN Ex. 3.)  Defendant LA also notes that Plaintiff has filed at least fifteen cases over the last eleven months.  (RJN Ex. 1.)  Defendant LA adds that it has been a defendant on at least five of such cases.  (RJN Exs. 9, 11, 16, 20 and 21.)  Defendant LA then states there is no reasonable probability that Plaintiff will prevail in the instant case as a single cause of action is not alleged against any Defendant in this action.  Defendant LA asserts that Plaintiff should be required to post a security in this case and if Plaintiff fails to provide the requisite security the complaint should be dismissed.  Defendant LA suggests the Court set the security at a minimum of $10,000 or another reasonable amount to cover Defendant LA’s costs and time associated with defending Plaintiff’s repeat litigation.   

 

Defendant City of Long Beach joins in the instant motion, and requests that any security ordered against Plaintiff apply equally to benefit Defendant City of Long Beach.  

 

Defendant Lutheran Social Services of Southern California join the instant motion, requesting any order requiring Plaintiff to post security apply equally for its benefit as well.   

 

No opposition has been filed.   

 

Tentative Ruling 

Code of Civil Procedure section 391.1 states in relevant part: “[t]he motion for an order requiring the plaintiff to furnish security shall be based upon the ground, and supported by a showing, that the plaintiff is a vexatious litigant and that there is not a reasonable probability that¿they¿will prevail in the litigation against the moving defendant.”   

 

The Court agrees that it previously declared Plaintiff a vexatious litigant in Neel Karia v. Dignity Health St. Mary Medical Center, Case No. 25LBCV01194, on January 6, 2026.  (RJN Ex. 3.)  The first condition is met.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 391, subd. (b)(4) [a vexatious litigant is a person who has previously been declared to be a vexatious litigant by any state or federal court of record in any action or proceeding based upon the same or substantially similar facts, transaction, or occurrence].)   

 

There is not a reasonable probability that Plaintiff will prevail in the litigation against Defendant LA because there is no statute alleged in the complaint that makes Defendant LA liable as a public entity.  (Gov. Code, § 815, subd. (a).)  Further, the Court agrees that the complaint is unintelligible.   

 

Moreover, the Court agrees that its holding in Neel Karia v. Dignity Health St. Mary Medical Center, Case No. 25LBCV01194 stated “[a]s to the currently pending cases, any defendant may seek Code of Civil Procedure sections 391.1 - 391.4 relief, requiring a meritless or frivolous case be dismissed unless Plaintiff furnishes ordered security.”  (RJN Ex. 3.)    

 

For the foregoing reasons, the instant motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff shall furnish security in the amount of $3,000 for the benefit of Defendant LA, Defendant City of Long Beach and Defendant Lutheran Social Services of Southern California in this action no later than 4:30 p.m. on August 13, 2026.   

 

The action is stayed pending proof that such security has been furnished.   

 

The Court will sign the proposed order submitted by Defendant LA. 

Case Number: 26LBCV00114    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Defendants Long Beach Memorial Medical Center; Memorialcare Long Beach Medical Center, dba Long Beach Memorial Medical Center; Memorial Health Services, sued and served as Memorialcare’s Motion to Strike Allegations of Pre-Death Pain and Suffering and Punitive and Exemplary Damages in Complaint 

Moving Parties:  Defendants Long Beach Memorial Medical Center; Memorialcare Long Beach Medical Center, dba Long Beach Memorial Medical Center; Memorial Health Services, sued and served as Memorialcare Starbucks Corporation (“Defendants”) 

Responding Party: Plaintiff Steven Curtis, individually and as successor in interest to Dolores Aracely Perez, deceased (“Plaintiff”) 

 

Procedural Background 

On January 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Long Beach Memorial Medical Center; Memorial Care Long Beach Medical Center; Memorial Care and Does 1 through 100, alleging the following claims:  

  1. Wrongful Death based on Medical Malpractice (CCP §377.60); and  

  1. Medical Malpractice through a Survival Action (CCP §377.20).  

 

On May 1, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion to strike.  On June 16, 2026, the Court continued the instant motion to July 14, 2026.  (June 16, 2026 Notice re: Continuance of Hearing and Order.)   

 

As of July 8, 2026, no opposition has been received.   

 

Meet and Confer  

The requirements are satisfied.  (Firth Decl., ¶¶ 2-4, Exs. A-B; Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a).)   

 

Discussion 

 

Pre-Death Pain and Suffering Allegation 

Code of Civil Procedure section 377.34 states in relevant part:  

“(a) In an action or proceeding by a decedent's personal representative or successor in interest on the decedent's cause of action, the damages recoverable are limited to the loss or damage that the decedent sustained or incurred before death, including any penalties or punitive or exemplary damages that the decedent would have been entitled to recover had the decedent lived, and do not include damages for pain, suffering, or disfigurement.  

 

(b) Notwithstanding subdivision (a), in an action or proceeding by a decedent's personal representative or successor in interest on the decedent's cause of action, the damages recoverable may include damages for pain, suffering, or disfigurement if the action or proceeding was granted a preference pursuant to Section 36 before January 1, 2022, or was filed on or after January 1, 2022, and before January 1, 2026.” 

 

The complaint in this action was filed on January 16, 2026.  Thus, pre-death pain and suffering and such are stricken.   

 

The instant motion is GRANTED without leave to amend as to this ground.  Complaint page 6, lines 12-16: “2. DOLORES ARACELY PEREZ’ pre-death pain and suffering damages as available under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.34(b) up to the limits outlined in Civil Code section 3333.2 for the second cause of action for negligence. This is separate, apart, and in addition to the general damages sought by STEVE CURTIS in the first cause of action. (Ng v. Superior Court (2025) 108 Cal.App.5th 382, 388.)” is ordered stricken.   

 

Punitive Damages  

The Court finds that insufficient allegations have been pled to warrant punitive damages.   

 

Tentative Ruling 

The Court GRANTS the instant motion without leave to amend as to pre-death pain and suffering and with 10 days leave to amend as to punitive damages.
