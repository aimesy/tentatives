Case Number: 24CHCV02951    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 9/11/26                                                                           TRIAL DATE: 5/17/27

Case #24CHCV02951

 

MOTION TO CONSOLIDATE

 

Motion filed on 4/29/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff George Manolakos

JOINING PARTY: Defendants Martin Outdoor Media, LLC; Apex Outdoor Advertising, LLC and Stephen James Dunn

RESPONDING PARTY: all other parties

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order consolidating the following cases: Glenn Curtis Kort, et al. v. Martin Outdoor Medial, LLC, et al. LASC Case No.24CHCV02951 and George Manolakos v. Steven Dunn, et al. LASC Case No.24CHCV03163 for all purposes.

 

RULING: The motion is granted.

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

These actions arises out of a motor vehicle accident that occurred on 9/27/22.  On 8/16/24, Plaintiffs Glenn Curtis Kort, et al. filed their action against Martin Outdoor Media, LLC, et al (24CHCV02951) and on 9/3/24, Plaintiff George Manolakos filed an action against Steven Dunn, et al. (24CHCV03163). 

 

On 10/7/25, the actions were consolidated and assigned to Dept. F47.   (See 10/7/25 Minute Order).    

 

The Kort Action currently has a 5/17/27 trial date whereas the Manolakos action has an 8/30/27 trial date. 

 

All parties, except Defendant Robin Ghermezi have signed a stipulation to consolidate the actions.

 

On 4/29/26, Plaintiff George Manolakos filed and served the instant motion seeking an order consolidating the following cases: Glenn Curtis Kort, et al. v. Martin Outdoor Medial, LLC, et al. LASC Case No.24CHCV02951 and George Manolakos v. Steven Dunn, et al. LASC Case No.24CHCV03163 for all purposes.  On 5/13/26, Defendants Martin Outdoor Media, LLC; Apex Outdoor Advertising, LLC and Stephen James Dunn filed and served a notice of joinder in the motion.  No opposition or other response to the motion has been filed.

 

ANALYSIS

 

When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the Court, the Court may order a joint hearing or trial of any or all the matters in issue in the actions; it may order all the actions consolidated and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.  See CCP 1048(a).  Consolidation of actions promotes judicial economy and prevents contradictory or inconsistent rulings arising from the same underlying events.  Id.

 

Here, both actions arise from the same motor vehicle accident and involve common defendants.  As such, the actions involve common issues, evidence and witnesses.  Consolidation will promote justice and judicial economy; avoid duplicative costs and proceedings; and avoid potential inconsistent rulings. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.     

 

 


Case Number: 24CHCV04101    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 9/11/26                                                              TRIAL DATE: 3/8/27

Case #24CHCV04101

 

MOTION TO QUASH SUBPOENA

 

Motion filed on 5/4/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Ana Martinez; National Retail Transportation, Inc. and National Retail Systems, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiffs Vardan Sargsysan and Kristine Sargsyan

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order quashing Plaintiffs’ subpoena of records from Defendant Ana Martinez’s former employer JB Hunt Transport Services, Inc.

 

RULING: The motion is granted. 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This personal injury action arises from a two-vehicle collision on 11/22/22 on the Glenoaks Boulevard offramp exit in or near Pacoima/San Fernando, California.  Plaintiff Vardan Sargsyan (Vardan) was driving a 2022 Kia Forte with passenger Plaintiff Kristine Sargsyan (Kristine) (collectively, Plaintiffs).  Vardan made a left turn when the vehicle collided with Defendant Ana Martinez (Martinez) who was also making a left turn while operating a tractor-trailer owned by Defendants National Retail Transportation, Inc. and National Retail Systems, Inc.  On 11/7/24,  Plaintiffs filed this action alleging causes of action for Motor Vehicle Negligence and General Negligence.  Plaintiffs and Defendants both claim the other is at fault for the accident. 

 

On 4/10/26, Plaintiffs issued a subpoena to Martinez’s former employer, JB Hunt Transport Services, Inc. with a production date of 5/8/26, seeking:

 

“Any and all employment records including, but not limited to, personnel records, workers' compensation files, correspondence, performance evaluations, application for employment, resumes, signed acknowledgment for policies and/or procedures, training records, pre-employment exam records, injury/health, disability records, write-ups, written warnings, disciplinary/investigation reports, incident reports, claims, performance plans, termination/separation agreements, and any other documents related to her driving skills contained in the employment file pertaining to Ana Martinez . . .”

 

(See Marr Decl., Ex.1)

 

On 5/4/26, Defendants’ counsel requested that Plaintiffs withdraw the subpoena because it was overbroad and invaded Martinez’s privacy.  After not receiving a response, on the same day, Defendants filed and served the instant motion seeking an order quashing Plaintiffs’ subpoena of records from Defendant Ana Martinez’s former employer JB Hunt Transport Services, Inc.  Plaintiffs have opposed the motion and Defendants have filed a reply to the opposition. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

CCP 1985.6(f)(1) provides:

 

“Any employee whose employment records are sought by a subpoena duces tecum may, prior to the date for production, bring a motion under Section 1987.1 to quash or modify the subpoena duces tecum. Notice of the bringing of that motion shall be given to the witness and the deposition officer at least five days prior to production. The failure to provide notice to the deposition officer does not invalidate the motion to quash or modify the subpoena duces tecum but may be raised by the deposition officer as an affirmative defense in any action for liability for improper release of records.”

 

Contrary to the assertion in the opposition, the instant motion is timely.  Five days prior to the production date fell on Sunday, 5/3/26.  Therefore, Defendants timely filed and served the motion on the next court day, 5/4/26.  See CRC 1.10; CCP 12, 12a, 12b.  Plaintiff’s citation to CCP 1005(b) is confusing, at best.  (See Opposition, p.4:2-7).  The instant motion was filed and served on 5/4/26, well beyond the 16 court days + 2 court days notice required when a motion is served electronically and Plaintiffs had more than the required statutory notice to file and serve an opposition.

 

The subject subpoena is overbroad and an unreasonable invasion of Martinez’s right to privacy as it seeks any and all employment records regarding Martinez from a prior employer.  The opposition seems to concede that Martinez’s records from a prior employer cannot be used as character evidence in this case.  (See Opposition, p.6:26-p.7:6).  While the opposition argues that Plaintiffs are entitled to obtain evidence regarding Martinez’s driving skills and qualifications as a driver of a semi-truck/tractor trailer, the subject subpoena is not limited to such records.  (See Opposition, p.5:13-14).  Moreover, Martinez’s employment records from a prior employer would not necessarily “establish objective facts concerning Martinez’s professional qualifications, training, experience, and familiarity with operating tractor-trailers similar to the vehicle involved in this subject incident.”  (See Opposition, p.7:3-5).  Martinez could have received additional training and/or experience subsequent to her employment with JB Hunt Transport Services, Inc. and before or during her employment with Defendants National Retail Transportation, Inc. and National Retail Systems, Inc. 

 

Martinez’s qualifications and training while she was employed at JB Hunt Transport Services, Inc., a non-party to this case, is not relevant to the liability of the Defendants’ in this case.  As such, Plaintiffs have failed to justify the subject subpoena’s unreasonable invasion into Martinez’s privacy.   

 

CONCLUSION

The motion is granted. 

 

 


Case Number: 24CHCV04826    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 9/11/26

Case #24CHCV04826

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES

(Form Interrogatories, Set 1)

 

Motion filed on 3/26/26. 

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Horace Ting

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Nathan Gonzalez

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Plaintiff Nathan Gonzalez to provide further responses, without objections, to Form Interrogatories, Set 1, within 15 days.  Additionally, Defendant Horace Ting requests an order that Plaintiff and/or Plaintiff’s attorney, Abdul ‘Sal’ Wakil, pay Defendant sanctions in the amount of $869.95.

 

RULING: The motion is placed off calendar. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of an automobile accident that occurred on 1/8/23.  On 12/31/24, Plaintiffs Saleen Delgado and Nathan Gonzalez filed this action against Defendant Horace Ting (Defendant) seeking to recover for personal injuries and damages allegedly resulting from the accident.  On 2/26/25, Defendant answered the complaint.

 

On 2/26/25, Defendant, electronically served Plaintiff Nathan Gonzalez (Plaintiff) with Form Interrogatories, Set 1.  Plaintiff failed to serve responses to the discovery resulting in Defendant filing a motion to compel Plaintiff to serve verified responses without objection.  On 11/21/25, the Court granted the motion and ordered Plaintiff to serve verified responses, without objections, on or before 11/27/25.  (See 11/21/25 Minute Order).  Defendant contends that Plaintiff served responses on 12/8/25 by emailing the responses to email addresses which were not designated by Defendant’s counsel for electronic service in this action and Defendant did not consent to receive service at those addresses.  (See Motion, p.2:9-15, p.4:8-10, p.5:2-7, p.5:17-p.6:27; Hovsepian Decl. ¶4).  Defendant claims to have not become aware of the service of the responses until 2/17/26.  Thereafter, Defendant claims to have sent a meet and confer letter regarding deficiencies in the responses.  (Hovsepian Decl. ¶5).  Plaintiff failed to provide further responses. 

 

On 3/26/26, Defendant filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling Plaintiff to provide further responses, without objections, to Form Interrogatories, Set 1, within 15 days.  Additionally, Defendant requests an order that Plaintiff and/or Plaintiff’s attorney, Abdul ‘Sal’ Wakil, pay Defendant sanctions in the amount of $869.95.

 

No opposition or other response to the motion has been filed.

 

ANALYSIS

 

The proofs of service attached to the motion, separate statement and proposed order show that the documents were electronically served on Plaintiff’s counsel at: sal@sawlawgroup.com However, the email address of record for Plaintiff’s counsel as shown in eCourt is: info@sawlawgroup.com.

 

Considering Defendant’s argument that the service of the responses on 12/8/25 was not valid because the email was not sent to the email address designated by Defendant’s counsel for electronic service in this action, the Court cannot find that the service of the motion on Plaintiff’s counsel at sal@sawlawgroup.com is valid service since it is not the email address designated by Plaintiff’s counsel in eCourt, despite the fact that it is listed on the complaint and on other documents filed by Plaintiff’s counsel in this action.

 

Moreover, this is not the first time there have been issues with the service of motions by defense counsel on Plaintiff.  (See 11/21/25 Minute Order).  Notably, the Defendant’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions and Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions Admitted filed and served on 2/17/26 were served on Plaintiff’s counsel at both sal@sawlawgroup.com and info@sawlawgroup.com.  However, as noted above, the instant motion was only served on Plaintiff’s counsel at sal@sawlawgroup.com, which is not Plaintiff’s counsel’s email address of record in this action. 

 

There is no opposition or other response to the motion by Plaintiff to cure the defect in notice.

 

The Court further notes that the none of the exhibits referenced in the declaration of attorney Hovsepian and claimed to be attached thereto are attached

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is placed off calendar.
