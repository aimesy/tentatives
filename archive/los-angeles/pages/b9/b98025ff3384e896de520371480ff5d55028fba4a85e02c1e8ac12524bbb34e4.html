TENTATIVE RULING

 

The Court hereby GRANTS final approval and awards/approves the following:

(1) $47,250 for attorney fees to Class Counsel, Lipeles Law Group, APC; (2) $6,575.06 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) enhancement payment of $7,500 to the class representative, Devon Bethune; (4) $8,775 (65% of $13,500 PAGA penalty) to the LWDA; and (5) $4,950 for settlement administration costs to Phoenix Settlement Administrators.

 

Plaintiff’s counsel shall submit a proposed Order and Judgment, consistent with this ruling containing all requisite terms, including the class definition, release language, and a statement that no class members requested exclusion. Plaintiff shall also file with the Court the Motion for Attorney’s Fees which was submitted and served via Case Anywhere but not filed with the Court, as is required. 

 

The Court orders class counsel to file a final report summarizing all distributions made pursuant to the approved settlement, supported by declaration. The Court will set a non-appearance date of _____________________________ for review of the final report, which is ordered to be filed at least five court days beforehand.

 

BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff Devon Bethune sued his former employer, Defendant Valley Light Center for Social Advancement, for alleged wage and hour violations. Plaintiff seeks to represent a class of Defendant’s current and former non-exempt employees.

On November 6, 2024, Plaintiff filed his Class Action Complaint against Defendant for: (1) failure to pay overtime (Cal. Labor Code §§ 510, 511, 558, 1194, 1198; IWC Wage Order No. 4); (2) failure to pay minimum wages (Cal. Labor Code §§ 1194, 1197, 1197.1, 1198; IWC Wage Order No. 4); (3) failure to pay for meal periods not provided (Cal. Labor Code §226.7; IWC Wage Order No. 4); (4) failure to pay for rest periods not provided (Cal. Labor Code §226.7; IWC Wage Order No. 4); (5) failure to maintain records and provide accurate itemized wage statements (Cal. Labor Code § 226); (6) failure to pay wages due on termination (Cal. Labor Code §§ 201-203); (7) failure to reimburse business expenses (Cal. Labor Code § 2802); (8) failure to allow inspection of employment records (Cal. Labor Code § 1198.5); and (9) unfair/unlawful business practices (Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code §§ 17200 et seq.).

On January 23, 2025, Plaintiff filed his First Amended Class Action Complaint (“FAC”) in the Los Angeles Superior Court, adding a claim pursuant to the Private Attorney General Act of 2004, Cal. Lab. Code §§ 2699 (PAGA), et seq.

On July 17, 2025, the parties virtually attended via Zoom an all-day mediation with mediator Lindsey Bayman, Esq., which ultimately resulted in settlement. The terms of settlement were finalized in the long-form Class Action and PAGA Settlement Agreement, a copy of which was filed with the Court on January 26, 2026. 

On April 16, 2026, the Court granted preliminary approval of the settlement.

Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered (see Declaration of Connor Tevenan (“Tevenan Decl.”).) Now before the Court is the Motion for Final Approval of the Settlement. Plaintiff’s separate Motion for Attorney’s Fees was submitted and served via Case Anywhere but not filed with the Court, as is required.

ANALYSIS OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

 

A.     Does a presumption of fairness exist? 

The Court preliminarily found in its Order of April 16, 2026 that the presumption of fairness should be applied.  No facts have come to the Court’s attention that would alter that preliminary conclusion.  Accordingly, the settlement is entitled to a presumption of fairness as set forth in the preliminary approval order.

B.      Is the settlement fair, adequate, and reasonable?

The settlement was preliminarily found to be fair, adequate and reasonable.  Notice has now been given to the Class and the LWDA. 

  Reaction of the class members to the proposed settlement.

Number of class members: 53 (Tevenan Decl., ¶3.)   

Number of notice packets mailed: 53 (Id. at ¶5.)    

Number of undeliverable notices: 0 (Id. at ¶7.)  

Number of opt-outs: 0 (Id. at ¶8.)

Number of objections: 0 (Id. at ¶9.)

Number of participating class members: 53 (Id. at ¶11.)

Average individual payment: $994.81 (Id. at ¶14.)

Highest individual payment: $1,865.18 (Ibid.)

The Court finds that the notice was given as directed and conforms to due process requirements.  Given the reactions of the Class Members and the LWDA to the proposed settlement and for the reasons set for in the Preliminary Approval order, the settlement is found to be fair, adequate, and reasonable.

 

C.      Attorney Fees and Costs

            Class Counsel requests an award of $47,250 (35%) in fees and $6,575.06 in costs. (Memo ISO Class Counsel’s at 1:27-2:1.) The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $47,250 (35%) in fees and $10,000 in costs (¶3.2.2).

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 254.)  Here, class counsel request attorney fees using the percentage method, as crosschecked by lodestar. (Memo ISO Class Counsel’s at pp. 1-15.)

In common fund cases, the Court may employ a percentage of the benefit method, as cross-checked against the lodestar. (Laffitte v. Robert Half Int’l, Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.5th 480, 503.) The fee request represents 35% of the gross settlement amount, which is above the average generally awarded in class actions.  (See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 [“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”].)

Counsel’s percentage-based fee request is lower than the unadjusted lodestar, and would represent application of a multiplier of approximately 0.47x.

The 35% fee request is at the higher end of percentages ordinarily approved in cases of this type. Here, there will be a delay in payment of the fees as this settlement is funded in installments. Further, the fee award amounts to less than one-half of the fees actually expended in favor to the class and aggrieved parties.  Notice of the fee request was provided to class members in the notice packet and no one objected. (Tevenan Decl., ¶9, Exhibit A.) Accordingly, the Court awards fees in the amount of $47,250. 

 

As for costs, Class Counsel is requesting a cost amount of $6,575.06. This is less than the $10,000 cap estimated at preliminary approval, which was not objected to. (Tevenan Decl., ¶9, Exhibit A.) Counsel represents that their costs include, but are not limited to: filing and service fees, Case Anywhere, and expert fees. (Schelly Decl. ISO Final, ¶53, Exhibit B.) The costs appear to be reasonable in amount and reasonably necessary to this litigation.

 

Based on the above, the recommendation is to award $47,250 in fees and $6,575.06 in costs.

 

D.     Incentive Award

The class representative, Devon Bethune, seeks an enhancement payment of $10,000 for his contributions to the action. (Memo ISO Class Counsel’s Fees at 15:9.)    

In connection with the final fairness hearing, named Plaintiffs must submit declarations attesting to why they should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.  The named Plaintiffs must explain why they “should be compensated for the expense or risk he has incurred in conferring a benefit on other members of the class.”  (Clark v. American Residential Services LLC (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 785, 806.)  Trial courts should not sanction enhancement awards of thousands of dollars with “nothing more than pro forma claims as to ‘countless’ hours expended, ‘potential stigma’ and ‘potential risk.’ Significantly more specificity, in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of financial or other risks incurred by the named plaintiffs, is required in order for the trial court to conclude that an enhancement was ‘necessary to induce [the named plaintiff] to participate in the suit . . . .’”  (Id. at 806-807, italics and ellipsis in original.)

Plaintiff represents that his contributions to this litigation include: participating in interviews and phone conferences, producing documents, reviewing pleadings, reviewing documents and data provided by Defendant, keeping in contact with his attorneys regarding the case status, and remotely attending the mediation. He estimates spending 30 hours on the case. (Declaration of Devon Bethune ISO Final, ¶6.)

Based on the above, as well as the benefits obtained on behalf of the class, the Court grants an enhancement payment in the amount of $7,500 to Plaintiff.  Plaintiff’s contribution to this litigation are admirable but not so substantial as to warrant a higher award.

 

E.      Settlement Administration Costs

            The settlement administrator, Phoenix Settlement Administrators, is requesting $4,950 for the costs of settlement administration. (Tevenan Decl., ¶17.) This equals the estimate of $4,950 provided for in the Settlement Agreement (¶3.2.3) and disclosed to class members in the Notice, to which there were no objections. (Tevenan Decl., ¶9, Exhibit A.) Based on the above, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $4,950.
