Case Number: 21PSCV00617    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: G

Defendants Fleet Capital, Inc., Inco Builders, and Sam Ostayan’s Motion for an Order Awarding Fees and Release of Plaintiff Diana Culkin’s Bond

Respondent: Plaintiff Diana Culkin

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants Fleet Capital, Inc., Inco Builders, and Sam Ostayan’s Motion for an Order Awarding Fees and Release of Plaintiff Diana Culkin’s Bond is GRANTED in a reduced amount.  Defendants Fleet Capital, Inc., Inco Builders, and Sam Ostayan are AWARDED a total of $5,635.00.

BACKGROUND

This is a wrongful foreclosure action.  On July 30, 2021, plaintiff Diana Culkin (Culkin) filed the Complaint.  On February 24, 2023, Culkin filed the operative Second Amended Complaint, alleging causes of action for (1) violation of California’s nonjudicial foreclosure statutes and (2) wrongful foreclosure.  (On May 4, 2023, the court sustained a demurrer without leave to amend and granted a motion to strike affecting all other causes of action in the Second Amended Complaint.)

On February 9, 2022, defendant Inco Builders filed the Cross-Complaint, alleging causes of action for (1) trespass and (2) slander of title.

On October 29, 2024, the court issued the Judgment in favor of defendants Fleet Capital, Inc., Sam Ostayan, and Inco Builders (collectively, Defendants).  On December 16, 2024, Culkin appealed the court’s Judgment.  On September 22, 2025, the Court of Appeal affirmed the court’s Judgment.  On December 10, 2025, the Supreme Court declined to review the appeal.

On February 26, 2026, Culkin filed a “motion to set aside,” seeking reconsideration and the vacating of the court’s judgment.  On April 2, 2026, the court denied the motion to set aside.

On April 9, 2026, Culkin filed a motion to vacate judgment, seeking the same relief based on the substantially similar arguments as the previous motion to set aside.  On May 14, 2026, the court declared Culkin a vexatious litigant and required her to post $5,000.00 bond before it considered the motion.  On May 21, 2026, Culkin posted bond.  On June 10, 2026, the court denied the motion to vacate judgment.

On June 16, 2026, Defendants filed this motion for release of bond and fees.  On July 14, 2026, Culkin filed the opposition, and on July 15, 2026, the supervising judge permitted the opposition.  On August 6, 2026, Defendants filed the reply.

This motion is set for hearing on August 13, 2026.

ANALYSIS

Defendants seek the release of Culkin’s vexatious litigant bond and attorney’s fees based on Culkin’s unsuccessful motion to vacate judgment.  For the following reasons, the motion is GRANTED in a reduced amount.

Legal Standard

“[I]f, after hearing the evidence upon the motion, the court determines that the plaintiff is a vexatious litigant and that there is no reasonable probability that the plaintiff will prevail in the litigation against the moving defendant, [then] the court shall order the plaintiff to furnish, for the benefit of the moving defendant, security in such amount and within such time as the court shall fix.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.3, subd. (a).)  The statute defines “security” as “an undertaking to assure payment, to the party for whose benefit the undertaking is required to be furnished, of the party’s reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees and not limited to taxable costs, incurred in or in connection with a litigation instituted, caused to be instituted, or maintained or caused to be maintained by a vexatious litigant.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 391, subd. (c).)

Discussion

The court declared Culkin a vexatious litigant on May 14, 2026 and required her to post $5,000.00 bond.  (See 5/14/2026 Min. Order, p. 1.)  The purpose of the bond is to defend against frivolity from a vexatious litigant “for the benefit of the moving defendant.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.3, subd. (a); see also Holcomb v. U.S. Bank Nat. Assn. (2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 1494, 1504, holding, “The vexatious litigant statutes were enacted to require a person found a vexatious litigant to put up security for the reasonable expenses of a defendant who becomes the target of one of these obsessive and persistent litigants whose conduct can cause serious financial results to the unfortunate object of his attack.  The purpose of the statutory scheme is to deal with the problem created by the persistent and obsessive litigant. . . .”)  The court denied the motion to vacate judgment on June 10, 2026.  (See 6/10/2026 Min. Order, p. 1.)  In denying the motion, the court found that Culkin reasserted previously made allegations of litigation misconduct.  Culkin did not base her motion to vacate judgment on “new or different facts, circumstances, or law.”  (6/10/2026 Order Re: Court’s Final Ruling, p. 2, quoting Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (b).)  Thus, the court finds Defendants are entitled to the $5,000.00 bond under Section 391.3, subdivision (a).

Defendants also request $635.00 incurred in opposing the motion to vacate judgment and in connection with this motion for release of bond and fees.  (See Mot., p. 1.)  However, the court finds that the purpose of the $5,000.00 bond is to cover Defendants’ reasonable expenses, and there is no reason to require Culkin to pay any additional amount where the bond amount sufficiently covers Defendants’ expenses.  Thus, the court does not award an additional $635.00 for attorney’s fees.

Therefore, the motion for release of bond and fees are GRANTED in the reduced amount of $5,000.00.

CONCLUSION

For these reasons, the motion for an order awarding fees and release of bond is GRANTED in a reduced amount.  Defendants Fleet Capital, Inc., Inco Builders, and Sam Ostayan are AWARDED a total of $5,000.00.

Case Number: 23PSCV04012    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: G

Plaintiff/Petitioner Adriana Leticia Chavez Haro’s Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Plaintiff/Claimant Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez, a Minor

Respondent: NO OPPOSITION

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff/Petitioner Adriana Leticia Chavez Haro’s Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Plaintiff/Claimant Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez, a Minor is GRANTED.

BACKGROUND

This is a wrongful death action.  On May 31, 2023, defendants Kaiser Foundation Hospitals and Southern California Permanente Medical Group (collectively, Kaiser Permanente) allegedly admitted decedent Sergio Rodriguez Jimenez at the Kaiser Permanente Ontario Medical Center (Kaiser Ontario) for severe anxiety and suicidal ideation.  On June 1, 2023, Kaiser Ontario allegedly transferred Sergio Rodriguez Jimenez to nonparty Aurora Charter Oaks Behavioral Health Center (Aurora) via an ambulance that was allegedly owned and operated by defendants American Medical Response Ambulance Service, Inc. (AMR Ambulance Service), American Medical Response of Inland Empire, Inc. (AMR Inland Empire), American Medical Response of Southern California, Inc. (AMR Southern California), American Medical Response West, Inc. (AMR West), American Medical Response, Inc. (AMR), and Global Medical Response, Inc. (GMR).  Sergio Rodriguez Jimenez allegedly died by suicide from self-inflicted wounds to his neck with a pair of scissors from the ambulance.  Sergio Rodriguez Jimenez is survived by plaintiff Desiree Montserrat Rodriguez Chavez, plaintiff Jennifer Sofia Rodriguez Chavez, plaintiff/claimant Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez, and plaintiff/petitioner Adriana Leticia Chavez Haro (collectively, the Family).

On December 29, 2023, the Family filed the Complaint, alleging causes of action for (1) wrongful death, (2) negligent infliction of emotional distress (by Jennifer Sofia Rodriguez Chavez), (3) negligent infliction of emotional distress (by Adriana Leticia Chavez Haro), and (4) survival.

On May 5, 2026, the parties informed the court that they settled the case.  On July 6, 2026, Adriana Leticia Chavez Haro filed this petition for approval of minor’s compromise, which is unopposed.

This case is set for an order to show cause re: dismissal (settlement) and a hearing on the petition on August 13, 2026.

ANALYSIS

Adriana Leticia Chavez Haro seeks the court’s approval of a settlement reached between all parties.  For the following reasons, the petition is GRANTED.

Legal Standard

An enforceable settlement of a minor’s or incompetent’s claim can only be consummated with court approval.  (Prob. Code, §§ 2504, 3500, 3600 et seq.; Code Civ. Proc., § 372.)  For this purpose, a petition for approval must be presented to the court, and until it is granted, there is no final settlement.  (Scruton v. Korean Air Lines Co. (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 1596, 1603–1606.)  Any settlement agreement therefore is voidable by the minor’s guardian ad litem.  (Id. at 1606.)

Probate Code section 3600 et seq. governs how the settlement proceeds are to be paid.  Pursuant to Probate Code section 3601, the order shall approve payment of reasonable expenses from the settlement as follows:

The court making the order or giving the judgment referred to in Section 3600, as a part thereof, shall make a further order authorizing and directing that such reasonable expenses (medical or otherwise and including reimbursement to a parent, guardian, or conservator), costs, and attorney’s fees, as the court shall approve and allow therein, shall be paid from the money or other property to be paid or delivered for the benefit of the minor or incompetent person.  (Prob. Code, § 3601, subd. (a).)

In cases pursuant to Probate Code section 3601, “unless the court has approved the fee agreement in advance, the court must use a reasonable fee standard when approving and allowing the amount of attorney’s fees payable from money or property paid or to be paid for the benefit of a minor or a person with a disability.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.955(a)(1).)  In doing so, “[t]he court must give consideration to the terms of any representation agreement made between the attorney and the representative of the minor or person with a disability and must evaluate the agreement based on the facts and circumstances existing at the time the agreement was made, except where the attorney and the representative of the minor or person with a disability contemplated that the attorney’s fee would be affected by later events.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.955(a)(2).)

Discussion

Pursuant to the settlement agreement reached between the parties, Kaiser Permanente, AMR, and AMR Southern California agree to pay a total of $75,000.00 to Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez.  (See Pet., ¶ 10.)  The petition does not request that any of the settlement funds for Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez be used for medical expenses, attorney’s fees, or other expenses.  (See Pet., ¶¶ 12–16.)

“In determining whether to grant a guardian ad litem’s petition to approve a settlement of the ward’s claims, a court must determine whether it is reasonable and in the minor’s best interest.”  (Chui v. Chui (2022) 75 Cal.App.5th 873, 899.)  Generally, courts must consider the following factors: 1) the circumstances of the accident or other incident giving rise to the litigation, particularly the facts bearing on the payor’s potential liability and the minor's damages; 2) the amount of the proposed settlement; 3) how the settlement will be paid (e.g. in a lump sum or via an annuity); 4) the nature of the injury and the minor’s current medical status and prognosis; and 5) the amount of attorneys’ fees and other transaction costs being requested.  (See, e.g., Kostakos v. Holewinski (Dec. 6, 2019, 37-2018-00029286-CU-PA-CTL) [nonpub. opn.], applying Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.)

Here, the entire $75,000.00 for Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez will be deposited in insured accounts in a financial institution in this state, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the court.  (See Pet., ¶ 18b(2); see also Pet., Attach. 18b(2).)  Upon review, the court finds the amount awarded to Sergio Jonathan Steve Rodriguez Chavez to be reasonable and finds the disposition of the funds is proper.

Accordingly, the petition to confirm minor’s compromise is GRANTED.

CONCLUSION

For these reasons, the petition for approval of minor’s compromise is GRANTED.
