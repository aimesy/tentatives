DEPARTMENT 400 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV12736    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 400

            The Court tenders the following tentative decision in the matter Kim Nguyen v. Natay Solomon, et al., Los Angeles County Superior Court case number 24STCV12736, set for hearing on September 15, 2026.  Maxim Healthcare Services, Inc. and Adam Lavoy (collectively, Defendants) move for orders compelling Liberty Behavioral & Community Services, Inc. (Liberty), Jaynolan Community Services (Jaynolan), California Care 4U / Bernard R. Schwan (CC4U), and California Psychare (CP) to comply with deposition subpoenas for the production of business records. No opposition brief has been filed in response to any of the present motions. The Court will address each matter in turn.

 

 

Defendants’ motions to compel are granted.

 

Defendants move to compel production of certain documents requested from third parties Liberty, Jaynolan, CC4U, and CP pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1. “If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court . . . may make an order . . . directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).) Production was due on July 9, 2026, but the documents were not produced. (Vejtasa Decl. ¶ 9.) Thus, the present motions are proper.

 

Defendants seek Natay Solomon’s prior records from other care centers. They argue that this information is relevant because it will help to determine which parties had knowledge of Natay’s prior conduct and tendencies before the incident, establishing whether the relevant incident was foreseeable to various parties. Natay has previously authorized the disclosure of records from Kaiser, LAUSD, The Help Group, and the Regional Center, indicating that he has waived any right to privacy concerning these behavioral records. Indeed, Defendants have not received any objections to the relevant subpoenas from counsel for Natay or Plaintiff. (Id. Decl. ¶ 8.) Accordingly, the present motions to compel are granted.

 

Conclusion:

 

             The Court grants Defendants’ motions to compel.  Documents to be produced within 15 days.
