20STCV23910 Dupree, et al. v. County of Los Angeles, et al.

August 7, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PETITION FOR APPROVAL OF MINORS’ COMPROMISE OF A CLAIM

 

 

      Plaintiffs are heirs and survivors of Tyson Martin, who was killed by nominal defendant, Wendell Martin. The third amended complaint alleges Defendants were negligent in failing to remove Tyson from his home, where he was sexually and physically abused. The minor Plaintiff, S.M., suffered injuries from sexual and physical abuse.

      The parties settled this case to be distributed to Tyson’s mother, Bridgette Dupree in the amount of $745,000, and $50,000 to S.M. to be deposited into a blocked account.

      S.M. sought therapy from Jon R. Conte Ph.D., who evaluated her for psychological damage. SM currently does not present with symptoms of trauma or extreme stress (.pdf p. 20.) Medi-Cal paid for medical expenses incurred for therapy and has agreed to take $9,710.01 to settle its lien. (Att. 12.)

      A minor’s settlement is valid if approved by the court after a hearing.  (Prob. Code, §§3505, 3500.) Plaintiffs’ counsel will not deduct costs but will take $10,000 in attorney’s fees (20 percent), which the Court finds reasonable, leaving a net settlement amount of  $30,289.99, to be deposited into a blocked account.

Petitioner and Claimant must appear at the hearing, unless the Court finds good cause to excuse their appearance. (Cal. Rules of Court, 7.952.) Subject to a hearing, the Court is inclined to grant the petition.

                                                           



Case Number: 24STCV31395    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV31395 Perez v. Galvan

Friday, August 7, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER TAKING OFF CALENDAR DEMURRER AND MOTION TO STRIKE CROSS COMPLAINANT’S VERIFIED CROSS-COMPLAINT (9477).  ORDER SETTING ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: STRIKING OR VACATING THE DEFAULT

 

      The Demurrer to the Cross-Complaint and Motion to Strike filed by Cross-Defendant, Jose Perez, (Cross-Defendant or Perez) is procedurally defective. Cross-Complainant filed the Cross-Complaint on August 25, 2025. As Cross-Defendant did not respond to the pleading, the clerk entered default against Cross-Defendant on October 16, 2025.

      Cross-Defendant successfully moved to vacate default when the Court granted Cross-Defendant’s motion and vacated the October 16, 2025, default. (M.O. 3.26.26.) The Court independently reviewed the Court’s electronic file and determined that the clerk entered Cross-Defendant’s default on May 7, 2026. Neither party addressed the second entry of default in the papers submitted in connection with the demurrer and motion to strike.

      Although Cross-Defendant attached a proposed demurrer to his prior motion for relief from default, the proposed pleading was not separately filed. The Court’s March 26, 2026, order vacating the original default neither deemed the proposed demurrer filed nor directed the clerk to file it. Cross-Defendant thereafter filed the present demurrer and motion to strike on May 8, 2026, one day after the clerk entered the second default.

      A defaulting party may not file or prosecute a demurrer while the default remains in effect. The subsequent filing of an answer or demurrer is unauthorized unless the default is first set aside. (A & B Metal Products v. MacArthur Properties, Inc. (1970) 11 Cal.App.3d 642, 647.) Accordingly, the Court cannot presently consider the merits of the demurrer or motion to strike.

      The circumstances surrounding the second entry of default, however, require further inquiry. Cross-Defendant had appeared through counsel, successfully moved to vacate the original default, and attached to that motion the proposed demurrer he intended to file. Cross-Complainant therefore knew that Cross-Defendant intended to defend the cross-complaint. The Court’s March 26, 2026, order did not specify when Cross-Defendant was required to file the proposed responsive pleading. Forty-one days after notice of that order was served, Cross-Complainant obtained entry of Cross-Defendant’s default. Cross-Defendant filed the contemplated demurrer the following day.

      The record presently before the Court does not disclose whether Cross-Complainant warned Cross-Defendant’s counsel that Cross-Complainant intended to request entry of default or otherwise provided counsel a final opportunity to file the responsive pleading. The Court therefore makes no finding on that issue at this time. If Cross-Complainant sought entry of default without reasonable advance warning to counsel known to represent a party who intended to defend the action, however, such conduct would implicate the principles discussed in Fasuyi v. Permatex, Inc. (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 681, 701–702, LaSalle v. Vogel (2019) 36 Cal.App.5th 127, 134–137, and McClain v. Kissler (2019) 39 Cal.App.5th 399. Those authorities recognize an ethical obligation to warn opposing counsel before seeking default and disapprove efforts to obtain a tactical advantage through entry of default without reasonable notice.

The Court also recognizes that Cross-Defendant did not timely file the proposed demurrer after obtaining relief from the original default. The attachment of a proposed pleading to a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 473 does not itself constitute the filing of that pleading, and Cross-Defendant was not entitled to wait indefinitely merely because the March 26, 2026, order did not specify a deadline. The present record therefore raises questions concerning the conduct and diligence of both parties.

      A defaulting defendant’s right to participate in the litigation is terminated, "and the subsequent filing of an answer or demurrer on his part is unauthorized and void, unless upon proceedings duly had, the default is first set aside." (A & B Metal Products v. MacArthur Properties, Inc. (1970) 11 Cal.App.3d 642, 647.) Accordingly, the demurrer and motion to strike are taken off calendar pending resolution of the second entry of default. The Court sets an Order to Show Cause regarding whether the May 7, 2026, entry of default should be vacated or stricken for December 1, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department 414.  Cross-Complainant shall file and serve a declaration stating: (1) whether Cross-Complainant or counsel notified Cross-Defendant or his counsel, before requesting entry of default, that Cross-Complainant intended to seek default; (2) the date, manner, and substance of any such notice; (3) whether Cross-Defendant or counsel responded; and (4) any other facts relevant to the request for entry of default. Cross-Defendant shall file and serve a declaration stating: (1) when Cross-Defendant and counsel received notice of the March 26, 2026 order; (2) why the proposed demurrer was not filed before May 8, 2026; (3) whether Cross-Complainant or counsel warned that a default would be requested; (4) the date, manner, and substance of any communications concerning the responsive pleading or default; and (5) any other facts relevant to whether the May 7, 2026 default should remain in effect. In lieu of the Order to Show Cause, the parties may submit a stipulated order vacating the May 7, 2026,  entry of default and resetting the demurrer and motion to strike for hearing on the Court's regular law and motion calendar.  Upon approval of the stipulation, the Order to Show Cause will be vacated.

     

     

 

       


Case Number: 25STCV11413    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV11413 Ofelia Britt v. Nissan North America, Inc.

Friday, August 7, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL DISCLOSURES AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 871.26 (7324)

 

I.         BACKGROUND

      This action arises from alleged violations of the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act in connection with a warranty contract issued by Defendant manufacturer for a 2021 Nissan Sentra.

      Plaintiff argues Defendant failed to disclose initial documents as required by statute which were due on October 12, 2025. Plaintiff asks for an order compelling Defendant’s production of documents and issuing mandatory sanctions of $2,500 against Defendant, Nissan North America, Inc.

      As of August 5, 2026, Plaintiff did not file a reply brief.

II.        LEGAL STANDARDS

      The statute at issue, which became effective January 1, 2025, and applies to this case, requires both parties to disclose documents 60 days after the answer or responsive pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(b); (h)(1). Plaintiff met and conferred by email, however, Defendant did not comply. (Hill decl., Ex. 1.)    

      The court “shall impose” sanctions of $2,500 against the Defendant’s attorney to be paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements unless good cause is shown. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26 (j)(1).)

III.       DISCUSSION

      Defendant states that the disclosure was due October 13, 2025, however, contends the parties agreed to suspend their mutual obligations temporarily while the parties pursued informal resolution of this action.

      Following receipt of this motion, Defendant prepared and served initial disclosures and document production on June 23, 2026. Defendant contends there is nothing for the Court to compel. Defendant asserts that Plaintiff improperly expands the scope of documents required to be produced.  

      There is no record of an agreement to suspend disclosures. Defendant refers to an email exchange which does not document an agreement to pause disclosure obligations. (Hill decl., Ex. 1.)  Rather, Plaintiff’s counsel informed Defendant that initial disclosures were past due in 463 cases. (Id.) Moreover, nothing in the statute permits the parties to avoid their mandatory obligations by stipulation.

      Defendant has not shown good cause for the delay. Defendant has since complied with its obligations. (Sweeney decl., Ex. A, B.). Defendant contends that the disclosure requirement is not mandatory because there is an exception: “unless the party failing to comply shows good cause.” (Opp.  8:1-4; Cal. Code Civ. Procedure §871.26 (j).) Defendant has not shown good cause. Additionally, imposition of sanctions is mandatory regardless of whether Defendant was acting in good faith.

IV.       CONCLUSION

      As Defendant has since untimely served its initial disclosures of documents, an order compelling their production is no longer necessary. However, the court imposes sanctions of $2,500 against Defendant’s attorney payable to Plaintiff within 15 business days. (Cal. Code Civ. Procedure § 871.26(j)(1).)

Case Number: 25STCV23355    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV23355 Pedro Armando Serna v. Christian Guardado, et al.

Friday, August 7, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER TAKING OFF CALENDAR PLAINTIFF, PEDRO ARMANDO SERNA’S, MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO PLAINTIFF’S DEMAND FOR INSPECTION AND PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE, PROPOUNDED ON DEFENDANT, LOURDES ANGUIANA; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS (6334)

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF, PEDRO ARMANDO SERNA’S, MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO PLAINTIFF’S DEMAND FOR INSPECTION AND PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE, PROPOUNDED ON DEFENDANT, CHRISTIAN GUARDADO; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS (1980)

 

I.       ARGUMENTS

      This action arises from an alleged motor vehicle accident. Plaintiff requests an order to compel further responses to document requests served on Defendants, Christian Guardado and Lourdes Anguiano and to impose sanctions.

      The court has reviewed both motions, oppositions, and replies thereto, separate statements and all declarations in support.

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      A motion to compel further responses to a document request is proper where the requesting party believes the statement of compliance is incomplete or a representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive and/or an objection in the response is without merit or too general.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310.) The moving party is required to submit a declaration attesting that the parties met and conferred prior to filing the motion. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.220.)

III.    DISCUSSION

      On June 3, 2026, the parties submitted a joint separate statement.  The only requests remaining at issue are Requests for Production of Documents Nos. 15 and 33, which request Guardado’s cellular phone records in the moments leading up to, during, and shortly after the collision to determine whether Guardado was contemporaneously using his cell phone at the time of the collision.

      The Court presumes that the discovery at issue consists of Requests for Production of Documents Nos. 15 and 33 propounded to Defendant Christian Guardado. As described in the joint separate statement, those requests seek Guardado's cellular phone records. In contrast, Request for Production No. 15 propounded to Defendant Lourdes Anguiano does not seek cell phone records; it requests documents upon which Anguiano relied or referenced in responding to Plaintiff's Special Interrogatories, Set One. Plaintiff did not propound Request for Production No. 33 to Anguiano. Accordingly, the Court construes the present motion as directed solely to Guardado's Requests for Production Nos. 15 and 33. 

      RPD 15 addressed to Christian Guardado requests any and all cell phone records (to include but not limited to phone bills) which relate to the incident, within 30 minutes before or 30 minutes after the incident. Defendant responded with objections. In his opposition, Guardado argued that Plaintiff must present case-specific facts why Defendant’s telephone use has any probative value and case-specific facts about the accident that distracted driving was a potential contributing factor to this accident. (Opp. 8:13-20.)

      Plaintiff alleges a claim for negligence, asserting the driver negligently operated the car. Plaintiff is entitled to discover all the conduct constituting negligence substantially contributed to the incident that caused damage to Plaintiff. The complaint provides sufficient “specific” facts to warrant discovery of the records.

      RPD 33 requests documents relating to any cellular phone use by Defendant within ten

minutes of the incident. This is equally discoverable for the same reasons.

      Defendants contend in the joint separate statement that “Defendants no longer use Verizon as their cellular service provider and have tried but have been unable to access the records of their old account with Verizon.” (Jt SS, .pdf 3:2-4.) Defendant did not provide this response under penalty of perjury. Rather Defendant objected to the request.  The statute requires the responding party to affirm, where the party is unable to comply with the request, that the item is not “in the possession, custody, or control of the responding party” and provide the address of the person who does have the documents. (Cal. Code Civ. Procedure § § 2031.280.)

      Plaintiff files a supplemental declaration stating that counsel has since been in contact with counsel for Cellco Partnership d/b/a Verizon Wireless (“Verizon”) regarding the subpoenaed records responsive to Requests for Production Nos. 15 and 33. Verizon advised it could not release the records without Defendant’s signature and authorization. (Supp Meagle decl., ¶ 2.) This confirms that the records are available and remain in Defendants’ control contrary to Defendants’ claim.  Requests 15 and 33 seek relevant and discoverable records.

      Plaintiff is entitled to an award of sanctions because Defendant’s objections do not have merit, nor has Defendant shown substantial justification for objecting and refusing to produce the records. (Cal. Code Civ. Procedure § 2023.030.) Plaintiff spent and will have spent 11 hours to prepare the Motion re Christian, the reply, and to appear. Plaintiff’s hourly rate of $400 is reasonable. Accordingly, sanctions of $4,460 is imposes plus $60 in filing fees. (Zheng decl., ¶ 10.)

IV.   CONCLUSION

      The parties appear to have resolved the requests propounded on Lourdes because the remaining requests about cell records were propounded to Christian. Therefore, the motion regarding Anguiana’s responses is taken off calendar (6334).

      The motion to compel further responses to Request for Production of Documents served on Christian Guardado is GRANTED. Defendant is ordered to serve verified, code-compliant responses to requests 15 and 33 without objection and production of documents responsive to the request within 20 days. The Court imposes sanctions of $4,460 in fees and $60 in costs against Defendant Christian Guardado and his counsel Courtney D. Flannery & Associates, jointly and severally, payable to Plaintiff within 20 days.


Case Number: 25STCV23431    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV23431 Borunda v. American Honda Motor Co., Inc.

Friday, August 7, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT, AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.’S, MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S INITIAL RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE, AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (8391)

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT, AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.’S, MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S INITIAL RESPONSES TO SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE, AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (8960)

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT, AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.’S, MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S INITIAL RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE, AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (3475)

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING IN PART DEFENDANT, AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.’S, MOTION FOR AN ORDER THAT THE TRUTH OF MATTERS SPECIFIED IN DEFENDANT’S REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION, SET ONE ARE DEEMED ADMITTED BY PLAINTIFF AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (6735)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      This action alleges claims for violations under the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act in connection with a warranty issued by Defendant for a 2020 Honda Ridgeline.

      Defendant requests an order compelling Plaintiff to serve initial responses to discovery and for an order deeming admitted Plaintiff’s responses to requests for admission. Defendant served discovery on December 8, 2025, and Plaintiff failed to respond to date. Defendant requests imposition of sanctions incurred for each of four motions.

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      Where a party does not timely respond to interrogatories or a request for production of documents, the court has authority to compel a response. (Code Civ. Proc., §2030.290(b), 2031.020) Untimely responses result in a waiver of objections. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(a), Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300 (a).)

      Where a party fails to respond to requests for admission, the court can deem the requests admitted against the non-responding party unless it finds that the non-responding party has subsequently served before the hearing, a proposed response to the requests that substantially complies with statutory requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280 subd. (c)). Imposition of sanctions is mandatory where a party’s failure to respond to the requests for admission necessitates the motion. Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280 subd. (c).

III.    DISCUSSION

      Plaintiff does not dispute that responses were not timely served. Plaintiff has since served objections on January 16, 2026, and further verified responses on July 27, 2026. Therefore, Plaintiff contends that the motions are moot.

      Plaintiff seeks a waiver of objections. The Court has discretion to relieve a party from this waiver on its determination that both of the following conditions are satisfied: (1) the party has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance with statute, and (2) the failure to serve a timely response was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, §2030.290 (a).)

      To be clear, Defendant’s motions seek an order to compel initial responses, not further responses, as referenced in Plaintiff’s counsel’s declaration. Defendant’s reply briefs acknowledge Plaintiff’s untimely responses and argue that Plaintiff is not entitled to a waiver of objections. Defendant contends that the request is procedurally and subsequently defective, because Plaintiff did not file a motion for relief from waiver objections.

      The part of the respective statutes governing relief from waiver does not require a separately noticed motion for that relief. A separately noticed motion for relief from waiver for the same discovery at issue does not promote judicial efficiency. Defendant responded to that request in the reply briefs, all of which the Court has considered.

      The declaration of Lara Abdo does not state what occurred that could be considered mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. Moreover, the declaration addresses responses to Volkswagen Group of America, who is not a defendant in this matter. The motions do not request an order compelling further responses. Accordingly, the request for a waiver of objections is denied.

      As Plaintiff subsequently served verified responses to the requests for admission, the request for a deemed admitted order is denied.

      The motions are not moot as Defendant requests imposition of sanctions. Plaintiff has not shown substantial justification for failing to timely respond to discovery. The Court awards sanctions of $2,160 in fees and costs incurred for each of four motions.

IV.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s three motions to compel initial responses to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and request for production of documents are GRANTED. The Court does not deem the requests for admission admitted. Plaintiff is ordered to serve verified responses and production of documents without objections to all discovery at issue within 10 days.

      The Court imposes sanctions against Plaintiff and counsel, Strategic Legal Practices, APC, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,160 incurred for each of four motions [total $8640], payable to Defendant within 20 days.
