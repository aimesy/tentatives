Case Number: 22STCP02989    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Maricopa Meadows Homeowners Association’s Motion for Award of Attorney Fees and Costs is granted in the amount of $1,410.00 in attorney fees and $95.55 in costs.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On August 9, 2022, this Court entered as judgment a sister-state judgment from the County of Maricopa, State of Arizona in favor of Plaintiff / Judgment Creditor Maricopa Meadows Homeowners Association (“Judgment Creditor”) and against Defendants / Judgment Debtors Stephen S. Brown and Jane Doe Brown (“Judgment Debtors”). Judgment Creditor filed a Motion for Award of Attorney’s Fees and Costs, which the Court granted May 16, 2024. (Minute Order, 05/16/24.)

 

Judgment Creditor filed the instant Motion for Award of Attorney’s Fees and Costs on June 25, 2026. The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 685.080. Judgment Creditor served the Motion upon Judgment Debtors by first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The motion is accompanied by a request for judicial notice of (1) portions of the Master Declaration of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Maricopa Meadows recorded in the Pinal County, Arizona Recorder's Office on March 19, 2004 as Instrument No. 2004-019765; (2) the judgment

entered in the Maricopa Justice Court, County of Pinal, State of Arizona on November 6, 2013, entitled Maricopa Meadows Homeowners Association v. Stephen S. Brown and Jane Doe Brown with case number CV2012-0564; and (3) the judgment on Sister-State Judgment entered in the above-entitled action, in the Los Angeles County Superior Court, case number 22STCP02982.

 

The request for judicial notice is granted pursuant to California Evidence Code section 452, subdivisions (c) and (d).

 

Entitlement to Attorney Fees and Costs

 

“The judgment creditor may claim under this section the following costs of enforcing a judgment: . . . (6) Attorney's fees, if allowed by Section 685.040.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 685.070, subd. (a).) Code of Civil Procedure, section 685.040 states: “The judgment creditor is entitled to the reasonable and necessary costs of enforcing a judgment. Attorney’s fees incurred in enforcing a judgment are not included in costs collectible under this title unless otherwise provided by law. Attorney’s fees incurred in enforcing a judgment are included as costs collectible under this title if the underlying judgment includes an award of attorney’s fees to the judgment creditor pursuant to subparagraph (A) of paragraph (10) of subdivision (a) of Section 1033.5.” Also, the motion must be brought with two years of the incurred costs. (Code Civ. Proc., § 685.080, subd. (a).)

 

Based on the award of attorney’s fees in the judgment from the sister-state case, Plaintiff is also entitled to attorney’s fees incurred enforcing its judgment here pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 685.040. (Motion, RJN, Exh. 2, p. 2.) The Motion is timely filed with respect to fees incurred between August 23, 2024 and the hearing on the instant Motion. (Motion, Bailio Decl., ¶¶6-11 and Exh. A.)

 

Calculation of Attorney Fees and Costs

 

The Court’s objective is to award attorney fees at the fair market value based on the particular action.  (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132.)  “The reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work.”  (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.) “‘[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate . . . .’” (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1134.)  The lodestar method is based on the factors, as relevant to the particular case: “(1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award.”  (Id. at 1132.) “The ‘‘experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court, and while his judgment is of course subject to review, it will not be disturbed unless the appellate court is convinced that it is clearly wrong.’’” (Id.) A negative modifier may be applied when duplicative work has been performed. (Thayer v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 819.)

 

Plaintiff submits the declaration of its attorney, Austin Baillio (“Baillio”), in support of its request for attorney fees. Baillio declares that he and the other attorney, Garren R. Laymon, billed $395.00 per hour, with some work charged on a flat fee basis. (Motion, Baillio Decl., ¶6 and Exh. A.) The paralegals billed at $175.00 per hour. (Id. at ¶9 and Exh. A.) The Court finds the work billed at an hourly rate by the paralegals, and for Baillio’s appearance at the hearing on the instant Motion, to be supported and reasonable. The paralegals followed up with a private investigator and searched databases for information about Judgment Debtors’ military status, potential bankruptcies, and potential assets. (Ibid.) The two flat rate items were $500.00 to prepare an affidavit of judgment renewal (presumably in the Maricopa Justice Court) and $400.00 for drafting the instant Motion. With respect to the affidavit of judgment renewal, the billing statement sets forth the tasks undertaken, which the Court finds support the rate charged. Therefore, Judgment Creditor is awarded attorney’s fees of $1,410.00.

 

Regarding costs incurred, Judgment Creditor seeks $242.55 for file management software provider charges, the filing fee for the Affidavit of Judgment Renewal, postage, and background investigation. (Motion, Baillio Decl., Exh. A., p. 2.) The only filing fee in the itemized list is for the Affidavit of Judgment Renewal. The Court declines to award costs for file management software provider charges, which the Motion does not discuss. Judgment Creditor is awarded the remaining costs in the amount of $95.55.

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Plaintiff Maricopa Meadows Homeowners Association’s Motion for Award of Attorney Fees and Costs is granted in the amount of $1,410.00 in attorney fees and $95.55 in costs.

 

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 24STLC07982    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Andres Cano Sanchez’s Motion Regarding Meet and Confer is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On November 4, 2024, Plaintiff Andres Cano Sanchez (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for breach of contract and common counts against Defendant Sandra P. Lemus (erroneously sued as Sandra P. Lemus Abrego De De La Rosa) (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer, in propria persona, on December 9, 2024. The action came for trial on May 4, 2026, at which time the Court found that no trial documents had been filed and that the parties had not complied with the Third Amended Standing Order. (Minute Order, 05/04/26.) Pursuant to oral stipulation, the Court continued the trial date to June 2, 2026 and ordered the parties’ compliance with the Third Amended Standing Order, including a meet and confer at least 20 days before trial, and filing of a joint witness list at least five days before trial. (Ibid.)

 

On June 2, 2026, the Court found that, again, the parties had not complied with Third Amended Standing Order, nor filed trial documents. (Minute Order, 06/02/26.) The trial date was continued to August 3, 2026, the parties were again ordered to comply with the Standing Order (now the Fourth Standing Order.) On July 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion Regarding Meet and Confer.

 

On August 3, 2026, the parties appeared for trial without having met and conferred, filed joint witness lists or exhibits lists, and without hard copy binders containing both parties’ exhibits.  The Court conferred with the parties regarding trial preparation and ordered the parties to returnfor trial on August 6, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The instant motion is denied as moot based on the Court’s discussion with the parties on August 3, 2206.  The Motion is also denied for procedural reasons.

 

First, Plaintiff filed a proof of service with respect to the instant Motion attesting that it was mailed to Defendant on July 23, 2026. (Proof of Service, file 07/30/26, ¶¶4-6.) This was only ten court days prior to the hearing date, in violation of the statutory notice requirements. A motion and notice of hearing date must be served at least 16 court days prior to the hearing; if served by mail the time increases by five days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005.) Plaintiff’s failure to serve Defendant with the Motion and notice of hearing by July 10, 2026 is a violation of due process and of the statutory notice requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.)  The Court cannot grant a motion if the opposing party is not served with sufficient time to respond.

 

Additionally, the Motion is not accompanied by a memorandum of points and authorities showing any legal basis for the relief sought. A memorandum of points and authorities is required to accompany a motion under Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1113. “The memorandum must contain a statement of facts, a concise statement of the law, evidence and arguments relied on, and a discussion of the statutes, cases, and textbooks cited in support of the position advanced.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1113(b).) Indeed, Plaintiff’s failure to provide a memorandum as required by the Rule may be considered an “admission that the [request] is without merit and cause for its denial.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1113(a), (b); In re Marriage of Falcone & Fyke (2012) 203 Cal.App.4th 964, 976.) The Motion advises the Court that the parties have been unable to meet and confer, as ordered, but does not otherwise identify any evidence or arguments or legal basis for the Court to act.

 

Therefore, Plaintiff Andres Cano Sanchez’s Motion Regarding Meet and Confer is denied. As indicated, the Court conferred with the parties at the August 3, 2026 trial date.  The Court believes that conference addressed the relevant topics as which the parties were to meet-and-confer, making this Motion moot, in any event.

Case Number: 26STLC03279    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 26

 

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Goldie Nwachuku’s Demurrer to the Complaint is sustained without leave to amend.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Best Solar 4 Less Inc. dba Eco Home Builders (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the instant action against Defendant Goldie Nwachuku (erroneously sued as “Goldie Wanchuku”) (“Defendant”) on May 14, 2026. On July 6, 2026, Defendant filed and served the instant Demurrer to the Complaint. Defendant served Plaintiff with the Demurrer on the same day by electronic mail and first-class mail. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Initially, the Court notes that the Complaint was filed by Victor Mendieti on behalf of the business entity “Best Solar 4 Less Inc. dba Eco Home Builders.” (Compl., p. 1.) It is black letter law that a corporation cannot represent itself in court. (Clean Air Transport Systems v. San Mateo County Transit Dist. (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 576, 578 (citing Merco Constr. Engineers, Inc. v. Municipal Court (1978) 21 Cal.3d 724, 729-730).) This rule applies to all entities regarded as separate from their owners, including partnerships and unincorporated associations. (See Clean Air Transport Systems, supra, 19 Cal.App.3d at 578.) Plaintiff must appear through a licensed attorney if it intends to proceed with this action.

 

The Demurrer is accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. (Demurrer, Anyia Decl., ¶2.) Defendant demurs to the Complaint for failure to allege sufficient facts, uncertainty, and failure to allege facts showing whether the contract is written, verbal, or implied. (Demurrer, p. 2:6-11.) Special demurrers, however, are not permitted in the Limit Civil Court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (c).) Therefore, the Court will not consider the Demurrer for uncertainty or for failure to allege facts showing whether the contract is written, oral, or implied.

 

Regarding whether the Complaint fails to allege sufficient facts in support of the purported causes of action for breach of contract, common counts, foreclosure of mechanic’s lien, quantum meruit, and unjust enrichment, Defendant demurs on the basis that no causes of action are attached in support of the Complaint, and as a such, it fails to allege the elements of any of the named causes of action. Regardless of whether a cause of action is attached or not to a Judicial Council form complaint, “a reviewing court must examine the particular allegations in the form pleading and determine whether those allegations satisfy the pleading requirements established by California law.” (Esparza v. Kaweah Delta Dist. Hospital (2016) 3 Cal.App.5th 547, 555.) In People ex rel. Dept. of Transportation v. Superior Court (1992) 5 Cal.App.4th 1480, the Court of Appeals ruled that

checking a box on a Judicial Council complaint form was insufficient to comply with the general requirement that a complaint state facts constituting cause of action. (People ex rel. Dept. of Transportation v. Superior Court (1992) 5 Cal.App.4th 1480, 1484.) “[I]n order to be demurrer-proof, a form ‘complaint must contain whatever ultimate facts are essential to state a cause of action under existing statutes or case law.’” (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff’s Complaint attaches no causes of action. (Compl., ¶8.) Instead, it alleges a few sentences with respect to all the named causes of action, as follows: “Plaintiff performed construction labor, services, and furnished materials for improvements to defendant's property. Defendant failed to pay the remaining balance owed. Plaintiff recorded a Mechanics Lien against the subject property.” (Id. at ¶9.) Plaintiff then seeks damages of $23,100.00. (Id. at ¶10.) It also attaches a variety of exhibits including “Contract, Approved Plans, and Permit Documents,” “Recorded Mechanic’s Lien,” “Approved Change Order,” “Proof of Payments Received,” photographs, and communications. However, none of the attached exhibits are explained in the alleged facts or even referenced. Evidentiary facts “found in recitals of exhibits attached to a complaint or superseded complaint [ ] can be considered on demurrer,” but the exhibits must be incorporated by reference. (See Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94.) Therefore, the Court cannot consider the exhibits attached to the Complaint for purposes of the instant Demurrer.

 

 

A cause of action for breach of contract must allege (1) the existence of the contract; (2) Plaintiff's performance of the contract; (3) defendant’s breach of the contract; and (4) the resulting damages to the Plaintiff. (Lortz v. Connell (1969) 273 Cal.App.2d 286, 290.) The Complaint does not allege the existence of a contract. It simply alleges that Plaintiff performed work and furnished materials to Defendant’s property. Nor is there an allegation of Plaintiff’s performance of obligations under a contract. The allegation of labor performed and materials furnished is unconnected to any agreement. Likewise, the allegation of Defendant’s failure to pay “remaining balance owed” or the amount of Plaintiff’s damages cannot be linked to any agreement between the parties. The Demurrer to the first cause of action for breach of contract is sustained.

 

The elements for a cause of action for foreclosure of a mechanic’s lien, set forth at Civil Code section 8400 to 8848, are (1) entitlement to a lien; (2) proper service of preliminary notice; (3) timely recordation of the claim of lien; and (4) timely filing of the foreclosure action. Only the first element is potentially alleged in the Complaint; no facts regarding the remaining elements are present. The Demurrer to the third cause of action for foreclosure of mechanic’s lien is sustained.

 

Although the common count in the Complaint is not named, a claim for goods and services rendered must allege (1) the plaintiff provided goods or services; (2) at the request of, or for the benefit of, the defendant; and (3) the reasonable value of those goods or services has not been paid. (CACI 371.) A cause of action for quantum meruit similarly must allege the reasonable value of the benefit provided. (Pacific Bay Recovery, Inc. v. California Physicians' Services, Inc. (2017) 12 Cal.App.5th 200, 214.) The Complaint does not allege the reasonable value of the goods or services rendered by Plaintiff for Defendant’s benefit. Moreover, “[w]hen a common count is used as an alternative way of seeking the same recovery demanded in a specific cause of action, and is based on the same facts, the common count is demurrable if the cause of action is demurrable.”  (See McBride v. Boughton (2004) 123 Cal. App. 4th 379, 394 (“McBride’s common count must stand or fall with his first cause of action, and we therefore uphold its dismissal for the same reasons.”)  So here.  The Demurrer to the second and fourth causes of action is sustained.

 

Finally, for unjust enrichment, Plaintiff must allege “receipt of a benefit and unjust retention of the benefit at the expense of another.” (Professional Tax Appeal v. Kennedy-Wilson Holdings, Inc. (2018) 29 Cal.App.5th 230, 238.) No ultimate facts are alleged that Defendant’s retention of the labor performed and materials furnished is unjust because, as recited above, no information is provided regarding what labor was performed or what materials were furnished. The allegation that Defendant owed Plaintiff a certain amount is a legal conclusion unsupported by any ultimate facts regarding why that amount was owed. Therefore, the Demurrer to the fifth cause of action is also sustained.

 

Nor has Plaintiff filed any opposition to demonstrate on what basis leave to amend might be granted. As the pleading party, Plaintiff bears the burden to demonstrate that leave to amend should be granted, which must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) Therefore, leave to amend is denied.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Goldie Nwachuku’s Demurrer to the Complaint is sustained without leave to amend.
