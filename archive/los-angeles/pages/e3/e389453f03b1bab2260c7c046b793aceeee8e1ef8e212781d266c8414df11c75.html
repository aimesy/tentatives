Case Number: 23STCV20228    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 514

Mondragon v. Bodega Latina Corporation
23STCV20228
Defendant’s Motion for Leave to Withdraw Admission

Tentative Ruling

The motion is denied.  Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is denied.

Background

In this matter, Plaintiff Anthony Mondragon alleges (“Plaintiff”) that on April 29, 2023, he slipped and fell while shopping in a supermarket owned, operated, or controlled by defendants.

Plaintiff filed the complaint in this action on August 23, 2023, naming as defendants Bodega Latina Corporation, Doe Store Manager, and Does 1 through 100, and asserting causes of action for (1) premises liability and (2) general negligence.

On September 15, 2023, Plaintiff amended the complaint to name Chedraui USA, Inc. as Doe 1.

On October 16, 2023, Chedraui USA, Inc., also erroneously sued as Bodega Latina Corporation (“Defendant”), filed an answer to the complaint.

In April and May 2026, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed Doe Store Manager and Bodega Latina Corporation without prejudice.

Currently before the Court and set for hearing on August 14, 2026, is a motion filed by Defendant on June 16, 2026, for leave to withdraw its admission in response to Plaintiff’s Request for Admission (Set Two), No. 60.  Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 9, and Defendant filed a reply on July 16.

The motion was initially set for hearing on July 23.  On July 23, the parties stipulated orally to continue the hearing to August 14.

Trial is set for February 8, 2027.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.300 governs requests to withdraw or amend responses to requests for admission.  Section 2033.300 provides:

“(a) A party may withdraw or amend an admission made in response to a request for admission only on leave of court granted after notice to all parties.

(b) The court may permit withdrawal or amendment of an admission only if it determines that the admission was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect, and that the party who obtained the admission will not be substantially prejudiced in maintaining that party’s action or defense on the merits.

(c) The court may impose conditions on the granting of the motion that are just, including, but not limited to, the following:

(1) An order that the party who obtained the admission be permitted to pursue additional discovery related to the matter involved in the withdrawn or amended admission.

(2) An order that the costs of any additional discovery be borne in whole or in part by the party withdrawing or amending the admission.”

The Court of Appeal discussed section 2033.300 at length in New Albertsons, Inc. v. Superior Court (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1403. The Court of Appeal stated that the statute “eliminates undeserved windfalls obtain through requests for admission and furthers the policy favoring the resolution of lawsuits on the merits.” (Id. at p. 1418.)   

Although the trial court has discretion in ruling on a motion brought under section 2033.300, that discretion “is not unlimited” and “must be exercised in conformity with the spirit of the law and in a manner that serves the interests of justice.” (Id., at p. 1420.)

“Because the law strongly favors trial and disposition on the merits, any doubts in applying section 2033.300 must be resolved in favor of the party seeking relief. Accordingly, the court’s discretion to deny a motion under the statute is limited to circumstances where it is clear that the mistake, inadvertence, or neglect was inexcusable, or where it is clear that the withdrawal or amendment would substantially prejudice the party who obtained the admission in maintaining that party's action or defense on the merits.”

(Id. at pp. 1420-1421.) 

Objections to Evidence

Plaintiff asserts 10 objections to Defendant’s evidence.  Objections Nos. 8 and 9 are sustained; the remainder of the objections are overruled.

Discussion

The Court begins with a threshold issue.

It its briefing and supporting evidence, Defendant discusses its responses to Requests for Admissions (“RFAs”) Nos. 59 and 60.  In its notice of motion and motion and motion, however, Defendant clearly states that it seeks to withdraw its response to RFA No. 60 (only), with no reference to RFA No. 59.

The Court cannot grant relief not requested in the notice of motion and motion.  Accordingly, the only issue before the Court is whether to grant Defendant’s request for leave to withdraw its response to RFA No. 60.

At some point in the litigation, likely in 2024, Plaintiff propounded Requests for Admission (Set Two).  This document contains only two RFAs.  In RFA No. 59, Plaintiff asked Defendant to admit that it “negligently caused” the incident in which Plaintiff fell and was injured.  In RFA No. 60, Plaintiff asked Defendant to admit its negligence “was a substantial factor in causing harm” to Plaintiff.  (See Derflinger Decl., Exh. 1; Elliott Decl., Exh. 1.)

It appears from the evidence in the record that in early November 2024, the parties were discussing a variety of issues relating to discovery and trial scheduling.  On November 7, 2024, Defendant’s then-counsel, Rosemary Do, sent an email to Plaintiff’s counsel stating the following:

“[Y]ou have agreed that, if my client [will] admit RFA Nos. 59-60 in a verified response by November 15, 2024, you will withdraw Request for Admissions Nos. 39-58; the entire set of Special Interrogatories to Defendant, Set Two; and Request for Production Nos. 36-37.  Additionally the remaining Requests for Admissions and Requests for Production are now due December 20, 2024.”

(Elliott Decl., Exh. 8.)

On November 14, 2024, Defendant served its responses to RFAs Nos. 59 and 60.  The responses were verified by Adrian Rios (identified in the verification as Defendant’s National Coordinating Counsel, although he later testified that he is Defendant’s National Claim Director).  In its verified response, Defendant stated “admit” to both RFA Nos. 59 and 60.  (Derflinger Decl., Exh. 1; Elliott Decl., Exh. 1.)

On June 9, 2025, Defendant filed a Substitution of Attorney form (which had been executed on May 1, 2025), replacing Ms. Do with Bryan Aghakhani of the Bordin Semmer LLP law firm.

On December 12, 2025, Mr. Rios was deposed in this litigation.  In the deposition, Mr. Rios repeatedly testified that the responses to RFAs Nos. 59 and 60 were based on the recommendations of Ms. Do, Defendant’s attorney at the time the responses were executed and served.  (Elliott Decl., Exh. 6, at 27-28, 31, 37-41.)

In support of this motion, Mr. Rios filed a declaration.  In the declaration, Mr. Rios identifies himself as Defendant’s General Liability Manager and states that when he verified the responses admitting RFAs Nos. 59 and 60, he did so based on the advice of counsel, who informed him that in negotiating various issues with opposing counsel, “an admission of negligence would facilitate obtaining a trial continuance to allow discovery to be completed for a possible mediation.  (Rios Decl., ¶ 7.)

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.300, subdivision (b), a motion for leave to withdraw or amend an admission in response to an RFA can be granted only if the Court determines (among other things) “that the admission was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.”  On this record, the Court cannot make such a determination.  To the contrary, from the evidence in the record, it is abundantly clear that Defendant’s admission was in no way, shape, or form the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.  Rather, it was a fully informed litigation decision that Defendant made, after consulting with and on the advice of counsel, for the purpose of obtaining either an extension of discovery or a trial continuance, or perhaps both.

The motion is denied.

In his opposition, Plaintiff seeks sanctions.  In many circumstances, filing an unsuccessful discovery motion can be considered a misuse of the discovery process giving rise to sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.010 & § 2030.030.)  Here, however, Plaintiff seeks sanctions in the amount of $143,817.  (Opp. at p. 17.)  That request is entirely inflated and completely untethered to any amount of reasonable attorney’s fees or other expenses incurred by Plaintiff in opposing the motion.  Accordingly, Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is denied.

Conclusion

The Court DENIES the motion of Defendant Chedraui USA, Inc. to withdraw its admission in response to Plaintiff’s Request for Admission, No. 60.

The Court DENIES Plaintiff’s request for sanctions.

Counsel for the moving party is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV14122    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 514

Burnett v. Vis A Vis Healthcare Medical Group of NY PLLC
24STCV14122
Motion to be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant

Tentative Ruling

The motion is granted.

Counsel must submit a new proposed order.

Background

This matter arises out of an employment relationship.  On June 5, 2024, Daniel Raju Burnett (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint with fourteen causes of action against Vis A Vis Healthcare Medical Group of NY PLCC dba Heal Doctors PC (“Defendant) and Does 1 through 20.

On July 12, 2024, Defendant filed an answer.

On July 22, 2026, Rachel Chatman and Benesch, Friedlander, Coplan & Aronoff LLP (“Counsel”) filed this motion to be relieved as counsel for Defendant.

No opposition has been filed.

Trial is set for August 31, 2026.

Legal Standard

The court may order that an attorney be changed or substituted at any time before or after judgment or final determination upon request by either client or attorney and after notice from one to the other. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. (b).) An attorney is permitted to withdraw where conflicts between the attorney and client make it unreasonable to continue the representation. (See Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct 3-700(C)(1).) “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133.)    

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made the mandatory forms approved by the Judicial Council.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)   All forms must be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).) The court may delay effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) 

Discussion

Counsel has filed the required notice, declaration, and proposed order on the client and all parties in this matter, as required. 

Counsel’s declaration states that Defendant is in the process of winding down, has no assets, and has no funds to pay the firm for the continuing defense of this action.  Counsel further reports that the client has instructed counsel to seek to withdraw and no longer to take any action to defend this litigation.

Good cause has been shown for the relief requested.  All substantive and procedural requirements are satisfied.  The motion is granted.

The Court notes Defendant is a corporate entity (a PLLC).  “[A] corporation, unlike a natural person, cannot represent itself before courts of record in propria persona, nor can it represent itself through a corporate officer, director or other employee who is not an attorney.”  (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1145.)  Accordingly, it appears that unless Defendant retains new counsel, the trial in this action will proceed as an uncontested matter.

The proposed order submitted requires some modifications.

Item 2 must be completed following the hearing.

In Item 3, box b must be checked.

In Item 5, box a must be checked.

Item 6 must be completed.

In Item 7a, the date of the Final Status Conference (August 18, 2026) must be included.

Counsel is ORDERED to submit a revised proposed order forthwith.

The order granting this motion is effective upon the filing of a proof of service showing service of the signed order (and not merely the Minute Order) on the client.  Until the order is effective, counsel continues to represent Defendant.

Moving counsel is ordered to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCP03068    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 514

Blue Hill Specialty Insurance Company v. Rodrigo
25STCP03068
Respondent’s Motion to Compel Arbitration

Tentative Ruling

The motion is granted in part.

Background

On August 12, 2025, Blue Hill Specialty Insurance Company (“Blue Hill”) filed a petition for issuance of superior court file number – uninsured motorist claim, naming as respondents Shammika Rodrigo (“Rodrigo”), Varand Harouian (“Harouian”), and Irina Grigorian (“Grigorian”).

On June 8, 2026, Rodrigo filed this motion to compel arbitration.  On June 16, 2026, Harouian and Grigorian filed a joinder to the motion to compel arbitration.

Blue Hill filed an opposition on June 17, and Rodrigo filed a reply on June 22.

The hearing was initially scheduled for July 1 and was continued.

Legal Standard

Insurance Code section 11580.2, subdivision (f) covers underinsured or uninsured motorist coverage and states in pertinent part:

“The policy or an endorsement added thereto shall provide that the determination as to whether the insured shall be legally entitled to recover damages, and if so entitled, the amount thereof, shall be made by agreement between the insured and the insurer or, in the event of disagreement, by arbitration.  The arbitration shall be conducted by a single neutral arbitrator.  …  If the insured has or may have rights to benefits, other than nonoccupational disability benefits, under any workers’ compensation law, the arbitrator shall not proceed with the arbitration until the insured’s physical condition is stationary and ratable.  In those cases in which the insured claims a permanent disability, the claims shall, unless good cause be shown, be adjudicated by award or settled by compromise and release before the arbitration may proceed.  Any demand or petition for arbitration shall contain a declaration, under penalty of perjury, stating whether (i) the insured has a workers’ compensation claim; (ii) the claim has proceeded to findings and award or settlement on all issues reasonably contemplated to be determined in that claim; and (iii) if not, what reasons amounting to good cause are grounds for the arbitration to proceed immediately.  ….”

(Ins. Code, § 11580.2, subd. (f).)  

The provisions of Insurance Code section 11580.2 are deemed to be a part of every uninsured motorist policy in California. (See Quintano v. Mercury Casualty Co. (1995) 11 Cal.4th 1049, 1053; Harford Fire Insurance Co. v. Macri (1992) 4 Cal.4th 318, 324.)  The arbitration mandated by section 11580.2, subdivision (f) is a form of contractual arbitration governed by the California Arbitration Act (the “CAA”).  (Pilimai v. Farmers Insurance Exch. Co. (2006) 39 Cal.4th 133, 141.) 

Code of Civil Procedure 1281.2, which is part of the CAA, authorizes a party to petition a court for an order enforcing an arbitration agreement:

“On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party thereto refuses to arbitrate such controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists, unless it determines that: 

(a) The right to compel arbitration has been waived by the petitioner; or 

(b) Grounds exist for the revocation of the agreement. 

(c) A party to the arbitration agreement is also a party to a pending court action or special proceeding with a third party, arising out of the same transaction or series of related transactions and there is a possibility of conflicting rulings on a common issue of law or fact ....” 

(d) … If the court determines that a party to the arbitration is also a party to litigation in a pending court action or special proceeding with a third party as set forth under subdivision (c), the court (1) may refuse to enforce the arbitration agreement and may order intervention or joinder of all parties in a single action or special proceeding; (2) may order intervention or joinder as to all or only certain issues; (3) may order arbitration among the parties who have agreed to arbitration and stay the pending court action or special proceeding pending the outcome of the arbitration proceeding; or (4) may stay arbitration pending the outcome of the court action or special proceeding.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.2.) 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 sets forth the procedure for the selection of an arbitrator:

“If the arbitration agreement provides a method of appointing an arbitrator, that method shall be followed. If the arbitration agreement does not provide a method for appointing an arbitrator, the parties to the agreement who seek arbitration and against whom arbitration is sought may agree on a method of appointing an arbitrator and that method shall be followed. In the absence of an agreed method, or if the agreed method fails or for any reason cannot be followed, or when an arbitrator appointed fails to act and his or her successor has not been appointed, the court, on petition of a party to the arbitration agreement, shall appoint the arbitrator.

When a petition is made to the court to appoint a neutral arbitrator, the court shall nominate five persons from lists of persons supplied jointly by the parties to the arbitration or obtained from a governmental agency concerned with arbitration or private disinterested association concerned with arbitration. The parties to the agreement who seek arbitration and against whom arbitration is sought may within five days of receipt of notice of the nominees from the court jointly select the arbitrator whether or not the arbitrator is among the nominees. If the parties fail to select an arbitrator within the five-day period, the court shall appoint the arbitrator from the nominees.”

Discussion

Respondent Rodrigo seeks a court order compelling the parties to arbitrate their dispute.  Respondents Harouian and Grigorian join in the request.

Rodrigo argues that arbitration is required under the terms of the insurance policy at issue, pursuant to Insurance Code section 11580.2.  Blue Hill does not deny that arbitration is required.  (See Opp. at pp. 12-13.)  In fact, Blue Hill has agreed to arbitrate; moreover, the parties have selected R.A. Carrington to serve as the arbitrator.  (Opp. at p. 13; see also Kaye Decl., Exh. E.)

The evidence in the record shows, however, that Blue Hill – although nominally agreeing to arbitrate – has unreasonably delayed the initiation of the arbitration.  In May 2025, Rodrigo served a demand for arbitration.  In August 2025, just over a year ago, Blue Hill filed the petition in his action.  By no later than April 2026, the parties had selected the arbitrator.  And yet, Blue Hill has refused to schedule the arbitration.

Under the facts here, this constitutes a “refusal” to arbitrate within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2.  As both sides agree that the dispute is arbitrable, and both sides agree that R.A. Carrington should serve as the arbitrator, the motion to compel arbitration is granted.

Rodrigo also requests that the Court order that the arbitration hearing be scheduled before the end of 2026.  That request is denied.  The arbitrator (with input from the parties) will schedule the arbitration hearing.  But even if (as Blue Hill contends) certain discovery must be taken before the arbitration hearing, that is no excuse for failing to initiate the arbitration, engage the arbitrator selected by the parties, and schedule the arbitration with him.  Any disputes regarding discovery and the appropriate timing of the arbitration hearing can be discussed with, and resolved by, the arbitrator.

The motion is granted in part.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS IN PART the motion of Respondent Shammika Rodrigo (joined by Respondents Varand Harouian and Irina Grigorian) to compel arbitration.

The Court ORDERS the parties to initiate the arbitration with the arbitrator selected by the parties (R.A. Carrington) and engage the arbitrator forthwith.

The Court DENIES the request for a court order regarding the scheduling of the arbitration hearing.  The scheduling of the hearing is for the arbitrator to determine.

The Moving Party is ORDERED to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV08770    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 514

Subia v. FCA US, LLC
25STCV08770
Motion to be Relieved as Counsel for Plaintff
Defendant’s Motion for Sanctions

Tentative Ruling

The motion to be relieved is granted.  Counsel must submit a revised proposed order.

The motion for sanctions is denied.

Background 

This case arises out of the purchase of a 2020 Dodge Charger by Jonathon Subia (“Plaintiff”).

On March 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed the complaint in this action against FCA US, LLC (“FCA”), Hoblit Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram (collectively “Defendants”), and Does 1 through 10, asserting causes of action for (1) violation of subdivision (d) of Civil Code section 1793.2, (2) violation of subdivision (b) of Civil Code section 1793.2, (3) violation of subdivision (a)(3) of Civil Code section 1793.2, (4) breach of the implied warranty of merchantability (Civil Code sections 1791.1, 1794, & 1795.5.), (5) negligent repair, and (6) fraudulent inducement-concealment.

On January 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (“FAC”).

On February 2, 2026, Defendants each filed an answer to the FAC.

There are currently two motions pending before the Court and set for hearing on August 14, 2026.

First, on June 16, 2026, FCA filed this motion for sanctions, asserting that Plaintiff provided incomplete initial disclosures.

No opposition to this motion has been filed.

On August 4, 2026, FCA filed a notice of non-opposition.

Second, on July 16, 2026, Mani Arabi of Strategic Legal Practices, APC (“Counsel”) filed a motion to be relieved as attorney of record for Plaintiff.

No opposition to this motion has been filed.

Trial is set for February 16, 2027.

Legal Standard

Motion to be Relieved

The court may order that an attorney be changed or substituted at any time before or after judgment or final determination upon request by either client or attorney and after notice from one to the other. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. (b).) An attorney is permitted to withdraw where conflicts between the attorney and client make it unreasonable to continue the representation. (See Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct 3-700(C)(1).) “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133.)    

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made the mandatory forms approved by the Judicial Council.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)   All forms must be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).) The court may delay effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) 

Motion for Sanctions

“Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (b).)

“Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows:

(1) A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff's attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j).)

Discussion

Motion to be Relieved

Counsel has filed the required notice, declaration, and proposed order on the client and all parties in this matter, as required. 

Counsel’s declaration states that there has been a complete breakdown in the attorney-client relationship and that Plaintiff has stopped responding to communication from Counsel. 

Good cause has been shown for the relief requested.  All substantive and procedural requirements are satisfied.  The motion is granted.

The proposed order submitted on July 16 submitted requires some modifications.

In the box on the right side of the page near the top, the name of the judicial officer must be corrected.

Item 2 must be completed following the hearing.

In Item 3, box b must be checked.

In Item 5, box a must be checked.

Counsel is ORDERED to submit a revised proposed order forthwith.

Motion for Sanctions

FCA seeks sanctions arising from Plaintiff’s failure to fully comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26.

By statute, Plaintiff was required to provide documents regarding the following:

“(1) Mileage of the motor vehicle as of the date of the disclosure described in subdivision (b).

(2) Primary driver or drivers of the motor vehicle.

(3) If the motor vehicle is primarily used for a business purpose, whether more than five vehicles are registered to the business.

(4) Whether the plaintiff is still in possession of the motor vehicle.

(5) Address where the motor vehicle is located.

(6) Whether the plaintiff is an active or prior member of the Armed Forces.

(7) Whether the motor vehicle has been involved in a collision or accidents reported to insurance prior to the nonconformity, and if so, the approximate date of the collision, the name of the insurance company, and any applicable claim number.

(8) Whether the motor vehicle has any aftermarket modifications done after purchase of the motor vehicle, and if so, a list of each modification.

(9) Dates and mileages for presentations that are not included in the provided repair orders or the location of where the information may be found.

(10) Whether the plaintiff has had any pre-suit communications with the manufacturer, including, but not limited to, any restitution or replacement request.

(11) Any need for an interpreter for purposes of a deposition.”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (g).)

FCA has presented evidence that Plaintiff failed to disclose documentation regarding:

“(1) Sales or lease agreement.

(2) Copy of current registration.

(3) Any finance information, account information, including payment history and

estimated payoff amount, and any loan modification agreements.

(4) Any repair orders, including to third-party repair facilities or the location of where information relating to repair orders may be found.

(5) Documents detailing all underlying claimed incidental damages.

(6) Information pertaining to the market value of the motor vehicle that is currently in the consumer's possession.

(7) Any written, pre-suit communications with the manufacturer, including, but not limited to, any restitution or replacement request.”

Further, FCA has presented evidence Plaintiff failed to disclose complete information regarding:

“(1) Mileage of the motor vehicle as of the date of the disclosure;

(3) If the motor vehicle is primarily used for a business purpose, whether more than five vehicles are registered to the business.

(6) Whether the plaintiff is an active or prior member of the Armed Forces.

(7) Whether the motor vehicle has been involved in a collision or accidents reported to insurance prior to the nonconformity, and if so, the approximate date of the collision, the name of the insurance company, and any applicable claim number.

(8) Whether the motor vehicle has any aftermarket modifications done after purchase of the motor vehicle, and if so, a list of each modification.

(9) Dates and mileages for presentations that are not included in the provided repair orders or the location of where the information may be found.

(10) Whether the plaintiff has had any pre-suit communications with the manufacturer, including, but not limited to, any restitution or replacement request.”

No opposition has been filed.

The Court finds Plaintiff failed to provide a complete initial disclosure.  Absent good cause, sanctions against counsel are mandatory.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j).)

Here, however, the Court has received evidence that there is a breakdown in the attorney-client relationship and that Plaintiff has ceased communicating with his counsel.  Under these circumstances, good cause has been shown not to impose sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel.

Accordingly, the motion is denied.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS the motion of Mani Arabi, Esq. and Strategic Legal Practices, APC to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff.

The Court ORDERS Counsel to submit a revised proposed order addressing the issues set forth above forthwith.

The order granting this motion to be relieved is effective upon the filing of a proof of service showing service of the signed order (and not merely the Minute Order) on the client.  Until the order is effective, Counsel continues to represent Plaintiff.

The Court DENIES the motion of Defendant FCA US, LLC for sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel.

Plaintiff’s counsel is ordered to give notice.
