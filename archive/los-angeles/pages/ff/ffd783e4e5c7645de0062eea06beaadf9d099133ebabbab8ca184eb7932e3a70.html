Case Number: 23VECV03196    Hearing Date: June 25, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Rose v. Loren Properties, Case no. 23VECV03196

Hearing date June 25, 2026

Plaintiff Rose’s Motion to Compel Depositions of PMKs - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Rose sues defendants Loren Properties, LLC, Westside Habitats, LLC, Mehta and Graham for premises liability. Plaintiff moves to compel the depositions of Loren Properties and Westside Habitats’ employees and PMKs, as well as for sanctions. The motion is unopposed.

A party may move for an order compelling the attendance and testimony of a deponent where the deponent fails to appear for examination following the service of a deposition notice. Code Civ. Proc. §2025.450.

Plaintiff served deposition notices 2/11/26 to Lopes, Moseler, Hernandez, Garcia, Tucker and Mehta and the PMK of Westside Habitat. Plaintiff offers the declaration of counsel Smith, with communications between the parties as exhibit 1 and copies of the deposition notices as exhibit 2. See Decl. Smith, paras. 3-4. No exhibits are attached. While the motion is unopposed, the court cannot compel the depositions without admissible evidence that notices were served and the depositions did not occur. The matter is continued to allow for the missing exhibits to be filed.

 


Case Number: 24VECV04017    Hearing Date: June 25, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Gillispie v. Lyft, Case no. 24VECV04017

Hearing date June 25, 2026

Plaintiff’s Motion to Set Aside Dismissal - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Gillispie sues defendants Uber Technologies, Inc., Rasier, LLC, Rasier-CA, LLC, Hertz Global Holdings, Inc. and Wilson for injuries arising out of a motor vehicle accident. The court dismissed the action without prejudice on 12/16/25 for plaintiff’s failure to appear at hearing on 12/16/25. See 12/16/25 Min. Order. Plaintiff moves to set aside the dismissal pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. §473(b). The motion is unopposed.

“The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a . . . dismissal . . . taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence . . . or excusable neglect.” Code Civ. Proc. §473(b).

Plaintiff offers the declaration of counsel Keshishyan. Following multiple rounds of attempted service and amendment, an OSC re: failure to default Uber and Rasier as well as an OSC re: failure to serve Hertz and Wilson was set for 11/14/25. Decl. Keshishyan, paras. 1-26. The court continued the hearings to 12/16/25 and set an OSC re: dismissal for that date. Id. at para. 26.

Plaintiff’s counsel failed to appear at the 12/16/25 hearing, resulting in a dismissal without prejudice. Decl. Keshishyan, paras. 26-27. Plaintiff’s counsel asserts he was unaware of this dismissal and continued attempting to litigate this matter until June, when this motion was filed. Id. at paras. 28-29.

“Notwithstanding any other requirements of this section, the court shall, whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney's sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any . . . (2) resulting . . . dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the . . . dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” Code Civ. Proc. §473(b). Plaintiff has offered the declaration of counsel asserting the failure to appear 12/16/25 was not intentional. Dismissal was entered 12/16/25; this motion was filed 6/1/26 and is therefore timely by 15 days.

California favors adjudicating the merits of a case. This motion is unopposed. GRANTED.


Case Number: 26VECV00459    Hearing Date: June 25, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Santoro v. Anderson, Case no. 26VECV00459

Hearing date June 25, 2026

Defendant Anderson’s Demurrer to the Fourth Amended Complaint with Motion to Strike

Plaintiff Santoro sues defendants Anderson, Valencia Naturals, Inc., Natural Life Laboratories and Concept Naturals, Inc. for breach of contract – settlement agreement, breach of contract – asset purchase agreement, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing – asset purchase agreement and declaratory relief.

Plaintiff, shareholder in the corporate defendants, alleges he entered into a settlement agreement with the corporation which Anderson, also a shareholder, allegedly breached. Anderson demurs to the first and third causes of action and moves to strike. Plaintiff opposes.

Anderson requests judicial notice of the asset purchase agreement, attached to the 4AC as exh. B. GRANTED pursuant to Evid. Code §452(d).

Defendant Anderson’s Demurrer to the Fourth Amended Complaint

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Anderson demurs to the first cause of action for breach of contract – settlement agreement. Per Code Civ. Proc. §425.10, where recovery of money damages is demanded, the complaint must state the amount demanded. See Code Civ. Proc. §425.10. “Procedural due process requires ‘that a defendant be given notice of the existence of a lawsuit and notice of the specific relief which is sought… a defendant who has been served with a lawsuit has the right, in view of the relief which the complainant is seeking from him, to decide not to appear and defend. However, a defendant is not in a position to make such a decision if he or she has not been given full notice.” Yu v. Liberty Surplus Ins. Corp. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 1024, 1031.

Plaintiff alleged Anderson breached the confidentiality clause by telling his former spouse and staff about the issues. 4AC, paras. 9-10. Plaintiff alleged “damages well in excess of the jurisdictional minimum of this court to be determined at the time of adjudication of this matter” and prayed for compensatory damages, without alleging any specific amount. Id. at para. 30; Prayer 1, para. 1.

Plaintiff argues the allegation that he suffered damages in excess of the jurisdictional minimum is sufficient, citing Acree v. General Motors Acceptance Corp. (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 385, 398 for the premise that “[w]here the fact of damages is certain, as here, the amount of damages need not be calculated with absolute certainty. The law requires only that some reasonable basis of computation be used, and the result can be a reasonable approximation.” This is contrary to law.

Yu, supra, held a prayer for compensatory damages according to proof without an actual amount demanded was insufficient under Code Civ. Proc. §425.10. Schwab v. Rondel Homes (1991) 53 Cal.3d 428, 434 held mere allegations of minimum jurisdiction amount were insufficient to satisfy notice requirement of §425.10. Acree, supra, states at least an approximate amount must be pled. Id. at 398. Conclusory, open-ended allegations that damages sought exceed the jurisdictional minimum are insufficient. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Anderson demurs to the cause of action for breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. “Allegations that a breach was wrongful, in bad faith, arbitrary, and unfair are unavailing; there is no tort of ‘bad faith breach’ of [a] contract.” Guz v. Bechtel National, Inc. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 317, 352. To state a claim for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, plaintiff must allege defendant’s wrongful conduct was contrary to the contract’s purpose and the parties’ expectations. Cordoba Corp. v. City of Industry (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 145, 156.

Plaintiff alleges Anderson purposefully frustrated the asset purchase agreement, which stated defendant would transfer ownership and title to a Lamborghini to plaintiff to cover a portion of monies owed. 4AC, paras. 16-17, RJN 1. Plaintiff alleges Anderson purposefully set the sale price of the car too high, causing delay in sale. 4AC, para. 17. Plaintiff alleges the car was sold for less than what Anderson owed, and Anderson failed to make up the shortfall. 4AC, paras. 17-21. Plaintiff alleges Anderson failed to make credit card payments, as required by the agreement. 4AC, paras. 21-24. Plaintiff asserts these violations as the basis for the third cause of action. 4AC, para. 39.

Anderson argues allegations regarding the timing of the car sale and the final sales price cannot form the basis of the third cause of action, as the language of the agreement controls. See Qualcomm, Inc. v. Certain Underwriters at Lloyd’s, London (2008) 161 Cal.App.4th 184, 191. Section 3.C of the agreement states: “[Plaintiff] agrees to collaborate with [Anderson] with respect to the sales price at which the Vehicle is to be sold… In the event [plaintiff] sells the vehicle in a transaction not approved by [Anderson], the Shortfall Payment… shall not be owed by [Anderson] to [Plaintiff.]” RJN 1, section 3.C.

Plaintiff alleges: (1) Anderson insisted on a list price of $279,500 and $259,000, above the sale price of $195,000; (2) the vehicle did not sell for six months; and (3) Anderson only agreed to $195,000 if the sale was credited at a price of $202,500 for purposes of the “Shortfall Payment.” See 4AC, paras. 16-17, 39. Plaintiff’s argument that Anderson’s sale price was unreasonable or his conduct prolonged the sale, frustrating plaintiff’s receipt of the proceeds, is unavailing. The purchase agreement states Anderson could set the sales price. The timing of the sale, as well as the price at which it sold, cannot form the basis of a breach of the covenant of good faith claim.

Anderson argues the remaining allegations supporting the third cause of action are duplicative of the breach of contract claim or are superfluous. Plaintiff alleges Anderson failed to make the credit card payments, as required by the agreement. 4AC, paras. 21, 39. This is a merely an allegation that Anderson breached the underlying agreement and is duplicative of the second cause of action.

Plaintiff alleges Anderson had liquid funds with which to pay plaintiff but failed to do so, with intent of harming plaintiff’s business. 4AC, paras. 22-24, 39. The alleged motive of a breaching party is irrelevant under contract law. See Applied Equipment Corp. v. Litton Saudi Arabia Ltd. (1994) 7 Cal.4th 503, 516. Plaintiff’s allegations that Anderson failed to pay are allegations sounding in breach of contract. Plaintiff failed to allege actionable facts which give rise to a claim for breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing, rather than merely breach of the agreement. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendant Anderson’s Motion to Strike

The court sustained Anderson’s demurrer to plaintiff’s first and third causes of action with 15 days leave to amend. The motion to strike targets allegations incorporated into these causes of action, as well as prayers for relief arising therefrom. The motion to strike is MOOT.
