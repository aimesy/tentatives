Tentative Ruling

Gelvy Vealsquez Ortiz v. City of Santa Monica, et al., Case No. 24SMCV02676

Ortiz’s Motion for Leave to Amend

Hearing Date: July 20, 2026

Motion for Leave to Amend

This is a personal injury action arising from a car accident that occurred when Defendant Alevizos’ vehicle struck Plaintiff Ortiz while he was sitting at a City of Santa Monica Big Blue Bus stop. Ortiz initially sued Alevizos, the City of Santa Monica, and the architects who designed the Big Blue Bus stop for the City of Santa Monica, Lorcan O’Herlihy Architects (“O’Herlihy”).

On June 24, 2025, The Court sustained O’Herlihy’s demurrer to Ortiz’s initial complaint without leave to amend, finding that Plaintiff could not reasonably state a claim under the Government Tort Claims Act against O’Herlihy. (Minute Order (June 24, 2025.), p. 3.)

Ortiz now moves for leave to amend to add a claim for professional negligence against O’Herlihy based on City of Santa Monica discovery responses allegedly showing that O’Herlihy designed the bus stop to allow all individuals sitting at the bus stop a line of sight to approaching buses but negligently failed to appreciate the increased danger caused by placing some seats closer to the street. (Motion for Leave to Amend, p. 8.)

In opposition, O’Herlihy argues that the proposed amendment brings identical factual claims as the claims that were dismissed without leave to amend, so the Court should not grant Ortiz another opportunity to litigate facts that have already been found inadequate. (Opposition, p. 6.) O’Herlihy also argues that Ortiz’s professional negligence claim is barred by the statute of limitations and Plaintiff’s failure to timely file a certificate of merit. (Id. at p. 10.) Finally, O’Herlihy argues that it would be prejudiced if leave to amend is granted and that Ortiz’s unjustified delay in seeking leave to amend warrants denial. (Id. at p. 13.)

In reply, Ortiz argues that its proposed professional negligence claim is legally distinct from the claim dismissed by the demurrer. (Reply, p. 5.) Ortiz argues that the statute of limitations and the certificate of merit do not bar the claims because it has defenses to the statute of limitations and because it can file the necessary certificate of merit before filing the proposed amended complaint because the proposed amended complaint would be the first pleading to claim  professional negligence against an architect. (Id. at pp. 10-13.) Ortiz argues that O’Herlihy will not be prejudiced by the amendment because it remains a cross-defendant in this matter. Ortiz argues that the record establishes their diligence in seeking leave to amend. (Id. at pp. 16-18.)  

Procedural and Jurisdictional Issues

On July 7, 2026, the Court requested additional briefing on its ability to grant a motion for leave to amend after a demurrer is sustained without leave to amend but the defendant is not dismissed.

On July 10, 2026, Ortiz submitted a supplemental brief arguing that the demurrer sustained without leave to amend is not final judgment until the party is dismissed. Here, O’Herlihy was never dismissed. Ortiz contends that O’Herlihy is still participating as a cross-defendant, and that the demurrer ruling is not a final judgment because it does not resolve the underlying legal issues.

On July 10, 2026, O’Herlihy submitted a brief on the Court’s ability to grant Ortiz’s motion. O’Herlihy argues that the Court has authority to dismiss it from Ortiz’s complaint now pursuant to the 2025 demurrer, rendering that judgment final. O’Herlihy also argues that Ortiz’s motion should be construed as a motion for reconsideration under Code Civ. Proc. § 1008 and that the motion for leave to amend does not satisfy § 1008’s requirements.

An order sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend is not immediately appealable. It is only appealable when the Court enters judgment dismissing the party. (Cardenas v. Horizon Senior Living (2022) 78 Cal.App.5th 1065, 1069.) “It is clear that where there has been a judgment of dismissal after demurrer sustained without leave to amend or leave to amend is granted but plaintiff fails to amend within the time allowed, the action is finally terminated by the judgment because there is no longer any pending undetermined action to dismiss. It should also be clear that an action is not subject to dismissal where issues of law leading to its final determination have been submitted. . . . It is only by the entry of the judgment that plaintiff is in a position to test the correctness of the court’s ruling since there is no appeal from a ruling on a demurrer but only from the ensuing judgment.” (Berri v. Superior Court In and For City and County of San Fracisco (1955) 43 Cal.2d 856, 859-860, emphasis added.)

After a demurrer to a complaint is sustained, a plaintiff’s right to amend afterward “is always of grace, not of right.” (Gautier v. General Tel.Co. (1965) 234 Cal.App.2d 302, 310.) A noticed motion for leave to amend is required. Such a motion may be denied if it is filed following an unwarranted delay. (Leader v. Health Industries of America, Inc. (2001) 89 Cal.App.4th 603, 613.)

Here, the Court’s 2025 demurrer ruling did not resolve the merits of this case to the extent that the plaintiff was in a position to test the correctness of the court’s ruling on appeal. As a practical matter, Ortiz could not appeal because O’Herlihy did not enter judgment. By denying this motion on procedural grounds, the Court would effectively be preventing Ortiz from attempting to state a claim against O’Herlihy for professional negligence because they did not try before the 2025 demurrer was heard. Though O’Herlihy has a good procedural argument, the Court will consider Ortiz’s motion for leave to amend in the interest of the Court’s general preference for hearing cases on the merits.

On July 7, 2026, O’Herlihy submitted a proposed judgment dismissing it from the action, which would render the 2025 judgment final. Pursuant to the above reasoning, the Court declines to grant that judgment.

Motion for Leave to Amend

“[I]t is an abuse of discretion to deny leave to amend where the opposing party [is] not misled or prejudiced by the amendment.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1048 (“Kittredge”).) Prejudice exists where the amendment would require delaying the trial, resulting in loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation. (Solit v. Tokai Bank, Ltd. New York Branch (1999) 68 Cal.App.4th 1435, 1448 (“Solit”).)  

A motion for leave to amend must include a separate declaration specifying: “(1) The effect of the amendment; (2) Why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) The reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(b).) A motion for leave to amend must “[i]nclude a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a)(1).) Moreover, a motion to amend must “[s]tate what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed” to be deleted or added, if any, and must set forth the page, paragraph, and line number of such allegations. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a)(2)-(3).)  

O’Herlihy’s argument that Ortiz’s claim for professional negligence is barred by the statute of limitations and his failure to file a certificate of merit are arguments the Court would consider on a demurrer. Based on the Court’s initial review of Ortiz’s proposed amended complaint, its professional negligence claims against O’Herlihy are not obviously futile. Since filing this motion, Ortiz has filed a certificate of merit for its claim against O’Herlihy that appears to comply with statutory requirements. Whether or not the certificate is timely and adequate is also better decided on a demurrer.  

Though Ortiz’s factual claims against O’Herlihy are similar to the original complaint, Ortiz presents those facts in a different legal framework, supported by new discovery responses from the City of Santa Monica. Those discovery responses were only obtained recently and show that Ortiz brought this motion without unreasonable delay.

O’Herlihy will likely not be prejudiced by this amendment. Trial in this matter is not scheduled until March 2027, giving O’Herlihy plenty of time to challenge the amended complaint and prepare its defense. O’Herlihy’s legal expenses also do not constitute prejudice. Regardless of whether this motion is granted, O’Herlihy will incur legal fees associated with this case because it is still a cross-defendant.

Conclusion

Ortiz’s Motion for Leave to Amend is GRANTED. Ortiz is ordered to file the first amended complaint within ten (10) days.

Amanda Torah Solomon v. Volkswagen Group of America, Case No. 25SMCV04224

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production

Hearing Date: July 20, 2026

Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production

On March 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion to Compel Further Responses to its Request for Production (Set One). Plaintiff received Volkswagen’s initial response to its Request for Production of Documents (Set One) on November 14, 2025. Plaintiff argues that Volkswagen’s response contained primarily objections and that Volkswagen did not produce responsive documents. (Motion to Compel Further Response, p. 5.) Plaintiff argues that Volkswagen supplemented its discovery responses on February 9, 2026, but that those responses were also inadequate and did not provide a privilege log.

Pursuant to Department P’s standing order, Plaintiff scheduled an IDC for May 1, 2026. On May 1, 2026, the IDC was continued to June 5, 2026. Plaintiff failed to appear for the June 5, 2026, IDC. Based on Volkswagen’s representations at the IDC, the Court found that this motion would be moot once the documents are served.

On July 7, 2026, Volkswagen filed opposition contending that the motion is moot because it served the requested information about its warranty procedures on February 3, 2026, and served Plaintiff’s service bulletins on July 7, 2026.  (Wafer Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.)

On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply arguing that Volkswagen did not adequately respond to its RFPs 15-29 because the July 7, 2026, supplemental responses did not contain warranty information. Plaintiff submits Volkswagen’s July 7, 2026, supplemental responses, which contain only Technical Service Bulletins and Service Information related to the subject vehicle.

Neither Volkswagen nor Plaintiff has submitted copies of any of the February 3, 2026, discovery responses that Volkswagen states contain the warranty information Plaintiff seeks. The Court therefore cannot determine if those responses satisfied Plaintiffs’ RFPs 15-29. The Court continues this motion for thirty days, to August 20, 2026, and orders the Parties to meet and confer on this motion.

If this motion is not moot, the parties are ordered to submit a joint statement five court days before the hearing describing which requests for production remain at issue and attaching the discovery responses relevant to each remaining Request for Production.

This Motion is continued until August 20, 2026. The Joint Statement is due by August 13, 2026.


Case Number: 25SMCV06490    Hearing Date: July 20, 2026    Dept: P


Tentative Ruling

A to A Properties LLC v. CMJ Recovery NV, LLC, et al., Case No. 25SMCV06490

Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to Amend

Hearing Date: July 20, 2026

Motion for Leave to Amend

Plaintiff sues defendants CMJ Recovery NV, LLC; Joel Strulovics; and CMJ Healthcare Holdings, LLC for breach of guaranty, fraud, and negligent misrepresentation related to a guaranty defendants executed for Plaintiff’s tenants’ lease obligations at 21965 Saddle Peak Road, Topanga, CA.

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for leave to amend, adding claims for voidable transfer, conspiracy to commit voidable transfer, declaratory relief, and intentional interference with contract, and adding Malibu Recovery Center LLC, Aliya Health Group, LLC (Florida); Aliya Health Group LLC (Delaware); and Marc Beck off as defendants.

On July 7, 2026, Defendants filed a notice of non-opposition to this motion.

The trial court has discretion to allow amendments to pleadings in the furtherance of justice. (Code Civ. Proc. § 473, subd. (a)(1).) “Any judge, at any time before or after commencement of trial, in the furtherance of justice, and upon such terms as may be proper, may allow the amendment of any pleading.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 576.) “There is a policy of great liberality in permitting amendments to the pleadings at any stage of the proceeding.” (Berman v. Bromberg (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 936, 945 [citations omitted].) “An application to amend a pleading is addressed to the trial judge’s sound discretion.”  (Ibid. [citations omitted].) A judge may deny a motion for leave to amend where a moving party has been dilatory in seeking leave to amend and such delay has prejudiced the opposing party. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490 (“Hirsa”).) “It is difficult to understand how [a named defendant] can be prejudiced by amendment to add an additional theory of liability against it.” (Ibid.) 

“[I]t is an abuse of discretion to deny leave to amend where the opposing party [is] not misled or prejudiced by the amendment.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1048 (“Kittredge”).) Prejudice exists where the amendment would require delaying the trial, resulting in loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation. (Solit v. Tokai Bank, Ltd. New York Branch (1999) 68 Cal.App.4th 1435, 1448 (“Solit”).)  

A motion for leave to amend must include a separate declaration specifying: “(1) The effect of the amendment; (2) Why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) The reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(b).) A motion for leave to amend must “[i]nclude a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a)(1).) Moreover, a motion to amend must “[s]tate what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed” to be deleted or added, if any, and must set forth the page, paragraph, and line number of such allegations. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a)(2)-(3).)  

Plaintiff argues that this motion arises from discovery responses showing that the debtor defendants diverted funds to affiliated organizations and individuals, who are named as defendants in the proposed amended complaint. (Motion for Leave to Amend, p. 6.)

Following a review of Plaintiff’s documents, the Court finds that the Delshad Declaration and proposed amended complaint satisfies the California Rules of Court and shows that Plaintiff moved promptly for leave to amend. Absent opposition, the Court also finds that Defendants will not be prejudiced by the amendment.

Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to Amend is GRANTED.
