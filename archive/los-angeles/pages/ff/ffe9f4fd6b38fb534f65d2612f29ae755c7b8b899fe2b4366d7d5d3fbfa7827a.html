Case #24CHCV02266

 

MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION

 

Motion filed on 4/1/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Magic Mountain, LLC

RESPONDING PARTY: Non-Party Jawaun Graham

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling third-party witness, Jawaun Graham, to attend his deposition and provide testimony, within 10 days of the hearing on the motion.  Additionally, Defendant requests sanctions be imposed on Graham in the amount of $3,338.78, to be paid within 10 days. 

 

RULING: The motion is placed off calendar. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of Plaintiff Tiffani Rodriguez’s (Plaintiff) claim that she sustained personal injuries in the children’s slide area at Defendant Magic Mountain, LLC’s (Defendant) Six Flags Hurricane Harbor water park.  On 6/21/24, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant for premises liability.  On 8/14/24, Defendant answered the complaint. 

 

In response to written discovery, Plaintiff identified Jawaun Graham (Graham) as a witness and provided his address and telephone number.  (Cherazaie Decl., Ex.C).  On 2/11/26, Defendant served an amended notice of deposition for Graham’s deposition for 3/24/26.  (Id., Ex.D).  On 2/23/26, Defendant personally served a Deposition Subpoena on Graham.  (Id., Ex.E-F).  Graham failed to appear for his deposition.  (Id., Ex.G).

 

On 4/1/26, Defendant filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling third-party witness, Jawaun Graham, to attend his deposition and provide testimony, within 10 days of the hearing on the motion.  Additionally, Defendant requests sanctions be imposed on Graham in the amount of $3,338.78, to be paid within 10 days.    

 

ANALYSIS

 

CRC 3.1346 provides:

 

“A written notice and all moving papers supporting a motion to compel an answer to a deposition question or to compel production of a document or tangible thing from a nonparty deponent must be personally served on the nonparty deponent unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail or electronic service at an address or electronic service address specified on the deposition record.”

 

Here, the proof of service/declaration of service attached to the motion indicates that the motion was served by email.  (See Proof of Service attached to motion, pdf 52).  On the Service List attached to the Proof of Service, there is a notation that Graham was purportedly “served by mail and personal service” with no email address listed for Graham.  (Id., pdf 53).  However, there is no declaration of service attesting that Graham was served by mail or personal service with the motion. 

 

Additionally, Plaintiff’s discovery response identifying Graham as a witness lists his address as 616 Aerick St., Unit 3, Inglewood, CA 90301.  (See Cherazaie Decl., Ex.B, C – Special Interrogatory 5 and response thereto).  The Deposition Subpoena also lists Graham’s address as the Aerick St. address.  (Id., Ex.E).  However, the proof of service for the Deposition Subpoena indicates that it was personally delivered to Graham at 5418 ½ Thornburn St., Los Angeles, CA 90045.  (Id., Ex.F).  While it is possible that Graham was personally served with the Deposition Subpoena at a location other than his home, no explanation for the different address is provided.  Further, the service list attached to the defective proof of service for the motion, indicates that the motion was purportedly mailed and personally delivered to Graham at the Thornburn St. address.  Again, no explanation is provided as to why the motion would be mailed to the Thornburn St. address.

 

If Defendant has evidence that Graham’s current address is the Thornburn St. address, evidence of such should be provided in a subsequent motion to explain why the Deposition Subpoena was served at that address and/or why a subsequent motion would be properly served on Graham at that address.    

 

There is no response to the motion from Graham to cure the defect in notice. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

Due to the defect in service of the motion, the matter is placed off calendar. 

 


Case Number: 24CHCV02702    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: F47

Case #24CHCV02702

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES

(Demands for Production of Documents, Set 3)

 

Motion filed on 2/27/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Serene Fakhouri

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Magic Mountain, LLC

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Defendant Magic Mountain, LLC, to, within 10 days, serve further verified responses and production of documents in response to Plaintiff’s demands for production of documents, Set 3, Nos. 148, 149, 150 and 151.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests an order imposing sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,600.00.

 

RULING: The request to compel further responses is moot.  Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is granted.  Defendant’s request for sanctions is denied.    

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of injuries allegedly sustained by Plaintiffs Serene Fakhouri (Serene); Cassandra Fakhouri (Cassandra’s claims were dismissed with prejudice on 11/19/25) and Reyan Fakhouri (Reyan) (collectively, Plaintiffs) as a result of riding the Riddler’s Revenge roller coaster at Defendants Magic Mountain, LLC and Six Flags Entertainment Corporation’s theme park.  Reyan is the mother of Serene and Cassandra.   

 

On 7/24/23, Plaintiffs visited Defendants’ amusement park and rode the Riddler’s Revenge roller coaster.  Plaintiffs allege that while on the ride, their heads and bodies were jerked around like rag dolls to the point where they all sustained traumatic brain injuries.  Additionally, Plaintiffs allege that when they got off the ride, they exhibited post-concussion symptoms.  Serene also exhibited left facial droop and slurred speech.   

 

Plaintiffs have sued Defendant Magic Mountain LLC (Defendant) and others for: (1) General Negligence, (2) Premises Liability, (3) Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress, (4) Strict Products Liability – Manufacturing Defect, (5) Strict Products Liability – Design Defect, and (6) Strict Products Liability – Failure to Warn. 

 

On 8/15/25, Serene/Plaintiff served Defendant with Demands for Production of Documents, Set 3.  (Khorsandi Decl., Ex.1).   After extensions to respond, on 10/14/25, Defendant served responses, which included only objections in response to Demands 148, 149, 150 and 151.  (Id., Ex.2-3).  Defendant provided a verification on 10/16/25.  (Id., Ex.4).  On 11/5/25, the parties began to meet and confer regarding responses which Plaintiff found deficient.  (Id., Ex.5).  Defendant requested multiple extensions to respond to Plaintiff’s meet and confer letter.  (Id., Ex.6).  On 1/28/26, Defendant sent a letter claiming not to know what Plaintiff was requesting in Demands 148 and 149.  (Id., Ex.6).  Defendant also asserted that based on the privilege objections asserted, it would not provide any documents in response to Demands 150 and 151.  Id.  On 2/20/26, the parties counsel met and conferred telephonically without any resolution.  Id.

 

On 2/27/26, Plaintiff filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling Defendant  to, within 10 days, serve further verified responses and production of documents in response to Plaintiff’s demands for production of documents, Set 3, Nos. 148, 149, 150 and 151.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests an order imposing sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,600.00. 

 

On 2/27/26, Defendant served supplemental responses.  (Cherazaie Decl., Ex.F).  On 4/14/26, Plaintiff sent a meet and confer letter regarding Defendant’s supplemental responses.  (Id., Ex.G).  On 4/17/26, Defendant served a third supplemental response to the subject document demands.  (Id., Ex.H).    

 

On 7/14/26, Defendant filed and served an opposition to the motion.  On 7/20/26, Plaintiff filed and served a reply to the opposition.    

 

ANALYSIS

 

The reply states that “[o]nly after Plaintiff filed the instant Motion did Defendant finally serve substantive supplemental responses.”  (See Reply, p.3:1-2; Gonzalez Decl. ¶6).  As such, the issue of compelling further responses to the subject document demands is moot.  The imposition of sanctions is the only remaining issue.  (See Reply, generally). 

 

Contrary to the assertion in the opposition, the Court finds that Plaintiff is also entitled to an award of sanctions against Defendant and its counsel.  The parties agreed to 2/27/26 as the extended deadline for a motion to compel further responses.  (Khorsandi Decl., Ex.6 pdf 105).  After the 2/20/26 telephonic meet and confer, with regard to Demands 148, 149, 150 and 151, Defendant did not indicate that it would be serving any further responses or extending the deadline for Plaintiff to file a motion to compel further responses.  (Id., pdf 101).

 

Thereafter, Plaintiff filed the instant motion on 2/27/26 and served same on Defendant on 2/27/26 at 5:12 p.m.  (Gonzalez Decl., Ex.1).  On 2/27/26 at 6:33 p.m., Defendant served supplemental responses to Demands 148, 149 and 150, but did not serve a verification until 3/12/26.  (Id., Ex.2-3).  An unverified response is equivalent to no response.  Appleton (1988) 206 CA3d 632, 635.  On 4/14/26, Plaintiff sent a meet and confer letter regarding Defendant’s response to Demand 151.  (Id., Ex.4).  Defendant did not serve a supplemental response to Demand 151 until 4/17/26.  (Id., Ex.5).  Defendant served the corresponding verification on 4/27/26.  (Id., Ex.6).     

 

Defendant’s failure to provide supplemental/further responses before receiving Plaintiff’s motion on the agreed upon deadline for a motion to compel further responses caused Plaintiff to unnecessarily incur attorney’s fees and costs related to this motion. 

 

As such, the Court finds that sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, are warranted.  CCP 2031.310(h).  Further, the Court finds that the $2,600.00 in sanctions requested is reasonable.  (See Khorsandi Decl. ¶¶16-17).   

 

CONCLUSION

 

The request to compel further responses is moot.  Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is granted.  Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, are ordered to pay $2,600.00 in sanctions within 30 days.

 

Defendant’s request for sanctions is denied.

Case #24CHCV02702

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES

(Form Interrogatories, Set 1)

 

Motion filed on 3/13/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Serene Fakhouri

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Magic Mountain, LLC

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Defendant Magic Mountain, LLC to, within 10 days, serve a further verified response to Form Interrogatories, Set 1, Nos. 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 15.1 and 16.10.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests an order imposing sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,600.00.

 

RULING: The motion is granted as set forth below. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of injuries allegedly sustained by Plaintiffs Serene Fakhouri (Serene); Cassandra Fakhouri (Cassandra’s claims were dismissed  with prejudiced on 11/19/25) and Reyan Fakhouri (Reyan) (collectively, Plaintiffs) as a result of riding the Riddler’s Revenge roller coaster at Defendants Magic Mountain, LLC and Six Flags Entertainment Corporation’s theme park.  Reyan is the mother of Serene and Cassandra.  (Second Amended Complaint (SAC) ¶4). 

 

On 7/24/23, Plaintiffs visited Defendants’ amusement park and rode the Riddler’s Revenge roller coaster.  (SAC ¶22).  Plaintiffs allege that while on the ride, their heads and bodies were jerked around like rag dolls to the point where they all sustained traumatic brain injuries.  (See SAC ¶¶25-27).  Additionally, Plaintiffs allege that when they got off the ride, they exhibited post-concussion symptoms.  Id.  Serene also exhibited left facial droop and slurred speech.  Id. 

 

Plaintiffs have sued Defendant Magic Mountain LLC (Defendant) and others for: (1) General Negligence, (2) Premises Liability, (3) Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress, (4) Strict Products Liability – Manufacturing Defect, (5) Strict Products Liability – Design Defect, and (6) Strict Products Liability – Failure to Warn. 

 

On 8/25/25, Serene/Plaintiff served Defendant with Form Interrogatories, Set 1.  (Gonzalez Decl. Decl., Ex.1).  After several extensions, on 11/3/25, Defendant served responses.  (Id., Ex.2, 4).  On 12/30/25, the parties then began to meet and confer regarding responses which Plaintiff found deficient during which the parties agreed to extend the deadline to file a motion to compel further responses until 3/13/26.  (Id., Ex.3-4).  Ultimately, Defendant did not supplement its responses to Form Interrogatories Nos. 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 15.1 and 16.10.  (Id., Ex.4). 

 

Therefore, on 3/13/26, Plaintiff filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling Defendant to, within 10 days, serve a further verified response to Form Interrogatories, Set 1, Nos. 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 15.1 and 16.10.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests an order imposing sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,600.00.  Defendant has opposed the motion and Plaintiff has filed a reply to the opposition. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

The scope of discovery is broad allowing a party to obtain information regarding any matter that is not privileged which is relevant to the subject matter of the pending action or the determination of a motion, if the matter is itself admissible or appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.  See CCP 2017.010.

 

A party must respond separately under oath to each interrogatory by providing an answer containing the information requested; an exercise of the party’s option to produce writings or an objection to the interrogatory.  CCP 2030.210(a). 

 

CCP 2030.220 provides that: “(a) Each answer in a response to interrogatories shall be as complete and straightforward as the information reasonably available to the responding party permits. (b) If an interrogatory cannot be answered completely, it shall be answered to the extent possible. (c) If the responding party does not have personal knowledge sufficient to respond fully to an interrogatory, that party shall so state, but shall make a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain the information by inquiry to other natural persons or organizations, except where the information is equally available to the propounding party.”

 

On receipt of a response to interrogatories, if the propounding party deems an answer to an interrogatory is evasive or incomplete; an exercise of the option to produce documents is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate or an objection is without merit or too general, the propounding party may move to compel a further response.  CCP 2030.300(a).

 

Here, Defendant has improperly interposed meritless objections to each of the Form Interrogatories at issue and has otherwise failed to provide adequate responses.  Form Interrogatories 12.2, 12.3, 12.4 and 16.10 seek information as to whether Defendant has  interviewed individuals; whether Defendant has obtained written or recorded statements from any individual concerning the incident; whether Defendant knows of any photos, films or videos depicting any place, object or individual concerning the incident or Plaintiff’s injuries; and whether Defendant has any document concerning present physical, mental, or emotional condition of any plaintiff in this case from a health care provider not previously identified, except for expert witnesses.  If Defendant responds in the affirmative, each of the interrogatories requests follow-up information.  Defendant responded to each of these form interrogatories, which have been approved by the Judicial Council, with objections based on attorney-client and/or attorney work-product privileges.  Such objections are without merit as the interrogatories are merely requesting general information not any confidential communications between the attorney and client and/or the attorney’s work-product.

 

The substantive responses provided in response to these Interrogatories after these objections are also insufficient.  It cannot be determined whether any information is being withheld based on the meritless objections.  Additionally, Defendant has failed to provide all of the information requested by the subparts of the interrogatories.  Further, with regard to Form Interrogatory 12.4, Defendant has improperly limited the response to the incident.       

 

Form Interrogatory 15.1 asks Defendant to identify each denial of a material allegation and each special affirmative defense in its pleading and to provide supporting information for each.  Defendants objections on the grounds that the interrogatory is premature and seeks information protected by the attorney-client privilege and/or attorney work product doctrine are without merit.  If Defendant does not have any facts, witnesses, or documents to support its denials and/or defenses more than 2 years after this case was filed, it must state so in a verified discovery response. 

 

The Court finds that sanctions against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, are warranted.  CCP 2031.310(h).  Further, the Court finds that the $2,600.00 in sanctions requested is reasonable considering Plaintiff’s counsel has already reduced the amount from the actual time expended in relation to the interrogatories at issue and issues with interrogatories which were resolved before the filing of the motion.  (See Gonzalez Decl. ¶¶16-17).  

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.  Defendant is ordered to provide further responses, without objections, to Form Interrogatories, Set 1, Nos. 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 15.1 and 16.10 within 20 days.  Sanctions are imposed against Defendant and its counsel, Sanaz Cherazie and Amaro Baldwin, LLP, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,600.00, payable within 20 days.

 

Defendant’s request for sanctions is denied. 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 


Case Number: 24CHCV02884    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 8/7/26                                                                TRIAL DATE: 10/12/26

Case #24CHCV02884

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND ANSWER

 

Motion filed on 3/20/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and Audi Valencia

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiffs Victoria Noel Amoscato

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order granting Defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (Volkswagen) and Audi Valencia (Audi) leave to file amended Answers to include an additional “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion/Issue Preclusion” affirmative defenses.  Specifically, Volkswagen seeks to add a 29th affirmative defense of “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion/Issue Preclusion” and Audi seeks to add a 25th affirmative defense of “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion/Issue Preclusion.”

 

RULING: The motion is granted as set forth below. 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This lemon law case arises out of Plaintiffs Victoria Noel Amoscato and Tiny Bird Acupuncture/Wellness’ (Plaintiffs) lease and eventual purchase of a 2019 Audi Q3 (the Vehicle) which was manufactured and/or distributed and warranted by Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (Volkswagen).  (Complaint ¶¶7-10).  Plaintiffs contend the Vehicle suffered from defects, nonconformities, maladjustments or malfunctions which Defendant was unable to repair despite a reasonable number of attempts to do so.  (Id. ¶¶12-15).

 

On 8/9/24, Plaintiffs filed this action against Volkswagen and Defendant Audi Valencia (collectively, Defendants) for Breach of Express Warranty Obligations Under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act and Breach of Implied Warranty Obligations Under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.  On 9/9/24, Defendants each filed Answers to the complaint.

 

On 3/20/26, Defendants filed and served the instant motion seeking an order granting Defendants leave to file amended Answers to include an additional “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion/Issue Preclusion” affirmative defense.  Specifically, Volkswagen seeks to add a 29th affirmative defense of “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion/Issue Preclusion” and Audi seeks to add a 25th affirmative defense of “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion/Issue Preclusion.”

 

Plaintiffs have opposed the motion and Defendants have filed a reply to the opposition. 

 

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

Defendants’ Request for Judicial Notice is granted.  Plaintiffs’ objection to the Request for Judicial Notice is denied.

 

CCP 473(a) provides:

 

“(1) The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by adding or striking out the name of any party, or by correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect; and may, upon like terms, enlarge the time for answer or demurrer. The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.

(2) When it appears to the satisfaction of the court that the amendment renders it necessary, the court may postpone the trial, and may, when the postponement will by the amendment be rendered necessary, require, as a condition to the amendment, the payment to the adverse party of any costs as may be just.”

 

Leave to amend an answer is usually liberally granted because denying such leave will permanently deprive the defendant of asserting the proposed defense.  See Hulsey (1990) 218 CA3d 1150, 1159.  It is an abuse of discretion to deny such leave, even if sought at the time of trial, if there is no prejudice to the plaintiff and denial will deprive the defendant of the opportunity of asserting a defense.  See Higgins (1981) 123 CA3d 558, 564-565; Berman (1997) 56 CA4th 936, 945.

 

Defendants’ Answers already assert affirmative defenses of “res judicata” and “claim preclusion,” which Defendants contend apply to any claim based on an alleged sunroof defect.  (See 27th and 28th Affirmative Defenses in Volkswagen’s 9/9/24 Answer; 23rd and 24th Affirmative Defenses in Audi’s 9/9/24 Answer).  However, through discovery and further investigation, Defendants contend that they discovered: (1) the Vehicle is included in the Sunroof Class Action, (2) Plaintiffs did not opt out of the class action settlement, and (3) Plaintiffs  complained of sunroof leak issues with the Vehicle.  (Kim Decl. ¶¶4-5).  Based on the foregoing, Defendants seek to assert a specific “Release and Bar” affirmative defense expressly tied to the Sunroof Class Action to ensure that Defendants may fully assert that Plaintiffs have released any claims relating to alleged sunroof defects in the Vehicle.  

 

The opposition is based on the argument that Defendants inexplicably delayed in seeking leave to amend their answers which has caused substantial prejudice to Plaintiffs.  Plaintiffs have failed to establish that any delay in seeking leave to amend has caused the type of prejudice that would warrant denial of the motion. 

 

While the trial date has been set, it is still more than 2 months away and the jury is not about to be impaneled.  Contrast P&D Consultants, Inc. (2010) 190 CA4th 1332, 1345 citations omitted.  Additionally, Plaintiffs have failed to establish a lack of diligence on the part of Defendants and/or their attorneys in discovering the applicability of the proposed additional defense and/or seeking leave to add such defense.  While Defendants and/or their counsel may have had knowledge of the class action settlement when they filed the original answers, discovery in this case revealed its applicability to Plaintiffs and the claims made in this case.  Further, Defendants’ original answers already include defenses based on claim preclusion and res judicata.  The proposed additional defense merely narrows and clarifies the issues.

 

The opposition arguments regarding the applicability, scope and effect of the class action settlement on Plaintiffs’ claims improperly seek to adjudicate the merits of the proposed additional defense at this time. 

 

Any prejudice Plaintiffs claim by allowing the proposed amendments can be resolved by continuing the trial date, which has never been continued in this case, and extending the discovery cut off date to correspond to the  new trial date. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.  Defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and Audi Valencia are ordered to separately file their amended Answers.  The Court notes that the proposed First Amended Answer of Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. incorrectly titled the proposed 29th affirmative defense as “Reservation of Defenses” despite the fact that the body of the 29th affirmative defense relates to the affirmative defense of “Release and Bar/Res Judicata/Claim Preclusion.”  (See Kim Decl., Ex.C).  Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. is ordered to correct the error in the First Amended Answer filed and to serve a corrected copy of the First Amended Answer on Plaintiffs. 

 

The Court will discuss with counsel the need for a trial continuance.
