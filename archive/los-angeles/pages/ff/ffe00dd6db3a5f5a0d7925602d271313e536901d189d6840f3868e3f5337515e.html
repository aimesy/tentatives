DEPARTMENT 529 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV02725    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: 529

SAMHO TOUR, INC. v. A-JU TOURS, INC.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS (SET TWO)

 

Date of Hearing:          7/16/26                                   Trial Date:       11/23/26

Department:               529                                          Case No.:         25STCV02725 

 

Moving Party:             Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc.

Responding Party:       Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On January 31, 2025, Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the operative Complaint against Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. (“Defendant”), alleging a sole cause of action for violation of Unfair Competition. The Complaint alleges that Defendant advertised and listed a price for a product or service that did not include all required fees or charges in violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200. (Compl., ¶39.) The Complaint further alleges that Defendant failed to pay its workers the required minimum wages and for overtime hours worked, thus avoiding proper and full payment of unemployment insurance, workers’ compensation insurance premiums, social security tax, and Medicare tax in violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200. (Id.)

 

On April 7, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint and the operative Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200; and (2) violation of California False Adverting Law - Business and Professions Code Section 17500.

 

On May 9, 2025, Plaintiff filed an Answer to the Cross-Complaint. 

 

On June 16, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents (Set Two). On July 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition. On July 9, 2026, Defendant filed a Reply.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) is GRANTED in part. The request for sanctions is also DENIED.

 

Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. is ordered to provide further, verified and objection-free responses and produce responsive documents to Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) nos. 71-133, 134-173, and 174-175 within 30 days of this order.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A party who receives a demand for production, i.e., a demand for inspection, copying, testing, etc. must respond separately to each individual item requested with either a statement indicating compliance, a statement that the party lacks the ability to comply, or an objection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).)

 

A party may then move for a further response to requests for production of documents where an answer to the requests are evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

Notice of the motion must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).)

 

Finally, California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345 requires that all motions or responses involving further discovery contain a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses. (Cal. Rules Ct., Rule 3.1345, subd. (a)(3).)¿¿

 

ANALYSIS

 

Defendant moves for an order compelling Plaintiff to provide further responses to Defendant’s Request for Production of Documents (“RFP”) (Set Two) nos. 71-133, 134-173, and 174-175 on the grounds that these requests are reasonably calculated to lead to discovery of admissible evidence pertaining to (1) the parties competing unfair competition law violation claims, especially, Defendant’s unclean hands defense; and (2) Plaintiff’s representation to the United States government that was a transportation company versus a tour company to obtain a $7.5 million grant.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff admits that the “TOUR DOCUMENTS” requested per RFP nos. 71-133 are relevant to the claims and defenses advanced by the parties in this action. (Opp. at 2:17-19.) However, Plaintiff argues that the burden imposed by these requests is significant because it requires production of documents in many different forms including cash register receipts, invoices, restaurant bills, receipts from National Parks or hotels, and handwritten records. (Id. at 3:9-12.) Essentially, Plaintiff contends it would take dozen of hours to locate and assemble these records. (Shin Decl., ¶5.) An objection on the basis of “burden must be sustained by evidence showing the quantum of work required” and “to support an objection of oppression there must be some showing either of an intent to create an unreasonable burden or that the ultimate effect of the burden is incommensurate with the result sought.” (West Pico Furniture Co. of Los Angeles v. Superior Court In and For Los Angeles County (1961) 56 Cal.2d 407, 417.)  The court agrees that the requests are overbroad and burdensome.   Defendant is not entitled to “all documents” relating to this tours, but only to records of the hours worked by its employees on those tours and the wages earned, as well as any document calculating the net revenue or profit from each tour, if such calculations are made for each tour.  Plaintiff is ordered to produce these documents within 30 days.    

 

Plaintiff also concedes that the “EMPLOYMENT RECORDS” requested per RFP nos. 134-173 are relevant to the parties claims and counterclaims in this action. (Opp. at 3:21-4:6.) On the other hand, Plaintiff asserts that it does not keep expense reports submitted by its tour guides or bus drivers, therefore, the only way to comply is to search thousands of tour records conducted in 2024 and 2025 to determine which tours the 41 workers submitted expense reports for, which could take over a hundred hours if not more. (Id. at 4:5-14.)  The court agrees that the requests are overly broad and harassing and would invade the privacy rights of third parties (the employees).  Plaintiff is to provide records reflecting all hours worked by these employees and sums paid to them, whether as wages or reimbursements.  Such records to be produced within 30 days.  

 

With respect to RFP nos. 174-175, which seeks applications and supporting documentation submitted by Plaintiff to obtain “CERTS” a/k/a Coronavirus Economic Relief for Transportation Services grant in 2021, Plaintiff objected on the grounds that they are irrelevant to any of the claims or defenses in this action. Contrarily, Defendant indicates that these documents relate to its unclean hands defense and “CERTS” does not have to be mentioned specifically in Defendant’s Answer. (See Pacific Tel. & Tel. Co. v. Superior Court (1970) 2 Cal.3d 161, 173 [“[t]he scope of examination should not be limited unless the information sought is clearly privileged or irrelevant, and inquiry should not be limited to matter relevant only to the precise issues presented by the pleadings.’ ”].)

 

Accordingly, Defendant has demonstrated that further responses to the RFPs at issue are warranted.

 

Request for Monetary Sanctions

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure Section 2031.310, subdivision (h), “the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).)

 

Here, Defendant requests $10,523.41 against Plaintiff consisting of: (1) 1.0 hr attempting to meet and confer; (2) 7.0 hours preparing the motion and separate statement; (3) 3.0 hours reviewing the opposition, preparing the reply, and attending the hearing at an hourly rate of $950.00 plus $73.41 filing fee. (Florence Decl., ¶8.) The court finds that plaintiff’s position was substantially justified in opposing these requests and denies the request for sanctions.

 

 

SAMHO TOUR, INC. v. A-JU TOURS, INC.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL compliance with deposition subpoena

 

Date of Hearing:          7/16/26                                   Trial Date:       11/23/26

Department:               529                                          Case No.:         25STCV02725 

 

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.

Responding Party:       Non-Party Mutual Trans, Inc.  

 

BACKGROUND

 

On January 31, 2025, Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the operative Complaint against Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. (“Defendant”), alleging a sole cause of action for violation of Unfair Competition. The Complaint alleges that Defendant advertised and listed a price for a product or service that did not include all required fees or charges in violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200. (Compl., ¶39.) The Complaint further alleges that Defendant failed to pay its workers the required minimum wages and for overtime hours worked, thus avoiding proper and full payment of unemployment insurance, workers’ compensation insurance premiums, social security tax, and Medicare tax in violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200. (Id.)

 

On April 7, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint and the operative Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200; and (2) violation of California False Adverting Law - Business and Professions Code Section 17500.

 

On May 9, 2025, Plaintiff filed an Answer to the Cross-Complaint. 

 

On May 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Compliance with Deposition Subpoena. On June 10, 2026, Non-Party Mutual Trans, Inc. On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Compliance with Deposition Subpoena is GRANTED.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A motion to compel compliance with a deposition subpoena may be sought under either Code of Civil Procedure §§ 1987.1 or 2025.480. “If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), . . . may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).) “The following persons may make a motion pursuant to subdivision (a): [¶](1) A party. [¶](2) A witness. [¶](3) A consumer described in Section 1985.3. [¶](4) An employee described in Section 1985.6. [¶](5) A person whose personally identifying information, as defined in subdivision (b) of Section 1798.79.8 of the Civil Code, is sought in connection with an underlying action involving that person's exercise of free speech rights.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (b).)

 

ANALYSIS

 

Plaintiff moves to compel Non-Party Mutual Trans, Inc.’s (“MTI”) compliance with the deposition subpoena served on April 9, 2026 on the grounds that MTI is a participant or witness to Defendant’s alleged unlawful and unfair business practices because MTI provides Defendant with buses and bus drivers for its tours, thus the entities operate as a single enterprise. (Mot. at 4:20-25.) In opposition, MTI argues that the subpoena is overbroad and irrelevant because it is based on an unpleaded theory that it is a single enterprise with Defendant. (Opp. at 6:21-24.)

 

Here, it is undisputed that MTI provides transportation services, which includes providing Defendant with buses and bus drivers needed to operate Defendant’s tours. (Seror Decl., ¶12.) It also undisputed that the theory of “single enterprise” or alter ego is not pleaded in the operative Complaint. (Reply at 2:15-16.) Nevertheless, California courts have held that “[t]he scope of examination should not be limited unless the information sought is clearly privileged or irrelevant, and inquiry should not be limited to matter relevant only to the precise issues presented by the pleadings.’ ” (Pacific Tel. & Tel. Co. v. Superior Court (1970) 2 Cal.3d 161, 173 [emphasis added].) Thus, whether or not these claims were pleaded are inapposite for purposes of discovery. The subpoena seeks documents evidencing any contracts or agreement MTI entered into with Defendant from 7/1/24 through 12/31/25; documents regarding payments MTI received from Defendant from 7/1/24 through 12/31/25; documents identifying MTI’s officers and directors; and documents related to any services MTI provided to Defendant in connection to specific tours for specific dates in 2024. These documents are related to Plaintiff’s claims that Defendant engages in unfair and unlawful business practices by failing to pay its workers minimum wage and overtime hours. MTI contention that these requests are overbroad and burdensome disregards that most of these requests are limited in time and scope. For example, Request for Production no. 11 seeks: “All DOCUMENTS related to any services YOU provided to A-JU in connection with a tour described as Mode Signature, which ran from November 21, 2024 through November 29, 2024 and originated in Los Angeles.” (Plf. Sep. Stmt. at 31:27-32:2.) This request is limited to the Mode signature tour, which took place in Los Angeles from 11/21/24 through 11/29/24 (eight days).

 

The objection regarding vagueness also fails. “[N]either ambiguity nor burden are of themselves sufficient grounds for denying the right to discovery in toto, although either may, if present in sufficient degree, constitute an abuse of the discovery process which can and should be limited or eliminated under the court's discretionary power to make any order consistent with justice.” (Cembrook v. Superior Court In and For City and County of San Francisco (1961) 56 Cal.2d 423, 428.) “If [the court] finds some requests too ambiguous to allow intelligent reply, it may sustain objection to them or, more consistently with justice, it may order such questions to be rephrased.” (Id. at 430.) MTI has not shown that the requests are  ambiguous such that it cannot find the documents requested or provide an intelligible response. With respect to the objection based on confidential information, the parties entered into a Stipulated Protective Order, which expressly contemplated and incorporates protections for confidential documents produced by non-parties subject to subpoenas in this action. (Stip. Prot. Order, 2/4/26 at ¶13.) Accordingly, Plaintiff has demonstrated that compliance with the subpoena is warranted.

 

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Compliance with Deposition Subpoena is GRANTED.




SAMHO TOUR, INC. v. A-JU TOURS, INC.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS (SET TWO)

 

Date of Hearing:          7/16/26                                   Trial Date:       11/23/26

Department:               529                                          Case No.:         25STCV02725 

 

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.

Responding Party:       Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On January 31, 2025, Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the operative Complaint against Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. (“Defendant”), alleging a sole cause of action for violation of Unfair Competition. The Complaint alleges that Defendant advertised and listed a price for a product or service that did not include all required fees or charges in violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200. (Compl., ¶39.) The Complaint further alleges that Defendant failed to pay its workers the required minimum wages and for overtime hours worked, thus avoiding proper and full payment of unemployment insurance, workers’ compensation insurance premiums, social security tax, and Medicare tax in violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200. (Id.)

 

On April 7, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint and the operative Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 17200; and (2) violation of California False Adverting Law - Business and Professions Code Section 17500.

 

On May 9, 2025, Plaintiff filed an Answer to the Cross-Complaint. 

 

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents (Set Two). On July 2, 2026, Defendant filed an Opposition. On July 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Further Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) is GRANTED. The request for sanctions is also GRANTED.

 

Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. is ordered to provide further, verified and objection-free responses to Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) nos. 47 through 49 within 30 days of this order.

Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. is also ordered to pay $2633.00 in monetary sanctions to Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. within 30 days of this order.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A party who receives a demand for production, i.e., a demand for inspection, copying, testing, etc. must respond separately to each individual item requested with either a statement indicating compliance, a statement that the party lacks the ability to comply, or an objection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).)

 

A party may then move for a further response to requests for production of documents where an answer to the requests are evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

Notice of the motion must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).)

 

Finally, California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345 requires that all motions or responses involving further discovery contain a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses. (Cal. Rules Ct., Rule 3.1345, subd. (a)(3).)¿¿

 

EVIDENTIARY OBJECTIONS

 

Defendant objections to paragraphs 2, 8, and attorney’s fees paragraphs 1 and 3 in the declaration of Matthew L. Seror in support of Plaintiff’s compel further motion. Objections based on lack of foundation, hearsay, and lack of personal knowledge should be overruled unless there is a reason to believe that a witness, including an expert witness, would not be able to lay a foundation and authenticate these facts at trial. (See Sweetwater Union High School Dist. v. Gilbane Building Co. (2019) 6 Cal.5th 931, 947-948.) Thus, Defendant’s objections are OVERRULED.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

Plaintiff moves for an order compelling Defendant to provide further responses and responsive documents to Plaintiff’s Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) nos. 47, 48, and 49 on the grounds that these requests seek information (1) regarding the scope and nature of the relationship between Defendant and Mutual Trans, Inc. (“MTI”); and (2) whether Defendant outsources its unlawful and unfair business practice of underpaying its bus drivers to MTI. (Mot. at 8:20-9:9, Seror Decl., ¶14, Ex. G.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that these requests are not narrowly tailored to discovering evidence regarding driver compensation and are cumulative and duplicative of the discovery Plaintiff seeks from MTI via subpoena. (Opp. at 4:1-23.) However, the purpose for which Plaintiff seeks these documents from Defendant is to discover admissible evidence related to whether or not MTI and Defendant truly have a “vendor-vendee” relationship as opposed to a “alter ego” or “single enterprise” type of relationship, wherein Defendant’s interrogatory answers contend that the former is true. Likewise, there is no guarantee that the documents that Defendant has in its possession that are responsive to these requests are identical to documents MTI has in its possession. Therefore, Plaintiff has shown that good cause exists to seek these documents.

 

Request for Monetary Sanctions

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure Section 2031.310, subdivision (h), “the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).)

 

Here, Plaintiff requests $3,507.00 against Defendant consisting of: (1) 0.45 hours revising and reviewing the motion; (2) 2.0 hours reviewing the opposition, preparing the reply brief, and attending the hearing  at an hourly rate of 740.00 for Mr. Seror; and (3) 12.4 hours researching, drafting, and preparing the instant motion by Lauren Kilger at an hourly rate of $205.00. To the extent that Mr. Seror is Ms. Kilger’s superior, it is common practice to attest her attorney’s fees request and Defendant’s cite to no case or statutory law prohibiting such conduct.  While the court finds the hourly rates reasonable, the amount to time spent by Kilger is not. The court reduces the sanction to reflect 4 hours of Kilger’s time as the reasonable amount to spend on this simple motion. 

 

Accordingly, the Court awards monetary sanctions in the amount of $2633.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Further Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) is GRANTED. The request for sanctions is also GRANTED.

 

Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. is ordered to provide further, verified and objection-free responses to Request for Production of Documents (Set Two) nos. 47 through 49 within 30 days of this order.

 

Defendant A-Ju Tours, Inc. is also ordered to pay $2633 in monetary sanctions to Plaintiff Samho Tour, Inc. within 30 days of this order.

 

 


Case Number: 26STCP02137    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: 529

INTELIFUND, LLC v. M.A.

 

motion for an order approving transfer of structured settlement payment rights

 

Date of Hearing:          July 16, 2026                           Trial Date:       None

Department:               529                                          Case No.:         26STCP02137

 

Moving Party:             Petitioner Interlifund, LLC

Responding Party:       None

 

BACKGROUND

 

On June 8, 2026, Petitioner Interlifund, LLC (“Petitioner”) commenced this action, and on June 10, 2026, Petitioner filed the operative First Amended Petition for Approval of Transfer of Structured Settlement Payment Rights (the “Petition”) on behalf of real party in interest, Magely Amezcua (“Payee”).

 

On June 12, 2026, Petitioner filed the instant motion for an order approving of Payee’s transfer of structured settlement payment rights to Petitioner.

 

As of July 13, 2026, no opposition has been filed.

 

Ruling

 

The Motion for an Order Approving Transfer of Structured Settlement Payment Rights is GRANTED.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Legal Standard

 

“A direct or indirect transfer of structured settlement payment rights is not effective and a structured settlement obligor or annuity issuer is not required to make any payment directly or indirectly to any transferee of structured settlement payment rights unless the transfer has been approved in advance in a final court order based on express written findings by the court that:

 

(1)   The transfer is in the best interest of the payee, taking into account the welfare and support of the payee’s dependents.

(2)   The payee has been advised in writing by the transferee to seek independent professional advice regarding the transfer and has either received that advice or knowingly waived, in writing, the opportunity to receive the advice.

(3)   The transferee has complied with the notification requirements pursuant to paragraph (2) of subdivision (f), the transferee has provided the payee with a disclosure form that complies with Section 10136, and the transfer agreement complies with Sections 10136 and 10138.

(4)   The transfer does not contravene any applicable statute or the order of any court or other government authority.

(5)   The payee understands the terms of the transfer agreement, including the terms set forth in the disclosure statement required by Section 10136.

(6)   The payee understands and does not wish to exercise the payee’s right to cancel the transfer agreement.”

(Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (a).) 

“When determining whether the proposed transfer should be approved, including whether the transfer is fair, reasonable, and in the payee’s best interest, taking into account the welfare and support of the payee’s dependents, the court shall consider the totality of the circumstances,” including the 15 circumstances described in Section 10139.5(b). (Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (b).) 

“Following a transfer of structured settlement payment rights ...,” “[t]he structured settlement obligor and the annuity issuer shall, as to all parties except the transferee, be discharged and released from any and all liability for the transferred payments.” (Ins. Code, § 10139.5, subd. (e)(1)).) 

All future statutory references are to the Insurance Code unless otherwise specified.

Discussion

 

The Court makes the following findings pursuant to Section 10139.5.

 

A.     Section 10139.5(a) Requirements

 

(1)   Section 10139.5(a)(1) requirements have been satisfied: The transfer agreement is in the best interest of the Payee.

 

In support of the motion, Payee has submitted a declaration, attesting to the following facts.

 

Payee entered into the original structured settlement agreement in 2016, when she was a minor. (Motion, Exh. 5 (Amezcua Decl.), ¶ 3, 4.) The agreement arose from a settlement of a third party, personal injury action she brought against Lamon School District. (Id. ¶ 4.) As part of the settlement in that case, Plaintiff received the following structured settlement payments: “96 monthly payments of $500.00 beginning on September 24, 2027; four [4] annual payment of $15,000.00 starting on September 24, 2026; a lump sum payment of $20,000.00 due and payable on September 24, 2027; a lump sum payment of $25,000.00 due and payable on September 24, 2030; and a lump sum payment of $51,066.37 due and payable on September 4, 2035[.]” (Ibid.) The sum of those amounts equals $204,066.37.

 

Payee and Petitioner have entered into a Structured Settlement Payments Purchase Agreement (the “Agreement”), which states that, in exchange for $74,879.87, Payee has agreed to sell, assign, and convey the following structured settlement payments (totaling $159,066.37) to Petitioner: “96 monthly payments of $500.00 beginning on September 24, 2027 through and including August 24, 2035 [¶] •1 lump sum payment of $15,000.00 due and payable on September 24, 2026 [¶] •1 lump sum payment of $20,000.00 due and payable on September 24, 2027 [¶] •1 lump sum payment of $25,000.00 due and payable on September 24, 2030 [¶]•1 lump sum payment of $51,066.37 due and payable on September 4, 2035 (‘Assigned Payments’).” (Motion, Exh. 2 – a copy of the Agreement, p. 1.)

 

The Court finds that Payee has established the transfer will be in her best interest. Payee testifies that the original settlement was intended as compensation for her injuries. (Amezcua Decl., ¶ 3.) However, she is no longer being treated for those injuries. (Id. ¶¶ 3, 4.) In addition, she is 20 years old, single, and has no minor children or other dependents. (Id. ¶ 4.) She makes $3,000 per month from her employment as a receptionist. (Ibid.) She intends to use the $74,879.87 she will receive from Petitioner under the Agreement to purchase a new vehicle and get into a new apartment closer to her work. (Ibid.)

 

Therefore, the Court finds Section 10139.5(a)(1) satisfied.

 

(2)   Section 10139.5(a)(2) requirements have been satisfied: Payee has been advised in writing by the transferee to seek independent professional advice regarding the transfer and has knowingly waived, in writing, the opportunity to receive the advice.

 

Payee states that she was advised to seek independent counsel and financial advice in connection with this Petition and understands the nature and terms of the Agreement but has chosen not to seek such advice and wants to move forward with the transaction. (Amezcua Decl., ¶¶ 11, 13, 14.) Therefore, the Court finds Section 10139.5(a)(2) satisfied.

 

(3)    Section 10139.5(a)(3) requirements have been satisfied.

 

Section 10139.5(a)(3) requires the Court to find: “The transferee has complied with the notification requirements pursuant to paragraph (2) of subdivision (f) [of the statute], the transferee has provided the payee with a disclosure form that complies with Section 10136, and the transfer agreement complies with Sections 10136 and 10138.”

Petitioner has submitted a copy of the disclosure form required by Section 10136. (Motion, Exh. 4 – a copy of the “Disclosure Notice Required By Law”.) The statute required Petitioner to provide Payee with the Disclosure required by that statute at least 10 days before she signed the Agreement. Here, Payee signed the Agreement on May 24, 2026, before signing the Disclosure on May 26, 2026. However, Payee testifies that she received the Disclosure at least 10 days before she signed the Agreement. (Amezcua Decl., ¶ 10.)

 

Therefore, the Court’s finds that Petitioner complied with Section 10136.

 

The Court also finds that the Agreement complies with Section 10138 because it does not contain any of the voidable and unenforceable provisions prohibited by that statute.

Lastly, the Court finds that Petitioner has complied with Section 10139.5(f)(2) requirements, including submitting proof of service showing compliance with the notification requirements under that statute.

(4)   Section 10139.5(a)(4) requirements have not been satisfied: The transfer agreement contains language that is not fair, reasonable, and/or in the best interest of the Payee within the meaning of Section 10137.

 

Section 10139.5(a)(4) requires the Court to ensure that the proposed transfer does not contravene any applicable statute (e.g., Section 10137).

 

Section 10137 states: “A transfer of structured settlement payment rights is void unless a court reviews and approves the transfer and finds the following conditions are met:

 

(a)    The transfer of the structured settlement payment rights is fair and reasonable and in the best interest of the payee, taking into account the welfare and support of his or her dependents.

 

(b)   The transfer complies with the requirements of this article, will not contravene other applicable law, and the court has reviewed and approved the transfer as provided in Section 10139.5.”

 

The Court finds the Agreement to be fair and reasonable and in the best interest of the Payee. 

 

(5)   Section 10139.5(a)(5) requirements have been satisfied: Payee understands the terms of the transfer agreement, including the terms set forth in the disclosure statement required by Section 10136.

 

The Court finds that Section 10139.5(a)(5) requirements have been satisfied because Payee has testified that she understands the terms of the Agreement and Disclosure. (Amezcua Decl., ¶¶ 10, 13.)

 

(6)   Section 10139.5(a)(6) requirements have been satisfied: Payee understands and does not wish to exercise the payee’s right to cancel the transfer agreement.

The Court finds that Section 10139.5(a)(6) requirements have been satisfied because Payee has testified that she does not wish to exercise her right to cancel the agreement. (Amezcua Decl., ¶ 14.)

B.      Section 10139.5 (b) Requirements

The Court has considered the totality of the circumstances concerning the Agreement and finds all other parts of the Agreement to be fair, reasonable, and in the Payee’s best interests.

C.       Section 10139.5(c) Requirements

The Court finds that Petitioner has satisfied the requirements under Section 10139.5(c) including providing the Payee’s name, address, and age. (Amezcua Decl., ¶ 4.)

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Motion for an Order Approving Transfer of Structured Settlement of Payment Rights is GRANTED.
