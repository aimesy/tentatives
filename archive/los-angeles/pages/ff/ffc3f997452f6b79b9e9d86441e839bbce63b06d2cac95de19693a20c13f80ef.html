Darren Cain v. Bellhops Moving, LLC

Case No.: 24STCV09965

Hearing Date: August 12, 2026

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

The Court hereby GRANTS final approval and awards/approves the following:

(1) $38,333.33 for attorney fees to Class Counsel, Haines Law Group, APC; (2) $11,438.35 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) enhancement payment of $5,000 to the class representative, Darren Cain; (4) $7,500 (75% of $10,000 PAGA penalty) to the LWDA; and (5) $3,000 for settlement administration costs to Phoenix Settlement Administrators.

 

The Court will set a non-appearance date of August 24, 2027 for review of a final report, which shall be filed by August 20, 2027.

 

BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff Darren Cain sued his former employer, Defendant Bellhops Moving, LLC, for alleged wage and hour violations. Defendant operates a residential and corporate moving service throughout California. Plaintiff seeks to represent a class of Defendant’s current and former non-exempt employees. 

On April 19, 2024, Plaintiff filed the initial class action complaint against Defendant asserting causes of action for: (1) Failure To Pay All Overtime Wages (Labor Code §§ 204, 510, 558, 1194, 1198); (2) Minimum Wage Violations (Labor Code §§ 1182.12, 1194, 1194.2, and 1197); (3) Meal Period Violations (Labor Code §§ 226.7, 512, 558); (4) Rest Period Violations (Labor Code §§ 226.7, 516, 558); (5) Failure To Reimburse Necessary Business Expenditures (Labor Code §§ 2802, 2804); (6) Wage Statement Violations (Labor Code § 226, et seq.); (7) Waiting Time Penalties (Labor Code §§ 201-203); and (8) Unfair Competition (Bus & Prof Code § 17200, et seq.).  

On June 24, 2024, after exhausting his administrative remedies with the LWDA, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint to add a ninth cause of action for civil penalties under PAGA, premised on the same underlying Labor Code violations.

On January 23, 2025, the parties participated in a mediation session with Kevin Barnes, Esq., which ultimately resulted in settlement. The terms of settlement were finalized in the long-form Stipulation of Settlement (“Settlement Agreement”), a copy of which was filed with the Court on August 19, 2025. 

On January 27, 2026, after the parties filed further briefing and a revised Class Notice to address concerns raised by the Court, preliminary approval of the settlement was granted.

Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered (see Declaration of Kevin Lee (“Lee Decl.”).) Now before the Court is the Motion for Final Approval of the Settlement.

 

SETTLEMENT CLASS DEFINITION

 

·         “Settlement Class” means: All current and former non-exempt employees who worked for Defendant in California between April 19, 2020 and the date of preliminary approval or May 3, 2025, whichever is earlier (the “Class Period”). (¶1)

·         “PAGA Aggrieved Employees” means: All current and former non-exempt employees who worked for Defendant in California between April 19, 2023 and the date of preliminary approval or May 3, 2025, whichever is earlier (the “PAGA Period”). (Id.)

(¶4.B.ii)

o   Waiting Time Amount: Twenty-Five percent (25%) of the Net Settlement Amount shall be designated as the “Waiting Time Amount.” The Waiting Time Amount shall be distributed in equal, pro-rata shares to each participating Settlement Class member who separated their employment from Defendant at any time between April 19, 2021 and the end of the Class Period. (¶4.B.iii)

o   The remainder of the Net Settlement Amount will be distributed to each participating Settlement Class member based on their proportionate number of workweeks worked during the Class Period, by multiplying the remaining Net Settlement Amount by a fraction, the numerator of which is the Settlement Class member’s total workweeks worked during the Class Period, and the denominator of which is the total number of workweeks worked by all participating Settlement Class members during the Class Period. ANALYSIS OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

 

A.     Does a presumption of fairness exist? 

The Court preliminarily found in its Order of January 27, 2026 that the presumption of fairness should be applied.  No facts have come to the Court’s attention that would alter that preliminary conclusion.  Accordingly, the settlement is entitled to a presumption of fairness as set forth in the preliminary approval order.

B.      Is the settlement fair, adequate, and reasonable?

The settlement was preliminarily found to be fair, adequate and reasonable.  Notice has now been given to the Class and the LWDA. 

  Reaction of the class members to the proposed settlement.

Number of class members: 109 (Lee Decl., ¶3.)  

Number of notice packets mailed: 109 (Id. at ¶5.)    

Number of undeliverable notices: 0 (Id. at ¶7.)  

Number of opt-outs: 0 (Id. at ¶8.)

Number of objections: 0 (Id. at ¶9.)

Number of participating class members: 109 (Id. at ¶11.)

Average individual payment: $433.29 (Id. at ¶14.)

Highest individual payment: $2,942.02 (Ibid.)

The Court finds that the notice was given as directed and conforms to due process requirements.  Given the reactions of the Class Members and the LWDA to the proposed settlement and for the reasons set for in the Preliminary Approval order, the settlement is found to be fair, adequate, and reasonable.

 

C.      Attorney Fees and Costs

            Class Counsel requests an award of $38,333.33 in fees and $11,438.35 in costs. (Memo ISO Final at 3:24-25.) The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $38,333.33 (33 1/3%) in fees and $15,000 in costs (¶3.C.4).

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 254.)  Here, class counsel request attorney fees using the percentage method, as crosschecked by lodestar. (Memo ISO Final at pp. 11-15.)

In common fund cases, the Court may employ a percentage of the benefit method, as cross-checked against the lodestar. (Laffitte v. Robert Half Int’l, Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.5th 480, 503.) The fee request represents one-third of the gross settlement amount, which is the average generally awarded in class actions.  (See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 [“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”].)

Counsel’s percentage-based fee request is lower than the unadjusted lodestar, and would represent application of a multiplier of approximately 0.45x.

Here, the fee request represents a reasonable percentage of the total funds paid by Defendant. Notice of the fee request was provided to class members in the notice packet and no one objected. (Lee Decl., ¶12, Exhibit A.)

 

As for costs, Class Counsel is requesting a cost amount of $11,438.35. This is less than the $15,000 cap estimated at preliminary approval, which was not objected to. (Lee Decl., ¶12, Exhibit A.) Counsel represents that their costs include but are not limited to: filing and service fees, copying and postage, and mediation fee. (Moen Decl. ISO Final, ¶28, Exhibit 3.) The costs appear to be reasonable in amounts and reasonably necessary for this litigation.

 

Based on the above, the recommendation is to award $38,333.33 in fees and $11,438.35 in costs.

 

D.     Incentive Award

The class representative, Darren Cain, seeks an enhancement payment of $5,000 for his contributions to the action. (Memo ISO Final at 16:10-11.)    

In connection with the final fairness hearing, named Plaintiffs must submit declarations attesting to why they should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.  The named Plaintiffs must explain why they “should be compensated for the expense or risk he has incurred in conferring a benefit on other members of the class.”  (Clark v. American Residential Services LLC (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 785, 806.)  Trial courts should not sanction enhancement awards of thousands of dollars with “nothing more than pro forma claims as to ‘countless’ hours expended, ‘potential stigma’ and ‘potential risk.’ Significantly more specificity, in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of financial or other risks incurred by the named plaintiffs, is required in order for the trial court to conclude that an enhancement was ‘necessary to induce [the named plaintiff] to participate in the suit . . . .’”  (Id. at 806-807, italics and ellipsis in original.)

Plaintiff represents that his contributions to this litigation include: searching for documents for use in the lawsuit, providing information and documents to his attorneys, having calls with them to discuss his claims and Defendant’s practices, discussing the case status and strategy, assisting his attorneys in preparing for mediation, and reviewing the settlement. He estimates spending 15-20 hours on the case. (Declaration of Darren Cain ISO Final, ¶¶4-9.)    

Based on the above, as well as the benefits obtained on behalf of the class, the recommendation is to grant the enhancement payment in the amount of $5,000 to Plaintiff.

 

E.      Settlement Administration Costs

            The settlement administrator, Phoenix, is requesting $3,000 for the costs of settlement administration. (Lee Decl., ¶17.) This equals the estimate of $3,000 provided for in the Settlement Agreement (¶3.C.2) and disclosed to class members in the Notice, to which there were no objections. (Lee Decl., ¶12, Exhibit A.) Based on the above, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $3,000.
