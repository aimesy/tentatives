Case Number: 23VECV00281    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Sexton v. Kenmore Appliances, Case no. 23VECV00281

Hearing date September 10, 2026

Defendants Electrolux & White Consolidated’s Motion for Summary Judgment

The estate of Sexton and plaintiffs R.M. and R.S. Beltz sue defendants Kenmore Appliances, Frigidaire, Electrolux Home Products, Inc., landlord Conover and others for personal injury and wrongful death. Decedent, who leased a converted garage apartment from Conover, was killed when the stove exploded. Electrolux Consumer Products, Inc. and White Consolidated Industries, Inc. (“WCI”), the remaining defendants, jointly move for summary judgment.

Plaintiffs’ opposition filed 7/20/26 advanced a causation theory that a faulty knob allowed the stove to actuate, causing the explosion. Plaintiffs requested a continuance pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. §437c(h), arguing depositions of Electrolux employees Graham, Strickland and Parker were anticipated to produce testimony regarding knowledge and failure to recall. See Decl. Hernandez, para. 28, exh. BB. The court granted the request, continued the hearing and allowed supplemental briefing. 8/5/26 Min. Order.

A motion for summary judgement must be granted if no triable issue of material fact exists as to each cause of action. Code Civ. Proc. §437c(a), (c); Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843. To obtain summary judgment, moving party must prove each element of the cause of action. Harris v. Rudin, Richman & Appel (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 1332, as modified Mar. 5, 2002. A defendant can do so by showing plaintiff cannot establish one or more elements of the claim or there is a complete defense to that claim. Aguilar, supra, at 853. Once moving party meets this burden, the burden shifts to responding party to show a triable issue of material fact as to that cause of action or a defense thereto. Harris, supra, at 1337.

Defendants move for summary judgment on the negligence and strict products liability claims, as well as the derivative survival claim. Defendants argue plaintiffs have not produced evidence of causation linking the allegedly defective knob to decedent’s injuries. Causation is a necessary element of negligence and strict products liability claims. See Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917; see also Soule v. GM Corp. (1994) 8 Cal.4th 548, 560. The survival cause of action can only be sustained if one or more of plaintiffs’ other claims survives summary judgment. See Code Civ. Proc. §377.34.

On 2/5/21 an explosion occurred in the converted garage apartment unit owned by Conover. SSMF 1. The explosion caused injuries which lead to decedent’s death. Decedent leased the premises for approximately 3 years prior to his death. SSMF 2. Decedent stated immediately prior to the explosion, he attempted to light the subject stove. SSMF 3.

The stove was manufactured by Electrolux in 2013 for Sears under the Sears Kenmore brand. SSMF 6. Sears filed for bankruptcy in October 2018. SSMF 7. Sears was responsible for the ultimate design of the stove, sale and distribution. SSMF 8-9. WCI did not design, manufacture, or distribute the stove. SSMF 10.

The stove was designed and certified to applicable ANSI Z21.1/CSA 1.1 standard for Household Cooking Gas Appliances. SSMF 11, 27. There were no known defects when the stove left the custody and control of Electrolux. SSMF 12. There were no known complaints or issues with the stove following its purchase and installation in the subject property. SSMF 13-16.

Following the explosion, Los Angeles Fire Department (“LAFD”) and Southern California Gas Company (“SoCal Gas”) personnel inspected the scene. SSMF 19. SoCal Gas personnel pressure-tested the gas lines and appliances and found no measurable gas leak. SSMF 20. Per the LAFD report, “[t]he circumstances leading to the explosion and what caused natural gas to fill the room are unknown.” SSMF 21.

On 2/24/21 investigators retained by Conover’s insurance company State Farm and investigators retained by plaintiffs pressure-tested the gas system and stove. SSMF 22. State Farm’s investigator concluded the stove did “not have any gas leaks at the time of the fire/explosion.” SSMF 23. In response to discovery, plaintiffs failed to produce any evidence showing a defect in the stove or establishing causation. SSMF 29.

The undisputed facts presented by defendants were sufficient to shift the burden to plaintiffs. In response, plaintiffs were granted leave to conduct further discovery, deposing Electrolux employees Graham and Parker. Plaintiffs argue their testimony establishes a triable issue of fact as to “the [s]ubject [s]tove’s defective design… [and] triable issue of fact related to their negligent failure to warn.” Sup. Opp., 1:25-27.

Graham attended the CPSC Independent/Joint Gas and Electric Range Knob Working Group Meetings on 6/27/24, 3/18/25, 4/15/25 and 6/10/25. Supp. Decl. Hernandez, exh. 3, 64:15-19, 146:15-147:6, 243:21-244:4, 245:14-247:4. Parker attended the CPSC Independent/Joint Gas and Electric Range Knob Working Group Meetings on 6/27/24, 1/14/25, 3/18/25, 1/29/26 and 3/18/26. Id. at exh. 4, 19:2-18, 63:7-64:18, 65:2-68:7, 128:2-130:19, 118:19-119:10.

Electrolux employees Chad Strickland, Peter Silman and Steve Baas are identified as attendees at CPSC Independent/Joint Gas and Electric Range Knob Working Group Meetings. All were confirmed as having roles related to safety and compliance: (1) Graham: Senior Safety and Compliance Engineer (see Decl. Hernandez, exh. 3, 119:17-21); (2) Parker: Senior Safety and Compliance Engineer (Id. at exh. 4, 9:11-14); (3) Strickland: Director of Product Safety and Group and Graham’s and Parker’s supervisor (Id. at exh. 3, 66:3-66:15); (4) Silman: Safety and Compliance Manager (Id. at 139:13-140:3); (5) Baas: Safety and Compliance Department (Id. at 141:17-142:16).

Plaintiffs argue the Graham and Parker depositions establish the inadvertent activation, or actuation, of gas ranges was discussed at the CPSC meetings attended by them and other Electrolux representatives. Decl. Hernandez, exh. 3, 40:11-43:7; exh. 4, 84:21-85:2, 85:7-11. Plaintiffs argue the CPSC was aware of incidents of fire and deaths due to inadvertent activation of range knobs, whether from people bumping the knobs or pets activating the range. Id. at exh. R, CPSC Meeting Log, 000002-03. This does not establish causation in the instant case.

The LAFD, while unable to determine how the subject property filled with natural gas, did conclude the explosion originated from the kitchen area when decedent attempted to light the subject stove. SSAMF 2-3. Fire Investigator Beutz of Advanced Analysis Inc.’s report documents (1) the Subject Electrolux Gas Range – identified as “the natural gas cooktop” – was unable to be ruled out “as having been the source of the ignition” and (2) the material first ignited was “explosive gas vapors.” SSAMF 8. Plaintiffs argue these facts, coupled with the purportedly known and widespread risk of accidental actuation (see SSAMF 12-17), are sufficient to create a triable issue of fact as to causation. Plaintiffs offer the declaration of Fire Investigator Mumper to assert Electrolux was on notice of the alleged dangers. SSAMF 18, 31, 35.

The threshold for products liability requires evidence of causation. “[T]he plaintiff bears an initial burden of making ‘a prima facie showing that the injury was proximately caused by the product’s design.’ This showing requires evidence that the plaintiff was injured while using the product in an intended or reasonably foreseeable manner and that the plaintiff’s ability to avoid injury was frustrated by the absence of a safety device, or by the nature of the product’s design.” Perez v. VAS S.p.A. (2010) 188 Cal.App.4th 658, 678. Plaintiffs failed to proffer any evidence, such as investigation, declarations, reports or signs of damage on the subject stove itself, to establish causation.

Plaintiffs offer no evidence that the alleged risk of accidental actuation occurred in this case. Testimony from Electrolux employees regarding general issues discussed at CPSC meetings three years after the subject incident establish neither causation nor knowledge by either defendant. Nothing in Parker’s or Graham’s testimony states CPSC attributed any alleged inadvertent knob actuation complaints to Electrolux appliances generally, let alone regarding the stove at issue.

Plaintiffs fail to provide evidence that decedent inadvertently actuated the subject stove knob, or other evidence that the subject stove was the actual or proximate cause of the explosion. No question of material fact has been raised. It is insufficient that investigative reports regarding the incident failed to identify an alternative cause; the reports did not identify the subject stove as the cause. Speculation alone cannot create a triable issue of material fact. Plaintiffs’ own expert witnesses and investigators have not stated the subject stove caused the explosion. The court granted plaintiffs leave to conduct additional discovery on these issues. Plaintiffs fail to meet their burden to show a triable issue of material fact as to causation. Thus, the motion must be GRANTED.

Tentative Ruling

Hernandez v. FCA US, Case no. 24VECV01456

Hearing date September 10, 2026

Plaintiff Hernandez’s Motion to Compel Compliance

Plaintiff Hernandez, in pro se following counsel’s motion to be relieved, sues defendant FCA US, LLC, alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Act. Plaintiff moves to compel compliance with defendant’s alleged promise to serve further responses to RFPs, set one, and for monetary sanctions. Defendant opposes.

If a party filing a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling under Sections 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, 2031.240, and 2031.280 thereafter fails to permit the inspection, copying, testing, or sampling in accordance with that party’s statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance. Code Civ. Proc. §2031.320.

Plaintiff served RFPs, set one, on defendant 6/5/25. Decl. Rucker, para. 4, exh. 1. Defendant served initial responses on 12/11/25, wherein defendant promised further production would occur as to requests nos. 1-2, 4-8, 14, 87-95, 97-98, 100 and 104. Id. at para. 5, exh. 2. No production occurred as of the 4/6/26 filing of this motion. Id. at para. 6.

Defendant argues the parties were operating under “an informal arrangement for all cases between [defendant] and [plaintiff’s former counsel.]” Decl. Hugret, para. 4. Defendant represents this arrangement allowed plaintiff’s counsel to serve discovery requests, while defendant received an open-ended extension to respond. Id. at para. 5. Defendant asserts plaintiff, once in pro se, at no point demanded responses prior to filing this motion. Id. at para. 5. Defendant served responses on 5/8/26. Id. at para. 6. No evidence of such request by plaintiff, in pro se, was provided.

Plaintiff’s counsel’s motion to withdraw on the basis of inability to contact plaintiff was granted. See decl., 8/4/26 Min. Order. No reply has been filed contesting defendant’s characterization of the agreement between defendant and plaintiff’s former counsel. There is no basis to find this motion was necessary, nor evidence that meet-and-confer efforts occurred prior to filing. No sanctions will issue. As responses have been served, the request for further responses is MOOT.


Case Number: 24VECV06200    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Boyakyan v. Jaguar Land Rover North America, Case no. 24VECV06200

Hearing date September 10, 2026

Defendant Jaguar’s Motion to Compel Discovery - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Boyakyan sues defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC, alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Act. Defendant moves to compel responses to form and special interrogatories, set one, and requests for production, set one. The motion is unopposed.

“If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the following rules apply... (b) The party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling response to the interrogatories.” Code Civ. Proc. §2030.290.

Defendant served discovery on 2/3/26, with responses due 3/9/26. Decl. Calabia, paras. 4-5. No extensions were requested or granted; plaintiff failed respond. Id. Following the deadline, defendant met-and-conferred with plaintiff’s counsel, who represented multiple times that responses would be served. Id. at para. 6, exh. E. No responses were served.

Defendant served supplemental discovery requests on plaintiff 5/27/26. Decl. Calabia, para. 7, exhs. F-G. Plaintiff failed to serve responses to the supplemental requests. Id at paras. 8-10. An order compelling production, without objection, is warranted.

Defendant requests sanctions of $1,000 against plaintiff and plaintiff’s counsel. Sanctions are mandatory and sanctions against counsel are warranted for the persistent failure to produce responses, despite representations that responses would be forthcoming. Defendant requests a reasonable hourly rate of $235/hour. Decl. Calabia, para. 11. Defendant’s request for 4 hours for motion drafting is granted, as is the requested $60 in filing fees.

GRANTED; full, code-compliant responses to discovery, set one, without objections to be served within 30 days. Joint and several sanctions of $1,000 to be paid within 30 days.


Case Number: 26VECV01312    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Ford Motor Credit Company v. Tumasyan, Case no. 26VECV01312

Hearing date July 7, 2026

Specially Appearing Defendant Tumasyan’s Motion to Compel Arbitration

Plaintiff Ford Motor Credit Company sues defendant Tumasyan for breach of a motor vehicle installment contract. Defendant specially appears and moves to compel arbitration. Plaintiff does not oppose but requests that the court order the parties to commence arbitration within 30 days, as the vehicle remains in defendant’s possession.

California public policy favors arbitration. Code Civ. Proc. §1280 et seq., Madden v. Kaiser Found. Hosps. (1976) 17 Cal.3d 699, 706. Under California’s Arbitration Act, where a valid written agreement to arbitrate exists, arbitration is mandatory. Code Civ. Proc. §1281.2. The opposing party bears the burden of proving any defense to arbitrability. See Pinnacle Museum Tower Ass’n. v. Pinnacle Mkt. Dev. (US), LLC (2012) 55 Cal.4th 223, 236. “[T]he doctrine of unconscionability has both a procedural and substantive element, the former focusing on oppression or surprise due to unequal bargaining power, the latter on overly harsh or one-sided results.” See Sanchez v. Valencia Holding Co., LLC (2015) 61 Cal. 4th 899, 910.

The Federal Arbitration Act codifies federal policy favoring arbitration, making an arbitration agreement in the Terms of Service “valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract…” 9 U.S.C. §2. “Courts must ‘rigorously enforce’ arbitration agreements according to their terms…” Am. Exp. Co. v. Italian Colors Rest. (2013) 570 U.S. 228, 233.

The parties concur that a valid arbitration agreement exists. See Mtn. 4:2-6; see also Decl. Ahearn, exh. A. Plaintiff does not oppose the request for the court to issue an order compelling arbitration; plaintiff requests the matter be ordered to arbitration within 30 days. As this is a collections matter with the vehicle remaining in defendant’s possession, this request is reasonable. GRANTED; the parties are ordered to binding arbitration per the terms of the installment contract within 30 days. The court will set an OSC re: compliance for 60 days.
