DEPARTMENT 517 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV09541    Hearing Date: August 25, 2026    Dept: 517

The Court is granting Defendant's unopposed motion to dismiss this action with prejudice in light of Plaintiff's demonstrated failure to comply with the mandatory early deposition requirement of Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26  and the Court's order requiring Plaintiff to sit for that deposition.  Under section 871.26, subdivision (j)(3), Defendant is entitled to the dismissal it seeks.  Further, Defendant is entitled to recover its costs in this action from Plaintiff's counsel.


Case Number: 25STCV18990    Hearing Date: August 25, 2026    Dept: 517

Plaintiff Elizabeth Gonazlez de Cardona (“de Cardona”) sued Los Angeles County Metropolitan Authority (“LACMTA”) and Dennis Koonce (“Koonce”) for monetary damages to compensate her for injuries she suffered when the motor vehicle she was driving was rear ended by a LACMTA bus that Koonce was driving.  In her Complaint, de Cardona asserts claims for negligence against LACMTA and Koonce and a claim for negligent hiring, supervision, retention, or training against LACMTA.  Pending before the Court is de Cardona’s motion for an order compelling LACMTA to provide further responses to special interrogatories (“SROGs”) that she propounded on LACMTA.   The Court is granting the motion in part and denying it in part.

Through a belated opposition to the motion (it was eight days late and was filed just a day before de Cardona’s reply brief was due), LACMTA stated that it was conceding that Koonce was negligent in his operation of the LACMTA bus that rear ended de Cardona’s motor vehicle.  It also appears from its opposition that LACMTA is conceding negligence in connection with the hiring, supervision, retention, or training of Koonce.  LACMTA’s supplemental responses to the SROGs confirm these concessions.  To be sure, the Court is quite concerned about LACMTA’s very tardy opposition, for which LACMTA offered no explanation.  Nevertheless, the Court exercised its discretion to consider the opposition.  Under Diaz v. Carcamo, (2011) 51 Cal.4th 1148, 1155, LACMTA’s concessions on de Cardona’s claims and its supplemental responses reflecting the concessions render irrelevant the SROGs that seek information from LACMTA regarding Koonce’s employment and driving history, and the training, supervision, and discipline that Koonce received.  Those SROGs are numbers 8, 11, 12, 15-33, 35, and 37.  The motion is denied as to them.

LACMTA' s concessions do not, however, render irrelevant the remaining SROGs that are the subject of de Cardona’s motion, which are numbers 43, 44, 58, and 60.  All of four of those SROGs bear on evidentiary issues or the nature and extent of de Cardona’s claimed injuries stemming from the incident.   The Court agrees with de Cardona that LACMTA’s responses to SROG numbers 43, 44, 58, and 60 did not comply with the applicable requirements of the Code of Civil Procedure. The Court thus directs LACTMA to provide further, Code-compliant responses to those four SROGs within 21 days of this order.   

The Court also is granting de Cardona’s request in the motion for an order imposing monetary sanctions against LACMTA in the form of attorney’s fees that de Cardona incurred in connection with this discovery dispute.  In the Court’s view, LACMTA’s initial responses to the SROGs lacked substantial justification and forced de Cardona to file the motion seeking further responses.  An award of sanctions is thus warranted.  The Court is, however, reducing the amount of sanctions that de Cardona sought in the motion, which was $7,560.  This figure included time that de Cardona’s spent on the meet and confer process and the informal discovery conference that the Court held regarding the SROGs.  The Court is removing that time from its fees award.  This leaves nine hours of counsel’s time on the motion itself, at a billing rate of $500 per hour.  The Court is thus awarding de Cardona $4,500 in attorney’s fees.  The Court directs LACMTA to pay that amount to de Cardona’s counsel within 60 days of this order.


Case Number: 25STCV24667    Hearing Date: August 25, 2026    Dept: 517

The Court will not be issuing a tentative ruling in advance of the hearing this morning on the Plaintiffs' motion for leave to amend their Complaint to add a new Plaintiff, Jason Koontz.  The Court makes the following observations, however, for counsel for both sides to consider for purposes of the hearing.  First, the declaration of Plaintiffs' counsel in support of the motion does not comply with the requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b), which specifies that declarations supporting motions for leave to amend set forth
(1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.  That said, Plaintiffs' counsel could easily fix the defects in his declaration and refile the motion for leave to amend to add Koontz as a Plaintiff.  Second, the Court discerns no prejudice to the Defendants if Koontz were added to the case through the proposed amendment.  The case is still at a relatively stage.  Indeed, the trial has not yet been set.  Absent prejudice, motions for leave to amend should be liberally granted.
