DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

SACHIN P PATEL, 

 

         vs.

 

AG OPPORTUNITY ZONE FUND LLC, et al.

 Case No.:  25STCV37479

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  July 20, 2026

 

Defendants AG Opportunity Zone Fund Property Owner LLC’s [erroneously sued as AG Opportunity Zone Fund LLC] and CPMC, Inc. dba Westhome Property Management’s [erroneously sued as Westhome Property Management Inc.] demurrer to pro per Plaintiff Sachin Patel’s first amended complaint is overruled as to Plaintiff’s 2nd and 10th causes of action as to Defendant AG.  Defendants’ demurrer to the 4th, 5th, 6th, 8th, and 9th causes of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to Defendant AG.  Defendants’ demurrer to the 1st, 3rd, and 7th is sustained without leave to amend as to Defendant AG. 

Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th, and 10th causes of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to Defendant Westhome.

Defendants’ motion to strike is denied as moot.

 

           Defendants AG Opportunity Zone Fund Property Owner LLC [erroneously sued as AG Opportunity Zone Fund LLC] (“AG”) and CPMC, Inc. dba Westhome Property Management’s [erroneously sued as Westhome Property Management Inc.] (“Westhome”) (collectively, “Defendants”) demur to pro per Plaintiff Sachin Patel’s (“Patel”) (“Plaintiff”) first amended complaint (“FAC”).  (Notice of Demurrer, pg. 5.)

           Defendants also move to strike portions of Plaintiff’s FAC.  (Notice of MTS, pgs. 5-6.)

 

1.    Demurrer

Request for Judicial Notice

Plaintiff’s 7/6/26 request for judicial notice of (1) Minute Order, Case No. 25STUD13041, dated May 6, 2026 (P-RJN, Exh. A); (2) Certificate of Occupancy No. 231905, City of Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety, issued April 9, 2024, for 2528 S. Grand Avenue, Los Angeles, California 90007 (P-RJN, Exh. B); and (3) City of Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety Official Permit and Inspection Report Detail, Permit No. 19044-20000-15767, including the complete Permit Application Status History and Inspection Request History (P-RJN, Exh. C) is granted.

Plaintiff’s 7/6/26 request for judicial notice of Trulia.com Active Listing for Jasper Apartments, 2528 S. Grand Avenue, Los Angeles, California 90007, and Zillow.com Active Listing for Jasper Apartments, 2528 S. Grand Avenue, Los Angeles, California 90007 is denied.  The Court notes that Evidence Code §1553 does not “establish[] a presumption that printed representations of web content are accurate representations of the information that appeared on the screen,” as offered by Plaintiff.  The case law pertaining to this Code section refers to red light enforcement camera photographs, not images on the Internet.

Plaintiff’s 7/13/26 request for judicial notice of the Los Angeles Housing Department Notice and Order to Comply, Case No. 992307, issued June 18, 2026, by Inspector Rickey LeBlanc (P-RJN, Exh. F) is granted.

 

Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, the moving party must meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading to attempt to reach an agreement that would resolve the objections to the pleading and obviate the need for filing the demurrer.  (C.C.P. §430.41, emphasis added.)

Defendants’ counsel declares that on March 12, 2026, he met and conferred with Plaintiff telephonically and the parties were unable to reach an agreement.  (See Decl. of Adwani ¶¶4-6.)  Defendants’ counsel’s declaration is proper.  Therefore, the Court will consider Defendants’ demurrer.

 

           Background

           Plaintiff filed his initial Complaint on December 26, 2025.

           Plaintiff filed the operative FAC against Defendants on March 2, 2026, alleging ten causes of action: (1) breach of implied warranty of habitability; (2) breach of covenant of quiet enjoyment; (3) retaliation; (4) negligence; (5) unlawful business practices; (6) deceit/fraud; (7) declaratory relief; (8) restitution/unjust enrichment; (9) private nuisance; and (10) harassment.

           Defendants filed the instant demurrer and the accompanied motion to strike on March 27, 2026.  Plaintiff filed his oppositions on July 6, 2026.  Defendants filed their replies on July 10, 2026.  On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed an application for relief from CRC Rule 3.1113(d).

           Defendants object to Plaintiff’s 27-page opposition on the basis that it violates CRC, Rule 3.1113 and exceeds the page limit of 15 pages by 12 pages.  (Reply, pg. 2.)  In Plaintiff’s July 13, 2026, application for relief from CRC Rule 3.1113(d), Plaintiff attached a conforming 15-page opposition that conforms to the page limits.  The Court will consider the 15-page opposition attached to Plaintiff’s July 13, 2026, application for relief from CRC Rule 3.1113(d) in lieu of the original, overlong opposition. 

 

Summary of Demurrer

           Defendants demur to Plaintiff’s FAC on the basis that each cause of action fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against Defendants.  (Demurrer, pgs. 7-9; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)

 

Legal Standard

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.)  A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable.  (See Donabedian v. Mercury Insurance Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].)  For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law.  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital District (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

Failure to State a Claim

Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability (1st COA)

The elements of a cause of action for breach of the implied warranty of habitability “are the existence of a material defective condition affecting the premises’ habitability, notice to the landlord of the condition within a reasonable time after the tenant’s discovery of the condition, the landlord was given a reasonable time to correct the deficiency, and resulting damages.”  (Erlach v. Sierra Asset Servicing, LLC (2014) 226 Cal.App.4th 1281, 1297.)  The alleged defective condition must “affect the tenant’s apartment or the common areas which he uses.”  (Hinson v. Delis (1972) 26 Cal.App.3d 62, 70.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants failed to provide and maintain essential habitability services and conditions, including but not limited to: (a) lack of permanent, functioning heat in Unit 543; (b) recurring security and safety failures in common areas (recurring trespass and vandalism); and (c) unsanitary and unusable advertised pet amenities.  (FAC ¶95.)

Plaintiff alleges that he provided Defendants with timely and repeated notice of the foregoing defects.  (FAC ¶96.)  Plaintiff alleges that on December 12, 2023, he submitted a maintenance request reporting that the unit heater was not working and requested permanent repair (copy of the request and management response). (FAC ¶96.)  Plaintiff alleges that he made additional written and oral complaints regarding heating, security, and pet-amenity conditions.  (FAC ¶96.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants responded only with temporary measures and did not install permanent heat. (FAC ¶96.) 

This Court already ruled on May 6, 2026, at a bench trial in LASC Case No. 25STUD13041 that Defendant AG is the owner of the subject property and that Defendant breached the implied warranty of habitability because Defendant AG “failed to provide approved heating facilities capable of maintaining a room temperature of 70°F in [Plaintiff Patel’s] apartment during November and December 2025.”  (P-RJN, Exh. A at pg. 3 of 6.)  Accordingly, Plaintiff is collaterally estopped from asserting the same cause of action against Defendant AG.  However, Plaintiff can assert a cause of action against Defendant Westhome, which was not a party in LASC Case No. 25STUD13041.  However, Plaintiff fails to allege that Defendant Westhome was Plaintiff’s landlord.  Indeed, the Lease attached to the FAC indicates that “AG Opportunity Fund LLC dba Jasper” is the owner of the property who entered into the lease contract with Plaintiff.  (FAC, Exh. 1.)

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s 1st cause of action is sustained without leave to amend as to Defendant AG and with 20 days leave to amend as to Defendant Westhome.

 

Breach of the Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment (2nd COA)

“In the absence of language to the contrary, every lease contains an implied covenant of quiet enjoyment, whereby the landlord impliedly covenants that the tenant shall have quiet enjoyment and possession of the premises. [Citations] The covenant of quiet enjoyment ‘insulates the tenant against any act or omission on the part of the landlord, or anyone claiming under him, which interferes with a tenants right to use and enjoy the premises for the purposes contemplated by the tenancy.’”  (Andrews v. Mobile Aire Estates (2005) 125 Cal.App.4th 578, 588.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants’ failure to provide heat, combined with the pervasive safety and nuisance conditions described herein, materially interfered with Plaintiff’s right to quiet enjoyment under Civil Code §1927 and substantially reduced the Premises’ fair rental value.  (FAC ¶99.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and proximate result of the foregoing, Plaintiff suffered economic damages, including payment of rent for premises whose fair rental value was diminished during periods of unremedied violations, and other consequential losses.  (FAC ¶104.)  Plaintiff seeks damages measured by the difference between the rent paid and the fair rental value of Unit 543 during the periods of interference, together with remediation and relocation costs and other consequential damages according to proof.  (FAC ¶104.)

Plaintiff fails to allege that Defendant Westhome was Plaintiff’s landlord.  Indeed, the Lease attached to the FAC indicates that “AG Opportunity Fund LLC dba Jasper” is the owner of the property who entered into the lease contract with Plaintiff.  (FAC, Exh. 1.)  Therefore, Plaintiff fails to allege a cause of action against Defendant Westhome.

However, Plaintiff states a sufficient cause of action for breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment against Defendant AG.

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the 2nd cause of action is sustained as to Defendant Westhome with 20 days leave to amend and overruled as to Defendant AG.

 

Retaliation (3rd COA)

“Subdivision (c) of Civil Code section 1942.5 provides: ‘It is unlawful for a lessor to increase rent, decrease services, cause a lessee to quit involuntarily, bring an action to recover possession, or threaten to do any of those acts, for the purpose of retaliating against the lessee because he or she has lawfully organized or participated in a lessees’ association or an organization advocating lessees’ rights or has lawfully and peaceably exercised any rights under the law. In an action brought by or against the lessee pursuant to this subdivision, the lessee shall bear the burden of producing evidence that the lessor’s conduct was, in fact, retaliatory.’”  (Morrison v. Vineyard Creek L.P. (2011) 193 Cal.App.4th 1254, 1267.)

Plaintiff alleges that he engaged in protected activity by (a) reporting habitability and safety conditions to management and requesting repairs by email on multiple occasions; (b) serving a written habitability demand on Defendants; and (c) filing a complaint with the Los Angeles Housing Department on November 5, 2025, alleging unsafe conditions, unlawful billing practices, and retaliation.  (FAC ¶107.)

Plaintiff alleges that after he engaged in protected activity, Defendants took adverse actions against Plaintiff, including: (a) filing an unlawful detainer action against Plaintiff on December 11, 2025; (b) imposing or attempting to impose new or increased charges and payment restrictions in connection with disputed utility billing and rent collection.  (FAC ¶108.)  Plaintiff alleges that the timing and course of conduct support retaliatory purpose: Defendants’ adverse actions followed closely after protected activity and occurred while Defendants remained in material breach of habitability obligations.  (FAC ¶108.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and proximate result of Defendants’ retaliatory conduct, Plaintiff has suffered actual damages, statutory remedies under Civil Code § 1942.5, and irreparable harm for which injunctive relief is appropriate.  (FAC ¶111.)

Plaintiff fails to allege that Defendant Westhome was Plaintiff’s landlord.  Indeed, the Lease attached to the FAC indicates that “AG Opportunity Fund LLC dba Jasper” is the owner of the property who entered into the lease contract with Plaintiff.  (FAC, Exh. 1.)  Therefore, Plaintiff fails to allege a cause of action against Defendant Westhome.

This Court already ruled on the issue of whether Defendant AG retaliated against Plaintiff at the May 6, 2026 bench trial in LASC Case No. 25STUD13041.  (P-RJN, Exh. A at pgs 4-5 of 6 [“Accordingly, Defendants’ claim that the instant suit was filed in retaliation for protected conduct by Defendants fails.”].)

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the 3rd cause of action is sustained as to Defendant Westhome with 20 days leave to amend and sustained without leave to amend as to Defendant AG.

 

 

Negligence (4th COA)

“The elements of a cause of action for negligence are well established. They are ‘(a) a legal duty to use due care; (b) a breach of such legal duty; [and] (c) the breach as the proximate or legal cause of the resulting injury.’”  (Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants owed Plaintiff and other tenants a duty to exercise reasonable care to maintain the Premises and common areas in a reasonably safe condition, including a duty to implement reasonable security and property-control measures for controlled access areas, common areas, parking facilities, and other areas under Defendants’ control.  (FAC ¶113.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants breached that duty by failing to implement reasonable security and property-control measures despite ongoing notice of recurring trespass, vandalism, and unsafe conditions, including advisory notices and tenant reports.  (FAC ¶113.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendants’ breaches were a substantial factor in causing Plaintiff’s harm because Defendants failed to maintain reasonable security and to remedy known hazards, and therefore Plaintiff incurred economic losses and was deprived of the full use and enjoyment of Unit 543.  (FAC ¶115.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and proximate result of Defendants’ negligence, Plaintiff suffered economic damages, including rent overpayment for an unsafe and diminished premises photographs while paying “Luxury Rental Prices.”  (FAC ¶116.)

Plaintiff’s claim is barred by the economic loss rule.  The economic loss rule is a common-law doctrine designed to “prevent the law of contract and the law of tort from dissolving one into the other.”  (Robinson Helicopter Co., Inc. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979, 988.)  Under the rule, a plaintiff who suffers purely economic losses is limited to recovering in contract, as opposed to tort, “unless he can demonstrate harm above and beyond a broken contractual promise.”  (Id.)  “California law generally does not impose a duty of care to avoid causing purely economic losses.”  (Weimer v. Nationstar Mortgage, LLC (2020) 47 Cal.App.5th 341, 355.)

Plaintiff fails to allege harm above and beyond a broken contractual promise.  Further, Plaintiff fails to allege that Defendant Westhome was Plaintiff’s landlord.  Indeed, the Lease attached to the FAC indicates that “AG Opportunity Fund LLC dba Jasper” is the owner of the property who entered into the lease contract with Plaintiff.  (FAC, Exh. 1.)  Therefore, Plaintiff fails to allege a cause of action against Defendant Westhome.

Accordingly, Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s 4th cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Unlawful Business Practices/False Advertising & Fraud (5th & 6th COAs)

To set forth a claim for a violation of the UCL, Plaintiff must establish Defendants were engaged in an “unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business act or practice and unfair, deceptive, untrue or misleading advertising” and certain specific acts.  (Bus. & Prof. Code §17200.)  A cause of action for unfair competition “is not an all-purpose substitute for a tort or contract action.”  (Cortez v. Purolator Air Filtration Products Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 163, 173.)

“A plaintiff’s burden in asserting a fraud claim against a corporate employer is even greater. In such a case, the plaintiff must ‘allege the names of the persons who made the allegedly fraudulent representations, their authority to speak, to whom they spoke, what they said or wrote, and when it was said or written.’”  (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 645.)  

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants made the following untrue or misleading statements in connection with the lease and prospective lease of Unit 543: (a) Square footage: the Apartments.com listing and leasing email represented Unit 543 as “727 sq. ft.”; leasing email from Mr. Arron Savage; (b) Safety and livability: Defendants’ listing and leasing webpage described the property as walkable, biker paradise, and safe for tenants; (c) Pet amenities: Defendants advertised on their listing and leasing materials that the property was pet-friendly and included an on-site pet run and agility related amenities; (d) Omissions and concealment: Defendants omitted and concealed material conditions affecting safety, habitability, and the availability of advertised amenities.  (FAC ¶122.) 

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct result of Defendants’ unlawful, unfair, and fraudulent business practices, Plaintiff suffered loss of money and property.   (FAC ¶124.)  Plaintiff alleges that he reasonably relied on Defendants’ advertising and leasing representations in deciding to lease Unit 543 and executed the lease.  (FAC ¶124.)  Plaintiff alleges that he paid monthly rent and fees at the advertised price and was billed unlawful utility charges and retroactive adjustments.  (FAC ¶124.)

Plaintiff fails to allege fraud with the requisite specificity for representations made on behalf of the corporate Defendants.  Plaintiff fails to allege justifiable reliance on the alleged misrepresentations.  Because Plaintiff’s fraud cause of action fails, so too does the unfair business practices cause of action.

Accordingly, Defendants’ demurrer to the 5th and 6th causes of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Declaratory Relief (7th COA)

A cause of action for declaratory relief requires the following elements: (1) person interested under a written instrument or a contract; or (2) a declaration of his or her rights or duties (a) with respect to another or (b) in respect to, in, over or upon property; and (3) an actual controversy.  (C.C.P. §1060.) 

Plaintiff alleges that he is entitled to a judicial determination of (a) whether, and to what extent, Plaintiff is entitled to a reduction of rent or abatement of rent for periods during which Defendants failed to remedy habitability violations, and the method for calculating the fair rental value of Unit 543 during those periods; (b) whether Defendants’ billing and payment practices, including the allocation methodology, master-account billing, and retroactive adjustments imposed through Conservice and other vendors, comply with applicable law and the Lease, and whether any such practices are unlawful or void as applied to Plaintiff; (c) whether Plaintiff’s continued occupancy after providing notice of defects and after disputing charges constitutes a waiver of Plaintiff’s claims or defenses arising from Defendants’ prior misrepresentations, omissions, and unlawful billing practices (Plaintiff alleges it does not and that continued occupancy was under protest and to mitigate damages); and (d) whether Defendants’ actions and communications since Plaintiff’s protected activity constitute unlawful retaliation under California law and whether Defendants may lawfully continue such conduct.  (FAC ¶147.)

Plaintiff concedes in his opposition that his cause of action is partially retrospective, namely subsections (a)-(c).  However, Plaintiff’s subsection (d) is duplicative of his cause of action for retaliation.  This Court already ruled on the issue of whether Defendant AG retaliated against Plaintiff at the May 6, 2026 bench trial in LASC Case No. 25STUD13041.  (P-RJN, Exh. A at pgs 4-5 of 6 [“Accordingly, Defendants’ claim that the instant suit was filed in retaliation for protected conduct by Defendants fails.”].) 

Plaintiff fails to allege that Defendant Westhome was Plaintiff’s landlord.  Indeed, the Lease attached to the FAC indicates that “AG Opportunity Fund LLC dba Jasper” is the owner of the property who entered into the lease contract with Plaintiff.  (FAC, Exh. 1.)  Therefore, Plaintiff fails to allege a cause of action against Defendant Westhome.

Accordingly, Cross-Defendants’ demurrer to the 7th cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to Westhome and sustained without leave to amend as to Defendant AG.

 

           Restitution/Unjust Enrichment (8th COA)

“The elements for a claim of unjust enrichment are ‘receipt of a benefit and unjust retention of the benefit at the expense of another.’ [Citation.] ‘The theory of unjust enrichment requires one who acquires a benefit which may not justly be retained, to return either the thing or its equivalent to the aggrieved party so as not to be unjustly enriched.’ [Citation.]”  (Prakashpalan v. Engstrom, Lipscomb & Lack (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 1105, 1132.)

Plaintiff concedes that his cause of action for unjust enrichment is not an independent cause of action, and is dependent upon his claim for Unfair Business Practices.

Accordingly, Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s 8th cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

           Private Nuisance (9th COA)

“A nuisance is considered a ‘public nuisance’ when it ‘affects at the same time an entire community or neighborhood, or any considerable number of persons, although the extent of the annoyance or damage inflicted upon individuals may be unequal.’ A ‘private nuisance’ is defined to include any nuisance not covered by the definition of a public nuisance, and also includes some public nuisances. ‘In other words, it is possible for a nuisance to be public and, from the perspective of individuals who suffer an interference with their use and enjoyment of land, to be private as well.’”  (Mendez v. Rancho Valencia Resort Partners, LLC (2016) 3 Cal.App.5th 248, 261-262, internal citations omitted.)

“[T]o proceed on a private nuisance theory the plaintiff must prove an injury specifically referable to the use and enjoyment of his or her land. The injury, however, need not be different in kind from that suffered by the general public.”  (Koll-Irvine Center Property Owners Assn. v. County of Orange (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1036, 1041, internal citation omitted.) 

Plaintiff fails to allege a private nuisance cause of action.  Plaintiff merely states legal conclusions pertaining to the substantial interference of the nuisance and the unreasonableness of the nuisance.  (See San Diego Gas & Electric Co. v. Superior Court (1996) 13 Cal.4th 893, 938 [defining substantial and unreasonable interference].)

Accordingly, Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s 9th cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

           Landlord Harassment (10th COA)

LAMC §45.33 is codified under the Tenant Anti-Harassment Ordinance in Article 5.3 of the LAMC.  LAMC §45.35 explicitly provides for a private right of action in that “[a]n aggrieved tenant under this article . . . may institute civil proceedings as provided by law, against any landlord violating any of the provisions of this article and any person who aids, facilitates, and/or incites another to violate the provisions of this article, regardless of whether the rental unit remain occupied or has been vacated due to harassment.”  (LAMC §45.35.)

           Plaintiff alleges Defendants engaged in harassment by repeatedly preparing, serving, and publicly posting Three-Day Notices to Pay Rent or Quit despite knowledge of Plaintiffs’ payroll schedule, timely partial payments, and communications showing a good-faith basis to believe rent was being paid; Serving notices and making threats of eviction without a good-faith basis and for the purpose of coercing Plaintiffs to vacate; Posting notices in common areas and in view of neighbors and delivery personnel in a manner intended to humiliate and shame Plaintiffs; Engaging in a pattern of repeated notices and communications that substantially interfered with Plaintiffs’ comfort, peace, and quiet enjoyment of the premises; Taking the foregoing actions in retaliation for Plaintiffs’ complaints to the Los Angeles Housing Department and for Plaintiffs’ exercise of statutory and habitability rights; Creating and maintaining a hostile, intimidating, and coercive living environment designed to cause Plaintiffs to abandon the tenancy or surrender rights.

Plaintiff fails to allege that Defendant Westhome was Plaintiff’s landlord.  Indeed, the Lease attached to the FAC indicates that “AG Opportunity Fund LLC dba Jasper” is the owner of the property who entered into the lease contract with Plaintiff.  (FAC, Exh. 1.)  Therefore, Plaintiff fails to allege a cause of action against Defendant Westhome.

           Plaintiff sufficiently alleges a cause of action for landlord harassment against Defendant AG.

Accordingly, Defendants’ demurrer to the 10th cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to Defendant Westhome and overruled as to Defendant AG.

 

           Conclusion

Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s 2nd and 10th causes of action is overruled as to Defendant AG.  Defendants’ demurrer to the 4th, 5th, 6th, 8th, and 9th causes of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to Defendant AG.  Defendants’ demurrer to the 1st, 3rd, and 7th is sustained without leave to amend as to Defendant AG. 

Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th, and 10th causes of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to Defendant Westhome.

Defendants’ motion to strike is denied as moot.

TENTATIVE RULING

 

LARUE LYDELL ZAMORANO, 

 

         vs.

 

LISTER LEONARD.

 Case No.:  26STCV12028

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  July 20, 2026

 

Pro per Plaintiff LaRue Lydell Zamorano’s unopposed motion for a preliminary injunction is denied.

 

Plaintiff LaRue Lydell Zamorano (“Zamorano”) (“Plaintiff”), in pro per, moves unopposed for a preliminary injunction enjoining Defendant Lister Leonard (“Leonard”) (“Defendant”) from accessing, transferring, assigning, or attempting to collect any funds; and interfering with any proceeds due to Plaintiff, specifically any funds relating to the $665,000.00 tax refund identified by the Tax Title Services.  (Notice of Motion, pg. 1; C.C.P. §§525, 526, 527.) 

Plaintiff moves on the grounds that (1) Plaintiff is likely to prevail on the merits; (2) Plaintiff will suffer irreparable harm absent injunctive relief; (3) the balance of equities strongly favors Plaintiff; and (4) the injunction is necessary to preserve the status quo and enforce prior court orders.  (Notice of Motion, pg. 1.)

 

Background

Plaintiff filed her operative Complaint against Defendant on April 15, 2026, which the Court notes does not allege any substantive facts and repeats legal conclusions throughout the document.  (See Complaint.)

Plaintiff filed the instant motion on April 28, 2026.  As of the date of this hearing no opposition has been filed.

Plaintiff’s proof of service of summons indicates that both the complaint, summons, and instant motion were personally served on Defendant on April 29, 2026.  (See 4/30/26 POS Leonard.)

 

Legal Standard

The purpose of a preliminary injunction is to preserve the status quo pending final judgment in the case.  (See Scaringe v. J.C.C. Enterprises, Inc. (1988) 205 Cal.App.3d 1536.)  The status quo has been defined to mean the last actual peaceable, uncontested status which preceded the pending controversy.  (Voorhies v. Greene (1983) 139 Cal.App.3d 989, 995, quoting United Railroads v. Superior Court (1916) 172 Cal. 80, 87.) 

In determining whether to issue a preliminary injunction, the trial court considers two factors: (1) the reasonable probability that the plaintiff will prevail on the merits at trial; and (2) a balancing of the “irreparable harm” that the plaintiff is likely to sustain if the injunction is denied compared to the harm that the defendant is likely to suffer if the court grants a preliminary injunction.  (C.C.P. §526(a); 14859 Moorpark Homeowner’s Association v. VRT Corp. (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 1396, 1402; Pillsbury, Madison & Sutro v. Schectman (1997) 55 Cal.App.4th 1279, 1283.)

The Court’s determination is guided by a “mix” of the potential-merit and interim-harm factors; the greater the plaintiff’s showing on one, the less must be shown on the other to support an injunction.  (Butt v. State of California (1992) 4 Cal.4th 668, 678.)  However, a trial court may not grant a preliminary injunction, regardless of the balance of interim harm, unless there is some possibility that the plaintiff would ultimately prevail on the merits of the claim.  (Id.) 

The court must consider both factors.  The two factors are a sliding scale— the stronger the showing of probability of prevailing, the lesser showing is required for irreparable harm.  (Id.; The Right Side Coalition v. Los Angeles Unified School District (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 336 [reversing denial of preliminary injunction based solely on balancing of hardships without considering probability of prevailing].)  The plaintiff must make some showing of each factor.  (Jessen v. Keystone Savings & Loan Association (1983) 142 Cal.App.3d 454, 459.)  A court may not issue a preliminary injunction if the plaintiff cannot possibly prevail on the merits even if a strong showing of irreparable harm has been made.  (Butt, 4 Cal.4th at pgs. 677-678.)

On the first factor, a preliminary injunction may not issue unless the judge is persuaded that it is “reasonably probable” that the plaintiff will prevail on the merits.  (San Francisco Newspaper Printing Co. v. Superior Court (1985) 170 Cal.App.3d 438, 442.)  The judge does not determine the merits of the action or decide that the plaintiff necessarily will prevail.  The judge only determines whether there is a reasonable probability that the plaintiff will prevail.  (Youngblood v. Wilcox (1989) 207 Cal.App.3d 1368, 1372.)

 For the second factor, “irreparable harm” means that the defendant’s act constitutes an actual or threatened injury to the personal or property rights of the plaintiff that cannot be compensated by a damages award.  (Brownfield v. Daniel Freeman Marina Hospital (1989) 208 Cal.App.3d 405, 410.)  A plaintiff is not required to wait until suffering actual harm; threatened harm is enough.  (Southern Christian Leadership Conference v. Al Malaikah Auditorium Co. (1991) 230 Cal.App.3d 207, 223.)

The issue of irreparable harm is closely related to the issue of damages as an adequate remedy at law.  Monetary loss will not constitute irreparable harm unless the plaintiff also shows that the defendant is insolvent or unable to pay damages.  (Friedman v Friedman (1993) 20 Cal.App.4th 876, 890.)  While both residential and commercial real property are considered unique for purposes of irreparable harm (see Civ. Code §3387), damages may adequately compensate property owned for investment property which has an established value, and the foreclosing entity is solvent.  (Jessen, 142 Cal.App.3d at pg. 458.)  Where the owner intends to use the investment property and not simply sell it, money damages may not suffice, and irreparable harm may justify injunctive relief.  (Id.)

The showing must demonstrate the specific harm claimed; mere allegations of irreparable injury are insufficient.  (Leach v. City of San Marcos (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 648, 661 [“A mere allegation that such injury will result is not sufficient”.])  Conclusory statements, lay opinion, and attorney declarations will not suffice.  The irreparable harm must be imminent; a mere possibility or fear of harm is insufficient.  (Korean Philadelphia Presbyterian Church v. California Presbytery (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 1069, 1084.)  The court’s ruling on a preliminary injunction is not an adjudication of the merits, is not a trial, and does not require a statement of decision.  (Cohen v. Board of Supervisors, (1985) 40 Cal.3d 277, 286; People v. Landlords Professional Services, Inc., (1986) 178 Cal.App.3d 68, 70-71.)  The Court is not required to state its reasons for granting or denying a preliminary injunction; a cursory statement is sufficient.  (City of Los Altos v. Barnes (1992) 3 Cal.App.4th 1193, 1198.)

 

Discussion

Plaintiff fails to demonstrate she has a reasonable likelihood of success on her claims.  Plaintiff’s Complaint fails to state any facts to support any cognizable cause of action.  Plaintiff’s Complaint is composed of filler paragraphs, such as “Plaintiff alleges fact paragraph 1, detailing minority, fiduciary failure, coercion, concealment, incarceration, and delayed discovery.”  (Complaint ¶1.)  The declaration of Estate Administrator (Mother) attached to the Complaint is also filled with placeholder declarations, such as “I declare under penalty of perjury that paragraph 1 describes coercion, threats, mental health incapacity, and inability to enforce the judgment,” and is signed by an unidentified individual on April 11, 2026.  Because there is not reasonable probability of Plaintiff’s claim prevailing on the merits, the Court does not need to proceed to the second step of the analysis for balancing of harm.

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for preliminary injunction is denied.

 

Conclusion

Plaintiff’s unopposed motion for a preliminary injunction is denied.
