DEPARTMENT N LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23SMCV02511    Hearing Date: July 18, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiffs Harry Thomas Hutcherson and Lorna Hutcherson’s Motion for New Trial or, in the Alternative, Additur is DENIED.

Plaintiffs Harry Thomas Hutcherson and Lorna Hutcherson to give notice. 

REASONING

Plaintiffs Harry Thomas Hutcherson and Lorna Hutcherson (“Plaintiffs”) move the Court for a new trial or, in the alternative, additur on the grounds that the verdict is against the weight of the evidence, the damages are inadequate, and the Court erred in failing to read Defendants’ stipulation to admit liability to the jury. The Court notes that Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Appeal of the judgment on July 14, 2026, but it is “established that a motion for a new trial is collateral to the judgment and may proceed despite an appeal from the judgment.” (Varian Medical Systems, Inc. v. Delfino (2005) 35 Cal.4th 180, 191.)

Code of Civil Procedure section 657 sets forth the grounds upon which a verdict may be vacated, in whole or in part, and a new or further trial granted on all or part of the issues. “The determination of a motion for a new trial rests so completely within the court’s discretion that its action will not be disturbed unless a manifest and unmistakable abuse of discretion clearly appears.” (Jiminez v. Sears, Roebuck & Co. (1971) 4 Cal.3d 379, 387.)

The evidence at trial showed that Plaintiff Harry Thomas Hutcherson was sideswiped by Defendant Carlos Harding as Hutcherson was riding a bike, and when he visited the hospital after the accident on the same day, the doctor told him to go home and rest after giving him a boot to wear. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶¶ 3, 5, Exs. B, D.) Plaintiff saw additional medical providers on a lien basis and received treatment. This action came on for trial and resulted in a jury verdict on May 14, 2026, wherein the jury found that Plaintiff Harry Thomas Hutcherson had suffered $7,000 in past economic loss, $6,000 in past noneconomic loss, and $0 in future noneconomic loss, while also finding that Plaintiff Lorna Hutcherson had suffered $0 in harm as to her loss of consortium claim.

While the evidence showed that Plaintiff had received treatment on his lower back, Plaintiff’s expert at trial indicated that he was unaware of Plaintiff’s prior injuries to his lower back before the subject accident, and he told the jury that Plaintiff’s counsel had not provided any prior medical records to the expert. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. B.) The evidence showed that Plaintiff had preexisting back pain dating to 2012, and Defendants Precious Memories Limo, LLC, Jolie Limo, LLC, and Carlos Harding (“Defendants”)’s experts testified that spinal imaging showed preexisting issues before the accident. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶ 7, Ex. F.) Defendants’ expert also testified that the surgery performed to Plaintiff’s lower back was not reasonable or necessary, and the lumbar injection was also not diagnostic or reasonable. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶ 8, Ex. G.)

As to Plaintiff’s ankle treatment, the initial MRI did not show a ligament tear or injury, and a re-read of the MRI determining that surgery was necessary was not credible, as Defendants’ expert testified that the surgery performed is rarely done even for complete ligament tears, which did not occur here, and Plaintiff’s expert suffered from credibility issues that presumably affected the jury’s perception of his testimony. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶¶ 5, 6, Exs. D, E.) As to the morel-lavallee lesion, Defendants’ expert testified there is no pain management for such a lesion, and the liposuction performed was done for cosmetic purposes. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶ 9, Ex. H.) Further, there was no specific testimony at trial that connected the medical bills to the accident, i.e., Plaintiff showed treatment for certain conditions, but the jury did not link the treatment to harm sustained in the accident because Plaintiff failed to provide evidence as to this element.

Insofar as Plaintiffs contend that it was improper to award $0 in damages for loss of consortium to Plaintiff Lorna Hutcherson, it is not a requirement that one be awarded damages for loss of consortium if the plaintiff who suffered the primary harm was damaged, even if a loss of consortium claim flows from the other plaintiff’s harm. In other words, Plaintiffs were still required to prove the elements of loss of consortium, and the jury could reasonably conclude there were no damages here.

Finally, Plaintiffs argue that the Court’s failure to read the stipulation of liability to the jury was improper.  The Court advised the parties that the stipulation could be redacted in a manner that would warrant reading it to the jury, but Plaintiffs did not submit such a stipulation. (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. B.) Regardless, it was made clear to the jury during opening statements that Defendants stipulated to being responsible for the accident but contested the nature and extent of injuries suffered by Plaintiffs (Opp’n, Mendoza Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. A), so the reading of the stipulation would not have changed the result. In short, Plaintiffs have failed to show that a new trial is warranted. Accordingly, Plaintiffs Harry Thomas Hutcherson and Lorna Hutcherson’s Motion for New Trial or, in the Alternative, Additur is DENIED.

Case Number: 25SMCV04847    Hearing Date: July 18, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories is GRANTED. Defendant Margarita Sanchez, individually and doing business as Margarita’s Beauty Salon, shall serve code-compliant responses to Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, without objections, within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.

Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff’s Special Interrogatories is GRANTED. Defendant Margarita Sanchez, individually and doing business as Margarita’s Beauty Salon, shall serve code-compliant responses to Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Special Interrogatories, Set One, without objections, within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.

Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,250, payable by Defendant Margarita Sanchez, individually and doing business as Margarita’s Beauty Salon to Plaintiff Fernando Saenz and Plaintiff’s counsel within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.

Plaintiff Fernando Saenz to give notice. 

REASONING

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses and for a monetary sanction. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subds. (b), (c).) Failure to timely respond waives all objections, including privilege and work product. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).) Thus, unless the party to whom the demand was directed obtains relief from waiver, he or she cannot raise objections to the documents demanded. (Ibid.) There is no deadline for a motion to compel responses. Likewise, for failure to respond, the moving party need not attempt to resolve the matter outside court before filing the motion. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Where the motion seeks only a response to the interrogatories, no showing of “good cause” is required. (Contra Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1) [good cause requirement for motion to compel further responses].) All that need be shown in the moving papers is that a set of interrogatories was properly served on the opposing party, that the time to respond has expired, and that no response of any kind has been served. (Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)

Plaintiff Fernando Saenz (“Plaintiff”) served Defendant Margarita Sanchez, individually and doing business as Margarita’s Beauty Salon (“Defendant”) with his Form Interrogatories and Special Interrogatories, Set One, on February 10, 2026. (Mots., Arcos Decls. ¶ 7.) Responses were due on or before March 17, 2026, and defense counsel also sent Plaintiff a letter to request her responses by May 18, 2026. (Mots., Arcos Decls. ¶¶ 8-9.) To date, no responses have been received. (Mots., Arcos Decls. ¶ 9.)

Accordingly, Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories is GRANTED, and Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff’s Special Interrogatories is GRANTED. Defendant Margarita Sanchez, individually and doing business as Margarita’s Beauty Salon, shall serve code-compliant responses to Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Form Interrogatories and Special Interrogatories, Set One, without objections, within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.

If a motion to compel responses to interrogatories is filed, the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against the losing party “unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (c).) Further, “[t]he court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed, or opposition to the motion was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1348(a).)

Plaintiff requests $1,750 in monetary sanctions for each motion. The Court finds that monetary sanctions are proper, but the Court reduces the amount to $1,250, representing two hours spent preparing each motion and one hour spent appearing at the hearing on the motion, totaling five hours at the rate of $250 per hour. Thus, Plaintiff Fernando Saenz’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,250, payable by Defendant Margarita Sanchez, individually and doing business as Margarita’s Beauty Salon to Plaintiff Fernando Saenz and Plaintiff’s counsel within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.
