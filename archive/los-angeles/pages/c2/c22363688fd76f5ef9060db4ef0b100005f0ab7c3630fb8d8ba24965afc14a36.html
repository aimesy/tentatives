DEPARTMENT M LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

Dept. M issues tentative rulings in many, but not all motion hearings. There is no set time at which tentatives are posted. Please do not call the staff to inquire if a tentative will be posted. 

If parties are satisfied with the ruling, parties may submit on the tentative. However, if an opposing party does not submit, they will be permitted to argue. Please check with the other side before calling the courtroom to submit. The staff does not keep track of which parties submitted and which did not, so please do not ask. 

If a matter is also a scheduling hearing (CMC, TSC, OSC etc) an appearance is still required even if a party submits on the tentative ruling.

Natividad Serna, et al. v. BMW of North America, LLC, et al.

24TRCV01953

1.      Natividad Serna, et al.’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses    

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Natividad Serna, et al.’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses is granted in part.  

 

Background

 

Plaintiffs filed the Complaint on June 10, 2024. Plaintiffs allege the following facts. Plaintiffs purchased a 2023 BMW i4 which was manufactured and distributed by Defendant. The vehicle suffers from defects. Defendant has not been able to repair the vehicle. Defendant has refused to repurchase the vehicle. Plaintiff alleges violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (“Song-Beverly Act”).

 

Motion for Attorneys’ Fees

 

Civ. Code, § 1794(d) states: “If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.”

 

Plaintiffs move for attorneys’ fees and costs pursuant to Civ. Code section 1794(d) as the prevailing party in this action. The parties settled this action on March 11, 2026. The settlement agreement stated that the Court would determine the amount of reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs allowed to Plaintiffs as the prevailing party. Thus, the Court will determine the amount of reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs incurred via this noticed motion. 

 

“The trial court has “broad authority” to determine the amount of [reasonable attorneys’ fees].”  PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.  “[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate.”  Id. “California courts have consistently held that a computation of time spent on a case and the reasonable value of that time is fundamental to a determination of an appropriate attorneys' fee award.”.  Id. “The experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court, and while his judgment is of course subject to review, it will not be disturbed unless the appellate court is convinced that it is clearly wrong.”  Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 (internal quotation omitted.) 

 

Thus, Plaintiffs are entitled to attorneys’ fees under the lodestar method based on the reasonable amount of time the attorney spent multiplied by a reasonable rate. Plaintiffs request $54,757.79 in attorneys’ fees and costs.

 

Plaintiffs attached a full accounting of the billing records in this action. (Decl., Sepehr Daghighian, Ex. A). Plaintiffs’ counsel also provided a summary of the rates charged by the attorneys who worked on this case and their background and experience. The rates range from $350/hour to $700/hour. (Id. at ¶ 7-11).

 

“The lodestar adjustment method requires the trial court first to determine a touchstone or lodestar figure based on actual time spent and reasonable hourly compensation for each attorney. The touchstone figure may then be augmented or diminished by taking various relevant factors into account, including (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved and the skill displayed in presenting them; (2) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys; and (3) the contingent nature of the fee award, based on the uncertainty of prevailing on the merits and of establishing eligibility for the award. For Song–Beverly Consumer Warranty Act claims, [a] prevailing buyer has the burden of ‘showing that the fees incurred were “allowable,” were “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation,” and were “reasonable in amount.” Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 967, 998 (internal citations and quotations omitted). “Just as a court has discretion to increase the lodestar under several factors in such a case, it may also decrease it by looking at those same factors, including […] the nature of the litigation, its difficulty, the amount involved, the skill required in its handling, the skill employed, the attention given, the success or failure, and other circumstances in the case.” Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 160–161(internal quotations omitted).

 

The Court deems that, based on the nature of this case and the complexity of this case, the action does not warrant the addition of any multiplier or a deduction through the imposition of a negative multiplier.             The Court finds that the hourly rates charged were excessive considering the nature of this case, the settlement amount, the attorneys’ experience and background, the hourly rate in the community, and the type of work undertaken by counsel. The Court finds that $350/hour is a reasonable hourly rate for the type of work, case, and nature of this case.

 

The Court declines to reduce the hours spent litigating this action. While there may be certain areas of billing and time spent where an opposing party might have grounds to question, the Court cannot state that the hours expended were unnecessary or unreasonable. However, the Court does reduce the amount of time requested that is simply based on an estimated time, to review the opposition, prepare the Reply, and attend the hearing. Plaintiffs set forth an anticipated amount of hours of 7.5 hours. The Court reduces this to 2.5 hours.

 

The Court therefore finds that 66.6 hours is a reasonable amount of time expended at the hourly rate of $350.00/hour, for a total amount of $23,310.00. Costs and expenses in the amount of $3,855.29 are reasonable in amount and the costs are recoverable items of costs under CCP § 1033.5.

                                   

Therefore, the Court grants Plaintiffs’ Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, in part. Thus, the Court fixes reasonable attorneys’ fees in the amount of $23,310.00. The Court fixes the amount of costs and expenses in the amount of $3,855.29. The Court awards attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses in the total amount of $27,165.29.

 

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice of this ruling.

Historic Automobile Preservation Entity v. Home Budget Loans, et al.      

25TRCV00380

1.      Nyece Chaney’s Motion to Vacate Order Establishing Partition Referee    

 

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

            Nyece Chaney’s Motion to Vacate Order Establishing Partition Referee is taken off calendar.

 

            However, the Court, on its own motion, modifies the Court’s order of March 17, 2026. Paragraph 11(b) of the March 17, 2026 Stipulated Interlocutory Judgment for Partition by Sale and Order Appointing Referee is hereby stricken.

 

Background

 

            Plaintiff filed the Complaint on February 4, 2025. Plaintiff alleges the following facts. Plaintiff seeks an order of partition as to the following property: 610 Lairport Street, El Segundo, California 90245. On March 17, 2026, the parties reached a settlement. A Stipulated Interlocutory Judgment for Partition By Sale and Order Appointing Partition Referee was signed and filed   

 

            Motion to Vacate Order  

 

            Non-party Nyece Chaney (“Chaney”) moves for an order to modify the order appointing a partition referee. The motion is made to remove paragraph 11 of the order which reflects upon this non-party’s rights and interests. Chaney argues that, in Chaney v. Historic Auto Preservation Entity et al, 24TRCV00805, the Court entered a judgment on a jury verdict against Historic Automobile Preservation Entity (“HAPE”) in the amount of $9,815,558.00. That judgment is currently on appeal. Chaney contends that HAPE was unsuccessful in obtaining a stay of enforcement of the judgment. However, Chaney states that the order entered in the instant case obstructs her ability to enforce the judgment.

 

            Paragraph 11(b) of the order appointing a partition referee states as follows:

            “The Referee shall segregate HAPE’s 25% net proceeds (and any credits or debits otherwise allocable to HAPE) into a separate, interest-bearing subaccount (and specifically shall not invest HAPE’s net proceeds in the stock market or similar type investments) and shall not disburse those segregated funds until such time HAPE’s appeal in Nyece Chaney v. Automobile Driving Museum, et al. [LASC Case No. 24TRCV00805] has concluded, and upon further order of this Court.”

 

            Plaintiff argues that this non-party cannot participate in this partition action. Code Civ. Proc., § 872.720(a) states: “If the court finds that the plaintiff is entitled to partition, it shall make an interlocutory judgment that determines the interests of the parties in the property and orders the partition of the property and, unless it is to be later determined, the manner of partition.” (emphasis added).

 

            The Court believes that this argument is well-founded as a partition action and the interlocutory judgment should only determine the interests of the interests of the parties in the subject property. Thus, the argument that this non-party lacks standing to make this motion appears reasonable. On the other hand, the interlocutory judgment itself appears to improperly attempt to make orders that intrude upon the judgment of another Court and also attempts to obstruct and hinder the rights and interest of this moving third party.

 

            “[Code of Civil Procedure] sections 437c and 1008 limit the parties' ability to file repetitive motions but do not limit the court's ability, on its own motion, to reconsider its prior interim orders so it may correct its own errors.” Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1107. Pursuant to Le Francois v. Goel, the Court reconsiders and modifies the March 17, 2026 Stipulated Interlocutory Judgment for Partition by Sale and Order Appointing Referee.

 

            The Court strikes and eliminates paragraph 11(b) of the order which currently states: “The Referee shall segregate HAPE’s 25% net proceeds (and any credits or debits otherwise allocable to HAPE) into a separate, interest-bearing subaccount (and specifically shall not invest HAPE’s net proceeds in the stock market or similar type investments) and shall not disburse those segregated funds until such time HAPE’s appeal in Nyece Chaney v. Automobile Driving Museum, et al. [LASC Case No. 24TRCV00805] has concluded, and upon further order of this Court.” The above language is stricken from the judgment and order.

 

            The instant Court lacks jurisdiction to make any orders that would encompass upon any orders and judgments that were made in Case No. 24TRCV00805. If Plaintiff has in fact obtained a stay of enforcement of the judgment in that case, then, of course, the Referee must follow any lawful orders of the Court. On the other hand, if there is no stay of enforcement, as the third party Chaney adamantly insists, then, the March 17, 2026 judgment and order as currently written clearly infringes upon her interests and acts as an improper de facto stay of enforcement of judgment. The interlocutory judgment and order in the instant action cannot act as a backdoor method to impose rights and obligations upon third parties in a separate action. As noted above, by the own authority set forth by Plaintiff in its opposition, the interlocutory judgment must only determine the interests of the parties in the subject property and not make any determination of the interests and rights of non-parties.

 

            Thus, on the Court’s own motion, paragraph 11(b) of the interlocutory judgment and order is hereby stricken.

 

            Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

Luisa Vianey Gallardo v. SCJ Group, et al.    

25TRCV01944

  1. Luisa Vianey Gallardo’s Motion for Relief from Dismissal            

 

TENTATIVE RULING

     

            Luisa Vianey Gallardo’s Motion for Relief From Dismissal is granted.   

 

            Background

 

Plaintiff filed the Complaint on June 13, 2025. Plaintiff alleges that she was involved in a motor vehicle collision with Defendants.

 

            Motion to Set Aside Dismissal

 

CCP § 473(b) states, in relevant part: “The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.  . . .  Notwithstanding any other requirements of this section, the court shall, whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney's sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against his or her client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. The court shall, whenever relief is granted based on an attorney's affidavit of fault, direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties. . . .”

 

Plaintiff moves for an order granting relief from the entry of dismissal. The motion is brought pursuant to CCP § 473 et seq. on the grounds that the dismissal was entered due to the mistake, inadvertence, surprise, and/or neglect of Plaintiff’s counsel.  Plaintiff has set forth facts to support a showing that the dismissal was entered due to the mistake, inadvertence, surprise, and/or neglect of Plaintiff’s counsel. Plaintiff’s counsel failed to appear at the July 14, 2026 status conference due to a calendaring mistake. (Decl. Jesus Becerra, ¶¶ 4-13). None of the arguments made by Defendant are persuasive. The Court is mandated to grant this motion upon an attorney declaration attesting to the fact that the dismissal was entered due to his mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. In fact, the neglect need not even be excusable neglect.

 

However, Defendant is correct on one point. Defendant may be entitled to reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs.

 

Code Civ. Proc., § 473 (c)(1) states:

“Whenever the court grants relief from a default, default judgment, or dismissal based on any of the provisions of this section, the court may do any of the following:

(A) Impose a penalty of no greater than one thousand dollars ($1,000) upon an offending attorney or party.

(B) Direct that an offending attorney pay an amount no greater than one thousand dollars ($1,000) to the State Bar Client Security Fund.

(C) Grant other relief as is appropriate.

(2) However, where the court grants relief from a default or default judgment pursuant to this section based upon the affidavit of the defaulting party's attorney attesting to the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, the relief shall not be made conditional upon the attorney's payment of compensatory legal fees or costs or monetary penalties imposed by the court or upon compliance with other sanctions ordered by the court.”

 

The Court notes, however, that Defendant simply made a vague request and provided no amount that is sought nor any declaration to support the amount sought.

 

The motion must be granted pursuant to the mandatory provision of CCP § 473(b). The dismissal is hereby set aside.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this ruling.
