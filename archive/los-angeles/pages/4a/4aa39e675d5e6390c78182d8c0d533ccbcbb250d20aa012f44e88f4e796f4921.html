Plaintiff Maral Tamar Baghjedjian moves for an award of attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses pursuant to Civil Code § 1794, subdivision (d). Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (“Defendant”) did not oppose the motion.

Under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (“SBA”), prevailing buyers have the burden to show that the fees incurred were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation and were reasonable in amounts. (Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 967, 998 (Doppes).) The SBA requires the trial court to make an initial determination of the actual time expended; and then to ascertain whether under all the circumstances of the case the amount of actual time expended, and the monetary charge being made for the time expended are reasonable.” (Tidrick v. FCA US LLC (2025) 112 Cal.App.5th 1147, 1158.) The question whether attorneys’ fees were reasonably incurred may depend on circumstances including, “factors such as the complexity of the case and procedural demands, the skill exhibited and the results achieved.” (Goglin v. BMW of North America, LLC (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 462, 470. The appellate court reviews awards of attorney fees under Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d), for abuse of discretion. (Doppes, supra, 174 Cal.App.4th at p. 998.)

Plaintiff’s counsel, Arye C. Abraham, requests a base lodestar of $50,252.50, a 1.2 multiplier of $10,050.44, and $688.25 in costs and expenses, for a total award of $60,991.19. Mr. Abraham requests an hourly rate of $675 for this case, citing two other trial court orders from March 2025 and January 2026 in which his hourly rate of $625 was approved. Mr. Abraham declares that his rate is based on factors such as his years of litigation experience, which include more than 250 SBA actions, the skills and knowledge acquired over those years, and the rates charged by other attorneys in the area. (Abraham Decl., ¶ 6.)

The motion is unopposed and Defendant does not dispute the reasonableness of Plaintiff’s counsel’s hourly rate. However, the Court notes that the billing records supporting Plaintiff’s counsel’s lodestar amount includes time entries for “anticipated” work in reviewing and responding to any opposition motion. The motion is unopposed, therefore, the Court reduces the base lodestar by 10 hours, for a total reduction of $6,750. The Court also declines to award a multiplier because there is no showing that the case required any exceptional skill, beyond that possessed by counsel and reflected in the hourly billing rate, or that it required Plaintiff’s counsel to turn down other work.

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s motion in part and awards $43,502.50 in attorney fees and $688.25 in costs and expenses, for a total award of $44,190.75.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO DEEM REQUEST FOR ADMISSIONS ADMITTED

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

United Financial Casualty Company (“UFCC”) is the respondent in an uninsured motorist insurance arbitration initiated by claimants Arsamak Oziev, Iza Ozieva, Amalia Ozieva, Adeliya Ozieva, Salm Ozieva, Aila Ozieva, and Safina Ozieva (collectively, “Claimants”).

The superior court has exclusive jurisdiction over discovery disputes in uninsured motorist arbitrations. (Miranda v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 913, 927.) Due to the superior court’s exclusive jurisdiction over discovery disputes by statute, service of process is not required for the Court to acquire jurisdiction and discovery motions can be served in an ordinary manner. (Id., pp. 927-928.) Here, UFCC’s motion was served on opposing counsel by mail.

Where a party fails to timely respond to a request for admission, the propounding party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) The court shall grant a motion to deem admitted requests for admissions, “unless it finds that the party to whom the requests for admission have been directed has served, before the hearing on the motion, a proposed response to the requests for admission that is in substantial compliance with Section 2033.220.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

On October 27, 2025, UFCC served a set of requests for admission on each of the Ozieva Claimants. (Hill Decl., ¶¶ 3-9.) The motion is unopposed and it is undisputed that no responses have been served by any of the Ozieva Claimants. It also does not appear that any of the Ozieva Claimants have served substantially compliant responses before the hearing. Accordingly, the motion to deem admitted is GRANTED.
