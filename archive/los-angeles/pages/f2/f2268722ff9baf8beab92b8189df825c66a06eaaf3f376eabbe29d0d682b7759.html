DEPARTMENT 509 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV32734    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 509

iCargoEx Inc. v. C.H. Robinson Transportation Company, Inc.

DEFENDANT’S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT
 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant C.H. Robinson Transportation Company, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY(S): None

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

Plaintiff iCargoEx Inc. (Plaintiff) contracted with defendant C.H. Robinson Transportation Company, Inc. (Defendant) for the delivery of freight. Plaintiff alleges that a third party stole the freight from Defendant’s facility during the execution of the contract. Plaintiff brings a single cause of action for negligence.

Defendant now moves for summary judgment. No opposition was filed. [FN 1] 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment is GRANTED.

Defendant is ordered to file a [Proposed] Judgment consistent with this ruling. 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.  

DISCUSSION:

Motion For Summary Judgment

I. Legal Standard
\
The function of a motion for summary judgment or adjudication is to allow a determination as to whether an opposing party cannot show evidentiary support for a pleading or claim and to enable an order of summary dismissal without the need for trial. (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843.) In analyzing motions for summary judgment, courts must apply a three-step analysis: “(1) identify the issues framed by the pleadings; (2) determine whether the moving party has negated the opponent’s claims; and (3) determine whether the opposition has demonstrated the existence of a triable, material factual issue.” (Hinesley v. Oakshade Town Center (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 289, 294.) Thus, summary judgment is granted when, after the Court’s consideration of the evidence set forth in the papers and all reasonable inferences accordingly, no triable issues of fact exist and the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. (Code Civ. Proc. § 437c(c); Villa v. McFarren (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 733, 741.)

As to each claim as framed by the complaint, the defendant moving for summary judgment must satisfy the initial burden of proof by presenting facts to negate an essential element, or to establish a defense. (Scalf v. D. B. Log Homes, Inc. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1510, 1520.) Courts “liberally construe the evidence in support of the party opposing summary judgment and resolve doubts concerning the evidence in favor of that party.” (Dore v. Arnold Worldwide, Inc.¿(2006) 39 Cal.4th 384, 389.) A defendant has met its burden of showing that a cause of action has no merit if it demonstrates the absence of any single essential element of plaintiff’s case or a complete defense to plaintiff’s action. (Code Civ. Proc. § 437c(o)(2); Bacon v. Southern Cal. Edison Co. (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 854, 858.) Once the moving party has met the burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff to show via specific facts that a triable issue of material facts exists as to a cause of action or a defense thereto. (Code Civ. Proc. § 437c(o)(2).)

II. Analysis

Defendant moves for summary adjudication of the sole cause of action for negligence and therefore, summary judgment. Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s cause of action fails on numerous grounds.

First, the Court must “identify the issues framed by the pleadings.” (Hinesley, supra, 135 Cal.App.4th at p. 294.)

Plaintiff alleges: “Defendant C.H. Robinson owes a duty of care to its customers such as Plaintiff iCargoEx Inc. to exercise proper care in selecting a competent motor carrier and securely transmitting shipment details to the motor carrier that it selects so that the shipment details are not intercepted by an unauthorized third party.” (Compl. ¶14.) “Defendant C.H. Robinson also owes a duty of care to its customers such as Plaintiff iCargoEx Inc. to exercise proper care when releasing freight that it is holding in its facilities to a third-party motor carrier to ensure that the freight that it has contracted to ship is released to the proper party and not stolen.” (Compl. ¶15.) “C.H. Robinson breached the duty of care it owed to Plaintiff iCargoEx Inc. by not selecting a motor carrier who would maintain the security of the transmission of the shipment details so as to allow the details of the shipment of the Freight to be unsecured and stolen intercepted by an unauthorized third-party thief. (Compl. ¶16.) “C.H. Robinson also breached the duty of care it owed to Plaintiff iCargoEx Inc. by not securing the transmission of the shipment details to the legitimate motor carrier that was supposed to pick up the Freight and deliver it to BJ’s Wholesale located in Burlington NJ so as to allow the details of the shipment of the Freight to be unsecured and intercepted by an unauthorized third-party thief.” (Compl. ¶17.) “Defendant C.H. Robinson also breached the duty of care it owed to Plaintiff iCargoEx Inc. by not properly checking the identity and/or credentials of the fraudulent motor carrier who arrived at Defendant C.H. Robinson’s facility and stole the Freight.” (Compl. ¶18.) 

Next, the burden begins with Defendant to show that “one or more elements of a cause of action . . . cannot be established.” (Aguilar, supra, 25 Cal.4th at p. 853.) Here, Defendant meets this burden.

Defendant argues “Plaintiff is not the real party in interest and, thus, lacks standing to assert its negligence claim in the first place.” (Mot. at p. 9:6-8.) Indeed, “[t]he owner of the Cargo is Plaintiff’s customer EliteLux.” (UMF No. 24.) Except as otherwise provided by statute, every action must be prosecuted in the name of the real party in interest. (Code Civ. Pro. §367; Cloud v. Northrop Grumman Corp. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 995, 1004.) “Thus, a person who commences a judicial proceeding is required to ‘have a real interest in the ultimate adjudication.’ [Citation] Such an interest is lacking if the party has ‘neither suffered nor is about to suffer any injury of sufficient magnitude reasonably to assure that all of the relevant facts and issues will be adequately presented.’” (Tulare Medical Center Property Owners Assn. v. Valdivia (2026) 119 Cal.App.5th 967, 992.) Therefore, Plaintiff lacks standing to bring an action arising from the loss of freight since the real party in interest is the owner of the freight, EliteLux, and not Plaintiff. This satisfies Defendant’s burden to demonstrate that Plaintiff lacks standing to bring this action.

This switches the burden to Plaintiff to establish a triable issue of material fact. However, Plaintiff does not put forth any evidence because Plaintiff did not oppose this motion. This Court is aware of its duty to “liberally construe the evidence in support of the party opposing summary judgment and resolve doubts concerning the evidence in favor of that party.” (Dore v. Arnold Worldwide, Inc.¿(2006) 39 Cal.4th 384, 389.) But a plaintiff seeking to avoid summary judgment “must produce substantial responsive evidence sufficient to establish a triable issue of material fact on the merits of the defendant’s showing…For this purpose, responsive evidence that gives rise to no more than mere speculation cannot be regarded as substantial, and is insufficient to establish a triable issue of material fact.” (Sangster v. Paetkau (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 151, 163, emphasis added, [bare assertion that moving party “fabricated” evidence is insufficient to avoid summary judgment.]) Here, Plaintiff has not put forth any admissible evidence whatsoever. Therefore, Plaintiff’s cause of action for negligence fails as a matter of law because Plaintiff has not demonstrated it has standing to pursue this action.  [FN 2] 

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment is GRANTED.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

FN 1 - Plaintiff’s attorney was properly and timely served with the instant motion via email on April 17, 2026. (See Not. of Mot. at p. 4/6.)

FN 2 - Defendant further argues Plaintiff’s action fails for four other reasons. (See Mot. at pp. 9-14, [wrong defendant, preemption, improper venue, and unlawful brokering.]) As Defendant’s motion is granted entirely on the issue of standing, the Court need not reach a conclusion on these additional issues.

Case Number: 26STCV04056    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 509

Soleimani & Associates, APLC v. Liyan Massaband

MOTION TO COMPEL ARBITRATION
 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Soleimani & Associates, APLC

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendant Liyan Massaband

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

This is an action to recover the non-payment of attorney’s fees. Plaintiff Soleimani & Associates, APLC (Plaintiff) represented defendant Liyan Massaband (Defendant) in a prior family law matter. Plaintiff alleges Defendant owes $99,632.81 in past due fees and costs. Plaintiff alleges causes of action for: (1) breach of contract; (2) open book account; and (3) account stated.

Plaintiff now moves to compel the matter to arbitration. Defendant opposed.

TENTATIVE RULING:

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Arbitration is GRANTED. The action is stayed pending the results of the arbitration.

A Status Review/OSC re: Dismissal is set for July 22, 2027, at 8:30 a.m.

Moving parties to give notice, unless waived.

DISCUSSION:

Motion to Compel Arbitration

1. Legal Standard

“[T]he petitioner bears the burden of proving the existence of a valid arbitration agreement by the preponderance of the evidence . . . .” (Giuliano v. Inland Empire Personnel, Inc. (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 1276, 1284). “In determining whether an arbitration agreement applies to a specific dispute, the court may examine only the agreement itself and the complaint filed by the party refusing arbitration [citation]. The court should attempt to give effect to the parties’ intentions, in light of the usual and ordinary meaning of the contractual language and the circumstances under which the agreement was made.” (Weeks v. Crow (1980) 113 Cal.App.3d 350, 353.) “Doubts as to whether an arbitration clause applies to a particular dispute are to be resolved in favor of sending the parties to arbitration. The court should order them to arbitrate unless it is clear that the arbitration clause cannot be interpreted to cover the dispute.” (California Correctional Peace Officers Ass'n v. State (2006) 142 Cal.App.4th 198, 205.) 

“[A] party opposing the petition bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence any fact necessary to its defense. [Citation.] In these summary proceedings, the trial court sits as a trier of fact, weighing all the affidavits, declarations, and other documentary evidence, as well as oral testimony received at the court's discretion, to reach a final determination.” (Giuliano v. Inland Empire Personnel, Inc. (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 1276, 1284.)

“If a court of competent jurisdiction, whether in this State or not, has ordered arbitration of a controversy which is an issue involved in an action or proceeding pending before a court of this State, the court in which such action or proceeding is pending shall, upon motion of a party to such action or proceeding, stay the action or proceeding until an arbitration is had in accordance with the order to arbitrate or until such earlier time as the court specifies. . . .” (Code Civ. Pro. §1281.4.)

2. Analysis

California has a strong public policy in favor of arbitration as an expeditious and cost-effective way of resolving disputes. “Even so, parties can only be compelled to arbitrate when they have agreed to do so.” (Avila v. S. California Specialty Care, Inc. (2018) 20 Cal. App. 5th 835, 843.) “The party seeking to compel arbitration bears the burden of proving the existence of a valid arbitration agreement.” (Ibid.)

An arbitration agreement is a contractual agreement. “General contract law principles include that ‘[t]he basic goal of contract interpretation is to give effect to the parties’ mutual intent at the time of contracting. [Citations.] ... The words of a contract are to be understood in their ordinary and popular sense.” [Citations.] (Garcia v. Expert Staffing W., 73 Cal. App. 5th 408, 412–13.)  

A. Existence of An Arbitration Agreement

Plaintiff has the initial burden of producing “prima facie evidence of a written agreement to arbitrate the controversy.” (Gamboa v. Ne. Cmty. Clinic (2021) 72 Cal. App. 5th 158, 165.) “[I]t is not necessary to follow the normal procedures of document authentication.” (Condee v. Longwood Mgmt. Corp. (2001) 88 Cal. App. 4th 215, 218.)

The Declaration of Shahram Soleimani states: “[o]n or about August 15, 2022, Defendant, Liyan Massaband (“Defendant”) retained my firm Soleimani & Associates, APLC (“Plaintiff”), to represent her in her dissolution matter (“the “Underlying Matter”) by signing a written fee agreement (“Agreement”). Attached hereto and marked as Exhibit “A” is a true and correct copy of the Agreement.” (Soleimani Decl. ¶3.)  

In relevant part, the Agreement provides: 

You agree that should any dispute arise between us, relating to the interpretation or performance of this agreement, attorneys’ fees and/or costs, or any dispute regarding the nature and quality of the services provided hereunder, including any claims for damages against the attorney or any of its principals and/or associates, whether on a theory of malpractice, negligence, breach of contract, fraud, breach of fiduciary duty or otherwise, we agree with you that such dispute shall be subject to and submitted to BINDING ARBITRATION. in Los Angeles County, pursuant to the Los Angeles County Bar Association Program, following the procedures set forth in California Code of Civil Procedure, Sections 1282 and 1286; and, that the Arbitrator in such a proceeding may award reasonable attorneys’ fees to the prevailing party in the arbitration.

You recognize that submitting a controversy such as this to an arbitrator constitute a waiver of your right to a court or JURY TRIAL as well as the right to appeal the arbitrator's decision, and may also impose certain limitations upon discovery procedures. Despite this fact, you agree, as do we, to arbitrate any controversies that arise between us because of the relative speed and cost savings that often accompanies such arbitration proceedings.

It is further understood that the initiator of the arbitration proceedings shall advance all costs in connection therewith unless the other party files a cross-action in which event the fees and costs of the arbitration shall be borne proportionately by the parties in the ratio that their monetary claims bear to one another subject to ultimate possible re-allocation/re-apportionment at the discretion of the arbitrator. Service of process in connection with any such arbitration proceeding may be made by certified mail at the last known address of the other party. In the event that either party is required to take legal action against the other in connection with this written agreement, the prevailing party, in addition to other rights, shall be entitled to recover attorney's fees and costs whether or not a lawsuit is filed.

(Soleimani Decl., Exh. A at p. 6.)

Plaintiff has therefore met its initial burden to demonstrate an arbitration provision applicable to this current action.

This switches the burden to Defendant, who “bears the burden of producing evidence to challenge the authenticity of the agreement.” (Gamboa, supra, 72 Cal.App.5th at p. 165.) Defendant can do this in “several ways,” including by “declar[ing] under penalty of perjury that the party never saw or does not remember seeing the agreement, or that the party never signed or does not remember signing the agreement.” (Ibid.)

However, Defendant does not dispute the existence of the arbitration provision, or that the arbitration provision generally covers the dispute here. Instead, Defendant relies upon defenses to enforcement addressed below. 

B. Defenses to Enforcement

Defendant argues that Plaintiff has waived its right to arbitration by initiating this instant action. Defendant argues that “Plaintiff waived his right to compel arbitration by failing to commence arbitration and instead filing this lawsuit.” (Opp. at p. 3:9-10.)

A motion to compel arbitration may be denied when the contract law defense of waiver is applicable. (Quach v. California Commerce Club, Inc. (2024) 16 Cal.5th 562, 583-584.) “To establish waiver under generally applicable contract law, the party opposing enforcement of a contractual agreement must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the waiving party knew of the contractual right and intentionally relinquished or abandoned it.” (Id., at p. 584.) The standard of “clear and convincing evidence” requires one to “show that it is “highly probable” the fact is true.” (Ibid.) The knowledge required by waiver may be “actual or constructive.” (Outboard Marine Corp. v. Superior Court (1975) 52 Cal.App.3d 30, 41.) An intent to relinquish or abandon “may be proved by evidence of words expressing an intent to relinquish the right or of conduct that is so inconsistent with an intent to enforce the contractual right as to lead a reasonable factfinder to conclude that the party had abandoned it.” (Quatch, supra, 16 Cal.5th at p. 584.) This “inquiry is exclusively focused on the waiving party’s words or conduct; neither the effect of that conduct on the party seeking to avoid enforcement of the contractual right nor that party’s subjective evaluation of the waiving party’s intent is relevant.” (Id., at p. 585.)

Here, Defendant argues that Plaintiff waived arbitration by filing this instant action. Defendant points to the “Notice of Client’s Right To Fee Arbitration” which states “[y]ou will LOSE YOUR RIGHT TO ARBITRATION UNDER THIS PROGRAM if:… YOU FILE AN ACTION OR PLEADING IN ANY LAWSUIT WHICH SEEKS A COURT DECISION ON THIS DISPUTE OR WHICH SEEKS DAMAGES FOR ANY ALLEGED MALPRACTICE OR PROFESSIONAL MISCONDUCT.” (Exhibit 1 to the Opp.)

However, this document does not evidence an intentional waiver of Plaintiff’s right to arbitration. First, this notice does not appear to be a contract between the parties but merely a notice to Defendant. Second, this action is not seeking damages for malpractice or professional misconduct. Third, this provision appears to notice Defendant’s right to arbitration in a specific program. It does not appear to change any arbitration agreement between the parties. And finally, the same document states “No lawsuit or arbitration proceeding has yet been filed but may be filed if we do not resolve this claim.” (Exhibit 1 to the Opp.) This provision seems to indicate that Plaintiff here may file a lawsuit or proceed in a separate arbitration if the parties do not reach an agreement. Indeed, it appears that this notice was to allow Defendant to agree to arbitration without Court intervention but Defendant but failed to respond. (Soleimani Decl. ¶4, [“On or about December 14, 2023, Defendant was served with a Notice of Client’s Right to Fee Arbitration, however, Defendant failed to respond. Thereafter, on February 6, 2026, I filed a Complaint.]) 

Finally, the Court does not find Plaintiff’s initiation of this action as “clear and convincing evidence” of an intentional relinquishment of their right to arbitration. Plaintiff filed this motion on May 11, 2026, only ten days after Defendant filed their answer to this complaint. These actions are not indicative of an intentional waiver necessary to deny a motion to compel arbitration. 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Arbitration is GRANTED. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
