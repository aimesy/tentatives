Case Number: 24STCV12125    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV12125 Troncoso v. FCA U.S. LLC

Tuesday, September 15, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS.  (3704)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The complaint alleges Defendant breached an express warranty regarding a 2020 Jeep Grand Cherokee purchased by Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs move for an order compelling Defendant to serve responses to special interrogatories, set one, served on Defendant on July 2, 2024.

      As of September 9, 2026, Plaintiffs did not file a reply brief.     

II.     DISCUSSION

      Where a party fails to respond to interrogatories, the court has authority to compel a response. (Code Civ. Proc., §2030.290 subd. (b).) Untimely responses result in a waiver of objections. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290 subd. (a).) As Defendant did not serve responses, all objections are waived.

      Defendant argues that the parties had an informal arrangement for all cases between them wherein Plaintiffs’ counsel Defendant an open-ended extension to respond to Plaintiffs’ discovery. Defendant contends the arrangement provided it was not required to respond unless specifically requested by Plaintiff’s counsel. This motion undermines the parties’ agreement. However, Defendant states it served verified responses on June 3, 2026, one day after Plaintiffs filed this motion.

      Defendant did not submit copies of its belated responses purportedly served on Plaintiffs. Nor has Defendant submitted a copy of the parties’ written agreement granting Defendant an open-ended extension to respond to all discovery. Plaintiffs’ counsel states that Plaintiffs previously granted a limited extension of time to respond, but because the responses were substantially overdue, Plaintiffs revoked any prior extension by letter dated May 20, 2026. (Rucker decl., Ex. 4.)

      Accordingly, Plaintiffs are entitled to fees and costs as sanctions for Defendant’s failure to show substantial justification for failing to timely respond.

III.  CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ motion is GRANTED. Defendant is ordered to serve verified responses to the first set of special interrogatories without objection within 10 days. Defendant has not shown substantial justification for its failure to timely serve discovery responses. The Court imposes monetary sanctions of $1,155 ($385 x 3 hours) against Defendant, FCA US, LLC, payable to Plaintiffs within 10 days.

 


Case Number: 25STCV15519    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV15519 Jae Ho Son v. Cenocore, Inc., et al.

Tuesday, September 15, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DEFENDANTS' MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF'S FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES-GENERAL, SET ONE; AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS IN THE AMOUNT OF $882.50 AGAINST PLAINTIFF AND HIS COUNSEL (4886)

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DEFENDANTS’ MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF'S FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR ADMISSIONS, SET ONE; AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS IN THE AMOUNT OF $945.00 AGAINST PLAINTIFF AND HIS COUNSEL (0845)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      Plaintiff alleges wage and hour violations under the Labor Code.  Defendants move to compel Plaintiff to provide further responses to general form interrogatories and requests for admission. Defendants contend Plaintiff responded with “boilerplate” objections to interrogatory 2.6, evasive responses to interrogatory 12.1, and non-substantive responses to interrogatory 17.1 although 44 of the 45 requests for admissions were not unqualified admissions which therefore requires complete responses to interrogatory 17.1. Defendants contend Plaintiff refuses to supplement despite attempts to meet and confer. Defendants request imposition of monetary sanctions.

      With respect to the requests for admission, Defendants contend that the response to 41 of 45 requests is are evasive, incomplete and do not comply with statute.

      Plaintiff’s consolidated opposition to all discovery motions argues that Defendants failed to meaningfully meet and confer, that Plaintiff’s responses are code compliant, and that the Court should impose monetary sanctions on Defendants.

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      The Court can grant a motion to compel further responses to form interrogatories where the requesting party believes the responses are evasive and incomplete, that an objection is without merit, or an exercise of the option to produce documents is unwarranted. (Code Civ. Proc., sections 2030.300 subd. (a).) A party may move for an order compelling further responses to requests for admission if the requesting party deems that the responses are evasive or incomplete or an objection is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.290.)

 

III.  DISCUSSION

A.     Meet and confer

      Defendants satisfied their obligations to meet and confer. In response to Defendants’ May 5, 2026 detailed letter, Plaintiff reiterated that Plaintiff was not supplementing discovery requests because Plaintiff believed the responses were adequate. (Pham opposing decl. Ex. 3.)  This was preceded by numerous follow-up conversations about the discovery and other matters, including extensions of time for Plaintiff to respond to written discovery. (Id. Ex. 1, 2.)

B.     Form interrogatories

      FR 2.6. This interrogatory requests the identity and address of Plaintiff’s employers for the past five years. Plaintiff cites Alch v. Superior Court (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 1412 wherein the Court of Appeal determined that a non-party’s work history (e.g. names of employers, dates of employment, job titles, etc.) is protected by a right of privacy but subject to discovery where a “compelling need” is shown. (Alch at 1426-1427.)

      Defendants have not demonstrated why the information is generally relevant, much less Defendants’ “compelling need” for Plaintiff’s other work history information. This case seeks statutory penalties for Defendants’ alleged failure to provide overtime, rest periods, and failure to provide and maintain accurate wage statements and records. Defendants’ reply brief does not further elaborate on this issue. This interrogatory does not request foundational identifying witness information. (Reply 9:15-23.)

      FR 12.1 The request for identifying witness information with knowledge of the “incident” is relevant. Defendants’ argument has merit. Defendants contend that “incident” as defined for employment law actions encompasses the circumstances and events giving rise to the action and identification of individuals with knowledge of Plaintiff’s compensation arrangements and work schedule, at a minimum.

      Plaintiff’s “not applicable” response is evasive.

      FR 17.1 Plaintiff is required to provide facts, witnesses, and supporting documentation for every response to a request for admission that is not an unqualified admission. Where a party denies an admission, the party is required to disclose the factual basis for that non-admission.

      Plaintiff agrees that any response to a request for admission based on personal knowledge may entitle Defendants to a proper response, but Plaintiff should not be compelled to disclose attorney work product or privileged information. Plaintiff concedes that “[a]t most, any further response should be limited to the specific RFAs for which Plaintiff provided a denial or qualified

response and should exclude privileged or protected information.” (P’s (Opp.  Sep. Stmt. 9:17-19.)

      At issue here are 44 of the 45 requests for admission that Plaintiff denied. Defendants are entitled to the factual bases for each denial and supporting witness and evidentiary information.

C.     Requests for admission 3, 4, 6, 7, 9-45.

      None of Plaintiff’s objections have merit. The statement that Plaintiff cannot or deny because it is irrelevant is not a code-compliant response. As Defendants observe, there are only three permissible answers: admit, deny, or if the responding party lacks information or knowledge, “that party shall state in the answer that a reasonable inquiry concerning the matter in the particular request has been made, and that the information known or readily obtainable is insufficient to enable that party to admit the matter.” (Cal. Code Civ. Procedure § CCP § 2033.220.)

      Plaintiff’s response that “[w]hether or not Plaintiff was subjected to any timekeeping or

attendance tracking requirements does not absolve Defendants of its record-keeping obligation” is not code compliant. The requests are relevant to Plaintiff’s allegation that Defendants failed to abide by Labor Code requirements in a wage and hour case including the failure to provide accurate wage statements and records.

      The response that Plaintiff “cannot or admit or deny because the request is irrelevant” is not a code-compliant response, nor is it a valid objection. The requests are relevant to the particular Labor Code violations alleged.

      Plaintiff argues that an admission “does not, standing alone, establish Plaintiff’s employment classification, the hours he worked, or whether Defendants complied with their independent obligations under California wage-and-hour law.” (Opp.  SS 2:24-28.) What Defendants can ultimately prove is irrelevant.

IV. CONCLUSION

      Accordingly, Defendants' motion to compel plaintiff's further responses to form interrogatories is DENIED in part as to form interrogatory 2.6. The motion is GRANTED as to form interrogatories 12.1 and 17.1. Plaintiff is ordered to serve further responses within 10 days that are complete and code-compliant and without objections. The request for imposition of sanctions is granted, but in a reduced amount. Sanctions are imposed against Plaintiff and his counsel of record, Moon Law Group, PC, jointly and severally, in the amount of $400 payable to Defendants within 10 days.

      Defendants’ motion to compel further responses to requests for admission is GRANTED in full. Plaintiff is ordered to serve further, code-compliant responses within 10 days to requests 3, 4, 6, 7, 9-45, without objection. Sanctions of $945 are imposed against Plaintiff and his counsel

of record, Moon Law Group, PC, jointly and severally, in the amount of $945.00, payable to Defendants within 10 days.

 


Case Number: 25STCV18416    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV18416 Alfa Prosmarttech PTE, LTD, et al. v. Izrailbekova, et al.

Tuesday, September 15, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER SUSTAINING IN PART DEFENDANTS, ALEXSANDER SEMERDGIAN’S AND GALINA IZRAILBEKOVA’S, DEMURRER TO SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT (9404)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The second amended complaint (“SAC”) seeks recovery of more than $9 million dollars in funds allegedly stolen by Nigina Izrailbekova (“Izrailbekova”), from the five corporate entities (“Plaintiffs”) she was employed to serve. Plaintiffs allege seven causes of action for breach of contract, misrepresentation, conversion, and other claims arising from these alleged facts.

      The SAC alleges that Defendant Alexsander Semerdgian (“Semerdgian”), Izrailbekova’s husband, misappropriated the money from Plaintiffs’ accounts and moved the funds to accounts Defendants owned and controlled.  Plaintiffs allege that Defendant, Galina Izrailbekova (“Galina”) Izrailbekova’s mother, made false claims to conceal the theft and to delay Plaintiffs from discovering the full extent of Izrailbekova’s misconduct.

      Defendants demur to all seven causes of action as specifically discussed below.

 

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      A demurrer tests the sufficiency of a complaint as a matter of law and raises only questions of law. (Schmidt v. Foundation Health (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 1702, 1706.) In testing the complaint’s sufficiency, the court must assume the truth of the properly pleaded factual allegations as well as facts that can be reasonably inferred from those expressly pleaded facts. The court may also consider matters properly subject to judicial notice. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

      The court may not consider contentions, deductions, or conclusions of fact or law. (Moore v. Conliffe (1994) 7 Cal.4th 634, 638.) Plaintiff is required to allege facts sufficient to establish every element of each cause of action. (Rakestraw v. California Physicians Service (2000) 81 Cal.App.4th 39, 43.) Where the complaint fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action, courts should sustain the demurrer. Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10(e); (Zelig v. County of Los Angeles (2002) 27 Cal.4th 1112, 1126.)

      Sufficient facts are the essential facts of the case stated, "with reasonable precision and with particularity that is sufficiently specific to acquaint the defendant with the nature, source, and extent of his cause of action.” (Gressley v. Williams (1961) 193 Cal.App.2d 636, 643-644.)  Whether the Plaintiff will be able to prove the pleaded facts is irrelevant. (Stevens v. Superior Court (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 605, 609–610.)

      A demurrer may be sustained if a complaint is “uncertain.” Uncertainty exists where a complaint’s factual allegations are so confusing, vague, or ambiguous that they do not sufficiently apprise a defendant of the issues it is being asked to meet. (Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, fn. 2; Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10(f).)

III.    DISCUSSION

 

A.     Plaintiffs’ capacity to sue. SUSTAINED.

 

      Defendants argue that Plaintiffs Alfa Prosmarttech PTE, Ltd., Martorell S.R.O., and Suntronix PTE, Ltd. lack capacity to maintain this action because they are foreign corporations transacting intrastate business in California without having complied with Corporations Code section 2203. The SAC alleges that, “[t]o the extent any Plaintiff is deemed to transact intrastate business in California within the meaning of California Corporations Code §§ 191 and 2105, such Plaintiff has complied, or will comply, with all requirements of Corporations Code § 2203 to maintain this action.” (SAC ¶ 17.)

      The Court has already addressed the asserted capacity defect. Defendant Nigina Izrailbekova raised the same issue as to the same three Plaintiffs in her prior demurrer to the SAC. On August 4, 2026, the Court sustained the demurrer only as to the capacity of Alfa, Martorell, and Suntronix and ordered those Plaintiffs to file the necessary receipts establishing compliance with Corporations Code section 2203 by September 15, 2026. (M.O. 8/4/26.) The present demurrer therefore raises the same asserted disability that is already the subject of the Court’s September 15, 2026 Order to Show Cause.

      A foreign corporation need not qualify in California to commence an action. Rather, where Corporations Code section 2203 applies, the corporation may not maintain the action until it satisfies the statutory requirements. (United Medical Management Ltd. v. Gatto (1996) 49 Cal.App.4th 1732, 1739.) The resulting disability is ordinarily addressed by abating the action to permit compliance. (Id. at p. 1740 [“Ordinarily, the matter should be stayed to permit the foreign corporation to comply. If the foreign corporation plaintiff complies with section 2203, subdivision (c), by qualifying and paying fees, penalties and taxes, it may maintain the action. If the foreign corporation fails to comply, the matter should be dismissed without prejudice.”].

      Plaintiffs have now filed a response to the Court’s August 4, 2026, order describing their efforts toward compliance. Plaintiffs report that they obtained current home-jurisdiction documentation for all three corporations: certificates issued by the Singapore Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority on August 28, 2026, identifying Alfa and Suntronix as live companies, and an August 26, 2026 extract from the Slovak commercial register for Martorell. Plaintiffs acknowledge, however, that no Statement and Designation by Foreign Corporation has yet been filed in California for any of the three corporations.

      Plaintiffs further represent that Alfa faces an additional obstacle because its corporate name is already registered in California. According to Plaintiffs, Defendant Nigina Izrailbekova caused a California corporation bearing the name “Alfa Prosmarttech PTE. LTD.” to be formed in January 2025 without Alfa’s knowledge or authorization. Plaintiffs contend that Alfa therefore must either obtain relief concerning the conflicting name or qualify under an alternate name pursuant to Corporations Code section 2106. The Secretary of State record submitted with Plaintiffs’ response reflects that an entity named “ALFA PROSMARTTECH PTE LTD” was incorporated in California on January 16, 2025, with Nigina Izrailbekova identified as the incorporator and agent for service of process.

      Finally, Plaintiffs have not yet determined whether Alfa, Martorell, or Suntronix owes California franchise taxes for any period relevant to section 2203(c). Counsel represents that California tax counsel has been identified, and an engagement is being finalized, but that the necessary multi-year tax analysis cannot begin until after October 15, 2026. Plaintiffs propose thereafter to complete the required filings, pay the filing fees and $250 statutory penalty for each entity, pay any taxes determined to be due, and report their compliance to the Court.

      Accordingly, the circumstances underlying the Court’s August 4 ruling have not materially changed. The demurrer based upon lack of capacity is therefore SUSTAINED as to Alfa, Martorell, and Suntronix. This ruling does not impose a separate or additional compliance requirement. The capacity defect is the same defect already subject to the Court’s August 4, 2026, order, and Plaintiffs’ efforts to cure it will continue to be addressed through the pending Order to Show Cause. Plaintiffs do not contend that the statutory cure is complete. Rather, they have presented evidence of steps undertaken toward compliance and request additional time to complete the process. They request a written status report by November 19, 2026, and continuation of the OSC to a date on or after February 8, 2027.

     

B.     Second cause of action for Penal Code § 496 violation against Galina (mother). OVERRULED.

      Penal Code § 496 criminalizes the buying or receiving of stolen property knowing it was stolen or obtained “in any manner constituting theft or extortion.” (Pen. Code, § 496.) “Theft” includes taking personal property “by any false or fraudulent representation or pretense." (Pen. Code, § 484(a).)

      Galina argues the second amended complaint does not allege that Galina received or obtained Plaintiffs’ property or that Galina knew any money from Plaintiffs’ bank accounts had been stolen. Defendant claims that heightened pleading is required.

      The claim alleges that Semerdgian received, possessed, and controlled portions of the stolen funds that Izrailbekova transferred into separate accounts owned or under Semerdgian’s control that Semerdgian knew had been stolen from Plaintiffs. (SAC ¶ 56.) 

      Plaintiffs allege that Defendant Galina aided in concealing and withholding the property by knowingly making false statements to Plaintiffs about Izrailbekova’s interrogation by the FBI with the intent to deter Plaintiffs from continuing their investigation and discovering the disposition of their stolen property. (SAC ¶ 58.) Liability against Galina is based on a co-conspiracy in a common scheme to defraud Plaintiffs. (SAC ¶ 59.) That Galini allegedly made false statements about the FBI to deter Plaintiffs infers knowledge that the funds were wrongfully obtained.

 

C.     Third and fourth causes of action for negligent misrepresentation and intentional misrepresentation, respectively, against Galina. OVERRULED.

 

      A claim for fraud requires facts to support the following elements: (1) a misrepresentation, (2) made with knowledge of its falsity, (3) Defendant intended to defraud Plaintiff, i.e., induce Plaintiff’s reliance, (4) Plaintiff justifiably relied on the misrepresentation, (5) causing damage. (Nagy v. Nagy (1989) 210 Cal.App.3d 1262, 1268.)

      A claim for negligent misrepresentation differs only as to the second element, which requires proof that the defendants made the representation without reasonable ground for believing it to be true. (Ragland v. U.S. Bank National Assn. (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 182, 196.)

      Fraud claims are subject to strict requirements of particularity in pleading. (Id.) The particularity requirements necessitate pleading facts showing “how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered." (Stansfield v. Starkey (1990) 220 Cal.App.3d 59, 73.) The requirement “applies not only to the alleged misrepresentation, but also to the elements of causation and damage." (Moncada v. West Coast Quartz Corp. (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 768, 776.)

      Where fraud is alleged against a corporation, the pleading standards are even greater. Plaintiff must allege “the names of the persons who made the allegedly fraudulent representations, their authority to speak, to whom they spoke, what they said or wrote, and when it was said or written." (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal. 4th 631, 645.)

      Galina argues that Plaintiffs should specifically identify, to whom Galina allegedly made a misrepresentation, when it was made, by what means it was made, what exactly was said, and what each Plaintiff did in reliance on it.

      Plaintiffs allege that in or around August and September 2024, Izrailbekova made positive assertions of material fact including that Plaintiffs’ respective U.S. bank accounts had been frozen or otherwise restricted by the banks. (SAC ¶64.) The statements were made with intent to induce Plaintiffs to rely on these statements, delay their inquiries, and prevent Plaintiffs from discovering her ongoing embezzlement. (SAC ¶ 64.) Galina, allegedly acting as Izrailbekova’s agent, falsely told Plaintiffs that Izrailbekova had been confronted by the FBI and was instructed to cease all communications. These representations were not true, and Galina had no reasonable grounds for believing they were true. (SAC ¶ 65)

      In reliance on those statements, Plaintiffs were induced to forbear from taking immediate action to investigate their accounts, to secure their funds, and to terminate Izrailbekova’s access. This reliance was to Plaintiffs’ detriment as the delay allowed Izrailbekova to continue misappropriating funds from the accounts. (SAC ¶ 67.) Plaintiffs lost funds of $9,024,157.38 as a result of their reliance on Defendant’s false statements. (SAC ¶ 67.)

      Plaintiffs’ arguments in opposition have merit.  There are exceptions to the rule requiring specificity in pleading "which mitigate the rigor of the rule requiring specific pleading of fraud. Less specificity is required when ‘it appears from the nature of the allegations that the defendant must necessarily possess full information concerning the facts of the controversy,’ (Bradley v. Hartford Acc. & Indem. Co. (1973) 30 Cal.App.3d 818, 825 [106 Cal.Rptr. 718]); “[e]ven under the strict rules of common law pleading, one of the canons was that less particularity is required when the facts lie more in the knowledge of the opposite party ..." (Committee On Children's Television, Inc. v. General Foods Corp. (1983) 35 Cal.3d 197, 217.)

      Plaintiffs allege that Galina initiated these false statements to Plaintiffs’ principles by telephone and email, and therefore, the specifics lie more in Galina’s knowledge than the Plaintiffs. (SAC ¶ 37, 65, 74.). Contrary to Defendant’s reply, Plaintiffs specifically allege causation and damage, namely, that because of Plaintiffs’ reliance on Galina’s misrepresentations, Plaintiffs failed to take immediate action including terminating access to Plaintiffs’ accounts and initiating a forensic investigation, such that Defendants were able to steal funds totaling $9,024,157.38. (SAC ¶ 76-77.)

D.     Fifth cause of action for conversion alleged against Galina. OVERRULED.

      Defendant argues that Plaintiffs did not allege that Galina took or held any of Plaintiffs’ funds, rather Israilbekova transferred Plaintiffs’ funds to Sermedgian who received them. (SAC ¶ 81.) Plaintiffs concede this issue but argue that Galina participated in a conspiracy to embezzle and conceal the funds from Plaintiffs and that she assisted in the conversion by making false statements and fabricating an FBI story. (Opp.  10:3-7)

      To prevail on a claim for conversion, Plaintiffs must establish their ownership interest at the time of the conversion and Defendants’ wrongful act or dispossession of Plaintiffs’ property rights. (Moore v. Regents of University of California (1990) 51 Cal.3d 120, 136.) The basic elements are (1) the plaintiff's ownership or right to possession of personal property; (2) the defendant's disposition of the property in a manner that is inconsistent with the plaintiff's property rights; and (3) resulting damages. (Fremont Indemnity Co. v. Fremont General Corp. (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 97, 119.)

      Galina’s argument addresses whether she personally committed the underlying conversion but does not dispose of Plaintiffs’ aiding-and-abetting theory. Plaintiffs allege that Nigina wrongfully transferred and withheld specifically identifiable funds belonging to Plaintiffs and that Galina, with knowledge of the wrongful conduct, substantially assisted its continuation by falsely representing that Nigina had been confronted by the FBI and instructed to cease communicating with Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs further allege that the representation caused them to refrain from immediately investigating their accounts, securing their funds, and terminating Nigina’s access, thereby permitting the misappropriation and concealment of their funds to continue.

      Whether Galina ultimately possessed the requisite knowledge or whether her alleged conduct constituted substantial assistance presents factual questions not properly resolved on demurrer. At the pleading stage, the allegations are sufficient to state a claim based upon aiding and abetting conversion. The demurrer to the fifth cause of action is OVERRULED.

 

E.     Sixth cause of action for breach of fiduciary duty against Galina and Semerdgian. OVERRULED.

      Galina argues that Plaintiffs do not allege that Galina owed Plaintiffs a fiduciary duty; rather the alleged theory is based on conspiracy or aiding and abetting. Galina argues that she must have been capable of committing the target tort in order to support a conspiracy to breach fiduciary duty. Only Izrailbekova is alleged to have owed a fiduciary duty to Plaintiffs. (SAC ¶ 86.) Plaintiffs allege Semerdgian and Galina had knowledge of the fiduciary relationship between Izrailbekova and Plaintiffs and knowingly assisted in the breach by making false statements about the FBI to conceal the theft. (SAC ¶ 88.)

      Defendant relies on IIG Wireless, Inc. v. Yi (2018) 22 Cal.App.5th 630, 652 for the proposition that Defendant “must be capable of committing the target tort”, and since Plaintiffs do not allege Galina or Semerdgian owed Plaintiffs a fiduciary duty, they cannot be liable for conspiracy. (Dem 10:10-13.)  Plaintiffs do not dispute this principle, that a conspiracy requires that the defendant be independently capable of committing the underlying tort. (Opp.  11:5-8.)

      However, Plaintiffs argue that Defendants conflate the requirements for conspiracy with “aiding and abetting.” The argument has merit. Plaintiffs cite Casey v. U.S. Bank Nat. Assn. (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 1138, 1145 which affirms that in a claim for “aiding and abetting” the substantial assistance element is satisfied if defendant "actually knew those transactions were assisting the customer in committing a specific tort.” (Casey at 1145 fn. 2 ["under California law liability for aiding and abetting a tort differs fundamentally from liability based on conspiracy to commit a tort. For the reasons articulated so well in the Neilson opinion, we reject the banks' attempt to overlay the civil conspiracy “independent duty” requirement onto an aiding and abetting claim."].)

      Defendants concede the issue in reply and argue the sixth cause of action survives as an aiding-and-abetting claim, but Plaintiffs did not adequately allege that claim. (Reply, 8:25-9:3.) A claim for aiding and abetting in an intentional tort requires that defendant “(a) knows the other's conduct constitutes a breach of duty and gives substantial assistance or encouragement to the other to so act or (b) gives substantial assistance to the other in accomplishing a tortious result and the person's own conduct, separately considered, constitutes a breach of duty to the third person.” (Saunders v. Superior Court (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 832, 846, 33 Cal.Rptr.2d 438; Rest.2d Torts, § 876.) Mere knowledge that a tort is being committed and the failure to prevent it does not constitute aiding and abetting." (Fiol v. Doellstedt (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 1318, 1325–1326.)

      The second amended complaint alleges that Semerdgian and Galina had actual knowledge of the fiduciary relationship between Izrailbekova and Plaintiffs and knowingly provided substantial assistance in Izrailbekova’s breach of fiduciary duty when Galina made false statements to Plaintiffs to conceal the theft and Semerdgian received and controlled the stolen funds (SAC ¶ 88.) This claim is adequately alleged against both Defendants.  

IV.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, demurrer to the second amended complaint is SUSTAINED as to Plaintiffs’ capacity to maintain an action under Corp. Code, § 2203. Demurrer is OVERRULED in all other respects.

 


Case Number: 25STCV23355    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV23355 Pedro Armando Serna v. Christian Guardado, et al.

Tuesday, September 15, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING IN PART PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL NONPARTY CELLCO PARTNERSHIP DBA VERIZON WIRELESS’S COMPLIANCE WITH DEPOSITION SUBPOENA FOR PRODUCTION OF BUSINESS RECORDS (8989)

 

I.       ARGUMENTS

      This action arises from an alleged motor vehicle accident. Plaintiff requests an order to compel production of documents in compliance with a subpoena duces tecum served on non-party, CELLCO PARTNERSHIP DBA VERIZON WIRELESS, (“Verizon”) for the production of Defendant’s cell phone records.

      Plaintiff served the motion on the non-party and Defendants. No opposition has been filed.

II.     DISCUSSION

      Discovery materials may be obtained from a person who is not a party to the action by serving a deposition subpoena for production of business records and things. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.010.) A party seeking such records may move for an order to compel a non-party to comply with a deposition subpoena for production of records. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480.)

      The subpoena requests “All call detail records, text message logs, incoming and outgoing call records and subscriber information for [an identified number] for April 24, 2024.” (Khoubian decl., Ex. A.)  The records are relevant to the issue of Defendant’s potential use of a cellphone at or around the time of collision.

      Plaintiff requests imposition of monetary sanctions for misuse of the discovery process for the non-party’s failure to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery. (Cal. Code Civ. Procedure § 2023.010 (d).) The request is defective as the sanction request is not made in the notice. Requesting party must identify in the notice, the person, party and attorney against whom the sanction is sought as well as the type of sanction sought. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.) Due process requires that a party seeking sanctions give notice. (London v. Dri-Honing Corp. (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 999, 1008.)

III.  CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion is GRANTED. CELLCO PARTNERSHIP DBA VERIZON WIRELESS is ordered to produce without objection within 10 days documents that are responsive to the deposition subpoena served on June 12, 2026 (Khoubian decl., ¶ 3.) Plaintiff’s request for imposition of monetary sanctions is DENIED

Case Number: 25STCV24127    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV24127 JEANETTE FRANCE V. CITY OF LOS ANGELES

Tuesday, September 15, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR JUDGMENT ON THE PLEADINGS FILED BY DEFENDANT, LOS ANGELES DEPARTMENT OF WATER AND POWER (1162)

 

I.         BACKGROUND

      The lead matter is BC719459 Jeanette France v. Los Angeles Department of Water and Power, which alleged discrimination and related claims in violation of the Fair Employment and Housing Act. The lead matter ended in judgment entered on December 17, 2019 in favor of Defendant after the Court granted Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment.  

      In this action, deemed related to BC719459, the complaint alleges that Plaintiff sustained a work-related injury on January 9, 2017, for which Plaintiff filed a worker's compensation claim. Defendant terminated Plaintiff’s employment in retaliation while she was on approved worker's compensation leave. Plaintiff alleges eleven causes of action for violation of the Fair Employment and Housing Act and related employment claims.

      On May 6, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to deem Plaintiff a vexatious litigant and required a prefiling order before Plaintiff could commence new litigation.

II.   ARGUMENTS

      Defendant now moves for judgment on the pleadings on all eleven causes of action alleged in Plaintiff’s complaint filed August 18, 2025, on grounds the claims were previously litigated in BC719459 and resolved in Defendant’s favor, and therefore, res judicata bars the claims. The time for seeking relief from that judgment has long since expired. The FEHA claims are also barred by the three-year statute of limitations under Government Code § 12960.

      Defendant also argues that the non-FEHA claims for defamation and emotional distress are barred because Plaintiff failed to timely present a government claim. Defendant terminated Plaintiff in 2018. Plaintiff is not entitled to punitive damages against a government entity.

      Plaintiff argues in opposition that Defendant did not properly serve Plaintiff with its answer. Plaintiff requests relief from judgment from judgment in the lead matter based on extrinsic fraud as she was deprived of the opportunity to present her case and based on mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. Plaintiff argues the law favors disposition of a case on its merits, and that Plaintiff will suffer great hardship if this motion is granted. The Court also has authority to stay enforcement of judgment.

      The Court has reviewed the motion, opposition, and reply briefs, and all documents in support.

III.  LEGAL STANDARDS

       The two statutory grounds supporting a motion for judgment on the pleadings are either that the court does not have jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action or the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(B)). A motion for judgment on the pleadings performs the same function as a general demurrer and attacks only defects disclosed on the face of the pleadings or by matters subject to judicial notice. Respondents. Burnett v. Chimney Sweep (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 1057, 1065 (“a motion for judgment on the pleadings, like a demurrer, raises only questions of law ... .”)

      For purposes of this motion, all properly alleged material facts are deemed true as well as all facts that may be implied or inferred from those expressly alleged. (Fire Ins. Exchange v. Superior Court (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 446, 452).

II.        DISCUSSION

A.   Preliminary matters.

      Plaintiff’s contention that she was not properly served with the answer has no bearing on this motion. Defendant timely served Plaintiff with this motion by mailing and emailing the motion on June 10, 2026. Plaintiff timely filed an opposition which the Court has considered.

B.   Plaintiff’s request for relief

      Plaintiff requests relief from Defendant’s “Motion for Summary Judgment on the Pleadings” and asks that the Court deny the motion based on mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect citing Code of Civil Procedure § 473(b). There are no facts to support the request. As noted, the Court has considered the opposition.   

      The principle of extrinsic fraud does not apply and cannot defeat a motion for judgment on the pleadings which addresses the sufficiency of pleading.  The principle gives the Court authority to interfere on grounds of equity with a final judgment obtained “obtained or entered through fraud, mistake, or accident, or where the defendant in the action, having a valid legal defense on the merits, was prevented in any manner from maintaining it by fraud, mistake, or accident, and there had been no negligence, laches, or other fault on his part, or on the part of his agents. (Olivera v. Grace (1942) 19 Cal.2d 570, 575.)  There is no final judgment in this matter from which Plaintiff can seek such relief.

      If Plaintiff seeks relief from the order granting summary judgment in Defendant’s favor in the lead matter, the request is improperly raised in opposition to Defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleadings which concerns a different pleading. Defendant points out the order was issued on April 12, 2023 by the Hon. Anne Richardson. (D’s RJN Ex 2.) The Court grants Defendant’s request for judicial notice of Court records.

       This Court denied Plaintiff’s motion to vacate that judgment in 2025, which was affirmed by the Court of Appeal. (Opinion filed 7/31/26 in BC719459.) The lead matter is finally resolved. There is no evidence that Plaintiff was deprived of her rights to be heard in the lead matter. Nor has Plaintiff persuasively argued that this Court should stay enforcement of the order granting summary judgment.

      C.   Res Judicata

      In its primary aspect, res judicata, or “claim preclusion” prevents relitigation of the same cause of action in a second suit between the same parties or their privities where the claim was finally litigated. (DKN Holdings LLC v. Faerber (2015) 61 Cal.4th 813, 824.) The second aspect of res judicata known as collateral estoppel, or issue preclusion, prohibits the relitigation of issues decided in a prior proceeding. Issue preclusion applies "(1) after final adjudication (2) of an identical issue (3) actually litigated and necessarily decided in the first suit and (4) asserted against one who was a party in the first suit or one in privity with that party." (DKN Holdings LLC v. Faerber (2015) 61 Cal.4th 813, 825.) Collateral estoppel applies even if the second lawsuit alleges different causes of action. (Id.)

      Both the lead matter and this case assert claims arising from the same set of facts. The second amended complaint in the lead matter alleges that Plaintiff was subjected to a pattern of harassment because she filed a workers’ compensation claim in January 10, 2017 after suffering a work-related injury. (D’s RJN, Ex. 7, ¶¶ 8-11.) Thereafter, Plaintiff alleged Defendant terminated her employment on February 1, 2017. (Id., ¶ 14.) The second amended complaint in the lead matter alleged discrimination, retaliation, and related FEHA claims arising from these facts.

      In this action, the complaint alleges the same work-related injury that occurred on January 9, 2017, for which Plaintiff filed a workers’ compensation claim. (Complaint, ¶ 11.)  Plaintiff alleges that on February 1, 2017, she was abruptly terminated without explanation. (Id.) ¶¶ 13-14.) Plaintiff alleges eleven causes of action for retaliation, discrimination, and related FEHA claims, a tort claim for wrongful termination in violation of public policy, defamation and intentional infliction of emotional distress. The FEHA claims arising from the 2017 termination were finally litigated in the first action when the trial Court granted summary judgment in Defendant’s favor and entered judgment in favor of Defendant. (D’s RJN Ex. 8-10.)  

      Plaintiff is barred from re-litigating the FEHA claims arising from her termination.

      D.   The new claims for defamation and intentional infliction of emotional distress

      The defamation claim alleges that following Plaintiff’s termination, Defendant knowingly and falsely alleged that Plaintiff was terminated for poor job performance although Plaintiff did not have any history of disciplinary action or performance deficiencies. (Complaint, ¶ 45.) Plaintiff alleges she alleged the defamation claim in the lead matter. (Complaint, ¶ 46.)

      The claims for wrongful termination in violation of public policy and intentional infliction of emotional distress are also based on her February 1, 2017 termination. (Complaint, ¶ 116.) All three claims are equally barred by res judicata. Prior litigation between the same parties is a bar not only to issues which were actually raised but to causes of action which could have been litigated.” (Goldberg v. Frye (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 1258, 1264.)

IV.  CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleadings is GRANTED.

       

 


Case Number: 26STCV01098    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 414

26STCV01098 Rotibi v. M&Y Personal Injury Lawyers, APLC, et al.

Tuesday, September 15, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER TAKING OFF CALENDAR, DEFENDANTS’ ANTI-SLAPP MOTION TO STRIKE

PLAINTIFF'S COMPLAINT OR PORTION THEREOF PURSUANT TO CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE §425.16 (2488)// ORDER DISMISSING ACTION AGAINST DEFENDANTS M&Y PERSONAL INJURY LAWYERS, APLC, DANIEL REEVES AND KELSEY GITILIN (7463).

 

      This action arises from alleged legal malpractice. On July 24, 2026, the Court granted the motion of M&Y Personal Injury Lawyers, APLC, Daniel Reeeves and Kelsey Gitlin [Collective “the M&Y Defendants”] to declare Plaintiff a vexatious litigant pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 391.1 and 391.3. The Court further determined that Plaintiff did not have a reasonable probability of prevailing against the moving Defendants and ordered Plaintiff to furnish security in the amount of $15,000 within 20 days, or by August 13, 2026. (M.O. 7/24/26.)

      The Court's July 24, 2026 order further recognized that Code of Civil Procedure section 391.6 stays the litigation upon the filing of a motion pursuant to section 391.1 and, where the motion is granted, continues the stay until the required security has been furnished and the moving defendant has been given written notice thereof. Accordingly, the Court determined that it would not proceed with further litigation of the action, including the Special Motion to Strike pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 filed by State Farm Automobile Insurance Complany and Christopher Babadjanian [Collectively “the State Farm Defendants”], until the Court determined whether Plaintiff had complied with the order requiring security. (M.O. 7/24/26.)

      Plaintiff has not complied. The Court's record does not reflect that Plaintiff furnished the required $15,000 security by August 13, 2026, or at any time thereafter. Code of Civil Procedure section 391.4 provides that “[w]hen security that has been ordered furnished is not furnished as ordered, the litigation shall be dismissed as to the defendant for whose benefit it was ordered furnished.” The security ordered on July 24, 2026 was ordered for the benefit of the M&Y Defendants who brought the motion pursuant to section 391.1. Because Plaintiff failed to furnish that security as ordered, dismissal as to those Defendants is required.

      Accordingly, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 391.4, this action is DISMISSED as to the M&Y Defendants who moved for security and for whose benefit the $15,000 security was ordered.

      The dismissal of those Defendants does not, however, presently dispose of the action against the State Farm Defendants. Those Defendants did not move for the security previously ordered, and the $15,000 security was not ordered for their benefit. Section 391.4 therefore does not presently authorize dismissal as to them.

      Nor does the Court proceed with the State Farm Defendants' Special Motion to Strike. Code of Civil Procedure section 391.6 provides that when a motion pursuant to section 391.1 is filed before trial, “the litigation is stayed” and, if the motion is granted, the stay continues until 10 days after the required security has been furnished and the moving defendant given written notice thereof. In Hanna v. Little League Baseball, Inc. (2020) 53 Cal.App.5th 871, the Court of Appeal construed this language to mean that “the entirety of the litigation is stayed” upon the filing of a motion under section 391.1 and that the stay remains effective until the conditions specified in section 391.6 have occurred. (Id. at pp. 879-880.)

      Thus, although the security was ordered for the benefit of only the M&Y  Defendants, the statutory stay was not limited to litigation involving those Defendants. It applied to the litigation as a whole. Plaintiff has never furnished the security. The statutory condition for termination of the stay following the granting of the motion therefore has not occurred. The Court declines to treat the dismissal of the M&Y Defendants under section 391.4 as implicitly terminating a stay that section 391.6 expressly applies to the entirety of the litigation and conditions upon the furnishing of the required security.

      Accordingly, the stay under Code of Civil Procedure section 391.6 remains in effect as to further litigation against the State Farm Defendants pending further order of the Court. The Court therefore does not reach the merits of the remaining Defendants' Special Motion to Strike pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16, and that motion is taken OFF CALENDAR without prejudice.

      Nothing in this Order precludes the two remaining Defendants from seeking appropriate relief pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 391.1 through 391.4, including an order requiring Plaintiff to furnish security for their benefit. Any such motion shall be based upon the statutory requirements applicable to those Defendants, including whether there is a reasonable probability that Plaintiff will prevail in the litigation against the moving Defendants. The Court expresses no opinion at this time as to the merits of any such motion. The Clerk shall give notice.
