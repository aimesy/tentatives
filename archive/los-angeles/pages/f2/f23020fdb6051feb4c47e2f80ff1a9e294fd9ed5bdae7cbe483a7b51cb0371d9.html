APPLICATIONS TO APPEAR PRO HAC VICE

(CRC, rule 9.40)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

Plaintiffs Alliant Insurance Services, Inc. and Alliant Insurance Services, Inc.’s unopposed Application of Neda M. Manouchehri to Appear Pro Hac Vice is GRANTED.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice.

 

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

            On June 30, 2026, Plaintiffs Alliant Insurance Services, Inc. and Alliant Insurance Services, Inc. filed the instant Application to Appear Pro Hac Vice (the “Application”), which requests the Court permit Neda M. Manouchehri (“Applicant”) to appear as counsel pro hac vice in the instant matter.

 

To date, no Opposition has been filed regarding the Application.

 

II.              Procedural Issues

 

As an initial matter, the Court notes the Application was untimely filed and served under Code of Civil Procedure section 1005. Indeed, the Application was filed and served 16 court days prior to the instant hearing but, given that the Application was electronically served, needed to be served 16 court days and two calendar days prior to the instant hearing. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1005.)

 

The Court may not rule on the Application absent proper service and notice of hearing.

 

III.            Legal Standard

 

For out-of-state counsel to appear before a California court as counsel pro hac vice, out-of-state counsel must be (1) a member in good standing of another state bar, (2) eligible to practice in any United State court or the highest court in another jurisdiction, and (3) associated with an active member of the California Bar as attorney of record. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).)

 

Further, the application must state:¿ 

¿ 

(1) The applicant's residence and office address;¿ ¿ 

(2) The courts to which the applicant has been admitted to practice and the dates of admission;¿ 

(3) That the applicant is a licensee in good standing in those courts;¿ 

(4) That the applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court;¿ 

(5) The title of each court and cause in which the applicant has filed an application to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this state in the preceding two years, the date of each application, and whether or not it was granted; and¿ 

(6) The name, address, and telephone number of the active licensee of the State Bar of California who is attorney of record.¿ 

¿ 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(d).)¿ 

 

Out-of-state counsel must file a verified application and a proof of service, which provides that a copy of the application and a notice of hearing have been served on all parties and on the California Bar at its San Francisco office. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(c)(1).)

 

No person is eligible to appear as counsel pro hac vice if the person is: (1) A resident of the State of California; (2) Regularly employed in the State of California; or (3) Regularly engaged in substantial business, professional, or other activities in the State of California. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).)

 

Out-of-state counsel is also subject to the California Bar’s Rules of Professional Conduct. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(f).)

 

Finally, any application fee set by the State Bar of California must be paid by counsel pro hac vice. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(e).)

 

IV.            Discussion

 

Plaintiffs submit a verified Application for the Applicant to appear pro hac vice.

 

            Here, the verified Application provides the following information: (1) the Applicant’s residential address is 2020 N. Lincoln Park W, 3A, Chicago, IL 60614 and office address is Tressler LLP located at 233 S. Wacker Drive, 61st Floor, Chicago, IL 60606; (2) the Applicant was admitted to the State Bar of Illinois (Bar No. 6351208) on May 7, 2025, and is admitted to all courts of State of Illinois; (3) the Applicant is in good standing in each of the courts the Applicant has been admitted; (4) the Applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court; (5) the Applicant has not applied to appear pro hac vice in California in the preceding two years; and (6) Jihoon Kim (State Bar No. 289780) is the attorney of record with the following address: Tressler LLP 4 Park Plaza, Suite 1200 Irvine, CA 92614. (Verified App., Manoucheri Decl., Kim Decl., Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40.)

 

            Further, the Application and accompanying declarations provide that the Applicant paid the pro hac vice fee and served the Application to the State Bar of California. (Verified App., Kim Decl., Proof of Service; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40.)

 

Finally, Plaintiffs file proof of service demonstrating the Application and notice of hearing were served on the parties to the action. (See Verified App., Kim Decl., Proof of Service; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(c)(1).) Defendants have not opposed.

 

Thus, the unopposed Application is GRANTED.  

 

V.              Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiffs Alliant Insurance Services, Inc. and Alliant Insurance Services, Inc.’s unopposed Application of Neda M. Manouchehri to Appear Pro Hac Vice is GRANTED.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice.

On April 21, 2025, Torpedo World filed a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, alleging causes of action for Breach of Contract, Negligence, and Premises Liability.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

            On January 28, 2026, Plaintiff Chenglu Liu (“Plaintiff”) filed a Verified Complaint for Partition of Real Property against Defendants Na Xiao (“Xiao”), Honghua Shang (“Shang”), Puchun Cai (“Cai”) and Does 1-10. (Verified Compl., p. 1.)

 

            On February 9, 2026, the Court found the instant case and Case No. 26STCV02922 to be related. (2/9/26 Order.) The Court ordered the instant case to be the lead case. (2/9/26 Order.)

 

            On March 19, 2026, Xiao, proceeding pro se, filed an Answer.

 

            On April 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Recording of Pendency of Action Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 405.20 (Lis Pendens).

 

            On April 8, 2026, default was entered against Shang and Cai.

 

            On April 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Default Interlocutory Judgment of Partition and Appointment of Referee (the “Motion”).

 

            On May 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed a declaration made by Xiao attesting to Xiao’s support and consent of the Motion.

 

            On May 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Amended Proposed Judgment.

 

            On July 9, 2026, the Court continued the hearing on the Motion and ordered Plaintiff give notice. (7/9/26 Order.)

 

            On July 14, 2026, Moving party gave notice of the continued hearing.

 

To date, no Opposition to the Motion has been filed.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

“ ‘[P]artition’ is ‘the procedure for segregating and terminating common interests in the same parcel of property.’ ” (14859 Moorpark Homeowner’s Association v. VRT Corp. (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 1396, 1404-1405.)  It is a “ ‘ “remedy much favored by the law. The original purpose of partition was to permit cotenants to avoid the inconvenience and dissension arising from sharing joint possession of land. An additional reason to favor partition is the policy of facilitating transmission of title, thereby avoiding unreasonable restraints on the use and enjoyment of property.” ’ ”  (Cummings v. Dessel (2017) 13 Cal.App.5th 589, 596.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 872.720, subdivision (a) provides: “If the court finds that the plaintiff is entitled to partition, it shall make an interlocutory judgment that determines the interests of the parties in the property and orders the partition of the property and, unless it is to be later determined, the manner of partition.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.720, subd. (a).)  

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (a) provides: “A partition action may be commenced and maintained by any of the following persons: (1) A coowner of personal property. (2) An owner of an estate of inheritance, an estate for life, or an estate for years in real property where such property or estate therein is owned by several persons concurrently or in successive estates.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.210, subd. (a).) Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b) states that “an action between spouses or putative spouses for partition of their community or quasi-community property or their quasi-marital interest in property may not be commenced or maintained under this title.”

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 872.230 sets forth the requirements for a complaint seeking partition.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 872.250 provides that “[i]mmediately upon filing the complaint, the plaintiff shall record a notice of the pendency of the action in the office of the county recorder of each county in which any real property described in the complaint is located.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.250, subd. (a).) “From the time of filing the notice for record, all persons shall be deemed to have notice of the pendency of the action as to the property described in the notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.250, subd. (d).)

 

III.            Discussion

 

The Motion, and underlying action, seeks a partition of a residential duplex located at 952 E 9th St, Pomona, CA 91766, Assessor’s Parcel Number 8333-004-010 (the “Property”). (Motion, p. 5; Verified Compl., p. 1.) Plaintiff contends that Plaintiff owns a 33.33 percent interest in the Property and Defendants collectively hold 66.67 percent interest in the Property. (Motion, p. 5.)

 

A.    Interlocutory Judgment of Partition

 

The Motion is granted. The legal requirements for an interlocutory judgment of partition have been met.  

 

The law is that “an interlocutory judgment in a partition action is to include two elements: a determination of the parties’ interests in the property and an order granting the partition.”  (Summers v. Superior Court (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 138, 143, as modified June 27, 2018].)

 

First, where the pleadings do not raise any question regarding the ownership of the property, there is no factual issue for the Court to determine.  (See, e.g., Brown v. Brown (1950) 98 Cal.App.2d 142, 144-145 [finding in partition action that trial court erred in ruling on parties’ respective interests in real property because the pleadings did not raise the issue].)

 

Here, ownership of the Property is not at issue since Shang and Cai are default, Xiao filed an Answer agreeing to the allegations in the Verified Complaint, Xiao filed a declaration in support of the Motion, and the title history shows as follows: initially, the Property’s title constituted an undivided 2/3 interest between Liu and Xiaoyuan Yang as community property between husband and wife, and an undivided 1/3 interest between Shang and Cai as community property between husband and wife, as tenants in common; thereafter, a quitclaim deed by Liu granted Xiao a 1/3 interest in the Property. (Motion, Thomas Decl., ¶ 2-5, Exhs. 1-4.)  

 

Plaintiff consents to the entry of an interlocutory judgment of partition granting ownership of the Property as follows: 33.33 percent to Plaintiff and 66.67 percent to Defendants allocated as follows: 33.33 percent to Xiao and 16.67 percent to Shang and 16.67 percent to Cai. (Motion, p. 6; Summers, supra, 24 Cal.App.5th at p. 143.)

 

Second, Plaintiff, who is a tenant in common, is entitled to partition. “ ‘A co-owner of property has an absolute right to partition unless barred by a valid waiver.’ ”  (Orien v. Lutz (2017) 16 Cal.App.5th 957, 962, citing Code Civ. Proc., § 872.710, subd. (b).)  As such, “if the party seeking partition is shown to be a tenant in common, and as such entitled to the possession of the land sought to be partitioned, the right is absolute.”  (Bacon v. Wahrhaftig (1950) 97 Cal.App.2d 599, 603.) Indeed, “ ‘the right to partition is absolute, and cannot be denied, “either because of any supposed difficulty, nor on the suggestion that the interest of the cotenants will be promoted by refusing the application or temporarily postponing action.” ’ ”  (Priddel v. Shankie (1945) 69 Cal.App.2d 319, 325.)

 

Here, the Verified Complaint and the exhibits attached to the Motion demonstrate Plaintiff and Defendants are tenants in common. (See Motion, Thomas Decl., Exhs. 1-4; Verified Compl., ¶ 9, Exh. 1.) The Verified Complaint alleges that “Plaintiff has never waived the right to partition in writing or in any other manner.” (Verified Compl., ¶ 25.) Plaintiff has not waived the right to partition.  (Summers, supra, 24 Cal.App.5th at p. 143.)

 

“[T]he manner of partition—i.e., a physical division or sale of the property—is to be decided when or after the parties’ ownership interests are determined, but not before.”  (Id.; see Code Civ. Proc., § 872.720, subd. (a) [providing that the “manner of partition” can be decided in the judgment “unless it is to be later determined”].) 

 

The Court need not determine the manner of partition at this time given the appointment of a referee discussed below.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds the elements for an interlocutory judgment have been met. As such, the Court grants the motion for default interlocutory judgment of partition of the Property.

 

B.    Appointment of Partition Referee

 

The law in a partition action is that: “The court shall appoint a referee to divide or sell the property as ordered by the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 873.010, subd. (a).) In appointing a referee: “The court may: . . . Instruct the referee [and] [f]ix the reasonable compensation for the services of the referee and provide for payment of the referee’s reasonable expenses.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 873.010, subds. (b)(2)-(b)(3).) “The referee or any party may, on noticed motion, petition the court for instructions concerning the referee’s duties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 873.070.) Additionally, the referee has a broad range of authority over the Property as provided in Code of Civil Procedure section 873.060, which provides that “[t]he referee may perform any acts necessary to exercise the authority conferred by this title or by order of the court.” 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 873.050 provides, “[n]one of the following persons shall be appointed a referee under this title: (a) A clerk or deputy clerk of the court. (b) A former or present partner or employee of the judge. (c) A relative within the third degree of the judge or the judge’s spouse or the spouse of such a relative. (d) An owner of any interest in the property that is the subject of the action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 873.050.)

 

Plaintiff requests that this Court appoint a referee, Matthew L. Taylor (“Taylor”). (Motion, pp. 10-12.) Plaintiff presents evidence that Plaintiff has located and confirmed the availability of Taylor to act as the referee in this case. (Motion, Taylor Decl.) “Taylor has been an attorney since 1995 with a practice in real estate and receiverships, with a particular focus on partition referee work.” (Motion, pp. 10-11, Taylor Decl. ¶¶ 2-5.) The Motion provides Taylor’s resume, which indicates Taylor’s appointment as a receiver in multiple other cases and appointment as a partition referee in multiple cases. (Motion, pp. 10-11, ¶ 3, Exh. 13.) In a declaration accompanying the Motion, Taylor indicates that he is ready, willing and able to accept Plaintiffs’ nomination as referee. (Motion, Taylor Decl., ¶ 8.) Taylor indicates that he has no personal or professional affiliation with Plaintiff or Plaintiff’s attorney of record. (Motion, Taylor Decl., ¶¶ 10-11.) 

 

Accordingly, the Court finds Taylor is well-qualified to handle the referee appointment in this case.

II.             
Legal
Standard

 

“ ‘[P]artition’ is ‘the procedure for
segregating and terminating common interests in the same parcel of property.’ ”
(14859 Moorpark Homeowner’s Association v. VRT Corp. (1998) 63
Cal.App.4th 1396, 1404-1405.)  It is a “
‘ “remedy much favored by the law. The original purpose of partition was to
permit cotenants to avoid the inconvenience and dissension arising from sharing
joint possession of land. An additional reason to favor partition is the policy
of facilitating transmission of title, thereby avoiding unreasonable restraints
on the use and enjoyment of property.” ’ ” 
(Cummings v. Dessel (2017) 13 Cal.App.5th 589, 596.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section
872.720, subdivision (a) provides: “If the court finds that the plaintiff is
entitled to partition, it shall make an interlocutory judgment that determines
the interests of the parties in the property and orders the partition of the
property and, unless it is to be later determined, the manner of partition.” (Code
Civ. Proc., § 872.720, subd. (a).)  

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210,
subdivision (a) provides: “A partition action may be commenced and maintained
by any of the following persons: (1) A coowner of personal property. (2) An
owner of an estate of inheritance, an estate for life, or an estate for years
in real property where such property or estate therein is owned by several
persons concurrently or in successive estates.” 
(Code Civ. Proc., § 872.210, subd. (a).) Code of Civil Procedure section
872.210, subdivision (b) states that “an action between spouses or putative
spouses for partition of their community or quasi-community property or their
quasi-marital interest in property may not be commenced or maintained under
this title.”

 

            Code of
Civil Procedure section 872.230 sets forth the requirements for a complaint
seeking partition.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 872.250
provides that “[i]mmediately upon filing the complaint, the plaintiff shall
record a notice of the pendency of the action in the office of the county
recorder of each county in which any real property described in the complaint
is located.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.250, subd. (a).) “From the time of filing
the notice for record, all persons shall be deemed to have notice of the
pendency of the action as to the property described in the notice.” (Code Civ.
Proc., § 872.250, subd. (d).)

Cal.App.5th 138, 143, as modified June 27, 2018].)

 

First, where the pleadings do not

issue for the Court to determine.  (See,

957, 962, citing Code Civ. Proc., § 872.710, subd. (b).)  As such, “if the party seeking partition is
shown to be a tenant in common, and as such entitled to the possession of the
land sought to be partitioned, the right is absolute.”  (Bacon v. Wahrhaftig (1950) 97
Cal.App.2d 599, 603.) Indeed, “ ‘the right to partition is absolute, and cannot
be denied, “either because of any supposed difficulty, nor on the suggestion
that the interest of the cotenants will be promoted by refusing the application
or temporarily postponing action.” ’ ”  (Priddel

Here, the Verified Complaint and the

II.              Legal Standard

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 284 states that “the attorney in an action…may be changed at any time before or after judgment or final determination, as follows: (1) Upon the consent of both client and attorney…; (2) Upon the order of the court, upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 284; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)  “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133); see Lempert v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1173.)

 

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Counsel Forms MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion), MC-052 (Declaration), and MC-053 (Proposed Order). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) 

 

In addition, California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 subsection (d) requires that the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case by personal service, electronic service, or mail. If the notice is served by electronic service, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating that the electronic service address is the client’s current electronic service address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(2).) As used in this rule, “current” means:

 

“[T]he address was confirmed within 30 days before the filing of the motion to be relieved.

Merely demonstrating that the notice was sent to the client’s last known address and was not returned or no electronic delivery failure message was received is not, by itself, sufficient to demonstrate that the address is current.”

 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d).)

 

III.            Discussion

 

On June 10, 2026, Counsel moved the Court to be relieved as attorney of record for Plaintiff. (MC-051.) Counsel filed MC-051, MC-052, and MC-053 Forms. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) 

 

Counsel seeks to be relieved on the following grounds:

 

There has been an irreparable breakdown of the working relationship between counsel and the client, due to client’s lack of cooperation and failure to communicate. This conflict has recently arisen making it unreasonably difficult to continue representing the client. The details of the disagreement and conflict cannot be disclosed as it involves privileged communications. Little, if any, prejudice would befall the Plaintiff, Nancy Shavonna York, if an Order is issued permitting my firm to withdraw as attorneys of record in this case. All rights have been preserved for the Plaintiff following the filing of the comprehensive Complaint.

 

(MC-052, ¶ 2.)

 

The Court is satisfied with Counsel’s reasons for seeking to be relieved. (Cal. Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16.) The Court also finds the Motion satisfactorily complies with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.

 

However, MC-053 needs to be resubmitted with accurate dates.

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s Motion is GRANTED. However, the corrected MC-053 form entitled “Order Granting Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel-Civil" must be resubmitted with the Court within the next 5 days of this ruling providing all the accurate dates and future dates.

 

The Court’s Ruling and Attorney’s relief as Counsel of record for client is not effective until Proof of Service of the Order signed by the Court upon the client is served and filed in this action. Until then, counsel continues to be counsel of record. (Cal. Rules of Court 3.1362(e).)
