MOTION TO CONSOLIDATE

 

Motion filed on June 25, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority

RESPONDING PARTIES: Plaintiffs Oswaldo Sorto and Pamela Ann Smith

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority moves to consolidate Oswaldo Sorto v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, et al., Case No. 22STCV38869, and Pamela Ann Smith v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, Case No. 23STCV10475, for all purposes, with Case No. 22STCV38869 designated as the lead case.

RULING: Granted. The actions are consolidated for all purposes. Case No. 22STCV38869 is designated as the lead case.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On December 14, 2022, Plaintiff Oswaldo Sorto (Sorto) filed Case No. 22STCV38869 (Sorto Action) against Defendants Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (LACMTA), Doe Driver, and Does 1 through 20. On April 7, 2023, Sorto filed the operative First Amended Complaint alleging causes of action for negligence and statutory liability.

 

On May 10, 2023, Plaintiff Pamela Ann Smith (Smith) filed Case No. 23STCV10475 (Smith Action) against LACMTA, erroneously sued as Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transit Authority, and Does 1 through 50.

 

On January 14, 2026, the parties filed a stipulation requesting that the Smith Action be related to and consolidated with the Sorto Action for all purposes and assigned to Department F-46.

 

On February 17, 2026, this Court denied the request to relate the actions, finding that the parties’ stipulation did not establish materially changed circumstances from the prior determination that the actions were not related. This Court also denied consolidation based on the record and procedural posture then presented, without prejudice to a renewed noticed motion after the actions were related and assigned to the same department.

 

On June 25, 2026, Department 534 found the actions related, designated the Sorto Action as the lead case, and reassigned the Smith Action to Department F-46. The same day, LACMTA filed this motion. No opposition was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

“When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the court … it may order all the actions consolidated and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (a).) The purpose of consolidation is to enhance trial court efficiency by avoiding unnecessary duplication of evidence and the danger of inconsistent adjudications. (Todd-Stenberg v. Dalkon Shield Claimants Trust (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 976, 978-979.)

 

“There are two types of consolidation: a complete consolidation resulting in a single action, and a consolidation of separate actions for trial. Under the former procedure, which may be utilized where the parties are identical and the causes could have been joined, the pleadings are regarded as merged, one set of findings is made, and one judgment is rendered. In a consolidation for trial, the pleadings, verdicts, findings and judgments are kept separate; the actions are simply tried together for the sake of convenience and judicial economy.” (Sanchez v. Superior Court (1988) 203 Cal.App.3d 1391, 1396.) “Consolidation under Code of Civil Procedure section 1048 is permissive, and it is for the trial court to determine whether the consolidation is for all purposes or for trial only.” (Hamilton v. Asbestos Corp., Ltd. (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1127, 1149.)

 

These actions arise from a motor vehicle collision. Plaintiff Sorto alleges he was injured when a bus operated by Defendant LACMTA failed to stop and collided with his vehicle while he was stopped on westbound Adams Boulevard. (FAC ¶¶ 14-16.) Plaintiff Smith alleges she was injured by the negligent operation of a motor vehicle at Adams Boulevard and Grand Avenue. (Compl., MV-1, MV-2.) In support of the motion, LACMTA’s counsel, Albert De Blanc, Jr., declares that both actions arise from the same March 29, 2022 chain-reaction collision on westbound Adams Boulevard. (De Blanc Decl. ¶ 2.) He declares that an assault on the LACMTA bus operator caused the bus to contact the rear of Smith’s vehicle, which then contacted another vehicle that ultimately contacted the rear of Sorto’s vehicle. (De Blanc Decl. ¶ 3.) De Blanc further declares that consolidation will avoid duplicative discovery, motion practice, expert testimony, and trial preparation. (De Blanc Decl. ¶¶ 10-11.)

 

The parties stipulated that the actions arise from the same occurrence, involve the same or similar claims and substantially identical questions of law or fact, and are likely to require substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard separately. (De Blanc Decl. ¶ 7, Ex. A.) Although Sorto and Smith are different plaintiffs, all parties expressly agreed to consolidation for all purposes. (Ibid.)

 

In light of the moving papers, this Court finds that the Sorto Action and Smith Action involve common questions of law and fact. No opposition has been filed disputing LACMTA’s showing that the actions will involve overlapping evidence, discovery, expert testimony, and trial preparation.

 

Accordingly, this Court grants LACMTA’s request for complete consolidation of the two actions.

 

CONCLUSION

 

LACMTA’s Motion to Consolidate is GRANTED. Case Nos. 22STCV38869 and 23STCV10475 are consolidated for all purposes. Case No. 22STCV38869 is designated as the lead case. This order shall be filed in each action. Thereafter, all subsequent documents shall be filed only in the lead case. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(c).)

MOTION FOR ATTORNEYS’ FEES

 

Motion filed on June 25, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Anna L. Brief-Mueller

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant General Motors LLC

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Plaintiff requests $10,865.50 in attorney fees under Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d), consisting of $7,865.50 in incurred fees and $3,000 in anticipated fees for reviewing Defendant’s opposition, preparing a reply, and attending the hearing.

RULING: Granted in part. The Court awards $7,865.50 in attorney fees.

 

BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION

 

On May 19, 2025, Plaintiff Anna L. Brief-Mueller (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against General Motors LLC (Defendant) concerning a 2024 Chevrolet Silverado 1500. (Compl. ¶¶ 6, 11-12.) Plaintiff alleged five causes of action under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act for violations of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivisions (d), (b), and (a)(3), breach of express written warranty, and breach of the implied warranty of merchantability. (Compl., pp. 4-8.) Defendant filed its Answer on June 23, 2025.

 

The parties settled the action for $140,000, consisting of a repurchase of the vehicle and $56,692.19 in civil penalties, and agreed that Plaintiff could seek attorney fees under Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d). (Fennell Decl. ¶¶ 14-16.) On May 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Memorandum of Costs seeking $677.09 in costs. Plaintiff filed this motion seeking attorney fees on June 25, 2026.

 

On September 11, 2026, Defendant filed a Notice of Non-Opposition. Defendant does not oppose the $7,865.50 in fees actually incurred, but requests that the additional $3,000 in anticipated fees be disallowed because Defendant filed no substantive opposition. (Notice of Non-Opposition, pp. 1-2.)

 

Under Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d), “[i]f the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.” The Court must determine whether both the time expended and the amount charged for that time are reasonable. (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 817-818.) The lodestar method applies to attorney fee awards under section 1794, subdivision (d). (Id. at pp. 819-820.)

 

Here, the billing records reflect hourly rates of $510 and $525 for Joshua Fennell, $425 and $440 for Eesha Dalia, $440 for Bobby Walker, and $215 for paralegal work. (Fennell Decl. ¶¶ 17-19, 41, Ex. 19.) Fennell was admitted to practice in 2018 and has litigated numerous Song-Beverly matters both at Consumer Law Experts and at a prior lemon-law firm. (Fennell Decl. ¶ 17.) Dalia was admitted in 2019 and has experience in civil, employment, and transactional litigation. (Fennell Decl. ¶ 18.) Walker was admitted in 2018 and has litigated Song-Beverly matters on both the manufacturer and consumer sides. (Fennell Decl. ¶ 19.) Plaintiff also submits recent fee orders approving Fennell’s rates of $510 and $525 per hour and CLE associate rates of $440 per hour. (Fennell Decl. ¶¶ 34-36.) Defendant does not challenge the rates. The Court finds the rates reasonable.

 

Plaintiff seeks compensation for 17.7 hours of attorney and paralegal time actually expended. (Fennell Decl. ¶¶ 37, 39, Ex. 19.) The billing records reflect 3.3 hours by Fennell at $510 per hour and 4.3 hours at $525 per hour, 6.1 hours by Dalia at $425 per hour and 2.0 hours at $440 per hour, 0.1 hour by Walker at $440 per hour, and 1.9 hours of paralegal time at $215 per hour, for a total of 17.7 hours and a lodestar of $7,865.50. (Fennell Decl., Ex. 19.) The billing records reflect work on the complaint and initial disclosures, mediation and settlement, the memorandum of costs, and this fee motion. (Fennell Decl. ¶¶ 13, 37, Ex. 19.) Counsel states the billing records were reviewed and audited for unnecessary, duplicative, administrative, or excessive entries, which were removed or reduced. (Fennell Decl. ¶ 37.) Defendant does not challenge the hours incurred. The Court finds the 17.7 hours reasonable.

 

Plaintiff also requests $3,000 in anticipated fees for reviewing Defendant’s opposition, preparing a reply, and attending the hearing. (Fennell Decl. ¶¶ 38-39.) Defendant filed a Notice of Non-Opposition rather than a substantive opposition. No timely reply was filed, and Plaintiff submitted no supplemental declaration identifying any additional time actually incurred after the motion was filed. The record therefore does not establish that the anticipated $3,000 was actually expended or reasonably incurred. The Court declines to award that additional amount.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Plaintiff’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees is GRANTED IN PART. The Court awards Plaintiff $7,865.50 in attorney fees.
