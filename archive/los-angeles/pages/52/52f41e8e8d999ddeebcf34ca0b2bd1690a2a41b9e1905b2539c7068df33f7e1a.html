Discussion

            The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

Defendants Candy Boyd, Reginald Boyd and Boyd Funeral Home demur to the Second Amended Complaint as follows:

1.         Second Cause of Action (Fraud).

            Defendants argue that the 2AC fails to plead fraud with the requisite specificity and is uncertain.

            A demurrer for uncertainty is properly sustained where the complaint is so vague or uncertain that the defendant cannot reasonably respond, i.e., when the defendant cannot determine what issues must be admitted or denied, or what counts are directed against the defendant. (Khoury v. Maly’s of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616; Weil & Brown, Cal. Practice Guide: Civil Procedure Before Trial, supra, ¶ 7:85.)  Demurrers for uncertainty are disfavored and strictly construed “because ambiguities can reasonably be clarified under modern rules of discovery.” (Lickiss v. Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 1125, 1135.)

“To establish a claim for deceit based on intentional misrepresentation, the plaintiff must prove seven essential elements: (1) the defendant represented to the plaintiff that an important fact was true; (2) that representation was false; (3) the defendant knew that the representation was false when the defendant made it, or the defendant made the representation recklessly and without regard for its truth; (4) the defendant intended that the plaintiff rely on the representation; (5) the plaintiff reasonably relied on the representation; (6) the plaintiff was harmed; and (7) the plaintiff’s reliance on the defendant’s representation was a substantial factor in causing that harm to the plaintiff. (Citations omitted.)” (Manderville v. PCG&S Group, Inc. (2007) 146 Cal.App.4th 1486, 1498 [italics omitted].)

It is true that in order for a defendant to be liable for fraud, he or she must intend that a particular representation (or concealment) be relied upon by a specific person or persons. (5 Witkin, Summary of Cal. Law, supra, Torts, § 707, at p. 807.) However, it is also established that a defendant cannot escape liability if he or she makes a representation to one person while intending or having reason to expect that it will be repeated to and acted upon by the plaintiff (or someone in the class of persons of which plaintiff is a member). (Citations omitted.)

(Shapiro v. Sutherland (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 1534, 1548.)

            The 2AC alleges at ¶¶ 15 – 18 as follows: 

15. Before Plaintiffs committed to paying for Defendants’ services -- and thereafter as well - Defendants engaged in fraudulent representations and concealments in this regard including each and all of the following:

 

A. On or about the afternoon of January 15, 2021, at the Boyd Funeral Home, Defendant Candy Boyd represented to Plaintiff Shawn Seymore and Sherry Seymore (“Shawn and Sherry”) that Defendants would carefully handle the remains of Plaintiffs’ mother and would place her ashes into thirty pendants so that her children and other family members could keep her close to themselves. In fact, Ms. Boyd had no intention -- and knew that the other Defendants had no intention -- of placing Plaintiffs’ mother’s ashes into the pendants.

 

B. In making the foregoing representations, Ms. Boyd knew that Shawn and Sherry would communicate her words to the other bereaved members of the family and intended to induce their reliance on such representations. Ms. Boyd also knew the representations to be false. She also concealed from Shawn and Sherry that Defendants practice was to charge for services with respect to the Pendants that they had no intention to deliver.

 

C. In consultation with other family members, and in reliance upon Ms. Boyd’s concealments and in their ignorance of the facts that Ms. Boyd concealed, the late Ms. Seymore’s children paid Defendants’ asking price for their funeral services -- something they would not have done had they been told the truth by Ms. Boyd.

 

D. In engaging in the foregoing fraudulent acts, Ms. Boyd was acting on behalf of the other Defendants as part of both (a) Defendants’ particular plan and with the express authorization to defraud Plaintiffs’ family and (b) Defendants’ general scheme to defraud the public.

 

E. In the alternative, to the extent that the Defendants did not put Decedent’s ashes into the pendants due to Mr. Boyd’s physical capacity (as Defendants would later contend), Defendant Candy Boyd deliberately defrauded Plaintiffs in the afternoon of January 15, 2021 by representing to Shawn and Sherry that Defendants were capable of delivering services that Defendant Candy Boyd knew they could not deliver.

 

16. The pendants were all discovered to be empty in the latter half of April of 2021, and Ms. Seymore’s ashes which were withdrawn from her other cremated remains are missing and unaccounted for. When informed of this mis-, mal- and non-feasance on the part of the Boyds, the Boyds offered the explanation that Mr. Boyd has dementia and, on account of his ailment or disability, the remains of the Seymores’ mother, Imogene Elizabeth Seymore were mishandled and that this is why when the pendants were given to the Seymores, all of the pendants were empty (the “Feeble Explanation”).

 

17. In fact, Plaintiffs are informed and believe and thereon allege that the Boyds and their company have engaged in a pattern and practice of representing to Plaintiffs and others that they will prepare pendants containing the ashes of their loved ones, among other services (the “Fraudulent Representations”), and have deliberately failed to perform same, assuming that their failures to act will not be discovered and (that they would be able to charge a premium for unearned services while avoiding the costs of actually providing the same to their customers (the “Fraudulent Scheme”). In offering up the Fraudulent Representations to Plaintiffs and other similarly situated consumers in their time of grief, the Defendants encouraged and invited Plaintiffs and such consumers to place their trust in the Defendants, and in so doing took on fiduciary duties to the Plaintiff and other similarly situated consumers. Plaintiffs, for their part, accepted the invitation and put their trust in the Boyds, much to their later regret.

 

18. Even if Defendants’ Feeble Explanation for their wrong-doing were truthful (which Plaintiffs are informed and believes is not the case), such explanation suggests that the Boyd’s have taken on duties to the Plaintiff and other similarly situated consumers but have tasked one or more persons without the ability to carry out those duties with fulfilling their obligations to their customers (including Plaintiff), without any reasonable basis for believing those obligations will be carried out appropriately, thereby breaching their fiduciary duties to the Plaintiff and other consumers and thereby defrauding them.

     (2AC, ¶¶ 15 – 18 [bold emphasis added].)

            The foregoing allegations are sufficient to meet the specificity requirements required to plead fraud. .) “The sole issue raised by a general demurrer is whether the facts pleaded state a valid cause of action, not whether they are true. No matter how unlikely or improbable, plaintiff’s allegations must be accepted as true for the purpose of ruling on the demurrer. (Citation omitted.) Furthermore, plaintiff’s possible inability or difficulty in proving the allegations of the complaint is of no concern. (Citation omitted.)” (Kerivan v. Title Ins. & Trust Co. (1983) 147 Cal.App.3d 225, 229.)

            The demurrer to the second cause of action is OVERRULED.

            Defendants are to answer the Second Amended Complaint within 10 days.

Discussion

Plaintiffs Murrieta Holdings 2012-12 LLC and Zoe Epstein as Successor In Interest for David G. Epstein move for leave to file notice of pending action pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 405.36, which provides: 

Once a notice of pending action has been expunged, the claimant may not record another notice of pending action as to the affected property without leave of the court in which the action is pending.


(Code Civ. Proc. § 405.36.) 

In this action, the Complaint was filed on July 25, 2025. 

On August 25, 2025, Defendant Galloway Financial, LLC filed a motion to expunge lis pendens.

On September 16, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint. However, the Court expressly analyzed the motion to expunge lis pendens relative to the allegations in the Complaint, as the “pleading on which the notice is based.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 405.31)

            On September 29, 2025, the Court ordered the lis pendens expunged.

            On October 9, 2025[2], Plaintiff recorded a second lis pendens without leave of court. On October 15, 2025, Plaintiff filed a notice of pending action with the Court.

            The Court denied Plaintiffs’ subsequent motion for leave to record a lis pendens nunc pro tunc, and ordered the Notice of Pending Action filed with the Court on October 15, 2025 stricken and the Notice of Pending Action recorded on October 9, 2025 was ordered expunged.

            In light of the foregoing, the Court did not analyze the propriety of the notice of pending action relative to the allegations of the First Amended Complaint.

            Nothing in Code Civ. Proc. § 405.36 requires that the claimant make a showing that would be required to defeat a motion to expunge.  In this regard, Plaintiff, as the moving claimant, did not fully address the standards governing motions to expunge set forth in Civ. Proc. Code § 405.32, i.e., probable validity of the real property claim, as to each real property claim asserted in the First Amended Complaint.

            In this regard, the Court finds the best course of action to afford full due process and a complete record, is to allow Plaintiff to record the notice of pending action based upon the First Amended Complaint, and allow Defendants to file a separate motion to expunge lis pendens,[3] or for an order requiring Plaintiffs to post an undertaking pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 405.34.

            The motion for leave to file a lis pendens based upon the First Amended Complaint is GRANTED.  If Defendants so desire, the Court will allow them to specially set a hearing on a motion to expunge lis pendens or require an undertaking (Code Civ. Proc. § 405.34) for August 27, 2026 at 8:30, with briefing pursuant to code relative to that date.  In the event Defendants file such a motion, Plaintiffs are put on notice that they may be required to post an undertaking or tender the full amount of the loan proceeds they received, exclusive of interest.


[1] The Court notes that Defendant Galloway Financial LLC has a motion for leave to file a First Amended Answer set for August 27, 2026.  If all parties submit on this tentative ruling, Defendants may if desired use CRS to reserve a hearing for a motion to expunge/motion for undertaking on any date, then before filing the moving papers, contact the staff in Department 734 to ensure the appropriate rescheduling of the hearing. 

[2] The Notice of Lis Pendens filed with the Court does not reflect the October 9, 2025 recordation date, but Defendant indicates at Page 2:14 of the Opposition that this is the date of recordation. [3] While Defendants did address the arguments which would be presented in a motion to expunge, Plaintiff was only given a reply brief’s page limit to address such arguments. Because Code Civ. Code, § 405.32, Plaintiff was not required to fully address “probable validity” in this motion for leave.
