TENTATIVE RULING

 

The Court hereby GRANTS final approval and awards/approves the following:

(1) $300,000 (33 1/3%) for attorney fees to Class Counsel, D.Law, Inc., David Yeremian & Associates, Inc., and Lavi & Ebrahimian, LLP; (2) $32,986.04 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) enhancement payments of $7,500 each to Plaintiffs Jose Maldonado and Cintia Sevilla; (4) $75,000 (75% of $100,000 PAGA penalty) to the LWDA; and (5) $14,000 for settlement administration costs to Apex Class Action, LLC.

 

Plaintiffs’ counsel shall file a proposed Order and Judgment, consistent with this ruling containing all requisite terms, including the class definition, release language, and a statement that no class members requested exclusion. The Court orders class counsel to file a final report summarizing all distributions made pursuant to the approved settlement, supported by declaration. The Court will set a non-appearance date for submission of a final report for ______________. 

 

BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff Jose Maldonado and Cintia Sevilla sue their former employer, Defendants TPS Solutions, Inc. (“TPS”), and MESHLogistics, Inc. (“MESH”) (collectively “Defendants”), for alleged wage and hour violations. Plaintiffs seek to represent a class of Defendants’ current and former non-exempt employees.

On March 30, 2023, Plaintiff Maldonado commenced a Class Action by filing a Complaint alleging causes of action against Defendants for various Labor Code violations. On March 30, 2023, pursuant to Labor Code section 2699.3, subd. (a), Plaintiff Maldonado gave timely written notice to Defendants and the Labor & Workforce Development Agency (“LWDA”). On June 5, 2023, Plaintiff Maldonado filed a First Amended Complaint adding a cause of action for civil penalties under PAGA pursuant to Labor Code section 2699, et seq.

On October 12, 2023, Plaintiff Sevilla commenced a Class Action by filing a Complaint alleging causes of action against Defendants for various Labor Code violations. On October 12, 2023, pursuant to Labor Code section 2699.3, subd.(a), Plaintiff Sevilla gave timely written notice to Defendant TPS, Defendant Mesh and the LWDA. On December 18, 2023, Plaintiff Sevilla commenced a PAGA Action (Case No. 23STCV30800) alleging one cause of action against Defendants for civil penalties pursuant to PAGA, Labor Code Sections 2698, et seq.

On January 27, 2025, the Parties participated in an all-day mediation presided over by Monique Ngo-Bonnici which led to this Agreement to settle Plaintiffs’ claims. The terms of settlement were finalized in the long-form Class Action and PAGA Settlement Agreement (“Settlement Agreement”), a copy of which was filed with the Court on February 6, 2026.

On February 5, 2026, the Plaintiffs filed a Second Amended Complaint in the Action to combine the Plaintiffs and their claims. The operative complaint alleges causes of action for: (1) Failure to Pay Minimum Wages (including Regular Rate) in Violation of Labor Code sections 1194 and 1197; (2) Failure to Pay Wages and Overtime Under Labor Code §§ 510, 1194 and Wage Orders; (3) Meal-Period Liability Under Labor Code § 226.7 and Wage Orders; (4) Rest-Break Liability Under Labor Code § 226.7 and Wage Orders; (5) Failure to Comply with Labor Code § 245 et seq., and 246; (6) Failure to Provide Complete and Accurate Wage Statements in Violation of Labor Code § 226; (7) Failure to Keep Required Payroll Records Under Labor Code §§ 1174 and 1174.5; (8) Failure to Timely Pay All Earned Wages and Final Paychecks Due at Time of Separation of Employment and for Penalties Pursuant to Labor Code §§ 201, 202 and 203; (9) Violation of Business & Professions Code § 17200 et seq.; and (10) Penalties Pursuant to Labor Code § 2699, et seq.

On June 5, 2026, after the parties filed further briefing and a revised class notice to address concerns raised by the Court, preliminary approval of the settlement was granted.

Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered (see Declaration of Norma Ayala (“Ayala Decl.”).) Now before the Court is the Motion for Final Approval of the settlement.

 

SETTLEMENT CLASS DEFINITION

 

·         "Class" means all persons employed by Defendants in California and classified as a non-exempt hourly employee who worked for Defendants during the Class Period. (¶1.5)

·         “Class Period” means the period from October 12, 2019 to the earlier of the date that the Court grants Preliminary Approval or April 27, 2025. (¶1.12)

·         “Aggrieved Employee” means a person employed by Defendants in California and classified as a non-exempt hourly employee who worked for Defendants during the PAGA Period. (¶1.4)

·         “PAGA Period” means the period from April 12, 2022 to the earlier of the date that the Court grants Preliminary Approval or April 27, 2025. (¶1.31)

ANALYSIS OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

 

A.     Does a presumption of fairness exist? 

The Court preliminarily found in its Order of June 5, 2026 that the presumption of fairness should be applied.  No facts have come to the Court’s attention that would alter that preliminary conclusion.  Accordingly, the settlement is entitled to a presumption of fairness as set forth in the preliminary approval order.

B.      Is the settlement fair, adequate, and reasonable?

The settlement was preliminarily found to be fair, adequate and reasonable.  Notice has now been given to the Class and the LWDA. 

  Reaction of the class members to the proposed settlement.

Number of class members: 1,050 (Ayala Decl., ¶7.)   

Number of notice packets mailed: 1,050 (Id. at ¶9.)    

Number of undeliverable notices: 84 (Id. at ¶11.)

Number of opt-outs: 0 (Id. at ¶13.)

Number of objections: 0 (Id. at ¶14.)

Number of participating class members: 1,050 (Id. at ¶16.)

Average individual payment: $381.90 (Id. at ¶18.)

Highest individual payment: $1,501.62 (Ibid.)

The Court finds that the notice was given as directed and conforms to due process requirements.  Given the reactions of the Class Members and the LWDA to the proposed settlement and for the reasons set for in the Preliminary Approval order, the settlement is found to be fair, adequate, and reasonable.

 

C.      Attorney Fees and Costs

            Class Counsel requests an award of $315,000 (35%) in fees and $32,986.04 in costs. (Memo ISO Final at 2:3-5.) The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $315,000 (35%) in fees and $50,000 in costs (¶3.2.2).

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 254.)  Here, class counsel request attorney fees using the percentage method, as crosschecked by lodestar. (Memo ISO Final at pp. 11-15.)

In common fund cases, the Court may employ a percentage of the benefit method, as cross-checked against the lodestar. (Laffitte v. Robert Half Int’l, Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.5th 480, 503.) The fee request represents 35% of the gross settlement amount, which is above the average generally awarded in class actions.  (See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 [“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”].)

Counsel’s percentage-based fee request is higher than the unadjusted lodestar and would represent application of a multiplier of approximately 1.009x. Notice of the fee request was provided to class members in the notice packet and no one objected. (Ayala Decl., ¶14, Exhibit A.)

Fee Split: Each Plaintiff represents that he or she is aware of the fee splitting agreements between Class Counsel and signed an acknowledgement regarding this. (Declaration of Jose Maldonado ISO Prelim, ¶17; Declaration of Cintia Sevilla ISO Prelim, ¶12.)

Fees are set at 33 1/3% of the GSA or $300,000, which represents a reasonable percentage of the total funds paid by Defendant and rewards counsel for any risk they took in this case.  The Court finds there are no special circumstances warranting a higher percentage recovery than the one-third fee award that is typically provided.

 

            As for costs, Class Counsel is requesting a cost amount of $32,986.04. This is less than the $50,000 cap estimated at preliminary approval, which was disclosed to Class Members in the Notice and not objected to. (Ayala Decl., ¶14, Exhibit A.) Counsel represent that costs include but are not limited to: filing and service fees, Case Anywhere, deposition costs, mediation fees, and expert fees. (Kim Decl. ISO Final, Exhibit 4; Sloane Decl. ISO Final, Exhibit 1.) The costs appear to be reasonable in amount and reasonably necessary to this litigation.

 

Based on the above, the Court awards $300,000 in fees and $32,986.04 in costs.

D.     Incentive Awards

The class representatives seek enhancement payments of $10,000 each to Plaintiffs Jose Maldonado and Cintia Sevilla for their contributions to the action. (Memo ISO Final at 11:11-13.)  

In connection with the final fairness hearing, named Plaintiffs must submit declarations attesting to why they should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.  The named Plaintiffs must explain why they “should be compensated for the expense or risk he has incurred in conferring a benefit on other members of the class.”  (Clark v. American Residential Services LLC (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 785, 806.)  Trial courts should not sanction enhancement awards of thousands of dollars with “nothing more than pro forma claims as to ‘countless’ hours expended, ‘potential stigma’ and ‘potential risk.’ Significantly more specificity, in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of financial or other risks incurred by the named plaintiffs, is required in order for the trial court to conclude that an enhancement was ‘necessary to induce [the named plaintiff] to participate in the suit . . . .’”  (Id. at 806-807, italics and ellipsis in original.)

Each Plaintiff represents that his or her contributions to this litigation include: communicating with their attorneys about the case, providing documents, answering questions, preparing for and participating in the mediation, and reviewing the settlement. (Declaration of Jose Maldonado ISO Prelim, ¶10; Declaration of Cintia Sevilla ISO Final, ¶6.)  

Based on the above, as well as the benefits obtained on behalf of the class, the Court awards enhancement payments in the amount of $7,500 each to Plaintiffs Maldonado and Sevilla.

 

E.      Settlement Administration Costs

            The settlement administrator, Apex Class Action, LLC, is requesting $14,000 for the costs of settlement administration. (Ayala Decl., ¶21.) This equals the estimate of $14,000 provided for in the Settlement Agreement (¶3.2.3) and disclosed to class members in the Notice, to which there were no objections. (Ayala Decl., ¶14, Exhibit A.) Based on the above, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $14,000.
