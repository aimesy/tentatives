24VECV06161 LIRD V AVALONBAY

[TENTATIVE] ORDER:  Plaintiff Ana Del Carmen Lird’s Motion for Evidentiary Sanctions is DENIED.  

Plaintiff Ana Del Carmen Lird’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is DENIED.

Defendant AvalonBay Communities, Inc.’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,000.00 against Plaintiff Ana Del Carmen Lird and Counsel, jointly and severally.

Introduction

Plaintiff Ana Del Carmen Lird (Plaintiff) moved for evidentiary sanctions against Defendant AvalonBay Communities, Inc. (Defendant) under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 subdivision (c).  Plaintiff requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $3,250.00 be imposed against Defendant, representing attorney’s fees and costs incurred in relation to the instant motion.  Defendant opposes Plaintiff’s motion, and requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,250.00 be imposed against Plaintiff and her counsel of record, jointly and severally. 

Discussion

Plaintiff argues Defendant willfully or negligently destroyed or failed to preserve surveillance footage and photographs, contrary to Defendant’s policies, following Plaintiff’s slip and fall incident.  (Mot., p. 5.)  Plaintiff requests the Court impose evidentiary sanctions against Defendant for misuse of the discovery process, precluding Defendant from introducing evidence related to the withheld materials. (Mot., p. 12.)

In opposition, Defendant argues there was no spoliation of evidence, as photographs were not taken as part of Defendant’s employees’ investigation, and the surveillance camera was vandalized and inoperable at the time of Plaintiff’s alleged fall. (Opp., p. 3; Delashaw Decl., para. 4.)

In reply, Plaintiff argues Defendant’s vandalism argument is unsupported and does not excuse spoliation. (Rep., p. 3.)  Plaintiff argues Defendant has not disputed its failure to take or preserve photos required by Defendant’s own policies. (Rep., p. 5.) Finally, Plaintiff argues Plaintiff has been substantially prejudiced by Defendant’s failure to preserve its own evidence. (Rep., pp. 5-6.)

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 subdivision (c), evidentiary sanctions may be imposed prohibiting parties engaged in the misuse of the discovery process from introducing designated matters in evidence.  (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 2023.030, subd. (c).)  “When exercising its discretion to determine which form of sanction is most appropriate for a discovery violation, a trial court should consider various factors, including ‘the importance of the materials that were not produced—from the perspective of the offended party’s ability to litigate the case—and what prejudice, if any, the offended party suffered….’”  (Victor Valley Union High School District v. Superior Court of San Bernardino County (2023) 91 Cal.App.5th 1121, 1158 (Victor Valley).)     

The Court finds evidentiary sanctions are inappropriate given the lack of evidence of Defendant’s alleged discovery violations.  Defendant served verified discovery responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production on October 28, 2025. (Opp., p. 4; See also Plaintiff’s Mot., Ex. 1.)  In their responses, Defendant stated they did not locate any photographs after conducting a diligent search and reasonable inquiry, and that video surveillance never existed.  (Opp., p. 4; See also Plaintiff’s Mot., Ex. 1.)  Further, Plaintiff states Defendant did not take photographs following the alleged incident. (Mot., p. 5; Madison Decl., para. 4.)  Plaintiff asserts, in a conclusory fashion, that surveillance video was taken and was subsequently lost, deleted, or otherwise not preserved.  (Mot., p. 5.)

Therefore, the crux of Plaintiff’s argument lies in the assertion that Defendant’s failure to comply with its own policies and procedures regarding documentation constitutes spoliation.  The duty to preserve evidence arises when litigation is “reasonably foreseeable.” (Victor Valley, supra, 91 Cal.App.5th at p. 1144.)  The court in Victor Valley reasoned that the school district’s internal procedures for collecting and forwarding incident reports immediately following an incident were compelling evidence that the district recognized potential litigation. (Victor Valley, supra, 91 Cal.App.5th at p. 1156.)  Here, like the Victor Valley defendant, Defendant’s policies and procedures requiring employees to take photographs following any incident involving injury on the premises suggest Defendant’s anticipation of foreseeable litigation.

However, while Defendant’s policies may suggest an awareness that slip and fall incidents could lead to litigation, the existence of such a policy does not, by itself, establish that Defendant deliberately destroyed or failed to preserve evidence. “[A] party moving for discovery sanctions based on the spoliation of evidence must make an initial prima facie showing that the responding party in fact destroyed evidence that had a substantial probability of damaging the moving party’s ability to establish an essential element of his claim or defense.” (Williams v. Russ (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 1215, 1227.)  

Here, Plaintiff fails to provide evidence that Defendant deliberately chose not to photograph the scene or deliberately destroyed the surveillance footage. Plaintiff’s counsel declares Defendant’s manager, Satenik Gasparyan, confirmed in her May 11, 2026, deposition that no photographs were taken. (Madison Decl., para. 4.) However, Defendant’s counsel declares Satenik Gasparyan testified she would need to investigate the existence of the photographs and the surveillance footage. (Delashaw Decl., para. 5.)  Further, Defendant’s Person Most Knowledgeable (PMK) Carl Merin testified no photographs were taken as part of the investigation and the surveillance camera was inoperable at the time of Plaintiff’s alleged fall. (Delashaw Decl., para. 4.)  Plaintiff has not provided competent evidence, since Defendant’s PMK’s deposition clarifies Satenik Gasparyan’s uncertainty regarding whether photographs and surveillance footage was taken. 

Further, the Court finds Plaintiff has not met her burden of showing prejudice.  Plaintiff has not been prejudiced because she likely has access to photographs of the premises on the day of the incident.  Plaintiff’s son testified at his deposition that he recalled taking photographs of the location where Plaintiff allegedly fell and could likely locate them. (Delashaw Decl., para. 6.)  Plaintiff has not disputed that these photographs exist, has not produced them, and has not explained why they would fail to document the condition of the premises at the time of the incident.

The court in Victor Valley laid out a two-part test when determining which form of sanction is most appropriate for a discovery violation: (1) the importance of the materials that were not produced, “from the perspective of the offended party’s ability to litigate the case,” and (2) what prejudice, if any, the offended party suffered. (Victor Valley, supra, 91 Cal.App.5th at p. 1158.) Here, Plaintiff has access to photographs that show the location where Plaintiff allegedly fell, which supports her ability to litigate the case. Therefore, Plaintiff has not been substantially prejudiced as there is likely photographic evidence to produce to a jury, and the lack of Defendant’s photographs and surveillance footage does not hinder her ability to litigate.

Plaintiff’s motion for evidentiary sanctions is DENIED. 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 subdivision (a), a court may impose a monetary sanction ordering a party engaging in the misuse of discovery pay reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees.  (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 2023.030, subd. (a).)  A court may also impose this sanction on a party who unsuccessfully asserted another has engaged in the misuse of the discovery process. (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 2023.030, subd. (a).) 

Because this Court is denying Plaintiff’s motion, Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is DENIED. 

Defendant also requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,250.00 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030(a). Defendant’s counsel claims to have spent one hour reviewing the motion, three hours preparing the opposition, and anticipates spending one hour attending the hearing, at an hourly rate of $250.00. (Delashaw Decl., para. 9.) The Court finds three hours to prepare the Opposition excessive for a motion of this scope and reduces that component to two hours. The Court reduces the sanctions request to a reasonable amount of $1,000.00, consisting of one hour reviewing the motion, two hours preparing the opposition, and an anticipated one hour attending the hearing at an hourly rate of $250.00.  Defendant’s request for sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $1,000.00 against Plaintiff and counsel.

IT IS SO ORDERED, CLERK TO GIVE NOTICE.

Case Number: 25VECV03461    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: T

25VECV03461 ROSS V FCA

[TENTATIVE] ORDER:  Plaintiff Daniel Ross’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures is GRANTED.

Plaintiff Daniel Ross’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $2,500.00 against Defendant and Counsel, jointly and severally.

Introduction

            Plaintiff Daniel Ross (Plaintiff) moved to compel Defendant FCA US, LLC (Defendant) to produce initial disclosures under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 subdivisions (h)(6), (h)(7), (h)(8), (h)(9), (h)(10), (h)(12), and (h)(13). Plaintiff further requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,500.00 against Defendant and its counsel of record.  

Discussion 

            Plaintiff argues Defendant’s production of documents on October 17, 2025, was materially incomplete and unverified. (Mot., p. 5.)  

            First, Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 does not include a verification requirement.  Plaintiff points to the verification requirement set out in section 2016.090, which provides for certain initial disclosures required in all civil actions. (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 2016.090, subd. (a)(1).)  It further states that “disclosures under this section shall be verified either in a written declaration by the party or the party’s authorized representative, or signed by the party’s counsel.” (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 2016.090, subd. (a)(5).)  This requirement is limited to section 2016.090 by subdivision (a)(5).  Thus, the provision does not require verifications for other initial disclosures, such as those required under section 871.26.  Plaintiff’s citation to the legislative history surrounding Assembly Bill 1755, the legislation which produced the Song-Beverly initial disclosure regime, is inapposite. Even if such sources constituted binding authority, the specific report to which Plaintiff refers does not even discuss a verification requirement.  Thus, this argument is without merit. 

Next, Defendant argues Plaintiff’s motion should be denied as Defendant has provided the required initial disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 subdivision (h). (Opp., p. 3.)  Subdivision (h)(6) requires motor vehicle information reports for the subject vehicle.  Subdivision (h)(7) requires production of the warranty transaction history for the subject vehicle.  Subdivision (h)(8) requires production of required field actions applicable to the subject vehicle.  Subdivision (h)(9) requires production of published technical service bulletins for vehicles of the same year, make, and model of the subject vehicle reasonably related to its nonconformities.  Defendant has provided these initial disclosures. (Tremonti Decl., para. 2.)

On October 16, 2025, Defendant served its initial disclosure production, which included warranty claims records, sales brochures, vehicle information detail reports, glove box documents, the Monroney label, and applicable Technical Service Bulletins.  (Tremonti Decl., para. 2.)  Plaintiff argues Defendant must separately run and produce VIN-specific search results from a list of internal systems on the theory that those systems will return the complete, unfiltered universe of records for the Subject Vehicle in that system. (Mot., p. 7-10.) Section 871.26 subdivision (h) requires production of defined categories of documents, not production by any particular method or system. Because Defendant has identified specific documents responsive to each category, and Plaintiff has not shown those documents are incomplete or that responsive information exists in the named systems and nowhere in what was produced, Defendant has satisfied subdivisions (h)(6) through (h)(9).

Subdivision (h)(12) requires production of warranty policies and procedure manuals. Subdivision (h)(13) requires production of service manuals for the subject vehicle.  Defendant has provided these initial disclosures subject to a protective order. (Tremonti Decl., paras. 3, 6.)

Subdivision (h)(10) requires production of published information service bulletins for vehicles of the same year, make, and model of the subject vehicle reasonably related to its nonconformities.  Defendant has not provided these disclosures; however, Defendant asserts it has not identified any information service bulletins applicable to the subject vehicle, despite a reasonable search. (Opp., para. 6.) 

While Defendant’s opposition states a reasonable search was conducted, Defendant’s counsel’s declaration does not state as much.  Counsel merely declares “FAC has not identified any additional…Informational Service Bulletin…in its possession and reasonably related to the subject vehicle.”  (Tremonti Decl., para. 8.)  Counsel has not declared under penalty of perjury that a reasonable search was conducted to locate informational service bulletins.  The Court therefore finds Defendant has not shown sufficient good cause for failure to produce informational service bulletins under subdivision (h)(10).

Thus, Defendant has failed to provide all initial disclosures as required under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 subdivision (h), specifically Defendant has failed to provide initial disclosures under subdivision (h)(10).

Further, Plaintiff requests Defendant produce complete DealerConnect records, unverified complaint claims, and AutoPay claims, citing to Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785 (Robertson).  Plaintiff’s reliance on Robertson is misplaced.  The Robertson court did not hold that “every TNF visit and every AutoPay flash/reprogram constitutes a repair attempt under the Song-Beverly Act,” nor was the court specific as to what constitutes a repair attempt. (Mot., p. 11.)  The Robertson court addressed whether a reasonable number of attempts for repair were made of a vehicle and held that only occasions providing an opportunity to repair the specific nonconformity count as a repair attempt.  (Robertson, supra, 144 Cal.App.4th at p. 799.)  Section 871.26 does not specifically require these disclosures.  Defendant is therefore not required to produce DealerConnect records, unverified complaint claims, and AutoPay claims, as they go beyond the scope of section 871.26 subdivision (h).

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to compel initial disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 is GRANTED as to subdivision (h)(10).

Plaintiff requests monetary sanctions against Defendant and Counsel, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,500.00, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 subdivision (j)(1). Given the Court’s ruling, and Defendant’s failure to show good cause for failing to provide informational service bulletins, sanctions are mandatory under section 871.26 subdivision (j)(1).  Therefore, the Court imposes sanctions of $2,500.00 against Defendant and Counsel, jointly and severally.  Sanctions are to be paid by August 7, 2026 to the Client Trust Account of Plaintiff's counsel.  

Finally, Defendant requests the Warranty Administration Manual, attached as Exhibit 1 to Plaintiff’s counsel’s declaration be withdrawn from public record until it is properly filed under seal in accordance with the protective order, as it is a confidential FCA document. (Opp., p. 7.)  The document attached was not produced in this litigation.  Defendant needs to file a separate noticed motion for this in order for the Court to make express findings to justify the sealing.

IT IS SO ORDERED, CLERK TO GIVE NOTICE.

25VECV04729 MIDURYAN V TOYOTA

[TENTATIVE] ORDER:  Defendant Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc.’s Motion to Strike portions of the First Amended Complaint is GRANTED WITH 20 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND. 

Introduction

Defendant Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc. (Defendant) moves to strike portions of Plaintiff Ripsime Miduryan’s (Plaintiff) First Amended Complaint (FAC).  Specifically, Defendant moves to strike Plaintiff’s request for punitive damages within the sixth cause of action (COA) for fraudulent inducement – concealment.

Discussion

Defendant moves to strike the request for punitive damages.  Defendant argues the sixth COA fails as a matter of law thus the punitive damages request is improper. (Mot., pp. 5-6.)  Defendant further argues Plaintiff fails to allege specific facts as to malice, oppression, or fraud, and fails to allege specific facts as to a corporate entity. (Mot., pp. 7-8.)

First, Defendant has previously filed a demurrer and motion to strike portions of Plaintiff’s Complaint. (See 4/20/26 Minute Order.)  That demurrer placed into issue the sixth COA for fraud inducement – concealment, and the motion to strike requested punitive damages be stricken.  This Court overruled Defendant’s demurrer and granted the motion to strike on the grounds the Complaint insufficiently alleged punitive damages against a corporate entity. (See 4/20/26 Minute Order.)   

The sixth COA of Plaintiff’s Complaint and the sixth COA of Plaintiff’s FAC are identical, except the addition of the language “(and its directors, officers, employees, affiliates, and/or agents).” (FAC, paras. 53, 58; Compl., paras. 52, 57.)  This Court already found the sixth COA sufficiently pled when it overruled Defendant’s demurrer to the same claim in the original Complaint. (4/20/26 Minute Order.) Because the FAC’s fraud allegations are substantively unchanged, Defendant’s argument that the sixth COA fails as a matter of law is not persuasive.

The FAC also alleges “[a]ll acts of corporate employees as alleged were authorized or ratified by an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporate employer.” (FAC, para. 7.)  These additions are insufficient to plead punitive damages against a corporate entity.  Plaintiff alleges Defendant and its directors, officers, employees, affiliates, and/or agents actively concealed a known defect, but these allegations are conclusory in nature and lack specific facts. 

Plaintiff’s conclusory allegations made in the FAC, specifically paragraphs 7, 53, and 58, are unpersuasive. “Conclusory allegations” that defendant’s conduct was “malicious” are insufficient and subject to being stricken. (Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1036.) “In order to survive a motion to strike an allegation of punitive damages, the ultimate facts showing an entitlement to such relief must be pled by a plaintiff.” (Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255; Grieves v. Superior Court (1984) 157 Cal. App. 3d 159, 166; Blegen v. Superior Court (1981) 125 Cal. App. 3d 959, 962-963.) Conclusory allegations are not the same as ultimate facts. “The complaint must allege ultimate facts, not evidentiary facts or conclusions of law.” (Krug v Meeham (1952) 109 Cal.App.2d 274, 277.  Plaintiff must allege facts showing that a specific officer, director, or managing agent personally committed, authorized, or ratified the wrongful conduct, which Plaintiff fails to do.  For corporate defendants, punitive damages are only available if the wrongful act was committed by—or ratified by—an officer, director, or “managing agent.” A managing agent is not just a supervisor; they must have substantial authority over corporate policy that affects a broad segment of the company. There are no allegations in the FAC of misconduct or fraud against a specific officer, director, or managing agent, or authorization or ratification of any such misconduct or fraud.  The Court is persuaded by Defendant’s argument that Plaintiff does not plead facts sufficient for punitive damages against a corporate entity.

Defendant's motion to strike punitive damages is GRANTED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.  

IT IS SO ORDERED, CLERK TO GIVE NOTICE.
