Case Number: 24STCV19154    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 512

HEARING DATE:    Weds., September 16, 2026                            JUDGE /DEPT:            Mkrtchyan/512

                                    Calabasas

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Mountains Restoration Trust d/b/a TreePeople Land Trust

MOTION TO QUASH SUBPOENAS

(CCP § 1987.1)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

Plaintiff Mountains Restoration Trust d/b/a TreePeople Land Trust’s Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoenas for Production of Business Records Directed to YA Group; JS Held, Inc.; and CalPrivate Bank is GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN PART.

 

The Motion is GRANTED as to the CalPrivate Bank subpoena and is otherwise DENIED.  

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice.

REPLY:                     Filed on September 9, 2026                            [   ] Late                      [   ] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On July 31, 2024, Plaintiff Mountains Restoration Trust, Inc. d/b/a TreePeople Land Trust (“Plaintiff”) filed a Complaint for Declaratory Relief against Defendant City of Calabasas (“Defendant”). (Compl., p. 1.) The underlying action arises from disagreements between the parties regarding a Lease Management Agreement and Covenant Running with the Land pertaining to a “12-acre real property located at Old Topanga Canyon Road and Mulholand Highway known as Headwaters Corner [(‘Headwaters’)].” (Compl.)

 

On July 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoenas for Production of Business Records Directed to YA Group; JS Held, Inc.; and CalPrivate Bank (the “Motion”).

 

On September 2, 2026, Defendant filed an Opposition to the Motion.

 

On September 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

II.              Procedural Issues

 

As an initial matter, the Court notes the Motion improperly combines three motions into one – a motion to quash deposition subpoena directed at YA Group; a motion to quash deposition subpoena directed at JS Held, Inc.; and a motion to quash deposition subpoena directed at CalPrivate Bank.

 

Additionally, the parties did not participate in an Informal Discovery Conference prior to Plaintiff filing the instant Motion.

 

III.            Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, subdivision (a) provides, in pertinent part, as follows:

 

If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court’s own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).)  

 

IV.            Discussion

 

Parties’ Arguments

 

Plaintiff moves to quash three subpoenas for production of business records directed at three third parties – YA Group; JS Held, Inc.; and CalPrivate Bank. (See Motion.) In support of the Motion, Plaintiff provides copies of the subject subpoenas as well as Plaintiff’s Responses to Defendant’s Requests for Production (“RPDs”) served on Defendant on January 28, 2025. (See Motion, Serova Decl., Exhs. 1-2.)

 

In Opposition, Defendant contends the Motion should be denied in its entirety on the following grounds: (1) the Motion is moot as to YA Group as YA Group has already responded to the subpoena at issue; (2) documents regarding repair estimates from JS Held, Inc. are relevant to the issues in dispute “because these third parties were engaged by Plaintiff to produce estimates of the costs of repair of the Bartelle House and vehicle bridge”; and (3) CalPrivate Bank’s records regarding Plaintiff are relevant to Defendant’s cross-complaint for accounting of insurance monies owed for repair of the improved structures on the Bartelle parcel. (Motion, pp. 4-8.) Defense counsel submits a declaration in support of the Opposition. (Opp., Bush Decl.)

 

In Reply, Plaintiff states the Opposition does not cure the defects in the subject subpoenas directed to JS Held and CalPrivate Bank. (Motion, p. 1.) As to JS Held, Plaintiff argues the subpoena should be quashed because the underlying claim serving as the basis for the subpoena request is meritless. (Motion, pp. 2-3.) Further, the documents subpoenaed from JS Held would be inadmissible at trial as hearsay, and Defendant already possesses the estimates at issue and thus may not acquire duplicative records from a third party. (See Reply, pp. 2-3.) As to CalPrivate Bank, Plaintiff contends the subpoena is overbroad “because it seeks all statements and transaction records for TPLT’s bank account without limitation on the subject matter; that the City’s accounting cross-claim underlying the request fails as a matter of law because TPLT was not required to tender the insurance proceeds to the City and had no duty to account for them; and that TPLT already produced documents accounting for its receipt and use of the proceeds anyway, making the subpoena unnecessary….” (Reply, pp. 3-4.)

 

YA Group Subpoena

 

As to the subpoena directed to YA Group, the Court finds that the parties do not dispute that YA Group already provided answers to Plaintiff’s subject subpoena. (See Motion; Opp.; Reply.)

 

Thus, the Motion is DENIED AS MOOT as to the request to quash the subpoena directed to YA Group.

 

JS Held, Inc. Subpoena

 

As to the subpoena directed to JS Held, Inc., the subject subpoena requests the following documents:

 

1.     All DOCUMENTS concerning the preparation of any cost estimate, valuation, reconstruction estimate or report prepared by YOU in 2016 for Mountains Restoration Trust, Inc. (hereinafter “MRT”) regarding the June 4, 2016 total structural loss at the property 3815 Old Topanga Road, Calabasas, CA 91302 (“the PROPERTY”).

2.     All CORRESPONDENCE between YOU and MRT related to the work YOU performed for MRT in 2016 in connection with the PROPERTY.

3.     All DOCUMENTS reflecting payments received by YOU related to the work YOU performed for MRT in 2016 in connection with the PROPERTY.

 

(Motion, Serova Decl., Exh. 1.)

 

Here, the Court finds the subpoena request is relevant and narrowly tailored to address Defendant’s contentions that Plaintiff’s repair obligations under the parties’ agreement exceeded just merely a clean-up of the debris and more closely resembled a full rebuild. (See Opp., p. 6.)

 

Thus, the Motion is DENIED.

 

CalPrivate Bank Subpoena

 

As to the subpoena directed to CalPrivate Bank, the subject subpoena requests the following documents:

 

1. ALL bank statements for MRT/TPLT Account Number 250449 from June 1, 2016 through the present.

2. ALL bank records for MRT/TPLT Account Number 250449 showing any deposits from CITY OF CALABASAS from June 1, 2016 through the present.

3. ALL bank records for MRT/TPLT Account Number 250449 showing any deposits from Chubb Insurance, or “Chubb”, or “Chubb Ltd.” from June 1, 2016 through the present.

4. Records of ALL deposits made by MRT/TPLT in Account Number 250449 from June 1, 2016 through the present.

5. Records of ALL transactions made by MRT/TPLT, Account Number 250449, from June 1, 2016 through the present.

 

(Motion, Serova Decl., Exh. 1.)

 

Here, the Court finds these requests are overly broad in scope. While the Court acknowledges there is some relevance to the subpoenas’ requests as they seek information pertaining to the first amended Cross-Complaint’s claim for accounting, the subpoenas effectively ask for all communications and documents in CalPrivate Bank’s possession relating to Plaintiff and/or Plaintiff and the instant litigation. Given the sweeping nature of these requests, the Court finds the CalPrivate Bank subpoena is overly broad and thus unenforceable. (See e.g., Calcor Space Facility, Inc. v. Sup. Ct. (Thiem Indus., Inc.) (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 216, 222-223.)

 

To the extent Defendant wishes to gather information as to the financial accounts and payments made by Plaintiff in efforts to repair the Bartell house (see Opp., pp. 6-7), such information would be most appropriately, and more understandably, sought in a post-judgment phase of the action. However, this is not the current procedural posture of the case.

 

Further, the parties do not dispute that Defendant has other evidence addressing similar information as that sought in the subject bank records.

 

Thus, the Motion is GRANTED as to the CalPrivate Bank subpoena.

 

            Miscellaneous

 

Insofar as the parties dispute meet and confer efforts regarding the instant Motion, the Court finds that, while meeting and conferring tends to always be beneficial, it is not required under Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1.

 

To the extent the parties argue the merits of underlying claims in the FAC or FACC as grounds to either deny or grant the Motion, the Court finds these contentions improperly raised in the instant Motion. The Court’s role in ruling on a motion to quash deposition subpoena is not to delve into and adjudicate the merits of the underlying action or the face of a pleading – such contentions are raised in other motions, such as those that address the sufficiency of the allegations in a pleading and/or dispositive motions.

 

V.              Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff Mountains Restoration Trust d/b/a TreePeople Land Trust’s Motion to Quash Deposition Subpoenas for Production of Business Records Directed to YA Group; JS Held, Inc.; and CalPrivate Bank is GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN PART.

 

The Motion is GRANTED as to the CalPrivate Bank subpoena and is otherwise DENIED.  

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice.

CASE NUMBER:      25STCV11956                                              

NOTICE:                  OK                                                                 

                                                                                                                       

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION OF PERSON MOST KNOWLEDGEABLE

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Nina Glassman

RESP. PARTY:         Defendant Roots, Inc.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION

 (CCP § 2025.450)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Nina Glassman’s Motion to Compel the Deposition of the Person Most Knowledgeable or Qualified for Defendant is GRANTED. Defendant’s PMK is to be produced within 60 days of this order on a mutually agreed upon date and time.  The request for sanctions is DENIED.

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          Filed on September 2, 2026                            [   ] Late                      [   ] None

REPLY:                     Filed on September 9, 2026                            [   ] Late                      [   ] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On April 24, 2025, Plaintiff Nina Glassman (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Roots, Inc. (“Defendant”) and Does 1-20.

 

On July 14, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

 

On September 8, 2025, Defendant filed an Amended Answer.

 

On August 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel the Deposition of the Person Most Knowledgeable (“PMK”) or Qualified for Defendant (the “Motion”) and request for sanctions.

 

On September 2, 2026, Defendant filed an Opposition.

 

On September 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450, section (a) provides:  

 

If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.230, without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice. 

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)  

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.410 states, in pertinent part: “(a) Any party served with a deposition notice that does not comply with Article 2 (commencing with Section 2025.210) waives any error or irregularity unless that party promptly serves a written objection specifying that error or irregularity at least three calendar days prior to the date for which the deposition is scheduled, on the party seeking to take the deposition and any other attorney or party on whom the deposition notice was served.”

 

III.            Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant’s PMK and provides evidence of the parties’ communication regarding the PMK depositions, and a notice of deposition for Defendant’s PMK set for 7/30/26 to which Defendant objected and did not appear. (Motion, Kopple Decl., Exhs. A-F, Kopple Decl.) In the objection to the PMK deposition notice, Defendant’s counsel state the deposition date does not work for counsel and requests the parties discuss alternative dates and scheduling of the PMK deposition telephonically. (See Motion, Kopple Decl., Exhs. A-B, D.) Plaintiff requests sanctions in conjunction with the Motion in the amount of $4,623.41 against Defendant and defense counsel. (Notice of Motion.)

 

In Opposition, Defendant criticizes Plaintiff’s counsel’s professionalism and states that, contrary to the Motion’s claims, Defendant is cooperating with scheduling depositions. (Opp., pp. 1-4.) Further, Defendant states that the witnesses Plaintiff deposed in March 2026 “are essentially the same individuals who qualify as Persons Most Knowledgeable now. In March 2026, before current Defense counsel took over the case, Plaintiff deposed three relevant witnesses. Plaintiff deposed Diana Kang on March 12, 2026; Noah Warren on March 10, 2026, and Seth Blackburn on March 22, 2026. These witnesses have already testified to most, if not all, of the topics of examination in Plaintiff’s notice of PMK deposition. Now, Plaintiff is claiming he needs three more depositions.” (Opp., p. 5.) Defendant also contends that Plaintiff is using the additional depositions “as a second opportunity for a very extensive Request for Production of Documents.” (See Opp., p. 5.) Additionally, the Opposition also provides that three depositions have been scheduled for September, but the deposition of Omar Elarabi needs rescheduling as defense counsel’s office just learned that he suffers from a life-threatening medical condition. (See Opp., Rastegar Decl., p. 2.)  Finally, Defendant contends there are no grounds for sanctions to be awarded against Defendant in this case as Defendant has acted in good faith and has been cooperative at “every step.” (Opp., pp. 6-7.)

 

In Reply, Plaintiff states Plaintiff has offered 38 dates for the deposition of Defendant’s PMK, but Defendant has unreasonably rejected all the proposed dates. (Reply, pp. 1-2.) Plaintiff contends that Defendant is misusing discovery and states the PMK deponents were only previously deposed as percipient witnesses. (Reply, pp. 2-3.) Plaintiff states that during the percipient witness depositions, the deponents were not prepared to answer questions in conformance with PMK requirements. (Reply, p. 4.)

 

Here, the Court finds Defendant timely objected to the noticed deposition of Defendant’s PMK scheduled for July 2026 with a valid objection. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).) Further, the Court notes that the parties have been in active discussions regarding scheduling of Defendant’s PMK depositions prior to and following the Motion being filed, and Defendant provides evidence that PMK depositions are scheduled for September and one of the deponents needs to reschedule due to a medical condition. (See Motion; Opp.; Reply.)

 

Based on the totality of the circumstances, since the deposition has not been completed, the Court GRANTS the motion and orders that Defendant’s PMK be produced within the next 60 days. The Court reminds the parties to cooperate to schedule depositions at a mutually agreeable date and time. Considering that the opposing party has met and conferred to produce the PMK, the Court does not find that imposing sanctions is justified.

 

IV.            Conclusion & Order

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Nina Glassman’s Motion to Compel the Deposition of the Person Most Knowledgeable or Qualified for Defendant is GRANTED. Defendant’s PMK is to be produced within 60 days of this order on a mutually agreed upon date and time.  The request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On October 28, 2025, Plaintiff Rummel Mor Bautista (“Plaintiff”), proceeding pro per, filed the instant action against Defendant Ronald H. Marshall (“Defendant”) and Does 1-20, alleging Breach of Written Retainer and Quantum Meruit causes of action.

 

On June 30, 2026, default was entered against Defendant.

 

On July 1, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion to Strike (the “Motion”).

 

On August 18, 2026, the Court, in pertinent part, accepted the parties’ oral stipulation to vacate default of Defendant and ordered the default entered against Defendant on 6/30/26 to be vacated. (8/18/26 Order.)

 

On August 31, 2026, the Court, on its own motion, continued the hearing on the Motion from September 4, 2026, to September 16, 2026, and provided notice. (8/31/26 Order, Cert. of Service.)

 

On September 2, 2026, Defendant filed and served Notice of Ruling.

 

As of September 14, 2026, no Opposition to the Motion has been filed.

 

II.              Procedural Issues

 

As an initial matter, the Court notes that the Motion was originally filed while Defendant was in default. However, given that the default against Defendant was vacated and set aside prior to the hearing on the instant Motion, the Court proceeds to address the Motion on the merits.

 

III.            Legal Standard

 

California law authorizes a party’s motion to strike matter from an opposing party’s pleading if it is irrelevant, false, or improper. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 435, 436.) Motions may also target pleadings or parts of pleadings that are not filed or drawn in conformity with applicable laws, rules, or orders. (Code Civ. Proc. § 436, subd. (b).)

 

The Code of Civil Procedure also authorizes the Court to act on its own initiative to strike matters, empowering the Court to enter orders striking matter “at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 436.)

 

Finally, Code of Civil Procedure section 435.5 requires that “[b]efore filing a motion to strike pursuant to this chapter, the moving party shall meet and confer in person or by telephone with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that resolves the objections to be raised in the motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 435.5, subd. (a), emphasis added.)

 

IV.            Discussion

 

A.    Meet and Confer

 

In conjunction with the Motion, Defendant filed a meet and confer declaration, stating that defense counsel and Plaintiff emailed regarding the issues raised in the Motion to no avail. (Motion, DiRe Decl., ¶¶ 1-5.)

 

The Court finds the meet and confer requirement is not satisfied. However, an insufficient meet and confer process is not grounds to grant or deny a motion to strike.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a)(4).) 

 

B.    Motion to Strike

 

Defendant moves to strike paragraphs 9 and 21 in the Complaint and the prayer for attorneys’ fees in the Complaint on the grounds that the subject retainer agreement does not contain a fee-shifting clause or provision for an award of attorney fees, and Code of Civil Procedure section 1717 is also thus inapplicable. (Motion, pp. 1-2, 5-8.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1021 provides: “Except as attorney’s fees are specifically provided for by statute, the measure and mode of compensation of attorneys and counselors at law is left to the agreement, express or implied, of the parties; but parties to actions or proceedings are entitled to their costs, as hereinafter provided.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1021.) 

 

Civil Code section 1717 provides, in pertinent part : “In any action on a contract, where the contract specifically provides that attorney’s fees and costs, which are incurred to enforce that contract, shall be awarded either to one of the parties or to the prevailing party, then the party who is determined to be the party prevailing on the contract, whether he or she is the party specified in the contract or not, shall be entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees in addition to other costs…”

 

Here, the Complaint states that “[p]ursuant to the terms of the Retainer Agreement and Code of Civil Procedure Section 1717, Plaintiff is entitled to recover reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs incurred in bringing this action.” (See Compl., p. 4.) However, the subject Retainer Agreement attached to the Complaint does not contain terms stating that the prevailing party in a legal proceeding is entitled to an award of reasonable attorney’s fees. (Compl., Exh. A.) The Court may consider exhibits attached to the Complaint in making a determination regarding the face of a pleading. (See e.g., Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91.)

 

The Court finds that the Complaint, inclusive of its attached exhibits, fails to contain sufficient facts to support the claim for prevailing party attorneys’ fees and thus the attorneys’ fees request constitutes improper matter. (See Code Civ. Proc. §§ 435, 436.) Indeed, the subject retainer agreement between the parties is attached to the Complaint as Exhibit A and does not contain terms regarding prevailing party attorneys’ fees, which thus demonstrates that Code of Civil Procedure sections 1021 and 1717 do not permit the award of prevailing party attorneys’ fees in this matter absent a specific provision by statute. However, Plaintiff’s Complaint fails to identify a statute permitting the award of prevailing party attorneys’ fees besides Civil Code section 1717.

 

Notably, Plaintiff did not file an Opposition to the instant Motion.

 

As such, the Court GRANTS Defendant’s unopposed Motion and strikes the request for prevailing party attorneys’ fees in paragraphs 9 and 21 in the Complaint and in the prayer for relief.

 

V.              Conclusion & Order

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Ronald H. Marshall’s unopposed Motion to Strike is GRANTED as to the request to strike prevailing party attorneys’ fees references and requests in paragraphs 9 and 21 in the Complaint and in the prayer for relief.

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice.
