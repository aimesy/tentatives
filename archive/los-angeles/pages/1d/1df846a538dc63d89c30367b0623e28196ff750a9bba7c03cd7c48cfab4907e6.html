PLAINTIFF’S motion for judgment on the pleadings

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.      

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion.  No opposition was filed.   

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

            The court grants Plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.’s (“Plaintiff”) request for judicial notice of the Complaint, Plaintiff’s motion to deem request for admissions admitted, and the court’s order deeming Plaintiff’s request for admissions as admitted.

 

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves the court for a Judgment on the Pleadings against defendant Oscar J Melendez (“Defendant”) on the ground that the Complaint and Defendant’s responsive pleading entitle Plaintiff to a judgment as a matter of law.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion for judgment on the pleadings because the Complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action for breach of contract against Defendant and Defendant’s Answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense to the Complaint.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(1)(A).)  

The elements of a breach of contract cause of action are: (1) existence of contract; (2) plaintiffs’ performance or excuse for nonperformance; (3) defendants’ breach (or anticipatory breach); and (4) resulting damage. (Wall Street Network, Ltd. v. N. Y. Times Co. (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1171, 1178.) 

Plaintiff has alleged in its Complaint that (1) Plaintiff entered into a personal loan agreement with Defendant on October 6, 2023, agreeing to loan Defendant $45,000.00, (2) Plaintiff has performed all obligations to Defendant except those obligations Plaintiff was prevented or excused from performing, (3) on July 28, 2024, Defendant breached the agreement by failing to remit any further payment on the account, and (4) as a result of Defendant’s breach, Plaintiff suffered damages in the amount of $46,400.85.  (Compl., ¶¶ BC-1, BC-2, BC-3, BC-4; Ex. A.)  

Defendant’s Answer alleges that Defendant admits that all of the statements of the Complaint are true except that Defendant reached out to Plaintiff in March of 2024 and asked for a stop payment until January/February of 2025, but Defendant did not hear back until he received the summons.  (Answer, ¶ 3.b.(1).)  Thus, Defendant’s Answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense to the breach of contract cause of action.

Plaintiff argues that there are sufficient facts stated for Plaintiff’s causes of action for Breach of Contract (Implied in Fact), Money Lent/Money Paid, Open Book Account, and Account Stated.  The court denies Plaintiff’s motion for judgment on the pleadings as to those causes of action because Plaintiff has not alleged those causes of action in the Complaint.  Plaintiff only alleges a cause of action for breach of contract. (Compl., pp. 2 ¶ 8, 3.)

 

 

 

ORDER

            The court grants plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.’s motion for judgment on the pleadings without leave to amend as to the first and only cause of action in the Complaint for breach of contract.

The court will sign and enter judgment in favor of plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. in the form lodged with the court by Plaintiff on December 23, 2025, as modified by the court.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (h)(3).)  The court has stricken the award of $495 in costs from the proposed judgment.  Plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. may claim costs by serving and filing a memorandum of costs pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(a)(1). 

The court orders that (1) the Final Status Conference set for March 10, 2028, and (2) the Non-Jury Trial set for March 22, 2028, are vacated.

            The court orders plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. to give notice of this ruling and notice of entry of judgment. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[tentative] Order RE:

 

motion to be relieved as counsel for plaintiff

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff’s Counsel, Larry Chae of Strategic Legal Practices, APC           

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

Motion to be Relieved as Counsel for Plaintiff

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion.  No opposition papers were filed.

DISCUSSION

Larry Chae on behalf of Strategic Legal Practices, APC (“Plaintiff’s Counsel”) moves to be relieved as counsel for plaintiff Antonio Aguilar (“Plaintiff”) in this action.

“The question of granting or denying an application of an attorney to withdraw as counsel (Code Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. 2) is one which lies within the sound discretion of the trial court ‘having in mind whether such withdrawal might work an injustice in the handling of the case.’”¿ (People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 406 [internal quotations omitted].)¿ The court should also consider whether the attorney’s “withdrawal can be accomplished without undue prejudice to the client’s interests.”¿ (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

For a motion to be relieved as counsel under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2), California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) the proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-053)).¿¿¿¿¿ 

The court finds that Plaintiff’s Counsel (1) has served Plaintiff with the moving papers filed in connection with this motion (i) at Plaintiff’s last known address, which Plaintiff’s Counsel has been unable to confirm is current or locate a more current address after making reasonable efforts to do so, including by mailing the motion papers to Plaintiff’s last known address, return receipt requested and (ii) by electronic service, directed to the email used to communicate during this action, and (2) has served defendant General Motors, LLC with the moving papers filed in connection with this motion by email.  (MC-052, ¶¶ 3, subds. (a)(2), (b)(2)(a); June 1, 2026 Proof of Service.)  The court also finds that Plaintiff’s Counsel has shown sufficient reasons why the motion should be granted, and why Plaintiff’s Counsel has brought the motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) instead of filing a consent under section 284, subdivision (1).  (MC-052, ¶ 2.)

The court therefore grants Plaintiff’s Counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff.

Larry Chae of Strategic Legal Practices, APC will be relieved as counsel of record for plaintiff Antonio Aguilar effective upon the filing of the proof of service of the signed “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil” on the client.

The court orders Larry Chae of Strategic Legal Practices, APC (1) to give notice of this ruling and the signed “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil” to plaintiff Antonio Aguilar, and to all other parties who have appeared in this action, and (2) to file a proof of service of the notice of this ruling and the signed “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil” on plaintiff Antonio Aguilar and all other parties who have appeared in this action no later than July 30, 2026.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[tentative] Order RE:

 

(1)   defendant FCA US LLC’s demurrer to PLAINTIFF’S complaint

(2)   defendant FCA US LLC’s motion to strike punitive damages CLAIM from PLAINTIFF’S complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant FCA US LLC       

 

RESPONDING PARTIES:    Plaintiff Michael D Arnett

(1)   Demurrer to Complaint

(2)   Motion to Strike Punitive Damages Claim from Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with the demurrer and motion to strike.

 

BACKGROUND

Plaintiff Michael D Arnett (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against defendants FCA US LLC (“Defendant”) and Victorville Motors Chrysler Jeep Dodge Ram Fiat on June 30, 2025.

Defendant now moves the court for an order (1) sustaining its demurrer to Plaintiff’s first, second, third, fourth, and sixth causes of action on the ground that the causes of action are time-barred by the applicable statutes of limitations, (2) sustaining its demurrer to the third cause of action on the ground that it fails to state facts sufficient to support an additional and distinct claim for breach of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act under Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (a)(3), (3) sustaining its demurrer to the sixth cause of action on the ground that it fails to allege facts sufficient to state a claim for fraudulent inducement – concealment with the requisite particularity, and (4) striking from the Complaint Plaintiff’s prayer for punitive damages.  

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

            The court denies Defendant’s request for judicial notice of Exhibits 1 through 5.

DEMURRER

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the first, second, third, and sixth causes of action on the ground that the causes of action are time-barred by the applicable statutes of limitations because Plaintiff alleges that he discovered Defendant’s wrongful conduct shortly before the filing of the complaint (Compl. ¶ 37.) (Geneva Towers Ltd. Partnership v. City of San Francisco (2003) 29 Cal.4th 769, 781 [“In order for the bar of the statute of limitations to be raised by demurrer, the defect must clearly and affirmatively appear on the face of the complaint; it is not enough that the complaint shows that the action may be barred”].)

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the third cause of action on the ground that the Complaint fails to allege sufficient facts to support a claim because Plaintiff alleges that Defendant failed to make available to its authorized service and repair facilities sufficient service literature and replacement parts to effect repairs during the express warranty period. (Compl., ¶ 52.) (Civ. Code § 1793.2, subd. (a)(3) [“Every manufacturer of consumer goods sold in this state and for which the manufacturer has made an express warranty shall … [m]ake available to authorized service and repair facilities sufficient service literature and replacement parts to effect repairs during the express warranty period”].)

The court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the fourth cause of action for breach of the implied warranty of merchantability because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action because the cause of action is barred by the statute of limitations since (1) “[t]he statute of limitations for breaches of the implied warranty of merchantability is four years” after the accrual of the cause of action; (2) a cause of action accrues when the breach occurs, regardless of the lack of knowledge of the breach, which “occurs when tender of delivery is made, except that where a warranty explicitly extends to future performance of the goods and discovery of the breach must await the time of such performance the cause of action accrues when the breach is or should have been discovered[;]” (3) an implied warranty “is not a warranty that explicitly extends to future performance of the goods[,]’” such that the statute of limitations occurs upon tender of delivery and not “when the breach is or should have been discovered[;]” and (4) Plaintiff has alleged that they entered into a warranty contract with Defendant on September 14, 2019, for the subject vehicle (Compl., ¶ 7), which appears to allege the date of delivery thereof, but they filed this action more than four years later, on June 30, 2025.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Com. Code, § 2725, subds. (1), (2); Mexia v. Rinker Boat Co., Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1297, 1305-1306 [“California courts have held that the statute of limitations for an action for breach of warranty under the Song-Beverly Act is governed by the same statute of limitations for warranties arising under the Uniform Commercial Code: section 2725 of the Uniform Commercial Code”]; Cardinal Health 301, Inc. v. Tyco Electronics Corp. (2008) 169 Cal.App.4th 116, 143.)  

The court sustains the demurrer to the sixth cause of action for fraudulent inducement – concealment because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiff has not alleged the element of Defendant’s duty to disclose the engine defect since they have not alleged facts establishing a transactional relationship between themselves and Defendant, including because Plaintiff has not alleged facts establishing that they purchased the vehicle directly from Defendant or its agent and that, during the course of those direct dealings, Defendant concealed facts regarding the engine defect from Plaintiffs.[1]  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 40-41 [circumstances imposing a duty to disclose “presuppose a preexisting relationship between the parties” which “must necessarily arise from direct dealings between the plaintiff and the defendant; it cannot arise between the defendant and the public at large”].)

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  The court finds that Plaintiff has not articulated the facts that he could allege to render his fraud cause of action against Defendant sufficient and therefore sustains the demurrer without leave to amend.  

MOTION TO STRIKE

Defendant moves the court for an order striking the prayer for punitive damages. (Compl. Prayer, ¶ (e).)

The court grants Defendant’s motion to strike because Plaintiff has not alleged facts establishing that Defendant is guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).)  The court finds that Plaintiff has not articulated the facts that he could allege to render his prayer for punitive damages sufficient and therefore grants the motion to strike without leave to amend.

 

ORDER

            The court overrules defendant FCA US LLC’s demurrer to the first, second, and third causes of action.

The court sustains defendant FCA US LLC’s demurrer to plaintiff Michael D Arrnett’s fourth and fifth causes of action without leave to amend.

            The court grants defendant FCA US LLC’s motion to strike plaintiff Michael D Arrnett’s prayer for punitive damages without leave to amend. 

            The court orders defendant FCA US LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
