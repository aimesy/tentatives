Dept. F49

Date: 7/6/26

Case Name: Jaclyn De La Mora vs Kara Ellen Savely; Roland Savely; Leigh Madonna Requena; Anthony Requena; and Does 1-25

Case No. 24CHCV01241

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

JULY 6, 2025

 

MOTION FOR PROTECTIVE ORDER; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 24CHCV01241

 

Motion filed: 6/8/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Jaclyn De La Mora

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendants Kara Ellen Savely and Roland Savely

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court quashing or limiting subpoenas for Plaintiff’s medical and billing records issued to Kaiser Permanente and awarding sanctions in the amount of $3,060.00.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The motion is GRANTED. The request for sanctions is GRANTED IN PART.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from personal injuries sustained by Plaintiff Jaclyn De La Mora in a motor vehicle accident allegedly caused by Defendants. On April 5, 2024, Plaintiff filed the Complaint against Defendants Kara Ellen Savely, Roland Savely, Leigh Madonna Requena, Anthony Requena, and Does 1 through 25, alleging a single cause of action for motor vehicle negligence. On August 30, 2024, the Savelys filed an Answer. On June 26, 2025, the Requenas filed an Answer.

 

On June 30, 2025, the Requenas filed a Cross-Complaint against the Savelys and Roes 1 through 25, alleging apportionment of fault, indemnification, and declaratory relief. On July 2, 2025, the Savelys concurrently filed an Answer to the Cross-Complaint and a Cross-Complaint against the Requenas and Roes 1 through 25, seeking implied and equitable indemnity, contribution, and declaratory relief.

 

On June 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Protective Order (the “Motion”). On June 30, 2026, the Savelys filed an Opposition. On July 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

ANALYSIS

 

“If a subpoena requires the … production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things … at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by [a party], … may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).) Persons who may bring a motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1 include “[a] consumer described in Section 1985.3.” (Id., subd. (b)(3).)

 

A “consumer” includes “any individual … [who] has transacted business with, or has used the services of a subpoenaed witness. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1985.3, subd. (a)(2).) “Any consumer whose personal records are sought by a subpoena duces tecum and who is a party to the civil action in which this subpoena duces tecum is served may, prior to the date for production, bring a motion under Section 1987.1 to quash or modify the subpoena duces tecum.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1985.3, subd. (g).)

 

“The court shall limit the scope of discovery if it determines that the burden, expense, or intrusiveness of that discovery clearly outweighs the likelihood that the information sought will lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.020, subd. (a).) “The court shall make this determination pursuant to a motion for protective order by a party or other affected person.” (Ibid.)

 

A.    Preliminary Considerations

 

(1)   Request for Continuance

 

As a preliminary matter, the Savelys argue that the Motion was not properly served because it was served only on their attorney, Jeffrey Lerman (“Lerman”) and not on his assistant, resulting in the Motion not being properly calendared. (Opp’n, at p. 6.)

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1010, “[n]otices and other papers may be served upon the party or attorney.” Where a party to an action is represented by counsel, “the service of papers, when required, must be upon the attorney instead of the party, except service of subpoenas, of writs, and other process issued in the suit, and of papers to bring the party into contempt.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1015.)

 

Plaintiff’s Proof of Service indicates that the Motion was served on Lerman via email on June 5, 2026. (Mot., Proof of Service.) The Savelys provide no authority requiring service on their attorney’s assistant. The Court accordingly finds that this service complied with statutory requirements.

 

(2)   Legal Basis for Motion

 

The Savelys further contend that the Motion is improper because it is made under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.420. (Opp’n, at p. 4.) The Savelys assert that the subpoenas issued to Kaiser were issued independently of a deposition and that Plaintiff’s remedy is accordingly not a protective order, but to move to quash or modify the subpoenas pursuant to section 1987.1, which Plaintiff has not done. (Ibid.)

 

While it is true that section 2025.420 concerns subpoenas for the production of documents at oral deposition and no deposition was noticed here, the Motion raises Code of Civil Procedure sections 1987.1 and 2017.020 as alternate grounds for relief. (Mot., at pp. 6-7.) The relief Plaintiff requests is that the Court either quash the subpoenas or enter an order modifying them to limit production to Plaintiff’s specific injuries. (Mot., at p. 4.) The Savelys do not dispute that a limiting order is available pursuant to a section 2017.020 motion for protective order. The Court accordingly finds that Plaintiff requests appropriate relief under sections 1987.1 and 2017.020.

           

B.     Meet and Confer

 

A motion for protective order pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2017.020 must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. “A meet and confer declaration in support of a motion shall state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a).)

 

Plaintiff’s counsel attests that, on May 19, 2026, the day after he received copies of the subpoenas for Plaintiff’s medical records, he emailed a meet and confer letter to Lerman concerning the overbreadth of the requests. (Haddad Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) After not receiving a response to the letter, Plaintiff’s counsel called Lerman and left a voicemail detailing Plaintiff’s objection to the subpoenas. (Id., ¶ 5.) Having received no response to the voicemail and with an impending production date of June 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion. (Id., ¶¶ 5-6.)

 

Given the privacy interests at stake (see infra) and the abbreviated timeline for Kaiser’s compliance with the deposition subpoenas, the Court finds the foregoing meet and confer effort satisfactory.

 

C.    Motion for Quash or Modify Subpoenas

 

The Savelys issued two subpoenas to Kaiser Permanente’s medical and billing divisions on May 14, 2026. (Haddad Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.) The subpoenas request “[a]ny and all medical documents, papers and digital records, billing, pertaining to the care, treatment and examination of Jaclyn De La Mora, including, but not limited to all office, emergency room, inpatient and outpatient charts and records either hard copy or electronic and/or faxed, from April 14, 2014.” (Id., Exh. A.)

 

Plaintiff contends that the subpoenas are overbroad and violate Plaintiff’s right to medical privacy. Plaintiff seeks an order limiting the production of her medical records to the following injuries alleged by Plaintiff: (1) cervical spine sprain/strain; (2) thoracic spine strain/sprain; (3) lumbar spine sprain/strain; (4) right shoulder sprain/strain; (5) right knee sprain/strain; and (6) right hip pain (interstitial tear in mid-posterior part traverse acetabular ligament). (Mot., at p. 4.)

 

(1)   Considerations Balance in Favor of Preserving Plaintiff’s Privacy Interest

 

The California Constitution recognizes that individuals have an inalienable right to privacy. (Cal. Const., Art. 1, § 1.) That right, however, must be balanced against a litigant’s broad right to discovery of non-privileged matters that are relevant to the subject matter of the litigation. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.010.)

 

“A party asserting a privacy interest must establish a legally protected privacy interest, an objectively reasonable expectation of privacy in the given circumstances, and a threatened intrusion that is serious. [Citation.]” (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 552.) Once the party asserting privacy meets these threshold requirements, the Court must balance “the legitimate and countervailing interests” served by disclosure against feasible alternatives or protective measures that would serve the same interests. (Ibid.)

 

While Courts recognize that a plaintiff may implicitly waive, in part, the right to privacy by filing a lawsuit, the scope of waiver must be narrowly tailored to minimize the privacy intrusion and the information sought must be directly relevant to the litigation. (Britt v. Superior Court (1978) 20 Cal.3d 844, 858; Davis v. Superior Court (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1008, 1014.)

 

A patient has an essential privacy interest in his or her personal medical information. (Hill v. National Collegiate Athletic Assn. (1994) 7 Cal.4th 1, 41 [recognizing an interest in “limiting disclosure of confidential information about the bodily condition” to avoid embarrassment].) While Plaintiff’s initiation of this action operates as a partial waiver, the waiver extends only to information that is directly relevant to the action. (Britt, supra, 20 Cal.4th, at p. 858.)

 

Here, the subpoenas request “any and all” medical and billing records from April 14, 2014 until the present. The Court finds a request for 12 years of unrestricted medical and medical billing information to be a serious intrusion. Although Plaintiff concedes that evidence of preexisting conditions may be relevant to damages, Plaintiff argues that unlimited access to 12 years of records would encompass treatment involving sensitive matters wholly unrelated to the present claims. (Mot., at p. 7.) The Court agrees that the request is not sufficiently tailored to minimize the intrusion into Plaintiff’s medical and billing history.

 

In Opposition, the Savelys argue that limiting the subpoenas to the specific body parts requested by Plaintiff would exclude relevant records of sympathetic injuries. (Opp’n, at p. 5.) The Savelys contend that one injury can significantly impact a person’s entire musculoskeletal system. (Ibid.) This contention, however, without any showing of direct relevance, is not enough to justify wholesale disclosure of records. (Davis, supra, 7 Cal.App.4th, at p. 1017 [“Mere speculation as to the possibility that some portion of the records might be relevant to some substantive issue does not suffice.”].)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the Motion.

 

D.    Monetary Sanctions

 

Plaintiff moves for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2017.020 and 2025.420. (Mot., at p. 11.) Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.420, subdivision (h) permits sanctions against a party who unsuccessfully opposes a motion for protective order in the context of an oral deposition. There is no deposition notice at issue in the present Motion, and section 2025.420 is accordingly inapplicable.

 

Pursuant to section 2017.020, subdivision (b), “the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion for a protective order, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanctions acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanctions unjust.”

 

Plaintiff requests sanctions against the Savelys and their counsel of record in the amount of $3,060.00, calculated as six hours expended on research, meet and confer efforts, preparing the motion and supporting papers, and attending the hearing, at and hourly rate of $500, plus a $60 filing fee. (Haddad Decl., ¶ 7.)

 

While the Opposition avers that the Savelys’ counsel was on vacation and without email service from June 5, 2026 until June 15, 2026, hinting that a more thorough opposing argument would have been presented had he had more time to prepare, (Opp’n, at p. 6), the Court does not find that this circumstance justifies the service of a facially overbroad and intrusive deposition subpoena. Sanctions are accordingly warranted.

 

The Court finds Plaintiff’s counsel’s hourly rate to be reasonable. However, given that the Motion was a straightforward motion to limit the scope of the subpoenas at issue, the Court determines the total time reasonably expended in connection with the Motion to be three hours. At an hourly rate of $500, the Court finds that the total reasonable attorney fees in connection with the Motion are $1,560.00 (3 hours x $500, plus the $60 filing fee).

 

The request for monetary sanctions is thus GRANTED IN PART.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court GRANTS Plaintiff Jaclyn De La Mora’s Motion for Protective Order. The depositions subpoenas issued to Kaiser Permanente are limited to the following conditions: (1) cervical spine sprain/strain; (2) thoracic spine strain/sprain; (3) lumbar spine sprain/strain; (4) right shoulder sprain/strain; (5) right knee sprain/strain; and (6) right hip pain (interstitial tear in mid-posterior part traverse acetabular ligament).

 

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is GRANTED IN PART. Defendants Kara Ellen Savely and Roland Savely and their attorney of record are ordered to pay $1,560.00, jointly and severally, to Plaintiff’s counsel within 30 days.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 24CHCV04821    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 7/6/26

Case Name: Gor Muradyan v. General Motors LLC, and Does 1-100

Case No. 24CHCV04821

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

JULY 6, 2026

 

MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT, OR ALTERNATIVELY, FOR SUMMARY ADJUDICATION

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 24CHCV04821

 

Motion filed: 10/23/25

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant General Motors LLC

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Gor Muradyan

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court granting Defendant’s motion for summary judgment, or in the alternative, summary adjudication on all causes of action of the Complaint.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The Motion is CONTINUED.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from the alleged failure of Defendant General Motors LLC (“Defendant”) to conform a 2021 GMC Yukon XL (the “Subject Vehicle”) purchased by Plaintiff Gor Muradyan (“Plaintiff”) to warranty specifications.

 

On December 31, 2024, Plaintiff filed the Complaint against Defendant and Does 1 through 100, alleging breach of express and implied warranties pursuant to the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act and breach of express and implied warranties pursuant to the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act.

 

On March 24, 2025, Defendant filed its Answer.

 

On October 23, 2025, Defendant filed the instant Motion for Summary Judgment, or Alternatively, for Summary Adjudication (the “Motion”). On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On February 6, 2026, Defendant filed a reply. On June 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed a supplemental brief regarding developments in discovery. On June 25, 2026, Defendant filed a supplemental reply.

 

ANALYSIS

 

“A party may move for summary judgment in an action or proceeding if it is contended that the action has no merit or that there is no defense to the action or proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c(a)(1).)

 

“The motion for summary judgment shall be granted if all the papers submitted show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law. In determining if the papers show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact, the court shall consider all of the evidence set forth in the papers, except the evidence to which objections have been made and sustained by the court, and all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence, except summary judgment shall not be granted by the court based on inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence if contradicted by other inferences or evidence that raise a triable issue as to any material fact.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (c).)¿ 

 

A party may alternatively seek summary adjudication on whether a cause of action, affirmative defense, or punitive damages claim has merit or whether a defendant owed a duty to a plaintiff. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (f)(1).) “A motion for summary adjudication … shall proceed in all procedural respects as a motion for summary judgment.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (f)(2).)

 

The moving party “bears an initial burden of production to make a prima facie showing of the nonexistence of any triable issue of material fact.” (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850.) To meet that burden, the moving party must present evidence sufficient to show they are entitled to summary judgment as a matter of law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subds. (c) & (f)(2); Monticello Ins. Co. v. Essex Ins. Co. (2008) 162 Cal.App.4th 1376, 1385.)

 

A moving defendant “bears the burden of showing the court that the plaintiff ‘has not established, and cannot reasonably expect to establish,’ the elements of his or her cause of action.” (State v. Allstate Insurance Co. (2009) 45 Cal.4th 1008, 1017, internal quotations omitted.)

 

The pleadings serve as the “outer measure of materiality” in a summary judgment motion, delimiting the scope of the issues that may be triable. (FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima (1991), 231 Cal.App.3d 367, 381.) A moving defendant accordingly need only negate the theories of liability alleged in the complaint. (Nativi v. Deutsche Bank National Trust Co. (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 261, 290.)

 

A.    Preliminary Considerations – Request for Continuance

 

As a preliminary matter, Plaintiff asserts that the Motion should be denied as procedurally improper or continued because it was prematurely brought while discovery was incomplete. (Opp’n, at pp. 3-4.)  

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 437c, subdivision (h), the court must deny or continue a motion for summary judgment to permit further discovery “[i]f it appears from the affidavits submitted in opposition … that facts essential to justify opposition may exist but cannot, for reasons stated, be presented.”

 

“The decision to grant or deny a continuance request under section 437c, subdivision (h), is vested in the trial court’s discretion.” (Braganza v. Albertson’s LLC (2021) 67 Cal.App.5th 144, 152.) Such requests are to be liberally granted. (Ibid.) “[C]ontinuances under section 437c, subdivision (h), are virtually mandated upon a good faith showing by affidavit that a continuance is needed to obtain facts essential to justify opposition to the motion. [Citations.]” (Ibid., internal quotations omitted.) An affidavit under section 437c, subdivision (h) must show that “(1) the facts to be obtained are essential to opposing the motion; (2) there is reason to believe such facts may exist; and (3) the reasons why additional time is needed to obtain [or discover] these facts. [Citations.]” (Ibid., internal quotations omitted.) “A good faith showing that further discovery is needed to oppose summary judgment requires some justification for why such discovery could not have been completed sooner.” (Cooksey v. Alexakis (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 246, 257.) Lack of diligence may be a factor justifying refusal to grant a continuance under section 437c, subdivision (h). (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff’s counsel attests that the discovery ordered by the Court on January 22, 2026 bears directly on Defendant’s knowledge of recurrent defects in the Subject Vehicle, “whether the subject vehicle was repaired within a reasonable number of opportunities, and whether defects were improperly characterized as maintenance or recall-only issues. (Markarian Decl., ¶ 24.) On January 22, 2026, the Court ordered Defendant to serve supplemental responses to Plaintiff’s requests for production of documents. (Minute Order, Jan. 22, 2026, at p. 8.) Among the documents sought by those requests were those related to Defendant’s inspection and repair of the Subject Vehicle and notice of any defects. (Id., at pp. 6-7.) While Defendant served supplemental responses, the Court, in granting Plaintiff’s motion to enforce its January 22, 2026 Order, found the supplemental responses to be deficient. (Minute Order, May 22, 2026, at pp. 3-6.) Plaintiff’s counsel states that, despite the Court’s order, the same deficiencies remain in Defendant’s further supplemental responses. (Markarian Suppl. Decl., ¶¶ 12-13, Exh. “G.”) The Court notes that there is a pending motion to compel further responses to these same requests for production that were the subject of the Court’s January 22, 2026 Order. The motion is scheduled for hearing on December 15, 2026.

 

Plaintiff’s counsel also attests that he has been unable to complete a deposition of Defendant’s person most knowledgeable (“PMK”), whose deposition the Court compelled on March 20, 2026. (Markarian Suppl. Decl., ¶¶ 5-9; Minute Order, Mar. 20, 2026, at pp. 6-7.) Plaintiff’s counsel indicates that the deposition of Defendant’s PMK was suspended after Defendant’s counsel repeatedly instructed the witness not to answer questions regarding Defendant’s evaluation of the Subject Vehicle for repurchase. (Markarian Suppl. Decl., ¶ 9.) The Court notes that there is also a pending motion to compel the testimony of Defendant’s PMK.

 

In reply, Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s request for a denial or continuance based on the inability to depose Defendant’s PMK is meritless because Defendant waited nearly a year after filing the Complaint to notice the PMK deposition, failing to demonstrate diligence in seeking the deposition. (Reply, at p. 7.) As to the PMK deposition, the Court agrees that Plaintiff’s supporting declaration does not adequately establish why it could not be completed sooner.

 

The Discovery Act permits a plaintiff to serve a deposition notice on the defendant without leave of court on any date at least 20 days after the defendant’s appearance or service of the summons. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.210.) This action was filed on December 31, 2024. While it is unclear when the Summons and Complaint were served, Defendant answered the Complaint on March 24, 2025. Plaintiff could accordingly have served a notice of deposition as early as April 2025, and Plaintiff’s counsel does not explain why Plaintiff did not seek to depose Defendant’s PMK before December 29, 2025, when he initiated meet and confer regarding deposition scheduling. (See Markarian Decl., ¶ 17.) Given that Plaintiff served other discovery in May 2025, the failure to explain the delay in seeking to depose Defendant is notable. Failure to complete the PMK deposition is thus not an adequate ground to deny or continue the Motion.

 

As to document production, however, Plaintiff shows that he diligently sought production of documents concerning the Subject Vehicle’s repair history and Defendant’s knowledge of defects with the Subject Vehicle. Plaintiff’s counsel attests that Plaintiff served his Request for Production of Documents (Set One) on Defendant on May 25, 2025, shortly after Defendant answered the Complaint. (Markarian Decl., ¶ 3.) Defendant served responses on July 22, 2025, and Plaintiff promptly brought a motion to compel further responses on August 29, 2025. (Id., ¶ 6.) Defendant does not dispute Plaintiff’s diligence in seeking document production.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff that documents concerning the Subject Vehicle’s repair history and Defendant’s notice and characterization of any defects with the Subject Vehicle are relevant to establishing whether a reasonable number of repair attempts were made. Given that the basis of the Motion is that Plaintiff cannot establish that a sufficient number of repair attempts were made, (Mot., at p. 6), such evidence would be essential to opposing the Motion. Although Defendant argues that no additional evidence exists, the standard under section 437c, subdivision (h) is not whether facts actually exist, but whether there is reason to believe they may exist. The Court finds that Plaintiff’s demonstration of conduct preventing him from obtaining complete discovery responses is a sufficient basis to believe additional evidence may exist.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court CONTINUES the Motion.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court CONTINUES Defendant General Motors’s motion for summary judgment.
