Case Number: 22NWCV00876    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: L

JOHN A ROMO vs JUAN SOTO, et al.

CASE NO.: 22NWCV00876

HEARING: September 17, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#2

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Juan Soto’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions (Dismissal with Prejudice) for Failure to Obery Court Order Compelling Discovery is GRANTED, in part.

 

Defendant is awarded sanctions in the amount of $500.00 against Plaintiff, payable within 60 days.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for trademark infringement. On September 26, 2022, Plaintiff John A. Romo (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Juan Soto, Jerry Gonzales, Eddie Soto, Henry Sotelo, Don Jackson, Jaime Silbas, and Does 1 to 50.

 

On February 1, 2023, Defendants Don Jackson and Jaime Silbas were dismissed with prejudice.

 

On August 8, 2023, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC). The FAC alleges that Defendants Juan Soto, Jerry Gonzales, Eddie Soto, and Henry Sotelo infringed on Plaintiff’s trademark, “New Life Ela Car Club.” The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Trademark Infringement, 2) Trademark Infringement under the Lanham Act, 3) Unfair Competition under the Lanham Act, 4) Unfair Competition under California Business and Professions Code § 17200, 5) False Description, 6) Common Law Injury to Business Reputation, 7) Aiding and Abetting Trademark Infringement, and 8) Declaratory Relief.

 

On February 11, 2026, the Court granted Defendant Juan Soto’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Responses to 1) Requests for Production of Documents (Set One), 2) Special Interrogatories (Set One), and 3) Form Interrogatories (Set One). The Court also granted Defendant Juan Soto’s Motion for Order Deeming Truth of Facts and Genuineness of Documents. (2/11/26 Minute Order.)

 

Defendant Juan Soto (“Defendant”) now moves for terminating sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030, based on Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the Court’s February 11, 2026 order compelling discovery responses.

 

The Court notes that this Motion was continued on the Court’s own motion from August 20, 2026 to September 17, 2026.

 

No opposition has been filed as of September 16, 2026.


Legal Standard

 

California Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 permits courts to impose monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating sanctions against anyone engaging in conduct that constitutes a misuse of the discovery process. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.030, subs. (a)-(d).) Section 2023.010 provides a non-exhaustive list of conduct that is considered misuse of the discovery process, including, as relevant here, failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery and disobeying a court order to provide discovery. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.010, subdivisions (d), (g).)

 

In determining whether to impose terminating sanctions, trial courts must consider the totality of the circumstances, including whether the actions were willful; the detriment to the propounding party; and the number of informal and formal attempts to obtain the discovery. (Lang v. Hochman (2000) 77 Cal.App,4th 1225, 1246.) The discovery statutes evince an incremental approach to discovery sanctions, starting with monetary sanctions and ending with the ultimate sanction of termination. “Discovery sanctions should be appropriate to the dereliction, and should not exceed that which is required to protect the interests of the party entitled to but denied discovery.” (Laguna Auto Body v. Farmers Ins. Exchange, 231 Cal.App.3d at p. 487.) If a lesser sanction fails to curb misuse, a greater sanction is warranted; continuing misuses of the discovery process warrant incrementally harsher sanctions until the sanction is reached that will curb the abuse. (Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc. (2009) 174 Cal. App. 4th 967, 992.)

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves for terminating sanctions—specifically, to dismiss the action—based on Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the Court’s February 11, 2026 order compelling discovery responses. Pursuant to the Court’s order, Plaintiff was required to provide objection-free responses by a deadline of March 3, 2026. (2/11/26 Minute Order.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s failure to serve responses constitutes misuse of the discovery responses warranting terminating sanctions.

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s failure to provide responses has deprived Defendant of a fair opportunity to defend. Defendant argues that, at the time of this Motion was filed on March 9, 2026, trial was set for April 13, 2026. The Court notes that since this Motion was filed, trial has been continued to October 7, 2026. (See 5/19/26 Minute Order.)

 

Defendant also seeks monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,200.00.

 

No opposition has been filed.

 

The Court finds terminating sanctions to dismiss the case inappropriate at this time. Failure to obey one court order does not warrant terminating sanctions. Defendant fails to detail the efforts made to obtain Plaintiff’s compliance since the deadline to produce the discovery responses. Defense counsel only states that “[Plaintiff was] requested several times to serve discovery responses to litigate this case” and that “Plaintiff[] refused to comply with recovery several times were sanctioned and still were unable to comply with the judge’s orders. (Suarez Decl., ¶¶ 1-2.)

 

At this time, the Court finds further monetary sanctions and a further order compelling Plaintiff to comply with the Court’s February 11, 2206 order are appropriate.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions (Dismissal with Prejudice) for Failure to Obery Court Order Compelling Discovery is granted, in part.

 

Defendant is awarded reduced sanctions in the amount of $500.00 against Plaintiff, payable within 60 days

Case Number: 23NWCV01053    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: L

PAM CRAMPTON, et al. vs ROSE HILLS MORTUARY, L.P., A BUSINESS ENTITY, et al.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV01053

HEARING: September 17, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#3

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants Rose Hills Company, Jose Galvan, Griselda Cabral, Julie Martinez, and Todd Yasuda’s Motion for Order Authorizing Verification of Grave Vault is denied.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a negligence action. On April 5, 2023, Plaintiffs Pam Crampton and Debbie Shuck (“Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendants Rose Hills Mortuary, L.P., doing business as Rose Hills Memorial Park & Mortuary, PDN, Inc., Jose Galvan, Julie Martinez, Anita Stanchev, Griselda Cabral, Todd H. Yasuda, and Does 1 to 100. The Complaint arises out of Plaintiff’s “for injuries resulting from the movement of their deceased father’s remains, and/or headstone, and the subsequent cover up.” (Compl., ¶ 1.) Specifically, the Complaint alleges that decedent, Paul Crampton, (hereinafter, “Decedent”) was “(1) buried elsewhere at ROSE HILLS; (2) moved at some point in time to a different location at ROSE HILLS without any notice or consent (as evidenced by both the visible damage to his headstone and the fact that his headstone flower urn is now uncentered); (3) not buried at all, and instead was wrongfully cremated without any notice or consent, (4) initially buried then wrongfully disinterred and cremated without any notice or consent or (5) buried with other individuals without any notice or consent. “ (Compl., ¶ 43.)

 

The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Negligence, 2) Negligent Misrepresentation, 3) Intentional Misrepresentation, 4) Concealment, 5) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress, and 6) Breach of Contract.

 

Defendants Rose Hills Company (erroneously sued as Defendant Rose Hills Mortuary, L.P.), Jose Galvan, Griselda Cabral, Julie Martinez, and Todd Yasuda (hereinafter, “Defendants”) now move for an order “authorizing verification of grave vault” pursuant to Health and Safety Code sections 7525 and 7526.

 

No opposition has been filed as of September 16, 2026.


Legal Standard

 

“The remains of a deceased person may be removed from a plot in a cemetery with the consent of the cemetery authority and the written consent of one of the following in the order named:

(a) The surviving spouse.

(b) The surviving children.

(c) The surviving parents.

(d) The surviving brothers or sisters.”

 

(Health & Saf. Code, § 7525.)

 

“If the required consent can not be obtained, permission by the superior court of the county where the cemetery is situated is sufficient.” (Health & Saf. Code, § 7526.)

 

The Court notes that section 7526 was recently amended by A.B. 2331 on July 13, 2026 to state the following: “If the required consent cannot be obtained, permission by the superior court of the county where the cemetery is situated is sufficient. A court shall grant an application upon a showing of good cause.”

 

“[E]ach case involving disinterment must be considered in equity on its own merits.” (Maffei v. Woodlawn Memorial Park (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 119, 124, internal citations and quotations omitted.) “Each case decided under section 7256 must be decided on its own merits based on the particular circumstances involved.” (Id. at p. 125.) “However, there is a well-established presumption against removing the remains of a deceased person, i.e., against disturbing “the repose of the dead.’” (Ibid.) This presumption governs unless the petitioner seeking removal and reinterment presents “strong and convincing evidence showing that it would be unreasonable to refuse to make such a decree.” (Id. at p. 126.)

 

Discussion

 

Defendants request that the Court permit Defendant Rose Hills Company (hereinafter, “Rose Hills”) to “verify that the “5TS Oxford Copper” Vault (“Vault” -- attached as Exhibit A to the Nordschow Declaration), which was used for the interment of Decedent Paul Crampton (“Decedent”) on August 5, 1998 at Medallion Lawn, Lot 523, Grave 1 (the “Subject Grave”).” (Mot., 2:12-15.)

 

Defendants explain that Plaintiffs have refused to authorize Rose Hills to open the Subject Grave to confirm the presence of the Vault. (Nordschow Decl., ¶ 4; Doty Decl., ¶ 2.) Defendants argue that they need to inspect the vault in order to evaluate Plaintiff’s claims against them—specifically, if the Vault is secure and intact, then Plaintiffs’ claims that Defendants moved the Decedent’s remains would be defeated. Defendants imply that that alternative of opening the Vault (not merely digging up and inspecting the Vault), would be dangerous and emotionally disturbing to all parties involved. (Nordschow Decl., ¶ 4.) Defendants assert that Rose Hills has a protocol in place to safely verify that Decedent’s Vault remains in its original location and has not been opened following interment. (Nordschow Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

No opposition has been filed.

 

The Court is not persuaded that it has the authority to order verification of the Vault pursuant to the Defendants’ cited Health and Safety Code sections. A petition under Health and Safety Code sections 7525 and 7526 are generally used to obtain the court’s permission to remove and reinter a decedent’s remains. Those provisions are silent as to whether a court has authority to permit analysis/verification of a grave vault, which does not involve removal of remains.

 

The Court finds that this dispute involves Defendants’ right to certain discovery. Defendants acknowledges that this Motion “also constitutes a motion to compel plaintiffs to cooperate in discovery. “ (Mot., p. 5., fn. 2.) The Court encourages the parties to come to an informal agreement regarding this issue.

 

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion for Order Authorizing Verification of Grave Vault is denied.


Case Number: 23NWCV01111    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: L

MEDLINE MANAGEMENT CORPORATION, A CA CORP vs DAN KLOSS, et al.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV01111

HEARING: September 17, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#4

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant/Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Dan Kloss’ Motion for Trial Preference is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an unlawful detainer case (“UD Action”) regarding real property located at 10700-10712 Sessler Street, South Gate, CA 90280 (“Subject Property”). On April 12, 2023, Plaintiff Medline Management Corporation (“MEDLINE”) filed this action against Defendants Dan Kloss and Ioana Kloss, doing business as CNC Aero Machining.

 

On April 9, 2024, this case was consolidated with a related case titled Dan Kloss vs Jamal Dawood, et al., LASC Case No. 23NWCV02306 (“Title Action”). (4/9/24 Minute Order.). The Title Action was filed on April 12, 2023. A First Amended Complaint (FAC) was filed in that action on April 18, 2024. The FAC alleges that, in 2021, Defendants Jamal Dawood, Christine Armani-Dawood, Quantum Properties LLC, and Does 1 to 100 fraudulently induced KLOSS to grant his one-half interest in the Subject Property via a Grant Deed to Quantum Properties LLC (“Grant Deed”) in order to “safeguard” his interest. (FAC, ¶ 16.) In return, Defendants issued an LLC membership certification transferring a one-half interest in Quantum Properties LLC to Precision Forging Dies, Inc. However, the membership interest was intended to be transferred to KLOSS, not Precision Forging Dies, Inc. Through the action, KLOSS seeks “to cancel and rescind the fraudulently obtained Grant Deed or alternative, to involuntarily dissolve the LLC and have Plaintiff’s one-half interest in the Property transferred back to Plaintiff.” (FAC, ¶ 28.)

 

The Title Action FAC alleges the following causes of action:

 

1.    Cancellation Of Written Instrument (Civ. Code § 3412);

2.    Fraud;

3.    Negligent Misrepresentation;

4.    Breach Of Oral Contract;

5.    Financial Elder Abuse;

6.    Slander Of Title;

7.    Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress;

8.    Unfair Business Practices;

9.    Declaratory Relief; and

10.        Involuntary Dissolution.

 

Defendants Commercial Funding, LLC, Colony Capital Management LLC, Colony Capital LLC, and Shore Pine LLC were later named as Does 1 to 4, respectively.

 

On May 23, 2024, Defendant/Cross-Complainant Quantum Properties LLC (“QUANTUM”) filed a Cross-Complaint in the Title Action against Cross-Defendants Dan Kloss and Ioana Kloss, doing business as CNC Aero Machining, Anca Tiplea, and Roes 1 to 20.

 

On February 25, 2025, QUANTUM filed the First Amended Cross-Complaint (FACC), adding C&C Aerol Machining and Aerospace Machining Company, Precision Forging Dies, Inc. as Cross-Defendants. On May 29, 2025, QUANTUM filed the Second Amended Cross-Complaint.

 

On August 1, 2025, Defendant Commercial Funding, LLC filed a Cross-Complaint in the Title Action.

 

On August 3, 2026, Defendants Colony Capital Management LLC and Colony Capital LLC were dismissed without prejudice from the Title Action FAC.

 

Defendant/Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Dan Kloss (hereinafter, “Kloss”) now moves for a trial preference.

 

Plaintiff Medline Management Corporation, Defendants/Cross-Complainants Quantum Properties, LLC and Commercial Funding, LLC, and Defendants Jamal Dawood, Christine Armani-Dawood, Commercial Funding, LLC, and Shore Pine, LLC (hereinafter, “Opposing Parties”) oppose the Motion.

 

The Court notes that this is a renewed Motion for Trial Preference following the Court’s denial of such Motion without prejudice on July 21, 2026. (7/21/26 Minute Order.)

 

Trial is currently set for June 7, 2027.

 

Legal Standard

 

“On petition of any party over 70, [Code of Civil Procedure] section 36, subdivision (a), provides that the granting of calendar preference is mandatory in some circumstances.” (Fox v. Superior Court (“Fox”) (2018) 21 Cal.App.5th 529, 533.) A court must grant a trial preference under Code Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (a) where the following two factors are present: (1) the party has a substantial interest in the action as a whole; and (2) the health of the party is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudicing the party’s interest in the litigation. (Ibid.)

 

Where a motion for trial preference is granted, the clerk shall set the case for trial not more than 120 days from that date. (Koch-Ash v. Superior Court (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 689, 694.)

 

Discussion

 

Kloss requests a trial preference under Code of Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (a). Kloss is currently 77 years old. (Klass Decl., ¶ 3.) Kloss has a substantial interest in the action as the sole plaintiff in the Title Action. Further,

 

Dr. Sandy Liu, Kloss’ treating medical oncologist, states that Kloss has been diagnosed with metastatic prostate cancer with small cell neuroendocrine transformation, with metastasis to the liver and bone. (Liu Decl., ¶ 5.) Dr. Liu states that Kloss is only able to participate in limited litigation activities—specifically, during periods of increased symptoms following treatment, “his ability to sustain attention, process information, communicate clearly, and tolerate prolonged sitting or questioning is materially reduced.” (Liu Decl., ¶¶ 6-7.) During such periods, Kloss experiences “fatigue, pain, weakness, nausea, difficulty concentrating, and reduced stamina, which impair his ability to remain attentive, communicate with counsel, and tolerate extended questioning or court proceedings.” (Liu Decl., ¶ 9.) Dr. Liu finds that there is a “substantial medical risk” such limitations will become more severe in the next months. (Liu Decl., ¶¶ 8, 10.) Dr. Liu notes that, “[a]lthough Mr. Kloss may presently be able to perform limited litigation-related tasks on days when his symptoms are less severe, this should not be understood to mean that he is medically well or capable of participating normally.” (Liu Decl., ¶ 11.)

 

Kloss offers his own declaration to corroborate these symptoms. (Kloss Decl., ¶¶ 6, 8.) Kloss states that, when his symptoms are more severe, he has “difficulty sustaining the concentration and energy necessary for lengthy discussions with my attorneys, reviewing documents, preparing for testimony, or participating in other prolonged litigation activities.” (Kloss Decl., ¶ 13.) For example, Kloss’ deposition had to be scheduled based on his treatment schedule and ability to testify. (Zuetel Decl., ¶ 12.) Kloss participated in his deposition on July 17, 2026—however, it had to be stopped short because he developed a headache and became tired. (Kloss Decl., ¶ 14; Zuetel Decl., ¶ 13.) Further, Kloss’ counsel has observed that Kloss’ ability to participate varies day by day and that there are occasions when his symptoms have prevented counsel from discussing litigation matters with Kloss. (Zuetel Decl., ¶¶ 15-17.) Kloss’ counsel asserts that, if Kloss’ condition further declines, counsel will be unable to communicate with him, resulting in prejudice to his case. (Zuetel Decl., ¶¶ 18-19.)

 

Based on such evidence, Kloss argues that his health is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudice to his interest in the litigation. Kloss notes that, in analyzing whether to grant a trial preference, the Court need not weigh interests, such as convenience to the parties and scheduling issues.

 

Opposing Parties argue that Kloss’ additional evidence “does not establish the requisite causal connection between Plaintiff's health and the need for an expedited trial within 120 days,” especially where the current trial date is only nine months away. (Opp., 2:13-15.) Kloss concedes that he is presently able to participate, and has participated, in the litigation. Opposing Parties assert that the fact that Kloss requires scheduling accommodations does not warrant a trial preference. Further, Dr. Liu’s prognosis regarding Kloss’ potential future health decline is “speculative and temporally indefinite.” (Opp., 8:6.)

 

Alternatively, if the Court is inclined to grant a trial preference, Opposing Parties request that trial is set on January 15, 2027, the outermost limits of the 120-day period.

 

In reply, Kloss reiterates his arguments. Further, Kloss argues that the Opposing Parties have failed to disprove Dr. Liu’s medical opinion regarding Kloss’ future health decline. Regarding Opposing Parties’ argument that Kloss has been able to participate in the litigation, Dr. Liu stated that his limited participation “should not be understood to mean that he is medically well or capable of participating normally.” (Liu Decl., ¶11.)

 

The Court is persuaded that Kloss’ health is such that he would be prejudiced if a trial preference is not granted. Kloss demonstrates that 1) he suffers from a medical condition that severely limits to ability to participate in the litigation, and 2) there is a substantial likelihood that he condition will worsen over the coming months. As to the first point, Kloss offers evidence that he suffers from prostate cancer and is undergoing chemotherapy. (Liu Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) Kloss’ ability to participate in the litigation is affected by his condition and the symptoms caused by treatment. (Liu Decl., ¶ 7, 9.) The fact that Kloss is able to participate sporadically in the litigation when his symptoms are minimal does not necessarily mean that a trial preference is inappropriate. Kloss provides evidence that his condition is expected worsen over time. Specifically, Dr. Liu states that there is a “substantial medical risk” that Kloss’ current limitations will worsen over the next months. (Liu Decl., ¶¶ 8, 11.) Overall, the Court finds that a trial preference is appropriate based on Kloss’ current condition and its predicted trajectory.

 

Accordingly, Kloss’ Motion for Trial Preference is granted.

 

Trial is to be set within 120 days of this hearing pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (f).


Case Number: 24NWCV02228    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: L

FRANK ZARABI vs SAMAN ZAMAN, et al.

CASE NO.: 24NWCV02228

HEARING: September 17, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#6

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Frank Zarabi’s Motion Objecting to Sufficiency of Undertaking on Appeal is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a contractual fraud action. On July 18, 2024, Plaintiff Frank Zarabi (“ZARABI”) filed this action against Defendants Saman Zaman, Affordable Cars Inc., Burton Way Investments, LLC (“Burton Way”), and Does 1 to 20.

 

On October 17, 2024, ZARABI filed the First Amended Complaint, adding Defendant Camzam Investments, Inc.

 

On December 27, 2024, the Court entered dismissal without prejudice as to Defendant Affordable Cars Inc., doing business as Black and White Rent-a-Car.

 

On January 7, 2025, ZARABI filed the Second Amended Complaint against Defendants Saman Zaman (“ZAMAN”), Affordable Cars Inc., Burton Way Investments, LLC (“Burton Way”), Camzam Investments, Inc. (“Camzam”), and Does 1 to 50.

 

On February 14, 2025, Defendants/Cross-Complainants ZAMAN, Burton Way, and Camzam filed the Cross-Complaint against ZARABI and Roes 1 to 10. The Cross-Complaint alleges that, in June 2017, Burton Way entered into a valid contract with Mercedes-AMG GmbH (“Mercedes-AMG”) to purchase the Vehicle (“Mercedes-AMG Contract”), which would be specially produced and delivered in 2021. ZAMAN discussed a possible transfer of the Vehicle to ZARABI, on the condition that the Vehicle was delivered to Burton Way and all obligations between Mercedes-AMG and Burton Way were complete. Mercedes-AMG experienced production delays, such that the Vehicle’s delivery date was delayed multiple times. The Cross-Complaint primarily alleges that ZARABI independently communicated with Mercedes-AMG, despite prior admonitions by ZAMAN not to. Specifically, ZARABI falsely communicated that ZAMAN had entered into a contract with ZARABI to sell the Vehicle in violation of the Mercedes-AMG Contract, which prohibits transfer of rights to the Vehicle for a certain time period after delivery. The Cross-Complaint alleges that Mercedes-AMG terminated the Mercedes-AMG Contract with ZAMAN as a result of ZARABI’s communications. The Cross-Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Intentional Interference with Contractual Relations, 2) Intentional Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage, and 3) Negligent Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage.

 

On July 10, 2025, the Court granted ZARABI’s Anti-SLAPP Motion to the Cross-Complaint. (7/10/25 Order.)

 

On September 24, 2025, ZAMAN, Burton Way, and Camzam filed a Notice of Appeal as to the Court’s granting of ZARABI’s Anti-SLAPP Motion to the Cross-Complaint.

 

On October 9, 2025, ZARABI filed the Third Amended Complaint (TAC) against ZAMAN. The TAC alleges that, in March 2018, the parties entered into an agreement via email, by which ZARABI was to pay all Defendants $300,000.00 in exchange for the rights to a Mercedes AMG-One vehicle. The TAC alleges ZARABI made subsequent payments totaling $899,000.00. Defendants allegedly committed an anticipatory breach through acts suggesting that Defendants were going to sell the rights to the AMG-One to a third party. In May 2024, ZARABI exercised his contractual right to cancel the contract and demanded return of the $899,000.00 plus interest. Defendants returned only $500,000.00 to ZARABI. Defendants have failed to return the remaining $399,000.00 of ZARABI’s deposit, despite ZARABI’s demand.

 

The TAC alleges the following causes of action: 1) Breach of Written Contract, 2) Breach of Oral Contract, 3) Quantum Meruit, 4) Fraud, 5) Breach of Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing, 6) Violation of Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act (RICO), 7) Unlawful Practice in Sale or Lease of Consumer Goods or Services, 8) Open Book Account/Account Stated, and 9) Money Had and Received.

 

On February 19, 2026, the Court granted, in part, ZAMAN, Burton Way, and Camzam’s Motion to Stay Trial Proceedings Pending Appeal. The Court declined to stay the action as to ZARABI’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees set to be heard the same day and continued that Motion to May 19, 2026. (2/19/26 Minute Order.)

 

On May 19, 2026, the Court granted ZARABI’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees in the amount of $115,099.00 in attorney’s fees and $2,685.25 in costs (hereinafter, “Fee Order”). (5/19/26 Minute Order.)

 

On August 18, 2026, ZAMAN, Burton Way, and Camzam filed a Notice of Posting Bond to stay enforcement of money judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1. The attached undertaking is in the amount of $176,676.38, issued by American Contractors Indemnity Company (hereinafter,
“Undertaking”).

 

ZARABI now objects to the Notice of Posting Bond pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 995.920, subdivision (c). Specifically, ZARABI requests an order “(1) sustaining [ZARABI’s] objection to the sufficiency of the undertaking on appeal filed and served on August 17, 2026, by Defendants […] and (2) approving [ZARABI’s] applications for the appearance and examination of [Defendants] […].” (Notice, 2:5-12.)

 

Defendants ZAMAN, Burton Way, and Camzam (hereinafter, “Defendants”) oppose the Motion.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The beneficiary may object to a bond on any of the following grounds: […] (c) The bond, from any other cause, is insufficient.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 995.920, subd. (c).)

 

“(a) An objection shall be in writing and shall be made by noticed motion. The notice of motion shall specify the precise grounds for the objection. […]

 

(b) The objection shall be made within 10 days after service of a copy of the bond on the beneficiary or such other time as is required by the statute providing for the bond.

 

(c) If no objection is made within the time required by statute, the beneficiary is deemed to have waived all objections except upon a showing of good cause for failure to make the objection within the time required by statute or of changed circumstances.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 995.930, subds. (a)-(b).)

 

Discussion

 

ZARABI objects to Defendants’ undertaking on two grounds. First, Defendants have failed to file a notice of appeal or amended notice of appeal seeking appellate review of the Fee Order. Second, the Undertaking only names ZAMAN as principal. Burton Way and Camzam are not named on the Undertaking.

 

The Court will address each argument in turn.

 

          Whether a Separate Appeal Must be Filed as to the Fee Order

 

ZARABI argues that Defendants’ Undertaking is ineffective to stay enforcement of the Fee Order because Defendants have failed to file a separate notice of appeal for the Fee Order. ZARABI argues that the current pending appeal in Case No. B349929 is only regarding the Court’s July 31, 2025 order granting ZARABI’s Anti-SLAPP Motion to Strike Defendants’ Cross-Complaint. Such appeal does not encompass the Fee Order.

 

ZARABI notes that the Court has already rejected the argument that the Fee Order is subject to Code of Civil Procedure section 916, subdivision (a) automatic stay in its February 19, 2026 order on Defendants’ Motion to Stay Trial Proceedings Pending Appeal. Specifically, the Court cited to Dowling v. Zimmerman (“Dowling”) (2001) 85 Cal.App.4th 1400 and Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1, subdivision (a) in holding that enforcement of the Fee Order should not be stayed. The Court had already rejected Defendants’ arguments that “the fee order is exempt from the ordinary money-judgment rule because of its relationship to the B349929 appeal.” (Mot., 6:19-20.) Defendants cannot now attempt to file an undertaking and rely on the prior Notice of Appeal.

 

ZARABI further explains that, in Dowling, the fee order was part of the judgment on appeal, in contrast to the situation here. The appellant/plaintiff argued that enforcement of the fee order was automatically stayed because it was embraced by the appeal on the anti-SLAPP ruling. (Dowling, supra, 85 Cal.App.4th at pp. 1404–1406.) The Court of Appeal held that the money-judgment rule in Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1 required that the appellant/plaintiff post an appeal bond to cover the fee order. (Id. at p. 1434.) Here, Defendants’ Notice of Appeal clearly does not cover the Fee Order. ZARABI argues that “[i]f the fee award in Dowling—baked into the very judgment on appeal and unquestionably before the appellate court—did not receive automatic-stay protection merely because it flowed from the ruling under review, a later, separately entered fee order that was never even made the subject of a notice of appeal has no stronger claim to that protection here.” (Mot., 8:21-24.)

 

ZARABI preemptively argues that there is a distinct line of authority holding that, where a judgment already establishes an entitlement to fees and merely leaves the amount to be later determined, a notice of appeal from the judgment subsumes the later fee order, and no separate appeal is required, citing Grant v. List & Lathrop (“Grant”) (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 993, 998. However, such exception does not apply here because the Fee Order “arose from a separate, subsequent motion in which Plaintiff sought fees as the prevailing party on that special motion to strike under section 425.16, subdivision (c)(1)” and that was “an independent adjudication, not a mere quantification of an entitlement the appealed order already established.” (Mot., 9:18-20.) ZARABI argues that such post-judgment order is appealable in its own right and requires a separate notice of appeal.

 

In opposition, Defendants argue that the Court’s February 19, 2026 order essentially declined to impose a stay because Defendant had not posted an undertaking. Defendants have now posted an undertaking, and thus, enforcement of the Fee Order should be stayed pursuant to Dowling.

 

Defendants argue that the exception in the Grant case applies here—specifically, when a judgment awarded fees and provides for later determination of the amount of the fees, the notice of appeal as to that judgment “subsumes” any subsequent order setting the amount of the award. Here, the Court adjudicated ZARABI’s entitlement to attorney’s fees in its July 31, 2025 order on the anti-SLAPP Motion, and the May 19, 2026 order on the Motion for Attorney’s Fees merely quantified the amount of fees awardable. Thus, no separate notice of appeal is needed for the Fee Order because it is “subsumed” into the appeal of the anti-SLAPP Motion order.

 

In reply, ZARABI argues that the Grant case is distinguishable, because the appellant in that case affirmatively moved the Court of Appeal to construe its notice of appeal to reach the subsequent fee order. (Grant, supra, 2 Cal.App.4th at p. 998.) The Court of Appeal granted that motion. (Ibid.) Defendants have taken no similar action in its appellate case. Thus, any “subsummation” arguments should be addressed by the Court of Appeal.

 

ZARABI reiterates that Defendants’ position that the Notice of Appeal subsumes the Fee Order is directly contradicted by its arguments made in the Motion to Stay Trial Proceedings Pending Appeal. In that Motion, Defendants conceded that enforcement of the Fee Order was not automatically stayed pursuant to its Notice of Appeal and asked the Court to exercise its discretion in staying enforcement.

 

Firstly, the Court notes that an order granting attorney’s fees rendered subsequent to a ruling on an anti-SLAPP motion is not within the scope of the statutory provisions for direct appeal of a ruling on an anti-SLAPP motion. (See

Doe v. Luster (“Luster”) (2006) 145 Cal.App.4th 139, 145-150.) However, the Court acknowledges that, when an attorney’s fees order is rendered after a judgment of dismissal upon granting the anti-SLAPP motion, the order is directly appealable under Code of Civil Procedure section 904.1, subdivision (a)(2) as an order after an appealable judgment. (See Melbostad v. Fisher (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 987, 993, citing Luster, supra, 145 Cal.App.4th pp. 147-149.) Here, the parties essentially concede that the Court’s order granting ZARABI’s anti-SLAPP Motion was a final judgment and that the Fee Order is a post-judgment order.

 

ZARABI is correct that, generally a post-judgment order awarding or denying costs and/or attorney fees is appealable. (See Apex LLC v. Korusfood.com (2013) 222 Cal.App.4th 1010, 1015.) Further, when a party wishes to appeal a final judgment and a post-judgment attorney’s fees order, the normal procedure is to file to separate appeals for each order. (See Torres v. City of San Diego (2007) 154 Cal.App.4th 214, 222.)

 

However, a separate appeal from such an order is not always required to ensure vacatur of an attorney’s fees award upon reversal of the underlying judgment. The Court finds that the reasoning of the Grant case applies here. “[W]hen a judgment awards costs and fees to a prevailing party and provides for the later determination of the amounts, the notice of appeal subsumes any later order setting the amounts of the award.” (Grant, supra, 2 Cal.App.4th at p. 998; see also Green v. County of Riverside (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 1363, 1373 [“A cost award that is incidental to a judgment may be challenged on an appeal from the judgment even though the amount of costs was filled in on the judgment after the notice of appeal was filed.”].) In other words, where a judgment awards unspecified attorney’s fees and provides for later determination of the amount (e.g., via a fee motion), the failure to file a separate appeal from the subsequent fee order does not preclude review of the order on appeal from the underlying judgment. In effect, such principle establishes that a separate appeal is permitted, but not required, for appellate review of the post-judgment order.

 

Although the Grant case did not involve an underlying anti-SLAPP motion, the Court finds that its reasoning applies here. In the Court’s order on the anti-SLAPP Motion, the Court held the following:

 

“As the prevailing party, ZARABI is entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees, which may be requested in a separately noticed motion. (Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(c){1). ‘Any SLAPP defendant who brings a successful motion to strike is entitled to mandatory attorney fees.’ (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1131.)

 

ZARABI does not address attorney’s fees and costs in the Motion. ZARABI may file a separate motion to seek such fees and costs.”

 

(7/31/25 Order.)

 

The Court agrees with Defendants that the Court’s order established that ZARABI was entitled to attorney’s fees under the anti-SLAPP statute and the only remaining issue was the amount of costs. This is the exact situation contemplated by Grant. Thus, the Court is not persuaded by ZARABI’s argument that the Undertaking is void because no separate notice of appeal was filed as to the Fee Order. Setting aside the separate notice of appeal issue, under Dowling and Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1, the Court finds that a stay of enforcement of the Fee Order is appropriate pursuant to Defendants’ filing of an Undertaking.

 

          Failure to Name Burton Way and Camzam on Undertaking

 

ZARABI separately argues that the Undertaking is invalid as to Burton Way and Camzam because it only names ZAMAN as the principle. ZARABI asserts that “[a]n undertaking naming only one of several jointly and severally liable judgment debtors cannot stay enforcement against the debtors it does not name or bind.” (Mot., 10:12-13.)

 

In opposition, Defendants argue that ZARABI cites no authority for its contention. Defendants assert that the amount of the Undertaking complies with Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1 [requiring that the amount be one and one-half times the amount of the judgment if given by an admitted surety insurer]. Defendants note that ZAMAN is the president and sole owner of Camzam and is the managing member, CEO, and 90-95 percent owner of Burton Way. (DiGiannantonio Decl., ¶ 9, Ex. I.) Defendants assert that “amending or supplementing the bond to include Camzam and Burton Way would be a far less onerous and wasteful process than Zarabi’s request to pursue examination of the Defendants.” (Opp., 9:23-26.)

 

In reply, ZARABI reiterates its argument that the Undertaking is not binding on Camzam and Burton Way. ZARABI argues that the Court “should approve [ZARABI’s] debtor examination application as to all three judgment debtors, or at minimum as to Burton Way and Camzam, regardless of its ruling on Zaman.” (Reply, p. 8.)

 

The Court finds that ZARABI provides no authority that ZAMAN cannot file the Undertaking on behalf of all Defendants. The purpose of the Undertaking is accomplished. Thus, the Court is not persuaded that the failure to name Camzam and Burton Way on the Undertaking is grounds for objection.

 

Conclusion

 

Accordingly, ZARABI’s Motion Objecting to Sufficiency of Undertaking on Appeal is denied without prejudice.

 

ZARABI’s request to approve his applications for debtor’s appearance and examination of Defendants is denied.


Case Number: 25NWCV01522    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: L

RYAN SEDA vs LOUIS LUHUEI WU

CASE NO.: 25NWCV01522

HEARING: September 17, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#7

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Ryan Seda’s Motion for Order Substituting Jerry Chiatzeng Wu, as Personal Representative of the Estate of Louis Luhuei Wu, as Defendant in Place of Decedent Louis Luhuei Wu is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a tenants’ rights action regarding residential property located at 18331 Clarkdale Ave., Artesia, California 90701. On April 28, 2025, Plaintiff Ryan Seda (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Louis Luhuei Wu (“Defendant”) and Does 1 to 20.

 

Plaintiff now moves to substitute Jerry Chiatzeng Wu, Personal Representative of the Estate of Louis Luhuei Wu, as the Defendant in this action in place of deceased Defendant pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41.

 

Jerry Chiatzeng Wu, Personal Representative of the Estate of Louis Luhuei Wu, (hereinafter, “Personal Representative Wu”) specially-appearing, opposes the Motion. No reply has been filed as of September 16, 2026.


Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41 provides that “[o]n motion, the court shall allow a pending action or proceeding against the decedent that does not abate to be continued against the decedent’s personal representative, or to the extent provided by statute, against the decedent’s successor in interest, except that the court may not permit an action or proceeding to be continued against the personal representative unless proof of compliance with Part 4 (commencing with Section 9000) of Division 7 of the Probate Code governing creditor claims is first made.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.41.) 

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff explains that unbeknownst to Plaintiff at the time of filing, Defendant died on October 15, 2023, over a year before this action was filed. No personal representative had yet been appointed for his estate at the time the action was filed. Plaintiff’s counsel learned that Decedent’s son, Jerry Chiatzeng Wu, filed a petition for probate and was later was appointed as Defendant’s personal representative on July 1, 2026. (Kohanim Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

Plaintiff argues that he properly followed the Probate Code creditor-claim filing procedure, and thus, this substitution should be allowed under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41. On February 23, 2026, Plaintiff timely filed a Creditor’s Claim (Judicial Council Form DE-172) in the probate proceeding. (Kohanim Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. A.) On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff received notice that Jerry Chiatzeng Wu rejected the creditor claim in full. (Kohanim Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. 2.) The Allowance or Rejection of Creditor’s Claim (Judicial Council Form DE-174) was executed July 6, 2026 and served on Plaintiff by mail that same date. (Ibid.) Plaintiff filed this Motion within 90 days from the July 6, 2026 notice of rejection. (Kohanim Decl., ¶ 8.)

 

Plaintiff thus argues that this substitution is timely. Specifically, the one-year limitations period of Code of Civil Procedure section 366.2 was tolled from the date he filed the creditor’s claim (February 23, 2026) to the date the claim was rejected (July 6, 2026) pursuant to Probate Code section 9352, subdivision (a) and the reasoning of Estate of Holdaway (“Holdaway”) (2019) 40 Cal.App.5th 1049. Under Probate Code section 9353, Plaintiff was afforded 90 days after the notice of claim rejection (October 4, 2026) to commence or continue the action on the claim.

 

In opposition, Personal Representative Wu argues that Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41 does not authorize his substitution for Defendant where Defendant died before the action was commenced. Personal Representative argues that the statutory language applies when an action “pending” against a defendant thereafter dies, and plaintiff seeks to substitute in defendant’s personal representative. The related Probate Code section involving requirements for a filing a claim to continue an action requires that an action be “pending against the decedent at the time of death.” (See Prob. Code, § 9370, subd. (a).)

 

Personal Representative Wu further asserts that Plaintiff conflates issues of whether the credit-claim procedure affects limitations period tolling with whether substitution is allowed in this particular action via Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41. The Holdaway case did not discuss section 377.41—rather, the case addressed the tolling effect of the creditor claim procedure.

 

No reply has been filed.

 

The Court agrees with Personal Representative Wu. Pursuant to the plain language of Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41, a personal representative may be substituted for a deceased defendant in a “pending action.” There is no pending action if the defendant is deceased before the action is filed. (See e.g., Walter v. National Indem. Co. (1970) 3 Cal.App.3d 630, 634

 [“‘[I]f the plaintiff or defendant was dead before the action was begun, the judgment is void and subject to collateral attack, because he [n]ever was a party, i.e., the court never acquired jurisdiction of the person. [Citation.]”]; Woolley v. Seijo (1964) 224 Cal.App.2d 615, 620–621 [“It is settled that a judgment for or against a dead person is void only if the plaintiff or defendant was dead before the action was begun. (Citation.) It is equally well settled that where a party dies subsequent to the commencement of the action and after the court has acquired personal jurisdiction over him, the entry of judgment against him is a ‘mere irregularity’ which renders the judgment voidable only and therefore immune from collateral attack.”].) In other words, section 377.41 presupposes that a valid action was pending—i.e., it was commenced against a living defendant who subsequently died during the litigation.

 

The Court further agrees that the Holdaway case is inapplicable here. The case examined whether the timely filing of a creditor claim tolled the Code of Civil Procedure section 366.2 statute of limitations (for actions against a decedent) until it is acted on by the personal representative. (Holdaway, supra, 40 Cal.App.5th at p. 1051.) On the facts of that case, the Court of Appeal found that the creditor’s timely-filed claim tolled the statute of limitations until the personal representative rejected the claim pursuant to tolling provisions in Probate Code section 9352, and the personal representative’s demurrer the action should not have been sustained based on untimeliness. (Ibid.) Thus, the Holdaway case did not discuss Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41 or whether the personal representative of a defendant that died prior to the filing of an action could be substituted in for that defendant.

 

The Court does not opine on 1) the proper course of action Plaintiff was required to take where the Defendant died prior to the filing of this action or 2) whether this action was timely filed. The bottom line is that Code of Civil Procedure section 377.41 is not the proper procedure in this scenario.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion for Order Substituting Jerry Chiatzeng Wu, as Personal Representative of the Estate of Louis Luhuei Wu, as Defendant in Place of Decedent Louis Luhuei Wu is denied.


Case Number: 25NWCV03211    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: L

ALVARO PEREZ SERRATO, et al. vs CITY OF WHITTIER

CASE NO.: 25NWCV03211

HEARING: September 17, 2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#8

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiffs Alvaro Perez Serrato, Cirenia Peralta, and Richardo Perez’s Petition for Relief from Government Claim Filing Requirements is DENIED.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a negligence action. On September 9, 2025, Plaintiffs Alvaro Perez Serrato, Cirenia Peralta, and Richardo Perez (“Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendant City of Whittier and Does 1 to 100. The gravamen of the Complaint is that, on October 9, 2024, Norwalk Transit Bus #7 negligently collided with Plaintiffs’ vehicle, causing injuries. The Complaint alleges one cause of action for Negligence.

 

On November 24, 2025, Defendant City of Norwalk was named as Doe 1.

 

On March 20, 2026, Plaintiffs dismissed Defendant City of Whittier from the action with prejudice.

 

On April 14, 2026, the Court sustained Defendant City of Norwalk’s Demurrer to the Complaint without leave to amend. (4/14/26 Minute Order.)

 

Plaintiffs now petition for relief from government claim filing requirements pursuant to Government Code section 946.6.

 

The Court notes that the hearing on the Petition was continued from August 13, 2026 to today’s date. (8/13/26 Minute Order.)

 

Legal Standard

 

Government Code section 911.2 provides that “[a] claim relating to a cause of action for death or for injury to person or to personal property. . .shall be presented. . .not later than six months after the accrual of the cause of action.” (Govt. Code, § 911.2, subd. (a).)

 

If a claimant fails to file a claim within 6 months, the claimant can apply to the public entity for leave to present a late claim. (Gov’t. Code, § 911.4, subd. (a).) The application for leave to present a late claim must be submitted “within a reasonable time not to exceed one year after the accrual of the cause of action and shall state the reason for the delay in presenting the claim.” (Gov’t. Code, § 911.4, subd. (b).)

 

“If an application for leave to present a claim is denied or deemed to be denied pursuant to Section 911.6, a petition may be made to the court for an order relieving the petitioner from Section 945.4. The proper court for filing the petition is a superior court that would be a proper court for the trial of an action on the cause of action to which the claim relates. If the petition is filed in a court which is not a proper court for the determination of the matter, the court, on motion of any party, shall transfer the proceeding to a proper court.” (Gov’t. Code, § 946.6, subd. (a).) 

 

A petition for relief to file a late claim must indicate: (1)¿that¿the application was made to the board under¿Government Code section 911.4 and was denied or deemed denied; (2) the reason for failure to present the claim within the time limit specified in Section 911.2; and (3) the information required by Section 910. (Gov’t. Code, § 946.6, subd. (b)(1)-(3).) Further, “[t]he petition shall be filed within six months after the application to the board is denied or deemed to be denied pursuant to Section 911.6.” (Gov’t. Code, § 946.6, subd. (b).)¿¿ 

 

Government Code section 946.6, subdivision (c) states that “[t]he court shall relieve the petitioner from the requirements of Section 945.4 if the court finds that the application to the board under Section 911.4 was made within a reasonable time not to exceed that specified in subdivision (b) of Section 911.4 and was denied or deemed denied pursuant to Section 911.6 and that one or more of the following is applicable: [listing certain qualifying conditions, one of which must be met].”

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiffs now petition for relief from government filing requirements on grounds that their failure to present the claim was due to mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect under Government Code section 946.6, subdivision (c)(1) [“The failure to present the claim was through mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect unless the public entity establishes that it would be prejudiced in the defense of the claim if the court relieves the petitioner from the requirements of Section 945.4.”].)

 

Specifically, prior to the filing of this action on November 12, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a government claim with the City of Whittier based on their collision with a bus while travelling in Whitter. (Irvin Decl., ¶¶ 2-3, 5.) On March 23, 2025, City of Whittier denied the claim, although no reason was given for the denial. (Irvin Decl., ¶ 4.) On October 2, 2025, Plaintiffs received the Declaration of Kyle Cason, through which they learned that the subject bus was owned by the City of Norwalk. (Irvin Decl., ¶ 8.)

 

On October 30, 2025, Plaintiffs applied for leave to present a late claim with the City of Norwalk pursuant to under¿Government Code section 911.4 (“Application to Submit Late Claim”). (Irvin Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. A.) On November 4, 2025, Plaintiffs received notice from the City of Norwalk that the Application to Submit Late Claim was rejected. (Irvin Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. B.) Plaintiffs timely filed this Petition.

 

Plaintiffs further argues that the City of Norwalk will not be prejudiced if relief is granted. Plaintiffs thus argue that they should be relieved of the government claim filing requirements.

 

In opposition, Defendant City of Norwalk (hereinafter, “City”) argues that Plaintiffs improperly attempt to circumvent to the governmental claim filing requirements and that their petition for relief should be denied. The incident occurred on October 9, 2024. Plaintiffs failed to present a government claim to the City within 6 months pursuant to Government Code section 911.2. Further, Plaintiffs failed to apply for leave to present a late claim within one year of accrual of their claim pursuant to Government Code section 911.4, subdivision (b)—specifically, the deadline to apply was October 9, 2025 and Plaintiffs submitted their application on October 30, 2025. Thus, the Petition should be denied on procedural grounds.

 

Further, Plaintiffs cannot establish mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect under Government Code section 946, subdivision (c)(1). Plaintiffs’ own evidence establishes that their “mistake” in identifying the correct public entity was in actuality due to “an unverified assumption.” (Opp., 5:17.) Further, Plaintiffs made no attempt to investigate the reason for the City of Whitter’s denial from March 25, 2025, the date of the denial, to October 2, 2025, the date it received the Declaration of Kyle Cason. The City argues that Plaintiffs’ lack of diligence cannot constitute excusable neglect as a matter of law.

 

Lastly, the City would be prejudiced if relief is granted. Plaintiffs’ non-compliance has resulted in unreasonable delay, which has prejudiced the City’s ability to conduct discovery. The City has incurred costs to litigate this case, including for bringing its Demurrer that was sustained without leave to amend.

 

In reply, Plaintiffs reiterate their arguments. Further, Plaintiffs assert that a “factual question remains as to whether the City of Whittier communicated with or forwarded [Plaintiffs’] claim to the City of Norwalk after receiving it” and request “the opportunity to conduct limited discovery” regarding this issue before the Court rules on this Petition. (Repl., 5:11-17.)

 

The Court denies Plaintiffs’ Petition on two grounds. Firstly, the Court finds that Plaintiffs failed to submit a timely application for leave to present a late claim to the City of Norwalk within one year of claim accrual pursuant to Government Code section 911.4, subdivision (b). Such condition is required for a petition for relief. (See Gov’t. Code, § 946.6, subd. (c) [“[t]he court shall relieve the petitioner from the requirements of Section 945.4 if the court finds that the application to the board under Section 911.4 was made within a reasonable time not to exceed that specified in subdivision (b) of Section 911.4 […]”], emphasis added.) As noted by the City, Plaintiffs did not submit such application until October 30, 2025, over one year since the incident occurred on October 9, 2024. (See Irvin Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.)

 

Secondly, the Court previously sustained the City’s Demurrer to the Complaint without leave to amend on grounds that Plaintiffs were required to allege that they complied with the Government Claims Act—including the requirement of petitioning for and receiving relief from government claim filing requirements—prior to filing this action. The Court held the following:

 

“The Court finds that compliance with Government Code section 945.4, or alternatively, Government Code section 946.6, are prerequisites to filing an action against a public entity. Government Code section 946.6 requires a petition for relief be filed in a separate civil proceeding. (See Haning et al., Cal. Practice Guide: Personal Injury (The Rutter Group, September 2025 Update), ¶ 5:62.1, citing Gov’t. Code, § 946.6, subd. (a), [“The proper court for filing the petition is a superior court that would be a proper court for the trial of an action on the cause of action to which the claim relates.”] and Luers v. Smith (C.D. Cal. 1996) 941 F.Supp. 105, 107 [“Section 946.6 contemplates a petition for relief in a separate civil proceeding, not a motion in an action which has already been filed in either state or federal court.”].)

 

Further, only after the court grants a petition for relief under Government Code section 946.6 has been granted may the petitioner commence an action against the public entity. (See Gov’t. Code, § 946.6, subd. (f) [“If the court makes an order relieving the petitioner from Section 945.4, suit on the cause of action to which the claim relates shall be filed with the court within 30 days thereafter.”], emphasis added; see also Los Angeles City Sch. Dist. v. Superior Court (1970) 9 Cal.App.3d 459, 468 [“[Section 946.6] permits the claimant who was improperly refused consideration of his grievance to bypass the claim procedure altogether. He is thus relieved from the provisions of section 945.4 and may commence his lawsuit against the entity immediately.”].)

 

Here, Plaintiffs were required file their Petition for Relief and receive appropriate relief prior to filing this action. Plaintiffs now attempt to bypass the proper procedures by filing their Petition for Relief in the instant action. The Court thus finds that a stay pending hearing on the Petition would be inappropriate.

 

The Court finds that Plaintiffs cannot allege compliance with the claims presentation requirements. As it stands now, Plaintiff[s] [have] failed to comply with Government Code section 945.4 and no relief has been granted pursuant to Government Code section 946.6. The action is thus barred against the City.

 

Accordingly, the Demurrer to the Complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.”

 

(4/14/26 Minute Order.)

 

The Court agrees that the City would be prejudiced if the Petition was granted in light of the Court’s prior ruling.

 

The Court respectfully declines Plaintiffs’ request to allow limited discovery to be conducted.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ Petition for Relief from Government Claim Filing Requirements is denied.

Defendant Phyllis Saunders’ Motion for New Trial is DENIED.

This is an unlawful detainer action. On November 14, 2025, Plaintiffs Emeritus Corporation (“Emeritus”) and Brea Whitter, LLC, doing business as Brookdale Central Whittier, (“Brookdale”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendant Phyllis Saunders (“Defendant”). Brookdale is the owner of Premises, and Emeritus Corporation is the lessee and property manager of the Premises. (Compl., ¶ 4.) On April 24, 2024, the parties entered into a Residency Agreement, wherein Defendant agreed to pay $3,291.00 in monthly rent. On August 11, 2025, Plaintiffs served a 30-day notice to pay or quit on Defendant.

 

On June 25, 2026, the Court heard Plaintiff’s Motion for Summary Judgment (“MSJ”) and took the MSJ under submission. (6/25/26 Minute Order.) On July 1, 2026, the Court issued its ruling, granting the MSJ.

 

On July 16, 2026, Defendant filed a Notice of Intent to Move for New Trial.

 

On July 27, 2026, Defendant filed this Motion for New Trial pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 657, subdivisions (1), (6), and (7).

 

On August 7, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition. No reply has been filed as of September 16, 2026.

 

Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 657 provides, in relevant part, as follows:

 

“The verdict may be vacated and any other decision may be modified or vacated, in whole or in part, and a new or further trial granted on all or part of the issues, on the application of the party aggrieved, for any of the following causes, materially affecting the substantial rights of such party:

1. Irregularity in the proceedings of the court, jury or adverse party, or any order of the court or abuse of discretion by which either party was prevented from having a fair trial.

[…]

6. Insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, or the verdict or other decision is against law.

7. Error in law, occurring at the trial and excepted to by the party making the application.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 657.)

 

A new trial motion is available to challenge an order granting summary judgment. (See Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 858.) 

 

A party intending to move for a new trial shall file with the clerk and serve on adverse parties a notice of intention to move for new trial “[w]ithin 15 days of the date of serving notice of entry of judgment. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 659, subd. (a)(2).)

 

“Within 10 days of filing the notice, the moving party shall serve upon all other parties and file any brief and accompanying documents, including affidavits in support of the motion. The other parties shall have 10 days after that service within which to serve upon the moving party and file any opposing briefs and accompanying documents, including counter-affidavits. The moving party shall have five days after that service to file any reply brief and accompanying documents. These deadlines may, for good cause shown by affidavit or by written stipulation of the parties, be extended by any judge for an additional period not to exceed 10 days.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 659a.)

 

“[T]he power of the court to rule on a motion for a new trial shall expire 75 days after the mailing of notice of entry of judgment by the clerk of the court pursuant to Section 664.5 or 75 days after service on the moving party by any party of written notice of entry of judgment, whichever is earlier, or if that notice has not been given, 75 days after the filing of the first notice of intention to move for a new trial.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 660, subd. (c).)

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves for new trial. Defendant asserts that she served a Notice of Intent to Move for New Trial within 15 days of the date of Notice of Entry of Judgment on July 1, 2026. This Motion was filed within 10 days of that Notice Defendant notes that the deadline was on a weekend, extending the deadline to Monday. Thus, this Motion is timely.

 

Defendant argues that the Court erred in granting summary judgment in favor of Plaintiffs because the underlying 30-day Notice to Pay or Quit (“Notice”) is defective on its face. Specifically, the Notice fails to provide the name and telephone number to whom Defendant should direct payment by mail pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1161, subdivision (2) [stating that 3-day notice must state “the amount that is due, the name, telephone number, and address of the person to whom the rent payment shall be made, and, if payment may be made personally, the usual days and hours that person will be available to receive the payment”].)

 

The Notice provides the following options for payment:

 

“1. Payment may be mailed, addressed as follows:

 

Brookdale Central Whittier

8101 Painter Avenue

Whittier, CA 90602

 

2. Alternatively, payment may be made by personal delivery by any of the following means:

 

Mario Preston, Operations Specialist

Brookdale Central Whittier

8101 Painter Avenue

Whittier, CA 90602

 

Hours: Saturday through Sunday, 9:00 a.m. — 5:00 p.m.

Phone: (707) 575-7503”

 

(Compl., Exh. 2.)

 

Defendant argues that “while the statute provides that there are additional requirements for landlords who wish to demand personal or electronic payment, there are no demands for payment by mail which are exempt from the basic requirement to provide a name and telephone number.” (Mot., 6:27, 7:1-2.) Defendant cites SVF Grosvenor Del Rey Corp. v. Schwarz (“SVF Grosvenor”) (2026) 120 Cal. App. 5th Supp. 15, 19.)

 

Defendant asserts that there has been an irregularity in the in the proceedings of the court, insufficient evidence to justify the decision, or an error in law pursuant to Code of Civil procedure section 657, subdivision (1), (6)-(7). New trial is thus warranted.

 

In opposition, Plaintiffs first argue that the Motion is procedurally defective. Plaintiffs’ counsel declares that Mr. Donovan Haley was Defendant’s counsel of record when this Motion was filed. (Anguiano Decl., ¶ 5.) However, the Motion was filed by an attorney named Abe Rinck from Eviction Defense Network, who had not appeared in this case before and for whom no notice of substitution of counsel was ever filed. Such substitution is in violation of Code of Civil Procedure section 285 [“When an attorney is changed. . .written notice of the change and of the substitution of a new attorney, or of the appearance of the party in person, must be given to the adverse party. Until then he must recognize the former attorney.”]. Plaintiffs assert that “courts distinguish between individual attorneys, who are considered ‘counsel of record,’ and the firms or organizations where they work.” (Opp., 4:5-7.)

 

Plaintiffs further argue that the Motion substantively fails. Defendant attempts to argue three separate grounds for new trial based on the same alleged defect in the Notice.

 

Firstly, subdivision (1) of section 657 does not apply here because a party must move based on “upon affidavits” or “the minutes of the court” pursuant to that section. (See Code Civ. Proc.. § 658 [“When the application is made for a cause mentioned in the first, second, third and fourth subdivisions of Section 657, it must be made upon affidavits; otherwise it must be made on the minutes of the court.”].) Here, Defendant does not move based on affidavits or the court’s minutes.

 

Second, subdivision (6) of section 657 does not apply here. Defendant appears to argue that the Court’s decision is “against the law.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 657, subd. (6).) However, Plaintiffs assert that 1) courts mainly apply this subsection in cases that involve an improper verdict or a defective statement of decision, and 2) it does not overlap with any of the other grounds for new trial. Thus, because Defendant claims that her Motion is based on two other grounds, Defendant cannot also rely on subdivision (6).

 

Lastly, subdivision (7) of section 657 does not apply because the “excepted to” language indicates that the moving party must have timely objected to the “error in law.” Defendant does not argue that she did so. Defendant did not claim that the Notice was defective at any point prior to this Motion. Further, Defendant has failed to show that she will be prejudiced if the Motion is not granted based on this ground.

 

Plaintiffs lastly assert that, even if Defendant could establish that any of these grounds apply, the Motion still fails because the Notice is valid. Firstly, the name of a “person” under Code of Civil Procedure section 1161, subdivision (2) includes corporate entities. Here, the Notice contains the name of the corporate entity, “Brookdale Central Whittier.” Further, the pay-by-mail and pay-in-person instructions contain the same corporate name and address: Brookdale Central Whittier, 8101 Painter Avenue, Whittier, CA 90602. Plaintiffs argue that “[g]iven how the Notice was formatted and organized, a reasonable person would understand that the telephone number listed applied to the payment instructions generally, not just for payment in person.” (Opp., 8:9-12.)

 

Plaintiffs argue that Defendant’s cited case, SVF Grosvenor, should not be followed here. The Appellate Division’s opinion was based on the risk that the pay-or-quit notice was confusing to the tenant. Specifically, that case involved a notice in which the pay-by-mail and pay-in-person instructions were in “separate provisions on separate pages” of the notice, and the name and telephone number of the payee were only listed with the pay-in-person instructions. (SVF Grosvenor, supra, 120 Cal. App. 5th Supp. at p. 25.) The Appellate Division reasoned that there was a risk of tenant confusion, stating the following: “the tenant would have to search the notice. . .looking for a person’s name, telephone number and address, potentially leading to the rent being sent to a different person or a different address than intended by the landlord and/or rent not being provided in a timely manner. . .This would be inimical to the purposes of the statute: to ensure the tenant is provided clear information to avoid unnecessary evictions.” (Ibid.)

 

Here, the underlying Notice presents no similar risk of confusion as the notice in SVF Grosvenor. All of the contact information required by Code of Civil Procedure section 1161, subdivision (2) was listed on the same page and in the same paragraph of the Notice.

 

No reply has been filed. The Court notes that a reply was due five days after the opposition was served pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 659a. That deadline was August 12, 2026.

 

Firstly, the Court notes that Defendant has then-since filed a Substitution of Attorney naming Abraham Rinck as counsel of record. Thus, Plaintiffs arguments that the Motion is invalid to lack of substitution of counsel are moot.

 

The Court firstly finds that Defendant has failed to explicitly explain how either Code of Civil Procedure section 657, subdivisions (1), (6), or (7) apply as grounds for new trial. Subdivision (1) does not apply where Defendant does not rely on affidavits of the Court’s minute orders to show irregularity in proceedings. (See Code Civ. Proc.. § 658.) Subdivision (6) does not apply where Defendant contends that subdivisions (1) and (7) apply. (See Sanchez-Corea v. Bank of America (1985) 38 Cal.3d 892, 906 [“The ground [in subdivision (6) is separate and distinct from the other grounds listed in section 657 and does not include any, or all, of those other separate and distinct grounds for new trial.”]; see also McCown v. Spencer (1970) 8 Cal.App.3d 216, 229, fn. 3 [distinguishing subdivision (6) ground to subdivision (7) ground].) As to subdivision (7), Defendant has failed to demonstrate any “error in law,” let alone that any error was timely “excepted to.” Further, Defendant has failed to demonstrate prejudice due to the error. (See Donlen v. Ford Motor Co. (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 138, 147 [“To be entitled to a new trial, the moving party must also show the error was prejudicial; that it affected a substantial right and prevented him from obtaining a fair trial.”].)

 

Even if Defendant were to establish that any of these grounds applied, the Court finds that the Notice is not defective on its face.

 

The Court first acknowledges that the SVF Grosvenor case stands for the following rule: “[Under section 1161(2)], a notice which spells out that the tenant has the option to make a personal payment or payment by mail must state the name of a person and the person's telephone number and address for each option provided.” (SVF Grosvenor, supra, 120 Cal. App. 5th Supp. at p. 25.) In that case, the Appellate Division rejected the plaintiff’s argument that then notice was valid even though the pay-by-mail option did not contain a name and a telephone number because the personal payment option was sufficient. (Ibid.) In finding the notice was invalid, the Appellate Division affirmed the trial court’s granting of defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleadings. (Id. at p. 26.)

 

On its face, the SVF Grosvenor case appears to mirror the arguments made in this case. However, the Court finds that the case is distinguishable. Firstly, in that case, at the bottom of the first page of the Notice, it read “Payment must be made payable to SVF Grosvenor Del Rey and delivered as follows;” and following that statement, there was a pay-by-mail option identifying the payee’s name as “Leasing Office” with the address. (Id. at p. 19.) On the top of the second page, there was a personal payment option stating the name of an individual payee, the same address as the mailing address, and the hours of availability, and that the individual could be reached at a telephone number. (Ibid.)

 

In contrast, here, the Notice states the following on the second page: “Within thirty (30) days after service of this notice upon you, the total overdue payment in the amount stated above, must be delivered as follows: [directly below listing both pay-by-mail and pay-in-person options].” (Compl., Exh. 2.) The pay-in-person option names “Brookdale Central Whittier” as the payee. Caselaw acknowledges that a corporate entity, not necessarily a natural person, may be named as the payee. (See City of Alameda v. Sheehan (2024) 105 Cal.App.5th 68, 74-81 [holding that “‘person’ as used in section 1161(2) includes a corporation as well as a natural person”].) As to any argument that the pay-by-mail option does not contain a telephone number, the Court notes that 1) the hours of operation and the telephone number are below both options for payment and 2) nothing indicates that the hours and telephone number are specifically tied to the individual payee. The Court finds that it would be reasonable to interpret the telephone number as applicable to both the pay-by-mail and pay-in-person options. Based on these considerations, the Court agrees with Plaintiffs that any risk of confusion to the tenant present in the SVF Grosvenor case is not present in this case.

 

In summary, the Court is not persuaded that new trial is warranted based on the grounds cited by Defendant.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion for New Trial is denied

Background

 

This is a commercial unlawful detainer action regarding 6020-6034 Atlantic Blvd., Maywood, CA 90270 (“Property”). On April 2, 2026, Plaintiff Altero, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Adam Lujan Greenfield, Adam’s Studio, All Unknown Occupants, Tenants, and Subtenants, and Does 1 to 20.

Plaintiff now moves to consolidate this action with all related actions by Plaintiff against Defendants Adam Lujan Greenfield and All Unknown Occupants, Tenants, and Subtenants of the Property (hereinafter, “Defendants”)—specifically, LASC Case Nos. 26NWCV01167, 26NWCV01169, 26NWCV01172, 26NWCV01176, and 26NWCV01177.

 

On August 19, 2026, the hearing on this Motion was advanced from October 8, 2026 to today’s date. (8/19/26 Minute Order.)

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

Plaintiff’s Requests for Judicial Notice are granted as to Exhibits 1 to 10. 

 

Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1048, subdivision (a) states the following: “When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the court, it may order a joint hearing or trial of any or all the matters in issue in the actions; it may order all the actions consolidated and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (a).)

¿ 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.350, subdivision (a), states the following:

 

“(1) a notice of motion to consolidate must:

(A) List all named parties in each case, the names of those who have appeared, and the names of their respective attorneys of record;

(B) Contain the captions of all the cases sought to be consolidated, with the lowest numbered case shown first; and

(C) Be filed in each case sought to be consolidated.

(2) The motion to consolidate:

(A) Is deemed a single motion for the purpose of determining the appropriate filing fee, but memorandums, declarations, and other supporting papers must be filed only in the lowest numbered case;

(B) Must be served on all attorneys of record and all non-represented parties in all of the cases sought to be consolidated; and

(C) Must have a proof of service filed as part of the motion.¿”

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff has filed six post-foreclosure unlawful detainer actions against Defendants regarding six units in the Property. The other cases are LASC Case Nos. 26NWCV01167, 26NWCV01169, 26NWCV01172, 26NWCV01176, and 26NWCV01177. Plaintiff explains that it purchased the Property at a trustee’s sale, in which Plaintiff was issued three Trustee’s Deeds Upon Sale for the parcels upon which the Property sits. (RJN, Exhs. 2-4.) Defendants thereafter failed to vacate the Property, and thus, Plaintiff initiated these unlawful detainer actions. Plaintiff now moves to consolidate the six related actions so Department L may “summarily proceed with the unlawful detainer actions involving the same parties and the same facts, which are entitled to preference.” (Mot., 2:8-9.)

 

          Requirements of California Rules of Court, Rule 3.350

 

In the Notice of Motion to Consolidate, Plaintiff has listed all named parties in each case, the names of those who have appeared, and the names of their respective attorneys of record, in addition to the Department and case number. The Notice of Motion and Motion to Consolidate was served on Defendant Greenfield, as defendant to all cases. (See POS.)

 

However, the Court finds that the Notice of Motion to Consolidate was not filed in each related case pursuant to California Rules of Court, Rule 3.350(a)(1)(C) . Specifically, no Notice of Motion was filed in LASC Case No. 26NWCV01167, 26NWCV01169, 26NWCV01172, 26NWCV01176, and 26NWCV01177.

 

The Court therefore finds that the procedural requirements for a motion to consolidate have not been met. The Court will thus continue the Motion to allow Plaintiff to correct the deficiency.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Consolidate is continued to November 5, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. in Dept. L.

 

Plaintiff is to file an amended Notice of Motion in each related case and this case reflecting the continued date and to serve such amended Notice to Defendants. The Notice of Motion must be filed at least 5 calendar days prior to the hearing, or the Motion will be taken off-calendar.
