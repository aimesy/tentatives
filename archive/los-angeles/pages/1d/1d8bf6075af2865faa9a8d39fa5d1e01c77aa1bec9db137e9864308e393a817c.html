DEPARTMENT 509 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV14964    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 509

Marquita Lambert, et al. v. Martha Bryant Village II LP

MOTION TO STRIKE
 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Martha Bryant Village II LP

RESPONDING PARTYS:   Plaintiffs Marquita Lambert; De’vaughn Williams, a minor by and through his Guardian ad Litem, Marquita Lambert; Gwynneshia Age; Rondell Age III; Tamia Age; Londynn Age, a minor by and through her Guardian ad Litem, Gwynneshia Age; and Johnea Wade

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

This is a habitability action. Plaintiffs Marquita Lambert; De’vaughn Williams; Gwynneshia Age; Rondell Age III; Tamia Age; Londynn Age; and Johnea Wade (Plaintiffs) filed this complaint against defendant Martha Bryant Village II LP (Defendant) arising from disputes relating to a residential lease. Plaintiffs assert causes of action for: (1) breach of implied warranty of habitability; (2) breach of statutory warranty of habitability; (3) breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment; (4) negligence; (5) violation Of Civil Code Section 1942.4; (6) private nuisance; (7) violation of Tenant Anti-Harassment Ordinance; and (8) violation of Unfair Competition Law (Bus. & Prof. C. §§ 17200-09).

Defendant now moves to strike various allegations in the second amended complaint (SAC). Plaintiffs opposed. 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendant’s Motion to Strike is GRANTED IN PART.

Plaintiffs are given 30 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

Defendant is ordered to give notice, unless waived. 

DISCUSSION:

Motion to Strike
 
I. Meet and Confer

Before filing a motion to strike, the moving party “shall meet and confer…with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purpose of determining if an agreement can be reached that resolves the objections to be raised in the motion to strike…” (Code Civ. Pro. § 435.5, subd. (a).) 

The Declaration of attorney Roy A. Koletsky does not reflect that the meet and confer requirement was satisfied. The Koletsky Declaration only attests to an email with no telephonic meet and confer taking place. This is insufficient to satisfy the requirements to meet either telephonically or in person as required by Code of Civil Procedure, section 435.5(a).

However, failure to comply with the meet and confer requirements alone does not defeat a motion to strike. (Code Civ. Proc. §435.5, subd. (a)(4).) In the interest of judicial economy, the Court exercises its discretion to address the motion on its merits.

II. Legal Standard 
 
A motion to strike lies either (1) to strike any irrelevant, false or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) to strike any pleading or part thereof not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule or order of court. (Code Civ. Pro. § 436.) 
 
III. Analysis 
 
Defendant moves to strike portions of paragraph 39 as well as the entirety of paragraphs 46, 47, and 48.

A. Paragraph 39

Paragraph 39 of the SAC states:

The harassing acts in violation of the ordinance include but are not limited to, the following: 

a. Reducing or eliminating housing services required by a lease, contract, or law, including the elimination of parking if provided in the tenant’s lease or contract except when necessary to comply with a court order or local or state law, or to create an accessory dwelling unit or additional housing. 
b. Failing to perform, and timely complete necessary repairs and maintenance required by State, County, or local housing, health, or safety laws; or failure to follow applicable industry standards to minimize exposure to noise, dust, lead paint, asbestos, or other building materials with potentially harmful health impacts. 
c. Abuse of the right of access into a rental unit as established and limited by California Civil Code Section 1954, including entering or photographing portions of a rental unit that are beyond the scope of a lawful entry or inspection. 
d. Threatening a tenant, by word or gesture, with physical harm.
e. An Official Inspection Report for Record ID CO0399910 issued by the County of Los Angeles Department of Public Health on January 5, 2024. 
f. A Notice and Order to Comply for Case ID 902354 issued by the City of Los Angeles Housing Department Code Enforcement Division on July 2, 2024. 
g. A Complaint Investigation for Inspection No. DA4753280 issued by the City of Los Angeles Housing Department Code Enforcement Division on January 14, 2025.
h. Refusing to acknowledge or accept receipt of lawful rent payments as set forth in the lease agreement or as established by the usual practice of the parties or applicable law. 
i. Inquiring as to the immigration or citizenship status of plaintiffs, or by requiring any plaintiff to make any statement, representation, or certification concerning their immigration or citizenship status. 
j. Disclosing or threating to disclose to any person or entity information regarding the immigration or citizenship status of plaintiffs, whether in retaliation for engaging in legally protected activities or to influence them to vacate or for any other reason.
k. Engaging in an activity prohibited by federal, state, or local housing anti-discrimination laws.
l. Retaliating, threatening, or interfering with plaintiffs organizing activities, including forming or participating in tenant associates and unions.
m. Interfering with plaintiffs’ right to privacy or requesting information that violates a tenant’s right to privacy, including, but not limited to, residency or citizenship status or social security number, except as authorized by law.

(SAC ¶39.)

Here, Defendant moves to strike subdivisions (d), (e), (f), (i), (j), and (k). (Not. of Mot.) Defendant argues that “[t]hese subcategories do nothing more than copy the ordinance’s list of prohibited acts and paste them into the complaint without any underlying factual allegations. They are conclusory allegations and their inclusion serves no purpose other than to suggest serious misconduct, including threats of violence, immigration-based harassment, and housing discrimination, that Plaintiffs never allege actually happened.” (Mot. at p. 7:4-8.) 

However, paragraph 39 is not improper. Paragraph 39 directly alleges Defendant violated Civil Code, section 1942.4 in support of the fifth cause of action. Plaintiff alleges “harassing acts in violation of the ordinance include” the conduct listed in the subdivisions. While the Court recognizes that paragraph 39 appears to be a copied and pasted template, challenges to the sufficiency of the allegations or the availability of evidence is better suited for other motion types. Whether Defendant inquired “as to the immigration or citizenship status of plaintiffs,” for example, is not determinable on a motion to strike. Instead, these allegations are relevant to supporting a violation of Civil Code, section 1942.4.  [FN 1]  

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Strike paragraph 39 is DENIED.

B. Paragraphs 46, 47 and 48

Next, Defendant seeks to strike paragraphs 46, 47, and 48 from the complaint as they request damages improper for a UCL claim. Paragraph 46, 47, and 48 states:

As a direct and proximate result of these acts and omissions, Defendant has received and continues to hold ill-gotten gains belonging to Plaintiffs. As a direct and proximate result of Defendant’s unlawful business practices, Plaintiffs have suffered economic injuries including but not limited to damaged or lost personal property due to insect and vermin infestation, and mold and water damage. Defendant has profited from their unlawful, unfair, and fraudulent acts and practices by receiving full rental compensation without paying Plaintiffs and each of them for the damages each has incurred.

The adult Plaintiffs are entitled to restitution pursuant to Business & Professions Code sections 17203 and 17208 for money acquired by Defendants by means of their unfair business practices, in amounts not yet ascertained but to be ascertained at trial, including all rents unlawfully received and retained by Defendant and interest. The adult Plaintiffs are entitled to restitution for personal property lost or damaged due to insect, vermin, or mold infestation as well as water damage to items including but not limited to, furniture, clothing, bedding, appliances, and other property, in an amount to be determined at the time of trial.

Plaintiffs’ success in this action will enforce important rights affecting the public interest. Plaintiffs sue on behalf of the public as well as on behalf of themselves. Plaintiffs seek and are entitled to reimbursement of unpaid compensation, restitution, declaratory relief, and any other appropriate remedy.

(SAC ¶¶46-48.)  

However, a “UCL action is equitable in nature; damages cannot be recovered.” (Korea Supply Co. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. (2003) 29 Cal.4th 1132, 1144.) The “[p]revailing plaintiffs are generally limited to injunctive relief and restitution.” (Ibid, citing Cel-Tech Communications, Inc. v. Los Angeles Cellular Telephone Co. (1999) 20 Cal.4th 163, 179.) Here, the alleged property damage and declaratory relief is not supported by this cause of action. Because a “demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint or cross-complaint” is an immaterial allegation, these paragraphs are appropriate to strike under Section 436(a). (Code Civ. Pro. §431.10, subd. (b)(3).)

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Strike paragraphs 46, 47, and 48 is GRANTED WITH 30 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 26STCV01279    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 509

Yanira Cruz v. Michelle Aguilera, et al. 

MOTION FOR RELIEF FROM WAIVER OF DISCOVERY OBJECTIONS
 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants University Credit Union and Michelle Aguilera

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Yanira Cruz

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

This is an employment action. Plaintiff Yanira Cruz (Plaintiff) alleges she suffered discrimination and retaliation because of her disabilities during her employment with defendants University Credit Union and Michelle Aguilera, (collectively, Defendants.)

Defendants now move for relief from a waiver of discovery objections arising from late responses to Plaintiff’s First Sets of Request for Inspection, Production of Documents, Form Interrogatories and Special Interrogatories (collectively, the Requests). Plaintiff opposed.

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendants’ Motion for Relief from Waiver of Discovery Objections is GRANTED.  

Defendants are ordered to give notice, unless waived.

DISCUSSION:

Motion for Relief from Waiver of Discovery Objections

I. Legal Standard

The trial court has broad discretion to grant relief from waiver of objections on a noticed motion upon determining that: (a) the moving party subsequently served discovery responses that are substantially code compliant; and (b) the moving party’s failure to timely serve responses “was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290 (a), 2031.300 (a), and 2033.280 (a).)

II. Analysis

A. Background

On March 31 and April 1, 2026, Requests were served on Defendants’ counsel. Attorney Chang, the handling attorney at the time, did not calendar the due dates for these discovery requests before her last day at the firm on May 15, 2026. (Chang Decl. ¶¶2, 4.)

It wasn’t until May 18, 2026, that new counsel spoke with Plaintiff’s counsel regarding responses to these requests. (Smith Decl. ¶10.) At the end of the meeting, the parties agreed to an extension to June 5, 2026, to serve responses. (Ibid.) The parties granted each other various extensions until discovery responses were eventually produced on August 7, 2026. (Yu Decl. ¶¶7-10.) During this time, it appears there were several productive meetings through this period accomplishing meaningful goals such as reasonably limiting the scope of discovery and extending professional courtesies. To their credit, it appears that much was accomplished in this time. 

However, after Defendants provided responses on August 7, Plaintiff informed Defendants that they expected objection free responses. (Smith Decl. ¶10.) Plaintiff took the position that discovery objections were waived by Defendants for their failure to provide timely responses to discovery before the May 18, 2026, meet and confer and that Plaintiff would not relieve counsel of waiver. (Smith Decl. ¶13.) This motion followed.

B. Defendants Served Substantially Code Compliant Responses

Here, Defendants served substantially code compliant responses. “Defendant’s responses consist primarily of substantive details and facts and a limited number of objections concerning privacy and privilege. Defendant also produced 1,229 responsive documents.” (Mot. at p. 5:20-22.) The Declaration of Justin M. Kirk, which contains Defendants’ responses, highlights the substantiative responses within. (Kirk Decl. ¶10, Ex 5-7.)

In opposition, Plaintiff challenges the use of certain objections and argues that responses should have been provided without objection. However, Defendants were allowed to respond with objections only as to attorney-client or attorney work product privileges, per the agreement of the parties.  Any and all other types of objections were, in fact, waived.  [FN 1]  (See, Kirk Decl. ¶5, Ex. 3)

C. Defendants Demonstrated Excusable Neglect

Here, defense counsel’s neglect is excusable. The test of whether neglect was excusable is whether “‘a reasonably prudent person under the same or similar circumstances’ might have made the same error. [Citations.]” (Bettencourt v. Los Rios Community College Dist. (1986) 42 Cal.3d 270, 276.) Calendaring errors by office staff and attorneys have routinely been found to constitute excusable neglect. (See, e.g., Hu v. Fang (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 61, 64-65.) Once the scheduling error was discovered, Defendants counsel immediately informed Plaintiff’s counsel of the mistake and the parties worked together on a resolution. These actions demonstrate excusable neglect.

In opposition, Plaintiff highlights the fact that “Defendants filed this motion on August 21, 2026—ninety-nine days after May 14, 2026.” (Opp. at p. 17:24.) However, the Court recognizes the extensions given by the parties and the successful meet and confer efforts in those ninety-nine days. It would be blatantly unfair to use Defendants’ attempts at informal resolution of discovery issues without Court intervention to their disadvantage. (See Townsend v. Superior Court (1998) 61 Cal.App.4th 1431, 1434 [It “is a central precept to the Civil Discovery Act . . . that civil discovery be essentially self-executing.”]) Plaintiff’s remaining arguments have been considered and do not influence the analysis for relief.

In the end, Defendants’ counsel failed to properly calendar the Requests. Once discovered, both parties made meaningful strides in resolving their discovery disputes which culminated in Defendants’ responses filed on August 7, 2026. While untimely, those responses substantially complied with applicable discovery codes in all other respects. From the totality of the circumstances, including the evidence from both parties, nothing demonstrates any inexcusable neglect or other actions that would warrant denial of relief. This ruling does not disturb any agreements reached between the parties regarding discovery. Instead, it relieves Defendants of its waiver of discovery objections to the Requests arising from the late response. 

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion for Relief from Waiver of Discovery Objections is GRANTED.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
