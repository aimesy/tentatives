22STCV21977 Marc Alan Corbin, et al. v. 720 Normandie, LP et al.

Friday, July 31,  2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFFS’ JOINT MOTION TO COMPEL DEFENDANT CITYWIDE PROPERTY MANAGEMENT AND CONSULTING, INC. RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (9918)

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFFS’ JOINT MOTION TO COMPEL DEFENDANT CITYWIDE PROPERTY MANAGEMENT AND CONSULTING, INC. RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (6630)

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFFS’ JOINT MOTION TO COMPEL DEFENDANT CITYWIDE PROPERTY MANAGEMENT AND CONSULTING, INC. RESPONSES TO DOCUMENTS SET TWO AND THREE REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS; (1557)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The first amended complaint asserts claims arising from alleged uninhabitable conditions on property owned, managed, or controlled by Defendants. Plaintiffs allege eight causes of action arising from Defendants’ alleged failure to remedy the conditions. Plaintiffs substituted themselves in place of prior counsel. (Subst. 12.29.25.)

II.     DISCUSSION

      Plaintiffs served the following discovery on Defendant:

DISCOVERY

SERVED

Form Interrogatories, Set One

12/7/23

Request for Production of Documents, Set One

12/20/23

Request for Production of Documents, Set Two

8/14/25

Request for Production of Documents, Set Three

9/11/25

      All motions were filed on May 20, 2026.

A.     Form Interrogatories

      A motion to compel further responses to form interrogatories is proper where the requesting party believes the responses are evasive and incomplete, that an objection is without merit, or an exercise of the option to produce documents is unwarranted. (Code Civ. Proc., sections 2030.300 subd. (a).)  

      There is no evidence Defendant served verified responses on January 19, 2024. (Prince Decl., ¶ 4, Ex. A.) Although the responses are dated January 19, 2024, they are not accompanied by a proof of service. (Prince Decl., Ex. 1.) Moreover, the responses were not verified at that time. The verification Defendant now relies upon was signed on July 17, 2026, after Plaintiffs filed the present motion.

      Defendant also cannot establish that it served the responses by email on Plaintiffs' former counsel, as claimed. According to Defendant, it lost all email records predating April 17, 2025, as a result of a cyberattack. (Prince Decl., fn. 1.)

      There is no evidence that verified responses were ever served on Plaintiffs' counsel. Because unverified discovery responses are "tantamount to no responses at all," Defendant has failed to demonstrate that it timely served code-compliant responses. (Appleton v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 632, 636.) Accordingly, this is not a motion to compel further responses but to demand initial verified responses. The motion is GRANTED.

B.     Request for Production of Documents

      A motion to compel further responses to a document request is proper where the requesting party believes the statement of compliance is incomplete, or a representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive and/or an objection in the response is without merit or too general.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310.)

            Set One

            Plaintiffs contend Defendant never responded. Defendant provides a copy of responses to Set One purportedly served on February 14, 2024. However, a proof of service is not attached. (Prince decl., Ex. 1.22.) Additionally, the responses were not verified until July 17, 2026, after Plaintiffs filed this motion. (Prince decl., Ex. 1.23.) Defendant again contends it is unable to show proof that it served responses by email to prior counsel because of the cyber attack. (Prince decl., fn 1.)

      As there is no evidence that Defendant ever served verified responses, the motion is GRANTED.

            Sets Two and Three

      Defendant served responses to Set Two on October 29, 2025, which Defendant contends was the agreed upon deadline. (Prince decl., ¶ 27.) Defendant continued to meet and confer with Plaintiffs’ then counsel, but no further requests to meet and confer were made after the Court vacated the IDC. (Prince decl., ¶ 19, 23.)

      Defendant did not provide any proof of service of the October responses to sets two and three although Defendant contends responses were served on October 29, 2025. Additionally, there is no evidence that Plaintiffs’ former counsel agreed that Defendant’s deadline to respond was October 29, 2025. (Prince, decl. Ex. 7). Rather, Plaintiffs’ former counsel gave an October 24, 2025, deadline. (Prince Ex. 2.0, 7.0.) Mr. Prince declares that Plaintiff’s counsel gave Defendant an extension to respond by October 29, 2025, but does not produce evidence that this agreement was made. (Prince decl., ¶ 15.) Exhibit 5, referenced by Mr. Prince is an October 15, 2025, email from Plaintiffs’ counsel granting a two-week extension, without reference to discovery or a new deadline date.

      Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.

C.     Lack of a separate statement.

      Defendant argues that Plaintiffs were required to serve a separate statement. The content of the responses are not at issue because there is no evidence that Defendant timely served verified responses at all.

D.     Sanctions are warranted.

      Plaintiffs request costs that include $250 per hour for assistance in drafting each motion as well as filing fees. As self-represented litigants, Plaintiffs cannot recovery “attorney’s fees” but may recover costs that the litigant is liable to pay for out-of-pocket legal services.  (Mix v. Tumanjan Development Corp. (2002) 102 Cal.App.4th 1318, 1323.) A litigant in propria persona may recover attorney fees for the legal services of assisting attorneys. (Mix at 1324.)

      Accordingly, the Court imposes sanctions of $1,286 against Defendant, Citywide Management and Consulting, Inc. for the to motion to compel responses to form interrogatories; $1,286 for the motion to compel request for production of documents set 1; and $1,866 to prepare the motion re: documents requests two and three.  

 

V.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, all three of Plaintiffs’ motions to compel responses without objection is GRANTED. Defendant is ordered to serve verified responses to the form interrogatories without objections, and verified responses to documents requests two and three without objection, including production within 30 days.

      Defendant is ordered to pay sanctions to Plaintiff within 30 days as described above.

Case Number: 25STCV23562    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV23562 WEILER V. FRANDZEL ET AL.

Friday, July 31,  2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO STAY PROCEEDINGS PENDING RESOLUTION OF PLAINTIFF’S COUNSEL’S MOTION TO WITHDRAW AS COUNSEL (5618)

I.       BACKGROUND

      This action alleges claims for breach of implied warranty of habitability and related claims arising from conditions in Plaintiff’s residential unit. Plaintiff’s counsel asks for a 60-day stay following Plaintiff’s counsel’s motion to be relieved. Defendant unilaterally noticed Plaintiff’s deposition which should not occur while Plaintiff’s representation is in flux. Plaintiff’s counsel argues ethical obligations requires withdrawing counsel to take reasonable steps to avoid reasonably foreseeable prejudice to the rights of the client, such as giving the client sufficient notice of retain other counsel.

      Defendant opposes the motion because Plaintiff did not establish that a blanket stay is necessary. A stay would interrupt an active discovery dispute. If the Court grants the motion, Defendant asks that the stay exclude the informal discovery conference, the discovery dispute, and Defendant’s ability to pursue a discovery motion.

II.     DISCUSSION

      Plaintiff’s counsel has not demonstrated with any authority that the case should be stayed until Plaintiff can retain new counsel. The delay would disrupt the Court’s efficiency. Defendant would also suffer prejudice brought by delay in resolving the matter.

III.    CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, the motion for a stay is denied.

 

 

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF COUNSEL’S MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL FOR PLAINTIFF ERIN WEILLER (6075) 

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF’S COUNSEL’S MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL FOR PLAINTIFF ERIN WEILLER (8183) 

 

      Max S. Roberts and Julia Vendditti, as counsel for Plaintiff, Erin Weiler, file two separate motions requesting an order to be relieved as counsel of record because of a breakdown in the attorney-client relationship. Both counsel served the motion on the client by mail at an address confirmed within the last 30 days.

      Defendants have not demonstrated that the motion should be denied or deferred until after the Informal Discovery Conference.  While Defendants contend the withdrawal would disrupt an active discovery dispute, Defendant does not cite any authority that gives the Court discretion to force an attorney to represent a recalcitrant client. The proceedings will not be delayed since Defendant can continue to resolve the dispute directly with the Plaintiff.

      Both counsel have complied with the requirements of California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1362. Accordingly, both motions are GRANTED. The order is effective upon the filing of the proof of service of the signed order upon the client.
