GAMEZ, ET AL. v. COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 20STCV49018

HEARING:  09/21/2026 @ 10:30 A.M.

 

#20

TENTATIVE RULING

 

I.             Plaintiff’s motion to vacate the June 24, 2026 order is GRANTED. 

The Court sets a hearing date on November 20, 2026, at 9:00am, for an Order to Show Cause regarding the proper trial date in this matter.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

Background

 

As a result of alleged abuse and neglect by his foster parents and the County of Los Angeles, J.G., a nine-year-old male, died while in the care of his paternal aunt and uncle, Defendants Tomas and Maria Guadalupe Gamez. They were his foster parents. His mother, Maria Gamez, and two siblings, K.G. and A.G., sue the County of Los Angeles; social worker Leticia Garcia; social worker Gabria Alcala; social worker Francine Jimenez; J.G.’s father, Geraldo Gamez; J.G.’s uncle, Tomas Gamez; and J.G.’s aunt, Maria Guadalupe Gamez. The causes of action are the following: (1) wrongful death; (2) negligence; (3) negligent supervision; (4) negligent hiring and retention; (5) violation of Civil Code section 52.1; and (6) survival action. On October 29, 2021, Geraldo Gamez, individually and as personal representative of decedent, filed a cross-complaint against the same parties and decedent’s mother for the following: (1) violation of civil rights under 42 U.S.C. 1983; (2) Monell liability; (3) wrongful death; (4) breach of mandatory duties; (5) breach of duties under special relationship; (6) survival action; and (7) malicious prosecution. Cross-Complainant alleges that the social workers were acting with the course and scope of their duties as employees or agents of the county.

 

On February 18, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Settlement of the Entire Case.  On March 3, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Errata Stating that there were still unsettled defendants – Maria Guadalupepe and Tomas Gamez. 

 

On June 24, 2026, the Court held an Order to Show Cause Re: Dismissal (Settlement) where the entire case was dismissed without prejudice, except as to Defendants Mister Gamez and Maria Gadalupepe who were dismissed with prejudice as case was filed on December 22, 2020, and passed the 5-year statutory period.

 

On July 14, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion. There is no opposition.

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

Plaintiff requests that the Court take judicial notice of 18 documents from the Court’s file in this case. The request is denied. The Court need not take judicial notice of its own case file.

 

Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 583.310 provides that “an action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant.” 

 

An action which is not brought¿to trial¿within the prescribed period must be dismissed. (Code Civ. Proc. § 583.360,¿subd. (a).) These requirements are mandatory “and are not subject to extension, excuse, or exception except as expressly provided by statute.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 583.360,¿subd. (b).) The purpose of the five-year dismissal statute is to prevent the prosecution of stale claims where defendants could be prejudiced by loss of evidence and diminished memories of witnesses.¿(Lewis¿v. Superior¿Court (1985) 175 Cal.App.3d 366, 375, 220¿Cal.Rptr. 594.) 

  

Under¿Code of Civil Procedure¿section 583.340, “In computing the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article, there shall be excluded the time during which any of the following conditions existed:¿(a) The jurisdiction of the court¿to try the action was suspended;¿(b) Prosecution or trial of the action was stayed or enjoined;¿(c) Bringing the action to trial, for any other reason, was impossible, impracticable, or futile.” (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 583.340.)¿¿¿  

 

The statutory period runs from the date of filing of the original complaint. (Davalos v. County of Los Angeles (1983) 142 Cal. App.3d 57, 63.) 

 

This motion is made under Code of Civil Procedure sections 473(b), 473(d), 1008, 128(a)(8), 583.340(b), 583.340(c), 583.350, and 583.360, and under due process principles.

 

Plaintiff argues that the June 24, 2026 dismissal with prejudice should be vacated. The Court dismissed Tomas Gamez/Mister Gamez and Maria Guadalupe Gamez/Maria Guadalupe with prejudice on five-year grounds at an OSC re Dismissal (Settlement), and entered a separate Order of Dismissal as to “MISTER GAMEZ, et al.” on the Court’s own motion, even though no defendant had filed or served a noticed motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 583.360 and Plaintiffs had no meaningful opportunity to brief the statutory exclusions that applied.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d) authorizes the Court to set aside void judgments or orders. Section 473(b) permits relief from a dismissal or order entered through mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Code Civ. Proc. § 473(b).

 

Section 473(b) is remedial and should be liberally applied to permit decisions on the merits. (Zamora v. Clayborn Contracting Grp., Inc., 28 Cal. 4th 249, 254 (2002); Rappleyea v. Campbell, 8 Cal. 4th 975, 980 (1994).)

 

No judgment has been entered to Plaintiffs’ knowledge. (Whibley Decl., ¶ 20) The June 24th ruling is presently a minute order. (RJN, Ex. 13, p. 001.) The Court retains authority to correct or modify interim orders before final judgment. (Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal. 4th 1094, 1108; Darling, Hall & Rae v. Kritt (1999) 75 Cal. App. 4th 1148, 1156–57; Code Civ. Proc. § 128(a)(8).

 

The five-year statute is mandatory only after proper computation of excluded periods. Code Civ. Proc. §§ 583.310, 583.340, 583.360. Section 583.340(b) excludes the time during which prosecution or trial of the action was stayed or enjoined. Code Civ. Proc. § 583.340(b). Section 583.340(c) excludes time during which bringing the action to trial was impossible, impracticable, or futile. Code Civ. Proc. § 583.340(c). This determination is fact-intensive and requires the Court to consider all circumstances, including the plaintiff’s diligence. (Gaines v. Fid. Nat’l Title Ins. Co. (2016) 62 Cal. 4th 1081, 1100–02; Bruns, 51 Cal. 4th at 730–31.)

 

Discussion

 

Pursuant to the Code of Civil Procedure, an action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.310.) This excludes the time during which any of the following conditions existed: (a)¿the jurisdiction of the court to try the action was suspended; (b)¿prosecution or trial of the action was stayed or enjoined; (c)¿bringing the action to trial, for any other reason, was impossible, impracticable, or futile. (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.340(a-c).)

 

An action shall be dismissed by the court on its own motion or on motion of the defendant…if the action is not brought to trial within 5 years, excluding any tolling period. (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.360(a).)

 

For purposes of section 583.340, proceeding with trial may be impracticable or futile when it would be difficult to proceed with trial before other related litigation is fully resolved. (Reserve Ins. Co. v. Universal Underwriters Ins. Co., 51 Cal.App.3d 57, 61–62 (1975).) Moreover, [the] trial court must determine what is impossible, impracticable, or futile “in light of all the circumstances in the individual case, including the acts and conduct of the parties and the nature of the proceedings themselves.” (Bruns v. E-Commerce Exchange, Inc.¿(2011) 51 Cal.4th 717, 730.) 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) states,

 

“The court¿may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical¿mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or¿order directed, and¿may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party,¿set aside any¿void¿judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d), emphasis added.)¿ 

 

An order is considered “void on its face only when the invalidity is apparent from an¿inspection of the judgment roll or court record without consideration of extrinsic¿evidence.” (Pittman v. Beck Park Apartments Ltd. (2018) 20 Cal.App.5th 1009,¿1021.)¿ “There is no time limit to attack a judgment void on its face.” (Ibid.) However, if “the invalidity is not apparent [by an inspection of the judgment or the judgment roll], it may be set aside by motion, if such motion is filed within a reasonable time, i. e., not longer than the time set forth in Code of Civil Procedure, section 473.” (City of Los Angeles v. Morgan (1951) 105 Cal.App.2d 726, 730.) Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) contains no express time limit, and the California Supreme Court recently abrogated the rule that Section 473, subdivision (d) is subject to the two-year limit set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5. (California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn (2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, 225-226 [“We hold that a section 473(d) motion to vacate a judgment that is void for lack of proper service is not subject to the judicially imposed two-year limitation”].)¿¿ 

 

Plaintiffs also contend that the five-year period had not expired because time was excluded under Code of Civil Procedure section 583.340(b). The Court placed the case in special status as “Stay - Conditional Settlement,” stated that the settlement would be deemed conditional, set a settlement dismissal OSC, vacated all future dates, and advanced and vacated the final status conference and trial. During that period, Plaintiffs could not unilaterally bring the case to trial because the trial date had been vacated and the case remained in a court-created conditional settlement stay posture.

 

Because fewer than six months remained before the April 23, 2026, stipulated outside date when the Court placed the case in conditional-settlement stay status and vacated trial-related dates on February 18, 2026, Plaintiffs contend that Code of Civil Procedure section 583.350 applies. Plaintiffs therefore request that the Court reinstate the claims against the nonsettling defendants, lift or clarify the stay as to those defendants, and set trial within the applicable six-month period after the excluded period ends.  CCP §583.340(a). Here, it appears that the Court’s calculation of the 5-year-deadline may have been in error, and therefore, the Court sets an OSC re dismissal so that the exact date may be calculated by which Plaintiffs must bring this case to trial.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court GRANTS the motion to vacate and sets a hearing date on November 20, 2026, at 9:00am, for an Order to Show Cause regarding the proper trial date in this matter.


Case Number: 23NWCV00990    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: D

ELIDA CARDOZA, ET AL. V. CITY OF COMPTON, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  23NWCV00990

HEARING:  09/21/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#21

Defendant/Cross-Complainant’s MOTION TO BIFURCATE

 

Having considered the briefs filed by the parties and the arguments of counsel made at the hearing, the Court rules as follows:

 

Defendant City of Compton's motion for bifurcation is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant City of Compton (the “City”) moves to bifurcate the issues of liability and damages.

 

Background

 

On March 30, 2023, Plaintiffs filed the operative Complaint against the City, David Blake Jr. (Blake), and Does 1 through 50 (collectively, Defendants), asserting four causes of action: (1) Negligence (Wrongful Death); (2) Battery (Wrongful Death); (3) Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress; and (4) Violation of the Bane Act (Civil Code section 52.1.)

 

Plaintiffs allege that Blake unlawfully and without provocation shot and killed Julio Moises Cardoza (Decedent) in front of his family after accompanying Councilmember Isaac Galvan (Galvan) to the family’s home.  Plaintiffs claim the City negligently hired, trained, and supervised Blake, who was armed and acting within the scope of his employment at the time, making the City vicariously liable.  Plaintiffs further allege emotional distress and civil rights violations arising from witnessing the shooting.

 

Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1048, subdivision (b), provides: “The court, in furtherance of convenience or to avoid prejudice, or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy, may order a separate trial of any cause of action, including a cause of action asserted in a cross-complaint, or of any separate issue or of any number of causes of action or issues, preserving the right of trial by jury required by the Constitution or a statute of this state or of the United States.” 

Code of Civil Procedure section 598 permits the Court, “when the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economy and efficiency of handling the litigation would be promoted thereby, on motion of a party, after notice and hearing,” to order “that the trial of any issue or any part thereof shall precede the trial of any other issue or any part thereof in the case.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 598.) Evidence Code section 320 provides: “Except as otherwise provided by law, the court in its discretion shall regulate the order of proof.” Section 1048, subdivision (b), and Evidence Code section 320 together give a trial court broad discretion over the order in which issues are tried. (Grappo v. Coventry Financial Corp. (1991) 235 Cal.App.3d 496, 504 (Grappo) An order granting or refusing separate trials is disturbed only for a manifest abuse of that discretion. (Finley v. Superior Court (2000) 80 Cal.App.4th 1152, 1163 (Finley).) 

 

Analysis

 

Defendant argues that whether Defendant Compton is liable for the intentional criminal act of shooting and killing the decedent is a distinct legal and factual inquiry wholly separate from the extent of Plaintiffs' damages. Bifurcation is appropriate because the issue of liability will be expeditious and economic. It will involve fewer witnesses, whereas the issue of damages will necessitate the expenditure of much more money, time, and resources. This amount of evidence pertaining to the nature and extent of the medical care and treatment provided to Plaintiffs as well as the emotional toll of Julio Cardoza’s death will be unnecessary should the liability phase of the trial be found in Defendant’s favor.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that rather than promoting efficiency, bifurcation would cause unnecessary delay, burden witnesses, and waste judicial resources. Given the substantial overlap between liability and damages evidence, a single trial is the most efficient and just course. Under Code of Civil Procedure §§ 598 and 1048(b), bifurcation is discretionary and must serve judicial economy, witness convenience, or the interests of justice. Courts routinely deny bifurcation where: separately. (See Downey Sav. & Loan Assn. v. Ohio Cas. Ins. Co. (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 1072, 1086; Grappo v. Coventry Fin. Corp. (1991) 235 Cal.App.3d 396, 504.) Bifurcation would lengthen trial proceedings, require redundant testimony, and impose unnecessary burdens on witnesses, the Court, and the parties. Bifurcation would require multiple witnesses, including children, parents, and experts, to testify twice, disrupting their lives and significantly extending the length of trial. It would necessitate: Defendant has failed to demonstrate how bifurcation would serve judicial economy. Courts recognize that evidence relevant to liability often overlaps with damages. (Krouse v. Graham (1977) 19 Cal.3d 59, 78-79.) Bifurcation in this case would result in a duplicative and inefficient trial. The parties have identified a total of 21 witnesses: 17 witnesses for the liability portion and four witnesses which are exclusively damages (Sidra Corbin, Verenice Dutcher, Jose Gonzalez, Ena Luna). Of the 17 liability witnesses, four will also testify regarding damages (Elida, Julio and Michelle Cardoza and Det. Jason Estrada). Of the four witnesses identified by the Plaintiffs which are exclusively damages, Plaintiff does not intend to call all four. The incident and Plaintiffs damages are interconnected in an inseverable way. The jury will be able see and hear the nature and extent of Plaintiffs damages when they testify in the liability portion of the trial as they recount the events leading up to the death of their loved one. There is no way to sever the events from the loss that resulted as both Plaintiffs have NIED claims which will require the Plaintiffs to each recount the events each time they testify. Defendant also fails to establish undue prejudice warranting bifurcation under Code of Civil Procedure § 1048(b). Courts have consistently held that prejudicial is not synonymous with damaging. (People v. Doolin (2009) 45 Cal.4th 390, 439; Rufo v. Simpson (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 573, 597.)

 

In reply, Defendant argues that the issue of liability rests on the nature of Mr. Blake’s job duties and the time, place, and circumstances of the shooting. Alternatively, the issue of damages involves only the magnitude of Plaintiffs’ loss and the extent to which Plaintiffs’ lives have been impacted by Mr. Cardoza’s murder. There are, at most, four witnesses who are expected to testify on the issues of both liability and damages. Of these four witnesses identified by Plaintiffs (Elida Cardoza, Julio Cardoza, Michelle Cardoza, and Detective Jason Estrada), two of them (Michelle Cardoza and Detective Jason Estrada) would be unable to offer testimony relevant to the question of whether Mr. Blake was acting within the scope of his employment at the time of the shooting because they were not present at the time of the incident and have no personal knowledge of Mr. Blake’s employment with Defendant. Thus, Michelle Cardoza and Detective Estrada cannot offer any testimony relevant to the issue of liability, leaving at most two witnesses (Elida Cardoza and Julio Cardoza) who may offer testimony relevant to both issues.

 

As noted by Plaintiff, who carries the burden at trial, the parties have identified a total of 21 witnesses: 17 witnesses for the liability portion and four witnesses which are exclusively damages (Sidra Corbin, Verenice Dutcher, Jose Gonzalez, Ena Luna). Of the 17 liability witnesses, four will also testify regarding damages (Elida, Julio and Michelle Cardoza and Det. Jason Estrada). Of the four witnesses identified by the Plaintiffs which are exclusively damages, Plaintiff does not intend to call all four. The incident and Plaintiffs damages are interconnected. Here, the Court finds that the Plaintiff’s argument is well-taken and exercises its discretion to not bifurcate the trial. 

         

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant City of Compton's motion to bifurcate is DENIED.

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

The Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is GRANTED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

The clerk will give notice.

 

Plaintiff Ramon Molina, in propria persona, filed this action against Defendant Michael Hakimi a on June 27, 2025.  The only allegation stated on the Judicial Council Form Complaint is that “wrongfuly implanted when I was undeager & more as we gone along the years mini speaker min micerphone & GPS and some to rout my insides His ex wife of sister monica also told me of it.”

 

The Court noted that there were no appearances entered at the Case Management Conferences held on November 21, 2025, January 26, 2026 and June 24, 2026.  On January 26, 2026, the Court set an Order to Show Cause Re: Dismissal for Plaintiff’s Failure to Appear on 11/21/25/Pursuant to CCP §438(c)(3)(b) for June 24, 2026. 

 

At the hearing on June 24, 2026, the Court noted that no appearances were entered by the parties and no Case Management Statement had been filed.  The Court, on its own motion, set the matter for a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438.  The Order to Show Cause Re Dismissal for Plaintiff’s Failure to Appear on 11/21/25, 1/26/26 and 6/24/26/Pursuant to CCP §438(3)(b) was continued to September 22, 2026.

 

As of September 22, 2026, Plaintiff has not filed any documents in response to the Court’s order and has not filed proof of serving Defendant.  According to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, subdivisions (b)(2) and (c)(3), “[t]he court may upon its own motion grant a motion for judgment on the pleadings” on the basis that “the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant.”  Here, there are no causes of action specified on the Form Complaint and the allegations are composed of a single handwritten sentence.  Plaintiff has not filed any papers to clarify the basis for his claims and, thus, has not shown that amendment will cure this defect.

 

Accordingly, the Court’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is GRANTED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.
