Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: August 7, 2026

Case Name: Kunze v. Carpenter & Zuckerman, LLP, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV01356

Matter: Motion to Compel Further Responses

Moving Party: Plaintiff Domanic Kunze

Responding Party: Defendants Carpenter & Zuckerman and Paul Zuckerman

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is denied.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On January 17, 2025, Plaintiff Domanic Kunze filed a complaint for (1) legal malpractice, (2) breach of fiduciary duty, and (3) breach of contract.

On April 16, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”).  Plaintiff alleges that he lost his arm in an injury but that his former attorneys negligently settled his personal injury suit for only $21,402.49.

Plaintiff now seeks to compel Defendants Carpenter & Zuckerman and Paul Zuckerman to provide further responses to his requests for admission.

The Motion is denied. Seeking information about attorney departures relative to firm misconduct has no bearing on whether the standard of care was breached. Further, the term “catastrophic injury” is vague and subjective. For the remaining RFAs, Defendants either appropriately denied the RFA or stated they were unable to admit or deny the RFA. The Court cannot force more ideal responses.

No sanctions.

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV25814    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: August 7, 2026

Case Name: Law v. Activision Blizzard, Inc., et al.

Case No.: 25STCV25814 

Matter: Motion for Protective Order

Motions to Compel Further Responses (4x)

Ruling: The Motion for Protective Order is granted in part.

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel as to Interrogatories is granted.


Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel as to Documents is granted in part.


Defendants’ Motion to Compel as to Interrogatories is granted.


Defendants' Motion  to Compel as to Documents is granted in part.


Defendants to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is an employment action. 

There are five discovery motions at issue now.


  1. Protective Order

Defendants Activision Blizzard, Inc. and Sledgehammer Games, Inc. seek a protective order that distinguishes between confidential and highly confidential designations.

Good cause must be shown for the protective order, and the issuance and formulation of the protective order are within the Court’s discretion.  (See, e.g., Mercury Interactive Corp. v. Klein (2007) 158 Cal.App.4th 60, 106-7; Raymond Handling Concepts Corp. v. Superior Court (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 584, 588.)

The Court will enter its standard, model protective order with only ordinary confidential designations. Defendants failed to carry their burden to show justification for a highly confidential designation. The focus of this case is apparently Plaintiff’s communication abilities. 

The Motion is granted in part as set forth herein. No sanctions.


  1. Plainitff—FI employment

Plaintiff seeks to compel Sledgehammer to provide further responses to her form interrogatories, employment. 

The Motion is granted. Defendant’s boilerplate objections lack merit and now that a protective order has been entered, there is no basis not to identify or produce the subject documents.

Further responses are to be served within 20 days. No sanctions. 


  1. Plaintiff—Documents

Plaintiff seeks to compel Sledgehammer to provide further responses to her requests for production. 

The Motion is granted in part. 

For RFP no. 11, Defendant should produce a privilege log if a privilege applies. 

The Motion is denied as to RFP no. 14, which is overbroad. 

For the remaining RFPs, Defendant’s boilerplate objections lack merit and now that a protective order has been entered, there is no basis not to identify or produce the subject documents.

Further responses are to be served within 20 days. No sanctions. 


  1. Defendants—FI general

Defendants seek to compel further response to their form interrogatories, general, from Plaintiff.

The Motion is granted. The responses are incomplete and/or the objections lack merit. The term “incident” should in good faith be construed to mean Plaintiff’s termination. (Deyo v. Kilbourne (1978) 84 Cal.App.3d 771, 783.)

Further responses are to be provided within 20 days.

No sanctions.


  1. Defendants—Documents

Defendants seek to compel further response to their requests for production from Plaintiff.

The Motion is denied as to RFP nos. 54 and 62,  which are overbroad.

As to the remaining RFPs, the responses are deficient and the objections lack merit. If there is a privilege, then a privilege log should be provided. If Plaintiff cannot produce documents because they do not exist, Plaintiff should also affirm a diligent search and inquiry. 

The Motion is granted in part. Further responses are to be provided within 20 days.

No sanctions.

Defendants to give notice.


Case Number: 26STCV07943    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: August 7, 2026

Case Name: Johnson v. Montravena, et al.

Case No.: 26STCV07943

Matter: Motion to Quash Service of Summons 

Moving Party: Defendant James Blucker 

Responding Party: Plaintiff Patrick Johnson

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion to Quash is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


Defendant James Blucker now seeks to quash service of summons because “the clerk-issued summons identifies only ‘David Gadish, individually and as agent of Geffen Real Estate and DOES 1–20, inclusive’ as defendants. The summons does not state on its face that an Additional Parties Attachment is attached. Defendant Blucker is listed only on a separate SUM-200(A) Additional Parties Attachment. That defect is jurisdictional. A summons must be directed to the defendant over whom jurisdiction is sought. The summons here was not directed to Defendant Blucker.” 

A defendant may move to quash service of summons on the ground the Court lacks personal jurisdiction.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 418.10(a)(1).)  “Although the defendant is the moving party, the plaintiff must carry the initial burden of demonstrating facts by a preponderance of evidence justifying the exercise of jurisdiction in California.”  (In re Automobile Antitrust Cases I and II (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 100, 110.)

Defendant’s argument is well-taken. The initial summons is quashed with respect to Defendant Blucker. 

Plaintiff has now filed an amended summons naming Blucker. The proof of service was “by mail and acknowledgment of receipt of service.” No such acknowledgement, however, has apparently been provided such that service is not complete. Plaintiff should now complete service of the amended summons.

The Motion to Quash is granted.

Moving party to give notice.
