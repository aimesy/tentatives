[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER DISCOVERY RESPONSES FROM DEFENDANT GLENDALE ADVENTIST MEDICAL CENTER dba ADVENTIST HEALTH GLENDALE

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

August 18, 2026

 

)

 

 

On February 13, 2026, plaintiff Rafik Khudabhkhshyan (“Plaintiff”) filed this motion for an order compelling further responses from defendant Adventist Health Glendale (“Defendant”) to Request for Admission (“RFA”) Nos. 30 through 35, and 38, along with Form Interrogatory (“FROG”) No. 17.1. Plaintiff also requests monetary sanctions against Defendant and its counsel of record. After the initial hearing on July 8, 2026, the parties submitted supplemental briefing on July 15 and July 22, 2026.

The RFAs ask Defendant to admit certain facts about any investigations performed into the incident underlying this action, which involves Plaintiff falling off an operating table on September 1, 2023. The RFAs were the subject of an earlier motion Plaintiff filed on August 8, 2025 (which the Court granted on October 30, 2025).

Defendant’s latest responses include objections the Court previously discussed in its October 30, 2025, minute order. In that minute order, the Court stated that Defendant failed to meet its burden to show that Evidence Code section 1157 applies or that there were investigations by a medical staff committee or peer review committee. The Court also stated that “[t]o the extent that any information or findings were determined through investigations that were not conducted by medical staff or peer review committees, or Defendant’s attorneys, they [such information or findings] are discoverable.” Since Defendant failed to show that Evidence Code § 1157 applied to any of its responses, the objection was overruled.

Now, Defendant states that its responses to Plaintiff’s requests for admission were made “following a reasonable inquiry of the non-privileged information known or readily available” and that “Defendant is presently unable to admit or deny based on lack of sufficient knowledge or information.” Defendant argues that its responses to the RFAs are sufficient because the responses do not need to be based on privileged information. In the abstract, this is true; RFA responses do not need to be based on privileged information. However, the party claiming a privilege has the initial burden of proving the preliminary facts to show the privilege applies (Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Soc'y of New York, Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 596) and generally makes the required preliminary showing by declarations. (Citizens for Ceres v. Superior Court (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 889, 911.)

Here, Defendant fails to supply any evidence to show that the information or documents relied on are privileged. Defendant only asserts in its privilege log that fall event reports were “created in anticipation of litigation” or immune from discovery due to Evidence Code § 1151 without providing any evidentiary support that those protections apply. Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED. The Court overrules Defendant’s objections based on Evidence Code § 1151, the attorney-client privilege, and the attorney work product doctrine and Defendant is ordered to serve further responses to RFA Nos. 30-35 and 38, and FROG No. 17.1 within 20 days of the date of this answer. The documents identified in Defendant’s Further Response to Form Interrogatory No. 17.1(d) with respect to RFA Nos. 30-35 and 38 must be produced.

Sanctions are imposed upon Defendant and counsel of record in the reduced amount of $2,071.76, consisting of 5 hours at Plaintiff’s counsel’s reasonable hourly rate of $400 and a $71.76 filing fee, payable within 20 days of the date of this Order.

On July 20, 2026, defendant General Motors LLC (“Defendant”) filed this motion to tax costs claimed by plaintiff Emin Ter Saakyan (“Plaintiff”) after accepting a settlement offer made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 998 (“998 Offer”). Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s memorandum of costs should be reduced by $3,769.54. (Motion, p. 3.)  

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s claimed costs are not compensable because they were unreasonable and unnecessary. The Song-Beverly Act requires allowing costs “determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution” of the case, which is reviewed under the abuse of discretion standard. (Levy v. Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 807, 813; see also generally Duff v. Jaguar Land Rover N. Am., LLC (2022) 74 Cal.App.5th 491, 501 [summarizing Civ. C. § 1794(d)].) “If the items on a verified cost bill appear proper charges, they are prima facie evidence that the costs, expenses and services therein listed were necessarily incurred. [Citations.] Where the items are properly objected to, they are put in issue, and the burden of proof is upon the party claiming them as costs. [Citation.]” (Rappenecker v. Sea-Land Service, Inc. (1979) 93 Cal. App. 3d 256, 266.

The costs challenged by Defendant include: (1) $220.71 in filing & motion fees, (2) $150 in jury fees, (3) $2,720.90 in witness fees for expert David Gurul, (4) $196.18 for service of process, (5) $29.25 in fees for electronic filing or service, and (6) 452.20 in “other” costs described as “AppearMe service fee, mediation fee.”

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that jury fees were properly incurred in anticipation of trial and failure to pay jury fees before the initial case management conference would result in a waiver of a jury trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 631(c).) Therefore, Plaintiff could not have waited to see if the matter would go to trial before paying them. The Court agrees and denies the request to tax this amount from Plaintiff’s cost memorandum.

Second, Defendant complains that there is no indication of who Maddie Bright and Lisa Hernandez are and why they needed to be served with process. In opposition, Plaintiff sufficiently explains that these two individuals were served as agents of service of process for each of the two corporate defendants in this matter: Defendant and Camino Real Chevrolet. (Margarian Decl., Ex. A.) Nevertheless, Defendant should not have to pay for the cost to serve a different defendant. Accordingly, the charge to serve Camino Real Chevrolet is taxed in the amount of $134.23.

Third, retaining appearance counsel for two occasions at a cost of $95 each is common and reasonable. The mediation expense of $262.50 is also reasonable in the prosecution of this case because it represents Plaintiff’s 1/3 share of a mediation that involved three matter. The mediation occurred while the action was pending and was reasonably undertaken to facilitate resolution of the parties’ dispute. The Court therefore denies the request to tax these expenses.

Fourth, Plaintiff’s counsel cannot recover costs that have not been incurred, therefore the $22.70 in anticipated filing fees for Plaintiff’s pending fee motion is taxed from the memorandum of costs. (Civ. Code, § 1794(d) [awarding costs “reasonably incurred”]).

Fifth, the Court denies the motion to tax expert fees paid to David Gurul in the amount of $2,797.90. Plaintiff’s counsel states that Mr. Gurul was retained to inspect the Vehicle and evaluate the defects underlying Plaintiff’s claims, which “assisted counsel in evaluating the Vehicle’s condition, the reported defects, the repair history, liability, damages, civil-penalty exposure, and settlement value.” (Margarian Decl., ¶ 15.) The fact that Plaintiff did not designate a trial expert is irrelevant because the matter settled before it went to trial.

Finally, Plaintiff does not explain why the $29.25 sought for electronic filings or service was reasonably incurred or necessary to this action. Plaintiff’s counsel does not identify which filings are associated with the claimed costs and there is no invoice attached showing that those costs were incurred.

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s claimed costs of $4,302.94 are reduced by $186.18 for a total award of $4,116.76 in costs.

Defendant City of Alhambra (“Defendant”) moves for an order compelling further responses from plaintiff Anne Cho (“Plaintiff”) to Form Interrogatories Nos. 6.2 and 10.1. Although Plaintiff served updated responses before the hearing, Defendant contends that sanctions are appropriate because Plaintiff’s earlier responses were insufficient and required Defendant to file this motion.

Form Interrogatory No. 6.2 asks Plaintiff to identify each injury and the area of her body affected by the incident. Plaintiff’s responses improperly referenced medical records without specifying particular records and included objections on the grounds that she is not a medical expert who can provide medical diagnoses or expert opinion.

Form Interrogatory No. 10.1 asked Plaintiff to describe any complaints or injuries to the same parts of the body that she claims were injured in the incident underlying this action. In her initial response and subsequent amended responses, Plaintiff claimed she was unable to provide an answer because she has not “reviewed her medical records from birth to present time” and claimed she would have to speak with all her treating physicians. Plaintiff also claimed she “never experienced any neck and back pain in the same severity and frequency that [she] had after the slip-and-fall on February 7, 2024.” Furthermore, Plaintiff’s latest response states that she has not had any complaint or injury that involved the same part of her body, demonstrating that this motion could have been avoided in its entirety if Plaintiff did not continually provide evasive responses.

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Defendant’s request for sanctions and imposes sanctions upon Plaintiff and her attorney of record, jointly and severally, in the amount of $1,260, consisting of 1.5 hours to meet and confer and 2.7 hours to prepare this motion and supporting papers, billed at an hourly rate of $300. Sanctions are payable within 20 days of the date of this Order.

Plaintiff Christopher M. Zakhary moves for an order compelling further responses from defendant BMW of North America, LLC (“Defendant”) to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 23-32. No sanctions are requested.

Plaintiff leased a 2023 BMW 530e on March 10, 2023 and contends the vehicle was delivered with “serious defects and nonconformities to warranty including, but not limited to, emission, engine, electrical and suspension system defects” and developed other serious defects and nonconformities. (Compl., ¶¶ 9, 11.) Plaintiff alleges he presented the vehicle for repairs in December 2023 reporting the “service engine soon” light illuminating on the instrument cluster. (Compl., ¶ 12.) Subsequently, on three occasions in July, September, and December of 2025, Plaintiff presented the vehicle and reported that the “check engine” light was illuminated. (Compl., ¶¶ 13-15.)

RFP Nos. 23, 25-30: Plaintiff seeks any document which refers or relates to Defendant’s Warranty Policy and Procedural Manuals provided to its authorized repair facilities from 2020 to the present. The request is GRANTED in part and limited to Defendant’s Warranty Policy and Procedure Manuals provided to Defendant’s authorized repair facilities in California from March 10, 2023, to the present (not all documents “referring or relating” to the policies or manuals).

RFP No. 24: DENIED.

RFP No. 30-32: DENIED. These requests are not stated with reasonable particularity and are vague and ambiguous with respect to the defects that affect the vehicle at issue in this action.
