VAISA, SR., ET AL. v. DOLPHIN PLACE PARTNERS, LLC, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV01134 

HEARING: 09/24/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#8

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Petitioner John Angelo Vaisa Sr.’s Petitions for Approval of Compromise of Claim for Minor claimants Jacob Vaisa, Jonathan Vaisa, and Jaylene Vaisa are GRANTED.

 

II.            Petitioner Erika Vaisa’s Petitions for Approval of Compromise of Claim for Minor claimants Jesus Oswaldo Martinez and Stephanie Martinez are GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

There are five Petitions to Confirm Minors’ Compromises (Petitions) on calendar. Petitioner John Angelo Vaisa Sr. petitions for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds for minor claimants (1) Jonathan Vaisa, (2) Jaylene Vaisa, and (3) Jacob Vaisa. Petitioner Erika Vaisa petitions for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds for minor claimants (4) Jesus Oswaldo Martinez and (5) Stephanie Martinez.

 

Background

 

On April 12, 2023, Plaintiffs John Angelo Vaisa Sr.; Erika Vaisa; Jesus Oswaldo Martinez and Stephanie Martinez, minors, by and through their Guardian Ad Litem, Erika Vaisa; and Jaylene Angela Vaisa and Jonathan Joseph Vaisa, minors, by and through their Guardian Ad Litem, John Angelo Vaisa Sr. (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed this habitability action against Defendants Dolphin Place Partners, LLC; Dashmesh K Chopra; Ivon Monteon; Leticia Lopez; and Does 1 through 30 (collectively, Defendants). On June 2, 2023, Plaintiffs filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC). The FAC alleges that Plaintiffs, as tenants of 8434 Tweedy Lane #102, Downey, State of California, 90240 (Subject Premises) notified Defendants, the owners and managers of the Subject Premises, of mold in their unit, to which Defendants failed to take sufficient action. (FAC, ¶¶ 9-16.) The FAC asserts four causes of action: (1) breach of the implied warranty of habitability; (2) negligent training and supervision; (3) violation of Civil Code § 1942.4; and (4) violation of the Unfair Competition Law [Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17200].

 

On September 2, 2026, the Court granted in part and denied in part Plaintiffs’ ex parte application to advance the Petitions. The Petition as to Jacob Vasia was advanced to September 8, 2026 and all other Petitioners were advanced to September 24, 2026.

 

On September 8, 2026, the Petition to Confirm Minor’s Compromise as to Claimant Jacob Vasia first came on for hearing. The Court continued the hearing to today’s date to be heard concurrently with the four other Petitions to allow Petitioner to file an omitted attachment.

 

On September 14, 2026, Petitioners John Angelo Vaisa Sr. and Erika Vaisa (collectively, Petitioners) filed four Notice of Erratas to the Petitions.

 

Legal Standard

 

A Petition for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment for minor or person with a disability must conform with Code of Civil Procedure section 372 and Probate Code sections 2504 and 3600 et seq. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Such a petition “must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Moving papers should include the Petition of Guardian Ad Litem for Compromise of Disputed Claim of Minor or Person with Disability (Petition) on Form MC-350 and an Order Approving Compromise of Minor’s Claim (Order) Form MC-351.

 

Discussion

 

Petitioner John Angelo Vaisa Sr., as Guardian ad Litem and father of Claimants Jonathan Vaisa, age four; (2) Jaylene Vaisa, age seven; and (3) Jacob Vaisa and Petitioner Erika Vaisa, as Guardian ad Litem and mother of Claimants (4) Jesus Oswaldo Martinez, age seventeen; and (5) Stephanie Martinez, age fifteen move to approve the Petitions.

 

Based on the similarities between the Petitions, the Court will review them jointly in this ruling and note any relevant differences.

 

Petitioners provide Petitions on Form MC-350, Proposed Orders on Form MC-351, and Proposed Orders to Deposit Funds in Blocked Account on Form-355.
These Petitions are unopposed.

 

Allocation

 

Defendants will pay $25,000 to each Claimant as part of a global settlement to all Plaintiffs in the amount of $410,000. (Pet., ¶ 10; Attach.,10c.) As reflected in Attachment 10c, the agreement was agreed to verbally in a mediation. Petitioner contends that Defendants have not provided a long-form agreement. (Pet., ¶ 10c.)

 

Petitioners are each Plaintiffs in this action and will each receive $142,500. (Pet., ¶ 11b(5).) Petitioners indicate that the reason for the allocation is so Petitioners can use their net recovery towards purchasing a family residence. (Attach., 11b(6).)

 

Each Claimants’ net recovery will be $20,000. (Pet., ¶ 15.)

 

          Medical Expenses

 

The Petitions do not claim any medical expenses. (Pet., ¶ 12a.) Petitioners represent that there are no statutory or contractual liens for payment of Claimants’ medical expenses. (Pet., ¶ 12b(5).)  

 

          Attorney’s Fees & Costs

 

The Petitions seek approval of $5,000 in attorney’s fees from each Claimant. (Pet., ¶ 13a; Attach., 13a.) This represents a 20% contingency fee that Plaintiffs’ counsel charges for minors. (Golde Decl., ¶ 8.) Plaintiffs’ counsel provides the retainer agreement, signed by Petitioners, in which Petitioners agreed to a 25% contingency fee. Thus, Plaintiffs’ counsel voluntarily reduced the fee to 20% as to Claimant. (Golde Decl., ¶ 8; Attach., 17a.) The Petitions do not seek costs. (Pet., ¶ 13b.)

 

Based on the declaration of counsel and information provided in these Petitions, the Court finds this amount reasonable. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.955.)

 

          Disposition of Settlement Funds

 

Petitioners request that the proceeds be deposited in insured accounts in one or more financial institutions in this state, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the Court. (Pet., ¶ 18b(2).) Petitioners include the requisite information in Attachment 18b(2) submitted for each Claimant on September 14, 2026.

 

Conclusion

 

The Court finds the Petitions are fair and reasonable as to each Claimant.

 

Petitioners John Angelo Vaisa Sr. and Erika Vaisa’s petitions for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds for minor claimants (1) Jonathan Vaisa, (2) Jaylene Vaisa, (3) Jacob Vaisa, (4) Jesus Oswaldo Martinez, and (5) Stephanie Martinez are GRANTED.

 

The Court notes that the Proposed Orders reflect the hearing dates prior to the advancements or continuations. All five Petitions are heard and granted on this date.

 

 

Case Number: 23NWCV03447    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: P

CORTES-VIDALES v. GENERAL MOTORS, LLC, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV03447 

HEARING:  09/24/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#4

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Plaintiff’s motion for attorney’s fees is GRANTED in the total amount of $18,380.

 

II.            The Court will consider costs at the hearing on Defendant’s upcoming motion to strike or tax costs.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Delfino Cortes-Vidales (Plaintiff) moves for an award of attorney’s fees and costs.

 

Background

 

On October 26, 2023, Plaintiff filed this lemon law action against Defendants General Motors, LLC (Defendant), UAG Cerritos I, LLC dba Penske Chevrolet of Cerritos, and Does 1 through 20. This action arises out of alleged defects and nonconformities to warranty that Plaintiff experienced with his purchase of a new 2020 Chevrolet Traverse. (Complaint, ¶¶ 6-9.) The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) Breach of Implied Warranty of Merchantability under the Song-Beverly Warranty Act and (2) Breach of Express Warranty under the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

On December 10, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case indicating that the parties entered into a conditional settlement.

 

Legal Standard

 

The Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act provides for the award of attorneys’ fees to prevailing plaintiffs as follows: 

 

If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action. 

 

(Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).) 

 

“Courts have uniformly held that the party moving for statutory attorney fees or sanctions has the burden of proof.” (Mikhaeilpoor v. BMW of North America, LLC (2020) 48 Cal.App.5th 240, 247, citation omitted.) The matter of reasonableness of a party’s attorney fees is within the sound discretion of the trial court. (Bruckman v. Parliament Escrow Co. (1989) 190 Cal.App.3d 1051, 1062.) When assessing the amount of any attorney’s fee award, courts typically determine what is reasonable through the application of the “lodestar” method. Under the lodestar method, a base amount is calculated from a compilation of (1) time reasonably spent and (2) the reasonable hourly compensation of each attorney. (Serrano v. Priest (1977) 20 Cal.3d 25, 48); see also Meister v. Regents of University of California (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 437, 448-449 [holding that the lodestar method applies to statutory attorney fees award unless the underlying statute provides for another method of calculation].)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an award of attorney’s fees and costs in in the amount of $24,420.48, including $22,965 in attorney’s fees and $1,453.48 costs.

 

Entitlement to Fees

 

Plaintiff argues that he is the prevailing party pursuant to the parties’ settlement agreement via Defendant’s Code of Civil Procedure section 998 offer. (Nita Decl., ¶ 15.) Defendant does not dispute that Plaintiff is the prevailing party and thus entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees and costs.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff is entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees and costs.

 

Reasonableness of the Hourly Rates

 

A “reasonable” hourly rate is the prevailing rate charged by attorneys of similar skill and experience in the relevant community. (PLCM Group, Inc. v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.) “The experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court […].” (Ibid.) “[R]ate determinations in other cases, particularly those setting a rate for the plaintiffs' attorney, are satisfactory evidence of the prevailing market rate.” (Heritage Pacific Financial, LLC v. Monroy (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 972, 1009.) “Courts have uniformly held that the party moving for statutory attorney fees or sanctions has the burden of proof.” (Mikhaeilpoor, supra, 48 Cal.App.5th at p. 247, citation omitted.)

Plaintiff argues that the hourly rates are reasonable based on the risks of taking a case on a contingency basis and rates approved in prior cases.

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that Mr. Nita’s hourly rate should be reduced to $450 and Mr. Wisnewski’s to $350. Defendant contends the Court should use the 2023 “Real Rate Report: The Industry’s Leading Analysis of Law Firm Rates, Trends, and Practices” (Real Rate Report) and the General Liability by City data set for Los Angeles. Defendant emphasizes that the hourly rate is based on the prevailing rate of “or private attorneys in the community conducting noncontingent litigation of the same type.” (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal. 4th 1122, 1133.)

 

Here, the Court finds a reduction to $600 reasonable based on review of both parties’ evidence and Mr. Nita’s experience. The Court does not adjust Mr. Wisnewski’s hourly rate.

 

Of the 18.3 hours billed by Mr. Nita as put forth through the billing records, this amounts to a reduction of $457.50.

 

          Reasonableness of the Fees

 

The Court is vested with discretion to determine which claimed hours were reasonably spent and what an attorney’s reasonable hourly rate is. (Dover Mobile Estates v. Fiber Form Products, Inc. (1990) 220 Cal.App.3d 1494, 1501; see also Flannery v. California Highway Patrol (1987) 61 Cal.App.4th 629, 644 [“We readily acknowledge the discretion of the trial judge to determine the value of professional services rendered in his or her court.”].) “As the plain wording of section 1794, subdivision (d) makes clear, the trial court is ‘to base the fee award upon actual time expended on the case, as long as such fees are reasonably incurred—both from the standpoint of time spent and the amount charged.’” (Mikhaeilpoor, supra, 48 Cal.App.5th at p. 247, citation omitted.) “[C]ounsels’ time records [should be used] as the starting point for [a court’s] lodestar determination.” (Horsford v. Board of Trustees of CSU (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 397.) In assessing a motion for attorney’s fees, “[a] trial court may not rubber stamp a request for attorney fees, but must determine the number of hours reasonably expended.” (Morris v. Hyundai Motor America (2019) 41 Cal.App.5th 24, 38.)

 

Plaintiff seeks $22,965 in attorney’s fees, representing 62 hours of work, based on the nature and complexity of the litigation and Plaintiff’s counsel’s skill and expertise. Plaintiff supplies Plaintiff’s counsel’s billing records in support of the fee request. (Nita Decl., Exh. A.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s request does not reflect fees that were actually or reasonably incurred. Defendant seeks reductions arising out of pre-engagement work, clerical and administrative work, the use of templates, the instant motion, and other non-compensable inefficiencies.

 

Defendant first requests the Court reduce $300 for pre-engagement work done prior to the preparation of the retainer agreement. Plaintiff argues that pre-litigation fees are recoverable under the Song-Beverly Act. (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d) [includes fees “reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.”].) While pre-litigation fees are permitted, they must be reasonably incurred. The Court agrees with Defendant’s argument that preparing the “Lemon Law Representation Agreement” is an overhead business expense and reduces the 0.2 hours claimed on 10/11/2023 for a total reduction of $40.

 

Based on the use of templates, Defendant requests the Court reduce 7.3 hours for templated Motions in limine; 4 hours for templated trial documents; 3 hours for templated discovery requests and notices of deposition; and 1.5 hours for a templated complaint. Defendant argues that these documents are based on templates with little variation based on case. Defendant provides Motions in Limine in the instant action compared to that of a prior action to support this argument. (Bowton Decl., compare Exh. I, with Exh. J.) Defendant provides trial documents from this action compared with a prior action. (Bowton Decl., compare Exh. K, with Exh. L.) Defendant does the same with the complaint (Bowton Decl., compare Exh. A, with Exh. B) and deposition requests and notices. (Bowton Decl., compare Exh. C with Exh. D, Exh. E with Exh. F, and Exh. G with Exh. H.)

 

In reply, Plaintiff contends that the use of templated documents is factored into the hourly requests. Plaintiff emphasizes that 6.5 hours of the 7.8 hours requested for the Motions in Limine were billed by paralegals at their hourly rates. Plaintiff notes that he noticed two depositions and for each, only 0.3 hours at paralegal rates and 0.1 hours at attorney rates were billed.

 

Here, the Court finds the 1.5 hours billed for the complaint and 3.5 hours for the deposition notices reasonable. Based on the confirmed use of templates, the Court agrees that 6.5 hours of paralegal time for the Motions in Limine, excluding attorney review, is excessive. The Court reduces 4 hours on 11/10/2025 for a total reduction of $1,000. Further, based on the confirmed use of templates, the Court reduces 0.5 hours for review of the trial documents on 12/1/2025 for a total reduction of $312.50.

 

Defendant next weeks a reduction of 10.7 hours for clerical and administrative work throughout this action for calendaring, filing or preparing for filing, serving, creating folders, processing documents, and internal communications. Defendant shows that there are 57 separate entries for “serv[ing],” “[p]reparing file for” court appearances, “calender[ing].” (Nita Decl., Exh. A.) Plaintiff contends that Defendant does not point to exact tasks. Plaintiff also argues that these tasks such as calendaring or preparing the file require legal training.

 

The Court agrees that these tasks are non-compensable as they are purely administrative/clerical. (See Save Our Uniquely Rural Community Environment v. County of San Bernardino¿(2015) 235¿Cal.App.4th 1179, 1187 [finding that calendaring, internal filing, preparing proof of service are examples of tasks that have been found to be purely clerical and potentially excessive]; see also Collins v. City of Los Angeles (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 140, 159 [characterizing filing and serving documents as “noncompensable administrative tasks”].) Upon review of the billing records the Court reduces entries between 10/26/2023 and 10/30/2023 for retrieving conformed copies of documents and calendaring ($275); calendaring on 11/2/2023 and 11/22/2023 ($75); calendaring and serving on 12/19/2023 and 1/18/2024 ($125); calendaring and filing on 2/2/2024, 2/15/2024, 2/22/2024, 3/12/2024, 5/15/2024, 7/30/2024, 9/4/2024, 9/10/2024, 10/14/2024, 11/12/2024, 12/17/2024, 12/20/2024, 3/25/2025, 4/7/2025, 5/15/25, 10/27/2025, 11/10/2025, 12/1/2025, 12/3/2025, 12/10/2025, 12/10/2025, 12/15/2025 ($1,100). Thus, there is a total reduction of $1,575 for purely administrative and clerical tasks.

 

Defendant additional seeks a 7.1 hour reduction out of 9.6 hours billed or anticipated to be billed related to this motion. Here, the Court reduces 2 hours of hours billed between 5/1/2026 and 5/8/2026 as 6.2 hours for reviewing the billing, drafting the moving papers, and reviewing the drafts is unreasonably excessive given counsel’s experience. This is a total reduction of $1,200 (based on the reduced hourly rate of $600).

 

Lastly, the Court does not make reductions based on excessive communications to Plaintiff as Defendant fails to establish that they are unreasonable. The Court also does not make any reductions based on over-staffing as there were only two attorneys staffed on this case.

 

Accordingly, based on the reasonableness of the fee request, the Court reduces $4,127.50. Together with the reasonableness of the hourly rates, the Court reduces a total of $4,585.

 

In sum, Plaintiff’s motion for attorney’s fees is GRANTED in the total amount of $18,380.

 

This ruling does not address Plaintiff’s request for costs. Based on Defendant’s pending motion to strike or tax costs, the Court will rule on costs on the scheduled hearing date.

Case Number: 24NWCV04964    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: P

RUBALCAVA, JR. v. JAGUAR LAND ROVER NORTH AMERICA, LLC

CASE NO.: 24NWCV04964

HEARING: 09/24/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#5

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC’s motion to compel Plaintiff to appear for a deposition is GRANTED

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC (Defendant) moves to compel Plaintiff Jose L. Rubalcava, Jr. (Plaintiff) to appear for deposition.

 

Background

 

On December 20, 2024, Plaintiff filed this lemon law action against Defendant  and Does 1 through 10, inclusive. The complaint alleges that on December 18, 2020, Plaintiff purchased a 2020 Land Rover Range Rover Velar, VIN No.: SALYK2EX6LA253846 (Subject Vehicle). (Complaint, ¶ 8.) The complaint alleges the Subject Vehicle was delivered to Plaintiff with defects and nonconformities to warranty including engine, electrical, emission, and transmission system defects. (Complaint, ¶ 10.) The complaint asserts three causes of action: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of express warranty, (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of implied warranty, and (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act section 1793.2.

 

Legal Standard

 

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action…without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document…described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document…described in the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).) The motion “shall…set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(1).)

 

“Any party served with a deposition notice that does not comply with Article 2…waives any error or irregularity unless that party promptly serves a written objection specifying that error or irregularity at least three calendar days prior to the date for which the deposition is scheduled, on the party seeking to take the deposition and any other attorney or party on whom the deposition notice was served.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.410, subd. (a).)

 

Meet and Confer

 

“The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040, or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition…by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).)

 

Defendant details multiple attempts to meet and confer with Plaintiff regarding deposition dates and states that Plaintiff failed to respond. (Stoliker Decl., ¶¶ 8, 10, 12, 13.) While Defendant does not show whether the parties met and conferred as to all issues arising out of this motion, the Court proceeds to rule on the merits of this motion based on Plaintiff’s failure to respond to Defendant’s emails.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to compel Plaintiff to appear for a deposition with production of documents on the grounds that Plaintiff has failed to appear for three depositions or produce Code-compliant responses.

 

Defendant recounts that it first served a Notice of Deposition on May 5, 2025 for a June 10, 2025 deposition date. (Stoliker Decl., ¶ 3.) Plaintiff objected to the Notice of Deposition on the grounds that the deposition was unilaterally set and Plaintiff was not available. These same events repeated after Defendant served an Amended Notice of Deposition on April 10, 2026 and another on June 30, 2026. (Stoliker Decl., ¶¶ 5-9.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff contends that this motion should be denied or found moot because Plaintiff confirmed that he would appear for a deposition on September 24, 2026 at 10 AM. (Fernandez Decl., ¶ 14.)

 

In reply, Defendant confirms that this deposition date is confirmed but argues that Plaintiff only provided this date after Defendant filed this motion and that this date falls one day before the deposition cut-off and does not offer flexibility.

 

Here, Defendant establishes that it properly noticed Plaintiff’s deposition on multiple occasions and that Plaintiff has failed to appear. Given that this deposition has not yet been conducted, this motion is not moot.

 

Based on the foregoing, an order compelling Plaintiff to appear for deposition is warranted. Given that this deposition is scheduled to occur 30 minutes after the hearing on this motion, the Court will confer with the parties at the hearing about any relevant updates.

 

Next, Plaintiff argues that Defendant failed to file a separate statement with this motion. In reply, Defendant argues that it only seeks to compel Plaintiff’s attendance at the deposition. This is confirmed by the proposed order. Defendant’s motion does not address the content of the requests for production and thus the Court does not address it. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(a)(5) [“Any motion involving the content of a discovery request or the responses to such a request must be accompanied by a separate statement”].)

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff to appear for a deposition is GRANTED. Plaintiff Jose L. Rubalcava, Jr. is ORDERED to appear for the deposition scheduled for September 24, 2026 at 10 AM or at a different time prior to the discovery cut-off.

Case Number: 25NWCV01509    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: P

TORRES v. GENERAL MOTORS, LLC

CASE NO.: 25NWCV01509 

HEARING: 09/24/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#6

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Hugo Torres’s motion for a stay is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Hugo Torres (Plaintiff) moves to stay this action pending the Court of Appeal’s decision in Price v. General Motors [Price] (B353231).

 

Background

 

On April 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed this lemon law action against Defendant General Motors, LLC (Defendant) and Does 1 through 10. On July 2, 2026, the Court sustained Defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleadings in part and sustained it without leave to amend in part. On August 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC).

 

This action arises out of alleged defects and nonconformities to warranty Plaintiff experienced with his 2018 Chevrolet Malibu (Subject Vehicle). (FAC, ¶¶ 6-14.) The FAC asserts one cause of action: fraudulent inducement-concealment.

 

Legal Standard

 

“ ‘[A] court ordinarily has inherent power, in its discretion, to stay proceedings when such a stay will accommodate the ends of justice.’” (OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho (2019) 8 Cal.5th 111, 141, citation omitted.) “ ‘[T]he power to stay proceedings is incidental to the power inherent in every court to control the disposition of the causes on its docket with economy of time and effort for itself, for counsel, and for litigants.’” (Ibid., citing Landis v. North American Co. (1936) 299 U.S. 248, 254–55.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to stay this action pending a ruling in Price.

 

Plaintiff argues that “Price will decide a novel legal issue that is implicated in this case: whether a manufacturer’s post-filing election into the Song-Beverly Act’s new procedural regime can retroactively time-bar a complaint that was already on file when the new statutes of repose and limitations took effect.” (Mot., 3:8-11.) Plaintiff contends that any ruling in this Court may be subject to revision or reversal once Price is decided.

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that Plaintiff fails to show circumstances warranting a stay and that this requested is a delay tactic solely to benefit Plaintiff’s counsel. Defendant notes that thus far only a Notice of Appeal has been filed in Price and Plaintiff makes no showing of a potential timeline for this stay. (Perez Decl., ¶ 10.) Defendant further argues that Plaintiff does not show any hardship whereas Defendant would lose the ability to obtain timely deposition testimony from Plaintiff and discovery may get lost during an open-ended period. (Hightower Decl., ¶¶ 9-10; Perez Decl., ¶ 9.) Defendant also argues that a stay would be prejudicial because once the stay is lifted, the parties would be required to resume discovery in a backlog of cases on an accelerated schedule. (Perez Decl., ¶ 11.)

 

Here, the Court does not find that the balance of equities favor a stay based on Price. As of this time, there is no timeline on a potential ruling and Defendant establishes that a stay of an unknown duration would be prejudicial. This Court has also already ruled on Defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleadings in which these issues were raised. Further, in the event of a potential ruling in Price that may affect Plaintiff’s rights here, Plaintiff may seek recourse by filing motions for reconsideration or the like.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for a stay is DENIED.

CASTRO v. LUXURY LEASE PARTNERS LLC

CASE NO.: 25NWCV03179 

HEARING: 09/24/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#7

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Counsel Todd Friedman, Law Offices of Todd M. Friedman’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff Edson Castro is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Counsel Todd Friedman, Law Offices of Todd M. Friedman (Counsel) moves to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff Edson Castro (Plaintiff).

 

Background

 

On September 5, 2025, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant Luxury Lease Partners LLC alleging causes of action for breach of contract and violation of the Consumer Legal Remedies Act.

 

On January 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case.

 

On July 31, 2026, this motion first came on for hearing. The Court continued the hearing on this motion to August 12, 2026 to permit Counsel to file an omitted proof of service.

 

On August 12, 2026, this motion came on for hearing again. The Court continued the motion to today’s date and noted “[t]he Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is Denied until the settlement is completed.” (Order, 8/12/2026.)

 

Discussion

 

This is a continuation. Since the Court’s July 31, 2026 Order continuing the hearing on this motion to permit Counsel to file an omitted Proof of Service, Counsel filed a Proof of Service evidencing service via mail to Plaintiff on July 27, 2026. Counsel complies with the requirements to be relieved as counsel. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff is GRANTED. This Order will take effect upon the filing of proof of service of the signed order upon the client and on all parties that have appeared in the case.
