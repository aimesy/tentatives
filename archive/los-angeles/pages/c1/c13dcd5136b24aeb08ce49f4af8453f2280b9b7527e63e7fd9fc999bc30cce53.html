CASE NAME:           Anthony Arline v. Shirley Arline-Carter

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV17743

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE A CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Shirley Arline-Carter

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): None

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order granting leave to file a cross-complaint against Don’ta Lamont Arline, Kevin Rachon Arline, Sr., and Golden Rule Lending, LLC.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for leave to file a cross-complaint is GRANTED;

2.      Defendant to file their proposed cross-complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On July 17, 2024, Plaintiff Anthony Arline (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendant Shirley Arline-Carter (Defendant) with causes of action for: (1) recovery of property wrongfully transferred; (2) conversion; (3) unjust enrichment; and (4) order compelling accounting.

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff is a beneficiary of a trust, Defendant is the trustee of that trust, and has refused to make distributions pursuant to the trust provisions. The distribution is of real property located at 1553 E. 110th Street, Los Angeles, California.

 

On May 20, 2025, Defendant filed an answer.

 

On June 8, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for leave to file a cross-complaint. Oppositions were due on or before July 16, 2026. As of July 24, 2026, the court has not received any opposition.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

A cross-complaint may be either permissive or compulsory. Compulsory cross-complaints are based on allegations arising out of the same transactions or occurrences as the causes of action which the plaintiff alleges in his or her complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., (CCP) § 426.10, subd. (c).)¿Compulsory cross-complaint causes of action must exist at the time an answer to the original cross-complaint is¿required.¿(CCP § 426.30, subd. (a).)¿ All other cross-complaints are permissive.¿(CCP § 428.10.) If a party¿fails to¿file any cross-complaint before or when they file an answer, they must move the court for leave to file a cross-complaint.¿ (CCP § 428.50.) The court “shall” grant leave to file a compulsory cross-complaint,¿as long as¿the party¿demonstrates¿that,¿notwithstanding¿being late, the motion for leave is brought in good faith.¿ (CCP § 426.50.)¿The court “may” grant leave to file a permissive cross-complaint “in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.”¿ (CCP § 428.50, subd. (c).)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

A policy of liberal construction of section 426.50 to avoid forfeiture of causes of action is imposed on the trial court.¿ (Silver Orgs. v. Frank¿(1990) 217 Cal. App. 3d 94, 98-99.) ¿A motion to file a cross-complaint at any time¿during the course of¿the action must be granted unless substantial evidence of bad faith is¿demonstrated. ¿(Ibid.)¿¿ 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Defendant contends leave to file a cross-complaint is warranted because the proposed cross-complaint arises out of the same transaction or occurrence as Plaintiff’s complaint, the interests of justice support leave to file the cross-complaint, and Plaintiff will suffer no undue prejudice while denial will substantially prejudice Defendant.

 

A cross-complaint may be either permissive or compulsory. (c).)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

A policy of liberal construction of section 426.50 to avoid forfeiture of causes of action is imposed on the trial court.¿ (Silver Orgs. v. Frank¿(1990) 217 Cal. App. 3d 94, 98-99.) ¿A motion to file a cross-complaint at any time¿during the course of¿the action must be granted unless substantial evidence of bad faith is¿demonstrated. ¿(Ibid.)¿¿ 

 

Here, leave to file a cross-complaint is warranted. First, the proposed cross-complaint is based on the same transaction or occurrence as the underlying complaint – conveyance of the property with street address 1553 East 110th Street, Los Angeles, California. Indeed, the proposed cross-defendants are part of the property dispute concerning the APN, what Defendant transferred via quitclaim deed, or are currently the titleholder of the subject property. (Declaration of John H. Oh ¶¶ 4-5.) Second, there is no evidence of bad faith in seeking this relief. Finally, there is no evidence of undue prejudice to Plaintiff because he did not oppose this motion.

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Defendant’s motion for leave to file a cross-complaint.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for leave to file a cross-complaint is GRANTED;

2.      Defendant to file their proposed cross-complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV06682    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   July 29, 2026                                     

 

CASE NAME:           Steve Cortez v. General Motors, LLC

 

CASE NO.:                25STCV06682

 

MOTION FOR STAY OF PROCEEDINGS

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiff Steve Cortez

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendant General Motors, LLC

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order to stay all proceedings in this matter pending the Court of Appeal’s decision in Price v. General Motors (B353231).

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for stay of proceedings is DENIED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On March 10, 2025, Plaintiff Steve Cortez (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendant General Motors, LLC (GM) with causes of action for: (1) violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(d); (2) violation of Civ. Code § 1793.(b); (3) violation of Civ. Code § 1793.2(a)(3); (4) breach of the implied warranty of merchantability; (5) fraudulent inducement – concealment. 

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff purchased a 2017 Chevrolet Colorado that allegedly developed engine, transmission, and electrical defects within the warranty period, including stuttering on acceleration, check engine warnings, and persistent drivability and safety issues. Plaintiff alleges that GM failed to repair the defects after multiple attempts, did not provide the required repurchase or replacement remedy under the Song Beverly Act, and that the vehicle’s value became de minimis. Plaintiff further contends that GM knew of a dangerous 3.6L engine defect, concealed it from consumers, and authorized only superficial repairs that masked but did not fix the underlying problem.  

 

On October 28, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC) against GM with the same causes of action. 

 

On December 1, 2025, GM filed an answer. 

 

On May 1, 2026, GM filed a motion for judgment on the pleadings which the court GRANTED as to the first, second, third, and fourth causes of action without leave to amend and DENIED as to the fifth cause of action. 

 

On May 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for order to stay proceedings. On July 16, 2026, GM filed an opposition.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

A court ordinarily has inherent power, in its discretion, to stay proceedings when such a stay will accommodate the¿ends¿of justice. (OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho¿(2019) 8 Cal.5th 111, 141.) The power to stay proceedings is incidental to the power inherent in every court to control the disposition of the cause on its docket with the economy of time and effort for itself, for counsel, and for litigants. (Id.) Trial courts¿generally have¿inherent power to stay proceedings in the interest of justice and to promote judicial efficiency. (Freiberg v. City of Mission Viejo¿(1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1484, 1489.)¿

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Plaintiff contends the court should exercise its inherent authority to issue a stay pending resolution of the appeal in Price v. General Motors pending in the Second District Court of Appeal because it will determine a novel issue splitting the trial courts concerning GM’s post-filing election into the Song-Beverly Act’s new procedural regime as retroactively barring on-filed complaints.

 

GM argues a stay is not warranted because: (1) it is moot as the only remaining claim is not affected by the Court of Appeal case; (2) the motion does not comply with the CRC; (3) the balance of equities does not favor a stay; and (4) this motion is a delay tactic.

 

Here, a discretionary stay is not warranted. First, Plaintiff seeks a stay for an unknown period of time due to an appeal pending in an adjacent matter. This prejudices GM who is still litigating the surviving fraud-concealment claim. Second, Plaintiff is free to file a motion for reconsideration after the Court of Appeal rules, as needed. Therefore, the court is not inclined to exercise its discretion to issue a stay.

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion for stay of proceedings.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for stay of proceedings is DENIED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
