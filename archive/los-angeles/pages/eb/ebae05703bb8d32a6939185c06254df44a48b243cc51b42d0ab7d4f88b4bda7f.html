NATASHA TSUJI

 

         vs.

 

BIRD RIDES, INC., et al.

 

 Case No.: 22STCV02328

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

 

Plaintiff counsel Jacob Brown’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED, effective upon filing proof of service of the court’s order (MC-053).

 

Legal Standard

 

Motions to withdraw are routinely granted if made in the proper form and presented with proper notice. The court may issue an order allowing an attorney to withdraw from representation, after notice to the client.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) 

 

Discussion

 

Jacob Brown brings the instant motion for relief as counsel as to Plaintiff Natasha Tsuji.  

 

            The following documents are properly submitted: Civil Form MC-051 “Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” Civil Form MC-052 “Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” and Civil Form MC-053 “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel.”

 

            The motion is GRANTED.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 24STCV12088    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 309

TENTATIVE RULING

 

LORELAI WARREN, et al.

 

 

         vs.

 

FCA US LLC

 

 Case No.: 24STCV12088   

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

 

 

Plaintiffs’ motion to compel initial responses to their Special Interrogatories (Set One) is DENIED.

 

The Court declines to award sanctions at this time

           

On 5/13/2024, Plaintiffs Lorelai and Nichole J Warren (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed suit against FCA, US LLC, alleging violations of the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.

 

            On 6/3/2026, Plaintiffs moved to compel initial responses to their Special Interrogatories (Set One).

 

Discussion

 

            On 7/1/2024, Plaintiffs served Defendant with Special Interrogatories (Set One). Despite granting an extension to respond, Defendant has still, to date, failed to provide any response.

           

            In opposition, Defendant indicates that its failure to timely respond was based on an informal agreement between the parties wherein FCA was not required to respond to discovery served by Strategic Legal Practices (SLP) during the pendency of this arrangement unless specifically requested by SLP. Defendant explains that “[t]his process enabled the parties to more effectively and efficiently engage in settlement negotiations of active cases.” (Opp., 1: 17-20.)

 

            Moreover, Defendant indicates that after Plaintiff filed the instant motion, FCA served verified responses on 6/5/2026.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ motion to compel initial responses to their Special Interrogatories (Set One) is denied. Accordingly, the Court declines to award sanctions at this time.

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 24STCV33572    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

ARTISHA HICKMAN 

 

         vs.

 

THE MICHAELS ORGANIZATION, LLC et al.

 

 Case No.: 24STCV33572

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to quash is DENIED.

           

On 12/19/2024, Plaintiff Artisha Hickman (Plaintiff) initiated this action against the Michaels Organization, LLC, Javier Gomez, and Arrendondo. On 1/2/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC), alleging: (1) discrimination; (2) harassment; (3) retaliation; (4) failure to prevent; (5) failure to participate in good faith interactive process; (6) failure to provide reasonable accommodation; (7) retaliation; (8) retaliation; (9) wrongful termination; (10) defamation; (11) defamation; (12) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (13) violation of the California Family Rights Act; and (14) declaratory judgment.

 

            On 4/16/2026, Plaintiff moved to quash and for a protective order.

 

Discussion  

 

Plaintiff seeks to quash for a protective order in relation to the following discovery served by Defendant: 1) Redacted copy of any and all settlement agreements involving Artisha Hickman from attorney/law offices, D. Wayne Leech APC; and 2) Any and all documents reflecting a settlement payment was cleared and/or cashed by Artisha Hickman from attorney/law offices, D. Wayne Leech APC.

 

Courts recognize strong public policy interests in maintaining the confidentiality of settlement agreements and encouraging settlements. (Monster Energy Co. v. Schechter (2019) 7 Cal.5th 781) Courts typically discourage discovery, or even testimony, on underlying facts of a settlement may discourage the process, thus violating public policy. (Tower Acton Holdings v. Los Angeles County Waterworks Dist. No. 37 (2002) 105 Cal.App.4th 590.) When a party’s subpoena requests for personal financial records, such as settlement payment information, the opposing party can invoke privacy protections to prevent access to said documentation. (County of Los Angeles v. Superior Court (2021) 65 Cal.App.5th 621.)

 

Here, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s contention that documents from Plaintiff’s prior counsel related to an unrelated settlement agreement “may lead to admissible evidence relating to damages” is insufficiently specific.

 

The Court disagrees. 

 

First, Plaintiff argues that Defendant already had an opportunity to depose her as to questions of damages. However, in opposition, Defendant indicates that it requested these record because they are relevant to the credibility of Plaintiff to use for impeachment purposes. As described by Defendant,

 

During discovery, The Michaels Organization, LLC (“Defendant”) propounded Judicial Council Form Interrogatory No. 209.1 seeking information on any other employment lawsuits Plaintiff filed in the past 10 years. Plaintiff stated, under the penalty of perjury, that she did not file any employment lawsuits in the past 10 years. In preparation for Plaintiff’s deposition, Defendant uncovered Plaintiff’s wrongful termination lawsuit filed in 2020. At deposition, Plaintiff repeatedly denied a prior lawsuit. When shown the lawsuit, Plaintiff denied knowing the attorney identified as her counsel and testified that the lawsuit was filed without her consent or knowledge. At the conclusion of the deposition, Defendant served subpoenas on both counsel to the 2020 lawsuit. After the deposition, and after the subpoenas were served, Plaintiff amended her discovery responses from a “no” to a “yes” regarding prior employment lawsuits. She also amended her deposition testimony from a “no” to an “I don’t remember” regarding prior employment lawsuits. In the end, Plaintiff has responded under oath that she did not file another employment lawsuit in the past 10 years, that she did file another employment lawsuit in the past 10 years, and that she does not remember whether she filed another employment lawsuit.

 

            (Opp., 2: 7-20.)

           

            Thus, the Court agrees that the records are directly relevant to the credibility of Plaintiff and relevant for impeachment purposes.

 

            Crucially, Defendant’s subpoena request is narrowly tailored to only seek the specific unredacted portions: (1) the name of the document, (2) the description of the case that was at issue and/or settled, (3) the parties involved in the case that was at issue, and (4) Plaintiff’s signature on the settlement agreement.

 

This information is not subject to attorney client privilege, attorney work product, or disclosure of the substantive terms of the settlement agreement.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to quash is denied.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCP04987    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

R. WESTERN MONROE

 

         vs.

 

CITY OF SANTA CLARITA

 

 Case No.: 25STCP04987

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

 

 

 

Wiley Canyon LLC’s motion to dismiss is GRANTED.

 

On 12/24/2025, pro per Plaintiff R. Weston Monroe (Plaintiff) filed a petition for writ of mandate against City of Santa Clarita (the City).

 

On 4/7/2026, Specially appearing Wiley Cyn, LLC (Wiley) moved to dismiss Petitioner’s petition for writ of mandate, and to vacate Petitioner’s amendment to the Petition (incorrect name).  

 

Discussion

 

            Wiley moves to dismiss this action on the grounds that the petition presents a fatal, jurisdictional failure to comply with the mandatory requirements of the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA, Pub. Resources Code, §§ 21000, et seq.).

 

CEQA sets forth a bright-line rule requiring a petitioner to name as a real party in interest any recipient of an approval identified in the Notice of Determination (“NOD”). (Pub. Resources Code, §§ 21167.6.5, subd. (a).)

 

Here, the NOD filed by the City of Santa Clarita (City) identified Wiley Canyon, LLC as the Project applicant. However, Petitioner failed to name Wiley Canyon, LLC—or any iteration of that name—anywhere in the Petition. Instead, Petitioner: (i) named a completely unrelated third party LLC (Royal Clark Development Company LLC) in the caption; (ii) failed to name any real party in the body of the Petition; (iii) omitted any charging allegations against any Real Party in the Petition; and (iv) did not include any DOE defendants in either the caption or the body of the Petition.

 

Recognizing these defects after the CEQA 30-day statute of limitations expired, Petitioner attempted to address the defect by filing an ex parte Amendment to Complaint (Incorrect Name) on 2/3/2026. In that filing, Petitioner sought to correct the name of the Real Party to Wiley Canyon, LLC and, for the first time, purported to add DOES 1-20.

 

However, the Court agrees this is ineffectual for three reasons. First, substituting one distinct legal entity for another is not the correction of an “incorrect name” under section 473 of the Code of Civil Procedure; it is the addition of a new party after the statute of limitations has expired.

 

Second, Petitioner cannot “correct” a name to include DOES 1-20 when the original, filed Petition contained no named DOE defendants, nor any DOE allegations or causes of action against them. (See CCP § 474 [“When the plaintiff is ignorant of the name of the defendant, he must state that fact in the complaint, . . . and such defendant may be designated in any pleading . . . by any name, and when his true name is discovered, the pleading . . . must be amended accordingly; . . . “.]

 

            Third, Wiley Canyon was not named in the original Petition—in any form—within the 30-day window following the NOD, as required by CEQA. (Pub. Resources Code § 21167.6.5; CCP § 389, subds. (a) and (b); Citizens for a Better Eureka v. City of Eureka (2025) 111 Cal.App.5th 1114, 1126.) Because Wiley is a necessary and indispensable real party in interest, and the CEQA 30-day statute of limitations bars Petitioner from curing this omission, the Petition must be dismissed. (Id; CCP § 389.)

 

            Based on the foregoing, Wiley’s motion to dismiss is granted.

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV04420    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

CASSIE ELZY

 

         vs.

 

KIDDER MATHEWS INC., et al.

 

 Case No.: 25STCV04420  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendants to provide objection-free responses to her Requests for Production (Set Two) is GRANTED. Responses are due within 10 days of entry of this order.

 

Defendants are each sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,050, due with 20 days of entry of this order.

 

On 2/14/2025, Plaintiff Cassie Elzy (Plaintiff) filed suit against Kidder Mathews Inc. and Kidder Mathews of California, alleging: (1) sex/pregnancy discrimination; (2) violation of the Pregnancy Disability Leave Act; (3) retaliation; (4) CFRA interference; (5) failure to provide reasonable accommodation; (6) failure to provide reasonable accommodation; (7) failure to prevent discrimination and retaliation; (8) wrongful termination; (9) failure to timely pay wages; and (10) failure to reimburse business expenses.

 

            On 6/4/2026, Plaintiff moved Defendant Kidder Mathews of California, Inc. (Defendant KMCI) and Kidder Mathews of California, Inc. (collectively, Defendants) to provide responses to her Requests for Production (Set Two). Plaintiff seeks monetary sanctions in connection with the motion.

 

Discussion

 

On 1/13/2026, Plaintiff served the RFPs on Defendants by email. (Cruz Decl., ¶ 2, Exhs. A-B.) Defendants’ responses were due on or before 2/17/2026. (Id. at ¶ 3.) On 2/12/2026, counsel for Defendants requested an extension to 2/26/2026. Plaintiff granted the requested extension. (Id.) However, Defendants did not serve any responses at any time or request a further extension by 2/26/2026. (Id. at ¶¶ 4, 7.) To date, no responses have been provided.

 

In opposition, Defendants argue that there should be no waiver from objection because the original hearing date on this motion was 9/16/2026, but was accelerated to 7/29/2026. Defendant contends that if this motion were continued, it would be able to provide substantially compliant responses. However, this ignores the fact that responses were due in February 2026, and multiple extensions were granted. While Defendant explains that the delay in serving responses resulted from an inadvertent clerical error during the transition of legal assistants, the Court finds this inadequate to support a finding that relief of waiver should be granted. This motion was filed on 6/4/2026. As such, they were on notice of their failure to comply with discovery obligations long before this hearing.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendants to provide objection-free responses to her Requests for Production (Set Two) is granted. Responses are due within 10 days of entry of this order. Defendants are sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,050 ($350/hr x 3 hr), due with 20 days of entry of this order.

 

It is so ordered.

   Judge of the Superior Court

Case Number: 25STCV13236    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

CHRISTIAN COLUNGA

                          

         vs.

 

ASSOCIATION RESERVES, INC., et al.

                                         

 Case No.: 25STCV13236 

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

 

 

Defendant’s demurrer is OVERRULED.

 

            On 5/8/2025, Christian Colunga, an individual, on behalf of himself (Plaintiff), initiated this action. On 4/3/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC) against Association Reserves, Inc., the Nordlund Revocable Trust, Robert Nordlund, and Cathy Schrader (collectively, Defendants), alleging: (1) declaratory relief; (2) breach of contract; (3) breach of implied covenant; (4) violation of California Corporations Code section 31000; (5) violation of California Business and Professions Code section 31201; (6) negligent misrepresentation; (7) violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200; (8) injunctive and other relief.

 

            On 4/3/2026, Defendants demurred to Plaintiff’s FAC.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants argue that each of Plaintiff’s causes of action fail as a matter of law.

 

            As to the first cause of action, Defendant contends that Plaintiff does not allege he ever paid a franchise fee.  However, as noted by Plaintiff, Corporations Code section 31005(a) expressly recognizes franchise fees paid either "directly or indirectly."

 

Plaintiff alleges he was required to pay Jim Talaga (Talaga) a deposit of $25,000 and signed a promissory note in the amount of $1.7 million dollars to purchase the membership interests in the franchise. Additionally, Plaintiff alleges that in January 2023, Defendant ARI, an affiliate of the Washington franchise, (common ownership with Defendant the Nordlund Trust) received approximately $12,000 in franchise fees. And from January 1, 2023, through June 10, 2025, the Washington franchise allegedly paid $424,000 in franchise fees to ARI. (FAC ¶¶ 64, 72.) Defendants argue payments to a third-party do not suffice. However, Release 3-F excludes only payments made to unaffiliated third parties that are not paid for the right to enter or remain in the business: “Any fee or charge which the franchisee is required to pay to the franchisor or an affiliate of the franchisor for the right to engage in business is a franchise fee regardless of the designation given to, or the form of, such payment.” (Commissioner’s Release 3-F; Cal Corporations Code §31005 (a) (3); Cal Corporations Code §31011.)

 

 Whether or not Plaintiff’s payment, in fact, were qualified third party payments involves factual determinations not appropriately made at the pleading stage. Similarly, whether or not a franchise-franchisee relationship, in fact, existed is a factual determination not made at the pleadings stage.

 

            As for the second, third, fourth, and fifth causes of action, Defendant argues that there are insufficient allegations of a franchise relationship as well as fail to allege payment of a franchise fee. As set forth above, the Court finds the FAC’s allegations to be sufficient at the pleading stage as to both contentions.

 

            As for the sixth cause of action, Defendant argues that before 1/1/2023, California Corporations Code section 31306 pre-empted common-law claims based on misrepresentations covered by the California Franchise Investment Law (CFIL). (See Samica Enters., LLC v. Mail Boxes Etc. (C.D. Cal. 2008)  637 F. Supp. 2d 712, 721-22 (“[B]ecause Plaintiffs’ fraud claim appears to rest exclusively on alleged misrepresentations and omissions statutorily covered by the CFIL, and [Plaintiffs] cite no common law basis supporting the fraud recovery independent of the CFIL, section 31306 precludes Plaintiffs’ common law claims.”).

 

            However, Plaintiff alleges that “[o]n or about January 1, 2023, the sale of the Washington AR franchise to Colunga was concluded and effectuated by and through Defendants Nordlund, Schrader and ARI.” (FAC ¶61.) The legislature amended Section 31306 effective 1/1/2023, and Defendants do not argue that the current form of Section 31306 allows for preemption of fraud claims. Moreover, Plaintiff argues that Corporations Code section 31306 is retroactive: “Notably, the bill that amended CFIL Section 31306 also amended the CFRA and the CFIL. While Bus. & Prof. Code § 20041(c) of the CFRA was added to state that certain amendments apply only to franchise agreements "entered into, amended, or renewed on or after January 1, 2023," the amendments to the CFIL contained no such limitation.” (Opp., 12: 21-26.)

 

            After review of the applicable authority, the Court finds no preemption of Plaintiff’s fraud claims by the CFIL. The Court also finds that Defendant’s arguments related to the statute of limitations require factual determinations not properly made at the pleadings stage. This is similarly true as to the fifth cause of action.

 

            Finally, the Court finds Plaintiff has standing to bring this claim. Defendants argue that Plaintiff lacks standing because he allegedly purchased his membership interest from Talaga rather than directly from Defendants. However, Plaintiff alleges that Talaga was merely a sales intermediatory between Defendants and Plaintiff. (See FAC ¶ 63.) Plaintiff alleges that in 2019, while Colunga was working and residing full time in California, Defendant Nordlund and Talaga approached Colunga and offered Colunga the opportunity to purchase the Washington franchise. (FAC ¶ 51.) Plaintiff alleges that from August of 2019 to November 2020, Talaga, Nordlund and Colunga discussed in great detail the structure of the opportunity, market concepts, team concepts, how and when the transmission would take place etc. Defendants Nordlund and Schrader allegedly actively vetted Colunga and advised Colunga about the purchase of the franchise. (FAC ¶ 52.)

 

Where the franchisor reserves discretion to approve or reject a transferee based on criteria beyond objective financial qualification, the franchisor is deemed to have participated in the sale for purposes of Corporations Code section 31101. Defendant Nordlund was the majority owner and manager of the franchise AR-WA LLC, and pursuant to the Franchise LLC Operating Agreement had the right to approve any franchise sale or transfer. (See Art 7 ¶7.2.1 of the Franchise LLC Operating Agreement). (FAC ¶55.)

 

Whether or not the various alleged participants in this business deal had the requisite involvement, contact, or oversight for Plaintiff to be deemed a participant is clearly a factual determination not appropriately made at this stage.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer is overruled.

 

It is so ordered.
