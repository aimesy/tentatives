Case Number: 23STLC01127    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Nationwide Agribusiness Ins NAIC’s Renewed Motion to Enforce Settlement is denied.

 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

On February 14, 2023, Plaintiff Nationwide Agribusiness Ins NAIC (“Plaintiff”) filed this action for common counts against Defendants William Highsmith aka Wilia Highsmith 3 (“Defendant Highsmith”) and Osborne Williams aka Osborne T. Williams Jr. aka Williams Osbourne aka Osbourne Williams (“Defendant Williams”). Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case on February 27, 2023.

 

On November 18, 2024, Plaintiff filed a Stipulation and Order for Dismissal. The Court entered the stipulation and dismissed the action on the same day. (Stip and Order, filed 11/18/24.) On November 26, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Motion to Enforce Settlement. The Court denied the Motion to Enforce Settlement on January 28, 2026, citing that Plaintiff was not a party to the settlement agreement. (Minute Order, 01/28/26.) Instead, the settlement was signed by Defendants and by “Rich Gallion, Authorized Agent” on behalf of “Nationwide Mutual Insurance Company.” (Ibid.)

 

On April 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Amended Stipulation, Settlement Agreement, and Release. Plaintiff then filed the instant Motion to Enforce Settlement on April 2, 2026. For reasons unknown, Plaintiff re-filed the same Motion on May 15, 2026. The Motion was served on Defendants by first-class mail on March 31, 2026. To date, no opposition has been filed.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion to Enforce Settlement makes a renewed application to enforce the parties’ settlement agreement pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. Plaintiff previously sought the same relief in the Motion to Enforce Settlement filed on November 26, 2025.

 

When a party seeks the same relief that was previously denied, it must bring a renewed motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (b). (California Correctional Peace Officers Ass’n v. Virga (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 30, 43, fn. 11; see also Tate v. Wilburn (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 150, 156-157.) When a motion has been denied in whole or in part, the moving party may apply again for the same relief at a later time only upon “new or different facts, circumstances or law.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (b); see Graham v. Hansen (1982) 128 Cal.App.3d 965, 969-970.) Also, the statute requires the moving party “to show a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence earlier, which can only be described as a strict requirement of diligence,” the purpose of which is to incentivize parties “to efficiently marshal their evidence.” (Baldwin v. Home Sav. of America (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 1192, 1199 [citing Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 689-690].)

 

The instant Motion to Enforce Settlement is neither brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, nor fulfills its statutory requirements. The supporting declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel fails to address the earlier Motion to Enforce Settlement and related order dated January 28, 2026, demonstrate new or different facts, circumstances or law, nor provide a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence earlier. Therefore, the Court lacks the jurisdiction to grant the renewed Motion to Enforce Settlement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (e); see Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1106.)

 

Even if the Court were to consider the renewed Motion on the merits, the supporting declaration is too confusing to credibly attest to the payments made by Defendants. Plaintiff’s counsel declares that Defendants agreed to pay the principal sum of $13,086.73, plus filing fees in the sum of $740.00, plus mandatory E-filing fees in the sum of $25.90, for the total sum of $13,852.63. (Motion, Smith Decl., ¶5 and Exh. 1 at ¶4.) Defendants agreed to pay the settlement amount through monthly payments of $96.19 starting on February 27, 2023 until paid in full. (Ibid.) According to Plaintiff’s counsel, Defendants only made payments totaling $2,396.79, “for a total judgment amount of $13,554.13.” (Id. at ¶6.)  Plaintiff’s counsel then contradicts this statement by declaring that the judgment should be calculated based on payments received toward the principal in the sum of $2,300.62, for a total of $13,546.11 now due and owing.  (Id. at ¶¶7-8.)   Plaintiff also seeks pre-judgment interest, although the stipulation does not address entitlement to interest, nor does Plaintiff’s motion explain how the interest claimed was calculated.  Based on the Court’s review of Exhibit 1, Defendants’ default would require them to pay only the principal balance of $13,086.73, plus first appearance fees, less payments received.   (Exh. 1, ¶6.)   Given these inconsistencies, the Court finds the supporting declaration cannot be relied upon to accurately set forth the amount now due under the settlement agreement, and that the amount currently sought in the proposed judgment is not supported.  

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Plaintiff Nationwide Agribusiness Ins NAIC’s Renewed Motion to Enforce Settlement is denied.

Case Number: 24STLC04039    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Specially Appearing Defendant 168 Rivers Inc.’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons is granted. The default entered on January 6, 2026 against Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. is hereby vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service as to Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. is advanced from 06/08/2027 to 07/28/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Anthony Navarro (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Shin Restaurant, 168 Rivers Corporation, and 168 Rivers, Inc. (“Defendants”) on June 4, 2024. Proof of substitute service was filed as to each Defendant and their defaults were entered on January 6, 2026.

 

Defendants filed a Motion to Quash Service of Summons on January 8, 2026. After consideration of the papers, the Court placed the hearing on the first Motion to Quash off calendar on account of Defendants being in default and not being represented by counsel. (Minute Order, 02/03/26.)

 

On May 28, 2026, Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. filed the instant Motion to Quash Service of Summons through counsel. Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. served the Motion on Plaintiff through Attorney Ted Shin, Esq. by electronic mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. moves both to vacate the entry of default and quash service of the Summons and Complaint.

 

Motion to Vacate Entry of Default

 

Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. moves to vacate the entry of default on the grounds that Plaintiff’s service of the Summons and Complaint did not comply with the statutory requirements for service on a corporation, such that the Court did not obtain personal jurisdiction over Defendant. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 416.10; Dill v. Berquist Construction Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1444.) To demonstrate that the proof of service is not Code-compliant, Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. first argues that it is facially defective because it identifies “Alma Soto” as the process server, but then is signed by “Artesia Carter.” (Proof of Substitute Service, filed 12/01/25, ¶¶7-9.) The attached declaration of diligence also refers to the persons serving the papers as “Artesia Carter” and “Altares Carter.” (Id. at p. 3.)

 

Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. also contends that leaving the papers with “Jane Doe” was improper because there is no indication she was authorized to accept the papers on its behalf, and the process servers did not ask for the registered agent, Krisda Hanwirityaritha. The Court does not find that either of these issues demonstrates non-compliance with the statutory requirements for service. The declaration of diligence expressly states that the process server asked for “the agent for service of process 168 RIVERS INC.” (Ibid.) Also, the papers could be left with “Jane Doe” in lieu of the agent for service of process because the declaration of diligence identified her as “apparently in charge” and “in charge of the business.” (Ibid.) However, the Motion is correct to point out that delivering the papers at 7:42 pm does not satisfy the requirement to leave them during usual business hours. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (a).)

 

Based on the foregoing facial and substantive defects, the Court finds that service of the Summons and Complaint upon Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. does not substantially comply with the statutory requirements. Therefoe, the Court lacked personal jurisdiction over the moving party at the time it entered default. The request to vacate the entry of default is granted.

 

Motion to Quash Service of Summons

 

Regarding the request to quash service pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, the statute provides: “A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1), emphasis added.) A defendant has 30 days after the service of the summons to file a responsive pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 412.20, subd. (a)(3).) In light of the defective service detailed above, the Court finds good cause for the timing of the Motion more than 30 days after purported service.

 

Substantively, “[w]hen a defendant challenges the court’s personal jurisdiction on the ground of improper service of process ‘the burden is on the plaintiff to prove the existence of jurisdiction by proving, inter alia, the facts requisite to an effective service.’” (Summers v. McClanahan (2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 403, 413.) A proof of service containing a declaration from a registered process server invokes a rebuttable presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence, of the facts stated in the return. (Cal. Evid. Code, § 647; see American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.) Here, the proof of service of the Summons and Complaint is not attested to by a registered process server. (Proof of Substitute Service, ¶7.) Therefore, no rebuttable presumption of truth in the return of service exists and Plaintiff bears the burden of demonstrating that service upon Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. complied with the statutory requirements. As Plaintiff has not filed any opposition carrying this burden of proof, the request to quash service of the Summons is also granted.

 

Conclusion

 

Specially Appearing Defendant 168 Rivers Inc.’s Motion to Quash Service of Summons is granted. The default entered on January 6, 2026 against Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. is hereby vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service as to Defendant 168 Rivers, Inc. is advanced from 06/08/2027 to 07/28/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 24STLC05012    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Los Angeles Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Discovery Responses is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Defendant Los Angeles Metropolitan Transportation Authority (“Defendant”) propounded Form Interrogatories, Set One and Request for Production and Inspection of Documents, Set One, on Plaintiff Vilma Soyos (“Plaintiff”) on February 5, 2026. (Motion, Smith Decl., Exh. A.) Despite a meet and confer effort, Plaintiff has not served responses as of the filing of the instant Motion on May 18, 2026. (Id. at ¶¶3-7 and Exhs. B-E.)

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Discovery Responses and served it on Plaintiff through Attorney Sergei N. Gladkov, Esq. by electronic mail. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Initially, the Court notes that Defendant filed combined motions to compel responses with respect to two separate sets of discovery: (1) Form Interrogatories, Set One; and (2) Request for Production and Inspection of Documents, Set One. Filing a request to compel responses to two separate sets of discovery as a single motion creates the following problems. First, it negatively impacts the Court’s calendar by placing more motions on the calendar than slots have been provided by the online reservation system. Second, it risks confusion in the motion papers regarding the arguments and evidence with respect to each request. Finally, combining multiple discovery sets into a single motion allows the moving party to avoid paying the requisite filing fees. Statutorily required filing fees are jurisdictional and “it is mandatory for the court clerks to demand and receive statutorily required filing fees.” (See Duran v. St. Luke’s Hospital (2003) 114 Cal.App.4th 457, 460.) Although Defendant has been granted a fee waiver in this action, it should still make a separate motion reservations to address both sets of discovery.

 

Additionally, the Court finds that the discovery requests served on Defendant do not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 94, which allows only 35 written discovery requests in the Limited Civil Court unless otherwise authorized by court order or by stipulation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 94, subd. (a); § 95.) Defendant served Plaintiff with more than 35 form interrogatories alone. (Motion, Smith Decl., Exh. A, pp. 8-10.)  Based on the foregoing, the instant discovery Motion is denied. The Court will not grant a motion to compel discovery propounded in violation of the Code of Civil Procedure.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Los Angeles Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Discovery Responses is denied.

Case Number: 24STLC08416    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions is denied as to the requests, and granted as to the sanctions in the amount of $673.75. The sanctions are to be paid by Defendant Mammoital, Inc #2 dba Mammoital adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso’s Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) served Requests for Admissions, Set One, on Defendant Mammoital, Inc #2 dba Mammoital adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso’s Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammoital”) on March 27, 2026. (Motion, Brown Decl., Exh. 1.) As of the filing of the instant Motion to Deem Requests of Admission Admitted, on May 1, 2026, Defendant Mammoital had not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶¶6-7.) Plaintiff now moves for an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280. Defendant Mammoital filed an opposition on June 9, 2026 and Plaintiff replied on June 15, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to deem requests for admission can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to serve responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) The Court is required to deem the requests admitted unless substantially compliant responses are served prior to the hearing date. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Defendant Mammoital served responses to the Requests for Admissions, Set One, on June 4, 2026. (Opp., Hahn Decl., Exh. A.) Plaintiff acknowledges receipt of the responses and does not argue that they are not in substantial compliance with the Discovery Code. (Reply, p. 1:24-25.) The request to deem the admissions admitted, therefore, is denied.

 

The Court is required to award sanctions under the moving statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) Defendant Mammoital contends that sanctions should be denied because its failure to respond occurred when it lacked representation, such that its non-response was substantially justified. (Citing Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030.) The Court finds no basis to hold that self-representation by a corporation substantially justifies its failure to respond to discovery. Defendant Mammoital was made aware that its self-representation was improper in November 2025, but did not obtain new counsel for more than six months. (See Motion to Strike Answer, filed 11/07/25.)

 

Sanctions are granted against Defendant Mammoital in the amount of $673.75 based on one hour of attorney time billed at $600.00 per hour, plus $73.75 in fees. (Motion, Brown Decl., ¶9.)  The Court reduces the time because substantially identical motions were filed as to two other defendants in this case on the same date and much of the work across the motions and reply briefs is duplicative.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions is denied as to the requests, and granted as to the sanctions in the amount of $673.75. The sanctions are to be paid by Defendant Mammoital, Inc #2 dba Mammoital adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso’s Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days.

Case Number: 25STLC03488    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Wayne Roby’s Motion to Strike, or Alternatively, Order the Defendant to Retain Counsel is denied as to the request to strike Defendant’s General Denial and granted as to the request that Defendant retain counsel.

 

The Court sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Status of Defendant Khanna Enterprises, Ltd. dba Sheraton Agoura Hills’ Legal Representation for 07/22/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 in the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Plaintiff Wayne Roby (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Khanna Enterprises, Ltd. dba Sheraton Agoura Hills (“Defendant”) on April 30, 2025. Proof of service of the Summons and Complaint was not filed until October 1, 2025.  Defendant filed a General Denial on October 30, 2025.

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Strike, or Alternatively, Order the Defendant to Retain Counsel on March 30, 2026. Plaintiff served the Motion on Defendant by first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion to Strike, or Alternatively, Order the Defendant to Retain Counsel is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 436 on the grounds that Defendant, despite being a corporation, was not represented by an attorney at the time the General Denial was filed. Instead, the Denial is signed by “Rodney O. Hurt,” with no indication that individual is a licensed attorney. (General Denial, filed 10/30/25, p. 1.)

 

Motions to strike in the Limited Civil Court may only challenge pleadings on the basis that “the damages or relief sought are not supported by the allegations of the complaint.” (Code of Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (d).) The instant Motion does not pertain to whether the damages or relief sought are supported by the allegations, and therefore, cannot be raised in this Court.

 

Plaintiff, however, alternatively asks that the Court order Defendant to retain counsel. It is black letter law that a corporation cannot represent itself in court. (Clean Air Transport Systems v. San Mateo County Transit Dist. (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 576, 578 (citing Merco Constr. Engineers, Inc. v. Municipal Court (1978) 21 Cal.3d 724, 729-730).) This rule applies to all entities regarded as separate from their owners, including partnerships and unincorporated associations. (See Clean Air Transport Systems, supra, 19 Cal.App.3d at 578.) The Court finds Defendant filed the General Denial without proper legal representation and cannot appear without an attorney going forward.  The Court sets an order to show cause on the date indicated below.  If a Notice of Appearance of counsel is not filed, or counsel does not appear on Defendant’s behalf at the show-cause hearing, the Court will strike Defendant’s Answer.  Defendant is advised that failure to retain counsel may ultimately result in a Defendant’s default being entered and a default judgment against Defendant.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Wayne Roby’s Motion to Strike, or Alternatively, Order the Defendant to Retain Counsel is denied as to the request to strike Defendant’s General Denial and granted as to the request that Defendant retain counsel.


The Court sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Status of Defendant Khanna Enterprises, Ltd. dba Sheraton Agoura Hills’ Legal Representation for 07/22/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 in the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 26STLC00627    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang’s Common Law Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is continued to July 22, 2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff Tsang Han Wang (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Malwarebytes, Inc. (“Defendant”). Defendant filed its Answer on March 9, 2026. On March 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed (1) the Application for Leave to File Memorandum of Points and Authorities in Excess of Page Limit; (2) the Demurrer to Answer; and (3) the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings. On April 22, 2026, the Court sustained the Demurrer to the Answer with leave to amend as to the fifth through ninth and twenty-second affirmative defenses, and otherwise overruled the Demurrer. (Minute Order, 04/22/26.) Defendant filed its Amended Answer on May 12, 2026.

 

On May 14, 2026, Defendant filed a Motion to Declare Vexatious Litigant, which is set for hearing on July 7, 2026. Defendant filed its opposition to the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings on June 5, 2026, to which Plaintiff filed an objection and Notice of Violation of Stay on June 8, 2026

 

Discussion

 

Both parties agree that the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is stayed in light of Defendant’s Motion to Declare Vexatious Litigant, which is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 391.1. Section 391.6 expressly provides that “when a motion pursuant to Section 391.1 is filed prior to trial the litigation is stayed … until 10 days after the motion shall have been denied, or if granted, until 10 days after the required security has been furnished and the moving defendant given written notice thereof.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.6.) Accordingly, the hearing on the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is continued to July 22, 2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.  Defendant has already filed its opposition.  Plaintiff may file a reply in support of the Motion consistent with Code of Civil Procedure, §1005(b).
