Tentative Ruling

Schrage v. Fierro, Case no. 26VECV02151

Hearing date July 8, 2026

Defendants’ Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Schrage sues defendants Fierro for unlawful detainer. Defendants move for judgment on the pleadings, alleging defective 3-day notice. Plaintiff did not file written opposition.

A motion for judgment on the pleadings should be granted when, under “the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216. Code Civ. Proc. §438 permits a party to make a motion for judgment on the pleadings per Code Civ. Proc §438(c)(1)(B) that (i) the court has no jurisdiction; or (ii) the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

Defendants move for summary judgment, arguing the 3-day notice fails to include an expiration date. See Compl., exh. 2. Both the date of service and expiration date must be included on the face of a 3-day notice for the notice to be valid. Eshagian v. Cepeda (2025) 112 Cal. App. 5th 433, 457.

The court granted summary judgment in defendants’ favor in Schrage v. Fierro, case no. 25VECV05223, as plaintiff’s 3-day notice failed to include the date served and expiration date. See 3/26/26 Min. Order, case no. 25VECV05223. The 3-day notice in this matter contains the date served, 3/29/26 (See Compl., exh. 2), but fails to list an expiration date. Id.

California law is clear; failure to strictly comply with the notice requirements of Code Civ. Proc. §1161(2) is fatal to an unlawful detainer action. Eshagian, supra, at 459; Borsuk v. Appellate Division of Superior Court (2015) 242 Cal. App. 4th 607, 609. The 3-day notice is defective as a matter of law. GRANTED.
