DEPARTMENT 511 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV08505    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 14, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Denise McMillan v. Louis Darwin Pizzo, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                23STCV08505

 

MOTION FOR ATTORNEYS’ FEES

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiff Denise McMillan

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): None

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order awarding $19,890.00 in attorneys’ fees to Plaintiff.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for attorneys’ fees is DENIED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On April 17, 2023, Plaintiff Denise McMillan (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against Defendants Louis Darwin Pizzo and Home Depot USA, Inc. (Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) Breach of Contract, (2) Fraud, (3) Violations of the Unfair Business Practices Act, (4) Violations of the California Consumer Legal Remedies Act CCP § 1750, (5) Strict Liability, (6) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress, (7) Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress, (8) Negligence, (9) Negligence Per Se, (10) Financial Elder Abuse, (11) Conversion, and (12) Civil Liability Pursuant to Penal Code § 496(c). 

 

On August 7, 2024, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (FAC) with the same causes of action. 

 

According to the FAC, Plaintiff relied on Defendant Home Depot’s contractor referral network to engage Defendant Pizzo as a contractor to remodel her kitchen. Plaintiff further alleges that Defendant Pizzo was not a licensed contractor, destroyed her kitchen, stole from her, and abandoned the project. Plaintiff also alleges that Defendant Pizzo caused third parties to harass her and damage other property in retaliation for reporting him to the Contractor Licensing Board. 

 

On September 10, 2024, Defendant Home Depot U.S.A., Inc. erroneously sued as Home Depot USA, Inc. (Home Depot) filed a demurrer with motion to strike to the FAC. The court SUSTAINED the demurrer with leave to amend and DENIED the motion to strike.

 

On February 6, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative second amended complaint (SAC).

 

On April 11, 2025, Hone Depot filed a demurrer with motion to strike to the SAC. The court SUSTAINED the demurrer without leave to amend and DENIED the motion to strike as moot.

 

On August 18, 2025, the court ordered Home Depot dismissed.

 

On November 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed a request for entry of default judgment as to Defendant Pizzo which the clerk rejected.

 

On December 2, 2025, the court held a default prove-up hearing, entered default against Defendant Pizzo, and took the matter under submission.

 

On January 6, 2026, the court ruled on the submitted default prove-up hearing. The court found judgment lies to only the breach of contract and negligence causes of action.

 

On February 5, 2026, the court entered default judgment consistent with its January 6, 2026 ruling. The court entered judgment on the breach of contract cause of action in the amount of $18,855.02, $8,964.41 on the negligence cause of action, $15,900.21 in prejudgment interest on the breach of contract cause of action, $1,760.46 in prejudgment interest on the negligence cause of action, for a total award of $45,480.10. Additionally, the court awarded post-judgment interest at a rate of 10% per annum from the date of entry of Judgment until paid in full.

 

On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of intent to move for new trial.

 

On February 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a memorandum of costs.

 

On March 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed a memorandum of points and authorities in support of her notice of intent to move for new trial.

 

On May 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for attorneys’ fees.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

With respect to attorney fees and costs, unless they are specifically provided for by statute (e.g., Cal.¿Code Civ.¿Proc. (CCP)¿§§ 1032, et seq.), the measure and mode of compensation of attorneys and counselors at law is left to the agreement, express or implied, of the parties.¿(CCP § 1021.)¿ The prevailing party on a contract, which specifically provides for attorney fees and costs incurred to enforce the agreement, is entitled to reasonable attorney fees in addition to other costs.¿ (Civ. Code § 1717(a); CCP §§ 1032, 1033.5(a)(10)(A).)¿  

 

The court, upon notice and motion by a party, shall¿determine¿the prevailing party and shall fix, as an element of the costs of¿suit, the reasonable attorney fees.¿ (Civ. Code § 1717(a), (b).)¿ Any notice of motion to claim attorney fees as an element of costs under shall be served and filed before or at the same time the memorandum of costs is served and filed; if only attorney fees are claimed as costs, the notice of motion shall be served and filed within the time specified in CRC 3.1700 for filing a memorandum of¿costs.¿ (CRC 3.1702;¿Gunlock Corp. v. Walk on Water, Inc.¿(1993) 15 Cal.App.4th 1301, 1303, fn. 1.)¿ 

 

“It is well established that the determination of what constitutes reasonable attorney fees is committed to the discretion of the trial court, whose decision cannot be reversed in the absence of an abuse of discretion. [Citation.]”¿ (Melnyk v. Robledo¿(1976) 64 Cal.App.3d 618, 623 624.)  

 

The fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily “begins with the ‘lodestar’ [method], i.e., the number of hours¿reasonably expended¿multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate.”¿ (Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc.¿(2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 154.)¿ “[A] computation of time spent on a case and the reasonable value of that time is fundamental to a determination of an appropriate attorneys’ fee award.”¿ (Margolin v. Reg’l Planning Comm’n¿(1982) 134 Cal.App.3d 999, 1004.)¿ The lodestar figure may then be adjusted, based on consideration of factors specific to the case,¿in order to¿fix the fee at the¿fair market value¿for the legal services provided.¿ (See¿Serrano v. Priest¿(1977) 20 Cal.3d 25, 49 [discussing factors relevant to proper attorneys’ fees award].)¿ Such an approach anchors the trial court’s analysis to an objective determination of the value of the attorney’s services, ensuring that the amount awarded is not arbitrary.¿ (Id. at 48, fn. 23.)¿ The factors considered in determining the modification of the lodestar include “(1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award.”¿ (Mountjoy v. Bank of Am.¿(2016) 245 Cal.App.4th 266, 271.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

In challenging attorney fees as excessive because too many hours of work are claimed, it is the burden of the challenging party to point to the specific items challenged, with a sufficient argument and citations to the evidence.¿ (Premier Medical Management Systems, Inc. v. California Ins. Guaranty Assoc. (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 564.)¿ General arguments that¿fees¿claimed are excessive, duplicative, or unrelated do not suffice.¿ (Ibid.)¿ 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Plaintiff contends that the court should exercise its discretion to award attorneys’ fees to her under CCP § 1029.8 because Defendant Pizzo provided services requiring a license while he was unlicensed, the court entered judgment in Plaintiff’s favor, the fees are reasonable, and the motion is timely.

 

“When a plaintiff requests entry of judgment by default, a request for attorney fees must be made at the same time or fees are forfeited.” (Vincent v. Sonkey (2020) 59 Cal.App.5th 160, 161.) “A postdefault motion would be unfair ‘because a case in which a defendant’s default has been taken necessarily has no adversarial quality and the defaulted defendant would have no right to participate in the motion.’” (Id. at p. 164 [citing Garcia v. Politis (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 1474, 1479] [italics in original].)

 

Here, attorney’s fees are not warranted. Critically, Plaintiff is barred from seeking them now. Indeed, she did not make the request under this theory in either the SAC or at the default prove-up hearing. As articulated in Vincent, such an award would be unfair.

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys’ fees.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for attorneys’ fees is DENIED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 24STCV33307    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 14, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Morning Star, LLC, et al. v. Sea Star Estates Homeowners Association, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV33307

 

MOTION TO QUASH SUBPOENA

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Sea Star Estates Homeowners Association

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Morning Star, LLC

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order quashing and/or modifying the deposition subpoena for production of business records directed to third party Stacie Vince such that emails with Sea Star Estate’s counsel are not produced.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion to quash is GRANTED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On December 17, 2024, Plaintiffs Morning Star, LLC and Patrick Nazemi (Plaintiffs) filed a Complaint against Defendants Sea Star Estates Homeowners Association, Hovsep Kouzouyan, Hedayat Ahmadpour, and Claire Cabrey (Defendants) with eleven causes of action for: (1) Violation of Civ. Code § 5100(a)(2); (2) Violation of Civ. Code §§ 5500 and 5501; (3) Breach of Bylaws; (4) Declaratory Relief (Derivative Cause of Action); (5) Fraud (Derivative Cause of Action); (6) Fraud; (7) Trespass; (8) Trespass to Timber; (9) Negligence; (10) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress; and (11) Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress. 

 

According to the Complaint, Plaintiff Morning Star owns property, where Plaintiff Nazemi resides, located at 6368 Sea Star Drive, Malibu, California (the Property) in the Sea Star Estates subdivision. Plaintiffs allege that Defendant Sea Star violated various Civil Code sections pertaining to HOA Board elections, that the individual defendants hold themselves out as validly elected Board members or agents when they are not, fraudulently enter contracts, fraudulent approve architectural plans, allowed another property owner to cut down Plaintiffs’ trees, change the gate codes, prohibit access to tennis courts, and otherwise frustrate Plaintiffs’ use of the Property. 

 

On February 14, 2025, Defendants Hovsep Kouzouyan and Hedayat Ahmadpour (K&A) filed a demurrer to the Complaint which the court OVERRULED as to the Seventh and Eighth Causes of Action and SUSTAINED as to the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Ninth, Tenth, and Eleventh Causes of Action with 21 days’ leave to amend. 

 

On March 18, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Motion re: Appointment of Receiver for Sea State Estates Home Owners Association which the court GRANTED. 

 

On March 24, 2025, Defendants Sea Star Estates Homeowners Association and Claire Cabrey (Sea Star Estates) filed a demurrer with motion to strike that was mooted due to the court’s ruling on K&A’s demurrer. 

 

On June 10, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the operative First Amended Complaint (FAC). 

 

On July 14, 2025, Sea Star Estates filed a demurrer with motion to strike to the FAC. 

 

On July 29, 2025, K&A filed their demurrer to the FAC. 

 

On September 4, 2025, the court ruled on the demurrers with motions to strike as follows: Demurrers to the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Eighth, and Eleventh Causes of Action are OVERRULED, in part but the Fourth Cause of Action is SUSTAINED as to Cabrey only; Demurrers to the Ninth, Tenth, and Eleventh Causes of Action are OVERRULED as to Plaintiff Morning Star, LLC and SUSTAINED without leave to amend as Nazemi only; Motion to Strike is GRANTED in part as to Page 16, line 27-28, Paragraph 89: “As such, Plaintiffs are entitled to exemplary damages pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 3294 for sake of example to punish Defendants” and Page 23, line 25: “2. Exemplary Damages pursuant to Civil Code section 3294.” The Motion to Strike is otherwise DENIED. 

 

On September 17, 2025, K&A filed an Answer to the FAC.  

 

On September 18, 2025, Defendants Sea Star Estates and Cabrey filed an Answer to the FAC. 

 

On November 18, 2025, Proposed Plaintiffs-in-Intervention Keith B. Canter and Karen Elise Schoen, Trustees of the Canter Schoen Family Trust U/T/D March 17, 2015 (Intervenors) filed a motion for leave to intervene which the court GRANTED.

 

On December 2, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a motion for leave to file an amended complaint which the court DENIED.

 

On January 22, 2026, Intervenors filed a notice of related case to Case No. 26SMCV00214 Keith B. Canter v. Sea Star Estates Homeowners Association which the court GRANTED.

 

On January 27, 2026, Intervenors filed an amended notice of related case which the court GRANTED.

 

On June 1, 2026, Sea Star filed the instant motion to quash. On June 1, 2026, Non-party Stacey Vince filed a response to Sea Star’s motion to quash indicating she would not produce documents until the outcome of the motion to quash. On July 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On August 7, 2026, Sea Star filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

A party may bring a motion for an “order quashing the subpoena [for production of documents] entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1987.1(a), (b)(1).)

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Sea Star estates contends the motion is timely, the majority of the records sought contain attorney-client privileged and work-product protected emails, and there is no waiver.[1]

 

Plaintiff argues the privileges do not apply because Ms. Vince was never a board member or agent of Sea Star. Plaintiff adds that Sea Star already expressly and voluntarily waived the attorney-client privilege as to their communications with Gary Gitlin in written discovery. As to work-product protection, Plaintiff argues Sea Star is entitled, at most, to a privilege log, but otherwise asserts that this objection was waived.

 

Sea Star replies that she was treated as a board member, was acting in concert with board members to support the interests of the association, Sea Star did not waive privilege, limited document production is not wholesale waiver, and they do not object to a privilege log.

 

However, there is a threshold issue that is dispositive. Code of Civil Procedure section 1985(b) requires that a subpoena duces tecum issued before trial be accompanied by an affidavit showing good cause for production and setting forth in full detail the materiality of the requested documents to the issues in the action. The affidavit must provide specific facts sufficient to permit the court to determine good cause and materiality; conclusory assertions are insufficient. (Lee v. Superior Court (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1108, 1127–1128; Johnson v. Superior Court (1968) 258 Cal.App.2d 829, 835–837; McClatchy Newspapers v. Superior Court (1945) 26 Cal.2d 386, 396, 398.)

 

Plaintiff, the party issuing the subpoena, has neither attached nor presented an affidavit satisfying section 1985(b). Indeed, the absence of an affidavit presents a more fundamental defect than an affidavit containing an inadequate factual showing. A subpoena duces tecum has “no force or effect” where the affidavit required by section 1985 fails to comply with the statute. (Johnson, supra, 258 Cal.App.2d at p. 835.)

 

Without the required affidavit, the court lacks the factual showing necessary to determine whether the documents sought are material to the issues or whether good cause exists for their production. (Id. at p. 837.) The court therefore does not reach the parties' remaining arguments concerning the merits of the subpoena.

 

The subpoena is QUASHED pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1.

 

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Sea Star’s motion to quash.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion to quash is GRANTED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 26SMCV00342    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 14, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Morning Star, LLC v. Keith B. Canter, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                26SMCV00342

 

DEMURRER TO CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Cross-Defendant Patrick Farshad Nazemi

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendant/Cross-Complainant Keith B. Canter, Trustee of the Canter Schoen Family Trust u/t/d March 17, 2015

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      Demurrer to the entire cross-complaint as uncertain as to whether Nazemi is being sued in his individual capacity or as trustee of the Nevada Reliable Trust;

2.      Demurrer to the first and second causes of action for failing to state sufficient facts to constitute a cause of action.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Demurrers to the cross-complaint are OVERRULED;

2.      Nazemi to file his answer within 14 days of this ruling.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On¿January 26,¿2026, Plaintiff Morning Star, LLC (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against¿ 

Defendants¿Keith B. Canter and Karen Elise Schoen, Trustees of The Canter Schoen Family¿¿ 

Trust U/T/D March 17, 2015. The caption¿alleging¿two¿causes of action. First,¿Breaches¿ 

Declarations of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Sea Star Estates and, second, for¿ 

Breach of Restrictive Covenants.¿The body of the complaint, however, alleges three causes of¿¿ 

Action: First, Breaches Declarations of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Sea Star¿¿ 

Estates¿(relating to construction of improvements to existing Canter home);¿Second,¿Breaches¿ 

Declarations of Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions for Sea Star Estates (relating to original¿¿ 

construction); and, Third, Breach of Restrictive Covenants.¿ 

¿ 

On¿March 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for preliminary injunction¿that relates only to the first cause of action which the court DENIED without prejudice. 

¿ 

On March 27, 2026, Defendant/Cross-Complainant Keith B. Canter, Trustee of the Canter Schoen Family Trust u/t/d March 17, 2015 (Cross-Complainant) filed a cross-complaint against Cross-Defendants Morning Star, LLC and Patrick Farshad Nazemi, Trustee of the Nevada Reliable Trust (Cross-Defendants) with two causes of action for: (1) violation of the California Coastal Act; and (2) nuisance. According to the cross-complaint, Cross-Defendants maintain a tall ficus hedge and multiple palm trees on the Morning Star property that violate the landscaping restrictions in the Coastal Development Permit and unlawfully block ocean views from Cross-Complainant’s adjacent property. He further alleges that Nazemi intentionally planted and continues to maintain this non-native vegetation without required approvals, creating fire hazards, root intrusion, shade impacts, and substantial daily Coastal Act violations. Additionally, Cross-Complainant claims Nazemi placed surveillance cameras aimed at his home to harass and intimidate him, causing emotional distress, privacy violations, and property-value diminution. 

 

On April 28, 2026, Morning Star, LLC filed a demurrer to the cross-complaint which the court OVERRULED. As of September 8, 2026, Morning Star has not filed an answer to the cross-complaint.

 

On June 25, 2026, Nazemi filed the instant demurrer to the cross-complaint. On September 2, 2026, Cross-Complainant filed an untimely opposition. Replies were due on or before September 4, 2026. On September 8, 2026, Nazemi filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Meet and Confer¿ 

¿ 

Prior to filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to satisfy their meet and confer obligations pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41, and demonstrate that they so satisfied their meet and confer obligation by submitting a declaration pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41(a)(2) & (3). ¿The meet and confer requirement also applies to motions to strike. (CCP § 435.5.)¿ 

 

Here, the parties met and conferred via telephone on June 18, 2026. (Declaration of Patrick Nazemi ¶ 2.) This requirement is therefore met. 

 

Demurrer – Sufficiency of Facts¿ 

¿ 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿(Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.¿In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice.¿(Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)¿“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. …. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”¿(Hahn¿147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true. (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.) Courts also consider exhibits attached to the complaint and incorporated by reference. (See Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94.)¿¿ 

 

Demurrer – Uncertainty

 

CCP § 430.10(f) provides that a party may demurrer to a pleading because: “The pleading is uncertain. As used in this subdivision, “uncertain” includes ambiguous and unintelligible.”

 

However, “demurrers for uncertainty are disfavored, and are granted only if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” (Morris v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2022) 78 Cal.App.5th 279, 292.) “A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Chen v. Berenjian (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 811, 822.) “A demurrer for uncertainty should be overruled when the facts as to which the complaint is uncertain are presumptively within the defendant’s knowledge.” (Ibid.)

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The court DENIES Nazemi’s request for judicial notice as superfluous to this ruling.

 

Uncertainty

 

Nazemi contends the cross-complaint is fatally uncertain because it is not clear in what capacity he is being sued – individually or as trustee of The Nevada Reliable Trust.

 

Cross-Complainant argues this is a distinction without a difference since they alleged Nazemi is the alter ego of Morning Star.[1]

 

Nazemi replies that the court should not consider the late reply and that it is critical to specify whether he is sued individually or as trustee of the Nevada Reliable Trust to determine what assets would be liable on a judgment. He adds that the boilerplate alter ego allegations are defective.

 

After reviewing the cross-complaint, it is not uncertain. Notably, reading the cross-complaint in whole and in context, it is clear that Cross-Complainant seeks relief against Nazemi, individually, rather than as against the Nevada Reliable Trust. (See Galdjie v. Darwish (2003) 113 Cal.App.4th 1331, 1344-1345 [discussing common law context for a trustee’s liability for actions taken in the administration of their duties as trustee.]) To start, Cross-Complainant alleges that Nazemi is an alter ego of Morning Star, an LLC whose sole member is Nazemi as trustee as the Nevada Reliable Trust. (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 2, 3, 7.) Next, Nazemi resides at the Morning Star property. (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 9.) Nazemi allegedly maintains the palm and ficus trees on Morning Star’s property, planted those trees, and installed video cameras to face Cross-Complainant’s property.  (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 11, 19, 21, 25, 26, 27.) Indeed, Cross-Complainant alleges that Nazemi “on his own behalf and on behalf of Morning Star” violated Cross-Complainant’s privacy rights and property rights. (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 26, 27.) It is therefore clear, and not uncertain, that these claims are against Nazemi individually.

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES the demurrer for uncertainty.

 

First Cause of Action: Violation of the California Coastal Act

 

Nazemi contends this claim fails because the cross-complaint alleges that he had approval from the City of Malibu to plant the ficus trees.

 

Cross-Complainant incorporates by reference their arguments to Morning Star’s prior demurrer.

 

Nazemi replies that since he is not the alleged owner, he is not liable for violations under the Act and reiterates that the allegations show he subsequently obtained approval for the planting.

 

 

“Any person who violates any provision of this division may be civilly liable . . . .” (Pub. Res. Code § 30820(a).) “Civil liability may be imposed by the superior court in accordance with this article on any person who performs or undertakes development that is in violation of this division or that is inconsistent with any coastal development permit previously issued by the commission, . . . .” (Id. at subd. (a)(1).) 

 

After reviewing the cross-complaint, Cross-Complainant sufficiently stated a claim for violation of the California Coastal Act, in theory. First, Cross-Complainant alleges a violation of Coastal Development Permit No. 4-97-252, Special Condition No. 1. (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 13, 14, 16, 17.) Reading the allegations in full and in context, Cross-Complainant alleges that planting/maintaining the palm and ficus trees violate the prohibition on invasive, non-indigenous plant species articulated in Special Condition No. 1. (Cross-Compl. ¶ 14.) Whether this is true is outside the scope of this motion.

 

Additionally, the court rejects Nazemi’s argument that the claim is moot due to obtaining approval. The Cross-Complaint alleges that Nazemi sought “after-the-fact approval” which “was conditionally approved on January 24, 2023” but seeks penalties from the time it was noncompliant until it was compliant. (Cross-Compl. ¶ 19.) Put differently, Nazemi underquotes the allegations.

 

Finally, the court rejects Nazemi’s argument that he, as the non-owner, escapes liability at this stage. The Cross-Complaint alleges that the Act applies to “any person who performs or undertakes development that is in violation of this division . . . .” (Cross-Compl. ¶ 18.)

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES Nazemi’s demurrer to the first cause of action.

 

Second Cause of Action: Nuisance

 

Nazemi contends this claim fails because alleged blocked views, risk of wildfires, and tree roots without allegations of damage, and installation of security cameras on his own property cannot support the claim.

 

Cross-Complainant incorporates by reference their arguments to Morning Star’s prior demurrer.

 

Nazemi replies that since he is not the alleged owner, this claim fails. He adds that the security camera installation is insufficient as a matter of law to constitute a nuisance.

 

The elements for a private nuisance claim are: (1) interference with plaintiff’s use and enjoyment of plaintiff’s property; (2) invasion of plaintiff’s use and enjoyment involves substantial actual damage; and (3) interference is unreasonable as to the nature, duration or amount. (Today's IV v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 1137, 1176.)

 

Here, Cross-Complainant sufficiently stated a private nuisance claim against Nazemi. Critically, Nazemi allegedly caused a nuisance by installing video cameras on his property that surveil the Canter property. (Cross-Compl. ¶ 25.) The cameras allegedly face “three sides of the Canter property” and observe “all activity on the Canter property, including inside the interior of the residence.” (Ibid.) Indeed, Cross-Complainant alleges that Nazemi stated he “has observed Canter taking a shower in his bathroom.” (Ibid.) This is sufficient for the pleadings stage. (See Shoen v. Zacarias (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 1112, 1124-1125 [remaining nuisance claim concerning video cameras aimed at portions of property belonging to one party].) With this in mind, the court declines to address the alleged nuisance caused by the hedge.

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES Nazemi’s demurrer to the second cause of action.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Demurrers to the cross-complaint are OVERRULED;

2.      Nazemi to file his answer within 14 days of this ruling.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
