APPLICATION OF PLAINTIFFS FOR PRO HAC VICE ADMISSION

 

Application filed July 6, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiffs Kenneth Lawson and Monique Jourdain-Lawson

RESPONDING PARTY: N/A

NOTICE: Defective.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Admission of James D. Montgomery, Jr., Esq. to appear as counsel pro hac vice.

RULING: Continued to August 26, 2026.

 

BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION

 

On July 6, 2026, Plaintiffs Kenneth Lawson and Monique Jourdain-Lawson filed an application to admit James D. Montgomery, Jr., Esq. to appear pro hac vice in this action.

 

A non-licensee of the State Bar may appear as counsel pro hac vice in the Court’s discretion. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40, subd. (a).) An active State Bar licensee must be associated as attorney of record. (Ibid.) The applicant must file a verified application together with proof of service by mail of the application and notice of hearing on all appearing parties and on the State Bar at its San Francisco office. (Id., subd. (c)(1).) The application must contain the information required by subdivision (d). (Id., subd. (d).)

 

Here, Montgomery resides and maintains his office in Illinois and states that he is not a California resident and does not regularly practice or conduct other business in California. (Montgomery Decl., ¶ 2.) He has been admitted to practice before the Supreme Court of Illinois since November 1, 1982, is a member in good standing, and has never been suspended or disbarred. (Id., ¶¶ 1, 3-4.) Montgomery has filed no application to appear pro hac vice in California during the preceding two years. (Id., ¶ 5.) California attorney John C. Allen IV is associated as attorney of record. (Allen Decl., ¶¶ 1, 3; Montgomery Decl., ¶ 6.) Allen also declares that the application and proposed order were delivered to the State Bar with payment of the $50 application fee. (Allen Decl., ¶ 4.) The application therefore contains the information required by rule 9.40, subdivision (d).

 

The proof of service, however, does not comply with rule 9.40(c)(1). It is unsigned, does not identify the person who performed service, and does not list the State Bar as a recipient. It also reflects service by mail on July 7, 2026 for the July 29, 2026 hearing. Because section 1005(b) requires 16 court days’ notice plus five calendar days for service by mail within California, the application and notice of hearing were required to be mailed by July 2, 2026. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40, subd. (c)(1).)

 

CONCLUSION

 

The application is CONTINUED to August 26, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Department F46. No later than July 30, 2026, Plaintiffs must serve by mail a copy of the application and notice of the continued hearing on all appearing parties and on the State Bar at its San Francisco office and file a signed proof of service complying with Code of Civil Procedure section 1013a.

PLAINTIFF ELTON SPAIN’S MOTION FOR REASSIGNMENT OF ACTION TO THE CENTRAL DISTRICT

 

Motion filed on June 2, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Elton Spain

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Metcalf Construction Services, LLC

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Plaintiff seeks an order reassigning this action to the Central District at the Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

RULING: Denied.

 

BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION

Elton Spain (Plaintiff) filed the Complaint on May 13, 2022 against Metcalf Construction, Inc. and Joseph Dante Dalessio. Plaintiff filed this motion on June 2, 2026. Metcalf Construction Services, LLC (Defendant) filed an opposition on July 16, 2026. No timely reply was filed.

 

A party filing a motion must file a supporting memorandum unless the motion is listed in California Rules of Court, rule 3.1114. The Court may construe the absence of a memorandum as an admission that the motion lacks merit and as grounds for denial. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1113(a).)

 

Here, although Plaintiff’s notice states that the motion is supported by an attached memorandum of points and authorities and the Declaration of Daryl Crouse, neither document was filed. The Court therefore construes the absence of a supporting memorandum as an admission that the motion is not meritorious.

 

CONCLUSION

Plaintiff’s Motion for Reassignment is denied.
