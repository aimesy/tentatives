[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO VACATE DISMISSAL

 

Dept. U

8:30 a.m.

September 11, 2026

 

I.               BACKGROUND

On June 17, 2022, Plaintiff Mokhim Rasooli (”Plaintiff”) filed his original complaint against Defendants Mark Carillo (“Carillo”), Yi Sun Kim (“Kim”), and James Felton (“Felton”), alleging: (1) breach of contract; (2) fraud – failure to tell the truth; and (3) bankruptcy.  Plaintiff has previously been represented by counsel but is now self-represented.[1]

On January 3, 2023, the Court sustained, with leave to amend, Felton’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s original complaint.

On June 14, 2023, Plaintiff filed his first amended complaint against Defendants Joseph A. Cardella (“Cardella”), Carillo, and Zeyad Elalami (“Elalami”) without specifying any causes of action.

On July 8, 2024, Plaintiff filed his second amended complaint (the “SAC”) against Cardella, Carillo, and Elalami, alleging: (1) breach of contract against Elalami; (2) fraud against Elalami; and (3) common counts against Cardella and Carillo.

On August 27, 2024, Cardella filed his answer to the SAC, asserting his general denial and several affirmative defenses.  Also on that date, a default was entered against Carillo.

On March 3, 2025, the Court sustained, with leave to amend, Elalami’s demurrer for sufficiency and uncertainty as to both causes of action alleged against him in the SAC.  Plaintiff did not file a further amended complaint.

On March 19, 2025, Plaintiff filed a request for dismissal of Elalami.

On April 30, 2025, the Court set this case for trial on January 5, 2026.

On October 24, 2025, Plaintiff filed a request for dismissal (with prejudice) of Cardella.

On October 29, 2025, the Court set an OSC re: entry of judgment for November 24, 2025.  The OSC was as to Defendant Carillo, who was in default and who was the sole remaining Defendant.

On November 24, 2025, Plaintiff failed to appear on the OSC re: entry of judgment.  The Court thereupon set an OSC re: dismissal for failure to prosecute for December 26, 2025.

On December 2, 2025, at a non-appearance case review, the Court continued the hearing on the OSC re: dismissal for failure to prosecute to January 14, 2026.

On December 10, 2025, Plaintiff filed a notice of substitution removing Mr. Mann as his counsel and stating that Plaintiff was representing himself.

On December 23, 2025, Melvin Betnun filed a notice of substitution stating that he was replacing Mr. Mann as counsel.  Mr. Betnun’s statement was incorrect to the extent that, in fact, Plaintiff Rasooli was in pro per at the time Mr. Betnun substituted in.

On January 13, 2026, Mr. Betnun filed a request for default as to Mark Carillo for $5 million.  (While not germane to the current motion, the Court nonetheless notes that the operative SAC indicates $30,000 paid to Mr. Carillo and Plaintiff’s demand therein as to Mr. Carillo appears to be $25,000.)

On January 14, 2026, no one made an appearance at the hearing on the OSC re: dismissal for failure to prosecute.  The Court thereupon dismissed this case without prejudice under Code of Civil Procedure section 583.410 and California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1340.

On January 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed his motion to vacate and set aside the Court’s dismissal of the case based on attorney error.

On April 1, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s motion to set aside/vacate the dismissal of Mark Carillo.  The Court vacated the January 14, 2026, dismissal of Mark Carillo.  

On April 29, 2026, the Court dismissed Mark Carillo without prejudice.

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to vacate and set aside the dismissal entered on January 14, 2026, and on April 29, 2026.

As of 5:00 P.M. September 8, 2026, no opposition has been filed.

II.            LEGAL STANDARD

The Court may, “upon any terms as may be just,” relieve a party “from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against” the party through “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.” The application must be “accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed” and “made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months,” after the dismissal was taken. (Civ. Proc. Code § 473, subd. (b).) 

The Court shall vacate a resulting default, default judgment, or dismissal whenever the application is made within six months of entry, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, unless the Court finds the dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney’s fault. (Civ. Proc. Code § 473, subd. (b).)

III.          DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves to vacate the dismissal entered on January 14, 2026, and on April 29, 2026, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).  Plaintiff argues both dismissals resulted from mistake, inadvertence, and excusable neglect of Plaintiff’s former counsel.  Plaintiff contends his former counsel, substituted in during December 2025, relied on staff’s impression that a request for entry of default was required, however, the matter was already post-default and awaiting judgment.  Plaintiff contends Carillo has never appeared in this action, no evidence has been lost, and liability has already been established by default, therefore Carillo will suffer no harm or prejudice if the dismissal is vacated.

The January 14, 2026, dismissal of Carillo has already been vacated by a previously filed motion to set aside.  The Court vacated the January 14 dismissal on April 1, 2026, finding that Plaintiff’s former counsel’s explanation as to his mistaken belief regarding request for entry of default led to the dismissal.  (See 4/1/26 Minute Order.)  Plaintiff’s motion as to the January 14, 2026, dismissal is therefore moot.

Next, the Court finds the instant motion is timely.  The motion was filed on May 28, 2026.  Measured against the April 29, 2026, dismissal, the application falls within the six-month outer limit that governs section 473, subdivision (b).

However, Plaintiff fails to meet the requirements of section 473, subdivision (b).  The April 29, 2026, dismissal was made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 583.410 and California Rules of Court 3.1340 for failure to prosecute. (See 4/29/26 Minute Order.)  Plaintiff asserts he and his former counsel did not move forward with this matter after Defendant threatened Plaintiff and his counsel with sanctions if they maintained the action. (Mot., p. 2.)  Plaintiff argues his former counsel was “distracted” by Defendant’s threats. (Mot., p. 3.)

These asserted grounds all concern the conduct and state of mind of Plaintiff’s former counsel; however, Plaintiff only submits his own declaration.  Plaintiff’s declaration does not competently establish counsel’s reasons for failure to prosecute or that counsel’s neglect was excusable.  Further, Plaintiff’s application is not accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed, such as, as would be appropriate here, a default judgment package.

Plaintiff’s motion is denied without prejudice.

IV.          CONCLUSION

Plaintiff Mokhim Rasooli’s motion to set aside/vacate the dismissal of Mark Carillo entered on January 14, 2026, is DENIED as moot. 

 

Plaintiff Mokhim Rasooli’s motion to set aside/vacate the dismissal of Mark Carillo entered on April 29, 2026, is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Plaintiff Mokhim Rasooli is ORDERED to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL DEFENDANT’S COMPLIANCE WITH THE COURT’S MARCH 17, 2026, ORDER AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

Dept. U

8:30 a.m.

A.    BACKGROUND

 This case is a wrongful death action against an addiction treatment center.

On July 26, 2024, Plaintiffs Jeff Bynum (“Plaintiff”) and the Estate of Alexandria Bynum filed a complaint against Defendant Tarzana Treatment Centers, Inc. (“Defendant”) alleging a single cause of action for negligence.  The complaint alleges Plaintiff’s daughter Alexandria Bynum (“Decedent”) died of an overdose within hours of being negligently discharged from Defendant’s drug rehabilitation treatment facility.

On January 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel inspection of Defendant’s email system.  The Court granted the motion on March 17, 2026, entering a Stipulation and Order Re: Inspection and permitting Plaintiffs to inspect Tarzana’s computer and email system in the manner and scope set forth in the order.  On March 20, 2026, the inspection occurred.

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel Defendant’s compliance with the Court’s March 17, 2026, Order (the “Order”).

On August 18, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition brief.

B.    RULING

 

Plaintiff Jeff Bynum’s motion to compel compliance with the Court’s March 17, 2026, Order is GRANTED.

Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant and its counsel is DENIED.

 

C.    LEGAL STANDARD

 

“If a party filing a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling under Sections 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, 2031.240, and 2031.280 thereafter fails to permit the inspection, copying, testing, or sampling in accordance with that party's statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.320, subd. (a).)

D.    DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff moves for an order to compel Defendant’s compliance with the Court’s March 17, 2026, order.  Plaintiff argues he has been diligent in attempting to obtain important and necessary documents in this matter, including multiple sets of written discovery and a two-volume deposition of Defendant’s PMK.  However, Defendant refuses to comply with the Order.  Plaintiff argues Defendant’s HIPAA and third-party privacy concerns do not override the Order.  Plaintiff seeks sanctions against Defendant and their counsel.

            In opposition, Defendant argues Defendant is making diligent efforts to comply with the Order but that extensive redactions are required due to the highly sensitive private medical data of non-involved third parties.  Defendant’s counsel declares Defendant anticipates the redaction will be complete prior to the date of the hearing and that production will accordingly be forthcoming before that date. (Loeck Decl., ¶ 4.)

            There is no dispute Defendant has failed to fully comply with the Order.  Defendant concedes production remained incomplete as of August 18, 2026. The Court thus retains authority to order compliance. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.320, subd. (a).)  While Defendant argues Plaintiff’s motion should be denied because they plan to produce all documents prior to the hearing date, there is nothing in the current record indicating all documents have been produced.

            Therefore, Plaintiff’s motion to compel compliance is granted.  Defendant is ordered to complete its production of the documents obtained at the March 20, 2026, inspection of Defendant’s email system, consistent with the Order.  Plaintiff’s request that Defendant produce all the documents without redaction is denied.

            Plaintiff seeks monetary sanctions against Defendant and its counsel in the amount of $6,750.00 for the time and expense in preparing the ex parte application, the instant motion and all meet and confer efforts, and to pay Plaintiff’s expert bill for conducting the inspection and reviewing Defendant’s initial production.

            The court shall impose a monetary sanction “against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel compliance with a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.320, subd. (b).)

            The Court finds imposition of sanctions at this juncture would be unjust.  The substantial volume of material requiring redaction reasonably accounts for Defendant’s delay.  Further, Defendant’s counsel declares an intent to complete production prior to the hearing for this matter. 

E.    CONCLUSION

Plaintiff Jeff Bynum’s motion to compel compliance with the Court’s March 17, 2026, Order is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant and its counsel is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff is ORDERED to give notice.

I.               BACKGROUND

            This is a lemon law action. On¿August 26, 2025, Plaintiff Ludvin R. Ramos (“Plaintiff”) filed his complaint against Defendants Ford Motor Company and Does 1-50, alleging: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; (2) Breach of Implied Warranties Pursuant to Song-Beverly; (3) Failure to Promptly Repurchase Product Pursuant to Song-Beverly; and (4) Failure to Commence Repairs Within a Reasonable Time and to Complete them Within 30 Days Pursuant to Song-Beverly.  

            On July 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motions to compel initial discovery.

            On August 12, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for relief from waiver.

            Defendant filed an opposition to Plaintiff’s motions to compel on August 28, 2026.

            As of 5:00pm on September 8, 2026, nothing further has been filed.

II.            LEGAL STANDARD

a.     Motion for Relief of Waiver of Objections

“If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the party waives any objection to the requests, including one based on privilege.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)  However, a party may be relieved of that waiver if “(1) [t]he party has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance with [the Discovery Act]” and “(2) [t]he party’s failure to serve a timely response was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subds. (a)(1)-(2).) 

“If a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to serve a timely response to it,” the party “waives any objection to the demand, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300.)  “The court, on motion, may relieve that party from this waiver on its determination that both of the following conditions are satisfied: (1) The party has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance with Sections 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, 2031.240, and 2031.280,” and “(2) The party's failure to serve a timely response was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subds. (a)(1)-(2).)

The Civil Discovery Act does not include a definition of “substantial compliance,” and few cases have addressed the circumstances under which a response will be deemed not in substantial compliance. (See St. Mary v. Superior Court (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 762, 778 (St. Mary).) Substantial compliance means actual compliance with respect to the substance essential to every reasonable objective of the statute. (Id. at p. 790.) However, substantial compliance should not be understood as requiring actual compliance with every specific statutory requirement. (Ibid.)  

A court may not find that only some portions of a document containing responses are code-compliant, but must instead determine whether the document as a whole substantially complies. (Ibid. [this position is supported by the fact that there is an effective statutory vehicle to compel a responding party to cure unsatisfactory responses].) 

The statutory language “mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect” in the discovery statute should be interpreted using the same general principles developed in application of the identical language in section 473, subdivision (b). (Scottsdale Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 263, 275.) Although the party moving for relief under section 473 has the burden to show that the mistake, inadvertence, or neglect was excusable, any doubts as to that showing must be resolved in favor of the moving party. (New Albertsons, Inc. v. Superior Court (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1420.) 

b.     Motion to Compel Initial Responses to Requests for Production of Documents  

Where there has been no response to a demand to produce documents, the demanding party may seek an order compelling a response.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300.)¿ A party’s failure to timely respond waives all objections.¿ (Ibid.)¿ 

c.     Motion to Compel Initial Responses to Interrogatories  

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a response.¿ Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.¿ A respondent’s failure to timely respond waives all objections.¿ (Ibid.)¿ 

III.          DISCUSSION 

a.     Defendant’s Motion for Relief from Waiver is granted.

Defendant seeks relief from the waiver of objections on the basis that they served substantially compliant responses to Plaintiff’s Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production, Set One on June 18, 2026.  Defendant argues their failure to serve timely responses was a result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect, due to a typo in the electronic service address. 

On June 18, 2026, Defendant served substantive responses to Plaintiff’s requests, however mistakenly emailed the responses to “eservice@resalaw.com” rather than the designated service address, “eservice@rezalaw.com.” (Sullivan Decl., ¶ 4, Exh. B.)  The erroneous transmission generated a bounce-back email that automatically filtered into a spam folder and was therefore not seen by counsel or staff. (Sullivan Decl., ¶ 6.)  Upon learning of the clerical error through Plaintiff’s July 21, 2026, meet and confer letter, Defendant promptly investigated the issue and re-served the responses on July 22, 2026. (Sullivan Decl., ¶ 11.)

Defendant’s responses were in substantial compliance with the Discovery Act and sections 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, 2031.240, and 2031.280, because they included substantive responses as well as objections addressing the specific discovery propounded by Plaintiff.  

Further, Defendant’s failure to timely respond was due to counsel’s inadvertence and excusable neglect, as the responses were emailed to the wrong address due to a typographical error.

Therefore, Defendant’s motion for relief from waiver is granted.

b.     Plaintiff’s Motions to Compel Initial Discovery Responses are denied.

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant’s verified, objection-free narrative answers on the grounds Defendant failed to serve a timely response, and the responses served retained extensive objections.  Plaintiff contends Plaintiff electronically served Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production, Set One, on May 14, 2026.  Defendant’s responses were due June 16, 2026.  Plaintiff argues Defendant waived all objections when the June 16 deadline expired, and that Defendant’s belated responses do not moot Plaintiff’s initial-response motions.   

In opposition, Defendant argues it timely served its discovery, but to an email address that contained a typographical error, which was a result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.   Defendant maintains its responses otherwise substantially comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.230.  Defendant further avers Plaintiff did not serve his discovery requests until May 20, 2026, therefore Defendant’s responses were due on June 19, not June 16. (Wang Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.)

a.     The motions are denied as moot.

Defendant’s evidence supports Defendant’s argument that Plaintiff’s discovery responses were served on May 20, 2026.  (Wang Decl., ¶ 2, Ex. A.)  Defendant’s responses were due June 19, 2026. (Wang Decl., ¶ 3.)  Defendant served responses on June 18, 2026, but the electronic service address used for service contained a typographical error, due to a clerical error. (Wang Decl., ¶ 4.)  As discussed above, the Court grants Defendant’s motion for relief from waiver of objections.     

Further, a motion to compel initial responses asks only for responses where none were served. Serving late responses does not take away the Court’s authority to hear the motion; whether to go forward is left to the Court’s discretion. Once the responding party serves complete responses, the relief the motion sought has been provided, and the Court may deny the motion as moot. A party who believes the served responses are deficient must instead move to compel further responses. (Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 403–404, 408–409.)

Plaintiff admits responses were served prior to filing the instant motions to compel initial discovery.  Plaintiff contends the served responses are deficient because they contain boilerplate objections.  Therefore, the proper motion would be a motion to compel further responses.  Both Plaintiff and Defendant filed a “protective” separate statement with their motions.  However, Plaintiff only argues Defendant did not serve a timely response in his separate statement.

Therefore, the Court exercises its discretion to deny Plaintiff’s motions to compel initial discovery as moot. ¿ 

IV.          CONCLUSION 

Defendant Ford Motor Company’s motion for relief from waiver of objections is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Ludvin R. Ramos’s motions to compel Defendant Ford Motor Company’s initial responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production of Document, Set One, and Special Interrogatories, Set One, are DENIED. 

 

Defendant Ford Motor Company is ORDERED to give notice.

I.               BACKGROUND

This case is a quiet title action arising from an alleged wrongful foreclosure sale.

On January 21, 2026, Kohansameh filed her answer to the complaint.

On June 1, 2026, Kohansameh filed her application for a temporary restraining order (“TRO”) and order to show cause (“OSC”) re: preliminary injunction.

Also on June 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed its opposition brief.

On June 2, 2026, Judge Anthony Mohr granted in part Kohansameh’s application for a TRO and entered an order that Plaintiff be, among other things, “immediately restrained and enjoined from performing, directing, permitting, or causing any construction, alteration, or work of any kind at 23476 Palm Drive, Calabasas, California 91302, pending further order.”  (Order dated June 2, 2026, ¶ 1.)  Judge Mohr declined to issue part of Kohansameh’s proposed order that would require Plaintiff to immediately vacate the property.

On June 8, 2026, Kohansameh filed the instant motion for leave to file a cross-complaint.

On June 12, 2026, Judge Mohr set a hearing on an OSC re: preliminary injunction for July 6, 2026.

On June 17, 2026, Kohansameh filed an attorney declaration in support of her request for an order continuing the hearing on the OSC re: preliminary injunction.

On July 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed its first amended complaint against Kane, Bronzetree, RTI, Peak, and Kohansameh, alleging: (1) wrongful foreclosure; (2) cancellation of instruments; (3) promissory estoppel; and (4) quiet title.

As of 5:00 P.M. September 8, 2026, no opposition has been filed.

II.                  LEGAL STANDARD

“A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint...may file a cross-complaint setting forth...Any cause of action he has against any of the parties who filed the complaint...against him.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (a).)   “Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (b).)  A cross-complaint may name a person as a cross-defendant, whether or not already a party, if joinder would be permitted had the cross-complaint been filed as an independent action.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.20.)

“Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (c).)  Where the proposed cross-complaint asserts a related cause of action against a party who filed the complaint, leave is governed by section 426.50, under which the court “shall grant … leave … to file the cross-complaint … if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith,” a provision to be “liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of causes of action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.)  A motion to file such a cross-complaint “must be granted unless bad faith of the moving party is demonstrated where forfeiture would otherwise result.”  (Silver Organizations, Ltd. v. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 98-99.)

 

III.          DISCUSSION

Kohansameh moves for an order granting leave to file a cross-complaint for private nuisance, waste, trespass, fraud, negligence, and injunctive relief against Plaintiff, Cole Moscatel, RTI Properties, Inc., and Roes 1 through 100.  Kohansameh contends the proposed claims arise from newly discovered facts concerning alleged unpermitted and substandard construction at 23476 Palm Drive, Calabasas, CA (“Subject Property”). 

Kohansameh’s motion is granted.  First, the proposed cross-complaint satisfies section 428.10, subdivision (b).  This action is a quiet title action concerning title to and the condition of the Subject Property, and the proposed cross-complaint asserts claims, rights, and interests in that same property and controversy. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (b).)  The cross-claims arise directly from conditions at the Subject Property already at issue in this matter.

Second, to the extent the proposed cross-complaint names cross-defendants not already parties to this action, including Cole Moscatel and the Roe cross-defendants, their joinder is proper because the claims against them concern the same property and controversy.  Joinder would be permitted had the cross-complaint been filed as an independent action. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 428.10, subd. (b), 428.20.) Further, RTI Properties, Inc. is already a defendant in this action.

Third, the motion is filed in good faith.  Kohansameh received a notice from the City of Calabasas on May 20, 2026, regarding active code enforcement and unlawful, unpermitted construction at the Subject Property. (Kohansameh Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. 2.)  Kohansameh asserts she was unaware of the existence of unpermitted work prior to receiving the notice and filed the instant motion shortly after. (Kohansameh Decl., ¶ 4.)  The record discloses no bad faith, and section 426.50 must be liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of the related causes of action. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50; see also Silver Organizations, supra, 217 Cal.App.3d at pp. 98-99.)

Finally, the Court finds no prejudice to Plaintiff and further finds that granting leave serves the interest of justice.  No opposition has been filed, and trial is scheduled for June 21, 2027.  In fact, denial may lead to duplicative litigation, fragmented claims, and potential ongoing dangerous conditions at the property.

IV.          CONCLUSION

Defendant Lida Kohansameh’s motion for leave to file a cross-complaint against Plaintiff 9000 Arlington Ave LLC is GRANTED.
