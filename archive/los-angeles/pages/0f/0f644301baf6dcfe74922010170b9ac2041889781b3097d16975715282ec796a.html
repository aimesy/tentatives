Case Number: 24STLC08416    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 26

 

MOTION FOR JUDGMENT ON THE PLEADINGS-TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On November 18, 2024, Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) brought this action against Defendants Caruso’s Italian Kitchen dba Carusos Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy Adba Caruso's Italian Kitchen Bar & Grill (“Defendant Caruso's Italian Kitchen”); Hani Mammo aka John Caruso aka Hani Moudafar Mammo aka Hani Johnny Mammo aka Hani Mammoital aka Giovanni Caruso aka Frances Vicuna, an individual doing business as Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso's Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammo”); Mammo Enterprises LLC dba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammo Enterprises”); Mammoital Inc. #2 dba Mammoital adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso's Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammoital”).

 

On May 1, 2025, Defendants’ Demurrer to the Complaint was overruled as to the first, second, and sixth causes of action, sustained without leave to amend as to the third cause of action, and sustained with leave to amend as to the fourth cause of action. (Minute Order, 05/01/25.) On May 20, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint alleging (1) open book account; (2) account stated; (3) breach of contract; (4) breach of personal guaranty; and (5) fraudulent inducement.

 

Defendants Caruso’s Italian Kitchen, Mammo, Mammo Enterprises, and Mammoital filed an “Answer to the Complaint” on June 23, 2025 through counsel. Defense counsel then substituted out as counsel for Defendants on October 27, 2025. On December 31, 2025, the Court denied Plaintiff’s Motions to Strike Defendants’ Answer on account of them being unrepresented. (Minute Order, 12/31/25.) The Court also set an Order to Show Cause Re: Status of Legal Representation of Defendants, which it discharged on March 26, 2026. (Ibid.) Defendants filed Substitutions of Attorney on May 19, 2026.

 

Defendants Mammo Enterprises and Mammoital, Inc. filed the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings on June 22, 2026. Defendants Mammo Enterprises and Mammoital, Inc. also filed a Motion to Strike Defendant Mammoital, Inc. #2’s Answer, which is concurrently set for hearing with the instant Motion. Plaintiff filed an opposition to the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings on September 14, 2026 and Defendants Mammo Enterprises and Mammoital, Inc. replied on September 15, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendants Mammo Enterprises and Mammoital Inc. move for judgment on the pleadings with respect to the first through third causes of action of the First Amended Complaint. The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438. Subdivision (c)(1)(B) permits judgment on the pleading against the plaintiff if “the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(1)(B).)

 

As the moving Defendants point out, the standard for a motion for judgment on the pleadings is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer. That is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.  (Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.) Matters which are subject to mandatory judicial notice may be treated as part of the complaint and may be considered without notice to the parties. Matters which are subject to permissive judicial notice must be specified in the notice of motion, the supporting points and authorities, or as the court otherwise permits. (Id.)  The motion may not be supported by extrinsic evidence. (Barker v. Hull (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 221, 236.)

 

A statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, et seq. must also be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 439 [moving party must file declaration demonstrating an attempt to meet and confer in person or by telephone, at least five days before the date a motion for judgment on the pleadings is filed].) A statutory motion may be granted with or without leave to file an amended pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (h)(1).)

 

Preliminary Considerations

 

The Motion is not supported by the mandatory meet-and-confer declaration, or any kind of supporting declaration, as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 439. However, failing to meet and confer is not grounds to deny the request for judgment on the pleadings. (Code Civ. Proc., § 439, subd. (a)(4).) Defendants Mammo Enterprises and Mammoital, Inc. are admonished for failing to comply with the meet and confer requirement and warned that future non-compliance may result in the Motion being taken off calendar, or continued.

 

Moving defendants also request that the Court take judicial notice of certain documents, but then make no mention of the Request for Judicial Notice in the Motion. The Request for Judicial Notice is denied for lack of relevance. (See OneTaste Inc. v. Netflix, Inc. (2025) 116 Cal.App.5th 174, 194.)

 

The Court will consider the Motion as filed on behalf of Defendant Mammoital pending the decision on the Order to Show Cause re Defendant’ Mammoital’s corporate status, which the Court set for hearing on October 19, 2026.  As indicated in the Court’s ruling on Plaintiff’s Motion to Strike, issued contemporaneously with this Order, if Defendant Mammoital fails to demonstrate revivor, its Answer will be struck. If it does demonstrate revivor, it will cure any present deficiency.

 

Plaintiff argues that the Motion is improper because the Court previously overruled Defendants’ Demurrer to counts one and two of the Complaint.  Under Code of Civil Procedure section 438, a motion for judgment on the pleadings may be brought even if “[t]he moving party has already demurred to the complaint or answer, as the case may be, on the same grounds as is the basis for the motion provided for in this section and the demurrer has been overruled, provided that there has been a material change in applicable case law or statute since the ruling on the demurrer.” (Code of Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (g)(1).) This limitation does not apply because the instant motion does not raise the same arguments as the prior demurrer.

 

Sufficiency of the Allegations Against Defendant Mammo Enterprises

 

Moving defendants seeks to strike counts one through, which open book account, account stated, and breach of contract respectively.  All three causes of action are alleged against all Defendants. Moving defendants argue that the Complaint does not plead facts with respect to either moving defendant to state a claim, such as any allegations that either moving defendant is indebted to Plaintiff, or entered into an agreement with Plaintiff, and that alter-ego allegations are not sufficiently pled.

 

The arguments do not support striking the causes of action.  As to counts one and two, common counts must allege indebtedness running from the defendant to the plaintiff, a debtor-creditor relationship, and a sum due. (Farmers Ins. Exchange v. Zerin (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 445, 460.) The First Amended Complaint alleges these facts as to all Defendants. Specifically, it alleges that “Defendants, each of them, became indebted to Plaintiffs Assignor in the amount of ‘the DEBT’ for a balance due on a book account for goods sold and delivered and/or services rendered by Plaintiffs Assignor to Defendant(s) at Defendants' request.” (FAC, ¶19.) The debt is defined as the outstanding sum of $31,416.76. (Id. at ¶2.) These allegations are reiterated for the second cause of action. (Id. at ¶21.) Moving defendants do not cite any portion of the First Amended Complaint to demonstrate that the allegations are insufficient, let alone address the allegations cited above.

 

As to count three, for breach of contract, the Motion is brought on the basis that the subject Agreements only identify “John Caruso” as the signatory and there is no allegation that moving defendants signed or entered into the Agreements. The First Amended Complaint does allege that “Defendant(s)” entered into the First and Second Agreements. (FAC, ¶¶25, 30.) However, the First Agreement is attached to the First Amended Complaint and refers to the customer as “Caruso’s Italian Kitchen” and “Two Guys from Italy.” (Id. at Exh. 1, p. 1.) The Second Agreement also only names “Caruso’s Italian Kitchen” as the customer. (Id. at Exh. 2, p. 1.) Both Agreements are signed by John Caruso. (Id. at Exhs. 1-2.) An allegation that moving defendants entered into the Agreements, therefore, is not expressly borne out by the Agreements themselves.

 

The Court nonetheless finds the claim sufficiently pled based on the alter-ego allegations.  One customer, “Caruso’s Italian Kitchen” is named as a co-defendant. Both customers in the Agreements, “Caruso’s Italian Kitchen” and “Two Guys from Italy”, are pled as the dbas of other defendants, including Defendant Mammoital.  (FAC, ¶¶6-9.)  The signatory “John Caruso” is also identified as a dba.  (FAC, ¶7.)  The FAC alleges that all defendants were alter-ego and/or successors-in-interest of the other defendants, including alleging that all defendants “conduct the same business (ie: restaurants) with defendant HANI MAMMO” at the head, that all “share a unity of interest and ownership,” and that the “corporate defendants are merely instrumentalities or conduits of defendant HANI MAMMO in pursuit of a single business venture,” among other allegations.  (See id., ¶¶13-19.)   

 

Moving defendants challenge the sufficiency of the alter ego allegations as conclusory and argue that a series of more specific facts must be alleged. (Citing Sonora Diamond Corp. v. Superior Court (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 523, 538–539; Leek v. Cooper (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 399, 415.) But the Court agrees with Plaintiff that moving defendants ask the Court to impose too great a burden at this stage.   Sonora Diamond Corp. involved an evidentiary inquiry into alter ego and did not concern the sufficiency of pleadings. (Sonora Diamond Corp., supra, 83 Cal.App.4th at 540.) In Leek, the Court of Appeals ruled that the pleading must allege “sufficient facts” not “specific facts”, and the allegations assessed by the court were vastly dissimilar to those at issue here. (Leek, supra, 194 Cal.App.4th at 415.) As other decisions have recognized, ultimate facts are sufficient at this stage.  (Rutherford Holdings, LLC v. Plaza Del Rey (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 221, 236 [“Defendants argue that Rutherford failed to allege specific facts to support an alter ego theory, but Rutherford was required to allege only ‘ultimate rather than evidentiary facts.’”].)  These facts include commingling of funds and other assets, identical equitable ownership, failure to maintain corporate formalities, inadequate capitalization, or use of corporate assets for personal purposes. (Sonora Diamond Corp., supra, 83 Cal.App.4th at 538.)

 

The FAC satisfies the standard.  The allegation “that all defendants are alter-egos of each other as they conduct the same business (ie: restaurants) with defendant HANI MAMMO being the main or only officer, director, manager, and/or incorporator of all other named Defendants, and operate under defendant HANI MAMMO’s direct financing and control” alleges unity of interest and ownership. (FAC, ¶14.) The allegation that the corporate Defendants are “conduits of defendant HANI MAMMO in pursuit of a single business venture” alleges identical equitable ownership and use of corporate assets for personal purposes.  (Id. at ¶16.) The FAC alleges an inequitable result where it alleges that Defendant Mammo uses this unity of interest and ownership to perpetuate a fraud, i.e., to defraud Defendants’ creditors. (Id. at ¶¶13, 17.)

 

Accordingly, the Court will deny the motion for judgment on the pleadings.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is denied.

MOTION TO STRIKE - TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Strike Defendant Mammoital, Inc. #2’s Answer is denied.

 

The Court orders sets an Order to Show Cause re the Corporate Status of Defendant Mammoital, Inc. #2 for October 19, 2026. 

 

 

Analysis:

 

On November 18, 2024, Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) brought this action against the following four defendants: [1] Defendants Caruso’s Italian Kitchen dba Carusos Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy Adba Caruso's Italian Kitchen Bar & Grill (“Defendant Caruso’s”); [2] Hani Mammo aka John Caruso aka Hani Moudafar Mammo aka Hani Johnny Mammo aka Hani Mammoital aka Giovanni Caruso aka Frances Vicuna, an individual doing business as Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso’s Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammo”); [3] Mammo Enterprises LLC dba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammo Enterprises”); and [4] Mammoital Inc. #2 dba Mammoital adba Carusos Pizza adba Two Guys From Italy adba Caruso’s Italian Kitchen adba Rok N Sushi (“Defendant Mammoital”).

 

On May 1, 2025, Defendants’ Demurrer to the Complaint was overruled as to the first, second, and sixth causes of action, sustained without leave to amend as to the third cause of action, and sustained with leave to amend as to the fourth cause of action. (Minute Order, 05/01/25.) On May 20, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint alleging (1) open book account; (2) account stated; (3) breach of contract; (4) breach of personal guaranty; and (5) fraudulent inducement.

 

Defendants filed an “Answer to the Complaint” on June 23, 2025 through counsel. Defense counsel then substituted out as counsel for Defendants on October 27, 2025. On December 31, 2025, the Court denied Plaintiff’s Motions to Strike the Answer as to Defendant Caruso’s, Defendant Mammo Enterprises, and Defendant Mammoital due to lack of representation. (Minute Order, 12/31/25.) The Court set an Order to Show Cause Re: Status of Legal Representation of the Entity Defendants, which it discharged on March 26, 2026. (Ibid.) Defendants filed Substitutions of Attorney on May 19, 2026.

 

Defendants Mammo Enterprises and Mammoital filed a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, which is set to be heard concurrently with the instant Motion to Strike Answer. Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Strike Defendant Mammoital’s Answer on July 21, 2026. Defendant Mammoital filed an opposition on September 15, 2026 and Plaintiff replied on September 18, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion to Strike Answer is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 436 on the grounds that Defendant Mammoital has been a suspended corporation since 2021, and under the Revenue & Taxes Code is not entitled to participate in this action. Code of Civil Procedure section 436 allows the Court to “strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this State, a court rule, or an order of the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (b).) Under Revenue & Taxes Code sections 23301 and 23302, a corporation cannot defend a lawsuit if it is suspended for failing to pay taxes. (See Ransome-Crummey Co. v. Superior Court in and for Santa Clara County (1922) 188 Cal. 393, 397 [“During the time its taxes were unpaid, petitioner was shorn of all rights save those expressly reserved by the statutes. The right to institute or maintain actions is not included in this reservation, but is denied to corporations as a part of the penalty.”].)

Motions to strike in the Limited Civil Court may only challenge pleadings on the basis that “the damages or relief sought are not supported by the allegations of the complaint.” (Code of Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (d).) The instant Motion to Strike does not pertain to whether the damages or relief sought in Defendant Mammoital’s Answer are supported by the allegations, and therefore, cannot be raised in this Court by motion of a party. Furthermore, “the grounds for a motion to strike shall appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437, subd. (a).) Plaintiff’s Motion does not refer to any part of Defendant Mammoital’s Answer, nor makes a request for judicial notice of the fact of its purported corporate suspension. Instead, Plaintiff simply attaches what it contends is a true and correct copy of a suspension report to the Motion. (Motion, Brown Decl., ¶4 and Exh. 1.) The supporting declaration provides no facts from which the Court can determine the source or accuracy of the report. (Ibid.)

Therefore, the Motion fails to demonstrate that Defendant Mammoital’s Answer should be stricken, either from the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the Court must or may take judicial notice.  In its reply, Plaintiff argues that the law regarding suspended corporations is “self-executing,” but cites no authority for this rule. (Reply, p. 2:26-28.)

 

That said, Plaintiff is correct that “a suspended corporation may not prosecute or defend an action in a California court.”  (Timberline, Inc. v. Jaisinghani (1997) 54 Cal. App. 4th 1361, 1365.)  Defendant Mammoital admits the status, and states that it is in the process of obtaining a certificate of revivor.  Obtaining a certificate of revivor would excuse any past delinquency, with respect to the validity of filings.  (See id. at 1366 (“the revival of corporate powers by the payment of delinquent taxes has been held to validate otherwise invalid prior action.”).)  The Court may strike an improper pleading or otherwise enforce the principle at issue on its own motion, even if Plaintiff’s motion is not proper.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 436.)   “[I]f the corporation's status only comes to light during litigation, the normal practice is for the trial court to permit a short continuance to enable the suspended corporation to effect reinstatement (by paying back taxes, interest and penalties) to defend itself in court.” (Timberline, 54 Cal. App. 4th at 1367.)   Accordingly, the Court sets for hearing an Order to Show Cause re Defendant Mammoital’s corporate status and ability to represent itself on the date set below.  Defendant Mammoital must file and serve a declaration under penalty of perjury attaching any certificate of revivor no later than five days before the hearing date.  If Defendant Mammoital does not establish that its corporate status has been revived on or before that date, the Court will strike its Answer, and Plaintiff may seek entry of default.

 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion to Strike Defendant Mammoital, Inc. #2’s Answer is denied.

 

The Court orders sets an Order to Show Cause re the Corporate Status of Defendant Mammoital, Inc. #2 for October 19, 2026.

Case Number: 25NWLC31353    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendants Blackseries Campers, Inc. and Hongwei Qiu’s Motion for Relief from Entry of Default is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On June 13, 2025, Plaintiff Wang IP Law Group, PC (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Blackseries Campers, Inc. (“Defendant Blackseries Campers”) and Hongwei Qiu (“Defendant Qiu”). On December 16, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Application to Serve the Secretary of State. Proof of Service upon the Secretary of State was filed on December 31, 2025. Plaintiff filed Proof of Personal Service upon Defendant Qiu on March 4, 2026.

 

Following the lack of any responsive pleading by Defendants, the Court entered their default on April 7, 2026. Defendants filed the instant Motion for Relief from Entry of Default on August 19, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on September 10, 2026 and Defendants replied on September 21, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move to vacate the default and default judgment pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Under Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b), an application for relief must be made within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s or counsel’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) The six-month deadline is jurisdictional and not subject to tolling. (Manson, Iver & York v. Black (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 36, 42.)

 

The Motion must also be accompanied by a copy of Defendants’ proposed response to the action.

Attached to the Motion is a copy of Defendant Blackseries Campers’ proposed Answer to the Complaint, and a copy of Defendant Qiu’s proposed Demurrer to the Complaint. (Motion, Exhs. A and B.)

 

Improper Service

 

Defendants argue that the defaults were due to inadvertence, surprise, and excusable neglect because the service of summons and complaint was not property effected.

 

Defendant Blackseries Campers

 

The proof of service of the Summons and Complaint with respect to Defendant Blackseries Campers states that it was personally served by delivery of the papers to Greg Scarborough / Deputy Secretary of State Authorized to Accept. (Proof of Service, filed 12/31/25, ¶¶3-5.) The proof of service is attested to by a registered process server. (Id. at ¶7.) Therefore, it is entitled to a rebuttable presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence of the facts stated in the return. (Cal. Evid. Code, § 647; see American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)

 

Defendants’ Motion is supported by the declaration of Defendant Qiu, the CEO of Defendant Blackseries Campers, as well as its agent for service of process. (Motion, Qiu Decl., ¶1.) Defendant Qiu denies that he was ever being served with the Summons and Complaint. (Id. at ¶7.)  Defendant Qiu declares that because Defendant Blackseries Campers was being sued in other matters, on or about March 1, 2026, he searched the Los Angeles Superior Court website and discovered the instant action. (Ibid.) Defendant Qiu then arranged a meeting that month with Plaintiff’s counsel, Tommy SF Wang, during which they discussed arbitration of the attorney’s fees dispute. (Ibid.) After the meeting with Plaintiff’s counsel, he expected to receive notice of the arbitration proceedings and when he did not, he again looked at the Court website to see if the action was pending. (Id. at ¶¶8-9.) Upon seeing that it was, Defendant Qiu hired counsel. (Ibid.)  

 

The Court has no basis to find that service on Defendant Blackseries Campers was ineffective.  The Motion does not dispute that service of the Summons and Complaint upon Defendant Blackseries Campers was accomplished via the California Secretary of State, nor does the Defendant Qiu’s declaration address that service.  Under Corporations Code section 1702, “Upon the receipt of any such copy of process and the fee therefor, the Secretary of State shall give notice of the service of the process to the corporation at its principal office, by forwarding to such office, by registered mail with request for return receipt, the copy of the process or, if the records of the Secretary of State do not disclose an address for its principal office, by forwarding such copy in the same manner to the last designated agent for service of process who has not resigned.” (Corps. Code, § 1702, subd. (b).)

 

The Motion does not explain how service by the Secretary of State was connected to any mistake or inadvertence on Defendant Blackseries’ part that would excuse their default.   In reply, Defendants argue that because Plaintiff sent its process server to an address it knew was no longer good—19051 East Walnut Drive South—their lack of notice cannot be attributed to their neglect and should be attributed to Plaintiff’s improper choices. This is inaccurate. Plaintiff ultimately served Defendant Blackseries Campers through service on the Secretary of State. Its prior attempts at service at 19051 East Walnut Drive South are not relevant.

 

Similarly, Defendant also argues that the Notice of Entry of Default was sent to an address that Plaintiff “knew had been abandoned.”  (Reply, at p.4.)  That argument does not excuse Defendants’ failure to timely respond to the Complaint after service.  It may pertain to the timeliness of the motion under §473(b), but that is not contested.

 

Defendant Qui

 

The proof of service of the Summons and Complaint with respect to Defendant Qiu states that it was personally served by delivery of the papers to Defendant Qiu on March 3, 2026, at 18645 E. Gale Ave., Suite 205 City of Industry CA 91748. (Proof of Service, filed 03/04/26, ¶¶3-5.) The proof of service is not attested to by a registered process server. (Id. at ¶7.) Therefore, it is not entitled to a rebuttable presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence of the facts stated in the return. (Cal. Evid. Code, § 647; see American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)

 

As discussed above, Defendant Qiu declares that he attended a meeting with Plaintiff’s counsel on March 3, 2026, but was never served with the Summons and Complaint. This places the burden of demonstrating service upon Plaintiff.

Plaintiff’s counsel attests that Defendant Qiu was served at their meeting on March 3, 2026. (Opp., Wang Decl., ¶7.) Plaintiff also points to a WeChat message Defendant Qiu sent to Plaintiff’s counsel requesting to meet to discuss the lawsuit, as well as a message sent the day after the meeting stating that Defendant Qiu reviewed the Complaint and hoped to resolve the dispute without unnecessary litigation costs. (Id. at Exh. 4.) Plaintiff’s counsel responded right away that they would “continue to litigate this matter to its fullest extent.” (Ibid.) Plaintiff’s counsel also stated that Defendant Qiu had indicated he was represented by counsel and that defense counsel should contact Plaintiff’s counsel’s office directly. (Ibid.)

 

These messages credibly demonstrate that Defendant Qiu was served at the meeting on March 3, 2026, and was made aware of Plaintiff’s intention to continue litigating the action, regardless of any discussion regarding mandatory fee arbitration.  Defendant Qiu argues that “having invoked fee arbitration, Mr. Qiu expected to receive notice of arbitration proceedings.”  (Qui Decl., ¶9.)  Even if that was his belief, he inexplicably waited more than a month while he knew that a Complaint had been filed – from March 6 to April 16 – to even send a follow-up email, and in the meanwhile, did nothing to respond to the Complaint. This was not the conduct of a reasonably prudent person and does not justify vacating default.

 

Defendants argue for the first time in Reply, that Plaintiff obtained default “by stealth” because Plaintiff entered Defendants default after talking to Defendant Qiu in early March.  The application of this principle is less clear where the defendant is not represented.  (See Fasuyi v. Permatex, Inc., (2008) 167 Cal. App. 4th 681, 701 (referring “professional discourtesy to opposing counsel”).  Moreover, here, Plaintiff gave Defendants notice on March 4, 2026, that it intended to “continue to litigate this matter to its fullest extent.”  The Court does not find that default was taken by stealth.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendants Blackseries Campers, Inc. and Hongwei Qiu’s Motion for Relief from Entry of Default is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC05378    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Lourvey Sahana Bourdan’s Motion to Quash Subpoena for Production of Personal and Business Financial Records is granted.

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On July 14, 2025, Plaintiff Pacific Rejuvenation Medical (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Lourvey Sahana Bourdan (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered Defendant’s default on September 9, 2025.

 

On December 31, 2025, the Court granted Defendant’s first Motion to Quash Subpoena; on February 5, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s Motion to Vacate Entry of Default. (Minute Orders, 12/31/25 and 02/05/26.) The Court also granted Defendant’s second Motion to Quash Subpoena on March 9, 2026. (Minute Order, 03/09/26.) Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint on March 12, 2026; on April 16, 2026, Plaintiff added House on a Hill Entertainment LLC as a doe defendant. Following its failure to respond to the action, the Court entered its default on June 4, 2026. (Minute Order, 03/12/26.)  The Court denied Plaintiff’s Motion to Strike Defendant’s Answer on July 21, 2026. (Minute Orders, 03/12/26 and 07/21/26.)

 

Defendant filed and served the instant Motion to Quash Subpoena for Production of Personal and Business Financial Records on June 22, 2026. Defendant filed and served a supplemental declaration on June 23, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant brings the instant Motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, subdivision (a), on the basis that the subpoena issued to Wells Fargo is defective for lack of proper notice upon Defendant, seeks highly confidential information protected by the right of privacy, is premature, and is procedurally improper. Section 1987.1 allows a party, witness, consumer, or employee to move for “an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).) The Motion attaches copies of the letters sent to Defendant by Wells Fargo on June 10, 2026 and June 15, 2025, each of which refers to a “Legal Order to release Wells Fargo records” it received from Plaintiff. (Motion, Exh. A; Bourdan Decl., Exh. A.) Both letters indicate a production deadline of July 7, 2026. (Ibid.)  

 

Plaintiff contends that she did not receive notice of the subpoena as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 1985.3, which requires service “[t]o the consumer personally, or at his or her last known address, or in accordance with Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 1010) of Title 14 of Part 3, or, if he or she is a party, to his or her attorney of record.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1985.3, subd. (b)(1).) The Court finds that Plaintiff’s failure to give Defendant any notice of the subpoena as required by statute is grounds to quash it.

 

To the extent that Defendant also argues the subpoena should be quashed because it seeks highly confidential financial information to which Plaintiff has not demonstrated a compelling need, the Motion cites no authority beyond the “constitutional right to privacy.” (Motion, p. 2:11-17.) The California Constitution does broadly include privacy as an inalienable right in Article I, section 1, but does not speak to how that right is impacted by subpoenas in civil litigation. The Court, therefore, does not find this unsupported argument a basis to quash the subpoena. The Motion also argues that the subpoena is premature and procedurally improper because service of process on Defendant’s LLC is actively disputed. This argument also lacks any supporting citation to either evidence or legal authority. (Id. at p. 2:19-26.) If Defendant is referring to service upon House on a Hill Entertainment LLC, the Court has already entered its default and there is no pending challenge to that default. Finally, to the extent that Defendant argues the subpoena is overbroad and lacks relevance, it is unclear how Defendant can know what categories of information are sought without having been served with the subpoena. Nor can the Court evaluate the breadth and relevance of the subpoena without seeing a copy of it.

 

Based on the foregoing, the request to quash the subpoena issued to Wells Fargo by Plaintiff is granted for lack of proper notice to Defendant as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 1985.3.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Lourvey Sahana Bourdan’s Motion to Quash Subpoena for Production of Personal and Business Financial Records is granted.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services’ (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted.

 

Defendant KCK-Automotive Incorporated aka KCK Auto Sales Aka KCK Automotive is ordered to serve verified responses without objections to the Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Defendant KCK-Automotive Incorporated aka KCK Auto Sales Aka KCK Automotive is also ordered to pay Plaintiff’s counsel sanctions of $1,073.82 within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services (“Plaintiff”) served Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, on Defendant KCK-Automotive Incorporated aka KCK Auto Sales Aka KCK Automotive (“Defendant KCK-Automotive”) on February 23, 2026. (Motions, Aposhian Decl., Exh. 2.) Despite a meet and confer effort, Defendant KCK-Automotive did not timely serve verified responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶¶5-12 and Exhs. 3-6) Plaintiff filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, on July 30, 2026. Defendant KCK-Automotive filed oppositions on September 15, 2026 and Plaintiff filed a joint reply on September 18, 2026.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Further, the motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) In the oppositions, Defendant KCK-Automotive admits that the discovery responses were not timely served. (Opp., Sari Decl., ¶3.) It served responses after receipt of the instant Motions, on August 20, 2026. (Id. at ¶¶6-7 and Exh. A.) The oppositions cite City of Los Angeles v. Superior Court (2017) 9 Cal.App.5th 272, for the contention that the instant Motions are now moot, but the case makes no such holding. Instead, the Court of Appeals examined whether the Civil Discovery Act applies to proceedings under the California Public Records Act. (City of Los Angeles v. Superior Court (2017) 9 Cal.App.5th 272, 275.) Based on Defendant KCK-Automotive’s failure to timely respond to the propounded discovery, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling service of verified responses to the discovery requests without objections. In its reply, however, Plaintiff drops the request to compel responses and now only seeks sanctions. (Reply, p. 5:18-19.)

 

Plaintiff moves for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030. In City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, the Supreme Court observed:

 

It is already well-established that a court may not rely on [Code of Civil Procedure] section 2023.030 to override the limitations prescribed by any other applicable sanctions provision in the [Civil Discovery] Act. A court may invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 only when confronted with an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision. And where it invokes that authority, it is constrained by the long-settled rules generally governing the imposition of discovery sanctions under the Act.

 

(City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 74-75.) The moving statutes, Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300, make sanctions available when a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); § 2031.300, subd. (c).) The Court finds that Defendant KCK-Automotive’s oppositions are not warranted. Defendant KCK-Automotive contends that its failure to respond to the discovery was a calendaring error, but does not explain why it did not serve responses after the deadline to respond was extended, nor why it failed to respond to Plaintiff’s meet-and-confer letter dated May 6, 2026. (See Motion, Aposhian Decl., Exh. 4.) Instead, Defendant KCK-Automotive waited another three months to serve responses on August 20, 2026 and unjustifiably opposed the instant Motions. Therefore, Plaintiff is awarded sanctions in the reduced amount of $1,073.82, based on two hours billed at the reduced hourly rate of $500.00, plus $73.82 in costs. (Motion, Aposhian Decl., ¶13.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services’ (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted.

 

Defendant KCK-Automotive Incorporated aka KCK Auto Sales Aka KCK Automotive is ordered to serve verified responses without objections to the Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Defendant KCK-Automotive Incorporated aka KCK Auto Sales Aka KCK Automotive is also ordered to pay Plaintiff’s counsel sanctions of $1,073.82 within 20 days’ service of this order.
