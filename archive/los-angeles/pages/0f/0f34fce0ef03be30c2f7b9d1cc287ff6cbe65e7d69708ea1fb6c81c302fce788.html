Case Number: 22STCV17819    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Farer v. Greystar Holdings, Case no. 22STCV17819

Hearing date July 2, 2026

Defendant Southern California Gas Company’s Motion for Sanctions

Plaintiff bicyclist Farer sued defendants Greystar Holdings, Ruiz, GS Santa Fe Apartments, Southern California Gas Co. (“SoCal Gas”) and Boudreau Pipeline Corp. for injuries sustained when he was hit by a traffic cone struck by Ruiz’s car. Greystar and Ruiz have been dismissed. SoCal Gas moves for sanctions, asserting it is an improper defendant. Plaintiff opposes.

Per Code Civ. Proc. §128.5 a court may order a party to pay reasonable expenses, including attorneys’ fees, incurred as a result of bad faith or frivolous action or tactics, including the filing of a complaint. “Frivolous” means “totally and completely without merit or for the sole purpose of harassing” a party. Civ. Proc. Code §128.5(b). Per section 128.7, “by presenting to the court… a pleading… an attorney… is certifying that to the best of the person’s knowledge, information, and belief, formed after” a reasonable inquiry, the pleading is not being presented for an improper purpose, such as to harass or delay and allegations and other factual contentions have evidentiary support or are likely to have evidentiary support. Civ. Proc. Code §128.7(b).

SoCal Gas provided Kochem’s declaration to plaintiff in support of its request for voluntary dismissal on 11/18/24. SoCal argues the declaration “establishes that SoCal Gas had no involvement in Plaintiff’s alleged incident and was not working at or near 2771 North Garey Avenue during the relevant period.” Decl. Van Buren, para. 8. SoCal Gas argues it establishes “SoCalGas did not own, utilize, or place any traffic cones in the roadway at or near the location of the alleged incident.” Id. at para. 10.

Plaintiff opposes, arguing he has no obligation to accept SoCal Gas’ self-serving affidavit. Plaintiff argues the Kochem declaration established SoCal Gas was involved in a construction project near the location of injury, 9 months prior. See Decl. Van Buren, exh. 2. Plaintiff admits the Kochem declaration does not establish liability against SoCal Gas, but argues SoCal Gas’ involvement in the area is a basis to keep it in the case during discovery.

The complaint alleges plaintiff was injured at 2771 North Garey Avenue; plaintiff testified in deposition that he was injured on West Bonita Avenue. See Decl. Van Buren, para. 16, exh. 3. SoCal Gas argues neither the Kochem declaration nor any discovery linked SoCal Gas to the area where plaintiff was actually injured. SoCal Gas reasserts the only link between it and the area region was a gas-line installation project, 9 months earlier. Id. at exh. 2.

Plaintiff argues SoCal Gas failed to comply with safe harbor statues. Code Civ. Proc. §128.7 provides for a 21-day safe harbor period prior to filing a motion for sanctions. Plaintiff asserts the 21-day notice SoCal Gas served was defective, as it was served on incorrect email addresses and an old physical mailing address. See Decl. Pocrass, paras. 1-8. Plaintiff argues the motion must be denied on procedural grounds.

“It is well settled that the appearance of a party at the hearing of a motion and his or her opposition to the motion on its merits is a waiver of any defects or irregularities in the notice of motion.” Carlton v. Quint (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 690, 697.  Plaintiff filed opposition on 6/19/26. At hearing on 6/24/26 plaintiff’s counsel represented to the court that, notwithstanding the typo in one of the service email addresses, his opposition was based on the merits of the motion, and he did not need further time for briefing. 6/24/26 Min. Order. Any defect in service was waived.

SoCal Gas’ motion is unavailing. Sanctions under Code Civ. Proc. §§128.5 and 128.7 are intended to rectify frivolous filings which no reasonable attorney could find a basis for bringing. Ponce v. Wells Fargo Bank (2018) 21 Cal.App.5th 253, 263. Here, while tenuous, a relationship exists between SoCal Gas and the incident location, mechanism and other parties involved in the incident. To the extent SoCal Gas argues plaintiff’s claims have no merit, that is to be raised via a motion for summary judgment or judgment on the pleadings. SoCal Gas offered Kochem’s declaration, but the relation between SoCal Gas and the remaining defendants provides a basis such that naming SoCal Gas is not inherently frivolous. SoCal Gas offered no evidence that plaintiff named SoCal Gas purely to harass or for any other improper purpose. A purportedly tenuous basis is not grounds for sanctions, but for summary judgment or judgment on the pleadings. DENIED.


Case Number: 23VECV02364    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Vista Land Properties v. TI Industry Corporation, Case no. 23VECV02364

Hearing date July 2, 2026

Plaintiff Vista Land’s Motion for Leave to File First Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Vista Land Properties LLC sues defendants TI Industry Corporation, 33 Valley, LLC and others for contractual fraud arising out of a real property development agreement. Plaintiff moves for leave to file a first amended complaint, to add new parties and causes of action. Defendants TI Industry, T. and E. Itskovich filed an untimely opposition on 6/15/26; the court continued the hearing to allow for a reply. See 6/16/26 Min. Order.

Per Code Civ. Proc. §473(a)(1) "[t]he court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading." Code Civ. Proc. §576 provides that "[a]ny judge, at any time before or after commencement of trial, in the furtherance of justice, and upon such terms as may be proper, may allow the amendment of any pleading." Per California Rules of Court rule 3.1324(a), a party seeking leave to amend must include: (1) the proposed amendment or amended pleading; (2) a statement identifying the allegations in the original pleading to be deleted; and (3) a statement identifying the allegations to be added.

Plaintiff seeks leave to add: (1) new defendants, the project design professionals and funds control servicers; (2) six causes of action; and (3) new supporting allegations. See Decl. Mass, exh. 2. Plaintiff argues these new defendants, causes of action and allegations ought to have been included previously and were discovered after current counsel substituted into the case. Id. at para. 3.

Plaintiff argues this motion is timely, as counsel substituted into the case March 2026 and undertook the case review which led to the proposed amendments. Decl. Mass, paras. 2-3. Counsel consulted with forensic design professionals, which had not been done previously, leading to the attempt to name additional defendants. Id. at paras. 22-25. Plaintiff argues the new causes of action against current defendants arise from the same operative facts so do not expand the scope of discovery or prejudice current defendants. Plaintiff argues that, as no trial has been set, there is no prejudice. Id. at para. 30.

Defendants argue the proposed amendment is untimely, as facts giving rise to the proposed amendment have been in plaintiff’s possession since plaintiff completed destructive testing in 2021. See Decl. Frederick, paras. 5-11. Defendants argue new counsel substituting into the case does not constitute new facts, just a different legal strategy.

Defendants further argue addition of new defendants is time-barred. “The straightforward rule is that amendment after the statute of limitations has run will not be permitted where the result is the addition of a party who, up to the time of the proposed amendment, was neither a named nor a fictitiously designated party to the proceeding.” Ingram v. Superior Court (1979) 98 Cal.App.3d 483, 492. Defendants argue the identity and descriptions of the proposed new defendants have been known to plaintiff since 2021, so attempts to add them now is time-barred. See Thompson v. Palmer Corp. (1956) 138 Cal.App.2d 387, 390.

Plaintiff admits the proposed amendments are not based on new information, but on new counsel’s case review. See Reply, 2:10-15. Even if a good amendment is proposed in proper form, unwarranted delay may be a basis for denial. California Concrete Co. v. Beverly Hills Savings & Loan Ass’n. (1989) 215 Cal.App.3d 260, 272.

However, the burden is on defendants to identify actual prejudice, not mere allegations that further discovery will be necessary. Mesler v. Bragg Mgmt. Co. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 290, 297. Defendants fail to identify new witnesses, written discovery or destructive testing that would need to occur regarding proposed new claims against current defendants. Further, to the extent that adding new defendants is untimely and time-barred, such argument is to be raised by those new defendants, not by current defendants.

Had the proposed amendment necessitated further discovery, or if trial were approaching, prejudice might have been established such that leave to amend should be denied. Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 486–488. As no need for discovery has been shown, and trial is not set, there is no actual prejudice, and it would be an abuse of discretion to deny leave to amend. GRANTED; the court will discuss scheduling with the parties at hearing.


Case Number: 24VECV04709    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Lim v. Bien Padre Foods, Case no. 24VECV04709

Hearing date July 2, 2026

Defendants’ Demurrer to the Second Amended Complaint

Plaintiff brothers Alfonso and Albino Lim sue defendants Benito Lim and Bien Padre Foods LLC, in which plaintiffs share a minority stake. They bring four causes of action; defendants demur to the first, second and fourth causes of action; plaintiffs oppose. The court sustained defendants’ demurrer to the first amended complaint with leave to amend. 12/2/25 Min. Order.

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Defendants demur to plaintiff’s first cause of action for violation of Corp. Code §§17701.13 and 17704.10. Plaintiffs allege a right under §1704.10 to inspect and copy all records of the company required to be maintained under §17701.13. SAC para. 16. Plaintiffs allege defendants breached §§17701.13 and 17704.10 by failing to make documents available to plaintiffs. Id. at para. 17.

The court previously sustained defendants’ demurrer to this cause of action, finding plaintiffs failed to make a “reasonable request” to inspect as required by §17704.10(b)(1). 12/2/25 Min. Order. Plaintiffs allege they made such request via a 12/5/25 email to defendants’ counsel, to which defendants failed to respond. SAC para. 13, exh. A.

The SAC alleges Bien Padre “is governed by a written Operating Agreement of Bien Padre Foods, LLC, dated effective July 1, 2004 (‘Operating Agreement’).” SAC para. 7. Defendants argue the operating agreement sets forth the process by which a records request must be made. See Decl. Martorell, exh. A. Per section 18.1: “Any and all notices and demands shall be in writing, they shall be served either personally or by certified mail or by telegraph… Any notice or demand shall be given to the Members at the addresses set forth by them in this Agreement.” Id. The operating agreement states any such request must be served on the members or Bien Padre Foods, LLC at 1459 Railroad Avenue, Eureka, CA 95501. Id. Defendants argue plaintiffs’ 12/5/25 email letter to counsel is insufficient and noncompliant with the operating agreement, so is not a “reasonable request” to inspect per §17704.10(b)(1).

Plaintiffs argue §17704.10(b)(1) requires the request to be reasonable and argues service on counsel is reasonable. This is unavailing. Defense counsel is not general counsel for Bien Padre in its capacity as an LLC. See Decl. Martorell, para. 2. Nor does defense counsel have authorization to accept service for Bien Padre outside this litigation. Id.

Counsel retained for litigation is not a general agent of the client. Blanton v. Womancare, Inc. (1985) 38 Cal.3d 396, 405 (holding “[a]n attorney is not authorized, however, merely by virtue of his retention in litigation, to ‘impair the client’s substantial rights or the cause of action itself.’”) Further, §17704.10(i) allows delivery of demand by counsel but does not require acceptance. Similarly, plaintiffs’ reliance on CRC rule 1.4(a)(3)’s requirement that counsel keep clients updated on case developments is misplaced; being retained as counsel does not turn an attorney into a valid agent for service.

Service on defense counsel cannot constitute a reasonable request where defense counsel does not serve as general counsel, and where Bien Padre’s operating agreement sets forth a process for making such requests, which was not followed. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend; no further leave to amend will be granted.

Defendants demur to plaintiffs’ second cause of action for breach of fiduciary duty. The court previously sustained the demurrer to the negligence claim on the basis that the only duty owed by a manager “is limited to refraining from engaging in grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct, or a knowing violation of law.” 12/2/25 Min. Order. Under a breach of fiduciary duty claim, this standard remains the same as to duty. Corp. Code §§17704.09(c), (f)(1). “‘Gross negligence’ long has been defined in California and other jurisdictions as either a ‘want of even scant care’ or ‘an extreme departure from the ordinary standard of conduct.’” City of Santa Barbara v. Superior Court (2007) 41 Cal.4th 747, 777.

Plaintiffs allege Benito “breached his fiduciary duty to Alfonso and Albino by… violating: (i) Alfonso’s and Albino’s right to inspect the Company’s books and records as required by Operating Agreement §§10.4 and 10.5; (ii) failing to report to Alfonso and Albino regarding the Company’s business operations as required by Operating Agreement §9.2.a; and (iii) failing to make quarterly distributions.” SAC para. 22. Plaintiffs argue the duty owed by the manager of an LLC is “the same duties of loyalty and good faith as a partner owes to the partnership and its partners… thus [the manager] is obligated to act with the utmost loyalty and in the highest good faith…” Feresi v. The Livery, LLC (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 419, 425.

Plaintiffs’ arguments that dicta in Feresi, supra, expands obligations owed under §17704.09 is mistaken. Feresi cites to §17704.09 in its ruling. Section 17704.09(f) states: “In a manager-managed limited liability company, all of the following rules apply: (1) Subdivisions (a), (b), (c), and (e) apply to the manager or managers and not the members.” Section §17704.09(c) states the duty of care owed by a manager or member of an LLC is “limited to refraining from engaging in grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct, or a knowing violation of law.” To state a claim for breach of fiduciary duty, plaintiffs must allege facts showing either gross negligence or intentional misconduct.

Plaintiffs allege Benito impeded their right to inspect company records. SAC para. 22. This is unavailing; as above, plaintiffs failed to make a reasonable request pursuant to Bien Padre’s operating agreement and §17704.10.

Plaintiffs next allege Benito failed to make reports to plaintiffs as required by section 9.2.a of the operating agreement. No specific obligation exists in the operating agreement, which simply states “[t]he day-to-day activity of the LLC shall be managed by the Manager who shall be responsible for . . . accounting and reporting to Members.” Decl. Martorell, exh. A, para. 9.2.a. Further, plaintiffs do not allege when or how these alleged breaches of a duty to report occurred.

Plaintiffs finally allege Benito failed to make quarterly distributions as required by section 8.1 of the operating agreement. However, plaintiffs fail to allege the threshold fact of whether net cashflow existed such that distributions were required under section 8.1. See Decl. Martorell, exh. A, para. 8.1. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend. No further leave to amend will be granted.

Defendants demur to the fourth cause of action for breach of fiduciary duty against Benito, brought derivatively on behalf of Bien Padre. Plaintiffs allege Benito breached his duty of loyalty by “failing to account and hold as trustee any property, profit, or benefit derived by Benito in his conduct of the activities of the Company or derived from a use by Benito of Company property, and failing to use updated and functioning accounting software and ensure that all material documents and records of the Company were preserved.” SAC para. 34. Plaintiffs allege Benito acted with gross negligence by failing to use updated accounting software. SAC paras. 31, 33.

The duty of care owed by Benito to Bien Padre is the same as analyzed above. Section 17704.09 establishes a member’s duty of loyalty to an LLC, and therefore a manager’s duty of loyalty, is: “To account to the limited liability company and hold as trustee for it any property, profit, or benefit derived by the member in the conduct and winding up of the activities… to refrain… from having an interest adverse to the [LLC]… [and] to refrain from competing with the [LLC].” Corp. Code §17704.09(b).

Plaintiffs fail to offer specific factual allegations that Benito failed to hold any property, profit or benefit derived. Conclusory allegations are unavailing. Similarly, plaintiffs fail to offer allegations as to how the alleged use of “Quickbooks” accounting software (SAC para. 12) constitutes gross negligence. It is insufficient to merely label a business decision as grossly negligent; plaintiffs must offer allegations showing “‘want of even scant care’ or ‘an extreme departure from the ordinary standard of conduct.’” City of Santa Barbara, supra, at 777. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend. No further leave to amend will be granted.
