[TENTATIVE] RULING RE: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR RECLASSIFICATION

 

Dept. U

8:30 a.m.

July 21, 2026

 

I.                BACKGROUND

On Sunday, July 9, 2023, 13-year-old Plaintiff Joseph Birkner (“Birkner”) and two companions climbed a perimeter fence and trespassed onto a closed middle school campus for the purpose of skateboarding.  During their excursion, Birkner scaled a caged vending machine and used the vending machine to climb onto the school roof.  Unfortunately, while on the roof, the 180-lb. Birkner walked across a skylight, which broke under Birkner’s weight.  Birkner fell through the skylight and suffered significant injuries.

On April 2, 2024, Birkner, by and through Theresa Parham (“Parham”), his guardian ad litem, filed his original complaint against Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District (“LAUSD”).

On May 28, 2024, Plaintiff filed his first amended complaint against LAUSD, alleging a single cause of action for negligence under Government Code section 835.

On June 28, 2024, LAUSD filed its answer to the FAC.

Also on June 28, 2024, Cross-Complainant LAUSD filed its cross-complaint against Cross-Defendants Birkner and Parham, seeking/alleging: (1) total equitable indemnity; (2) partial equitable indemnity; (3) declaratory relief; (4) equitable indemnity-liability of parents and guardians; and (5) relief under Education Code section 48904 (a)(1).

On August 14, 2024, Cross-Defendants filed their answer to the cross-complaint.

On November 13, 2025, the Court granted LAUSD’s motion for summary judgment as to Plaintiff’s FAC.

On December 2, 2025, the Court entered judgment in favor of LAUSD and against Plaintiff.

On February 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed his second amended complaint against Defendants LAUSD and John Valencia d/b/a Valen Vending Company (“Valencia”), alleging: (1) negligence under Government Code section 835; and (2) negligence.

On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed his third amended complaint (the “TAC”) against Defendant Valencia, alleging a single cause of action for negligence.

On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed his motion to reclassify LAUSD’s cross-complaint from an unlimited civil case to a limited civil case.

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

“Whether an action qualifies as a limited or unlimited civil action is determined initially from the prayer or demand for relief in the plaintiff's complaint. Once classified as limited or unlimited that classification normally continues throughout the litigation. If, however, a matter has been designated as an unlimited action, and yet the amount of controversy is $25,000 or less, the statutory scheme authorizes ‘reclassification’ of the case as a ‘limited’ action and transfer of the matter to a superior court presiding over such actions.”  Ytuarte v. Superior Court (2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 266, 274, citing Code Civ. Proc., § 403.040 (a).  The jurisdictional minimum was subsequently increased to $35,000.00.  See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 85-86.

III.           DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves for an order reclassifying LAUSD’s cross-complaint from an unlimited civil case to a limited civil case.  Plaintiff asserts that LAUSD cannot recover more than $3,813.33 on the cross-complaint because LAUSD’s separate motion for summary judgment only claims $3,813.33 in property damage.  Plaintiff argues that it is a legal certainty that LAUSD will recover less than the $35,000.00 jurisdictional threshold on its cross-complaint, thus reclassification is warranted to avoid empaneling a jury to adjudicate a dispute over $3,813.33.

In addition to LAUSD’s cross-complaint, there is separately pending Plaintiff’s TAC against Valencia.  The TAC seeks general and special damages resulting from Plaintiff falling through the school roof.  The TAC characterizes Plaintiff’s injuries as “serious.”  (See, e.g., TAC, ¶ 13.)  Plaintiff presents no argument to show that his damages against Valencia as alleged in the TAC are necessarily less than the $35,000.00 jurisdictional threshold, especially when combined with the $3,813.33 in property damage claimed by LAUSD.

Code of Civil Procedure section 85 provides a mechanism for reclassification where “[t]he amount in controversy does not exceed thirty-five thousand dollars ($35,000).”  The statute continues, “As used in this section, ‘amount in controversy’ means the amount of the demand, or the recovery sought, or the value of the property, or the amount of the lien, that is in controversy in the action.”  Code Civ. Proc., § 85 (a).

The statute plainly states that the amount in controversy is determined by looking at the entire “action,” not by looking only at a specific pleading.  Other authorities reinforce that the overall scope of the litigation governs classification.  For example, when a cross-complaint filed in a limited civil case causes the entire action to exceed the limited civil jurisdictional limit, the case must be reclassified in its entirety as an unlimited civil case.  See Code Civ. Proc., § 403.030.  In other words, the statutory scheme treats the entire lawsuit as a single, unified action for purposes of jurisdictional classification.

Because the apparent value of all claims in this action likely exceeds $35,000.00, Plaintiff’s cited authorities provide no grounds for reclassification.  Plaintiff’s motion fails to address this issue.  The motion is denied.

The Court notes that Valencia has not yet filed a responsive pleading as to the TAC.  However, Plaintiff appears to be in contact with Valencia’s attorney, as he is included on the service list attached to Plaintiff’s motion.  By all appearances, Plaintiff has not abandoned his claim against Valencia.  If Plaintiff were to dismiss his claims against Valencia such that the only remaining claim to be adjudicated in this case was LAUSD’s claim for property damage in the cross-complaint, the Court might reconsider its analysis of Plaintiff’s reclassification request.  (The Court notes that Plaintiff would still need to show that his request for reconsideration is timely or that its untimeliness is excused for good cause.  See Code Civ. Proc., § 403.040.)  However, as it stands, the apparent value of all outstanding claims in this case likely exceeds $35,000.00; thus there is no mechanism for reclassification at this time.

IV.           CONCLUSION

Plaintiff Joseph Birkner’s motion for reclassification is DENIED.

 

The Clerk is ORDERED to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] RULING RE: DEFENDANT’S MOTION FOR AN ORDER COMPELLING PLAINTIFF JOSE RAUL CONTRERAS MATA’S INDEPENDENT MEDICAL EXAMINATION

 

Dept. U

8:30 a.m.

July 21, 2026

 

I.                BACKGROUND

This case arises from a motor vehicle accident that occurred on July 23, 2022.

On June 4, 2024, Plaintiffs Jose Raul Contreras Mata (“Mata”) and Alberto Cuamatzi Lugardo filed their complaint against Defendant Eliza Mae Serra, alleging: (1) motor vehicle negligence; and (2) general negligence.

On April 21, 2025, Defendant filed her answer to the complaint.

On February 12, 2026, Defendant noticed Mata’s independent medical examination (“IME”), with the IME set to take place on April 20, 2026.  Before the IME, Plaintiffs’ counsel notified defense counsel that Mata would require a Spanish interpreter.  Defense counsel arranged for a Spanish interpreter to be present, but the interpreter did not show up for Mata’s IME as scheduled.  Defense counsel attempted to secure an alternative interpreter but was unable to do so on short notice.  Thus, Mata’s IME did not go forward.  (Hsu decl., ¶¶ 3-8, Exhs. A, B.)

On April 21, 2026, counsel for both sides corresponded regarding Defendant’s intent to reschedule Mata’s IME.  Plaintiff’s counsel refused to reschedule the IME.  (Hsu decl., ¶ 9, Exh. C.)

On April 23, 2026, Defendant filed her motion to compel Mata’s IME.

As of 1:00 P.M. on July 15, 2026, Mata had filed no opposition brief.

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

“Any party may obtain discovery … by means of a physical or mental examination of (1) a party to the action, (2) an agent of any party, or (3) a natural person in the custody or under the legal control of a party, in any action in which the mental or physical condition … of that party or other person is in controversy in the action."  Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.020 (a).

III.           DISCUSSION

Defendant moves to compel Mata’s IME.  The motion is granted. 

“Any party may obtain discovery … by means of a physical or mental examination of (1) a party to the action, (2) an agent of any party, or (3) a natural person in the custody or under the legal control of a party, in any action in which the mental or physical condition … of that party or other person is in controversy in the action."  Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.020 (a).

Here, Defendant already noticed Mata’s IME, and Mata appeared on the noticed IME date.  However, apparently due to no fault of Defendant or defense counsel, a Spanish interpreter did not appear for the IME as scheduled.  As such, Defendant was unable to perform Mata’s IME.  When Defendant requested that the IME be rescheduled, Plaintiffs’ counsel refused.

The Court agrees with Defendant that if she is not allowed to schedule a second IME for Mata, her ability to defend against Plaintiffs’ claims in this case will be unduly prejudiced.  Especially given that the absence of a Spanish interpreter at the originally scheduled IME does not appear to be Defendant’s fault, Plaintiffs’ counsel will need to work with defense counsel to reschedule Mata’s IME within a reasonable timeframe.  Mata filed no opposition brief.  The motion is granted.

As to Defendants’ request that the IME occur at 1:30 p.m. on August 3, 2026, the Court declines to so specifically set the time and date.  Again, Plaintiffs’ counsel will need to work with defense counsel to reschedule Mata’s IME within a reasonable timeframe.  Given that this case is set for trial on November 16, 2026, the IME should occur no later than Friday, September 4, 2026, or on a later date mutually agreeable to both sides.

IV.           CONCLUSION

Defendant Eliza Mae Serra’s motion to compel Plaintiff Jose Raul Contreras Mata’s independent medical exam is GRANTED.  Plaintiff Jose Raul Contreras Mata is ORDERED to submit for a rescheduled independent medical exam no later than Friday, September 4, 2026.

 

Defendant Eliza Mae Serra is ORDERED to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT GRIMMWAY ENTERPRISES, INC.’S DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF’S FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Dept. U

8:30 a.m.

July 21, 2026

 

I.                BACKGROUND

This case arises from an alleged E. coli outbreak traced back to a bag of carrots sold at a Whole Foods grocery store.

            On October 23, 2025, Plaintiff Danette Pierce filed her original complaint against Defendants Grimmway Enterprises, Inc. (“Grimmway”) and Whole Foods Market California, Inc. (“Whole Foods”).

            On January 16, 2026, Cross-Complainant Whole Foods filed its cross-complaint against Cross-Defendant Grimmway.

            On January 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed her form amendment naming Defendant Mrs. Gooch’s Natural Food Markets, Inc. (“Gooch’s”) as DOE #1 in the complaint.

            On February 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed her first amended complaint (the “FAC”) against Defendants Grimmway and Whole Foods, alleging: (1) strict products liability; (2) failure to warn; (3) breach of express warranties; (4) breach of implied warranties; (5) negligent products liability; (6) violation of the Consumer Legal Remedies Act (the “CLRA”); and (7) violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200.

            On April 20, 2026, Grimmway filed its demurrer to the FAC and motion to strike portions thereof.

            On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed her form amendment correcting her name from “Danette Golden-Pierce” to “Danette Pierce.”

            On July 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed her opposition brief as to Grimmway’s demurrer and motion to strike.

            On July 7, 2026, Whole Foods dismissed its cross-complaint.

            On July 14, 2026, Grimmway filed its reply briefs as to its demurrer and motion to strike.

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

a.     Demurrer

A demurrer is a procedure to object to a pleading set forth under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, et seq.  Because a demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters, a demurrer analysis considers only those defects which appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.  SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.  When considering demurrers, courts assume the truth of the facts alleged in the pleading and read the allegations liberally and in context.  Schifando v. City of Los Angeles (2003) 31 Cal.4th 1074, 1081.

A demurrer for sufficiency attacks a pleading on the ground that it does not contain facts sufficient to state a cause of action.  Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10 (e); Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.  Parties may also demur to causes of action individually.  In either case, the procedure is also called a ‘demurrer for sufficiency,’ and it tests the pleading by itself, alone and disconnected from extraneous matters.  Hahn, at p. 747

A special demurrer for uncertainty may be brought under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10 (f) where the pleading is so poorly written that the defendant cannot reasonably respond.¿ Khoury v. Maly’s of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616 (Khoury).

b.     Motion to Strike

The Court has broad authority to strike any portion of a pleading drawn not in conformity with the law and its orders.¿ Code Civ. Proc., § 436.¿ The trial court shall strike an allegation of punitive damages on motion unless the plaintiff has pleaded ultimate supporting facts.¿ Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255 (Clauson).

III.           DISCUSSION

Grimmway demurs to Plaintiff’s sixth and seventh causes of action in the FAC on grounds of uncertainty.  Grimmway also moves to strike portions of the FAC on various grounds.

The demurrer is overruled.  The motion to strike is denied.

a.     Review of Allegations in the FAC

Plaintiff pleads that:

·       In October 2024, she bought and ate bag of organic carrots that turned out to be contaminated with E. coli. 

·       Grimmway grew and processed the carrots, while Whole Foods sold them under its “365” private-label brand. 

·       in late 2024, health agencies traced a multistate E. coli O121 outbreak to Grimmway’s organic carrots, prompting a recall in November of 2024 that covered the exact bag Plaintiff bought.  The outbreak sickened 48 people across 19 states, hospitalized 20, caused one case of hemolytic uremic syndrome, and killed one person in California.  

(FAC, ¶¶ 1-5, 12-17.)

Plaintiff further pleads:

·       Grimmway knew its carrot-growing and processing operations carried a risk of fecal contamination. 

·       Grimmway did not put adequate sanitation, water-quality, and testing safeguards in place, and kept shipping carrots after signs of contamination risk emerged without timely warning anyone.  

·       Whole Foods sold the tainted carrots under its own brand while representing them as safe and organic.  Plaintiff pleads this was done with conscious disregard for consumer safety and was authorized or ratified by company management.  (FAC, ¶¶ 19, 26-30.)

Plaintiff also pleads that:

·       She bought the carrots on October 7, 2024, refrigerated them, and started eating them on October 16, 2024, after washing and peeling them.   

·       By October 20, 2024, she had symptoms including painful urination, pelvic inflammation, chills, GI distress, and fatigue, and she became bedridden. 

·       She became too sick to travel to see her dying 95-year-old father.  

·       On November 6, 2024, after waking with severe back pain and blood in her urine, she went to a clinic and tested positive for E. coli, requiring ongoing medical treatment.  

(FAC, ¶¶ 31-36.)

b.     Plaintiff’s sixth and seventh causes of action are not uncertain as to Grimmway.

Grimmway demurs to the violation of the CLRA and violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200 causes of action based on uncertainty.  Grimmway argues that Plaintiff’s failure to specify which of the three (3) Defendants these causes of action are brought against and which specific acts by which Defendants support these causes of action creates “uncertainty and ambiguity for the Defendant as how to properly defend themselves against Plaintiff’s allegations.”  (Demurrer, p. 4:22-23.)  The Court disagrees.

The necessary elements of a CLRA cause of action are: (1) a consumer; (2) who suffers any damage; and (3) because of the use or employment by any person of a method, act, or practice declared to be unlawful by Civil Code section 1770.¿ (Civ. Code, §1780 (a); Buckland v. Threshold Ent., Ltd. (2007) 155 Cal.App.4th 798, 809, 811, overruled in other part by Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310, 326-327.)

The necessary elements for unfair¿business practices¿under Section 17200 are: (1) a business practice; (2) that is unfair, unlawful, or fraudulent; and (3) an authorized remedy.¿ (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17200;¿Paulus¿v. Bob Lynch Ford, Inc.¿(2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 659, 676.)

Plaintiff sufficiently specifies which acts and omissions by Grimmway contributed to her CLRA and unfair business practices claims.  Specifically, Plaintiff alleges Grimmway grew and processed the tainted carrots; knew its carrot-growing and processing operations carried a risk of fecal contamination; did not put adequate sanitation, water-quality, and testing safeguards in place; and kept shipping carrots after signs of contamination risk emerged without timely warning anyone.  Given these specific allegations against Grimmway, the Court disagrees that the complaint is impermissibly vague as against Grimmway.  Plaintiff’s allegations are sufficient for Grimmway to know what claims need to be admitted or denied.  This is sufficient to defeat a demurrer for uncertainty.  See Khoury, supra, 14 Cal.App.4th at p. 616.

As to Grimmway’s position that the FAC impermissibly fails to specify which defendants are included as to Plaintiff’s sixth and seventh causes of action in the headings, the Court also disagrees.  Plaintiff specifies that both causes of action are alleged against “all defendants.”  (See FAC, pp. 15:21, 17:17.)  There is nothing impermissible about alleging a single cause of action against multiple defendants.  Given that Plaintiff’s specific factual allegations set forth why she believes Grimmway is liable for her injuries, Plaintiff’s notation of “all defendants” as to her sixth and seventh causes of action is sufficient.

The demurrer is overruled.

c.      Plaintiff's claims for damages and alter ego allegations are not improperly alleged for purposes of a preliminary motion to strike.

Grimmway moves to strike Plaintiff’s allegations related to seeking punitive damages, prejudgment interest, attorneys’ fees, and treble damages.  Grimmway also moves to strike Plaintiff’s allegations related to alter ego liability.

The Court has broad authority to strike any portion of a pleading drawn not in conformity with the law and its orders.¿ Code Civ. Proc., § 436.  The trial court shall strike an allegation of punitive damages on motion unless the plaintiff has pleaded ultimate supporting facts.¿ Clauson 67 Cal.App.4th at 1255.

As to Plaintiff’s allegations related to seeking punitive damages, “In an action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract, where it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice, the plaintiff, in addition to the actual damages, may recover damages for the sake of example and by way of punishing the defendant.”  Code Civ. Proc., § 3294 (a).

Plaintiff alleges Grimmway knew its carrot-growing and processing operations carried a risk of fecal contamination.  Plaintiff alleges Grimmway did not put adequate sanitation, water-quality, and testing safeguards in place, and kept shipping carrots after signs of contamination risk emerged without timely warning anyone.  Plaintiff alleges Grimmway’s conduct caused an E. coli outbreak and resulted in serious injuries to dozens of people, including at least one death.  For purposes of a preliminary motion to strike, Plaintiff’s allegations are sufficiently particularized to show reckless, intentional, and oppressive conduct.

As to Plaintiff’s claim for attorneys’ fees, attorneys’ fees are recoverable under the CLRA.  See Civ. Code, § 1780.  Given that the Court is not invalidating Plaintiff’s CLRA claim as against Grimmway, Plaintiff’s claim for attorneys’ fees is not improper.

As to Plaintiff’s claim for prejudgment interest, Civil Code section 3288 authorizes the trier of fact, in its discretion, to award prejudgment interest “[i]n action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract, and in every case of oppression, fraud, or malice.”  Because Plaintiff’s claims sound in tort and because Plaintiff alleges oppressive conduct, section 3288 supports Plaintiff’s request for prejudgment interest for purposes of pleading.

As to Plaintiff’s claim for treble damages, Civil Code section 3345 authorizes treble damages in an action brought by or on behalf of a senior citizen to redress unfair or deceptive acts or practices.  For purposes of that section, a “senior citizen” is a person 65 years of age or older.  See Civ. Code, § 1761 (f).  Here, Plaintiff is a senior citizen within the meaning of the statute.  Plaintiff alleges she was born in 1958 and was 65 years of age or older at times relevant to this action.  To support a claim for treble damages under Civil Code section 3345, this is sufficient for purposes of pleading.

As to Plaintiff’s alter ego claims, the Court disagrees with Grimmway that Plaintiff’s alter ego allegations are improperly alleged in this multi-defendant action.  Nor are such allegations irrelevant to Plaintiff’s claims.  While Grimmway may ultimately be correct that Plaintiff’s alter ego allegations fail as against Grimmway/Whole Foods/Gooch’s, such facts are outside the four corners of the complaint and are thus not the proper subject of a preliminary motion to strike.

The motion to strike is denied.

IV.           CONCLUSION

Defendant Grimmway Enterprises, Inc.’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

Defendant Grimmway Enterprises, Inc.’s motion to strike portions of Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint is DENIED.

 

Defendant Grimmway Enterprises, Inc. is ORDERED to file its Answer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint within ten (10) days.  Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1320(j). 

 

Plaintiff is ORDERED to give notice.
