Motion To Quash Service of Summons and Complaint

Discussion

 

            The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

 

Specially appearing Defendant Joseph Barr moves to quash service of summons and complaint due to lack of personal jurisdiction.

 

            On a motion to quash for lack of personal jurisdiction, the plaintiff has the initial burden of proof:

“When a defendant moves to quash service of process” for lack of specific jurisdiction, “the plaintiff has the initial burden of demonstrating facts justifying the exercise of jurisdiction.” ( Vons, supra, 14 Cal.4th at p. 449.) “If the plaintiff meets this initial burden, then the defendant has the burden of demonstrating ‘that the exercise of jurisdiction would be unreasonable.’ “ ( Pavlovich, supra, 29 Cal.4th at p. 273, quoting Vons, at p. 449.)


(Snowney v. Harrah’s Entertainment, Inc. (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1054, 1062 [bold emphasis added].)

 

General Jurisdiction

 

            Engaging in a substantial, continuous or systematic course of business in forum states is insufficient to confer the exercise of general jurisdiction. The defendant’s presence in the forum state must render it the equivalent of the defendant’s “home state.” The U.S. Supreme Court case of Daimler AG v. Bauman (2014) 571 U.S. 117 prohibits the exercise of general jurisdiction over Defendant because California is not the equivalent of its “home state,” i.e., its principal place of business. (Id. at 122, 127.)

 

            Here, Plaintiff does not argue that a basis for general jurisdiction exists as to moving Defendant.

 

            As such, there is no basis for a finding of general jurisdiction.

 

Specific Jurisdiction

 

            “Specific jurisdiction may be asserted where the defendant has purposefully availed himself of forum benefits and the controversy is related to or arises out of the defendant’s contacts with the forum. (Citation omitted.) Sufficient minimum contacts for specific jurisdiction exist where a nonresident “ ‘deliberately’ has engaged in significant activities within a [s]tate [citation] or has created ‘continuing obligations’ between himself and residents of the forum. [Citation.]” (Citation omitted.)” (Hall v. Laronde (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 1342, 1346.)  Moreover, the exercise of specific jurisdiction must be fair and reasonable. (Anglo Irish Bank Corp., PLC v. Superior Court (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 969, 980.)

 

“When determining whether specific jurisdiction exists, courts consider the ‘ “relationship among the defendant, the forum, and the litigation.” ‘ (Citations omitted.) A court may exercise specific jurisdiction over a nonresident defendant only if: (1) ‘the defendant has purposefully availed himself or herself of forum benefits’ (citation omitted); (2) ‘the “controversy is related to or ‘arises out of’ [the] defendant’s contacts with the forum” ‘ (citation omitted); and (3) ‘ “the assertion of personal jurisdiction would comport with ‘fair play and substantial justice’ “ ‘ (Citations omitted.)

(Snowney v. Harrah’s Entertainment, Inc. (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1054, 1062.)

 

“In judging minimum contacts, a court properly focuses on ‘the relationship among the defendant, the forum, and the litigation.’ [Citations.]” (Citation omitted.) “Each defendant’s contacts with the forum State must be assessed individually.” (Id. at p. 790.)

 

(HealthMarkets, Inc. v. Superior Court (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 1160, 1167.)

 

[S]pecific jurisdiction “requires a showing not only that the defendant has ‘“purposefully directed”‘ its activities at the forum but also that ‘the litigation results from alleged injuries that “arise out of or relate to” those activities.’ [Citation.] There must be ‘a connection between the forum and the specific claims at issue.’ [Citation.] ‘If the operative facts of the allegations of the complaint do not relate to the [nonresident’s] contacts in this state, then the cause of action does not arise from that contact such that California courts may exercise specific jurisdiction.’” (Citation omitted.) “[T]he relatedness requirement is satisfied if ‘there is a substantial nexus or connection between the defendant’s forum activities and the plaintiff’s claim.’” (Citations omitted.)

 

(SK Trading Internat. Co., Ltd. v. Superior Court (2022) 77 Cal.App.5th 378, 389.)

 

            Here, specially appearing Defendant Joseph Barr admits in his Declaration that: (1) he is employed as a consulting licensed Professional Geologist and licensed Professional Engineer at Bay City Geology, Inc. (Barr Decl., ¶ 1); (2) In February/March 2019, Bay City entered into a written agreement with Jacob Emtestam to perform a geological and soils engineering investigation of the subsurface earth materials at 3338 Bonnie Hill Drive, Los Angeles, California. (Barr Decl., ¶ 7); (3) Bay City’s construction-phase role was limited to verifying that the retaining wall foundation/friction piles were excavated into bedrock consistent with the depths indicated on the structural engineering plans and Bay City’s geologic/soils report, and to verifying that retaining wall backfill compaction exceeded 90% of the laboratory-determined maximum density. (Barr Decl., ¶ 8.); (4) Barr’s work was limited to Bay City’s geotechnical scope, performed from North Carolina. (Barr Decl., ¶ 8.)

 

            By Barr’s own admission, he deliberately engaged in performing geotechnical work related to a parcel of real property located in California. The fact that Barr performed such work as an employee of Bay City pursuant to a contract Bay City entered into does not shield Barr from the exercise of personal jurisdiction over him:

 

In Calder v. Jones, supra, 465 U.S. 783, the United States Supreme Court affirmed the exercise of jurisdiction by a California court over the president/editor of the nonresident National Enquirer Corporation and the reporter/author of one of its articles, in a libel action. ( Id., at pp. 785-786 [79 L.Ed.2d at pp. 809-810].) The Calder defendants contended they had no responsibility for circulation of the article in California, and that as an author and an editor, they had no direct economic stake in their employer’s sales and no control over its marketing activity.

 

Calder regarded both the president/editor and the author as “employees,” and stated: “[Defendants] are correct that their contacts with California are not to be judged according to their employer’s activities there. On the other hand, their status as employees does not somehow insulate them from jurisdiction. Each defendant’s contacts with the forum State must be assessed individually. [Citation.]” (Citation omitted.) The defendants characterized their situation as that of a hypothetical welder employed outside California who works on a boiler which later explodes in California. They argued that the welder should not be liable because he has no control over and derives no benefit from his employer’s sales. The Supreme Court rejected the argument, reminding defendants they were not charged with “mere untargeted negligence,” but with intentional and allegedly tortious activity expressly aimed at California. Moreover, they “knew that the brunt of that injury would be felt by respondent in [California]” where the plaintiff lived and worked. (Citation omitted.) Thus, the court concluded the defendants were “primary participants in an alleged wrongdoing intentionally directed at a California resident, and jurisdiction over them is proper on that basis.” (Citation omitted.)

 

In Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc. (1984) 465 U.S. 770 [79 L.Ed.2d 790, 104 S.Ct. 1473], decided the same day, the Supreme Court was even more emphatic in its rejection of automatic jurisdictional immunity for corporate agents. “In addition to Hustler Magazine, Inc., Larry Flint [sic], the publisher, editor, and owner of the magazine, and L.F.P., Inc., Hustler’s holding company, were named as defendants in  ]  the District Court. It does not, of course, follow from the fact that jurisdiction may be asserted over Hustler Magazine, Inc., that jurisdiction may also be asserted over either of the other defendants. In Calder v. Jones, . . ., we today reject the suggestion that employees who act in their official capacity are somehow shielded from suit in their individual capacity. But jurisdiction over an employee does not automatically follow from jurisdiction over the corporation which employs him . . . . [Citations.] Each defendant’s contacts with the forum State must be assessed individually. [Citation.]” ( Id., at p. 782, fn. 13 [79 L.Ed.2d at p. 802].) Since the lower court found that jurisdiction could not be obtained over Hustler Magazine, Inc., it did not address the propriety of jurisdiction over the other defendants.

 

. . .

 

Whatever the purpose and function of the fiduciary shield doctrine, California’s long-arm statute ( Code Civ. Proc., § 410.10) expresses no intent to barricade transient tortfeasors from liability for their fraudulent conduct, and jurisdiction must be determined on the individual facts. Appellant’s complaint and declaration allege that Messinger and Carow perpetrated a fraud aimed at her in California. It is conceded that at some point they each made personal contact with her and allegedly knowingly and intentionally misrepresented facts that induced her to execute agreements and transfer securities in California. Moreover, they knew that “the brunt of that injury” would be felt by appellant “in the State in which she lives and works. . . .” (Citation omitted.) We have already determined that aside from their status as corporate fiduciaries, minimum contacts have been established. We think that given such facts, the analysis by the United States Supreme Court in Calder is appropriate here where the defendants were not mere employees, but, at least in the case of Messinger and Carow were the primary participants in negotiating and executing the buy/sell and employment agreements with appellant.

 

The essence of Calder is that intentional tortfeasors should be prepared to defend themselves in any jurisdiction where they direct their alleged tortious activity. . . .

 

(Taylor-Rush v. Multitech Corp. (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 103, 116-118 [bold emphasis added].)

           

The 1AC alleges:

 

38. The Encroachment is substandard, structurally unstable, and does not comply with applicable law including building codes.

. .

42. The Encroachment is degrading and failing, and, as a result, poses a significant risk to the Subject Property which is downhill therefrom.

 

(1AC, ¶¶ 38, 42.)

 

            In this regard, Plaintiffs’ claim arises out of Defendant Barr’s contact with California. The Court finds that Defendant Barr has sufficient minimum contacts with California by virtue of the work he performed relating to a parcel of land in California, such that he should have anticipated being sued in California for claims relating to that specific work.

 

            Defendant Barr’s argument that Plaintiff’s injury was caused by the work of others which Barr did not control goes to the merits of his defense. It is possible that such argument may support a dispositive motion in the future, but for purposes of personal jurisdiction, the evidence indicates that he purposely availed himself of forum benefits.

 

The Court now considers whether the exercise of jurisdiction over Barr would be fair and reasonable:

 

In determining whether the exercise of jurisdiction would be fair and reasonable, so as to satisfy the third requirement for the exercise of specific personal jurisdiction, a court must consider (1) the burden on the defendant of defending an action in the forum, (2) the forum state’s interest in adjudicating the dispute, (3) the plaintiff’s interest in obtaining relief, (4) “ ‘the interstate [or international] judicial system’s interest in obtaining the most efficient resolution of controversies,’ “ and (5) the states’ or nations’ shared interest “ ‘in furthering fundamental substantive social policies.’ “ (Asahi, supra, 480 U.S. at p. 113; see id. at p. 115.) “These considerations sometimes serve to establish the reasonableness of jurisdiction upon a lesser showing of minimum contacts than would otherwise be required. [Citations.] On the other hand, where a defendant who purposefully has directed his activities at forum residents seeks to defeat jurisdiction, he must present a compelling case that the presence of some other considerations would render jurisdiction unreasonable.” n6 (Burger King, supra, 471 U.S. at p. 477.)

 

(Anglo Irish Bank Corp., PLC v. Superior Court (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 969, 979-80 [bold emphasis added].)

 

1.   Re: The burden on the defendant of defending an action in the forum.

 

Barr would not have to personally appear at hearings, and the Court notes that Wolfe & Wyman LLP represents both specially appearing Defendant Barr and Defendant/Cross-Defendant Bay City Technology, Inc.  As such, Barr’s counsel is likely able to make appearances on his behalf without substantial additional cost.  Moreover, to the extent that his involvement in the matters in suit is a result of acts in the course and scope of his employment, Barr may be entitled to indemnification for his legal expenses pursuant to Labor Code section 2802.

 

As for discovery, it appears Barr will need to respond in some manner, as he is presumably a main witness (or the main witness) on behalf of Bay City. Further, Barr’s deposition would have to occur in the state of residence whether he is a party to this action or not, as this Court does not have the authority to compel an out-of-state resident to appear for a deposition in California. (Toyota Motor Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 197 Cal.App.4th 1107, 1113-14.)

 

The Court finds there is no substantial additional burden upon Barr by virtue of being required to participate in his action as a named party, as opposed to him being a witness on behalf of his employer.

 

      This factor favors the reasonableness of exercise of personal jurisdiction over Barr.

 

2.   Re: the forum state’s interest in adjudicating the dispute.

 

California has a substantial interest in adjudicating disputes concerning its resident’s contracts pertaining to real estate located in California.

 

      This factor favors the reasonableness of exercise of personal jurisdiction over Barr.

 

3.   Re: the plaintiff’s interest in obtaining relief.

 

      Plaintiffs have an interest in obtaining relief regarding a dispute pertaining to real estate owned in California.

 

      This factor favors the reasonableness of exercise of personal jurisdiction over Barr.

 

4.   Re: “ ‘the interstate [or international] judicial system’s interest in obtaining the most efficient resolution of controversies,’ “

 

            Barr lives in North Carolina. (Barr Decl., ¶ 4.) North Carolina does not appear to have an interest in efficient resolution of controversies regarding services pertaining to real estate improvements in California.

 

      This factor favors the reasonableness of exercise of personal jurisdiction over Barr.

 

5.   Re: the states’ or nations’ shared interest “ ‘in furthering fundamental substantive social policies.’ “

 

            The states’ shared interest in furthering fundamental substantive social policies is served by allowing a California court to adjudicate a dispute regarding services pertaining to real estate improvements in California.

 

      This factor favors the reasonableness of exercise of personal jurisdiction over Barr.

 

      Because all factors favor the reasonableness of exercise of personal jurisdiction over Barr, and because the Court has found that Defendant Barr has sufficient minimum contacts with California, the Court may properly exercise personal jurisdiction over Barr.

 

      The motion to quash is DENIED. Defendant is to respond to the First Amended Complaint within 30 days.

TENTATIVE RULING

Cross-Defendant Martin Monica doing business as Marty Monica Custom Tiles’s motion for sanctions pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 128.5 and 128.7 against plaintiff, William Horgan Construction; Paul W. Smigliani, Esq. and his law firm, Intelink Law Group, PC; and Shanna Burkholder, Esq. and her firm, Collins & Collins is DENIED on procedural grounds. 

ANALYSIS

Motion For Sanctions 

Cross-Defendant Martin Monica moves for sanctions pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 128.5 and 128.7 against plaintiff, William Horgan Construction; Paul W. Smigliani, Esq. and his law firm, Intelink Law Group, PC; and Shanna Burkholder, Esq. and her firm, Collins & Collins. The stated grounds for this motion are that the plaintiff and his counsel have violated Civil Code Section 128.5 and 128.7 by doing the following:

 

a. Knowingly filing a cross-complaint against the cross-defendant, MONICA, that was totally without any merit for the purpose of financial gain;

 

b. Continuing to pursue a cross-complaint against the cross-defendant, MONICA for the purpose of financial gain, despite having facts proving that the cross-complaint against MONICA was totally without any merit,

 

c. Knowingly and intentionally serving verified responses to discovery[1] served by cross-defendant, MONICA, containing untrue and perjured responses for the purpose of keeping MONICA in the case in order to reap unmerited financial gain;

 

d. Refusing to dismiss the cross-defendant, MONICA, despite receiving written notice from MONICA’s counsel of her intention to file a Motion for Summary Judgment unless MONICA was dismissed, for the purpose of keeping MONICA in the case in order to reap unmerited financial gain;

 

e. After the tentative ruling on MONICA’s Motion for Summary Judgment was published by the Court and prior to the hearing on the motion, attempting to dismiss the case in a tactical maneuver intended to prevent MONICA from being the prevailing party in the action in order to keep MONICA from recovering his court costs;

The Second District held in a recently-published decision:

Section 128.7, subdivision (c)(1) provides the “[n]otice of motion shall be served as provided in [s]ection 1010[2] … .” (Italics added.) To comply with section 1010, a notice of motion “must state when … it will be made … .” (§ 1010, italics added.) Reading these two provisions together, a notice of motion seeking section 128.7 sanctions must reflect when the motion will be heard. (Galleria Plus, Inc. v. Hanmi Bank (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 535, 537–538 [101 Cal.Rptr.3d 803] (Galleria Plus).) “Section 128.7’s incorporation of section 1010 is compulsory, not permissive.” (Id., at p. 538.) If the notice of motion fails to specify when the motion will be made, the document is “render[ed] … fatally defective. [Citations.]” (Ibid. [*7] ) Here, the spaces in the October 25, 2024 notice of motion designated for the date and time it was to be heard were left blank, thereby triggering the fatal flaw under sections 128.7 and 1010 as well as Galleria Plus.

. . . [*8]  . . .

The apparent flaw in the CRS did not relieve defendant of his obligation under sections 1010 and 128.7 to serve a notice of motion that reflects when the motion will be made. Indeed, if we were to find Galleria Plus inapplicable simply because it was decided before the era of electronic filing, we would be required to ignore the unambiguous statutory dictates of sections 128.7 and 1010—something we cannot do. (Switzer v. Wood (2019) 35 Cal.App.5th 116, 130 [247 Cal.Rptr.3d 114] [courts “‘must follow’” unambiguous statutory language]; Perez v. Torres (2012) 206 Cal.App.4th 418, 423 [141 Cal.Rptr.3d 758] [if statutory language is clear, an appellate court “must follow the plain meaning”].) Although defendant could not secure a hearing date from the CRS that was after the expiration of the safe harbor period, he could have filed an ex parte motion to request the trial court waive the timing restriction applicable to e-filed motions and allow defendant to set a hearing more than 21 days after service of the notice of motion.

 

(J.N. v. Goldberg (2026) 120 Cal.App.5th 544, 549-50 [bold emphasis and underlining added].)

Since J.N. is a reported decision, this Court is required to follow it.  (Auto Equity Sales, Inc. v. Superior Court (1962) 57 Cal.2d 450.)  Cross-Defendant Martin Monica has not demonstrated that a copy of the proposed § 128.5 and §128.7 motion was served upon Plaintiff 21-days prior to the September 22, 2026 hearing date with the September 22, 2026 hearing date included on the proposed motion served upon Plaintiff.

            As such, the motion for sanctions pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. §§ 128.5 and 128.7 is DENIED on procedural grounds.


[1] Code Civ. Proc. § 128.5 and 128.7 do not apply to discovery responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5(e), § 128.7(g).

[2] Code Civ. Proc. § 128.5(f)(1)(b) contains the same language regarding § 1010.
