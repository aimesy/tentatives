DEPARTMENT L LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23NWVC01111    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: L

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC’s Demurrer is taken OFF-CALENDAR.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an unlawful detainer case (“UD Action”) regarding real property located at 10700-10712 Sessler Street, South Gate, CA 90280 (“Subject Property”). On April 12, 2023, Plaintiff Medline Management Corporation (“Medline”) filed this action against Defendants Dan Kloss and Ioana Kloss, doing business as CNC Aero Machining.

 

On April 9, 2024, this case was consolidated with a related case titled Dan Kloss vs Jamal Dawood, et al., LASC Case No. 23NWCV02306 (“Title Action”). (4/9/24 Minute Order.). The Title Action was filed on April 12, 2023. A First Amended Complaint (FAC) was filed in that action on April 18, 2024. The FAC alleges that, in November 2021, Defendants Jamal Dawood, Christine Armani-Dawood, Quantum Properties LLC, and Does 1 to 100 fraudulently induced Kloss to grant his one-half interest in the Subject Property via a Grant Deed to Quantum Properties LLC (“Grant Deed”) in order to “safeguard” his interest. (FAC, ¶ 16.) In return, Defendants issued an LLC membership certification transferring a one-half interest in Quantum Properties LLC to Precision Forging Dies, Inc. However, the membership interest was intended to be transferred to Kloss, not Precision Forging Dies, Inc. Through the action, Kloss seeks “to cancel and rescind the fraudulently obtained Grant Deed or alternative, to involuntarily dissolve the LLC and have Plaintiff’s one-half interest in the Property transferred back to Plaintiff.” (FAC, ¶ 28.)

 

The Title Action FAC alleges the following causes of action:

 

1.    Cancellation Of Written Instrument (Civ. Code § 3412);

2.    Fraud;

3.    Negligent Misrepresentation;

4.    Breach of Oral Contract;

5.    Financial Elder Abuse;

6.    Slander of Title;

7.    Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress;

8.    Unfair Business Practices;

9.    Declaratory Relief; and

10.        Involuntary Dissolution.

 

Defendants Commercial Funding, LLC, Colony Capital Management LLC, Colony Capital LLC, and Shore Pine LLC were later named as Does 1 to 4, respectively.

 

On May 23, 2024, Defendant/Cross-Complainant Quantum Properties LLC (“Quantum”) filed a Cross-Complaint in the Title Action against Cross-Defendants Dan Kloss and Ioana Kloss, doing business as CNC Aero Machining, Anca Tiplea, and Roes 1 to 20.

 

On February 25, 2025, Quantum filed the First Amended Cross-Complaint (FACC), adding C&C Aerol Machining and Aerospace Machining Company, Precision Forging Dies, Inc. as Cross-Defendants. On May 29, 2025, Quantum filed the Second Amended Cross-Complaint.

 

On August 1, 2025, Defendant Commercial Funding, LLC filed a Cross-Complaint in the Title Action.

 

Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC (hereinafter, “CCM”) now demurs to the Title Action FAC. Defendants Colony Capital LLC (hereinafter, “CCL”) and Shore Pine LLC (hereinafter, “Shore Pine”) have filed a “Notice of Joinder in the Demurrer.”

 

The Court notes that, on August 3, 2026, CCM and CCL were dismissed from the FAC without prejudice.

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

The Court rules on CCM’s Requests for Judicial Notice as follows:

 

-      Omnibus Declaration of Dan Kloss in Support of Debtor’ Emergency Motion filed in the United States Bankruptcy Court on April 7, 2023 in the matter of In re Precision Forging Dies, Inc. (Case No. 2:23-bk-12015) (Exhibit 1) is denied.

-      Notice of Default recorded on 7/29/21 in the Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office, and Notice of Trustee Sale recorded on 11/3/21 at the Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office (Exhibit 2) is granted.

-      Grant Deed executed by Joanna Kloss and recorded on 11/29/21 in Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office (Exhibit 3) is granted.

-      Grant Deed executed by Dan Kloss and recoded on 12/29/21 in Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office (Exhibit 4) is granted.

-      Articles of Organization for Colony Capital Management, LLC (Exhibit 5) is denied.

 

The Court only takes notice of the existence of the documents and their filing/recording dates for purposes of notice, not for the truth of the matters asserted therein.  

 

Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that the parties meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or by video conference.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

On May 7, 2026, Counsel for CCM, CCL, and Shore Pine (and all Defendants) met and conferred via telephone with Kloss’ counsel but the parties were unable to come to a resolution. (Bryner Decl., ¶ 2.)

 

The Court finds that the meet and confer requirement has been met.

 

Discussion

 

CCM’s Demurrer primarily argues that CCM was not involved in the transactions between Dan Kloss, Quantum, and the Dawoods. Specifically, CCM was organized as a Wyoming LLC on July 13, 2023, approximately 1.5 years after the November 2021 subject transaction in which Kloss signed the Grant Deed. (See RJN, Exh. 5.) CCM argues that the FAC’s theories of liability based on agency, membership, alter ego, aiding/abetting allegations are inapplicable where CCM was formed after the subject transaction. Because all causes of action depend on upon the subject transaction, the entire FAC fails to state a claim against CCM.

 

CCM further argues that the ninth cause of action for declaratory relief, which requests a judicial determination regarding the membership certificates of Quantum Properties, and the parties’ rights and duties therein. Precision Forging Dies, LLC is one alleged member of Quantum. While the FAC seeks a determination of Quantum’s members, the FAC fails add Precision Forging Dies, LLC as a Defendant to this action. Precision Forging Dies, LLC should be added as in indispensable party to the FAC.

 

As previously noted, CCL and Shore Pine filed a Notice of Joinder to CCM’s Demurrer. On August 3, 2026, CCM and CCL were dismissed from the FAC without prejudice.

 

The issue is thus whether the Demurrer survives based on Shore Pine’s Notice of Joinder.

 

The Court acknowledges that, while there is no specific Code of Civil Procedure provision governing joinder for motions in general, a party can generally join another party’s motion by filing a Notice of Joinder. However, to be effective, the joinder must 1) be timely filed and served, 2) establish the necessary factual foundation to support the motion, and 3) request affirmative relief on behalf of the joining party. (See, e.g., Barak v. The Quisenberry Law Firm (2006) 135 Cal.App.4th 654, 661 [finding trial court did not abuse its discretion to entertain a joinder to an anti-SLAPP motion to strike, where the joinder requested affirmative relief and stated how the underlying motion would also be applicable to the joining party].)

 

Here, Shore Pines’ Notice of Joinder only states the following: “PLEASE TAKE NOTICE that Defendants Shore Pine, LLC and Colony Capital, LLC hereby join in Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC’s Demurrer to the Complaint and all of the causes of action therein, to wit: [all causes of action].” (See Notice of Joinder.) Thus, the Notice of Joinder fails to request any affirmative relief as to Shore Pines specifically. Further, it is not apparent from either the Demurrer or the Notice of Joinder how the Demurrer applies to Shores Pines. As noted by Plaintiff, the Demurrer’s “central premise was unique to [CCM]: it allegedly did not exist when the 2021 conveyance occurred and supposedly had no interest or stake in [Quantum]. Neither premise disposes of the claims against Shore Pine.” (Opp., 2:4-6.) Further, the bare-bones Notice of Joinder “supplies no Shore Pine-specific facts, formation record, operating agreement, membership record, or argument.” (Opp., 4:5-6.) The Court agrees.

 

Based on this procedural issue, the Court does not reach the merits of the Demurrer to the extent that the opposition and reply anticipate arguments that would have been made by Shore Pines in a proper joint Demurrer.

 

Accordingly, Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC’s Demurrer is taken off-calendar. 

Case Number: 24NWCV00192    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: L

#9 

TENTATIVE ORDER 

 

Plaintiff Gregory Gorman’s Motion to Compel Deposition of First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable and Request for Production of Documents is GRANTED. 

 

Plaintiff to give notice. 

 

Background 

 

This is a negligence action. On January 18, 2024, Plaintiff Gregory Gorman (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants First Transit, Inc. (“First Transit”), Jane Doe 1, and Does 1 to 10. 

 

On April 28, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC), naming Loreen Joseph as Jane Doe 1 and naming Transdev Group, S.A., Transdev North America, Inc., and Transdev Services, Inc. as Does 2, 3, and 4. 

 

On January 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Second Amended Complaint. 

 

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Third Amended Complaint (TAC). The TAC alleges that Plaintiff boarded a bus owned and operated by Defendant First Transit. The bus driver, Loreen Joseph, allegedly accelerated the bus before Plaintiff could sit down, causing Plaintiff to fall to the floor. The TAC alleges the following causes of action: 1) Negligence, 2) Negligence Training and Supervision, and 3) Common Carrier Liability. 

 

In November 2025, Plaintiff served its Notice of Deposition of Defendant First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable (“PMK”) and Request for Production of Documents, setting the deposition for January 27, 2026. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 27, Exh. 19; Smith Decl., ¶ 1, Exh. A.) On January 13, 2026, Defendants First Transit, Inc., Loreen Joseph, Transdev Services, Inc., and Transdev North America, Inc. served their objections. (Smith Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. B.) To date, First Transit has failed to produce its PMK for deposition. (Ibid.) 

 

Plaintiff now moves to compel the deposition of First Transit’s PMK with request for production of documents. Defendants First Transit, Inc., Loreen Joseph, Transdev Services, Inc., and Transdev North America, Inc. (hereinafter, “Defendants”) oppose.  

 

Evidentiary Objections 

 

Plaintiff’s evidentiary objections are overruled to the Declaration of Paul Smith in support of the Opposition are overruled as to Nos. 1-3. 

 

Defendant’s evidentiary objections to the Supplemental Declaration of Herbert N. Wiggins in support of the Reply to the extent it offers new evidence not offered in the original Motion.  

 
Legal Standard 

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a), a party may move to compel a deponent’s attendance and production for inspection of documents if after serving the deposition notice the responding party fails to serve valid objections and fails to appear for the deposition or produce any document described in the deposition notice. A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a) must (1) provide specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document and (2) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., §¿2025.450, subd. (b).) 

 

Meet and Confer 

 

“[W]hen the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, [the 

motion shall be accompanied] by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450 subd. (b)(2).) 

 

On January 13, 2026, Defendants served a meet and confer letter regarding their objections to Plaintiff’s Notice of Deposition. (Smith Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. C.) On March 3, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel attempted to meet and confer with Defense counsel, in which Plaintiff offered to pare down the categories or examination and requests for documents. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 28.) Between March 10, 2026 and March 18, 2026, the parties met and conferred further—however, it became apparent that Defendant First Transit would not produce their PMK for deposition. (Ibid.) On May 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion.  

 

The Court finds that the meet and confer requirement has been met.  

 

Discussion  

 

Plaintiff argues that there is good cause to compel the deposition of Defendant First Transit’s PMK, as such testimony and documents is relevant to the issue of punitive damages. Specifically, the deposition may shed light on the following issues: “1) Knowledge of the previous, substantially similar accidents involving passengers thrown to the floor; 2) Knowledge of the assessments in the hundreds of thousands and millions of dollars by OCTA against First Transit; and 3) Knowledge of poor training and supervision.” (Mot., 11:1-6.) Such information would tend to show that Defendants had knowledge of a dangerous condition/practice and failure to address such condition, which would support an award of punitive damages. Plaintiff has obtained information from other depositions of OCTA employees (First Transit’s employer) suggesting that Defendants had corporate knowledge of the repeated pattern of elderly, disabled passengers being thrown to the floor.  

 

In opposition, Defendants assert that the scope of the Notice of Deposition is inappropriate for the issues in this case. Defendants concede liability for the incident by “by acknowledging that Ms. Joseph was acting within the course and scope of her employment as a bus operator” when she caused Plaintiff’s fall. (Opp., 2:10-13.) Defendants assert that the only remaining issues in this case are whether Plaintiff suffered injury, the nature and extent of those injuries, and damages. The Notice of Deposition seeks information outside of the scope of the remaining issues in this case—specifically, it seeks “the maintenance history, service history, and operation manuals of the bus model despite no claims of equipment malfunction; incidents involving other passengers with no connection to Ms. Joseph, Plaintiff, or his claims; and evaluations, performance measurement reports, assessments and fines by the Orange County Transit Authority.” (Opp., 4:6-10.) Such categories and requests for production are not limited to a specific time period or jurisdiction. Defendants thus object to the Notice of Deposition’s categories for examination and requests for production of documents on grounds that they are not reasonably particularized and are overly broad in scope and time. 

 

Defendants notes that the Court has previously struck punitive damages from the FAC and the SAC. Defendants have filed a motion to strike punitive damages from the TAC, which is currently set for hearing on December 3, 2026.  

 

In reply, Plaintiff reasserts that the information sought is discoverable. Plaintiff asserts that the TAC contains new allegations regarding Defendants’ corporate knowledge to support a punitive damages claim and that Plaintiff should be able to conduct discovery to explore the theory. Plaintiff notes that the Court has not ruled on the viability of these new punitive damages allegations in the TAC.  

 

The Court notes that, under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.230, Plaintiff need only provide matters for examination to guide Defendant First Transit in selecting a PMK for deposition. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.230 [“[I]f the deponent named is not a natural person, the deposition notice shall describe with reasonable particularity the matters on which examination is requested. In that event, the deponent shall designate and produce at the deposition those of its officers, directors, managing agents, employees, or agents who are most qualified to testify on its behalf as to those matters to the extent of any information known or reasonably available to the deponent.”].) 

 

Here, the Court finds that the categories of examination are reasonably particularized such that Defendants can determine a PMK. The categories are further limited to the time period of 2014 to present and generally seek information regarding OCTA buses. To the extent the categories involve other jurisdictions (i.e., the entirety of California, all jurisdictions), the Court finds such categories are not reasonably particularized. Defendants need only determine a PMK who can testify as to OCTA buses. 

 

The Court further notes that the request for documents in conjunction with a deposition notice is not the equivalent of a request for production of documents under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 et seq. A request for production of documents in a deposition notice need only specify with reasonable particularity the materials or category of materials to be produced by the deponent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.220, subd. (a)(4).) A motion to compel production of documents related to a deposition only requires a showing of “good cause justifying production.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(1).) “Objections to the competency of the deponent, or to the relevancy, materiality, or admissibility at trial of the testimony or of the materials produced are unnecessary and are not waived by failure to make them before or during the deposition.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.460 subd., (c).) 

 

Here, the Court finds that the requests for production are reasonably particularized. The requests for production seek communications between the parties, safety protocols and training materials for bus operation, information regarding the subject bus, writings relating to the incident, writings from OCTA regarding incidents of passengers falling, etc. The Court finds good cause justifying the production—the requests would tend to prove Defendants’ corporate knowledge and ratification of a recurring dangerous practice, which could support a punitive damages award. The Court agrees with Plaintiff that his punitive damages theory is still viable at this point and that he is entitled to discover information that may lead to further supporting allegations. Further, the requests for production are limited to the time period of 2014 to present and seek information involving the subject bus or OCTA buses. The Court finds that such information is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence. 

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Deposition of First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable and Request for Production of Documents is granted, as modified. Defendant First Transit’s PMK is ordered to appear for a deposition and produce the requested documents. The parties shall meet and confer to determine the date, time, and location of the deposition. The deposition shall take place no later than 30 days from the date of the Court’s issuance of this order. The date may be extended by agreement of the parties.
