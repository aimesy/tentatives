(1)   Defendant Subaru of America, Inc.’s Motion to Strike or Tax Plaintiffs Laura Mayela Moreno and Michael Moreno’s Memorandum of Costs is GRANTED pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b).

The Court considers the moving papers filed on April 2, 2026, the opposition brief filed on June 11, 2026, and the reply brief filed on June 25, 2026.

BACKGROUND

Factual Background

On February 29, 2024, plaintiffs Laura Mayela Moreno and Michael Moreno (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed their Complaint against defendants Subaru of America, Inc. (“Defendant”) and DOES 1 through 50.  The Complaint includes the following causes of action: (1) Violation of Subdivision (d) of Civil Code section 1793.2; (2) Violation of Subdivision (b) of Civil Code section 1793.2; (3) Violation of Subdivision (a)(3) of Civil Code section 1793.2; (4) Breach of Express Written Warranty – Civil Code Sections 1791.2, subdivision (a), and 1794; and (5) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability – Civil Code sections 1791.1 and 1794.

On January 12, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case.

On February 26, 2026, the parties executed the Settlement Agreement and Release.  As part of this agreement, Plaintiffs received a sum of $9,500.00 and were designated as the “prevailing parties” for the purposes of any motion for attorneys’ fees and costs.

On March 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their Memorandum of Costs for $1,764.15.  (Declaration of Susan Suzy Haroutunian (“Haroutunian Decl.”), ¶ 4, Exh. A.)

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“Any notice of motion to strike or to tax costs must be served and filed 15 days after service of the costs memorandum.  If the cost memorandum was served by mail, the period is extended as provided in Code of Civil Procedure section 1013.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(1).)  The failure to timely file a motion to tax or strike costs constitutes a waiver of the right to object to costs.  (Douglas v. Willis (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 287, 289; see Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(3) [“The party claiming costs and the party contesting costs may agree to extend the time for serving and filing the cost memorandum and a motion to strike or tax costs.  This agreement must be confirmed in writing, specify the extended date for service, and be filed with the clerk.  In the absence of an agreement, the court may extend the times for serving and filing the cost memorandum or the notice of motion to strike or tax costs for a period not to exceed 30 days.”].)

A prevailing party claiming costs must file and serve a memorandum of costs either (1) within 15 days after the date of service of a notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5, (2) 15 days after the service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal, or (3) within 180 days after entry of judgment, whichever is first.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(a).)  Any motion to strike or tax costs must be served and filed 15 days after service of the memorandum, plus an additional 5 days if served by mail or 2 days if served electronically.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(1).)  “Unless objection is made to the entire cost memorandum, the motion to strike or tax costs must refer to each item objected to by the same number and appear in the same order as the corresponding cost item claimed on the memorandum of costs and must state why the item is objectionable.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(2).)

Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, subdivision (a), sets forth items allowable as costs.  Subdivision (b) lists the items that are not allowable as costs.  Generally, the court has discretion to allow costs that are not barred by subdivision (b) but are not listed under subdivision (a).  (Science Applications Internat. Corp. v. Superior Court (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 1095, 1103.)  However, under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act, if a buyer-plaintiff prevails in their action, “the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.”  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d), emphasis added.)  “[I]n enacting Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d) the Legislature intended the phrase costs and expenses to cover items not included in the detailed statutory definition of costs set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5.”  (Warren v. Kia Motors America, Inc. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 24, 42.)  Therefore, a buyer-plaintiff in a Song-Beverly action may recover typically “barred” costs and expenses, provided they were reasonably incurred in connection with commencing and prosecuting the action.

If an item of costs is expressly allowed by statute and if items appear on their face to be proper, the verified memorandum of costs is prima facie evidence of their propriety, shifting the burden of proof to the objecting party to show that the items are not “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation” or “reasonable in amount.”  (Benach v. County of Los Angeles (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 836, 855; Nelson v. Anderson (1999) 72 Cal.App.4th 111, 131-132 [“trial court erred in requiring additional proof from” the party claiming costs, where the party attacking costs had the burden]; Santantonio v. Westinghouse Broadcasting Co. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 102, 116, 121 [after a prima facie showing based on verified cost memorandum, objecting party has the burden to prove costs should be disallowed]; Ladas v. California State Auto. Assn. (1993) 19 Cal.App.4th 761, 773.)

But if an item of cost does not appear proper on its face, the burden of showing that it is reasonable and necessary shifts to the party claiming the cost.  (Nelson v. Anderson (1999) 72 Cal.App.4th 111, 131 (Nelson); Jones v. Dumrichob (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 1258, 1267; see Bach v. County of Butte (1989) 215 Cal.App.3d 294, 308 [“Only after such costs are challenged by a motion to tax do the parties need to justify their claims by submitting documentation of the costs they have incurred.”].)

DISCUSSION

Defendant contend that the Court should either strike the entire memorandum of costs or, alternatively, reduce the costs by $1,298.23; this amount includes: (1) $152.86 in filing and motion fees; (2) $150.00 in jury fees; (3) $116.39 in service of process fees; and (4) $879.00 for a deposition transcript.  (Motion, pp. 1:26-2:6.) 

Plaintiffs, who are the prevailing parties in this Song-Beverly case, are requesting reimbursement for the following costs: (1) $587.96 in filing and motion fees; (2) $150.00 in jury fees; (3) $147.29 in service of process fees; and (4) $879.00 for a deposition transcript.  (Haroutunian Decl., Exh. A.)

Filing fees, jury fees, service of process fees, and deposition costs are expressly authorized under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, subdivisions (a)(1), (a)(3)(A), and (a)(4).  Given that these expenses are explicitly permitted and appear proper, the verified memorandum of costs is prima facie evidence of their propriety.  This places the burden of proof on Defendant to demonstrate that the costs were not “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation” or “reasonable in amount.”  (Benach, supra, 149 Cal.App.4th at p. 855.)

Here, Defendant contends that the Court should strike the entire memorandum of costs, as Plaintiffs have failed to attach any invoices or receipts that would demonstrate that the costs were actually incurred.  (Motion, p. 3:15-19.)  Furthermore, Defendant posits that “[i]f Plaintiffs choose to submit documentation to meet their burden, the Court should, by default, strike any claimed costs which are not supported by documentation, in addition to any unreasonable or improperly claimed costs to which [Defendant] objects.”  (Motion, p. 3:20-22.) 

It is Defendants’ burden to demonstrate that the claimed costs were either not reasonably incurred in connection with the litigation or were unreasonable in amount, particularly after Plaintiffs have provided a memorandum of costs that serves as prima facie evidence of their validity.  The Court finds that Defendant has satisfied this burden by highlighting Plaintiffs’ failure to provide evidence that substantiates the incurred costs.  Consequently, Plaintiffs must demonstrate that the claimed costs were incurred in their opposition.  (Bach v. County of Butte (1989) 215 Cal.App.3d 294, 308 [“Only after such costs are challenged by a motion to tax do the parties need to justify their claims by submitting documentation of the costs they have incurred.”].)

Here, Plaintiffs failed to attach any invoices or receipts to prove that they paid any of the claimed costs.  Although Plaintiffs are not required to include receipts or invoices when filing their memorandum of costs, their failure to provide such documentation in their opposition prevents the Court from ascertaining whether the claimed costs were reasonably incurred, and/or were, in fact, incurred.

As such, Defendant’s motion is GRANTED.

ORDERS

1)      Defendant Subaru of America, Inc.’s Motion to Strike or Tax Plaintiffs Laura Mayela Moreno and Michael Moreno’s Memorandum of Costs is GRANTED.

2)      The Court strikes Plaintiffs’ memorandum of costs.

3)      Defendant is ordered to provide notice of this Court’s ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

LEGAL STANDARDS

Contempt is any act, in or out of court, “which tends to impede, embarrass or obstruct the court in the discharge of its duties.”  (In re Shortridge (1893) 99 Cal. 526, 532.)  Particular acts constituting contempt are enumerated by statute, including: “(d)isobedience of any lawful ... order, or process of the court”; “(d)isobedience of a subpoena duly served”; “refusing to ... answer as a witness”; “(d)isorderly, contemptuous, or insolent behavior toward the judge while holding the court, tending to interrupt the due course of ... trial.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1209, subd. (a); see Code Civ. Proc., § 1991 [disobedience of subpoena]; see also Pen. Code, § 166 [misdemeanor].)  In addition, courts have inherent power to punish acts that interfere with the orderly conduct of proceedings.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128, subd. (a)(3); In re Buckley (1973) 10 Cal.3d 237, 247.)  Any person who commits a contemptuous act may be punished for contempt, which includes attorneys and parties.  (Hallinan v. Superior Court in and for Kings County (1925) 74 Cal.App. 420, 426 [persisting in arguing objections after repeated warnings by the judge]; Hanson v. Superior Court (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 75, 84-85 [attorney misconduct during closing argument]; In re Coleman (1974) 12 Cal.3d 568, 573 [labor union violated temporary restraining order].)

When the contempt at issue is “indirect,” a more elaborate procedure is followed to notify the person(s) sought to be charged and allow them an opportunity to be heard.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1211–1218; see also Hanson v. Superior Court, supra, 91 Cal.App.4th at p. 81.)  “An affidavit must be presented to the court stating the facts constituting the contempt, an order to show cause must be issued, and hearing on the facts must be held by the judge.”  (Arthur v. Superior Court of Los Angeles County (1965) 62 Cal.2d 404, 407-408.)  Since the acts involved did not occur in the court’s presence, the affidavit must cover each element of the commission of the contempt.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1211.5.)  This affidavit serves as the “complaint” in an indirect contempt proceeding and must contain factual allegations based on firsthand knowledge.  (Lyon v. Superior Court of Los Angeles County (1968) 68 Cal.2d 446, 452 (Lyon).)

In indirect contempt proceedings based on disobedience of a prior court order, a valid judgment must meet “strict requirements.”  Each of the following must be established: (1) the rendition of a valid court order; (2) actual knowledge of the order; (3) ability to comply; and (4) willful disobedience of the order.  (Conn v. Superior Court (1987) 196 Cal.App.3d 774, 784 [repeated failures to turn over documents as ordered].)  Upon receipt of the affidavit, the court usually issues an order to show cause (“OSC”) why the person should not be held in contempt.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1212.)  Although not required, the alleged contemnor may respond to the affidavit and OSC by counter-affidavits.  These affidavits serve as the “answer” to the charging allegations in the original affidavit.  (Lyon, supra, 68 Cal.2d at p. 452.)  However, such affidavits are not mandatory.  The alleged contemnor may assert their defenses entirely at the hearing.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1217.)¿

“Upon the answer and evidence taken, the court or judge shall determine whether the person proceeded against is guilty of the contempt charged, and if it be adjudged that the person is guilty of the contempt, a fine may be imposed on the person not exceeding one thousand dollars ($1,000), payable to the court, or the person may be imprisoned not exceeding five days, or both.  In addition, a person who is subject to a court order as a party to the action, or any agent of this person, who is adjudged guilty of contempt for violating that court order may be ordered to pay to the party initiating the contempt proceeding the reasonable attorney’s fees and costs incurred by this party in connection with the contempt proceeding.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1218, subd. (a).)

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff requests an order of contempt against Hill due to Hill’s noncompliance with the Court’s order issued on December 1, 2025; this order mandated the provision of responses and the payment of $900.00 in sanctions.  (Motion, p. 2:4-7; Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 3.)  Furthermore, Plaintiff seeks to impose a fine of $1,000 on Hill, payable to the Court, as well as $600.00 in attorney’s fees to be paid to Plaintiff’s counsel.  (Motion, pp. 4:20-5:2.) 

As articulated in the legal standards section, Plaintiff is required to establish the following four elements: (1) the rendition of a valid court order; (2) actual knowledge of the order; (3) ability to comply; and (4) willful disobedience of the order.  (Conn v. Superior Court (1987) 196 Cal.App.3d 774, 784 [repeated failures to turn over documents as ordered] (Conn).)  Furthermore, because the alleged noncompliance with the Court’s order did not occur in the Court’s presence, an affidavit must address each element of the contempt charge.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1211.5; Arthur v. Superior Court of Los Angeles County (1965) 62 Cal.2d 404, 407-408.)  The amended declaration provided by Virginia Marsh addresses all the necessary elements.

Here, Marsh affirms that a valid court order was issued.  She states, “The court issued an order on 12/1/25 compelling [Hill] to produce discovery responses and to pay sanctions, both to be completed not later than 1/15/26.”  (Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 3.)  Although Hill contested the validity of the order on the grounds of alleged non-receipt of the motion, the Court upheld its ruling and denied Hill’s Motion to Vacate and Set Aside Order Granting Plaintiff Kamran Harsini’s Motion to Compel Further Discovery Responses and Award of Sanctions.  (May 21, 2026 Minute Order, pp. 4-5.)

Further, Marsh confirms that Hill was notified and possessed knowledge of the order.  She asserts, “I filed and served Notice of the Ruling on 12/2/25, which is reflected in the court’s website, and by a proof of service filed with that Notice.”  (Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 4.)  Additionally, Hill’s counsel submitted a declaration indicating that “ ‘[D]efense counsel first became aware of the existence of Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel through Plaintiff’s Notice of Ruling dated December 2, 2025.’ ”  (Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 5.)

Third, Marsh provides evidence of Hill’s ability to comply: “I have not received any information or statement from any source that would indicate that Defendant HILL, either directly or through counsel, was unable to comply with the court’s order compelling production of discovery and sanctions.”  (Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 5.)  While Hill asserts that this evidence fails to meet the criteria established in Conn, the Court disagrees.  (Opp., pp. 4:21-5:1.)  Hill has not provided any case law or statutory authority to substantiate this claim.  Furthermore, neither Hill nor its counsel contends that Hill lacked the capability to provide the required discovery or pay the sanctions.

Finally, Marsh presents evidence that Hill has willfully disobeyed the Court order.  She states, “To date, Plaintiff has not received discovery responses per the court’s order of 12/1/25, and has not received payment of sanctions.”  (Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 7.)  While Hill contested the order’s validity based on claims of non-service of the original motion, the Court found that Hill received the original motion and upheld the order’s validity.  (May 21, 2026 Minute Order, pp. 4-5.)  Therefore, the Court finds Hill’s assertion that its “noncompliance stems from a lack of notice and a pending challenge to the validity of the underlying order—not from willful disobedience or malicious intent to deny Plaintiff his right to discovery” to be unconvincing.  (Opp., p. 2:17-19.)  As such, the Court finds credible evidence of willful noncompliance.

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s motion.

Request for Fines and Attorney’s Fees

Plaintiff requests that the Court impose a fine of $1,000 on Hill, payable to the Court, as well as $600.00 in attorney’s fees to be paid to Plaintiff’s counsel.  (Motion, pp. 4:20-5:2.)  Plaintiff makes this request pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1218, subdivision (a).  (Motion, p. 4:21-23.)  Marsh’s declaration supports Plaintiff’s claim for attorney’s fees.  In her declaration, she states that she spent 2.0 hours on the motion at a rate of $300.00 per hour.  (Amend. Marsh Decl., ¶ 12.)  Here, Plaintiff’s request for attorney’s fees in the amount of $600.00 is reasonable.  However, imposition of $1,000.00 in addition to attorney’s fees is unnecessary.

 

 

ORDERS

1)      Plaintiff Kamran Harsini’s Motion for Order of Contempt Against Defendant the Hill Community Association is GRANTED.

2)      Plaintiff Kamran Harsini’s Request to Impose a $1,000 Fine is DENIED.

3)      Plaintiff Kamran Harsini’s Request for Attorney’s Fees is GRANTED.

4)      Hill is ordered to pay Plaintiff’s counsel $600.00 by July 16, 2026.

5)      Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this Court’s ruling.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
