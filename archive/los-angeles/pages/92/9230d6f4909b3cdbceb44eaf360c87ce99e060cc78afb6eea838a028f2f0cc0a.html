DEPARTMENT B LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23TRCV04305    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                       Plaintiff Jasmine Farmer

Responding Party:                  Defendant City of Los Angeles

Motion to Compel Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set Two

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

The motion is GRANTED. See ruling below.

BACKGROUND

On December 28, 2023, Jasmine Farmer filed a complaint against defendant City of Los Angeles for dangerous condition of public property.

On July 25, 2024, plaintiff filed an amendment designating Delta Air Lines, Inc. as Doe 1.

On February 25, 2025, plaintiff filed an amendment designating Hensel Phelps Parent 1 Inc. as Doe 2.

On May 19, 2025, Hensel Phelps Construction Co. filed a cross-complaint against Roes 1 through 100 for implied indemnity, contribution, and declaratory relief.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

45-Day Rule:  This motion must be served within 45 days after service of the response in question (extended if served by mail, overnight delivery, or fax; see CCP §1013); otherwise, the demanding party waives the right to compel any further response to the CCP §2031.010 demand.  CCP §§2031.310(c), 2016.050; see Sperber v. Robinson (1994) 26 Cal. App. 4th 736, 745.  The 45-day time limit is mandatory and jurisdictional.  Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal. App. 4th 1403, 1410.  The parties, however, can also agree in writing on a specific later date by which to file the motion to compel.  CCP §2031.310(c).

Meet-and-Confer Requirement:  The motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a declaration showing “a reasonable and good faith attempt” to resolve the issues outside of court (so-called “meet and confer”).  CCP §§2016.040, 2031.310(b)(2).

Separate Statement:  Any motion involving the content of a discovery request or the responses to such a request shall be accompanied by a separate statement.  This includes a motion to compel further responses to demand for inspection of documents or tangible things.  CRC Rule 3.1020(a)(3). 

Request for Production of Documents

On receipt of a response to an inspection demand, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further responses to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply:  (1) a statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete; (2) a representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive; or (3) an objection in the response is without merit or too general.  CCP § 2031.310(a).

A statement of compliance shall state that the production, inspection, and related activity demanded will be allowed either in whole or in part, and that all documents or things in the demanded category that are in the possession, custody, or control of that party and to which no objection is being made will be included in the production.  CCP § 2031.220. 

“A representation of inability to comply with [a] particular demand for inspection . . . shall affirm that a diligent search and reasonable inquiry has been made in an effort to comply with that demand.  This statement shall also specify whether the inability to comply is because the particular item or category has never existed, has been destroyed, has been lost, misplaced, or stolen, or has never been, or is no longer, in the possession, custody, or control of the responding party.  This statement shall set forth the name and address of any natural person or organization known or believed by that party to have possession, custody, or control of that item or category of item.”  CCP § 2031.230.

A motion to compel further response to requests for production “shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the inspection demand.”  CCP § 2031.310(b)(1).  “To establish ‘good cause,’ the burden is on the moving party to show both:  [1] Relevance to the subject matter (e.g., how the information in the documents would tend to prove or disprove some issue in the case); and [2] Specific facts justifying discovery (e.g., why such information is necessary for trial preparation or to prevent surprise at trial).  The fact that there is no alternative source for the information sought is an important factor in establishing ‘good cause’ for inspection.  But it is not essential in every case.”  Weil & Brown, Civil Procedure Before Trial, 8:1495.6 (citations omitted).  “Declarations are generally used to show the requisite ‘good cause’ for an order to compel inspection.  The declarations must contain ‘specific facts’ rather than mere conclusions.”  Id. at 8:1495.7 (citation omitted).  “The declarations may be on information and belief, if necessary.  However, in such cases, the ‘specific facts’ supporting such information and belief (the sources of the information) must also be alleged.”  Id. at 8:1495.8 (citation omitted).  “Most declarations are made by the attorney for the moving party, who is usually more familiar with the relevancy and ‘specific facts’ constituting ‘good cause’ for inspection.”  Id. at 8:1495.9.

“If ‘good cause’ is shown by the moving party, the burden is then on the responding party to justify any objections made to document disclosure (the same as on motions to compel answers to interrogatories or deposition questions. . . ).”  Id. at 8:1496 (citation omitted).

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Jasmine Farmer requests that the Court compel defendant City of Los Angeles aka City of Los Angeles, Department of Airports, aka Los Angeles World Airports further responses to plaintiff’s request for production of documents, set two, nos. 35-48.

The complaint alleges that this lawsuit arises from an incident that took place on April 11, 2023 at approximately 4:20 a.m., at the LAX Airport.  Complaint, ¶10.  The City of Los Angeles owned, occupied, leased, used, regulated, controlled, managed, maintained, operated, supervised, repaired, and possessed the roof and ceiling at the LAX Airport.  Id., ¶11.  At the time of the incident, plaintiff was working as a TSA agent.  On that day, she was tasked with checking in employees at the Known Crew Member podium in terminal 3.  Id., ¶12.  While performing this task, plaintiff was at a podium inside the airport.  Id., ¶13.  Above the podium are ceiling tiles.  Id., ¶14.  While plaintiff was checking in airline staff, a wet ceiling tile fell from the ceiling and struck her in the head. As a result, she suffered severe and permanent injury, including a traumatic brain injury.  Id., ¶15.

Plaintiff explains that on April 1, 2026, plaintiff served defendant LAWA with Request for Production of Documents, Set Two.  On April 2, plaintiff’s counsel reached out to defense counsel to provide “advance notice” of plaintiff’s intent to notice the deposition of LAWA’s PMQ and five LAWA employees.  On May 5, defense counsel requested a two-week extension but did not provide witness availability.  Plaintiff’s counsel granted a one-week extension to May 12.  On May 11, defense counsel requested another week extension, which plaintiff’s counsel granted to May 19.  On May 19, defense counsel requested an additional two-week extension, “citing ongoing delays in receiving responsive documents and information from LAWA.”  Plaintiff’s counsel granted an extension to May 26.  On May 26, defense counsel requested another extension.  Plaintiff’s counsel responded, granting one final extension and requiring that both the outstanding discovery responses and deposition availability for all six noticed LAWA witnesses be provided no later than June 2.  On June 2, defense counsel contacted plaintiff’s counsel by phone and advised that they were still working to obtain both discovery responses and deposition dates from LAWA and required additional time.  Plaintiff’s counsel declined to grant a further extension.  As of the filing date of the motion on June 10, plaintiff’s counsel had not received further responses. 

In opposition, LAWA contends that when it did not receive a “brief additional extension of time,” defendant served objection-only responses on June 2 and “expressly advised Plaintiff that substantive responses would be forthcoming.”  LAWA argues that the “rashly filed” motion is the result of plaintiff’s “failure to adequately engage in the meet and confer process.”  LAWA asserts that it has agreed to provide substantive responses to the discovery and “that Plaintiff hastily resorted to unnecessary motion practice does not mean a legitimate discovery dispute exists.”  LAWA contends that it did not file a response to the separate statement because defendant has already agreed to provide responses to the subject discovery subject to its objections.

In the reply (filed on July 15), plaintiff states that defendant has still not served substantive responses or any documents.  Plaintiff notes that defense counsel has represented that responses will be provided by the end of July.  Plaintiff argues that further meet and confer would not have resolved this dispute. 

The Court finds that plaintiff properly served the discovery requests.  Plaintiff’s counsel reasonably provided numerous extensions to respond.  Plaintiff’s counsel also reasonably declined to provide a further extension.  Defendant admittedly served objections only because plaintiff’s counsel did not provide any further extensions.  Defendant failed to file a response to plaintiff’s separate statement, not even attempting to justify any of defendant’s objections.

The Court finds that defendant’s objections lack merit. 

The motion is GRANTED.

Sanctions

Plaintiff requests $3867.50 ($595/hr. x 6.5 hrs.) in sanctions against defendant LAWA.  Defendant requests $960 in sanctions against plaintiff and plaintiff’s attorney.  The Court finds that sanctions are warranted against defendant in the amount of $2380 ($595/hr. x 4 hrs.).

ORDER

            The motion is GRANTED.  Defendant LAWA is ordered to serve further responses to plaintiff’s request for production of documents, set two, by July 31.

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is GRANTED.  Defendant LAWA is ordered to pay sanctions to plaintiff in the amount of $2380 within thirty days.

            Defendant LAWA’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

            Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of ruling.


Case Number: 26TRCP00194    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                      Allstate Northbrook Indemnity Company

Responding Party:                  None

Petition to Compel Arbitration of Underinsured Motorist

 

            The Court considered the petition and opposition.

RULING

            The petition is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

BACKGROUND

            On May 29, 2026, Allstate Northbrook Indemnity Company filed a petition to compel arbitration of underinsured motorist against Christopher Welch, pursuant to CCP §1281.2 and Insurance Code §11580.2

            On June 9, 2026, Allstate Northbrook filed another petition to compel arbitration of underinsured motorist.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

Under CCP § 1281, a “written agreement to submit to arbitration an existing controversy or a controversy thereafter arising is valid, enforceable and revocable, save upon such grounds as

exist for the revocation of any contract.”

Under CCP § 1281.2, “On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party thereto refuses to arbitrate such controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists . . . .”

DISCUSSION

            The Court finds that the petitions were improperly served.  The proof of service of the petition signed and filed on May 29, 2026, indicates that it was served by email.  The proof of service of the petition signed on May 9, 2026, and filed on June 9, 2026, with a hearing date of July 22, 2026 (on the caption), indicates that the petition was served by email on April 29, 2026 (before the petition was signed).  The “petition” filed on May 9 may have been intended to be a notice of hearing but there is no notice, only the date of the hearing on the caption.

            Under CCP §1290.4(a), “[a] copy of the petition and written notice of the time and place of the hearing thereof and any other papers upon which the petition is based shall be served in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice. 

(b) If the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made and the person upon whom service is to be made has not previously appeared in the proceeding and has not previously been served in accordance with this subdivision:  (1) Service within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.” 

The insurance policy attached to the petition does not state the manner of service.  Thus, service within California must be made in the same manner provided by law for the service of a summons in a civil action.  Service by email is thus improper.  Further, there is no notice of the hearing on the petition.  Also, the petition filed on June 9, indicates a signed date of the petition after it was purportedly served on April 29.

The petition is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.
