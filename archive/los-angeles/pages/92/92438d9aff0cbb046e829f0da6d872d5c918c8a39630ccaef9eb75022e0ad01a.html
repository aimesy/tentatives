Proc., § 372.) “ ‘[W]ithout trial court approval of the proposed compromise of the ward’s claim, the settlement cannot be valid.  [Citation.] [¶] Nor is the settlement binding [on the minor] until it is endorsed by the trial court.’ ” (Pearson v. Superior Court (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1333, 1338.) Proc., § 372, subd. (a)(1).)

Western Heritage Ins. Co. (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 249, 256, fn. 4, citing Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.)

Case Number: 25STCV05536    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 512

Case No.

25STCV05536

Case Name:

Varela, et al. v. Banuelos Martinez

Hearing Date:

September 21, 2026

 

Defaulting defendant(s)

RICARDO BANUELOS MARTINEZ


TENTATIVE RULING:

 

On February 26, 2025, Plaintiffs Anthony David Varela and Gabriela Ruby Vergel (“Plaintiffs”) filed the instant action against Defendant Ricardo Banuelos Martinez (“Defendant”) and Does 1-10, alleging seven causes of action based on Plaintiffs’ tenancy in Defendant’s property. (Compl., pp. 1-2.)

 

On April 1, 2025, Defendant, proceeding pro se, filed an Answer.

 

On May 22, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions and struck Defendant’s Answer filed on April 1, 2025. (5/22/26 Order.)

 

On July 9, 2026, default was entered against Defendant.

 

On July 15, 2026, Does 1-10 were dismissed.

 

Plaintiffs seek judgment against Defendant in the amount of $1,584,840, consisting of $784,905 in damages and $49,935.00 in attorney’s fees.

 

The Court finds that trebled rent disgorgement ($174,300) is not properly demanded in the Complaint. The Complaint pleads disgorgement of all rent illegally demanded "in an amount to be proven at trial" without listing a specific dollar figure.  (See Dhawan v. Biring (2015) 241 Cal. App. 4th 963.) Thus, the requested trebled rent disgorgement damages will not be awarded.

 

Further, the Court finds that punitive damages are not substantiated. While Plaintiff attempts to provide financial documents, Plaintiff has not provided adequate and substantiated evidence in support of assets with liabilities.  (See Adams v. Murakami (1991) 54 Cal. 3d 105; also see Baxter v. Peterson (2007) 150 Cal. App. 4th 673, multiple properties with estimated values could not sustain an award where "the record ...[was] silent with respect to ...liabilities."; Lara v. Cadag (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th 1061, 1065 (meaningful evidence required and “evidence of earnings is not by itself sufficient.”); Green v. Laibco, LLC (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 441, 452-54 (evidence of a defendant’s profit during the most recent twelve-month period, and evidence of defendant’s positive net worth, constituted the required meaningful evidence of financial condition)). The Court is not satisfied with the Hermansen (attorney) declaration. This is not sufficient and qualified declaration (e.g. an assessor or an investigator) to meet Plaintiffs’ burden of proof. Furthermore, the alleged absence of a recorded mortgage does not exclude unrecorded debts, judgments, or tax obligations. Thus, punitive damages will not be awarded based on insufficient evidence in support of assets with liabilities.

 

Further, the Court finds an attorney fee award that departs from the Local Rule schedule is warranted in this action given the unique circumstances of this case. However, Plaintiffs’ attorney fees are duplicative and excessive considering the limited workup of the case. Thus, attorney fees will be permitted in the amount of $35,000. (See Cruz v. Ayromloo (2007) 155 Cal.App.4th 1270; Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal. 4th 1122.)

 

Further, the Court finds that the amount requested for pain and suffering is excessive based on the circumstances, nature of the case, and the declarations submitted. (See Garcia v. Myllyla (2019) 40 Cal. App. 5th 990.) Based thereon, this amount is reduced to $100,000 per plaintiff.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Default Judgment Packet is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $345,605, reflecting $38,550 for relocation assistance, $20,600 for utility penalty, $51,455 for property damage/out of pocket expenses, $100,000 for pain and suffering per plaintiff, and $35,000 for attorney fees.

 

Moving party is to submit a proposed judgment reflecting the reduced amounts within 5 days of this order.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On August 10, 2026, Petitioner J.G. Wentworth Originations, LLC (“Petitioner”) filed a Verified Petition for Approval for Transfer of Payment Rights (the “Petition”). The Petition names the interested parties in this action as Jane Doe (“Payee”), an adult, and County of Los Angeles. (Pet., pp. 1-2.)

 

On August 18, 2026, Petitioner filed proof of service of the Petition and notice of hearing by mail on Payee and County of Los Angeles.

 

On August 28, 2026, Petitioner filed and served the instant Amended Petition that names the interested parties in this action as Payee and County of Los Angeles. (Pet., pp. 1-2.)

 

On September 8, 2026, Petitioner field and served amended exhibits A, B, and E to the Amended Petition.

 

No Opposition to the Amended Petition has been filed.

 

II.              Procedural Issues

 

As an initial matter, the Court addresses that, while the instant item set for hearing is a petition, the item on calendar for the instant hearing date is labeled in the Court’s system as a “motion.” Despite this misnomer, the Court finds that there is neither a motion filed nor set for hearing in this matter, and thus in substance it is the Petition/Amended Petition to be heard at the instant hearing.

 

III.            Legal Standard

 

“The California Legislature has adopted the Structured Settlement Protection Act (SSPA) (§ 10134 et seq.) to protect structured settlement payees from exploitation by factoring companies.” (RSL Funding, LLC v. Alford (2015) 239 Cal.App.4th 741, 745.) 

 

“The court-approval process requires the factoring company to file a petition in the county in which the transferor resides for approval of the transfer, attaching copies of the petition, the transfer agreement, the disclosure form, the annuity contract, any qualified assignment agreement and the structured settlement agreement, a list of the names and ages of the transferor's dependents, notice of the court hearing date, and notice of a right to respond.” (321 Henderson Receivables Origination LLC v. Sioteco (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 1059, 1066 [citing Ins. Code, §10139.5(c)].) 

 

“The California act…governing transfers of structured settlement payment rights defines a ‘“[s]tructured settlement agreement” [as] an arrangement for periodic payment of damages established by settlement or judgment in resolution of a tort claim in which the payment of the judgment or award is paid in whole, or in part, in periodic tax-free payments rather than a lump-sum payment.’” (Matthews v. Liberty Assignment Corp. (2016) 247 Cal.App.4th 71, 78.) 

 

“Annuity issuers and structured settlement obligors are defined as ‘interested parties’ under the SSPA (§ 10134, subd. (g)), and as such, are entitled to notice of petitions to authorize transfer of payments under a structured settlement agreement.” (RSL Funding, LLC, supra, 239 Cal.App.4th at p. 745.) 

 

Insurance Code §10137 provides: “A transfer of structured settlement payment rights is void unless a court reviews and approves the transfer and finds the following conditions are met: (a) [t]he transfer of the structured settlement payment rights is fair and reasonable and in the best interest of the payee, taking into account the welfare and support of his or her dependents [; and] (b) [t]he transfer complies with the requirements of this article, will not contravene other applicable law, and the court has reviewed and approved the transfer as provided in Section 10139.5.”  (Ins. Code, § 10137.)  The transfer agreement is effective only upon approval in a final court order.  (Ins. Code, § 10139.5(a).) 

 

Insurance Code §10139.5(a) requires the court to make the following findings: 

 

  1. The transfer is in the best interest of the payee, taking into account the welfare and support of the payee’s dependents. 
  1. The payee has been advised in writing by the transferee to seek independent professional advice regarding the transfer and has either received that advice or knowingly waived, in writing, the opportunity to receive the advice. 
  1. The transferee has complied with the notification requirements pursuant to paragraph (2) of subdivision (f), the transferee has provided the payee with a disclosure form that complies with Section 10136, and the transfer agreement complies with Sections 10136 and 10138. 
  1. The transfer does not contravene any applicable statute or the order of any court or other government authority. 
  1. The payee understands the terms of the transfer agreement, including the terms set forth in the disclosure statement required by Section 10136. 
  1. The payee understands and does not wish to exercise the payee’s right to cancel the transfer agreement. 

 

(Ins. Code, § 10139.5(a).) 

 

In addition, pursuant to Insurance Code section 10139.5(c):  

 

Every petition for approval of a transfer of structured settlement payment rights, except as provided in subdivision (d), shall include, to the extent known after the transferee has made reasonable inquiry with the payee, all of the following: 

 

  1. The payee’s name, address, and age. 
  1. The payee’s marital status, and, if married or separated, the name of the payee’s spouse.       
  1. The names, ages, and place or places of residence of the payee’s minor children or other dependents, if any. 
  1. The amounts and sources of the payee’s monthly income and financial resources and, if presently married, the amounts and sources of the monthly income and financial resources of the payee’s spouse.  
  1.  Whether the payee is currently obligated under any child support or spousal support order, and, if so, the names, addresses, and telephone numbers of any individual, entity, or agency that is receiving child or spousal support from the payee under that order or that has jurisdiction over the order or the payments in question. 
  1. Information regarding previous transfers or attempted transfers, as described in paragraph (11), (12), or (13) of subdivision (b).  The transferee or payee may choose to provide this information by providing copies of pleadings, transaction documents, or orders involving any previous attempted or completed transfer or by providing the court a summary of available information regarding any previous transfer or attempted transfer, such as the date of the transfer or attempted transfer, the payments transferred or attempted to be transferred by the payee in the earlier transaction, the amount of money received by the payee in connection with the previous transaction, and generally the payee’s reasons for pursuing or completing a previous transaction. The transferee’s inability to provide the information required by this paragraph shall not preclude the court from approving the proposed transfer, if the court determines that the information is not available to the transferee after the transferee has made a reasonable effort to secure the information, including making an inquiry with the payee. 

 

(Ins. Code § 10139.5(c).) 

 

IV.           Discussion

 

The verified Amended Petition states Payee agrees to transfer the following payments to Petitioner: 1 payment of $66,000.00 on January 28, 2027; 1 payment of $66,000.00 on January 28, 2028; 1 payment of $66,000.00 on January 28, 2029; 1 payment of $66,000.00 on September 28, 2029; 1 payment of $66,000.00 on January 28, 2030; and 1 payment of $66,000.00 on January 28, 2031.  (Amended Petition, ¶ 4.) The net amount paid to Payee would be $290,000.00.  (First Amended Petition, p. 4.) If approved, the Payee – who is 34 years old with one minor child and is currently unemployed – intends to use the money to have “financial means to pay overdue bills, purchase a vehicle and cover living expenses as will be set forth in the Declaration to be filed in this matter.” (Amended Petition, p. 4.)

 

However, Petitioner filed and served Amended Exhibits on September 8, 2026, that significantly alters the Amended Petition. Indeed, the Amended Exhibits include a new agreement between Payee and Petitioner that provides for a new net payment amount of $140,000 (see Amended Exhs., Exh. B) and a new transfer payment plan – “1 payment of $43,800.00 on January 1, 2027 B) 1 payment of $43,800.00 on January 1, 2028 C) 1 payment of $43,800.00 on January 1, 2029 D) 1 payment of $43,800.00 on January 1, 2030.” (See Amended Exhs., Exh. A, ¶ 2.) Thus, the Amended Petition is no longer accurate and up-to-date as to the arrangement between the parties.

 

Additionally, the Court notes the Amended Petition states a Payee Declaration is “to be filed in this matter” (Amended Petition, p. 4) – however, no such Payee declaration has been filed.

 

Also, the Amended Petition states a “copy of the annuity contract and a copy of any qualified assignment agreement” and a “copy of the underlying settlement agreement” will be supplemented as Exhibits C and D after Payee obtains the annuity documentation and settlement documentation (see Amended Petition, p. 5, fns. 1-2) – however, no exhibit C or D is provided in conjunction with the Amended Petition or Amended Exhibits.

 

            Finally, Petitioner filed and served the First Amended Petition and amended notice of hearing by mail at least 20 days prior to the instant hearing date; however, the Amended Exhibits, which significantly alter the Amended Petition, were not filed and served at least 20 days prior to the instant hearing. (Ins. Code, §§ 1039.5, subd. (f)(2) [“Not less than 20 days prior to the scheduled hearing on any petition for approval of a transfer of structured settlement payment rights under this article, the transferee shall file with the court and serve on all interested parties a notice of the proposed transfer and the petition for its authorization”], 1039.2 [“Any notice required by this article shall be deemed to have been given if addressed to the recipient’s last known address and deposited, first class postage paid, in the United States mail not less than five calendar days prior to the date on which notice is required”].)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Amended Petition is CONTINUED to allow Petitioner to file a second amended petition that addresses all the deficiencies noted herein.

 

The Court notes that the second amended petition should be inclusive of all referenced documents and up-to-date information regarding the subject transfer of payment rights between the parties. There should not be piecemeal filings regarding the second amended petition.

 

V.             Conclusion & Order

 

Petitioner J.G. Wentworth Originations, LLC’s Amended Petition for Approval for Transfer of Payment Rights is CONTINUED to Wednesday, October 21, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. in Department 512 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

 

At least 20 days prior to the next scheduled hearing, Petitioner is to file and serve a second amended petition addressing the deficiencies noted herein.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On February 6, 2026, Plaintiff Jordan Vandesteene (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for Violation of Statutory Obligations (Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act) against Defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC (“Defendant”) and Does 1-50.

 

On March 18, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer.

 

On July 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Imposition of Monetary and Evidentiary Sanctions Against Defendant for Repeated Noncompliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 (the “Motion”).

 

            On September 8, 2026, Defendant filed an Opposition.

 

            On September 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 “only applies to a civil action seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle pursuant to [Code of Civil Procedure section 871.20]” and requires, in relevant part, that, within 120 days after filing a responsive pleading, all parties have the right to conduct initial depositions of the plaintiff and defendant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subds. (a), (c).)

 

Further, Code of Civil Procedure also requires that “[w]ithin 90 days after filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall schedule a mediation to occur within 150 days after filing of the answer or other responsive pleading with a court-appointed or private mediator.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (d).) 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (j)(2) states that “[u]nless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: … A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff’s attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the provisions relating to depositions as prescribed in subdivision (c).”

 

III.            Discussion

 

Plaintiff seeks monetary and evidentiary sanctions on the following grounds: (1) while the parties completed mediation, Defendant failed to schedule a mediation within 90 days after the Answer was filed; (2) Plaintiff has not yet been able to depose entity Defendant’s person most qualified (“PMQ”).

 

In Opposition, Defendant contends that sanctions against Defendant are entirely unwarranted. First, Defendant states it has demonstrated good cause for the delay in the PMQ deposition because “Defendant's delay was not the result of bad faith, obstinance, or a desire to obstruct Plaintiff's case. Ultimately, some of the delay was caused due to miscommunication within JLRNA’s counsel’s firm which mistakenly caused JLRNA’s counsel to believe that the deposition was not agreed upon. However, Defendant did not refuse to produce its PMK at all at any time. As Plaintiff’s own exhibits demonstrate, Defendant was responsive to Plaintiff’s inquiries regarding the deposition of JLRNA’s PMK and informed Plaintiff that they were working with the PMK to provide their earliest availability to Plaintiff.” (Opp., p. 6, Forgy Decl., ¶ 7.) Further, Defendant contends the request for sanctions regarding mediation is moot and factually inaccurate given that “the factual record confirms that the parties actually conducted a successful mediation session on July 14, 2026, well before the instant motion was filed on July 30, 2026.” (Opp., p. 6.) The Opposition states that “[i]mposing monetary or evidentiary sanctions for a minor delay in the administrative scheduling of an event that subsequently occurred on time would serve no judicial purpose.” (Opp., p. 7.) Finally, the Opposition contends evidentiary sanctions are statutorily barred because, based on the foregoing, Defendant did not repeatedly fail to comply with the governing statute. (Opp., p. 7.)

           

            In Reply, Plaintiff states the Motion’s request for sanctions is not moot given that Defendant has still failed to provide its PMQ for deposition, and the Opposition fails to provide any evidentiarily-supported good cause for the delay. (See Reply, pp. 3-4.) Further, Plaintiff states the Opposition fails to provide any explanation for Plaintiff’s counsel’s failure to timely schedule mediation as well. (See Reply, p. 3, Wood Decl.)

 

Here, the parties do not dispute that Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 applies to the instant action.

 

As to mediation, the Court finds the parties do not dispute that they completed mediation within the 150-day deadline outlined in Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (d). The Court declines to award sanctions regarding the mediation given the mediation undisputedly occurred during the statutorily outlined window. To the extent the scheduling of the mediation involved some administrative delay, the Court does not find that Plaintiff has shown such negligible delay warrants sanctions.

 

            As to the deposition of Defendant’s PMQ, the Court finds monetary sanctions are warranted. The parties do not dispute that the deposition has not yet occurred. Defendant’s vague and ambiguous reference in the opposing papers to “a miscommunication” regarding the July deposition date is not adequately explained. Further, the deadline to conduct initial depositions in this matter was July 16, 2026, and it is now September 2026. Moreover, Defendant provides evidence that Defendant’s proffered deposition date to Plaintiff is for January 2027, and Plaintiff provides evidence this alternative deposition date was not offered until the instant Motion was already filed. (Opp., Forgy Decl., ¶ 11, Exh. A; Reply, Wood Decl., Exh. 1.) In light of these circumstances, the Court finds statutory sanctions of $2,500 against defense counsel regarding the deposition of Defendant’s PMQ is warranted. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subds. (c), (j)(2).)

 

            Given that the Court only finds one substantive instance of Defendant’s noncompliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 subdivision (b), (c), or (d), the Court does not find it has grounds to impose evidentiary sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 subdivision (j)(4), contrary to what Plaintiff requests. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j)(4) [“For a manufacturer’s or defendant’s repeated noncompliance with subdivision (b), (c), or (d), a court shall order that evidentiary sanctions attach precluding the manufacturer or defendant from introducing evidence at trial regarding whether the motor vehicle had a nonconformity that substantially impaired the use, value, or safety of the motor vehicle, or whether the motor vehicle was repaired to match the written warranty after a reasonable number of opportunities to do so”].)

 

            Thus, the Motion is GRANTED as to the request for monetary sanctions and otherwise DENIED.

 

            Monetary sanctions of $2,500 is awarded in favor of Plaintiff and against defense counsel, payable to Plaintiff’s counsel within 15 days.

 

IV.           Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff Jordan Vandesteene’s Motion for Imposition of Monetary and Evidentiary Sanctions Against Defendant for Repeated Noncompliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 is GRANTED as to monetary sanctions and otherwise DENIED.

 

Monetary sanctions of $2,500 is awarded in favor of Plaintiff and against defense counsel, payable to Plaintiff’s counsel within 15 days.

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On April 28, 2026, Plaintiff Angela Lopez (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Ojax Hospice, Inc. (“Ojax”); Lillian Villareal; Marilyn Mangabat; and Estate of Deanette Aguiluz (“Defendants”) and Does 1-5, alleging causes of action for Breach of Written Contract, Breach of Oral Contract, Unjust Enrichment, and Declaratory Relief.

 

On July 15, 2026, Mangabat, proceeding pro se, filed the instant Demurrer (the “Demurrer”).

 

On September 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

 

On September 14, 2026, Mangabat, now represented by counsel, filed a Reply.

 

III.            Legal Standard

 

“As a general matter, in a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Ibid.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

A general demurrer may be brought under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e) if insufficient facts are stated to support the cause of action asserted or under section 430.10, subdivision (a), where the court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the pleading. All other grounds listed in Section 430.10, including uncertainty under subdivision (f), are special demurrers.

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245).

II.              Discussion

 

A.    Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that “[b]efore filing a demurrer pursuant to this chapter, the demurring party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) The parties are to meet and confer at least five days before the date the responsive pleading is due. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(2).) Thereafter, the demurring party shall file and serve a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(3).) 

Here, Mangabat filed a declaration in support of the Demurrer attesting that Mangabat called Plaintiff’s counsel’s office and sent meet and confer correspondence regarding the issues raised in the Demurrer, but Mangabat and Plaintiff’s counsel were unable to adequately meet and confer prior to the instant Demurrer.

 

Given the circumstances, the Court finds the meet and confer requirement is satisfied.

 

B.    Demurrer

 

Mangabat demurs to the second and third causes of action on the grounds they fail to plead sufficient facts to state causes of action. Specifically, Mangabat contends that the Complaint fails to plead any facts stating Mangabat personally entered into any contract with Plaintiff, as demonstrated by the plain terms of the written agreement attached to the Complaint.

 

In Opposition, Plaintiff states the Complaint adequately pleads breach of an oral contract between Mangabat and Plaintiff and, as such, there exists an actual controversy between the parties. Thus, the Demurrer should be overruled as to both the second and third causes of action.

 

In Reply, Mangabat contends the Opposition fails to adequately rebut the contentions raised in the Demurrer. When reading the Complaint as a whole, Mangabat emphasizes that the Complaint fails to plead any kind of contractual relationship between Plaintiff and Mangabat personally. Mangabat reiterates arguments raised in the Demurrer and requests the Court sustain the Demurrer without leave to amend.

 

Cause of Action No. 2 – Breach of Oral Contract

 

A cause of action for breach of contract must plead “(1) the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for non-performance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) damage to plaintiff therefrom.” (Acoustics, Inc. v. Trepte Constr. Co. (1971) 14 Cal.App.3d 887, 913.) A written contract can be pled by alleging its making and attaching a copy to the complaint or by alleging the making and the substance of the relevant terms. (Construction Protective Services, Inc. v. TIG Specialty Ins. Co. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 189, 198-199.) The terms of an “oral contract may be pleaded generally as to its effect, because it is rarely possible to allege the exact words.” (Scolinos v. Kolts (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 635, 640.) Finally, an implied-in-fact contract can be alleged based on the promisor’s conduct. (Gomez v. Lincare, Inc. (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 508, 525.) 

 

“ ‘ “Where an agent is duly constituted and names his principal and contracts in his name and does not exceed his authority, the principal is responsible and not the agent.' [P] This view has been adopted in California. Where the signature as agent and not as a principal appears on the face of the contract, the principal is liable and not the agent. [Citations.]” ’ ” (Filippo Industries, Inc. v. Sun Ins. Co. (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 1429, 1442.)

 

Here, the Complaint, in pertinent part, states:

 

At all pertinent times, VILLAREAL and MANGABAT were senior level managing personnel at OJAX authorized to speak on behalf of the corporation and acting with the full authority and power of the corporation’s owner. Sometime in August 2023, Defendants VILLAREAL and MANGABAT entered into an oral contract with Plaintiff. Under the terms of the oral contract VILLAREAL and MANGABAT stated that all the stock of OJAX would be transferred to Plaintiff if she agreed to work for OJAX for a reasonable time as Administrator and Director of Nursing and Director of Patient Care and Services. In or about November 2023, Plaintiff went to work at OJAX as Administrator and eventually Director of Nursing and Director of Patient Care and Services. In or about August 2025, VILLAREAL and MANGABAT breached the oral contract by failing to transfer all of the stock of OJAX to Plaintiff. As a sole, direct, and proximate cause of the breach of oral contract. Plaintiff has been damaged in an amount greater than $200,000.00.

 

(Compl., ¶¶ 17-23.)

 

The Court finds these facts fail to plead liability of Mangabat as an individual on the alleged oral contract. While the Court acknowledges the oral contract is separately pled from the claim regarding the written contract that is also attached to the Complaint, the Court finds that the Complaint, as currently pled, does not plead adequate facts demonstrating the oral contract was made on behalf of Mangabat individually. Rather, the Complaint’s allegations plead an oral contract entered into by Mangabat as agent to the principal Ojax. (See Filippo Industries, Inc., supra, 74 Cal.App.4th at p. 1442.)

 

Thus, the Court finds the Complaint fails to state sufficient facts to plead the second cause of action against Mangabat. As such, the Demurrer is SUSTAINED as to the second cause of action.

 

Cause of Action No. 3 – Declaratory Relief

 

A complaint must show a proper subject for declaratory relief and an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to the rights or obligations of a party…. Declaratory relief operates prospectively—a remedy ‘to be used in the interests of preventive justice, to declare rights rather than execute them.’ [Citation.] A proper subject includes a declaration of one’s rights and duties under a contract. (§ 1060.) A controversy is ripe when it has reached, but has not passed, the point that the facts sufficiently have congealed to permit the court to issue a useful decision. [Citation.] The purpose of the declaration is to allow the parties to shape their conduct to avoid a breach…. There is no basis for declaratory relief where only past wrongs are involved.

 

(Cordoba Corp. v. City of Industry (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 145, 156-157.)

 

Here, the Complaint states that “an actual and justiciable controversy has arisen between Plaintiff and Defendants in that Plaintiff contends she is sole owner of OJAX and Defendants contend Plaintiff has no interest in OJAX. The controversy cannot be resolved without judicial intervention. Plaintiff has sought or is seeking all legal remedies but to no avail and equity may be the only remedy available to Plaintiff. As such Plaintiff seeks a Court declaration that she is sole owner of the stock of OJAX.” (Compl., ¶¶ 24-28.)

 

However, the Court finds, especially in light of the Court’s ruling on the second cause of action as to Mangabat, that the Complaint fails to state facts demonstrating an actual controversy exists between Mangabat and Plaintiff. The Complaint fails to allege any facts demonstrating that Mangabat owns OJAX stock or is the individual capable of making Plaintiff the sole owner of OJAX stock.

 

Thus, the Demurrer is SUSTAINED to the third cause of action so as it applies to Mangabat.

 

IV.           Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Defendant Marilyn Mangabat’s Demurrer is SUSTAINED WITH 30 DAYS’ LEAVE TO AMEND as to the second and third causes of action against Mangabat.

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice.
