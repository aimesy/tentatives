MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S DEPOSITION

 

Motion filed on August 6, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant, Kent Tanaka

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Armando Arturo Aragon

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Defendant seeks an order compelling Plaintiff to appear for deposition and $725 in monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel.

RULING: Granted in part. Monetary sanctions awarded in the reduced amount of $380 against Plaintiff only.

 

BACKGROUND

On July 18, 2023, Armando Arturo Aragon (Plaintiff) filed the Complaint against Kathleen Tanaka and Kent Tanaka (Defendant), alleging negligence and negligence per se arising from a July 19, 2021 motor vehicle incident. On August 6, 2026, Defendant filed this motion with the supporting declaration of Christopher B. Townsley and Exhibits A through C. No timely opposition was filed. On September 16, 2026, Defendant filed a Notice of Non-Opposition.

 

DISCUSSION

Deposition

 

A party may move to compel attendance and testimony when a deponent fails to appear or proceed after service of a deposition notice without having served a valid objection under section 2025.410. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).) The motion must include a meet-and-confer declaration or, where applicable, a declaration showing inquiry about the nonappearance. (Id., subd. (b)(2).)

 

Here, Defendant made several attempts to take Plaintiff’s deposition. (Townsley Decl. ¶ 2.) After Plaintiff did not appear for a February 23, 2026 deposition, Defendant noticed the deposition for April 7, 2026. (Ibid.) Plaintiff’s counsel objected to that date because of a trial conflict and offered April 29, 2026 as an available date. (Id., Ex. A.) Defendant then served a Second Amended Notice setting Plaintiff’s remote deposition for April 29, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. (Id., Ex. B.) On April 27, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel cancelled the agreed deposition and did not provide another date. (Id. ¶ 2.) Defendant thereafter sought a new firm date and, on July 6, 2026, sent a final meet-and-confer request advising that a motion to compel would follow if no date was provided. (Id., Ex. C.) Plaintiff’s counsel did not respond. (Id. ¶ 2.) On the record presented, Plaintiff did not serve a valid objection to the April 29 deposition notice or seek a protective order. Defendant has established grounds to compel Plaintiff’s deposition under section 2025.450, subdivision (a), and the record demonstrates sufficient meet-and-confer efforts under subdivision (b)(2).

Monetary Sanctions

 

If a motion to compel attendance is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the person subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).)

 

Here, Defendant requests $725 in sanctions. Counsel bills at $160 per hour and claims 2.5 hours preparing the motion, additional time associated with preparing for and appearing at the hearing, a $60 filing fee, and a $105 court reporter appearance fee. (Townsley Decl. ¶ 5.) The Court finds the $160 hourly rate reasonable but reduces the compensable attorney time to one hour for preparation and one hour for the hearing. The Court declines to award the $105 court reporter fee because the declaration does not sufficiently identify or substantiate that expense. No opposition was filed, thus Plaintiff has made no showing of substantial justification or other circumstances making sanctions unjust. Sanctions are awarded against Plaintiff in the reduced amount of $380, consisting of two hours at $160 per hour plus the $60 filing fee.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted in part. Plaintiff shall appear for deposition within 30 days after service of notice of this ruling. The parties shall meet and confer regarding the date. If they cannot agree, Defendant shall select a date within that period and serve an amended deposition notice stating the date, time, and manner of appearance.

 

Plaintiff shall pay Defendant $380 in monetary sanctions within 30 days after service of notice of this ruling. The request for monetary sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel is denied.

 

Case Number: 26CHCV01345    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: F46

Dept. F-46

Case Number: 26CHCV01345

 

PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE

 

Motion filed on July 7, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiffs Judith M. Brown-Williams and Alvin E. Williams

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant State Farm General Insurance Company

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Plaintiffs seek further responses and production in response to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One.

RULING: Denied.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On March 27, 2026, Plaintiffs Judith M. Brown-Williams and Alvin E. Williams (Plaintiffs) filed the Complaint against Defendant State Farm General Insurance Company (State Farm), alleging breach of contract and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

 

State Farm filed an Answer on May 6, 2026 and served verified responses to Plaintiffs’ Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, on June 9, 2026. The responses were verified by a State Farm Team Manager.

 

Plaintiffs filed the Motion on July 7, 2026. State Farm filed an Opposition and responsive separate statement on July 28, 2026. Plaintiffs filed a Reply on August 4, 2026.

 

On August 10, 2026, the Court continued the Motion to September 22, 2026 and ordered the parties to meet and confer concerning the proposed protective order and any remaining issues. The parties filed a Joint Status Report on September 15, 2026.

 

DISCUSSION

A party may move to compel a further response where a statement of compliance is incomplete, a representation of inability to comply is inadequate or incomplete, or an objection is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).) The moving party must also show good cause for the discovery sought. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).)

 

Following the Court-ordered meet and confer, the Joint Status Report identifies one remaining discovery request in dispute. Request for Production No. 4 seeks: “ALL ADJUSTER NOTES, LOGS, LOGS, DIARIES, OR REPORTS CONCERNING THE CLAIM.” (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 5.)

 

State Farm’s verified response to Request for Production No. 4 states:

 

“State Farm does not maintain a physical file folder with respect to most claims. Information regarding this claim is created, stored, managed and accessed primarily in and through the Enterprise Claims System (‘ECS’), a proprietary web-based system used by State Farm claims associates. ‘Claim File’ has a distinct meaning to State Farm based upon the portion of its Operation Guide that provides instructions on what to retrieve from ECS to generate a Claim File for production outside the ECS environment. State Farm will produce its Claim File (SF.Brown-Williams_J 000001-005684) as it does in the usual course of business pursuant to its Operation Guide 825-100. State Farm objects that the request seeks information protected by the attorney client privilege and/or attorney work product doctrine and is withholding portions of its Claim File that contain information protected by the attorney client privilege and/or is work product. State Farm will also produce the policy in effect at the time of the subject claim.

 

“To the extent that this request seeks production of information other than the ‘Claim File’ as described above, State Farm objects to this request as overbroad, vague, ambiguous, burdensome, and as seeking information that is neither relevant nor reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence. Further, the documents sought may also violate the attorney-client privilege and work product doctrine.”

 

Plaintiffs contend State Farm’s response does not establish that every responsive adjuster note, log, diary, and report has been produced because State Farm defines the Claim File by reference to its internal Operation Guide. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 5.) Plaintiffs seek confirmation that all responsive materials have been produced, including materials outside State Farm’s internally defined Claim File. (Jt. Status Rpt. at pp. 6-7.)

 

State Farm states that documents constituting its logs, diaries, and notes were produced at SF.BROWN-WILLIAMS_J000001 through J000692. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 7.) State Farm states that “[t]he log notes of State Farm’s claims personnel were all produced” at J000031 through J000159. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 7.) State Farm identifies additional responsive claims-handler notes at J000387 through J000413. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 7.) State Farm further states that reports by claims personnel are contained within the File History portion of the Claim File. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 8.)

 

Plaintiffs acknowledge that, after receiving State Farm’s correlation chart, they located most of the documents previously identified as missing and no longer contend those materials were omitted. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 5.)

 

Plaintiffs’ Motion seeks the complete electronic claim file and claim activity history, including claim notes, diary entries, audit trails, and related records. (Mot. at pp. 2, 10.) Plaintiffs’ Reply additionally questions archived or historical claim data, metadata, audit trails, system history, and other electronically stored information outside the PDF export. (Reply at pp. 5-6.) Request for Production No. 4, however, seeks adjuster notes, logs, diaries, and reports concerning the claim. (State Farm Resp. to RFP, Set One, No. 4.) The Court does not construe Request for Production No. 4 to encompass the broader categories of electronically stored information identified in the Motion and Reply.

 

 

As to the categories actually requested, State Farm has identified where the responsive notes and reports were produced and represents that all claims-personnel log notes were produced. (Jt. Status Rpt. at pp. 7-8.) Plaintiffs identify no remaining deficiency in State Farm’s response or production as to those categories. Accordingly, Plaintiffs have not established a basis under section 2031.310 for a further response or further production to Request for Production No. 4.

 

State Farm further states, as to Request for Production No. 4, that “[t]here are no other responsive, non-privileged documents that can be produced absent a stipulation for a protective order entered by the Court.” (State Farm Resp. Separate Statement at p. 14.) In the Joint Status Report, however, State Farm identifies the materials subject to the proposed protective order as portions of its claims-handling guidelines. (Jt. Status Rpt. at pp. 3-4.)

 

The parties did not agree upon a protective order. (Jt. Status Rpt. at pp. 2-4.) No motion for a protective order is before the Court. The Court therefore does not determine the terms of any protective order.

 

State Farm served a privilege log on July 9, 2026. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 6.) Plaintiffs raise particularized objections to entries in that log in the Joint Status Report. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 6.) State Farm states those particular objections were not raised during the parties’ court-ordered meet and confer. (Jt. Status Rpt. at p. 8.) The Court does not reach those privilege-log issues here.

 

CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One is DENIED.
