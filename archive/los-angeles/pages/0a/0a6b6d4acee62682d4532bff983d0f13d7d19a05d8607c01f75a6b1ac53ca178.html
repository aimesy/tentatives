DEPARTMENT 415 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 18STCV01407    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 415

  TENTATIVE RULING

 

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

August 11, 2026

CASE NUMBER

18STCV01407

MOTION 

Motion to Dismiss

MOVING PARTIES

Defendant City of Los Angeles, joined by Defendants Curtis Lepore and Rachel Brinck

OPPOSING PARTIES

Plaintiffs David Ortiz and Yuko Ortiz

 

MOTION

Defendant City of Los Angeles (“Defendant”) moves to dismiss the complaint of Plaintiffs David Ortiz and Yuko Ortiz (“Plaintiffs”) for delay in prosecution.  Plaintiffs oppose the motion.

DISCUSSION

While Defendant refers to the five-year rule in its memorandum of points and authorities, Defendant’s notice of motion cites Code of Civil Procedure section 583.130 (section 583.130) as the basis for the relief they seek.  The Court cannot grant relief based on authority Defendant fails to cite in the notice of motion, nor can it grant relief on grounds other than those set forth in the notice of motion.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1010 [“Notices must be in writing, and the notice of a motion, other than for a new trial, must state when, and the grounds upon which it will be made, and the papers, if any, upon which it is to be based”]; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1110(a) [“A notice of motion must state in the opening paragraph the nature of the order being sought and the grounds for issuance of the order”]; People v. American Surety Insurance Co. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 719, 726 [court concluded notice of motion made no reference to the fact of what the movant sought or the grounds for such relief, as required by rule 311(a) of the California Rules of Court, the predecessor to Rules of Court, rule 3.1110(a)].)  The significance of this principle is even more relevant in this instance in which self-represented litigants are involved, and they may specifically rely on the authority advanced in the motion. The Court does not find co-Defendants’ joinder in Defendant’s motion cured the deficiency.

Accordingly, the Court considers the motion as a motion to dismiss pursuant to section 583.130.

ANALYSIS

Section 583.130 requires a plaintiff to  proceed with “reasonable diligence” in prosecuting an action, but “all parties shall cooperate” in bringing the action to trial.  “[T]he policy favoring trial or other disposition of an action on the merits are generally to be preferred over the policy that requires dismissal for failure to proceed with reasonable diligence in the prosecution of an action in construing the provisions of this chapter [Dismissal for Delay in Prosecution].”  Therefore, Court must favor the policy of trial on the merits over the policy requiring dismissal for delay in prosecution.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.130; see Freedman v. Pacific Gas & Electric Co. (1987) 196 Cal.App.3d 696, 703 [same].) 

Plaintiffs filed their complaint in this matter on October 19, 2018.   Including times in which this case was subject to stays, it has been pending for more than seven years and ten months, and Plaintiffs have not yet brought the matter to trial.  However, the Court must consider the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Plaintiffs’ prosecution of this action.  (See California Rules of Court, Emergency Rule No. 10.)  In that regard, Los Angeles County Superior Courts were unable to try cases from March 10, 2020 to January 4, 2021.  However, it was impossible to conduct jury trials in this Department until May 11, 2021, primarily because this Court could not satisfy the social distancing requirements. 

Moreover, under Rules of Court, rule 3.1342(e), this Court must factor in matters including, but not limited to, the parties’ diligence in pursuing and conducting pretrial proceedings, the various extensions of time and other delays attributable to either party, the condition of the Court’s calendar, what best serves the interests of justice, and the apparent uncertainty of whether it was impossible and impractical to proceed to trial because of pending appellate and writ practice and the parties’ lack of clarity concerning if and when the matter was stayed for any reason.  The Court is obligated to consider such issues and any other fact or circumstance relevant to a fair determination of the issue before it in light of section 583.130’s general principles and ideals discussed above.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Consequently, the Court does not consider Plaintiffs’ failure to bring this matter to trial so unreasonable as to outweigh the policy of conducting a trial on the merits.  For that reason, in applying section 583.130, the Court denies the motion.

Defendant is ordered to provide notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.

 


Case Number: 21STCV03737    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 415

 

   TENTATIVE RULING

 

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

August 11, 2026

CASE NUMBER

21STCV03737, related to 21STCV02841

MOTION

Motion to Quash Subpoena

MOVING PARTY

Defendant, Cross-Complainant, and Cross-Defendant Paragon Resources Incorporated

OPPOSING PARTY

Defendant and Cross-Defendant Julian Ledesma

 

MOTION

              Defendant, Cross-Complainant, and Cross-Defendant Paragon Resources Incorporated (“Paragon”) moves to quash a deposition notice Defendant and Cross-Defendant Julian Ledesma (“Ledesma”) served for the deposition of Deponent Ken Blackman (“Deponent”).  Ledesma opposes the motion.

ANALYSIS

Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.420[1] provides, “The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any . . . deponent . . . from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense . . . .”

Paragon argues the deposition notice does not comply with section 2025.220 on several grounds.  Pursuant to that discovery statute, a notice of deposition must state the date, time, and place where the deposition will take place.  (§ 2025.220, subd. (a)(1).)  Paragon argues the deposition notice is defective because it does not specify an address where the deposition will take place.  However, the deposition notice specifies the deposition will be via Zoom.  As such, no physical address is required.

Paragon contends the deposition notice is defective because it does not include disclosure of the existence of a contractual relationship between Ledesma and the deposition officer or entity providing the services of the deposition officer.  (§ 2025.220, subd. (a)(8).)  However, if no relationship exists, no disclosure is necessary.

Paragon maintains the deposition notice is improperly drafted under section 2025.230.  If a deponent is a corporation or other entity, the deposition notice must describe with “reasonable particularity” the matters on which questions will be asked so the deponent can designate the “most qualified” persons to testify on its behalf.  (§ 2025.230.)  In this instance, however,  Ledesma noticed the deposition of Deponent, who is not an entity.  As such, the Court agrees Ledesma’s reference in the notice to section 2025.230 is improper.  In any event, Paragon cites no authority under which Deponent may avoid being deposed because of this erroneous reference, when the deposition notice specifies Ledesma intends Deponent to appear and testify as an individual.  As such, the deposition notice is proper notwithstanding the reference to section 2025.230.

Paragon also argues various statements in the deposition notice are improper.  Notwithstanding such statements, the notice suffices to compel Deponent to appear and to testify at the deposition.  Deponent’s appearance will not constitute a stipulation to any terms inserted in the deposition notice which require waiver of any valid objections or to payment of fees to which Ledesma would not otherwise be entitled.

The Court finds Paragon filed this motion without substantial justification such that sanctions are warranted.  (§ 2025.420, subd. (h).)  The Court imposes sanctions against Paragon and its counsel of record, Pacific Business Law, APC, in the amount of $1,000, which represents four hours of attorney time to prepare the opposition and attend the hearing at $250 per hour.  Paragon and its counsel of record, jointly and severally, are to pay sanctions in the amount of $1,000 to Ledesma, by and through counsel, within 30 days of the notice of this order.

Ledesma is to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.


[1]All statutory references hereafter are to the Code of Civil Procedure, unless otherwise indicated.


Case Number: 24STCV02870    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 415

   TENTATIVE RULING

 

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

August 11, 2026

CASE NUMBER

24STCV02870

MOTIONS 

Motions to Compel Discovery Responses

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Yilip International Co., Ltd.

OPPOSING PARTY

None

 

MOTIONS

            Plaintiff Yilip International Co., Ltd. (“Plaintiff”) moves to compel responses from Defendants Brian Kim (“Kim”) and the Choice Produce, Inc. (“Choice Produce, collectively, “Defendants”) to Form Interrogatories, set two (“FROG”), Special Interrogatories, set two (“SROG”), and Requests for Production, set two (“RPD”).  Plaintiff also moves to deem admitted matters specified in Requests for Admissions, set two (“RFA”).

ANALYSIS

In its March 11, 2026 order, the Court granted the motion for withdrawal of Defendants’ former counsel.  Therefore, as Choice Produce is an entity, it cannot appear in this action except through counsel.  (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1145; Gamet v. Blanchard (2001) 91 Cal. App. 4th 1276, 1284.)  The Court has provided Choice Produce with the opportunity to retain new counsel but, as of yet Choice Produce has not done so.  Accordingly, at the hearing of this matter, the Court will continue the motions for hearing as to Choice Produce.  The Court will also schedule an order to show cause why the Court should not strike Choice Produce’s answer to the operative complaint filed on April 4, 2024 on the same date.  If Choice Produce retains counsel prior to that hearing, the Court will consider the motions.  If the Court strikes Choice Produce’s answer, Plaintiff must proceed against Choice Produce in default. 

Plaintiff served the written discovery on Kim on March 30, 2026 by mail.  Kim’s responses were thus due by May 4, 2026.   As of the filing date of these motions, Plaintiff has not received responses from Kim.  Accordingly, the motions to compel responses to the FROG, SROG, and RPD are granted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300.  Kim is ordered to serve responses to Plaintiff’s discovery requests without objections within 30 days of service of this order.

Plaintiff also moves to deem admitted the matters specified in the RFA. When a party fails to respond to requests for admissions, the propounding party may move for an order the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction.  (Code of Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).)  The Court “shall” grant a motion to deem admitted the matters specified in the requests for admissions, “unless it finds that the party to whom the requests for admission have been directed has served, before the hearing on the motion, a proposed response to the requests for admission that is in substantial compliance with Section 2033.220.”  (Code of Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)  As Kim has not responded to the RFA, the Court grants the motion to deem admitted matters specified in the RFA.

Plaintiff seeks sanctions in connection with the motion.  The Court concludes Kim’s failure to timely respond to the discovery constitutes an abuse of the discovery process and awards sanctions.  The Court imposes sanctions against Kim in the amount of $1,290, which represents four hours of attorney time to prepare the motions and attend the hearing at $350 per hour, plus four filing fees.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff’s motions to compel Kim’s responses to the FROG, SROG, and RPD are granted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300.  Kim is ordered to serve verified responses, without objections, within 30 days of notice of this order.

Kim is deemed to have admitted matters specified in the RFA.

Kim is to pay sanctions in the amount of $1,290 to Plaintiff, by and through counsel, within 30 days of notice of this order.

Plaintiff is ordered to provide notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.
