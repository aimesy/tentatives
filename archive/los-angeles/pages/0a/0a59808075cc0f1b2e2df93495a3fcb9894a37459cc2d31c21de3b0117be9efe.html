PLAINTIFF SHILOH PARKER’S FIRST AMENDED MOTION TO STRIKE AND TAX COSTS

 

Background

Plaintiff Shiloh Parker (“Plaintiff”) filed this action on March 27, 2024 against Defendants Haley Freiberger and Kyle Freiberger (jointly, the “Freibergers”). The complaint alleges causes of action for (1) “fraud by intentional misrepresentation,” (2) “fraud by negligent misrepresentation,” (3) “breach of good faith and fair dealing covenant,” (4) violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200 et seq., and (5) breach of oral contract.[1]

            On October 22, 2024, “the Court grant[ed] the Freibergers’ motion to quash on the grounds that the Court lacks personal jurisdiction over the Freibergers.” (Order 10/22/24, 15:20-21.)

            Thereafter, on December 3, 2024, the Court signed the proposed judgment submitted by the Freibergers on October 31, 2024. (See Order 12/3/24, 13:3-4; Judgment 12/3/24.)

            On December 18, 2024, the Freibergers filed a memorandum of costs.

            On December 23, 2024, Plaintiff filed a motion to strike and tax costs and a request for sanctions. The Court did not previously hear this motion.

            Thereafter, on December 16, 2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended motion to strike and tax costs.

On July 2, 2026, the Freibergers, as Specially Appearing Defendants, filed a notice “withdraw[ing] their Memorandum of Costs filed on December 18, 2024, which sought costs in the amount of $2,447.63.” (Withdrawal, 2:2-3.) Plaintiff did not reply.

            Discussion

            The Freibergers’ notice of withdrawal explains that because they “no longer seek recovery of the costs claimed in that Memorandum of Costs, Plaintiff Shiloh Parker's Motion to Strike and Tax Costs and Request for Sanctions, currently set for hearing on September 23, 2026, is moot. Specially Appearing Defendants respectfully request that the Court vacate the September 23, 2026 hearing or deny the motion as moot.” (Withdrawal, 2:4-8.) The Freibergers clarify that “[t]his withdrawal is limited to the Memorandum of Costs filed on December 18, 2024, and does not waive any rights Specially Appearing Defendants may have to seek appellate costs, sanctions, attorneys’ fees, or any other relief not expressly withdrawn herein.” (Withdrawal, 2:9-11.)

            As a preliminary matter, the Court notes that Plaintiff’s notice of motion for the amended motion also seeks “sanctions against Defendants and Defendants’ attorney for filing this frivolous filing.” (First Am. Notice of Mot., 2:17-18.) However, Plaintiff’s memorandum in support does not appear to discuss or elaborate on this point. Thus, the only relief Plaintiff seeks is to tax or strike the Freibergers’ memorandum of costs. Without a memorandum of costs in which the Freibergers seek costs, no controversy remains, and the relief Plaintiff seeks is unavailable. Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to strike or tax costs is moot, and the Court denies the motion.

 

 

Conclusion

In light of the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to strike or tax costs is DENIED as MOOT.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this Order.¿¿

DEFENDANT FARMERS INSURANCE EXCHANGE’s DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF’S COMPLAINT

           

            Background

On March 18, 2026, Plaintiff George Ajrab (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Farmers Insurance Exchange, dba Farmers Insurance, alleging one cause of action for breach of contract.

Defendant Farmers Insurance Exchange (“Farmers”) now demurs to Plaintiff’s complaint. Plaintiff did not oppose. Farmers filed a “Reply Brief in response to Plaintiff George Ajrab’s (‘Plaintiff’) failure to submit Opposition to Defendant’s Demurrer to Plaintiff’s Complaint for Breach of Contract.” (Reply, 1:21-22.)

Legal Standard

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. ((Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) In testing the sufficiency of the complaint, the court assumes the truth of properly pleaded factual allegations, facts reasonably inferred from those expressly pleaded, and judicially noticed matters. ((Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the¿evidence or other extrinsic matters.¿Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.” ((SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) Accordingly, “[w]hether the plaintiff will be able to prove the pleaded facts is irrelevant to ruling upon the demurrer.” ((Stevens v. Superior Court (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 605, 609-610.)

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivisions (e) and (f), a demurrer may be filed if the pleading is uncertain or does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. ((Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment, otherwise, it is abuse of discretion. ((Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)

Discussion

A.    Allegations of the Complaint

Plaintiff’s Complaint alleges that on or about 2024, 2025, and 2026, Plaintiff and Farmers entered into written agreements with the essential terms as follows: “Plaintiff Ajrab entered into a contract with defendant to pay damages to plaintiff’s property for landlord protection regarding covering 8 apartment units he owned in San Bernardino, CA. Defendant Farmers Insurance agreed to pay plaintiff for damages to his property covered by the policy of insurance (Policy No.: 5004958350). Defendant breached the contract by failing to pay plaintiff’s property damages.” (Compl., p. 3, BC-1, subd. a.) Then, on or about 2024, 2025, and 2026, Farmers breached the agreement by “Defendants failed to pay damages for loss of rental income, deductibles, depreciation, copper wires, and air conditioning.” (Compl., p. 3, BC-2.) Plaintiff suffered damages due to this breach as follows: “Air conditioning - $13,876; Deductible - $10k; Depreciation - $10k; Rental Income - $48k; Copper Wire - $1,500.” (Compl., p. 3, BC-4.)

 

B.     Breach of Contract

Farmers demurs to Plaintiff’s Complaint because “Plaintiff not only failed to attach the Policy to the Complaint, but he also failed to plead its pertinent terms so as to make out a claim for breach of contract. California law requires a plaintiff suing on a written policy to either attach the policy or plead the material terms. Plaintiff did neither. As Plaintiff declined to attach a copy of the Policy to the Complaint, the Court is warranted in sustaining the Demurrer.” (Dem., 4:15-19.)

To state a cause of action for breach of contract, a plaintiff must be able to establish “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) the resulting damages to the plaintiff.” ((Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.) Under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (g), a demurrer is proper when “an action founded upon a contract, it cannot be ascertained from the pleading whether the contract is written, is oral, or is implied by conduct.”

Farmers contends that Plaintiff’s failure to attach the policy requires dismissal of the Complaint for breach of contract. Farmers’ counsel’s declaration in support explains that “Defendant Farmers Insurance Exchange did not issue any policy to Plaintiff with the policy number identified in the Complaint.” (Cordelli Decl., ¶ 2.) Farmers asserts that “Defendant attempted to avoid motion practice on this issue by requesting that Plaintiff voluntarily attach a complete copy of the Policy to the Complaint via amendment. Such an action would allow Plaintiff to amend the pleading to state a viable claim against the actual issuing insurer, who could be substituted into the case as a ‘Doe’ Defendant. However, Plaintiff declined to take this simple step despite Defendant’s counsel’s multiple efforts to resolve the issues in question without the need to file a Demurrer. Accordingly, the Court should now sustain the Demurrer to the Plaintiff’s Complaint and require him, at a minimum, to attach the Policy to the Complaint. If, as Defendant contends, the Policy in question was not issued by Farmers, then it will be entitled to a dismissal with prejudice, and Plaintiff will be free to pursue the insurer actually issuing the Policy identified in the Complaint.” (Dem., 8:6-14.)

As the court in Twaite v. Allstate Ins. Co. (1989) 216 Cal.App.3d 239, 252, noted, “[t]o state a cause of action for breach of contract, it is absolutely essential to plead the terms of the contract either in haec verba or according to legal effect.” Although Plaintiff’s Complaint alleges the general elements of a breach of contract cause of action, Plaintiff fails to allege the terms of the policy, what specific duties Farmers owed Plaintiff, or attach the policy for the purported breach. Without these, Plaintiff’s breach of contract cause of action is insufficient and warrants sustaining Farmers’ demurrer. Additionally, Farmers contends that Plaintiff does not have a policy with them, which requires dismissal of the Complaint. However, the Court is limited to the four corners of the Complaint and judicially noticed matters, so sustaining the demurrer without leave to amend on the basis of Farmers as an improper party is insufficiently supported. The Court sustains the demurrer with leave to amend because, from the allegations of the Complaint, Plaintiff’s defect is curable.

Conclusion

In light of the foregoing, Farmers’ demurrer to the first cause of action is SUSTAINED, with leave to amend. Plaintiff is ordered to file and serve an amended complaint, if any, within 20 days of the date of this Order. If no amended complaint is filed within 20 days, the Court orders Farmers to file and serve a proposed judgment of dismissal within 30 days of the date of this Order.

Farmers is ordered to give notice of this Order.
