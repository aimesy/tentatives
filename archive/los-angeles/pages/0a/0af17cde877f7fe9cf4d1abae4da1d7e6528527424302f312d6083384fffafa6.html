Case Number: 24STLC06939    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Kinecta Federal Credit Union’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees is continued to 08/27/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 in the Spring Street Courthouse. By 07/30/2026, Plaintiff is to file and serve a supplemental declaration that provides evidence in support of the lodestar for the attorney’s fees award. By 08/13/2026, Defendant is to file and serve a supplemental opposition.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Kinecta Federal Credit Union (“Plaintiff”) brought this action for breach of contract against Defendant Fernando Ponce (“Defendant”) on September 24, 2024. Defendant filed an Answer on November 14, 2024. The action came for trial on March 24, 2026, at which time the Court found in favor of Plaintiff. (Minute Order 03/24/26.) Two days later, Plaintiff filed a Memorandum of Costs. On April 10, 2026, the Court entered judgment and gave notice to both parties.

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Attorney’s Fees on April 22, 2026. Defendant filed an opposition on June 24, 2026, and Plaintiff replied on June 26, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Timing of Motion

 

Plaintiff brings the instant Motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1032, 1033.5 and Civil Code section 1717. A prevailing party is entitled to recover costs, including attorneys’ fees when authorized by contract, statute, or law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4); § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10).) A motion for attorney’s fees must be filed and served within the time for filing a notice of appeal under Cal. Rules of Court Rule 8.822. (Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1702(a).) Cal. Rules of Court Rule 8.822 states that an attorneys’ fees motion must be filed within the earlier of (1) 30 days after the trial court clerk serves the moving party with notice of entry of judgment; (2) 30 days after the moving party serves or is served by a party with a document entitled “Notice of Entry” of judgment or a file-endorsed copy of the judgment, accompanied by proof of service; or (3) 90 days after entry of judgment. (Cal. Rules of Court 8.822(1).) Here, the Motion was timely filed within 30 days of entry of judgment.

 

Entitlement to Attorney’s Fees as Prevailing Party

 

Plaintiff moves for attorney’s fees pursuant to the parties’ relevant contract provision, which states in relevant part: “You agree to pay all costs of collecting the amount you owe under this Agreement, including court and reasonable attorney fees.” (Motion, Marquez Decl., Exh. 1, p. 2, “Collection Costs”.) This is reiterated in the Credit Card Account Opening Disclosure that was sent to Defendant on May 28, 2022. (Id. at Exh. 2, p. 3.) Defendant argues in opposition that this provision cannot be enforced under Civil Code section 1717 because it is buried and boilerplate and not clearly stated. The provision on page 2, however, is not “buried”; it is clearly set apart from the other provisions. Nor does the opposition cite any authority for the proposition that a boilerplate fees provision cannot be enforced. (Opp., p. 3:24-26.)

 

Pursuant to this provision, as the party in whose favor judgment has been entered, the Court finds Plaintiff is entitled to recover attorney’s fees incurred in this action. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4).)

 

Calculation of Attorney’s Fees Amount

 

Plaintiff’s Motion does not address the applicable standard for determining the attorney’s fees award, which the Court now articulates. The moving party bears the burden of proof with respect to attorney’s fees. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (c)(5)(A).) Specifically, a party seeking fees has the burden of showing the fees incurred are “allowable,” are “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation,” and are “reasonable in amount.”  (Levy v. Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 807, 816.)

 

The Court’s objective is to award attorney’s fees at the fair market value based on the particular action. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132.) “The reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work.” (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.) “‘[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate . . . .’” (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1134.)  The lodestar method is based on the factors, as relevant to the particular case: “(1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award.”  (Id. at 1132.) “The ‘‘experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court, and while his judgment is of course subject to review, it will not be disturbed unless the appellate court is convinced that it is clearly wrong.’’” (Id.) A negative modifier was appropriate when duplicative work had been performed. (Thayer v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 819.)

 

The Motion seeks $14,665.14 in attorney’s fees based on time spent by attorneys and other, unidentified timekeepers. According to the supporting declaration, this was for time spent on the Complaint, to review the Answer, drafting written discovery, setting and preparing for a deposition, preparing for and attending trial, and drafting the instant motion. (Motion, Rocha Decl., ¶5 and Exh. A.) However, the attached billing records also reference drafting a motion for summary judgment before Plaintiff even propounded discovery, which the Court does not find reasonably necessary. (Id. at Exh. A, 03/12/25 and 05/01/25 entries). For this work, Plaintiff’s counsel billed $1,938.00. The other tasks mentioned in the attorney declaration appear reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation.

 

Regarding the reasonableness of the time spent, the Motion provides a single, conclusory paragraph. (Motion, p. 2:16-20.) The supporting declaration states that the two attorneys billed $315.00 an hour, and other timekeepers billed flat rates for their tasks. (Motion, Rocha Decl., ¶¶3, 8-9.) It is not explained whether these other timekeepers are attorneys, paralegals, support staff, or something else. (Ibid.) A copy of the billing entries for this action is attached, but it is not possible to determine from the entries why the amount billed for any given task was assigned, or reasonable. (See id. at Exh. A.) The entries do not correlate to a specific hourly rate, nor does the Motion explain how the flat rates were determined for any given task.  At least one entry for .50 hours correlates to an hourly rate of $340, which is higher than the billing rate for either attorney.  Likewise, another entry reflects a charge of $325.00 for one hour.  Since such a significant portion of the billing entries are for flat-rate tasks, the Court is unable to calculate a lodestar, which looks to the number of hours reasonably billed by the hourly rate. The flat-rate entries include neither metric.

 

Therefore, Plaintiff has failed to demonstrate that the amount of attorney’s fees billed in this action was “reasonable in amount” nor how the Court can determine the lodestar figure from the evidence provided.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Kinecta Federal Credit Union’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees is continued to 08/27/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 in the Spring Street Courthouse. By 07/30/2026, Plaintiff is to file and serve a supplemental declaration that provides evidence in support of the lodestar for the attorney’s fees award. By 08/13/2026, Defendant is to file and serve a supplemental opposition.

Case Number: 25STLC00559    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Infinity Insurance Company’s Motion to Continue Trial Date and All Related Cut-Off Dates is denied.

 

Trial scheduled for 07/23/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse is vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Entry of Default is scheduled for 07/23/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On January 23, 2025, Plaintiff Infinity Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed this action for automobile subrogation against Defendant Transdev Services (“Defendant”). Plaintiff filed Proof of Personal Service on April 28, 2026.

 

On May 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Continue Trial Date and All Related Cut-Off Dates. Plaintiff served Defendant with the Motion by first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date. 

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for a trial continuance to January 2027 or later, pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court rule 3.1332, which provides “[a]lthough continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The Court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (c).) Grounds for a continuance include the unavailability of trial counsel because of death, illness, or other excusable circumstances. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (c)(3).)

 

The court may look to the following factors in determining whether a trial continuance is warranted: (1) proximity of the trial date; (2) whether there was any previous continuance of trial due to any party; (3) the length of the continuance requested; (4) the availability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion; (5) the prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; and (6) whether trial counsel is engaged in another trial.  (See generally, Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (d)(1)-(11).) Additional factors for the court to consider include: a party’s excused inability to obtain essential testimony, documents, or other material evidence despite diligent efforts; whether all parties have stipulated to a continuance; and any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subds. (c), (d).)

 

Plaintiff argues that good cause exists for a continuance of the trial date, which is currently set for July 23, 2026, based on new counsel taking over the case at the end of April 2026. (Motion, Lee Decl., ¶4.)  The Court does not find good cause to continue the trial date given that Defendant was served with the Summons and Complaint on April 28, 2026 and still has not filed a responsive pleading. (Id. at ¶¶5-6; Proof of Personal Service, filed 04/28/26, ¶5.) The posture of the case is no longer one proceeding to trial, but to default. Accordingly, the Court denies the Motion, vacates the trial date, and sets an Order to Show Cause regarding entry of default.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Infinity Insurance Company’s Motion to Continue Trial Date and All Related Cut-Off Dates is denied.

 

Trial scheduled for 07/23/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse is vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Entry of Default is scheduled for 07/23/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 25STLC04303    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff IPFS Corporation of California’s Motion for Entry of Judgment Pursuant to Settlement Agreement and General Release is granted. The proposed order for entry of judgment filed on 04/06/2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 30, 2025, Plaintiff IPFS Corporation of California (“Plaintiff”) filed a Complaint against Defendant Pacifica of the Valley Corporation dba Pacifica Hospital of the Valley (“Defendant”) for money paid and advanced. Plaintiff alleges, inter alia, that “[w]ithin four (4) years last past, Defendants and each of them, became indebted to Plaintiff in the sum of $14,683.28 for monies paid and advanced by Plaintiff on behalf of Defendants . . . .” (Compl., ¶ 6.) On July 23, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

 

Plaintiff filed and served a Motion for Entry of Judgment Pursuant to Settlement Agreement and General Release, which the Court denied without prejudice on April 6, 2026. (Minute Order, 04/06/26.) The same day, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Entry of Judgment Pursuant to Settlement Agreement and General Release. The Motion was served on the same day via first-class mail to Defendant through Attorney Michael Newborn, Esq. To date, no opposition has been filed.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion is brought under Code of Civil Procedure, section 664.6, which states in relevant part:

 

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If requested by the parties, the court may retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (a).) Prior to January 1, 2021, “parties” under section 664.6 meant the litigants themselves, not their attorneys.  (Levy v. Superior Court (1995) 10 Cal.4th 578, 586.) The current statute provides that “parties” includes “an attorney who represents the party” and an insurer’s agent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (b).) The settlement must include the signatures of the parties seeking to enforce the agreement, and against whom enforcement is sought. (J.B.B. Investment Partners, Ltd. v. Fair (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 974, 985.) The settlement agreement complies with the statutory requirements set forth above because it was signed by both parties. (Motion, Angelo Decl., Exh. A, p. 5.)

 

The settlement provides that Defendant would pay Plaintiff the total amount of $15,000.00 in three separate installment payments pursuant to the following payment schedule: (1) $5,000.00 on or before October 1, 2025; (2) $5,000.00 on or before November 1, 2025; and (3) $5,000.00 on or before December 1, 2025. (Id. at Exh. A, ¶ 3.) The declaration also states that Defendant is in default under the agreement and has not cured it, allowing judgment to “be entered in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant for the full amount of the DEBT, less credit for any payments made prior to default.” (Id. at ¶5 and Exh. A, ¶¶4, 12-13.) “Debt” is defined as $14,683.28 in principal, interest on said amount at the rate of 16.25% per annum from December 28, 2024, $1,600.00 contractual attorneys’ fees and $489.50 costs. (Id. at Exh. A, p. 1.) As of the filing of the Motion, Defendant has not paid any part of the settlement. (Id. at ¶11.) Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to judgment in the amount of $14,683.28 (principal damages); $3,637.21 (interest calculated at 16.25% per annum between 12/28/24 and 07/09/26); $1,600.00 (contractual attorneys’ fees); and $489.50 (costs). (Id. at ¶12.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff IPFS Corporation of California’s Motion for Entry of Judgment Pursuant to Settlement Agreement and General Release is granted. The proposed order for entry of judgment filed on 04/06/2026 is signed and entered this date.

Case Number: 25STLC09056    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  

           

Plaintiff Jeremi Jerry’s Motion to Compel Further Responses from Plaintiff to Defendant’s First Set of Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On December 3, 2025, Plaintiff Jeremi Jerry (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the instant action for breach of contract against Defendant Damien Romel Farmer (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer on January 27, 2026. On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses from Plaintiff to Defendant’s First Set of Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions. Plaintiff served Defendant with the Motion by first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an order compelling Defendant’s further responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One, and an award of monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,500.00, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.310.

 

Procedural Requirements

 

Notice of the motion to compel further must be given “within 45 days of service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or any specific later date to which the requesting party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (c).) This time is extended based on the manner of service; service by electronic mail extends the deadline by two court days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (a)(3)(B).) Defendant served responses to the Request for Production of Documents, Set One, on March 5, 2026. (Motion, Jerry Decl., ¶3.) The instant Motion, however, was not filed and served until May 28, 2026, which was 84 days later. The 45-day deadline is mandatory and jurisdictional, meaning the Court cannot grant the instant Motion. (Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1409.)

 

Second, California Rules of Court Rule 3.1345 requires all motions or responses involving further discovery to include a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345, subd. (a).) Alternatively, “the court may allow the moving party to submit a concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(3).) The Motion is also not accompanied by a separate statement, nor has the Court allowed Plaintiff to submit a concise outline of the dispute.

 

Finally, the motion must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1).) Plaintiff declares that he sent Defendant a meet and confer letter on May 10, 2026, but does not attach a copy for the Court’s consideration. (Motion, Jerry Decl., ¶5.) This is not sufficient to demonstrate a good faith meet and confer effort.

 

The Motion, therefore, fails to demonstrate Plaintiff’s compliance with any of the mandatory procedural requirements.


Conclusion


Plaintiff Jeremi Jerry’s Motion to Compel Further Responses from Plaintiff to Defendant’s First Set of Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions is denied.
