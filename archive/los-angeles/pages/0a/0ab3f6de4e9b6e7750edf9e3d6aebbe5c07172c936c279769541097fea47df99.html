Case Number: 24STLC02644    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings against Defendant Elvin Castellanos is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On April 12, 2026, Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed this action for automobile subrogation against Defendant Elvin Castellanos (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading and entry of default judgment, the parties stipulated to set aside the default judgment. Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint on November 6, 2026. On April 6, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Monetary Sanctions against Defendant. (Minute Order, 04/06/26.)

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings against Defendant on May 13, 2026. Defendant filed an opposition on July 23, 2026, and Plaintiff replied on July 28, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The standard for ruling on a motion for judgment on the pleadings is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer, that is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.  (Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.)  Where the moving party is the plaintiff, judgment on the pleadings may be granted on the basis that “that the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause or causes of action against the defendant and the answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense to the complaint.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(1)(A).) Matters which are subject to mandatory judicial notice may be treated as part of the complaint and may be considered without notice to the parties. Matters which are subject to permissive judicial notice must be specified in the notice of motion, the supporting points and authorities, or as the court otherwise permits. (Id.) The motion may not be supported by extrinsic evidence. (Barker v. Hull (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 221, 236.)

 

While a statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, et seq. must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration, there is no such requirement for a motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to the common law. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 439 [moving party must file declaration demonstrating an attempt to meet and confer in person or by telephone, at least five days before the date a motion for judgment on the pleadings is filed].) The instant Motion is supported by a meet and confer declaration. (Motion, Espinosa Decl., ¶¶1-2 and Exhibit 1.)

 

The instant Motion is accompanied by a request for judicial notice of the Answer to the Complaint, the Motion to Deem Admissions, and the Court’s Order granting the motion.  (RJN, ¶¶ 1-3, Exhs. 1-3.) The Court takes judicial notice of the contents of the Answer to the Complaint, as being the pleadings of the parties in this action, not for the truth of those matters. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d); Sosinsky v. Grant (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1548, 1560–1569.) To the extent set forth below, the Court takes judicial notice of the admissions pursuant to Cal. Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d). (Cal. Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d); see also Cloud v. Northrop Grumman Corp. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 995, 999; Evans v. California Trailer Court, Inc. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 540, 549) [holding that the court may take judicial notice of matters that cannot be reasonably controverted, including “admissions and concessions.”].)

 

The admissions deemed by Defendant do not support judgment on the pleadings. As relevant here, Defendant is deemed to have admitted that Defendant failed to drive with reasonable care, that Defendant’s driving was the sole cause of the accident with Plaintiff’s insured, that Defendant was 100% at fault in causing the collision, and, as a result of the collision, Defendant caused the Plaintiff’s insured to incur damages of at least $10,783.83, and that Plaintiff, the insurer, has been damaged in that sum, which it paid to its insured. (Motion, RJN, Exh. 2, at Nos. 2, 3, 5, 7-8.) Plaintiff argues that these deemed admissions should be treated as “judicial admissions” that negate the affirmative defenses in the Answer, warranting judgment on the pleadings in Plaintiff’s favor.

 

The Court finds the deemed admissions insufficient because the admissions do not controvert all of the affirmative defenses in the Answer, including specifically the affirmative defense that Plaintiff has failed to mitigate damages.  (Motion, RJN, Ex. 1 (Answer), p. 3:3-8.) The admissions address the amount of damage caused but not whether Plaintiff could have avoided any portion of the damages through reasonable efforts. The RFAs include an omnibus admission that all of Defendant’s affirmative defenses “lack merit and evidentiary support.” (Motion, RJN, Exh. 2 at No. 9.) This is a wholly legal conclusion and not a proper request for admission or matter for judicial notice. (See Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 2033.010 (admissions must pertain to “the truth of specified matters of fact, opinion relating to fact, or application of law to fact”); Cal. Evid. Code, §452(h) (notice may be taken of “facts and propositions not reasonably subject to dispute”).  There are no “facts” in an admission that defenses “lack merit and evidentiary proof.”  Accordingly, at least one affirmative defense remains at issue and for that additional reason judgment on the pleadings is not appropriate.

 

Further, the Court does find the admissions used appropriately here. “[A]fter a matter is deemed admitted, the scope and effect of the admission must be determined by the trial court.” (Fredericks v. Filbert Co. (1987) 189 Cal. App. 3d 272, 278) (citation omitted).)   The Court’s discretion encompasses the determination whether the opportunity to present additional evidence may provide relevant context as to the meaning and scope of the deemed admissions.  (See Columbia Casualty Co. v. Northwestern Nat. Ins. Co.,  231 Cal.App.3d 457.)  The concern is grounded in due process: “The notice of motion for judgment on the pleadings is thus notice to be prepared to argue legal sufficiency of allegations; it is not notice to come forward with factual support for what is alleged. This process recognizes that there may in fact be evidence which challenges the truth of the allegations of the pleading under attack but defers the challenge until a motion for summary judgment or a trial.” (Id.)  Similarly in Fredericks, the court observed, “[t]he court must have discretion to admit evidence to elucidate and explain an admission, because the admission of a fact does not always reflect the party’s reasonable understanding of that fact.” (189 Cal. App. 3d at 278.)  The Court declines to determine at this time that Defendant should not be afforded the opportunity to attempt to proffer evidence to elucidate and explain the meaning of State Farm’s broadly worded requests for admission, and for that additional reason does not grant them conclusive effect in a motion for judgment on the pleadings.

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings against Defendant Elvin Castellanos is denied.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 26STCP01373    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Judith Parker’s Amended Petition to Release Property from Mechanic’s Lien is granted. Petitioner’s request for attorney’s fees is granted in the amount of $1,800.00 and the request for costs is denied without prejudice.

 

Petitioner is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this ruling within 20 days.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Petitioner Judith Parker (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition for Release of Mechanic’s Lien against Respondent United 1 Construction (“Respondent”) on April 1, 2026. Petitioner filed proof of service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing on April 9, 2026. At the initial hearing on May 1, 2026, the Court denied the Petition without prejudice to Petitioner filing and serving an Amended Petition that includes the legal description of the property. (Minute Order, 05/01/26.)

 

On May 19, 2026, Petitioner filed and served an Amended Petition and an Amended Verification to Petition for Release of Mechanic’s Lien. On July 13, 2026, the Court set the Amended Petition for hearing on August 5, 2026 and gave notice to Petitioner. Petitioner filed a Notice of Ruling on July 14, 2026, demonstrating notice of the new hearing date on Respondent by certified mail. No response to the Petition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Petitioner moves for an order releasing the mechanic’s lien recorded by Respondent against their property. Under Civil Code section 8484, the petition for release order must be verified by the petitioner and must allege the following:

 

(a) The date of recordation of the claim of lien. A certified copy of the claim of lien shall be attached to the petition.

(b) The county in which the claim of lien is recorded.

(c) The book and page or series number of the place in the official records where the claim of lien is recorded.

(d) The legal description of the property subject to the claim of lien.

(e) Whether an extension of credit has been granted under Section 8460, if so to what date, and that the time for commencement of an action to enforce the lien has expired.

(f) That the owner has given the claimant notice under Section 8482 demanding that the claimant execute and record a release of the lien and that the claimant is unable or unwilling to do so or cannot with reasonable diligence be found.

(g) Whether an action to enforce the lien is pending.

(h) Whether the owner of the property or interest in the property has filed for relief in bankruptcy or another restraint prevents the claimant from commencing an action to enforce the lien.

 

(Civil Code, § 8484.) The Amended Petition is verified and now fully complies with the above requirements by including the legal description of the Property. (Amended Pet., ¶4.) Petitioner served Respondent with the Amended Petition and Notice of Ruling by certified mail, as permitted by Civil Code section 8486, subdivision (b). (Proof of Service, filed 05/19/26; Notice of Ruling, pp. 4-9.) The Court grants the Amended Petition in favor of Petitioner.

 

Additionally, Petitioner seeks attorney’s fees of $2,925.00 pursuant to Civil Code section 8488, which provides that “The prevailing party is entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees.” (Civ. Code, § 8488, subd. (c).) As the party in whose favor the Court grants the Amended Petition, Petitioner is entitled to an award of reasonable attorney’s fees under this statute. Petitioner’s counsel bills at $450.00 per hour, but does not justify the rate. (Pet., Ryan Decl., ¶2.) For a straightforward and unopposed petition to release mechanic’s lien, the Court finds the billing rate excessive and reduces it to $400.00. Petitioner’s attorney contends he billed 5.5 hours to draft the demand letters and Petition, and one hour to prepare for and attend the hearing. (Id. at ¶3.) The time spent to draft the demand letters and Amended Petition, which is only five pages, is reduced to 3.5 hours. Therefore, Petitioner is awarded attorney’s fees of $1,800.00. 

 

Petitioner also moves for costs pursuant to Civil Code section 8488, subdivision (c), but the statute refers only to attorney’s fees. (Amended Pet., ¶17.) The request for costs is denied.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner Judith Parker’s Amended Petition to Release Property from Mechanic’s Lien is granted. Petitioner’s request for attorney’s fees is granted in the amount of $1,800.00 and their request for costs is denied without prejudice.

 

Petitioner is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this ruling within 20 days.

 

 

 

Moving party to give notice.
