Case Number: 23STCV30345    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 13 - Jane Doe D.R. v. Paramount Plaza, LLC, et al.

Plaintiff Jane Doe D.R.’s Motion to Compel Site Inspection of Paramount Plaza

Plaintiff Jane Doe D.R. moves to compel Defendants Paramount Plaza LLC, Jamison Services, Inc., and Jamison Properties (collectively “Defendants”) to allow Plaintiff to inspect Paramount Plaza.

“A party may demand that any other party allow the party making the demand, or someone acting on the demanding party's behalf, to enter on any land or other property that is in the possession, custody, or control of the party on whom the demand is made, and to inspect and to measure, survey, photograph, test, or sample the land or other property, or any designated object or operation on it.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(d).) A party demanding inspection may move to compel further responses if “[a] statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete,” “[a] representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive,” or “[a]n objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(a).)

          As an initial matter, the Court notes that the parties have since met and conferred and narrowed the issues in dispute. Plaintiff represents in reply that Defendants agreed to allow the inspection of areas not under construction: “lobbies; plazas; elevators; hallways; the area between the two Paramount Plaza buildings; exterior entry points; and the parking garage.” (Reply, at p. 2.) However, the parties did not reach a consensus regarding whether Defendants would allow Plaintiff to inspect the security guard stations on the property. Therefore, Plaintiff asks the Court to compel Defendants to allow inspection of this area.

The Court cannot grant the relief that Plaintiff seeks. Plaintiff makes this motion under CCP § 2031.310(a), which only allows the Court to compel a further response to a demand for inspection. While CCP § 2031.320(a) allows the Court to compel compliance with a demand for inspection, it can only do so once Defendant has provided a statement of compliance. (See CCP § 2031.320(a) [“If a party filing a response to a demand for inspection . . . thereafter fails to permit the inspection . . . in accordance with that party's statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance.”].) Defendant has not provided a statement of compliance.  

          But for this procedural issue, the Court would have granted the request to allow inspection of the security guard station, which appears to be narrowly tailored and of limited duration. The Court encourages the parties to meet and confer further to resolve this narrow discovery issue.

Disposition

Plaintiff Jane Doe D.R.’s motion to compel site inspection of Paramount Plaza is denied.


Case Number: 24STCV06424    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 14 - BHFC Operating, LLC v. Trea SH Brockman

    Defendant Trea SH Brockman, LLC's Motion to Compel Further Resopnses to Requests for Production

          Defendant Trea SH Brockman, LLC moves to compel Plaintiff BHFC Operating, LLC to serve further response to requests for production, nos. 28 and 30. 

Meet and Confer

Motions to compel further responses must include “a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.310(b)(2).) The moving party must make a “reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040.) This rule aims “to encourage the parties to work out their differences informally so as to avoid the necessity for a formal order.” (Clement v. Alegre (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1277, 1293.) 

          Plaintiff argues that Defendant did not meet and confer in good faith before filing this motion. The Court agrees. On May 29, 2026, Defense counsel emailed Plaintiff’s counsel agreeing to extend its deadline to serve supplemental responses to June 15, 2026 if Plaintiff complied with various conditions. While Defendant argues in its motion that it communicated “it would grant the extension only if Plaintiff accepted specific conditions, in writing,” (Reply, at p. 11), the correspondence itself does not reflect this requirement (see Seddigh Decl., Ex. A, at p. 1.) Defendant filed this motion on June 12, 2026, without following up with Plaintiff’s counsel regarding whether supplemental responses were forthcoming; Plaintiff supplemented its responses on June 15, 2026. (Id., Ex. B.) Plaintiff’s counsel and Defense counsel were still engaged in meet and confer efforts when Defendant filed this motion. This demonstrates that Defense counsel did not wish to meaningfully meet and confer with Plaintiff’s counsel regarding the discovery issues before seeking relief from this Court.

However, the Court recognizes that Plaintiff has unreasonably delayed addressing and resolving these discovery disputes. Thus, notwithstanding Plaintiff’s failure to meet and confer, the Court exercises its discretion to consider the merits of the discovery disputes. 

Requests for Production

          Defendant moves to compel further responses to request for production nos. 28 and 30. A requesting party may move to compel further responses if “[a] statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete,” “[a] representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive,” or “[a]n objection in the response is without merit or too general.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).) The Court exercises its discretion to consider the content of Plaintiff’s third supplemental responses when ruling on this motion.

          Plaintiff made incomplete statements of compliance in response to both requests. “A statement that the party to whom a demand for inspection … has been directed will comply with the particular demand shall state that the production … will be allowed either in whole or in part, and that all documents or things in the demanded category that are in the possession, custody, or control of that party and to which no objection is being made will be included in the production.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.220.)

After objecting to each request, Plaintiff’s third supplemental responses state, in relevant part: “Responsive documents are produced at [specified Bates Nos.].”

This is not a proper statement of compliance.  A statement of compliance must state that “all documents or things in the demanded category that are in the possession, custody, or control of that party and to which no objection is being made will be included in the production.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.220.)  A statement of compliance in whole must use the word “all” because it states under oath not only that the responding party will produce some documents, but also that the responding party is not withholding any responsive documents. To the extent Plaintiff intended to make only partial statements of compliance or are withholding documents, the responses are evasive and do not clearly identify which documents are being withheld subject to which objections as required. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.240, subd. (b)(1).) Specifically, for request no. 28, Plaintiff does not identify why weekly reports are being withheld. Defendant is entitled to further responses.

Sanctions

          Defendant moves for an extreme $20,991.95 in sanctions against Plaintiff and its counsel for 47.9 hours of work. (Hochhausler Decl., ¶ 16.) In opposition, Plaintiff seeks $7,200 in sanctions against Defendant and its counsel. “[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction … against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (CCP § 2031.310(h).)

          The Court exercises its discretion not to impose sanctions on any party. While Plaintiff’s responses were not code-compliant, Defendant failed to fully exhaust its meet and confer obligations under the Code, which potentially would have eliminated the need for this motion.

Disposition

Defendant Trea SH Brockman, LLC’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production is granted as to compelling further responses only. Plaintiff BHFC Operating, LLC is ordered to serve further verified Code-compliant responses to request for production nos. 28 and 30 within 21 days. 


Case Number: 24STCV23505    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 16 - Oscar Javier Madrid Barahon, et al. v. Kay Compton Investors LLC, et al.

          Defendant Hwy 111 Hotel Group, LP’s Demurrer and Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Second Amended Complaint

          Defendant Hwy 111 Hotel Group, LP demurs to Plaintiffs’ second amended complaint (SAC) that alleges causes of action for (1) breach of implied warranty of habitability; (2) breach of statutory warranty of habitability; (3) breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment; (4) negligence; (5) violation of civil code section 1942.3; (6) private nuisance; and (7) violation of unfair competition law.

Background

This case concerns habitability issues at real property that Defendant owned from July 3, 2018, to September 19, 2019. In September 2024, the Plaintiffs, which include 14 adults and 17 minors, initiated this action to recover on their claims

The Court previously sustained Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ first amended complaint on the grounds that their claims were barred by the statute of limitations. Specifically, the Court found Plaintiffs did not adequately allege facts showing tolling under the continuous accrual doctrine or delayed discovery rule.  For the delayed discovery rule, the Court reasoned:

The allegations on the face of the FAC do not support a reasonable inference that Plaintiffs did not discover the substandard living conditions arising in 2018-2019 until several years later. Nor does the Court find it plausible that Plaintiffs did not reasonably discover major alleged habitability issues, such as vermin and rodent infestations, lack of hot water, and plumbing deficiencies, until recently.

(1/30/26 Minute Order.)

The Court granted Plaintiffs leave to amend.

Statute of Limitations

A demurrer should be sustained where “the complaint shows on its face that the statute [of limitations] bars the action.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Services (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.)

 

“The discovery rule provides that the accrual date of a cause of action is delayed until the plaintiff is aware of her injury and its negligent cause.” (County of Santa Clara v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2006) 137 Cal.App.4th 292, 317.) To rely on the discovery rule, the plaintiff “must specifically plead facts to show (1) the time and manner of discovery and (2) the inability to have made earlier discovery despite reasonable diligence.” (Fox v. Ethicon Endo-Surgery, Inc. (2005) 35 Cal.4th 797, 808.) 

In the SAC, Plaintiffs allege more facts to support the delayed discovery rule. Specifically, Plaintiffs now allege:

The extent of the water damages caused by such leaks and the extent of the mold and mildew caused thereby were not immediately known or visible to Plaintiffs and could not readily be discovered through reasonable inspection; Plaintiffs were not authorized to conduct any invasive/destructive testing of any of the drain pipes, water supply pipes, pumps or any part of the plumbing system, including removing any pipes or pumps, testing pipes for leaks, or opening any walls or ceilings to access any such pipes, or to conduct any sampling or testing for mold inside any walls or ceilings where the plumbing leaks originated.

(SAC, ¶ 8.)

While these allegations show that Plaintiffs were unable to make an earlier discovery, they do not demonstrate the time and manner of the discovery as required. For example, Plaintiffs do not allege a date on which they discovered the leaks or mold, nor any way they discovered them, such as a repair or inspection. Without facts alleging the time and manner of discovery, Plaintiffs cannot invoke the discovery rule.

Disposition

          Defendant Hwy 111 Hotel Group, LP demurrer to Plaintiffs’ second amended complaint is sustained with 21 days’ leave to amend.

          Because the demurrer is sustained with leave to amend, the motion to strike is moot.        


Case Number: 25STCV31013    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 15 - Babak Aaron Paz, et al. v. Tesla, Inc.

Plaintiffs Babak Aaron Paz and Anahita Tajbakhsh’s Motion for Attorney Fees

          Plaintiffs Babak Aaron Paz and Anahita Tajbakhsh move for a total of $25,027.73 in attorney fees and costs under Civil Code § 1794(d). The prevailing plaintiff may recover “the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.” (Civ. Code, § 1794(d).)

Plaintiffs seek $21,707.50 for 38.9 hours incurred on the case at a $375-$575 hourly rate. Plaintiffs also seek an additional $1,149.48 in costs. Plaintiffs use a 1.1 multiplier on the attorney fees, for an additional $2,170.75.

Hourly Rates

Plaintiff requests 38.9 hours of fees from 2 licensed attorneys, Tamara Artolozaga and Isaac Kohen. Plaintiffs present evidence of both attorneys’ qualifications and experience. (See Kohen Decl., ¶¶ 15-19.) They bill $375 and $575 per hour respectively.

Based on its knowledge of the legal market, its review of the credentials and experience of Plaintiff’s attorneys, and the particular tasks performed by counsel, the Court finds Plaintiffs claim excessive hourly rates for counsel. This was a straightforward case that did not require superior expertise. Thus, the Court will reduce Artolozaga’s rate to $300 per hour and Kohan’s rate to $375 per hour.

Hours

In calculating the lodestar, the court must determine whether the tasks performed by an attorney were necessary and whether the amount of time billed for each task was reasonable. (Baxter v. Bock (2016) 247 Cal.App.4th 775, 793.) The moving party has the burden of proof on these issues. (Ibid.) A reduced award is justified when a case is “overlitigated” and the bills are “padded.” (Morris v. Hyundai Motor America (2019) 41 Cal.App.5th 24, 38.) For a “voluminous fee application,” courts may “make across-the-board percentage cuts either in the number of hours claimed or in the final lodestar figure.” (Warren v. Kia Motors America, Inc. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 24, 41 (Warren); accord Morris, supra, 41 Cal.App.5th at p. 40 [“The court could properly have made an across-the-board reduction of 30 percent”].) 

Plaintiffs’ counsel billed an excessive number of hours for some tasks. For example, Plaintiffs’ counsel billed many hours for drafting papers substantially similar to those filed in other cases. The Court knows this from its own experience. For example, Plaintiff’s counsel is billing 6.5 hours to draft this fee motion plus another 4.5 hours to review the opposition, draft the reply, and attend the hearing. (Kohen Decl., Ex. 1 at p. 3.) However, this fee motion differs very little from another fee motion Plaintiffs’ counsel filed in another case in this Department—Monoshevich v. Ford Motor Company (Case no. 24STCV11936). In addition, Plaintiffs’ counsel improperly bills for administrative or clerical tasks that could have been done by a secretary. For example, Kohen billed a total of 3.2 hours (or $1,840) on vehicle surrender logistics. (Id., at pp. 2-3.) Billing that much time at a $575 rate for such tasks is not reasonable.

After reviewing all billing records, the Court exercises its discretion to reduce Plaintiffs’ counsel’s hours claimed by 30%. 

Multiplier

Plaintiffs move for a 1.1 multiplier. Multipliers may be awarded based on factors including “(1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, [and] (4) the contingent nature of the fee award.”  (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132.) After considering all relevant factors, the Court finds a lodestar enhancement is not warranted. This case did not involve novel or difficult questions. Plaintiffs’ counsel did not demonstrate exceptional skill in litigating the case. Counsel’s hourly rates adequately account for representation on contingency. (See Horsford v. Board of Trustees of California State University (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 395.)

Other Costs

Plaintiffs seek $1,149.48 for costs. “[I]n enacting Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d) the Legislature intended the phrase ‘costs and expenses’ to cover items not included in ‘the detailed statutory definition of “costs” ’ set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5.” (Warren v. Kia Motors America, Inc. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 24, 42.) Defendant challenges several specific items as not reasonably incurred. “In awarding costs, a trial court’s first determination ... is whether the statute expressly allows the particular item, and whether it appears proper on its face. If so, the burden is on the objecting party to show them to be unnecessary or unreasonable.” (Benach v. County of Los Angeles (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 836, 855, internal quotes and citations omitted.)

The Court finds that all costs except two were reasonably incurred. Defendant challenged a $100 fee for an unnamed expert and $75 for database research that is available as a subscription, not for individual searches. and will award the requested amount of costs in full. Plaintiff did not address or provide evidentiary support for either cost. The Could will tax $175 from Plaintiff’s request, bring the cost award to $974.48.

Therefore, the Court will award a total of $11,012.48 attorney fees and costs on this motion. This includes (1) 35.6 hours x .7 at an adjusted rate of $375 per hour ($9,345) and (2) 3.3 hours x .7 at an adjusted rate of $300 per hour ($693). This totals $10,038. The Court will also award $974.48 in costs, for a total of $11,012.48.

Disposition  

          Plaintiffs Babak Aaron Paz and Anahita Tajbakhsh’s motion for attorney fees is granted in part in the total amount of $11,012.48 in fees and costs.
