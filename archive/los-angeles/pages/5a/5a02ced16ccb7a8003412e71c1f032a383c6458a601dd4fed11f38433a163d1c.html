DONNOVAN LEWERY v. CLUB DB LOUNGE, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 19STCV30200 

HEARING: 07/10/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#14

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Counsel Mansfield Collins’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Defendant Club DB Lounge is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Counsel Mansfield Collins (Counsel) moves to be relieved as counsel for Defendant Club DB Lounge (Defendant).

 

Background

 

On August 26, 2019, Plaintiff Donnovan Lewery (Plaintiff) filed this action against Defendants Club DB Lounge, Hayk Gevorkyan, Frontline Protection, Inc., and Does 1 through 25 for negligent hiring, retention, and supervision, premises liability, assault, battery, and intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

Following a jury trial, a judgment on special verdict was entered in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant Downey Billiards LLC dba Club DB Lounge.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The attorney in an action or special proceeding may be changed at any time before or after judgment or final determination, as follows: ¶ 1. Upon the consent of both client and attorney, filed with the clerk, or entered upon the minutes; ¶ 2. Upon the order of the court, upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 284.)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires that the following be submitted in support of an attorney’s motion to be relieved as counsel pursuant Code of Civil

Procedure section 284, subd. (2): (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (Form MC-051); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subd. (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subd. (1) (Form MC-052); (3) a proof of service evidencing service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) a proposed order relieving counsel (Form MC-053). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (d), (e).)

 

Discussion

 

Counsel moves to be relieved as counsel for Defendant.

 

Here, in compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, Counsel submits a notice of motion and motion on Form MC-051 directed to Defendant. Counsel also submits a declaration on Form MC-052 demonstrating good cause to be relieved as counsel for Defendant. Specifically, Counsel states that “[t]he services covered in [his] retainer agreement have been concluded, [he] ha[s] been discharged.” (Collins Decl., ¶ 2.) Counsel provides a proposed order on Form MC-053 and proof of service on Defendant. Therefore, the Court finds that Counsel has complied with the requirements under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Defendant is GRANTED. This Order will take effect upon the filing of proof of service of the signed order upon the client and on all parties that have appeared in the case.

Case Number: 24NWCV02123    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: P

MIGUEL GARCIA v. HYUNDAI MOTOR AMERICA

CASE NO.: 24NWCV02123  

HEARING: 07/10/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#18

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Miguel Garcia’s motion to reopen discovery is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Miguel Garcia (Plaintiff) moves to re-open discovery to conduct the deposition of Defendant Hyundai Motor America’s (Defendant) person most knowledgeable.

 

Background

 

On July 11, 2024, Plaintiff filed this lemon law action against Defendant and Does 1 through 10, inclusive. This action arises out of Plaintiff’s October 24, 2020 purchase of a 2020 Hyundai Elantra, VIN No.: 5NPD84LF0LH605564 (Subject Vehicle) and its accompanying warranties. (Complaint, ¶ 8.) Plaintiff alleges the Subject Vehicle was delivered with defects and nonconformities to warranties including engine, emission, and electrical system defects. (Complaint, ¶ 10.) The complaint asserts three causes of action: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of express warranty, (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of implied warranty, and (3) violation of Song-Beverly Act section 1793.2.

 

On May 11, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s ex parte application to continue trial. On May 13, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s ex parte application to continue trial.

 

On May 14, 2026, this matter was called for trial. After peremptory challenges, this matter was returned to this Court. (Order, 5/14/26.) The Court continued the trial date to July 27, 2026. (Order, 5/14/26.)

 

On June 10, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s ex parte application requesting clarification on whether discovery remained closed following the 5/14/26 Order. The Court stated: “The Court clarifies: discovery is closed and the May 14, 2026 Order did not re-open discovery. At no point in the Order did the Court re-open discovery.” (Order, 6/10/26.)

 

On June 10, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s ex parte application to re-open discovery but specially set the instant motion for today’s date.

 

Legal Standard

 

“On motion of any party, the court may grant leave to complete discovery proceedings, or to have a motion concerning discovery heard, closer to the initial trial date, or to reopen discovery after a new trial date has been set. This motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2024.050 subd. (a).)

 

“In exercising its discretion to grant or deny this motion, the court shall take into consideration any matter relevant to the leave requested, including, but not limited to, the following: (1) The necessity and the reasons for the discovery. (2) The diligence or lack of diligence of the party seeking the discovery or the hearing of a discovery motion, and the reasons that the discovery was not completed or that the discovery motion was not heard earlier. (3) Any likelihood that permitting the discovery or hearing the discovery motion will prevent the case from going to trial on the date set, or otherwise interfere with the trial calendar, or result in prejudice to any other party. (4) The length of time that has elapsed between any date previously set, and the date presently set, for the trial of the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.050, subd. (b)(1)-(4).)

 

Meet and Confer

 

A motion to reopen discovery “shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2024.050 subd. (a).)

 

Plaintiff’s counsel emailed defense counsel regarding reopening discovery and defense counsel was not agreeable. (Schmitt Decl., ¶ 12.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to reopen discovery to conduct the deposition of Defendant’s PMK.

 

Plaintiff contends that he has been diligent in trying to depose Defendant’s PMK. Plaintiff argues that Defendant has ignored attempts to meet and confer regarding the deposition of Defendant’s PMK over the course of the litigation. Plaintiff states that he first noticed the deposition of Defendant’s PMK on September 4, 2024 and requested Defendant provide alternative dates at that time. (Schmitt Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) Plaintiff states Defendant provided boilerplate objections and responded that it would work with Plaintiff’s counsel to schedule a mutually agreeable date. (Schmitt Decl., ¶ 5.) Plaintiff states that Defendant served an amended notice of deposition on November 4, 2024, but never received a response. (Schmitt Decl., ¶¶ 6-7.)

 

Plaintiff further contends that reopening discovery would not prevent this case from going to trial on July 27, 2026. Additionally, Plaintiff argues that it is entitled to depose Defendant’s PMK and the purpose of the discovery statutes will be furthered by reopening discovery.

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that this motion is a result of Plaintiff’s lack of diligence with discovery. Defendant states that it has no record of receiving the November 4, 2024 amended notice of deposition. (Williams Decl., ¶¶ 3-5.) Defendant emphasizes that even if Plaintiff did notice a deposition in November of 2024, Plaintiff does not provide any explanation as to why he let the issue of deposing Defendant’s PMK lie dormant for over a year and a half, passed the date initially set for trial. Defendant notes that Plaintiff could have met and conferred had it realized that Defendant had not responded at all to the amended notice.

 

Here, the Court does not find that Plaintiff was diligent in pursuing this discovery. Between the time Plaintiff first served a notice of deposition of Defendant’s PMK to the initial trial date, the record shows both Plaintiff and Defendant brought and litigated discovery motions. Plaintiff does not explain why the parties met and conferred regarding other discovery but Defendant specifically “stonewalled” Plaintiff as to the deposition of its PMK. (Mot., 3:5.) While the Court agrees that the necessity of the discovery factor weighs in favor of Plaintiff, Plaintiff does not adequately explain why he did not prioritize such discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.) Plaintiff’s lack of diligence is most highlighted by the fact that this matter was called for trial on May 14, 2026, yet Plaintiff only raised this issue after this matter was initially called for trial. 

 

Additionally, the Court finds that Defendant establishes that reopening discovery would be prejudicial and disrupt trial preparation. Specifically, Defendant contends that conducting this deposition prior to July 27, 2026, would divert resources, require witness preparation, and potentially create new disputes on the eve of trial.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to reopen discovery is DENIED.


Case Number: 24NWCV04835    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: P

JOEL VILLANUEVA, ET AL. v. AMAZON LOGISTICS, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.:  24NWCV04835

HEARING: 07/10/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#16

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants Front Door Logistics LLC and Anthony Ray Acuna’s motion to continue trial and related deadlines is GRANTED. The Court will confer with the parties to select new trial and FSC dates.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendants Front Door Logistics LLC and Anthony Ray Acuna (Defendants) move to continue trial and all related pretrial deadlines.

 

Background

 

On December 11, 2024, Plaintiffs Joel Villanueva, Socorro Villanueva, and Isaiah Mathew Sanchez, a Minor by and through his Guardian Ad Litem Brenda Villanueva (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed this action against Defendants Amazon Logistics, Inc., Amazon Nationwide Logistics, Front Door Logistics, LLC, Anthony Ray Acuna, and Does 1 to 50. This action arises out of injuries Plaintiffs sustained on April 17, 2024 while driving westbound on Telegraph Road when Defendant Acuna, driving a vehicle branded with the Amazon corporate logo, collided into Plaintiffs’ vehicle. (Complaint, ¶¶ 36-37.) The complaint asserts three causes of action: (1) negligence, (2) negligent entrustment, and (3) negligent hiring, training, supervision, and retention.

 

A Final Status Conference is currently scheduled for September 3, 2026 and Trial is currently scheduled to begin on September 14, 2026.

 

On May 28, 2026, the Court denied Defendants’ ex parte application to shorten time to hear this motion.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Although continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance.  Circumstances that may indicate good cause include… (6) A party’s excused inability to obtain essential testimony, documents, or other material evidence despite diligent efforts; or (7) A significant, unanticipated change in the status of the case as a result of which the case is not ready for trial.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(c).)

 

The Court, in making its determination to continue the trial should consider the following factors: (1) The proximity of the trial date; (2) Whether there was any previous continuance, extension of time, or delay of trial due to any party (3) The length of the continuance requested; (4) The unavailability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion or application for a continuance; (5) The prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; (6) If the case is entitled to a preferential trial setting, the reasons for that status and whether the need for a continuance outweighs the need to avoid delay; (7) The court’s calendar and the impact of granting a continuance on other pending trials; (8) Whether trial counsel is engaged in another trial; (9) Whether all parties have stipulated to a continuance; (10) Whether the interests of justice are best served by a continuance, by the trial of the matter, or by imposing conditions on the continuance; and (11) Any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(d).)

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move to continue the current trial date of September 14, 2026 to a date in March of 2027 as well as the final status conference and all pre-trial deadlines.

 

Defendants move on the grounds that Defendants’ lead trial counsel, Stacey Walker, will be unavailable for the current trial date because she will not be medically cleared to travel due to late-stage pregnancy. Ms. Walker details attempts to stipulate to a trial continuance beginning on May 11, 2026. (Walker Decl., ¶¶ 5-9.) Ms. Walker notes that the day before Plaintiff Sanchez’s deposition, Plaintiffs’ counsel advised that Plaintiff Sanchez would be unavailable and that all remaining depositions would need to be continued to June. (Walker Decl., ¶¶ 11-12.) Ms. Walker states that this is the first request for a trial continuance. (Walker Decl., ¶ 14.) Additionally, Ms. Walker states that counsel for Amazon does not oppose a trial continuance. (Walker Decl., ¶ 13.) Additionally, Ms. Walker states: “As lead counsel for my clients, I am responsible for trial strategy, expert coordination, discovery management, and pretrial preparation. Proceeding without me at trial would substantially prejudice defendants and risk disruption of trial proceedings. (Walker Decl., ¶ 15.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that late-stage pregnancy does not constitute an excusable circumstance under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(c)(3). Plaintiffs argue that pregnancy is not sudden or unforeseen. Plaintiffs additionally argue that Defendants do not show why their other attorney of record, Jeffrey Y. Choi, cannot take over as trial counsel. Lastly, Plaintiffs argue that the requested continuance is long and prejudices Plaintiffs.

 

Here, the Court finds that Defendants establish proper grounds under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(c)(3) – “[t]he unavailability of trial counsel because of…other excusable circumstances.” Plaintiffs do not point to authorities requiring that the “excusable circumstances” be “sudden” or “unforeseeable.” (Opp., 2:20.) Moreover, as argued by Defendants in reply, Plaintiffs do not establish authorities requiring a different attorney to take over as lead counsel before considering a trial continuance. Further, the Court agrees with Defendants that Plaintiffs do not establish prejudice by mischaracterizing that this case is “is already over two years old.”  (Opp., 7:13.)

 

The Court finds that Defendants establish that a trial continuance is warranted based on the unavailability of their lead trial counsel. Moreover, other factors weigh in favor of a continuance, including that this is the first trial continuance, Defendants moved for this relief well in advance of the current trial date, and that the Amazon defendants do not oppose a trial continuance.

 

Defendants’ motion to continue trial and related deadlines is GRANTED. The Court will confer with the parties to select new trial and FSC dates.


Case Number: 25NWCV04077    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: P

ADAM GUILLEN v. MAIN ELECTRIC SUPPLY COMPANY, LLC

CASE NO.: 25NWCV04077 

HEARING:  07/10/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#15

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Proposed Plaintiff-In-Intervention Insurance Company of the West’s motion for leave to intervene is GRANTED.

 

II.            Insurance Company of the West is ORDERED to file and serve its complaint-in-intervention attached to its moving papers within 5 days of this Order

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Proposed Intervenor Insurance Company of the West (Proposed Intervenor) moves to intervene in this action.

 

Background

 

On November 18, 2025, Plaintiff Adam Guillen (Plaintiff) filed this personal injury action against Defendant Main Electric Supply Company, LLC (Defendant) and Does 1 through 10. The complaint alleges that on December 21, 2023, Plaintiff was making a delivery as part of his employment at 8146 Byron Rd., Whittier, CA 90606 (Subject Premises) owned by Defendant. While following Defendant’s unloading procedure, a steel bar fell and struck Plaintiff on his head, resulting in injuries. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) general negligence and (2) premises liability.

 

Legal Standard

 

An intervention takes place when a nonparty becomes a party to an action by “[j]oining a plaintiff in claiming what is sought by the complaint, “[u]niting with a defendant in resisting the claims of a plaintiff,” or “[d]emanding anything adverse to both a plaintiff and a defendant.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (b)(1)-(3).) A nonparty petitioning for leave to intervene must include a copy of the proposed complaint or answer in intervention and state the grounds for the intervention. (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (c).)

 

The Court shall permit an intervention if “[a] provision of law confers an unconditional right to intervene” or “[t]he person seeking intervention claims an interest relating to the property or transaction that is the subject of the action and that person is so situated that the disposition of the action may impair or impede that person's ability to protect that interest, unless that person's interest is adequately represented by one or more of the existing parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (d)(1)(A)-(B).)

 

Alternatively, the Court may “permit a nonparty to intervene in the action or proceeding if the person has an interest in the matter in litigation, or in the success of either of the parties, or an interest against both.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (d)(2).)

 

Discussion

 

Proposed Intervenor moves to intervene in this action as this action arises out of an injury which occurred while Plaintiff was in the course and scope of his employment with Proposed Intervenor’s insured, CoFreight, Inc. (Employer). This motion is unopposed.

 

Proposed Intervenor moves on the grounds that it has paid workers’ compensation benefits on behalf of its insured’s employee and is now subrogated to the rights and claims of its insured pursuant to Labor Code section 3850.

 

Labor Code sections 3852 and 3853 provide that an employer against whom a claim for workers’ compensation damages was made may join in an action against a third party arising out of the incident for which the workers’ compensation payment was made. (Lab. Code, §§ 3852, 3853.) “[Labor Code] [s]ection 3853 states that when an action is brought against a third party by either the employer or the employee, ‘the other may, at any time before trial on the facts, join as party plaintiff.’” (Jordan v. Superior Court (1981) 116 Cal.App.3d 202, 206 [quoting Lab. Code, § 3853].) An employer includes the employer’s insurer. (Lab. Code, § 3850, subd. (b).)

 

As the workers’ compensation insurer for Plaintiff’s Employer, Proposed Intervenor establishes a statutory right to intervene.

 

Proposed Intervenor’s motion for leave to file a complaint-in-intervention is GRANTED.

 

Proposed Intervenor is ORDERED to file and serve its complaint-in-intervention attached to its moving papers within 5 days of this Order.

 


Case Number: 25NWCV04179    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: P

JOHN DOE v. CATHOLIC ARCHDIOCESE OF LOS ANGELES

CASE NO.: 25NWCV04179

HEARING: 07/10/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#19

REVISED TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Specially Appearing Defendants’ motion to quash service
of summons and complaint and dismiss this action is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Attorneys specially appear as Does (Specially Appearing
Defendants) move for an order quashing service of summons and complaint and
dismissing this action against Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles, Archbishop
Jose Gomez, Saint Paul of the Cross Catholic Church, and Cardinal Roger Mahony
for lack of jurisdiction.

 

Background

 

On November 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed this action against
Defendants Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles, Archbishop Jose Gomez, Saint
Paul of the Cross Catholic Church, Father Michael Baker, Cardinal Roger Mahony,
and the Vatican. The complaint alleges Plaintiff “seeks redress for severe and
ongoing harm from sexual abuse by Catholic priest Father Michael Baker in 1979,
and the subsequent systemic cover-up and criminal enterprise by Defendants in
violation of California law and the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt
Organizations (RICO) Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1962.” (Complaint, ln. 29-31.) The
complaint asserts three causes of action: (1) negligence, (2) intentional
infliction of emotional distress, and (3) violation of the RICO Act, 18 U.S.C.
§ 1962.

 

Legal Standard

 

If service of process is insufficient a defendant may move
to quash service of summons on or before the last day to plead in response to
the complaint or within such further time as the court may allow for good
cause. (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10 subd. (a)(1).)

 

Discussion

 

Specially Appearing Defendants move to quash service of
summons and complaint and dismiss this action for lack of jurisdiction. Specially
Appearing Defendants bring this motion on the grounds that there is no court
order finding a reasonable and meritorious cause of action against Specially
Appearing Defendants, the Court lacks personal jurisdiction over Specially
Appearing Defendants, and the Court lacks fundamental jurisdiction over the
claims.

 

Plaintiff filed this action seeking damages as a result of
alleged childhood sexual assault. As such, because Plaintiff was 40 years or
older at the time of filing this action, Plaintiff is required to file
certificates of merit executed by an attorney and by a licensed mental health
practitioner. (Code Civ. Proc., § 340.1, subds. (f), (g).) Specially Appearing
Defendants argue Plaintiff failed to file these mandatory certificates and
therefore, the Court lacks personal and fundamental jurisdiction over
Defendant.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that he has complied with
Code of Civil Procedure section 340.11 in good faith. Plaintiff argues that he
has obtained and filed three Certificates of Merit and that his efforts
demonstrate good faith compliance with the statutory requirements. Plaintiff
also argues that Specially Appearing Defendants have actual notice of
Plaintiff’s claims and that the purpose of notice has been achieved. Further,
Plaintiff contends that equitable consideration support denying this motion.

 

In reply, Defendant reiterates its arguments that Plaintiff
has not complied with the requirements under Code of Civil Procedure section
340.11. Defendant also notes that the requirements for a certificate of merit
applies equally to self-represented litigants. (Jackson v. Doe (2011)
192 Cal.App.4th 742, 752 [“To excuse pro per plaintiffs from the certificates
of merit requirement would create a cavernous loophole in the statute, a
loophole that would defeat its salutary goal.”].)

 

“In any action subject to subdivision (e), a defendant shall
not be served, and the duty to serve a defendant with process does not attach,
until the court has reviewed the certificates of merit filed pursuant to
subdivision (f) with respect to that defendant, and has found, in camera, based
solely on those certificates of merit, that there is reasonable and meritorious
cause for the filing of the action against that defendant. At that time, the
duty to serve that defendant with process shall attach.” (Code Civ. Proc., §
340.11, subd. (h).) “In the absence of a voluntary submission to the authority
of the court, compliance with the statutes governing service of process is
essential to establish that court's personal jurisdiction over a defendant.” (Dill
v Berquist Cons. Co., Inc. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1439.)

 

Here, Plaintiff does not provide evidence that he complied
with the certificate of merit requirements under Code of Civil Procedure
section 340.11.

 

The Court briefly notes that Code of Civil Procedure section
340.1, subdivision (j) provides: “The failure to file certificates in
accordance with this section shall be grounds for a demurrer pursuant
to Section 430.10 or a motion to strike pursuant to Section
435.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 340.1, subd. (l); Doe v. San Diego-Imperial
Council (2015) 239 Cal.App.4th 81, 87 [“[T]he Legislature's act of
designating a demurrer and motion to strike as the means to challenge a
plaintiff's failure to file the required certificate indicates it “ ‘view[ed]
the certificates as an aspect of the complaint.’”].) Specially Appearing
Defendants do not establish authority that a motion to quash service of summons
is the proper motion compared to that established in Code of Civil Procedure
section 340.1.

 

However, “[a] demurrer may be sustained without leave to
amend where plaintiff fails to file the certificates of merit in accordance
with section 340.1, subdivision (h) before the statute of limitations
expires or within 60 days of filing the complaint.” (Jackson, supra,
192 Cal.App.4th at p. 752.) In considering substance over form, the effect
of the instant motion is the same as if Specially Appearing Defendants had
brought a demurrer based on the same argument.

 

Accordingly, Specially Appearing Defendants’ motion to quash
and dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction is GRANTED.
