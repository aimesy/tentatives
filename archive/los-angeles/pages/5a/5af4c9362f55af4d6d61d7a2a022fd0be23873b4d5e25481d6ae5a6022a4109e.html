Case Number: 23NWCV01916    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: P

NOEMI MONROY-ROMAN v. ROSITA GENERO, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV01916 

HEARING: 9/17/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#10

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Noemi Monroy-Roman’s motion to compel further deposition and production of documents of Defendant Auto Company XXIII’s PMQ is GRANTED IN PART and MOOT IN PART.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Noemi Monroy-Roman (Plaintiff) moves to compel further deposition and production of documents of Defendant Auto Company XXIII dba Autonation Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram Valencia’s (Defendant) person most qualified (PMQ).

 

Background

 

Plaintiffs and Yeizon Rodriguez filed this action against Defendants Rosita Genero and Diego Genero on June 22, 2023 and Does 1 to 50. The complaint alleges that on or about October 21, 2022, Plaintiffs were travelling Northbound on I-710 approaching Frontage Road and Miller Way when Defendant Rosita Genero, driving behind Plaintiffs, struck Plaintiffs’ vehicle from behind, resulting in injuries. (Complaint, ¶¶ 8-9.) The complaint asserts two causes of action against all defendants: (1) negligence and (2) negligence per se.

 

On December 6, 2024, Plaintiffs requested dismissal without prejudice as to Defendant Diego Genero. On the same date, Defendant Diego Genero was dismissed without prejudice.

 

On May 5, 2025, Plaintiffs filed two Amendments to Complaint replacing Doe 1 with Autonation.com, Inc and Doe 2 with Autonation, Inc.

 

On March 5, 2026, the Court heard and took Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to produce its PMQ for deposition off calendar as moot after the parties failed to meet and confer. (Order, 3/5/26.)

 

On August 11, 2026, the Court held an Informal Discovery Conference (IDC) at Plaintiffs’ request regarding the issues raised by the instant motion.

 

On August 14, 2026, when this motion first came on for hearing, the Court continued the hearing to allow the parties more time to work toward an informal resolution based on the Court’s guidance at the IDC. The Court requested further briefing or declarations regarding any updates following the IDC.

 

On September 1, 2026, this motion came on for hearing again. At the time of the hearing, the Court was not in receipt of any filings. The Court continued the motion again to today’s date and ordered the parties to file briefing.

 

On August 31, 2026, Defendant filed a declaration. The Court was not in receipt of the declaration at the September 1, 2026 hearing.

 

On September 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed a declaration.

 

Legal Standard

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a), a party may move to compel a deponent’s attendance and production for inspection of documents if after serving the deposition notice the responding party fails to serve valid objections and fails to appear for the deposition or produce any document described in the deposition notice. A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a) must (1) provide specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document and (2) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., §¿2025.450, subd. (b).) Further deposition may be taken of a “person [who] was examined as a result of that person's designation to testify on behalf of an organization under Section 2025.230.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.610, subd. (c)(1).)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiffs move to compel Defendant to produce its PMQ for further deposition and further production of documents. Plaintiffs argue that Defendant refuses to produce a PMQ for deposition as to Defendant’s employee and whether the employee was in the course and scope of her employment during the subject incident.

 

Following the IDC, the Court ordered the parties to apprise the Court of what remains at issue in this motion. Defendant filed a declaration attesting to the following: “As discussed at the IDC and proven over and over, Genero was not in the couse [sic] and scope of her employment with CDJR at the time of the Subject Accident. Accordingly, CDJR has produced all documents that the Parties and the Court discussed at the IDC.” (Crosby Decl., ¶ 7.) Defendant proceeds to list the documents it produced, which Defendant attests are the documents the Court discussed with the parties at the IDC. (Crosby Decl., ¶ 8.) Defendant produced:

 

·        The Declaration of Natalie Huerta;

·        The Declaration of Anthony Quartarone;

·        Time sheets for all of CDJR’s employees that attended the training on the date of the Subject Accident, October 21, 2022. Including Defendant Rosita Genero, Mario Anleu, and Gonzalo Gomez;

·        Training transcript reports for all CDJR employees that attended the training on the date of the Subject Accident, October 21, 2022, showing the date that the employees returned to CDJR following the training—October 24, 2022;

·        Intraoffice personnel records for all the CDJR employees that attended the training on the date of the Subject Accident; albeit, redacted due to confidentiality and privacy rights.

 

(Crosby Decl., ¶ 6.)

 

In Plaintiffs’ declaration, filed on September 14, 2026, Plaintiffs acknowledge that Defendant has supplemented certain documents. Plaintiffs claim two issues are pending. First, is “unredacted versions of documentation [Defendant] provided in advance of the June 15, 2026 deposition of its person most qualified James Garwick. Such documentation includes redacted copies of emails dated October 14, 2022 and during the week of October 17, 2022, sent to training attendees and Defendant’s staff. Defendant redacted these emails’ senders and receivers and signature lines, thus obfuscating relevant identities.” (Rofael Decl., ¶ 6.)

 

Second, Plaintiffs claim the following is pending: “Defendant CDJR has not provided all relevant documentation pertaining to the training sessions Defendant Genero attended. For instance, schedules of the training sessions were not provided, and these may indicate whether trainees were supposed to travel to auto dealerships on the incident date of October 21, 2022, as Defendant Genero alleged in her deposition testimony and verified discovery responses.” (Rofael Decl., ¶ 9.) The Court notes that Plaintiffs do not apprise the Court as to which category of examination or document requests the supplemental production or pending requests correspond to. Therefore, the Court finds the motion moot as to issues not raised in the supplemental declaration.

 

Plaintiffs state that “[t]he emails’ senders and receivers would enable Plaintiffs to determine who coordinated the training sessions from which Defendant Genero departed, at Defendant CDJR’s direction, to its Valencia facility. It was during this trip that the subject incident occurred.” (Rofael Decl., ¶ 6.) Plaintiffs contend that two declarations by two employees is not sufficient as they are individuals under Defendant’s employ.

 

A request for production of documents in a deposition notice need only specify with reasonable particularity the materials or category of materials to be produced by the deponent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.220, subd. (a)(4).) Where production of documents is sought in connection with the deposition, the motion must set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450 subd. (b)(1).) These facts must also be set forth in a separate statement filed by the moving party. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(a)(5).) This burden “is met simply by a fact-specific showing of relevance.” (TBG Ins. Servs. Corp. v. Superior Court (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 443, 448.)

 

Here, based on the discussions from the IDC, Defendant has properly submitted the names and contact information of people who were present at the orientation on the date of the incident and the transcript reports of its employees who attended the training, which show the date that the employees returned to Defendant. (See Crosby Decl., ¶ 6.) Following the IDC, Plaintiffs establish that the two pending document production requests, the unredacted emails from that orientation week and schedule indicating whether trainees were going to auto dealerships on the subject orientation date, are relevant to whether Genero was in the scope of her employment with Defendant. Defendant has not submitted a written opposition to support its objections. To the extent that Defendant does not possess the requested documents as represented in its declaration, Defendant can assert such points in its further discovery responses.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant is ORDERED to produce further documents to the two requests for production, the unredacted emails from the relevant orientation week and schedules indicating whether trainees were going to auto dealerships on the subject orientation date, within 20 days of this Order. This date may be extended pursuant to agreement between the parties.

 

The rest of the motion is MOOT pursuant to the supplemental production.

 

Sanctions

 

Based on the IDC and delay in apprising the Court of the remaining issues, the Court declines to award monetary sanctions.


Case Number: 23NWCV02182    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: P

HELEN MARIE STEPHENS v. BIG LOTS, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV02182

HEARING: 09/17/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Helen Marie Stephens’s motion to set aside dismissal is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Helen Marie Stephens (Plaintiff) moves to set aside the dismissal entered on February 5, 2026.

 

Background

 

On July 13, 2023, Plaintiff filed this premises liability action against Defendants Big Lots Stores, LLC, erroneously sued as Big Lots, Inc. (Defendant), Big Lots Store 1910, Manager Celine, and Does 1 through 50. The form complaint alleges that Plaintiff sustained injuries on July 15, 2021, after slipping and falling on a clear liquid on the floor at Big Lots Store 1910, located at 5227 Lakewood Blvd., Lakewood, CA 90712. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) general negligence and (2) premises liability.

 

On June 5, 2024, Plaintiff filed Requests for Dismissal as to Defendants Manager Celine and Big Lots Store 1910.

 

On September 11, 2024, Defendant filed a Notice of Filing of Bankruptcy indicating that Defendant filed for protection in bankruptcy under Chapter 11. This action was automatically stayed pursuant to the bankruptcy.

 

On February 5, 2026, during a Non-Appearance Case Review Re: Bankruptcy, the Court dismissed this action without prejudice after no further status reports were received.

 

On August 20, 2026, Defendant filed a Status Report Re: Bankruptcy noting that this action remains stayed.

 

Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) provides for discretionary and mandatory relief for mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), “[t]he court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to set aside the Dismissal entered on February 5, 2026.

 

Plaintiff moves for mandatory relief on the grounds that Plaintiff’s counsel “mistakenly believed that I did not need to attend the “Non-Appearance Case Review Re: Bankruptcy” hearing.” (Osborne Decl., ¶ 9.)

 

Plaintiff additionally moves pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) on the grounds that the Dismissal Order is void or entered in excess of the Court’s jurisdiction.

 

Here, the Court agrees the Order dismissing this action is void. “The law is clear that a nonbankruptcy court may not dismiss but may only stay an action in which a defendant has filed for bankruptcy.” (Traweek v. Finley, Kumble, Wagner, Heine, Underberg, Manley, Myerson & Casey (1991) 235 Cal.App.3d 1128, 1135.) “Judicial proceedings in violation of the automatic stay are void. [Citation.] Accordingly, the trial court had no power to dismiss the case, and the dismissal was void.” (Sindler v. Brennan (2003) 105 Cal.App.4th 1350, 1353 [noting that a motion to set aside under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) is the correct procedure to challenge a dismissal during a bankruptcy stay].)

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to set aside the dismissal is GRANTED. This action remains stayed pursuant to Defendant’s bankruptcy.


Case Number: 26NWCV00761    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: P

KARINA SANCHEZ, ET AL. v. EVER ALEJANDRO RIVERA, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 26NWCV00761 

HEARING: 09/17/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#12

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants Ever Alejandro Rivera and Neri Arroyo’s motion to strike portions of the complaint is GRANTED with 30 days LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendants Ever Alejandro Rivera and Neri Arroyo (Defendants) move to strike portions of the complaint seeking punitive damages.

 

Background

 

On February 27, 2026, Plaintiffs Karina Sanchez and Anthony Beltran (Plaintiffs) filed this personal injury action against Defendants and Does 1 through 25. The form complaint alleges that on November 23, 2025, at Florence Ave & Crafton Ave, Bell, CA 90201, Plaintiffs sustained injuries after Arroyo negligently entrusted a vehicle to Rivera and Rivera swerved the vehicle and collided into Plaintiffs’ vehicle to avoid rear-ending the vehicle in front of his. The complaint alleges that this is a hit and run incident as Rivera proceeded to flee the scene. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1).) The Court may, upon a motion, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper, strike any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (a).) The Court may also strike all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (b).) The grounds for a motion to strike are that the pleading has irrelevant, false or improper matter, or has not been drawn or filed in conformity with laws. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437.)

 

Punitive damages may be recovered upon a proper showing of malice, fraud, or oppression. (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).) “Malice” refers to conduct “intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.” (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(1).) “Oppression” means despicable conduct subjecting a person to cruel and unjust hardship, in conscious disregard of the person’s rights. (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(2).) “Fraud” means an “intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.” (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(3).)

 

“In order to survive a motion to strike an allegation of punitive damages, the ultimate facts showing an entitlement to such relief must be pled by a plaintiff.” (Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255.) The basis for punitive damages must be pled with specificity; conclusory allegations, devoid of any factual assertions, are insufficient to support a conclusion that parties acted with oppression, fraud or malice. (Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1042.)

 

Meet and Confer

 

“Before filing a motion to strike…the moving party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a).)

 

The parties adequately met and conferred. (Jozana Decl., ¶ 4.)

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move to strike the following portions of the complaint seeking punitive damages: (1) Page 3, paragraph 14, section a(2); (2) Page 5, “Defendant then proceeded to flee the scene to avoid liability. This was a HIT AND RUN incident…”; and (3) Page 5, “reasonably knowing that Defendant…”

 

The complaint alleges: “Defendant was about to rear-end the vehicle directly in front of him. To avoid impact, Defendant swerved his car to the left, and made impact and collided into Plaintiffs' vehicle. Defendant then proceeded to flee the scene to avoid liability. This was a HIT AND RUN incident. Plaintiffs were able to get a video of Defendant's license plate.” (Complaint, p. 5.) Further, the complaint alleges “causing Plaintiffs' to follow Defendant to capture his license plate to provide to police demonstates [sic] despicable conduct with a willful and conscious disregard for the safety of others, including motorists and pedestrians lawfully using the roadway. In fact, VC 20001 allows for imprisonment in state prison for up to four years if an individual causes serious injury and flees the scene, which was the situation here. The fact that the VC punishes indiviudals [sic] with prison time for this conduct showcases the severity of the nature of hit and run incidents and how despicable this conduct is, which again was the sitatuon [sic] here.” (Complaint, p. 5.)

 

Defendants argue that the complaint does not meet the heightened requirements for establishing a claim for punitive damages.

 

In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that Defendants conflate intent to injure with the definition of malice. Plaintiffs argue that hit and runs constitute despicable conduct.

 

To allege a ‘conscious disregard’ of a plaintiff’s rights as opposed to an actual intent to harm, Civil Code section 3294 imposes additional requirements of ‘despicable’ and ‘willful’ conduct. (College Hospital Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 713.) “As amended to include this word [despicable], the statute plainly indicates that absent an intent to injure the plaintiff, ‘malice’ requires more than a ‘willful and conscious’ disregard of the plaintiffs' interests. The additional component of ‘despicable conduct’ must be found.” (Id. at p. 725, citations omitted.) “In order to justify an award of punitive damages on [the basis of a conscious disregard of the safety of others], the plaintiff must establish that the defendant was aware of the probable dangerous consequences of his conduct, and that he wilfully and deliberately failed to avoid those consequences.” (Taylor v. Superior Court (1979) 24 Cal.3d 890, 895-96.)

 

In motor vehicle collision cases, courts have identified:

 

[A]llegations of intoxication, excessive speed, driving with defective equipment or the running of a stop signal, without more, do not state a cause of action for punitive damages. [Par.] On the other hand, if the facts show that the defendant intentionally drove his vehicle at a high speed into an intersection crowded with pedestrians, or if he drove at a high speed through a crowded residential area where children were playing in the street, a legitimate inference of actual malice perhaps could arise. 

 

(Dawes v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 82, 90, citation omitted.) “The failure to stop and render aid has a two-fold significance: (1) It may result in liability for the aggravation of injuries sustained in the accident or additional injuries incurred after it; (2) it may constitute evidence of responsibility for the accident.” (Karl v. C.A. Reed Lumber Co. (1969) 275 Cal.App.2d 358, 362 [addressing hit and runs in the context of jury instructions], citing Brooks v. E.J. Willig Truck Transp. Co. (1953) 40 Cal.2d 669, 679.)

 

Here, based on the allegations of the complaint, the conduct that allegedly caused the injury was Arroyo swerving into Plaintiff’s vehicle “[t]o avoid impact” because “Defendant was about to rear-end the vehicle directly in front of him.” (Complaint, p. 5.) The conduct in hitting and running occurred after the conduct that caused the subject collision. The complaint does not allege that Defendants willfully and deliberately failed to avoid the conduct that arose in injuries. (See Taylor, supra, 24 Cal.3d at p. 895-96.) The complaint does not allege that further injuries occurred based on the conduct after the collision. Moreover, to the extent that the hit and run shows consciousness, coupled with the allegations of the cause of the collision it does not rise to a conscious disregard of Plaintiffs’ rights. “Alone, it is not sufficient to establish liability, but flight immediately after an accident is a circumstance that may be considered with other facts in the case as tending to show a consciousness of responsibility for the accident.” (Karl, supra, 275 Cal.App.2d at p. 362.) Thus, the Court agrees that the allegations in the complaint are insufficient to establish that Defendants were motivated to injury Plaintiffs for purposes of alleging a punitive damages claim.

 

Accordingly, Defendants’ motion to strike portions of the complaint is GRANTED with 30 days LEAVE TO AMEND.

MARJORIE SPENCER v. BOBBI MIESEN, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV01421 

HEARING:  09/17/2206 @ 9:30 AM

 

#13

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants Bobbi Miesen and Tom Miesen’s demurrer to the complaint is MOOT.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendants Bobbi Miesen and Tom Miesen demur to the second through eleventh causes of action in the complaint.

 

On September 2, 2026, Plaintiff Marjorie Spencer (Plaintiff) filed a First Amended Complaint (FAC).

 

“A party may amend its pleading once without leave of the court at any time before the answer, demurrer, or motion to strike is filed, or after a demurrer or motion to strike is filed but before the demurrer or motion to strike is heard if the amended pleading is filed and served no later than the date for filing an opposition to the demurrer or motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 472, subd. (a).)

 

Upon the timely filing of the FAC, the complaint is no longer the operative pleading. Thus, the demurrer to the complaint is MOOT.
