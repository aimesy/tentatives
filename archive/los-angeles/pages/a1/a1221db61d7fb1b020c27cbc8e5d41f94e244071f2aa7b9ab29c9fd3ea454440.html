[tentative] Order RE:

(2)   MOTION TO COMPEL REPSONSES BY DEFENDANT YWCA OF GLENDALE AND PASADENA, TO PLAINTIFF DESTINY KRISTAL DURAN’S FIRST SET OF SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duran

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

(1)   Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories—General Set One and for Monetary Sanctions

(2)   Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories Set One and for Monetary Sanctions

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with these motions.  No opposition papers were filed.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duran (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order (1) compelling responses by YWCA of Glendale and Pasadena (“Defendant”) to Plaintiff’s first set of form interrogatories—general and first set of special interrogatories, and (2) an award of monetary sanctions in the amount of $548.76 on each motion.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motions to compel responses because (1) Plaintiff has shown that Defendant did not serve timely responses to her first set of form and special interrogatories and (2) Defendant did not file opposition papers or other evidence with the court establishing that responses have since been served on Plaintiff.  (Haddad Decl., ¶¶ 3-18; Exs. 1, 3, 6 [proof of electronic service of Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories—General Set One and Special Interrogatories Set One on Defendant’s counsel].)  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).)

The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $548.76, against Defendant on Plaintiff’s motion to compel initial responses to first set of form interrogatories—general.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)

The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $548.76, against Defendant on Plaintiff’s motion to compel responses to first set of special interrogatories.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)

ORDER

            The court grants plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duncan’s motions to compel responses to first set of form interrogatories—general and first set of special interrogatories and for monetary sanctions as follows.

            Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, the court orders defendant YWCA of Glendale and Pasadena to serve on plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duran full and complete verified answers, without objections and which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.220-2030.250, to plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duran’s form interrogatories—general set one and special interrogatories set one, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.

            The court orders defendant YWCA Glendale and Pasadena to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duran in the total amount of $1,097.52 within 30 days of the date of service of this order.

            The court orders plaintiff Destiny Kristal Duran to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[tentative] Order RE:

 

motions to be relieved as counsel for plaintiff

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiffs’ Counsel, Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya of Dolan Law Firm, PC 

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

Motions to be Relieved as Counsel for Plaintiffs

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with these motions.  No opposition papers were filed.

DISCUSSION

Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya on behalf of Dolan Law Firm, PC (“Plaintiffs’ Counsel”) have filed separate motions to be relieved as counsel for plaintiffs Marlen Solano and Ulises Rangel (“Plaintiffs”) in this action.

“The question of granting or denying an application of an attorney to withdraw as counsel (Code Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. 2) is one which lies within the sound discretion of the trial court ‘having in mind whether such withdrawal might work an injustice in the handling of the case.’”¿ (People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 406 [internal quotations omitted].)¿ The court should also consider whether the attorney’s “withdrawal can be accomplished without undue prejudice to the client’s interests.”¿ (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

For a motion to be relieved as counsel under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2), California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) the proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-053)).¿¿¿¿¿ 

The court finds that Plaintiffs’ Counsel’s moving papers are deficient.  First, the two proofs of service filed on July 9, 2026, show that Plaintiffs’ Counsel served Plaintiffs with the moving papers on July 9, 2026.  Thus, Plaintiffs’ Counsel did not timely serve the moving papers.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b) [“all moving and supporting papers shall be served and filed at least 16 court days before the hearing . . . and if the notice is served by facsimile transmission, express mail, or another method of delivery providing for overnight delivery, the required 16-day period of notice before the hearing shall be increased by two calendar days”].)  Second, Plaintiffs Counsel’s declaration also states that “Attorney has personally served the client with copies of the motion papers filed with this declaration.”  (MC-052, ¶ 3a.(1).)  However, the proofs of service show that Plaintiffs’ Counsel served the moving papers on Plaintiffs by overnight delivery, not personal service.  Third, pursuant to California Rules of Court rule 3.1362(d), notice of motion and the moving papers may be given by personal service, electronic service, or mail, not overnight delivery.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).)

The court therefore denies Plaintiffs’ Counsel’s motions to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiffs.

ORDER

The court denies Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya of Dolan Law Firm, PC’s motions to be relieved as counsel without prejudice.

The court orders Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya of Dolan Law Firm, PC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

DATED:  July 16, 2026
