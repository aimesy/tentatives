25AVCV00347 MICHELE M. CARUSO vs CAMACHO AUTO
SALES, INC



Rulings on Motions in Limine.



The following are the Court’s rulings on the parties’
Motions in Limine (“MILs”). Because the following rulings on the motions
in limine are based upon evidence known at the present time, these
rulings are only tentative and may be subject to change as the evidence
unfolds. (See Scott v. C.R. Bard, Inc. (2014) 231 Cal.App.4th
763.) 



 



PLAINTIFF’S MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 1 TO EXCLUDE EVIDENCE
PLAINTIFF WAS TREATED ON “LIEN BASIS” OR THAT PLAINTIFFS’ DOCTORS HAVE A
FINANCIAL INTEREST IN THE LITIGATION.



DENIED. That a treating physician has provided
services secured by a lien on Plaintiff’s recovery in the action bears on the
physician’s bias and is admissible. (Evid. Code, §§ 721, subd. (b), 780, subd.
(f); see also Qaadir v. Figueroa (2021) 67 Cal.App.5th 790,
808 .) Admissibility of lien information for this purpose is not to be used for
arguing that Plaintiff failed to mitigate damages by seeking lien treatment
instead treatment through an insurer if Plaintiff remains responsible for
medical bills. (Pebley v. Santa Clara Organics, LLC (2018) 22
Cal.App.5th 1266, 1269, 1275-1278.)



 



PLAINTIFF’S MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 2: TO EXCLUDE EVIDENCE
THAT PLAINTIFF WAS REFERRED TO DOCTORS BY AN ATTORNEY.



DENIED in part, GRANTED in part. The evidence is
generally relevant and admissible. (Qaadir v. Figueroa (2021) 67
Cal.App.5th 790, 808 (“We agree the referral evidence was relevant
to the question of the reasonable value of the lien-physicians’ medical care
because it may show bias or financial incentives on the part of the
lien-physicians. If a lien-physician wants future referrals from a lawyer and
understands that the lawyer benefits from inflating a client's medical bills,
that incentive might encourage the lien-physician to inflate its current bill
to please the lawyer and win future referrals.”).) However, to the extent
Defendant seeks to examine Plaintiff on communications with her attorney regarding
referrals, such examination is precluded by the attorney-client privilege. The
information may come in through examination of the physicians.



 



PLAINTIFF’S MOTION AND MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 3 TO EXCLUDE
EVIDENCE, TESTIMONY, OR REFERENCE TO THE ILLNESS OR DEATH OF GUSTAVO EFRIAN
CAMACHO’S WIFE OR ANY CLAIM THAT IT CAUSED HIS ABSENCE FROM THE DEALERSHIP.



GRANTED.



 



DEFENDANT’S MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 1 TO EXCLUDE EVIDENCE OR
REFERENCE TO POST-INCIDENT YELLOW OR REFLECTIVE TAPE ON VEHICLE DISPLAY RAMPS
AS SUBSEQUENT REMEDIAL MEASURES.



GRANTED. Evidence that yellow-and-black tape was
physically added to the ramps after the incident is inadmissible when offered
to prove negligence or culpable conduct. (Evid. Code, sec. 1151.) However, if
Defendant disputes feasibility of high-visibility marking/tape, denies
control/ownership, or offers testimony that warnings were unnecessary,
Plaintiff may seek admission of post-incident tape evidence for the limited
purposes permitted by Evidence Code section 1151 (feasibility, control, or
impeachment), subject to Evidence Code section 352 and a limiting instruction.
(Evid. Code, § 1151; Alcaraz v. Vece (1997) 14 Cal.4th 1149, 1169.)
