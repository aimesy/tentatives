Case Number: 23SMCV05502    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant/Cross-Defendant Parker Noel’s Motion for Leave to File a First Amended Cross-Complaint is GRANTED. Noel to separately file the first amended cross-complaint within 10 days of this order.

Noel to give notice.

Noel represents discovery revealed new and different information supporting the proposed new defendants and additional causes of action. Noel denies any prejudice. Noel concludes with a position that all claims relate back to the original filing date. A proposed copy of the first amended complaint is included. [Declaration of James G. Bohm, ¶¶ 4-5, Ex. A-B.]

Pickford Real Estate, Inc. dba Berkshire Hathaway Home Services California Properties (Pickford) in opposition challenges the proposed pleading as unreasonably delayed without excuse, and generating great prejudice. Pickford also challenges any basis of liability pled against the proposed, new cross-defendants.

Noel in reply challenges any premature determination as to the merits of any claims against the proposed new defendants, denies any substantial prejudice, and maintains a sufficient showing of reasonable conduct leading to the proposed amended cross-complaint. Counsel also presents a supplemental reply more specifically articulating the timeline for the motion, based at least in part on the October 23, 2025, and December 16, 2025 depositions of Leslie Gornik and Patrick McKenna. The depositions led to the discovery of the additional facts supporting the new claims. [Supp. Decl. James G. Bohm, ¶¶ 5-6.] The new proposed defendants were substituted into the case on May 5, 2026, and the motion was filed on May 8, 2026. 

While the original motion and declaration lacked specific information regarding the timing of the exact discovery, the reply and supplemental declaration sufficiently explain the basis for the later discovery. The delays between the last deposition in December 2025, and the filing of the motion in May 2026, in no way demonstrates dilatory conduct supporting a finding of material prejudice. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.)  The trial remains set for June 2027, thereby leaving Pickford sufficient time to address any and all new allegations.  (Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 486-488.) 

Pickford otherwise lacks standing to address any positions as to the proposed, but not yet appearing new cross-defendants. Regardless, Pickford establishes no bar as a matter of law. Cross-Defendatns may proceed via demurrer or motion to strike. (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Sup.Ct. (Marker, U.S.A.) (1989) 213 Cal. App.3d 1045, 1048; California Casualty Gen. Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1985) 173 Cal.App.3d 274, 280–281 disapproved of on other grounds by Kransco v. American Empire Surplus Lines Ins. Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 390.)

The motion for leave to amend is granted. Noel to separately file the proposed first amended cross-complaint within 10 days of this order.

Case Number: 24SMCV05091    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant Seyfarth Shaw LLP’s motion to compel further responses to deposition questions
is DENIED.

Seyfarth Shaw LLP to give notice. 

REASONING

Defendant Seyfarth Shaw LLP (Seyfarth) moves to compel further responses to deposition questions from counsel for Health Holdings, Inc., Team Health, LLC; AmeriTeam Services, LLC, and Chase Dennis Emergency Medical Group, Inc. (Plaintiffs) Lawrence Eastwood regarding Exhibits F, G, and H, as attached to the declaration of Jonathan Brody. 

Legal Standard
If a deponent fails to answer any question … the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (a).) A party may also move for production of documents. (Ibid.) A motion to compel answers to a deposition must be filed within 60 days of the completion of the “deposition record,” and accompanied by a meet and confer letter. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subds. (a-b).)

Request for Judicial Notice
The court takes judicial notice of its own prior orders and policies.
The court takes judicial notice of the existence of filed pleading in the district court action, but cannot take notice as to the content of any filed document for the truth of the matter asserted. To the extent Plaintiffs appear to present the pleading as a source of authority, the court cannot take judicial notice of unpublished rulings or pleadings. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.1115(a); Rittiman v. Public Utilities Com. (2022) 80 Cal.App.5th 1018, 1043 (footnote 18).)

Motion
The motion lacks any specific address as to the timing of the prior deposition. The deposition in question was presented under seal. Given the April 29, 2026, IDC on the subject matter, the court finds the motion timely.

The subject information involves questions from Seyfarth regarding certain prior deposition exhibits relied upon during mediation in the underlying class action case. In house counsel, Eastwood was instructed not to answer the questions on grounds of the mediation privilege.

Seyfarth challenges the objections on grounds that the content of the depositions constitutes a document prepared in the ordinary course of litigation and not specific to any mediation proceeding. Seyfarth additionally contends the depositions were taken and the transcripts prepared by Seyfarth during representation, thereby allowing Seyfarth to waive any privileges.

Plaintiffs in opposition both maintains applicability of the mediation privilege, and challenge the claim of unilateral waiver of the privilege related to the preparation of mediation documents on behalf of Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs cite the April 29, 2026, IDC order, whereby the court found the August 30, 2021, email “compelling as a ‘start date’ for the later submitted Seyfarth documents which are argued to be mediation related.”

Seyfarth in reply notes that Plaintiffs both bring an action for legal malpractice, yet seek to exclude the evidence relating to “trial within a trial” standard. Seyfarth denies any of the sought after information encompasses the mediation, and Plaintiffs otherwise fail to meet the criteria for mediation confidentiality. 

Attorney Client Privilege
Seyfarth maintains no attorney client privilege applies, and Plaintiffs offer no counter position. Plaintiffs never objected at deposition, and apparently concede to waiver through placement of the conduct at issue in the subject action. (Evid. Code, § 958.)

Mediation Privilege
Evidence Code section 1119 bars discovery of evidence regarding “anything said or any admission made for the purpose of, in the course of, or pursuant to, a mediation or a mediation consultation is admissible or subject to discovery, and disclosure of the evidence shall not be compelled, in any arbitration, administrative adjudication, civil action, or other noncriminal proceeding in which, pursuant to law, testimony can be compelled to be given.” (Evid. Code, § 1119, subd. (a).) Unlike the attorney client privilege exception, a malpractice claim will in no way create an exception under the mediation privilege barring a showing of explicit waiver. (Cassel v. Superior Court (2011) 51 Cal.4th 113, 118-119; see Evid. Code, § 1122; Simmons v. Ghaderi (2008) 44 Cal.4th 570, 588.) Seyfarth raises no position regarding waiver.

Seyfarth offers no challenge to the prior finding by the court regarding the “compelling” August 30, 2021, “start date” for the beginning of the mediation process. Seyfarth instead seeks to distinguish any date limitations with a position that the sought after documents were previously produced as part of another routine aspect of any case defense “months before the mediation was scheduled.” Said documents “would have existed ... with or without a mediation.” [Declaration of Jonathan Brophy, ¶¶ 7-8, 11-13; Declaration of Michael Afar, ¶¶ 5-10, 12.]

The mediation privilege “applies to any writing ‘prepared for the purpose of, in the course of, or pursuant to, a mediation or a mediation consultation.’” (Lappe v. Superior Court (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 774, 784.) Even if the documents were presented for mediation, such presentation does not render them under the mediation umbrella privilege. (Ibid.; Evid. Code, § 1120; Rojas v. Superior Court (2004) 33 Cal.4th 407, 417.)

A review of the work leads to a finding of a document routinely produced for a client in a case of this nature, while also clearly instructive to any mediation proceedings for purposes of establishing parameters and valuation. [Brophy & Afar Decl.] The information appears integral to both mediation as well as the crux of the subject action regarding the omission of a number of persons from the class thereby requiring a second settlement agreement.

On a threshold note, all entries in the separate statement in support of the motion identify emails dated from September 1, 2021, through November 11, 2021. As stated in the IDC order, said emails constitute a compelling basis for a finding of the mediation privilege. Seyfarth seeks to establish a factual distinction between the original pre-mediation prepared document relative to the same referenced document in the privilege protected emails. The operative document in question predates the August 31, 2021, email preceding the mediation. 

Plaintiffs rely on the intertwined nature of the Seyfarth work relative to potential impending mediation positions. Plaintiffs also contend that a routine practice by Seyfarth in no way negates the purpose of the confidentiality statute itself. 

The legal malpractice claim relies at least in part on a claim that Plaintiffs relied on the insufficiently determined number of potential claimants in entering into the settlement agreement only to later discover the error. In-House counsel for Plaintiff Team Health Holdings, Inc. and Chase Dennie Emergency Medical Group, Inc., and deponent, Lawrence Eastwood, states that the Seyfarth prepared document was presented for the purpose of developing a settlement strategy prior to the first mediation. [Declaration of Lawrence Eastwood, ¶¶ 10-30.] 

The relied upon information somewhat if not substantially framed potential considerations relied upon prior to entering mediation and executing the settlement agreement. [Ibid.] Reliance on information related to mediation can fall under the privilege. (Amis v. Greenberg Traurig LLP (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 331, 340.) In other words, even accepting the existence of the routinely prepared content as part of a pattern and practice for wage and hour litigation by Seyfarth, the motion still lacks a meaningful distinction as to any separation of purpose. Plaintiffs relied upon the established parameters prior to participating in mediation. Reframing based on prior conduct and routine will not counter the state of mind of attorney Eastwood in considering settlement. The sought after distinction between preceding documents and documents presented for purposes of mediation and settlement lack a material distinction under the statutory policy. [Ibid.]

Communications “‘made for the purpose of, in the course of, or pursuant to, a mediation or a mediation consultation’ and are not subject to disclosure. .... This would include an order prohibiting questioning or soliciting information in a deposition or by written discovery as to the content of the” documents utilized for mediation. (Wimsatt v. Superior Court (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 137, 159.) Reliance on the temporal gap between the scheduling and participation and mediation insufficiently overcomes the burden regarding the claimed request for preparation by the client for purposes of preparing for mediation and potential settlement. 

The motion to compel further response is DENIED.

Case Number: 25SMCV02996    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Sean Jordan’s Motion for Sanctions is DENIED. 

Jordan to give notice. 

REASONING

Legal Standard
Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 requires that an attorney sign all pleadings, petitions, notices of motions, and other similar papers.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (a).) Thus, by presenting a pleading or other similar paper to the court, an attorney is certifying that to the best of his or her knowledge, information, and belief, “formed after an inquiry reasonable under the circumstances,” all of the following conditions are met:

 (1) It is not being presented primarily for an improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in the cost of litigation.

(2) The claims, defenses, and other legal contentions therein are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law.

(3) The allegations and other factual contentions have evidentiary support or, if specifically so identified, are likely to have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery.

(4) The denials of factual contentions are warranted on the evidence or, if specifically so identified, are reasonably based on a lack of information or belief.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (b).)  

The section “authorizes trial courts to impose sanctions to check abuses in the filing of pleadings, petitions, written notices of motions or similar papers.” (Musaelian v. Adams (2009) 45 Cal.4th 512, 514.) A violation of any of these certifications may give rise to sanctions. (Eichenbaum v. Alon (2003) 106 Cal.App.4th 967, 976.) An objective standard of review applies to section 128.7. (San Diegans for Open Government v. City of San Diego (2016) 247 Cal.App.4th 1306, 1318; Bockrath v. Aldrich Chemical Co., Inc. (1999) 21 Cal.4th 71, 82.) Whether a claim is meritless or for the sole purpose of harassment must be evaluated by examining whether the factual allegations of the claim had evidentiary support. (580 Folsom Associates v. Prometheus Development Co. (1990) 223 Cal.App.3d 1, 22.) “Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 imposes a lower threshold for sanctions than is required under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5. This is because Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 requires only that the conduct be ‘objectively unreasonable,’ while Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5 also requires ‘a showing of subjective bad faith.’ (Citation.)” (Guillemin v. Stein (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 156, 167.)

Self Represented Party

“Although self-represented litigants are not entitled to special treatment, they are entitled to the same treatment as a represented party. ... ‘Trial judges must acknowledge that in propria persona litigants often do not have an attorney's level of knowledge about the legal system and are more prone to misunderstanding the court's requirements.’ (Citation). When one party has counsel and the other does not, the trial court ‘should monitor to ensure the in propria persona litigant is not inadvertently misled, either by the represented party or by the court... [S]pecial care should be used to make sure that verbal instructions given in court and written notices are clear and understandable by a layperson.’” (Petrosyan v. Prince Corp. (2013) 223 Cal.App.4th 587, 594.)

July 29, 2025, Declaration
The motion arises from the July 29, 2025, filed declaration for an automatic 30-day extension to respond to the complaint. Plaintiff contends that paragraph 4 of the statement regarding the inability to meet and confer due to the health conditions of Plaintiff at the relevant time constitutes a false statement. [Motion, 3:4-15.]

The paragraph reads: “4. In accordance with Defendants’ meet and confer requirements, on July 18, 2025, I sent Plaintiff SEAN JORDAN (“Plaintiff”) written correspondence requesting that we meet and confer via telephone regarding deficiencies in his complaint that are subject to demurrer. On July 22, 2025, Plaintiff replied to me via email stating that due to health conditions that Plaintiff relates he is suffering from, he would not be able to confer telephonically. As such, we have not been able to meet our obligations to attempt to meet and confer thus far.”

Jordan represents denial of any stipulated extension on July 30, 2025, but also admits to insisting on “in writing” conference only, with an “in person” meeting upon a grant of “reasonable accommodation, if “strictly necessary.” [Motion, 3:18-24.]

On February 12, 2026, Defense counsel “declined to withdraw or correct the challenged declaration,” thereby leading to the subject motion. [Motion, 3:25-4:3.]

Safe Harbor
Defendant in opposition challenges the motion on grounds of lack of compliance with the safe harbor provision, lack of articulation of sanctionable conduct, and lack of facts supporting sanctions. 

On a procedural level, the motion alleges compliance with the safe harbor provision on July 30, 2025. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c)(1).) Plaintiff appears to rely on a July 30, 2025, email, whereby Plaintiff wrote in part:

“I do not stipulate to any extension. As stated repeatedly, I remain willing to meet and confer in writing—or, if strictly necessary, in person with reasonable accommodation. I declined a telephone conference solely for documented medical and language-access reasons. Any contrary statement in your 29 July 2025 declaration is false and misleading. Your declaration fails to satisfy CCP § 430.41(a)(1): it provides no specific legal basis for any objection to any cause of action. Accordingly, the declaration is non-compliant and cannot support an extension. 

“Unless, by Friday, 1 August 2025, you deliver all of the following, I will file a declaration asking the Court to deny your extension: 

“1. A red-lined letter or table identifying every cause of action you challenge; 2. For each cause, the precise element you claim is missing; 3. The controlling authority supporting each objection; 4. Corrections to the false statements in your 29 July declaration regarding (a) method of service, (b) my repeated offers to confer, and (c) the nature of my accommodation request. 

“Your assertion of overnight or first-class service is incorrect; I have never been served in that manner. If you contend otherwise, produce proof of service. Likewise, describing my accommodation request as a refusal to confer is materially misleading. If—and only if—you comply in full by the deadline, I will consider a without-prejudice extension, reserving all rights, including filing a First Amended Complaint on or before 15 August 2025. Please confirm by close of business 1 August that you will provide the required objections, correct your declaration, and restrict future communication to writing unless another modality is expressly agreed. If you fail to comply, I will file my declaration seeking denial of the extension and any other appropriate relief.” [Declaration of Sean Jordan.]

The safe harbor language requires actual identification of the offending item and a demand for withdrawal. “Notice of motion shall be served as provided in Section 1010, but shall not be filed with or presented to the court unless, within 21 days after service of the motion, or any other period as the court may prescribe, the challenged paper, claim, defense, contention, allegation, or denial is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected. If warranted, the court may award to the party prevailing on the motion the reasonable expenses and attorney's fees incurred in presenting or opposing the motion. Absent exceptional circumstances, a law firm shall be held jointly responsible for violations committed by its partners, associates, and employees.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c)(1).)

The email accuses counsel of a false statement, but by no means demands the categorical and specific withdrawal of paragraph (four) 4. Plaintiff only demands a meet and confer statement by certain deadlines. The safe harbor provision therefore lacks compliance.

Substantive Sanctions Request
Jordan continues to rely on the alleged false statement. Counsel in opposition cites to Plaintiff’s own July 22, 2025, email exchanges whereby Plaintiff represents unavailability to meet and confer due to “surgery, school and medical treatment.” [Jordan Decl.] Jordan indicates a preference for email correspondence, but also ultimately agrees to an extension to August 25, 2025. [Jordan Decl.] The demurrer was filed on August 11, 2025.  Plaintiff in reply describes the opposition as “meritless and pretextual.” Plaintiff proceeds to describe the extension as an effort to “evade accountability.”

Plaintiff lacks any factual support regarding the lack of veracity regarding paragraph 4 (four) given Plaintiff’s own attached exhibits of corresponding email from July 22 and July 30, 2025. If Plaintiff wishes to proclaim under oath as to the falsity of the content of the emails and therefore potentially admitting to a false presentation of fact to this court in the instant motion, Plaintiff may make such a representation iina separate motion with the understanding of the Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination.

Underlying Statutory Compliance
Defendant maintains that even if the court considered the merits, Defendant remained entitled to an automatic extension. 

“The parties shall meet and confer at least 5 days before the date the responsive pleading is due. If the parties are not able to meet and confer at least 5 days before the date the responsive pleading is due, the demurring party shall be granted an automatic 30-day extension of time within which to file a responsive pleading, by filing and serving, on or before the date on which a demurrer would be due, a declaration stating under penalty of perjury that a good faith attempt to meet and confer was made and explaining the reasons why the parties could not meet and confer. The 30-day extension shall commence from the date the responsive pleading was previously due, and the demurring party shall not be subject to default during the period of the extension. Any further extensions shall be obtained by court order upon a showing of good cause.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(2).)

As addressed above, the extension was properly supported and factually agreed upon. The statement regarding unavailability to telephonically confer with Western Holdings Group dba Los Angeles Property Management Group supports the automatically available extension for time to file a responsive pleading, such as a demurer and/or motion to strike. The request was also made for purposes of examining potential insurance coverage, which possibly benefitted Plaintiff. Plaintiff’s response was provided after the initial filing deadline, thereby necessitating the extension declaration. The demand for a further meet and confer were only provided after the lapse of the deadline extension cutoff, and in order to avoid default. [Declaration of John Barbar, ¶¶ 3-8, 10, 12, 14.]

The declaration for extension was therefore timely and properly filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(2).) The court ruled on the demurrer and the case is now at issue. Nothing in the course of conduct establishes any prejudicial delay or other material impacts invoking the concerns of section 128.7.

Conclusion
The motion for sanctions is therefore denied. The request for counter sanctions in the opposition is also denied. Western Holdings Group dba Los Angeles Property Management Group needs to file an actual separate motion. 

Case Number: 26SMCV00432    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Stephen Shapiro, Trustee of The Stephen Shapiro Revocable 1996 Trust’s Demurrer to the Answer to the First Amended Complaint of Plaintiff Stephen Shapiro, Trustee of The Stephen Shapiro Revocable 1996 Trust is OVERRULED. 

Plaintiff Stephen Shapiro, Trustee of The Stephen Shapiro Revocable 1996 Trust to give notice. 

REASONING

Plaintiff Stephen Shapiro, Trustee of The Stephen Shapiro Revocable 1996 Trust submits the subject demurer on grounds that all but the second, fourth, sixth, ninth, sixteenth, twenty first, twenty fourth, and twenty ninth, thirty fourth, thirty fifth, and thirty sixth, affirmative defenses lack sufficient facts, with certain items duplicative as well.


Plaintiff challenges the identified defenses on general grounds of uncertainty. On the first, third, twentieth, twenty first, twenty seventh, twenty eighth, and thirty fifth affirmative defenses, Plaintiff contends said defenses represent a “new” matter without sufficient factual support, as opposed to a proper denial. Defendants in opposition maintain sufficiently pled defenses and deny any obligation to present evidentiary support under a heightened pleading standard. All defenses invoke an established and recognized legal doctrine. Defendants may plead alternative defenses even if potentially redundant. Plaintiff in reply contends Defendants fail to address the “new matter” position. Plaintiff also reiterates the lack of sufficient “ultimate facts” position, and duplicative positions. 

The court finds no support for the position that the “denials” in the identified defenses constitute a “new” matter as opposed to a traverse. The challenged defenses are directly responsive to the allegations in the complaint. Further, the general challenge to the affirmative defenses constitutes glib conclusion without sufficient substantive address establishing deficiencies. The court finds nothing improper in the defenses, thereby warranting a heightened pleading standard. 

Even if Plaintiffs established such support, the court finds the case law governing demurrer supports allowing further discovery and otherwise protecting a defendant’s right to preserve defenses, rather than risk waiver. The thirty sixth defense for Reservation of Additional Affirmative Defenses facilitates any potential, later sought after amendments following further discovery. The court declines to force further factual allegations at the risk of potentially waiving an affirmative defense based on further needed discovery at the beginning of litigation. Thus, barring a legal conflict established by the challenging party, the court adheres to the liberal policy of allowing affirmative defenses with the intent of the parties engaging in discovery for further clarification of any and all claims. The court therefore finds the argument challenging improper reliance on conclusions and the lack of ultimate facts insufficiently supported, and overrules the demurrer on this basis.

The demurrer is OVERRULED in its entirety. The parties may engage in discovery regarding support of the defenses. The case is at issue.
