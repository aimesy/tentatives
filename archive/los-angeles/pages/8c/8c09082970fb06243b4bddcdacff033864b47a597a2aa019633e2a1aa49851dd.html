Deny

 

I.                BACKGROUND

On May 1, 2024, Plaintiffs Andrea Ross Enoch, individually as successor-in-interest for the Estate of Brian Enoch, Adrian Enoch, Ryan Enoch, Cassidy Enoch, and Justine Madara (“Plaintiffs”) filed a complaint against Defendants Sherman Oaks Hospital, Berj Dermendjian, D.O., Robert Moghimi, M.D., Epic Medical Group, and Does 1 through 300, alleging causes of action for: (1) wrongful death based on medical malpractice (CCP section 377.60); (2) negligence – Thing v. LaChusa; and (3) medical malpractice through a survival action (CCP section 377.20).

On November 22, 2024, Plaintiffs filed an amendment to complaint identifying Doe 201 as North Valley GI Consultants.

On May 8, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a request to dismiss Defendant North Valley GI Consultants (Doe 201) and that Defendant was dismissed.

On April 1, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a request to dismiss Defendant Dr. Berj Dermendjian, D.O., and that Defendant was dismissed.

On April 17, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a request to dismiss Defendant Robert Moghimi, M.D. and that Defendant was dismissed.

On April 28, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a notice of settlement of entire case.

On May 29, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a motion to enforce the settlement agreement (the “Motion”). On June 11, 2026, Defendant Prime Healthcare Services – Sherman Oaks LLC dba Sherman Oaks Hospital  (“Defendant”) filed an opposition. On June 17, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a reply.

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, subdivision (a) states:

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

III.           DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs and Defendant entered a settlement agreement on March 31, 2026 (the “Settlement Agreement”). (Podgurski Declaration ¶ 3.) Plaintiffs move for an order enforcing the Settlement Agreement pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, entering judgment against Defendant in the sum of $500,000, awarding prejudgment interest, and awarding contractual attorneys’ fees and costs in the sum of $8,165.75. (Motion pp. 2, 9.)

In opposition, Defendant argues the Motion is moot because the settlement check was delivered to Plaintiffs’ counsel’s office on June 4, 2026. (Opposition pp. 3-4.)

In reply, Plaintiffs state they received the settlement funds. (Reply p. 2.) Plaintiffs argue they are still entitled to attorneys’ fees because Defendant failed to timely pay the settlement funds and Defendant cites no “case law holding that attorneys fees may be denied under a valid contractual provision because the payment ‘took longer than expected’.” (Reply pp. 2-3.)

The parties agree Defendant paid the settlement funds on June 4, 2026. (Reply p. 2; Opposition p. 3.) The only dispute remaining relates to attorneys’ fees and costs.

The Settlement Agreement contains an attorneys’ fees provision, stating: “If there is any litigation to enforce the Release or any of its terms, the prevailing party is entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees and costs from the losing party.” (Podgurski Declaration Ex. 1 pdf p. 9.)

Defendant argues the court should deny Plaintiffs’ request for fees because although “the payment of settlement funds took longer than expected” Defendant’s counsel “continued to communicate with Plaintiffs’ Counsel regarding the delay and providing assurances that the settlement funds would be sent to Plaintiffs’ Counsel immediately upon receipt.” (Opposition p. 4.) Defendant also argues the court should not award Plaintiffs costs and fees because Defendant “cooperated in good faith throughout the case and settlement process.” (Opposition p. 4.)

Defendant does not dispute it failed to timely deliver payment to Plaintiffs under the Settlement Agreement. It is irrelevant that Defendant’s counsel remained in contact with Plaintiffs’ counsel. Further, the attorneys’ fees and costs clause contains no exception for cooperating in good faith.

However, Defendant paid Plaintiffs the settlement funds. Plaintiffs cite no authority suggesting Plaintiffs are entitled to attorneys’ fees and costs, despite the Motion being moot. Plaintiffs’ reply also lacks analysis as to why Plaintiffs are entitled to attorneys’ fees and costs under the attorneys’ fees and costs clause when the underlying issue is moot.

The Motion is moot because Defendant paid Plaintiffs the settlement funds. There is no prevailing or losing party here, so costs and fees are not warranted.

IV.           CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the Motion is denied.

COURT’S [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

Motion to Enforce Compliance with the Court’s

February 27, 2026 Order

 

 

 

 

[THE FOLLOWING IS A TENTATIVE RULING IN THE ABOVE CASE]:

 

Deny

I.                BACKGROUND¿¿ 

¿ 

On April 18, 2024, Plaintiffs Kevin Quintero and Gabriela Quintero (“Plaintiffs”) purchased a 2024 Volkswagen Tiguan (the “Vehicle”). (Complaint ¶ 8.) The Vehicle was defective. (Complaint ¶ 10.)

 

On March 26, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a complaint against Defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 10, alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Act - breach of express warranty; (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act - breach of implied warranty; and (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act section 1793.2.

 

On July 11, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a motion to compel further responses to Plaintiffs’ Request for Production of Documents, Set One (the “Motion to Compel Further Responses”). On February 27, 2026, the court granted the Motion to Compel Further Responses, as limited by the court’s order, and directed Defendant to provide further responses within thirty days of the court’s order (the “February 27 Order”). (02/27/26 Minute Order p. 10.)

 

On May 18, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a motion to enforce compliance with the court’s February 27, 2026 order (the “Motion”). On June 11, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. On June 17, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a reply.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete. (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive. (3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).) “The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).)¿¿¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.010 states:

Misuses of the discovery process include, but are not limited to, the following:

(a) Persisting, over objection and without substantial justification, in an attempt to obtain information or materials that are outside the scope of permissible discovery.

(b) Using a discovery method in a manner that does not comply with its specified procedures.

(c) Employing a discovery method in a manner or to an extent that causes unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense.

(d) Failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery.

(e) Making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery.

(f) Making an evasive response to discovery.

(g) Disobeying a court order to provide discovery.

(h) Making or opposing, unsuccessfully and without substantial justification, a motion to compel or to limit discovery.

(i) Failing to confer or to attempt to confer, in person, by telephone, or by videoconference with an opposing party or attorney in a reasonable and good faith attempt to resolve informally any dispute concerning discovery, if the section governing a particular discovery motion requires the filing of a declaration stating facts showing that an attempt at informal resolution has been made.

III.           DISCUSSION

The Motion, Opposition, and Reply:

Plaintiffs move for an order compelling Defendant’s compliance with the February 27 Order. (Motion p. 2.) Specifically, Plaintiffs move to compel Defendant to provide further verified responses to Plaintiffs’ Request for Production of Documents, Set One (“RFPs”) Nos. 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11, and to produce documents responsive to RFP No. 31. (Motion p. 2.)  Plaintiffs state Defendant’s counsel did not respond to Plaintiffs’ counsel’s meet and confer efforts. (Motion p. 7.) Plaintiffs also seek terminating and monetary sanctions against Defendant. (Motion p. 2.)

In opposition, Defendant argues the Motion is moot because it served Plaintiffs with further supplemental responses on March 30, 2026. (Opposition pp. 5-6.) Defendant also states it provided further supplemental responses on June 11, 2026. (Opposition p. 5.) Defendant states Defendant’s counsel did not respond to the meet and confer efforts because Defendant’s counsel was preparing for trial, traveling to, and attending an in-person final status conference and out of the office. (Motion p. 6.) Finally, Defendant argues sanctions should be denied because it acted in good faith in complying with court’s February 27 Order. (Opposition pp. 7-10.)

In reply, Plaintiffs argue Defendant’s counsel ignored Plaintiffs’ counsel’s meet and confer efforts, and Defendant’s June 11, 2026, further response to RFP No. 31 remains non-complaint. (Reply p. 4.) Specifically, Plaintiffs argue Defendant’s response “is not a clear, unequivocal statement of compliance under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.220” and instead “Defendant qualified and limited its response by referring only to ‘Repair Order and VTA’ cases’ which creates ambiguity. (Reply p. 5.)

Meet and Confer:

Motions to compel further responses must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(2).)

Plaintiffs’ counsel submits a declaration stating they repeatedly called and emailed Defendant’s counsel but received no response. (Hozven Declaration ¶¶ 12-14.)

Defendant’s counsel submits a declaration stating they acknowledge Plaintiffs’ counsel’s May 5, 2026, and May 6, 2026, meet and confer correspondence, but Defendant’s counsel was preparing for trial and traveling to and attending an in-person final status conference on May 6, 2026. (Simpson-James Declaration ¶ 5.) Defendant’s counsel also states:

Defendant further acknowledges Plaintiffs’ May 14, 2026 and May 18, 2026 meet and confer correspondences wherein Plaintiffs’ counsel attempted to reach me by telephone for the first time. In response to Plaintiffs’ emails, Plaintiffs received an automatic response stating that I was out of the office. However, despite Plaintiffs’ notice that I was out of office until May 25, 2026, Plaintiffs’ counsel still attempted to “reach” me while knowing I was unavailable. Immediately thereafter, Plaintiff filed the instant motion on May 18, 2026.

(Simpson-James Declaration ¶ 6.)

It is unclear to the court whether Defendant’s counsel immediately went from preparing for trial to being out of the office for the entire period between May 6, 2026, and May 25, 2026. There is no evidence Defendant’s counsel communicated with Plaintiffs’ counsel until serving Plaintiffs with further supplemental responses on June 11, 2026, the same day Defendant filed its opposition. (Simpson-James Declaration ¶ 8.)

The court finds Plaintiffs’ counsel satisfied the meet and confer requirement. The court further finds Defendant’s counsel fails to set forth an explanation satisfactory to this court for Defendant’s counsel’s failure to respond to Plaintiffs’ counsel’s numerous meet and confer efforts.

RFP Nos. 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, and 31:

Plaintiffs’ reply indicates only RFP No. 31 remains at issue. (Reply pp. 4-5.) Plaintiffs argue Defendant’s response is ambiguous as to whether Defendant has produced all responsive documents. (Reply pp. 4-5.)

Defendant’s response to RFP No. 31, served to Plaintiffs June 11, 2026, states:

VWGoA will comply with this Request in whole. Production of all responsive documents in Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.'s possession, custody, or control are allowed. VWGoA has produced all responsive documents in its possession, custody, or control, Repair Order and VTA cases (Exhibit C bates number 000012-55,000063-000076).

(Simpson-James Declaration Ex. C pdf p. 49.) Plaintiffs argue this response is ambiguous because, as for RFP Nos. 7 through 11 “Defendant expressly stated: ‘VWGoA has produced all responsive documents in its possession, custody, or control.’ Defendant then separately identified the documents produced.” (Reply p. 5.)

The court finds Defendant’s response is sufficient. Defendant states it has “produced all responsive documents in its possession, custody, or control . . .” (Simpson-James Declaration Ex. C pdf p. 49.) The court interprets Defendant’s subsequent references to documents as references to the documents Defendant is stating are responsive to the request.

Thus, it is unnecessary for the court to order Defendant produce a further response to RFP No. 31.

Sanctions:

“The discovery statutes evince an incremental approach to discovery sanctions, starting with monetary sanctions and ending with the ultimate sanction of termination.” (Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc.¿(2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 967, 992.) “Discovery sanctions ‘should be appropriate to the dereliction, and should not exceed that which is required to protect the interests of the party entitled to but denied discovery.’” (Id.) “If a lesser sanction fails to curb misuse, a greater sanction is warranted: continuing misuses of the discovery process warrant incrementally harsher sanctions until the sanction is reached that will curb the abuse. ‘A decision to order terminating sanctions should not be made lightly. But where a violation is willful, preceded by a history of abuse, and the evidence shows that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with the discovery rules, the trial court is justified in imposing the ultimate sanction[.]’” (Id.)

 

Plaintiffs request the court impose terminating and monetary sanctions. (Motion pp. 9-11.)

The court finds Defendant’s counsel fails to offer a sufficient explanation for their failure to respond to Plaintiffs’ counsel’s meet and confer attempts. The court further finds that Defendant’s initial supplemental responses, dated March 30, 2026, did not comply with the court’s February 27 Order.  For example, the February 27 Order directed Defendant to provide further responses to RFP Nos. 1 to 29 and 31 as limited by the court’s order, within thirty (30) days. (02/27/26 Minute Order p. 10.)  As for RFP Nos. 1 to 14, the court found “[t]he RFPs are reasonably particularized and are not overbroad, vague, or ambiguous. They clearly seek documents relevant to this action relating to the Vehicle, Defendant’s affirmative defenses, or Plaintiffs.” (02/27/26 Minute Order p. 3.) In Defendant’s further response to RFP No. 8, dated March 30, 2026, Defendant stated:

VWGoA objects on grounds that Plaintiff has not designated the items being sought for production by specifically describing each individual item or by reasonably particularizing each category of item (CCP § 2031.030 (c)(1)). VWGoA also objects to this request on grounds that it is overbroad and compound. VWGoA also objects on grounds that it is invasive of the trade secret privilege and seeks confidential and proprietary business information, disclosure of which could damage VWGoA's competitive position in the marketplace. VWGoA also objects to this request on grounds that it assumes facts and lacks foundation.

Subject to and without waiving the foregoing objections, VWGoA has produced all recalls notices, technical service bulletins, and dealer advisories pertaining to any warranty repairs performed on the subject vehicle. Please see the TSBs/Recalls (Exhibit G).

(Simpson-James Declaration Ex. A pdf pp. 12-13.)

Reasserting the same objections the court addressed in its order and found were meritless is not complying with the court’s order. It should not require a second noticed motion for Defendant to comply with its discovery obligations when there is already a court order addressing Defendant’s objections and directing Defendant to provide responses.¿¿

Defendant has now produced responses to all the RFPs Plaintiffs argue in the Motion were non-compliant with the court’s February 27 Order. Thus, the court concludes terminating sanctions are not warranted. Plaintiffs’ request for monetary sanctions in the form of $500 “for each day Defendant fails to fully comply with the Court’s Orders” is likewise not warranted here because Defendant has now produced responses compliant with the February 27 Order. (Motion p. 12.)

Finally, Plaintiffs state their counsel “has incurred recoverable costs and attorney’s fees.” (Motion p. 10.) Plaintiffs request no specific sum of sanctions for costs and attorneys’ fees. Thus, the court will award none.

IV.           CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiffs’ request the court order Defendant to produce further supplemental responses is denied as moot. Plaintiffs’ requests for terminating and monetary sanctions are also denied.

COURT’S [Tentative] ORDER RE:      

 Motion to Compel Further Discovery Responses

 

 

[THE FOLLOWING IS A TENTATIVE RULING IN THE ABOVE CASE]:

Grant, in part, and deny, in part.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from a motor vehicle accident that occurred on June 7, 2023. (Complaint p. 5.) Plaintiff Guadalupe Jimenes Ribera (“Plaintiff”) sustained injuries arising from the accident. (Complaint p. 5.)

 

On May 8, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Vivi Edri, Avivit Edri, and Does 1 to 20, alleging causes of action for: (1) motor vehicle; and (2) general negligence and negligence per se.

 

On March 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel Defendant Vivi Edri and Avivit Edri. further responses to Form Interrogatories and Production of Documents and for sanctions (the “Motion”). On May 12, 2026, Defendants Vivi Edri and Avivit Edri (“Defendants”) filed an opposition. On May 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.             SEPARATE MOTIONS

 

Plaintiff filed a single motion to compel further responses to two sets of discovery from two Defendants. (Motion p. 2.) The Motion should have been filed as four motions, one for each discovery set and each Defendant.

 

Combining motions deprives the court of filing fees, unfairly jumps ahead of other litigants, and negatively impacts the court’s calendar by placing more motions on the calendar than slots have been provided by the court’s reservation system.

 

“[I]t is mandatory for court clerks to demand and receive the fee required by statute before documents or pleadings are filed.” (Duran v. St. Luke’s Hospital (2004) 114 Cal.App.4th 457, 459.) Plaintiff is ordered to pay the requisite filing fees.

 

III.           TIMELINESS

A motion to compel further responses to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling must be brought within 45 days of receiving the challenged responses, or as otherwise agreed upon in writing.¿ (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.310, subd. (c).) A motion to compel further responses to interrogatories must be brought within 45 days of receiving the challenged responses, or as otherwise agreed upon in writing.¿ (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.300, subd. (c).)¿ Electronic service extends the deadline by two court days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (a)(3)(B).)

 

Defendants served Plaintiff with responses to the Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production of Documents (“RFPs”) on January 20, 2026, by electronic service. (Gomez Declaration ¶¶ 2, 4; Ex. 2 pdf pp. 51, 69.) Plaintiff filed the Motion less than 45-days, plus two court days for electronic service, after January 20, 2026. Thus, the Motion is timely.

 

IV.           MEET AND CONFER

Motions to compel further responses to interrogatories and demands for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling must be accompanied by meet and confer declarations.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300, subd. (b)(1), 2031.310, subd. (b)(2).)¿ “A meet and confer declaration in support of a motion shall state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a).)

 

In opposition, Defendants argue Plaintiff failed to adequately meet and confer. (Opposition p. 2.) In reply, Plaintiff argues the meet and confer efforts were sufficient and Defendants’ counsel ignored Plaintiff’s counsel’s requests to meet and confer by telephone. (Reply p. 3.)

 

Plaintiff’s counsel submits a declaration stating they sent Defendants’ counsel three meet and confer letters. (Gomez Declaration ¶¶ 5, 7, 9.) Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040, subdivision (a) requires meeting and conferring in person, by telephone, or by videoconference. Meeting and conferring by letter is insufficient.

 

Plaintiff failed to satisfy the meet and confer requirements.

 

V.              PLAINTIFF’S SEPARATE STATEMENT

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(a) requires motions to compel further responses to interrogatories and a demand for inspection of documents or tangible things be accompanied by a separate statement. Per California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(c): 

A separate statement is a separate document filed and served with the discovery motion that provides all the information necessary to understand each discovery request and all the responses to it that are at issue. The separate statement must be full and complete so that no person is required to review any other document in order to determine the full request and the full response. Material must not be incorporated into the separate statement by reference. The separate statement must include--for each discovery request (e.g., each interrogatory, request for admission, deposition question, or inspection demand) to which a further response, answer, or production is requested--the following:  

(1) The text of the request, interrogatory, question, or inspection demand; 

(2) The text of each response, answer, or objection, and any further responses or answers; 

(3) A statement of the factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses, answers, or production as to each matter in dispute; 

(4) If necessary, the text of all definitions, instructions, and other matters required to understand each discovery request and the responses to it; 

(5) If the response to a particular discovery request is dependent on the response given to another discovery request, or if the reasons a further response to a particular discovery request is deemed necessary are based on the response to some other discovery request, the other request and the response to it must be set forth; and 

(6) If the pleadings, other documents in the file, or other items of discovery are relevant to the motion, the party relying on them must summarize each relevant document. 

Trial courts have discretion to deny motions to compel discovery because of defective separate statements. (Mills v. U.S. Bank (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 871, 893.)

Plaintiff’s separate statement fails to set forth the definitions for all of the referenced defined terms. For example, Plaintiff’s discovery refers to the “INCIDENT”, “RELATED TO”, and “RELATING TO[.]” (See, e.g., Separate Statement pp. 2, 6, 9.) But Plaintiff’s separate statement fails to set forth those definitions. Thus, Plaintiff’s separate statement does not comply with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345.

VI.           LEGAL STANDARD

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete. (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive. (3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).) “The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).)¿¿¿¿ 

“In a motion under subdivision (a) relating to the production of electronically stored information, the party or affected person objecting to or opposing the production, inspection, copying, testing, or sampling of electronically stored information on the basis that the information is from a source that is not reasonably accessible because of the undue burden or expense shall bear the burden of demonstrating that the information is from a source that is not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or expense.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (d).)¿¿¿ 

If the responding party responds to interrogatories with inappropriate objections or evasive or incomplete answers, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., §¿2030.300, subd. (a).) The responding party bears the burden of justifying any objection or insufficient answer to a special interrogatory. (Coy v. Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 210, 220–21 [“Defendants here had the burden of showing facts from which the trial court might find that the interrogatories were interposed for improper purposes. The statute does not require any showing of good cause for the serving and filing of interrogatories.”].)

 

VII.         DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendants to provide further responses to Form Interrogatories and RFPs. (Motion p. 2.)

Form Interrogatory No. 9.1 states: “Are there any other damages that you attribute to the INCIDENT? If so, for each item of damage state: (a) the nature; (b) the date it occurred; (c) the amount; and (d) the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number of each PERSON to whom an obligation was incurred.” (Separate Statement p. 2.)

Defendants responded:

Objection. This interrogatory seeks information that is not relevant to the subject matter of this action and as such not likely to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence since responding party is not making an affirmative claim for damages in their capacity as responding party. Further, the interrogatory invades responding party's privacy rights under the United States and California constitutions, statutory law, and common law.

(Separate Statement p. 2.)

Form Interrogatory No. 9.1 seeks basic relevant background information about damages. If Defendants are not attributing damages to the incident, Defendants may say so. Nothing before the court indicates it invades privacy rights.

Defendants are ordered to provide a further response to Form Interrogatory No. 9.1.

Form Interrogatory No. 9.2 states: “Do any DOCUMENTS support the existence or amount of any item of damages claimed in interrogatory 9.1? If so, describe each document and state the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number of the PERSON who has each DOCUMENT.” (Separate Statement p. 3.)

Defendants responded:

Objection. This interrogatory seeks information that is not relevant to the subject matter of this action and as such not likely to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence since responding party is not making an affirmative claim for damages in their capacity as responding party. Further, the interrogatory invades responding party's privacy rights under the United States and California constitutions, statutory law, and common law.

(Separate Statement p. 3.)

Form Interrogatory No. 9.2 seeks basic relevant background information about damages. If Defendants are not attributing damages to the incident, Defendants may say so. Nothing before the court indicates it invades privacy rights.

Defendants are ordered to provide a further response to Form Interrogatory No. 9.2.

Form Interrogatory No. 12.4 states:

Do YOU OR ANYONE ACTING ON YOUR BEHALF know of any photographs, films, or videotapes depicting any place, object, or individual concerning the INCIDENT or plaintiff's injuries? If so, state: (a) the number of photographs or feet of film or videotape; (b) the places, objects, or persons photographed, filmed, or videotaped; (c) the date the photographs, films, or videotapes were taken; (d) the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number of the individual taking the photographs, films, or videotapes; and (e) the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number of each PERSON who has the original or a copy of the photographs, films, or videotapes.

(Separate Statement p. 4.)

Defendants responded:

(a) Five (5) photographs (b) Scene of the incident (c) 06/07/2023 (d) Unknown (a) Four (4) photographs (b) Scene of the Incident (c) 06/07/2023 (d) Responding Defendant (a) Sixty (60) photographs (b) 06/19/2023 (c) Eric Ramirez, IAA Inc. (a) Eighty-one (81) photographs (b) Defendant's vehicle (c) Unknown (d) Unknown (e) Responding Defendant's insurance carrier and counsel

(Separate Statement p. 4.)

In opposition, Defendants state they are preparing a supplemental response to Form Interrogatory No. 12.4 containing the missing information. (Opposition p. 5.) Defendants are ordered to provide such supplemental response to Form Interrogatory No. 12.4.

RFP No. 4 seeks “Any and all reports, memoranda, letters, and/or notes RELATED TO the INCIDENT.” (Separate Statement p. 6.)

Defendants responded:

Objection. This demand is vague and ambiguous in that it does not define "reports, memoranda, letters, and/or notes" with sufficient particularity to allow responding party to determine whether the demand invades the attorney-client privilege and/or attorney work product privilege. Responding party further objects to this demand to the extent this request seeks to ascertain information which a consultant and/or expert witness has provided this responding party in the preparation of their case and premature disclosure of expert information in violation of CCP § 2034.010 et seq. The demand does not specifically or sufficiently describe each individual item or reasonably particularize a category of item sought by the demand as required by Code of Civil Procedure § 2031.030(c)(1). The demand seeks documents protected by the attorney work product privilege. A privilege log cannot and need not be provided based on the demand itself, because identifying the specific documents would disclose defense counsel's tactics and strategy in preparing the case for trial, which is protected attorney work product and not discoverable. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2018.020.) The document(s) sought is protected from disclosure by the attorney-client privilege. Any and all communication, documentation of said communication, and written communication between Defendants, their insurance carrier, and their counsel is privileged under the attorney client privilege and the attorney work product doctrine. This request is an impermissible attempt to force defendant's counsel to identify the date and subject matter of protected communications including but not limited to the entire file of defendant's counsel and their insurance carrier. Any information Defendant may have given to their insurance carrier concerning this incident, even one given in anticipation of litigation, is privileged communication, as being between attorney and client, and protected from disclosure. (Soltani-Rastegar v. Superior Court (1989) 208 Cal.App.3d 424, 426-427.) Defendant's insurance carrier was required to and defended her in this lawsuit through his attorney. Any information was intended for the information or assistance of the attorney so defending her. 

The court agrees with Defendants that this RFP is vague and ambiguous because it does not define reports, memoranda, letters, and/or notes and it also does not describe with reasonable particularity the categories requested.

Defendants are not ordered to provide a further response to RFP No. 4.

RFP No. 14 seeks “All DOCUMENTS identified, referred to, and relied on in YOUR response to Plaintiff's Form Interrogatories (Set One).” (Separate Statement p. 8.)

Defendants responded:

Objection. The demand seeks documents equally available to propounding party. Responding Defendant identifies Plaintiff's medical records. In addition to the medical records produced in response to Defendant's Request for Production of Documents Set 1, Responding Defendant is in possession of medical records that were subpoenaed, with due notice to Plaintiff's attorney, through Ontellus National, 170 E. Arrow Hwy., San Dimas, CA, 91773; (800) 229-7477; Ontellus.com. Therefore, good cause to justify this request for production is lacking. (See Code Civ. Proc., 2031.310(b)(1).)

Without waiving said objection and in regard to non-equally available documents, defendant responds:

• Estimate for Responding Defendant's vehicle, Exhibit "C"

• Photographs identified in Defendant's response to Form Interrogatory No. 12.4, Exhibit "A"

(Separate Statement pp. 8-9.)

Defendants’ response is insufficient. It is unclear what documents Defendants are referring to that are purportedly equally available to Plaintiff.

Defendants are ordered to provide a further response to RFP No. 14.

RFP No. 17 seeks: “All DOCUMENTS RELATING TO YOUR DRIVING RECORD (as used herein the term "DRIVING RECORD" means and refers to prior tickets, car accidents, collisions, citations, claims, or lawsuits against Defendant AVIVIT EDRI)[.]” (Separate Statement p. 9.)

Defendants responded:

Objection. This request is not limited to a reasonable time and scope in relation to the subject matter of this litigation and thus is overbroad, unduly burdensome and oppressive. The demand seeks production of a document(s) that is not relevant to any disputed fact that is of consequence to the determination of this action because the document(s) does not prove or disprove the issues of liability or damages. Moreover, the request is not reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.010.) The demand does not specifically or sufficiently describe each individual item or reasonably particularize a category of item sought by the demand as required by Code of Civil Procedure § 2031.030(c)(1). The demand seeks documents equally available to propounding party, and therefore good cause to justify this request for production is lacking. (See Code Civ. Proc., 2031.310(b)(1).) Further, this request is overly broad and invades the potential privacy of third persons.

(Separate Statement p. 10.)

In opposition, Defendants cite authority indicating evidence of a driver’s previous accidents would be inadmissible. (Opposition p. 9.) “In the context of discovery, evidence is ‘relevant’ if it might reasonably assist a party in evaluating its case, preparing for trial, or facilitating a settlement. Admissibility is not the test, and it is sufficient if the information sought might reasonably lead to other, admissible evidence.” (Glenfed Development Corp. v. Superior Court (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 1113, 1117, original italics.) Thus, admissibility is not the test.

However, as written, the definition of “DRIVING RECORD” is not limited to subjects that would normally be encompassed by a person’s driving record. It encompasses all claims and lawsuits involving Defendant. Plaintiff has not set forth specific facts justifying this RFP.

Defendants are not ordered to provide a further response to RFP No. 17.

RFP No. 23 seeks: “Any and all digital recordings from June 7, 2023 including but not limited to: dash camera footage, photos, audio, text messages or video recordings of the INCIDENT or surrounding the INCIDENT which is the basis of this case.” (Separate Statement p. 11.)

Defendants responded:

The production, inspection, copying, testing, sampling, and related activity demanded, will be allowed in whole, and all documents or things in the demanded category that are in responding party's possession, custody, or control are attached as follows: photographs from the scene of the incident and photographs of Plaintiff's and Defendant's vehicles, Exhibit "A".

(Separate Statement p. 11.)

Plaintiff argues Defendants’ response is insufficient because it references only photographs. (Separate Statement p. 11.) But Defendants state they will comply in whole. Thus, this argument is meritless.

Plaintiff also argues Defendants testified at their deposition there are additional photographs. (Separate Statement p. 11.) In opposition, Defendants state they have produced the referenced photographs. (Opposition p. 10.) Thus, no additional response here appears to be necessary.

Finally, Plaintiff states Defendants testified they have phone records from the date of the incident, and “[p]hone records for the date of the accident – which would show calls and text messages made or received at or around the time of the collision – are plainly responsive to this request.” (Separate Statement p. 11.)  The court is not persuaded phone records are “plainly responsive” to RFP No. 23’s request for “digital recordings[.]”

Defendants are not ordered to provide a further response to RFP No. 23.

RFP No. 24 seeks:

Any and all data, recordings, downloads, or printouts obtained from the Event Data Recorder (EDR), Airbag Control Module (ACM), Sensing Diagnostic Module (SDM), telematics system, or any other onboard recording device installed in the defendant vehicle involved in the collision described in the Complaint, including but not limited to data concerning speed, braking, throttle, seatbelt use, steering input, or airbag deployment for the INCIDENT.

(Separate Statement p. 12.)

RFP No. 25 seeks:

Any and all digital data, telematics data, black box recordings, or other electronic information generated by or stored within the subject vehicle or any system connected to it (including but not limited to the manufacturer's telematics service, such as OnStar, Ford Sync, or Uber app data) that relates to the vehicle's operation at or near the time of the collision alleged in the Complaint.

(Separate Statement p. 12.)

RFP No. 26 seeks: “Any and all documents, communications, or reports concerning the retrieval, download, or preservation of any event data recorder, telematics, or similar electronic vehicle data, including any third-party service used to extract or interpret such data.” (Separate Statement p. 12.)

Defendants responded to RFP Nos. 24, 25, and 26:

Objection. Event data recorder data can only be retrieved by an expert, and whether defense counsel hired an expert to obtain data from an event data recorder, and if so, any data obtained, is work product obtained by the initiative of counsel in preparing the case for trial. (See Williamson v. Superior Court (1978) 21 Cal.3d 829, 838 ["work product rules are designed explicitly to protect a party's expense and industry in seeking out expert testimony"].) As is the case for an attorney-obtained witness statement, counsel may have expended time and effort in identifying and locating the data, securing an expert to extract the data, and preserving said data, and plaintiff is not without recourse. Plaintiff may seek to inspect the vehicle and download the data for himself or herself to find out what information may be available that is relevant to the litigation. (Coito v. Superior Court (2012) 54 Cal.4th 480, 496.) Under such circumstances, "[a]bsent a showing that a [EDR] is no longer available or accessible, or some other showing of unfair prejudice or injustice (§ 2018.030, subd. (b)), the Legislature's declared policy is to prevent an attorney from free riding on the industry and efforts of opposing counsel (§ 2018.020, subd. (b))." (Coito, supra, 54 Cal.4th at p. 496.)

In reply, Plaintiff states Defendants’ counsel represented at the IDC that they do not have the electronic data but would arrange for Plaintiff’s expert to extract the data. (Reply p. 8.) Thus, no further response to RFP Nos. 24, 25, and 26 appears to be necessary.

Defendants are not ordered to provide further responses to RFP Nos. 24, 25, and 26.

VIII.      SANCTIONS

“[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300 subd. (c).¿ 

 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)  

 

Plaintiff seeks $2,075.58 in monetary sanctions against Defendants. (Motion p. 3.) This reflects four hours of attorneys’ fees to prepare the Motion and attend the hearing, at the rate of $500.00 per hour, and motion and filing fees in the sum of $73.80. (Gomez Declaration ¶ 11.)

 

The court finds Defendants failed to act with substantial justification and no other circumstances make imposing sanctions unjust. Thus, the court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.

 

IX.           CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the Motion is granted, in part, and denied, in part. Defendants are ordered to provide further responses to the Form Interrogatories and RFPs, consistent with the court’s order above, within thirty (30) days of this order.  The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the sum of $2,075.58, to be paid by Defendants to Plaintiff’s counsel within thirty (30) days of this order.
