MOTIONS FOR VACATUR and RECONSIDERATION

Hearing Date: 07/10/2026

CASE NO./NAME: 23BBCV01209 Unite Welding Enterprises, Inc. v. Dillon Construction Inc., et al.

Moving Party: Defendants, Dillon Construction Inc., et al

Responding Party: Plaintiff, Unite Welding Enterprises, Inc.

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: GRANT

 

BACKGROUND

This is a breach of contract case. On August 2, 2023, default was entered against Dillon Construction, Inc. (“DCI”). On May 13, 2026, default judgment was entered against DCI. On May 27, 2026, Defendants DCI, Edward Dean Roane, Christopher David Dillon, and Dillon Roane, a Joint Venture, Inc. (collectively “Defendants”) filed the instant motions for vacatur of the default judgment against Dillon Construction, Inc and reconsideration of the order setting a mediation date deadline.

 

DISCUSSION           

Defendants/Cross-Complainants move the Court for the following:

(1)   An order reconsidering, modifying, vacating, and/or continuing the Court’s Post Mediation Status Conference currently scheduled for July 21, 2026;

(2)   An order vacating, correcting, clarifying, and/or amending the Judgment entered on or about May 13, 2026 to remove DCI from the Judgment and any corresponding Attachment 5a thereto;

(3)   An order confirming that no default was entered against DCI and that DCI timely appeared and answered Plaintiff’s pleadings in this action;

(4)   An order prohibiting any enforcement activity against DCI arising from the Judgment pending correction of the Court’s records; and

(5)   Such other and further relief as this Court deems just and proper.

a.     Vacatur

Defendants argue that the inclusion of DCI in the May 13, 2026 Default Judgment was the result of a clerical error by this Court and should be corrected independently under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d). In support, Defendants argue that DCI had filed and served answers to the Complaint, First Amended Complaint, and Second Amended Complaint and that the Court had previously rejected default of DCI based on these answers.[1]  In Reply, Defendants more explicitly and persuasively argue that DCI responded to the First Amended Complaint and other amended complaints such that the default to the initial complaint should not be considered in operation.

            Code of Civil Procedure section¿473, subdivision (d) addresses a motion to vacate a void judgment or order.¿ That section states, “The court may, upon motion of the injured party,¿or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §¿473, subd. (d).)¿ 

The default entered in 2023 against DCI is not operable given DCI’s filing of an answer to an amended complaint. (Tidwell v. Henrick (1954) 124 Cal. App. 2d 64).  Accordingly, the default judgment including DCI was made in error and the Court hereby vacates it as to DCI pursuant to CCP section 473d.[2]  

b.     Reconsideration

Defendants’ motion for reconsideration is untimely. The order re mediation was made on March 24, 2026, and the motion was not brought until May 27, 2026. 

The real problem is that the moving party titled this as a motion for reconsideration. (By so titling the motion and bringing it under CCP 10008, Plaintiff made the motion untimely and also had the motion routed to the judicial officer who set the mediation.) It is more properly titled and considered a motion to continue.   

In any event, the Court hereby deems the motion to reconsider a motion to continue. The post-mediation status conference is scheduled for July 21, 2026. Defendants move this Court to continue the current mediation schedule because Defendants have obtained a $483,000 judgment against Arabo, LLC, Canplan Industries, LLC, and Agassi Halajyan (collectively “Owner Parties”), undertaken asset investigations relating to judgment debtors, identified California-based assets and real property, scheduled formal enforcement proceedings to commence on June 1, 2026, and identified a pathway toward substantial recovery that may materially affect or substantially resolve Plaintiff’s underlying claims. (Mot., p. 12, ln. 12-19.) In opposition, Plaintiff argues that waiting on Defendants to collect their judgment from the Owner Parties will have no effect on the mediation between Plaintiff and Defendants. (Opp., p. 6, ln. 7-11.)  The Court finds that good cause exists to continue the post-mediation status conference because Defendants have undertaken meaningful collection efforts. Accordingly, the post-mediation status conference is continued to September 28, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Dept. B at the Burbank Courthouse.

The Court further hereby grants DCI’s request to prohibit enforcement activity against DCI based on its decision to vacate/set aside the default judgment against DCI.  No such activity can take place until a judgment is entered against DCI.

[2] Plaintiff argues that a motion under Section 473 is untimely. However, the six-month time limit only applies to motions filed under section 473, subdivision (b). There is no time restriction for filing a motion under section 473, subdivision (d).

I.               BACKGROUND

            This case is a lemon law action filed by Plaintiffs AMHM, Inc. and Armen Ghazaryan against Defendants General Motors LLC (“General Motors”) and Casa de Cadillac (“Casa”).

            On November 19, 2024, Plaintiffs filed their complaint against Defendants.

            On January 2, 2025, General Motors filed its answer to the complaint.

            On February 19, 2025, Casa filed its answer to the complaint.

            On February 5, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their notice of settlement of the entire case.

            On March 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their motion for attorneys’ fees.

Also on March 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their costs memorandum, seeking $4,712.08 in litigation costs.

On March 30, 2026, General Motors filed its motion to strike or tax costs.

On June 23, 2026, General Motors filed its opposition brief as to the motion for attorneys’ fees.

Also on June 23, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their opposition brief as to the motion to strike or tax costs.

On June 29, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their reply brief as to the motion for attorneys’ fees.

Also on June 29, 2026, Defendants filed their reply brief as to the motion to strike or tax costs.

II.            LEGAL STANDARD

If a buyer-plaintiff prevails in an action under Civil Code section 1794, “the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.”¿ Civ. Code, § 1794 (d).¿

III.          DISCUSSION

            Plaintiffs seek an award of attorneys’ fees in the total amount of $93,097.50, representing 93.1 hours of attorney time at rates between $550.00/hour and $750.00/hour, plus 7.3 hours of legal assistant time at $150.00/hour, plus a 1.5 multiplier.  In opposition to the motion, General Motors argues Plaintiffs should be awarded no more than $15,977.69.  Ultimately, Plaintiffs will be awarded their attorneys’ fees in the reduced total amount of $40,155. 

Plaintiffs also seek the following $4,712.08 in litigation costs: $694.30 for filing and motion fees, $150.00 for jury fees, $51.63 for service of process, $2,795.90 for witness fees, $20.25 for fees for electronic filing or service, and $1,000.00 for “other” fees, specified as a mediation fee.  General Motors moves to tax a total of $4,141.65 from Plaintiffs’ costs memorandum.  The motion to tax costs will be granted only as to $750.00 in mediation fees.  Ultimately, Plaintiffs will be awarded their costs in the total amount of $3,962.08.

a.     65.1 hours of attorney time and 7.3 hours of legal assistant time is reasonable for Plaintiffs’ counsel to have spent working on this case.

The trial court has an obligation to award only those attorneys’ fees that are reasonable.¿ See PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1096 (PLCM Group); see also Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 (Ketchum). 

            Here, Plaintiffs seek an award of attorneys’ fees in the total amount of $93,097.50, representing 93.1 hours of attorney time at rates between $550.00/hour and $750.00/hour, plus 7.3 hours of legal assistant time at $150.00/hour, plus a 1.5 multiplier.  In opposition to the motion, General Motors argues Plaintiffs should be awarded no more than $15,977.69.

            Both sides’ positions as to the amount of attorneys’ fees that should be awarded in this case are not reasonable.  This case involved no motion practice (except as to fees).  However, counsel conducted substantial discovery. 

Plaintiffs’ request for more than 100.0 attorney/legal assistant hours is unreasonable in a lemon law case that settled and involved no motion practice (again, except for motions related to fees).  The total number of hours claimed in the billing records attached to the declaration of Attorney Hovanes Margarian is excessive.  Specifically:

·       Counsel claims 12.9 hours for pre-filing work in a routine lemon law case.  Such basic lemon law research and consultation tasks should have taken counsel no more than 8.0 hours to complete.  The Court will strike 4.9 hours from Plaintiffs’ request for attorneys’ fees.  (12.9 - 8.0 = 4.9.)

·       Counsel claims 57.3 hours for post-filing work, including discovery and settlement.  This is excessive.  Counsel should have been able to complete all tasks falling within this category of discovery and settlement activities in no more than 45.5 hours.  The Court will strike 11.8 hours from Plaintiffs’ request for attorneys’ fees.  (57.3 - 45.5 = 11.8.)

·       Counsel claims 17.3 hours for work on the motion for attorneys’ fees.  Such a basic, repetitive motion should have taken counsel no longer than 6.0 hours to complete.  This calculation is inclusive of time required to oppose General Motors’ motion to strike or tax costs and time to attend the single hearing on both motions.  The Court will strike 11.3 hours from Plaintiffs’ request for attorneys’ fees.  (17.3 - 6.0 = 11.3.)

In sum, many of Plaintiffs’ specific time allocations are for routine lemon law discovery practice that should not take counsel such large amounts of hours to complete.  Plaintiffs’ counsel appears to seek compensation at high rates for work that has been done hundreds of times previously and which should proceed in a fraction of the hours that are being claimed.

As to the 7.3 hours billed for legal assistant time, the Court disagrees with General Motors that any of these time-allocations were unreasonably claimed.  Plaintiffs will be allowed to recover for 7.3 hours of legal assistant time.

Ultimately, 65.1 hours of attorney time and 7.3 hours of legal assistant time is reasonable for Plaintiffs’ counsel to have spent working on this case.  This represents a reduction of 28 attorney hours from the 93.1 attorney hours originally claimed.  (4.9 + 11.8  + 11.3 = 28)  This award is inclusive of counsel’s work on the instant motion for attorneys’ fees and time to attend the hearing thereon.

b.     Plaintiffs will be awarded $40,155.00 in attorneys’ fees for work completed on this case.  (65.1 attorney hours at $600.00/hour + 7.3 legal assistant hours at $150.00/hour.)   

The trial court awards reasonable attorneys’ fees according to the lodestar.¿ See PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1096; see also Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132.¿¿¿ 

Plaintiffs’ counsel’s declaration states that attorneys who worked on this case bill at rates between $550.00/hour and $750.00/hour.  (See Hovanes decl., Exh. A.) 

The Court has reviewed and considered the parties’ arguments regarding setting reasonable rates in this case and concludes that average rate of $600.00 is a reasonable average hourly rate for attorneys in this matter given Plaintiffs’ attorneys’ experience.  The Court disagrees with Plaintiffs that the fact that this case was taken on contingency warrants a greater rate or a fee multiplier.  The contingency risk is built into a $600.00/hour rate.  $150.00/hour is a reasonable rate for legal assistants.

The Court will award Plaintiffs their reasonable attorneys’ fees for counsel’s work on this case up through and including the instant motion for attorneys’ fees and the hearing thereon in the total amount of $29,670.00.  (65.1 attorney hours at $600.00/hour + 7.3 legal assistant hours at $150.00/hour = $40,155.00)

c.     The motion to tax costs will be granted only as to $750.00 in mediation fees.

Plaintiffs seek the following $4,712.08 in litigation costs: $694.30 for filing and motion fees, $150.00 for jury fees, $51.63 for service of process, $2,795.90 for witness fees, $20.25 for fees for electronic filing or service, and $1,000.00 for “other” fees, specified as a mediation fee.  General Motors moves to tax a total of $4,141.65 from Plaintiffs’ costs memorandum.

To be recoverable, allowable costs must be “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation rather than merely convenient or beneficial to its preparation.”  Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5(c)(2). 

As to filing and motion fees, General Motors argues Plaintiffs’ claim is improper because notices, case management statements, and a notice of settlement are expenses that did not advance Plaintiffs’ case.  The Court disagrees with General Motors that such filings were not reasonably necessary to the conduct of litigation.  Plaintiffs will be allowed to collect these fees paid for routine filings.

General Motors argues Plaintiffs’ claimed filing and motion fees are also improper because Plaintiffs claim $47.34 in fees for anticipated filings of opposition and reply briefs in response to the motion for attorneys’ fees and the motion to tax costs.  General Motors argues Plaintiffs should only be allowed to recover costs actually incurred.  However, since General Motors filed its motion to tax costs, Plaintiffs have, in fact, filed opposition and reply briefs in response to those motions.  Thus, the fees were actually incurred.  Plaintiffs will be allowed to recover their fees for filings of opposition and reply briefs.

General Motors argues Plaintiffs should not be allowed to recover their $150.00 in jury fees because the case did not go to trial. The Court disagrees.  Plaintiffs were reasonably required to pay jury fees upfront to preserve their option for a jury trial if the case did not settle.  In this Court’s opinion, such costs were reasonably necessary to the conduct of litigation and are thus recoverable.

As to service of process, General Motors complains that Plaintiffs’ claim for $51.63 in costs is improper because Plaintiffs provide no documentation of who “Maddie Bright” is.  General Motors is incorrect.  Plaintiffs’ proof of service indicates Ms. Bright was served as General Motors’ agent for service of process.  (See Proof of Service filed December 12, 2024.)  Plaintiffs are allowed to recover costs incurred for the purpose of serving Ms. Bright.

As to expert fees, General Motors argues Plaintiffs should not be allowed to recover $2,795.90 in expert fees paid to “David Gurul” because the parties never exchanged expert discovery.  However, Mr. Gurul’s inspection of the vehicle appears to have been helpful in the settlement process.  Had this case proceeded to trial, Mr. Gurul’s inspection of the car would likely have been evidence used before a jury.  The Court agrees with Plaintiffs that the expert inspection was reasonably necessary at the time it was conducted, and that Plaintiffs should be allowed to recover Mr. Gurul’s fees under Civil Code section 1794 (d).

As to $20.25 in electronic filing fees, General Motors complains that Plaintiffs fail to provide documentary evidence of these costs with their costs memorandum.  However, Plaintiffs’ opposition brief explains these charges were “fees for electronic filing or service of documents through an electronic filing service provider. This is not an unexplained or suspicious charge. It is a minimal, ordinary litigation expense incurred in connection with filing and serving documents in this action through the Court’s electronic filing system and/or authorized electronic filing service provider.”  (Opposition, p. 7:1-5.)  The Court agrees with Plaintiffs that this explanation of $20.25 in electronic filing fees is sufficient without needing to provide specific documentary evidence of electronic service fees paid out.

As to $1,000.00 in “Other” fees, which are designated in the costs memorandum as a “Mediation” fee, General Motors incorrectly asserts that the “Other” fees include an “AppearMe Service Fee.”  (See Motion, p. 5:26-28.)  Plaintiffs designate the “Other” fee identified in the costs memorandum as being comprised only of a $1,000.00 mediation fee.  The Court rejects General Motors’ position that the Court should strike the AppearMe Service Fee, as no AppearMe Service Fee has been claimed.

In their opposition brief, Plaintiffs concede that they are only accurately entitled to ¼ of the $1,000.00 amount claimed for mediation, i.e., $250.00.  (See Opposition, p. 9:13-19.)  This is presumably because the mediation session covered four (4) separate cases, as reflected in the mediator’s invoice.  (See Hovanes decl., Exh. C.)  The Court will thus strike $750.00 (i.e., ¾ of the mediator’s fee) from Plaintiffs’ costs memorandum.  Plaintiffs will be allowed to recover just $250.00 (i.e., ¼ of the mediator’s fee).

In sum, the motion to tax costs will be granted only as to $750.00 related to mediation.  Plaintiffs will be allowed to recover their costs in the total amount of $3,962.08.  ($4,712.08 - $750.00 = $3,962.08.)

IV.          CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs AMHM, Inc. and Armen Ghazaryan’s motion for attorneys’ fees is GRANTED in the total reduced amount of $40,155.00. 

 

Defendant General Motors LLC’s motion to tax costs is GRANTED only as to $750.00 in mediation fees.  Plaintiffs AMHM, Inc. and Armen Ghazaryan are AWARDED their costs in the total amount of $3,962.08.  ($4,712.08 - $750.00 = $3,962.08.)

 

Plaintiffs AMHM, Inc. and Armen Ghazaryan are ORDERED to give notice.
