(1) Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with these motions.

DISCUSSION

Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

          Defendant General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) demurs to the fifth cause of action for fraudulent concealment alleged by plaintiff Jaime Bennett (“Plaintiff”) in the First Amended Complaint.

The court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the fifth cause of action for fraudulent inducement – concealment because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiff has not alleged that Defendant had a duty to disclose the facts regarding the subject defects in the vehicle, including because Plaintiff has not alleged facts establishing that Plaintiff purchased the vehicle directly from Defendant or its agent and that, during the course of those direct dealings, Defendant concealed facts regarding the defects from Plaintiff.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e); Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 40-41 [circumstances imposing a duty to disclose “presuppose a preexisting relationship between the parties” which “must necessarily arise from direct dealings between the plaintiff and the defendant; it cannot arise between the defendant and the public at large”]; Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 844, review dism. Dec. 18, 2024 [finding complaint sufficient and declining to hold claim barred on the ground that there was no relationship requiring Nissan to disclose known defects where plaintiffs alleged they bought the car from a Nissan dealership, that Nissan backed the car with its warranty, and Nissan’s authorized dealerships are its agents for purposes of the sale of Nissan vehicles to consumers].) 

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  The court finds that Plaintiff has not articulated the facts that Plaintiff could allege to render this cause of action against Defendant sufficient and therefore sustains the demurrer without leave to amend. 

 

 

Motion to Strike Portions of First Amended Complaint

Defendant moves the court for an order striking from the First Amended Complaint Plaintiff’s prayer for punitive damages.

The court grants Defendant’s motion to strike Plaintiff’s prayer for punitive damages because Plaintiff has not alleged facts establishing that Defendant is guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).)  The court grants Defendant’s motion without leave to amend because Plaintiffs have not articulated the facts that they could allege to render their claim for punitive damages sufficient against Defendant.  (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc., supra, 248 Cal.App.4th at p. 290.)  

 

ORDER

          The court SUSTAINS defendant General Motors, LLC’s demurrer to plaintiff Jaime Bennett’s fifth cause of action without leave to amend.

          The court GRANTS defendant General Motors, LLC’s motion to strike without leave to amend. The court orders that the prayer for punitive damages set forth on page 14, section g line 1 of the First Amended Complaint filed by plaintiff Jaime Bennett is stricken.

          The court orders defendant General Motors, LLC to serve and file an answer to plaintiff Jaime Bennett’s First Amended Complaint within 10 days of the date of this order.

The court orders defendant General Motors, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion for Attorney’s Fees, costs, and expenses

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Benjamin Zuniga

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.

Motion for Attorney’s Fees, Costs, and Expenses

The court considered the moving and opposition papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Benjamin Zuniga (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order awarding him attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses against defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (“Defendant”).

The court denies the motion because Plaintiff has not furnished the court with any evidence that Plaintiff is entitled to an award of fees and costs. Although Plaintiff’s papers reference a buyback agreement, no agreement is attached to the moving papers. Nor does Plaintiff’s counsel address the agreement in his declaration. (Rashtian Decl. ¶¶ 1-8.) Because Defendant’s opposition does not deny the existence of a settlement agreement, the court’s denial of this motion is without prejudice to filing a new motion that properly places a settlement of this action before the court and establishes that Plaintiff is a buyer who has prevailed in this action and is entitled to recover attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses.  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).)    

          If the parties have entered into an agreement to settle the entire case, California Rules of Court, rule 3.1385, subdivision (a)(1) requires that Plaintiff “immediately file written notice of the settlement . . . .” Plaintiff is required to file the notice on the mandatory Judicial Council form “Notice of Settlement of Entire Case” (CM-200).  The court may order a person, after notice and an opportunity to be heard, to pay reasonable monetary sanctions for failure without good cause to comply with that rule.  (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 2.30(b).)  The court expects the parties to comply with the requirements of the California Rules of Court. 

ORDER

The court DENIES plaintiff Benjamin Zuniga’s motion for attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses. The court orders that this ruling is without prejudice to filing a new motion for attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses that properly sets forth a basis to conclude that a party is entitled to recover attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses.

The court orders defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Francisco Estrada (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order compelling Defendant Ford Motor Company (“Defendant”) to serve further responses to Special Interrogatories Nos. 38 through 43, and 51.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve further responses to Special Interrogatory Nos. 38, 39, and 40 because the objections to the interrogatories are without merit.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a)(3).)

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve further responses to Special Interrogatory Nos. 41, 42, and 43 because (1) Defendant’s answers to the interrogatories are evasive and incomplete, and (2) the objections to the interrogatories are without merit.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a)(1)(3).)

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to serve a further response to Special Interrogatory No. 51 because (1) Defendant’s exercise of the option to produce documents under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.230 is unwarranted, and (2) the objections to the interrogatory are without merit.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a)(2)(3).)

ORDER

The court GRANTS plaintiff Francisco Estrada’s motion to compel further responses to Special Interrogatories Nos. 38-43, and 51.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.300, the court orders defendant Ford Motor Company to serve further, full and complete answers to plaintiff Francisco Estrada’s Special Interrogatories Nos. 38-43, and 51, without objections and which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.210-2030.250, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.¿ 

The court orders plaintiff Francisco Estrada to give notice of this ruling.

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant Aurora Charter Oak – Los Angeles, LLC (erroneously sued as Aurora Behavioral Health Care)

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Katarina Campbell

(1) Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

(2) Motion to Strike Portions of First Amended Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this demurrer and motion.

DISCUSSION

Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

          Defendant Aurora Charter Oak – Los Angeles, LLC (erroneously sued as Aurora Behavioral Health Care) (“Defendant”) demurs to the First Amended Complaint filed by plaintiff Katarina Campbell (“Plaintiff”) in its entirety.

          The court sustains the demurrer because the First Amended Complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) Psychiatric centers are immune from civil liability for the involuntary detention and treatment of a person suffering from a mental disorder. (Welf. & Inst. Code, § 5278.) Plaintiff’s Firs Amended Complaint seeks to hold Defendant liable for detaining her on a “5150 hold”—that is, involuntary detention under Welfare and Institutions Code section 5150—and for administering medication against her will. (Complaint pp. 1-3.) By the plain text of section 5278, Defendant is immune from liability for that conduct.

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  The court finds that Plaintiff has not articulated the facts that Plaintiff could allege to render this cause of action against Defendant sufficient and therefore sustains the demurrer without leave to amend. 

Motion to Strike Portions of First Amended Complaint

          Defendant moves the court for an order striking portions of the First Amended Complaint. The court denies this motion because it is moot as a result of the court’s order sustaining Defendant’s demurrer to the First Amended Complaint without leave to amend for the reasons stated above.

ORDER

The court SUSTAINS defendant Aurora Charter Oak – Los Angeles, LLC (erroneously sued as Aurora Behavioral Health Care)’s demurrer to plaintiff Katarina Campbell’s First Amended Complaint without leave to amend.

The court orders that all future hearings are advanced to the date of this hearing, August 7, 2026, and vacated.

The court orders that this action is dismissed with prejudice.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 581, subds. (f)(1), (m).)    

The court orders defendant Aurora Charter Oak – Los Angeles, LLC (erroneously sued as Aurora Behavioral Health Care) to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
