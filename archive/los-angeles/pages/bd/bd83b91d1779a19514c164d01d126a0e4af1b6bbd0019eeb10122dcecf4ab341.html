Case Number: 24STLC07134    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING: 

 

Plaintiff Mary Diaz Alvarado’s Motion For (1) Leave To File First Amended Complaint; (2) Reclassification Of Action From Limited To Unlimited Jurisdiction; (3) Continuance Of Trial And All Related Dates; and (4) Relief From Jury Waiver, is denied as to leave to amend, reclassification, and continuance of the trial and related dates. The Court grants the request for relief from waiver of a jury trial.  Plaintiff is ordered to post jury fees within five days of the date of this Order. Failure to post fees within that time will result in waiver that will not be excused.  

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Mary Diaz Alvarado (“Plaintiff”) brought this action for breach of motor vehicle negligence against Defendant Stephanie Anne Bentvsi (“Defendant”) on October 2, 2024. Trial in the matter was scheduled for April 1, 2026. Defendant filed an Answer on March 10, 2025. On February 10, 2026, the parties filed a stipulation to continue the trial date to August 17, 2026, with discovery and motion deadlines to follow.

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion For (1) Leave To File First Amended Complaint; (2) Reclassification Of Action From Limited To Unlimited Jurisdiction; (3) Continuance Of Trial And All Related Dates; and (4) Relief From Jury Waiver on July 29, 2026. On August 4, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s ex parte application to continue the trial date to November 20, 2026 and ordered that any request to continue discovery or motion cut-off dates must be made by noticed motion or stipulation. (Minute Order, 08/04/26.) Defendant filed an opposition to the instant Motion on September 9, 2026.

 

Motion for Leave to Amend and to Reclassify Action

 

The request for leave to amend is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a), which provides that “[t]he court may, in furtherance of justice and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).) A motion for leave to amend a pleading must also comply with the procedural requirements of California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, which requires a supporting declaration to set forth explicitly what allegations are to be added and where, and when the new evidence was discovered warranting the amendment and why the amendment was not made earlier. The motion must also include (1) a copy of the proposed and numbered amendment, (2) specifications by reference to pages and lines of the allegations that would be deleted and added, and (3) a declaration specifying the effect, necessity and propriety of the amendments, date of discovery and reasons for delay. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, subds. (a), (b).)

 

The policy favoring amendments and resolving all matters in the same dispute is “so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified. [Citation.]” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428.) “Although courts are bound to apply a policy of great liberality in permitting amendments to the complaint at any stage of the proceedings, up to and including trial [citations], this policy should be applied only ‘where no prejudice is shown to the adverse party . . . . [citation].  A different result is indicated ‘where inexcusable delay and probable prejudice to the opposing party’ is shown. [Citation].” (Magpali v. Farmers Group (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 487.) Where a proposed amendment opens an entirely new substantive area of injury on the eve of trial without any explanation for why the major change had not been made long before, denial of leave is appropriately within the court’s discretion.  (Id.)

 

Plaintiff requests leave to amend to allege “previously undiscovered symptoms” and to change the amount of damages alleged as a result of the motor vehicle accident. According to the supporting declaration, Plaintiff’s counsel only became aware of these symptoms—restless sleep, pain when sitting for prolonged periods, headaches, and fear associated with driving, particularly for extended periods—the week before the instant Motion was filed. (Motion, von Schulz Decl., ¶4.) Plaintiff’s counsel declares that these symptoms substantially affect Plaintiff’s quality of life and ability to work, including her ability to maintain her prior work schedule. (Ibid.) The declaration also states that Plaintiff continues to experience ongoing neck pain, right-knee pain, and lower-back pain. (Ibid.)

 

Defendant, in opposition, contends that the allegedly newly discovered symptoms were already known and addressed as to their scope and persistence during Plaintiff’s deposition, which was taken on July 15, 2025, almost two years after the August 21, 2023 collision.  In support, Defendant attaches excerpts from the deposition. The excerpts do not reflect that all of the newly discovered symptoms were addressed at the time.  Plaintiff did testify that the position in which she was sitting made her neck pain worse, and to continuing knee, back, and neck pain at that time. (Id. at Exh. A, pp. 25-29; 27:15-16.) The excerpts, however, do not reflect testimony about headaches, difficulty sleeping, fear of driving, or missing work as a result of the injuries (rather than for medical appointments). (See id. at Exh. A.)  Thus, at least some of the matters raised now, are newly raised.

 

The Court denies leave to amend for failure to comply with the requirements of California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, or to otherwise demonstrate that leave should be granted.  The supporting declaration does not set forth explicitly what allegations are to be added and where, nor does the Motion specify by reference to pages and lines what allegations would be deleted and added. (See Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.1324(a).)  Rather, the Motion attaches only a copy of the proposed amended Complaint which, unlike the initial Complaint, is submitted using a court form. (Notice of Motion, pp. 4-6.) 

 

Further, the Court rule requires the motion to include a declaration specifying, among other things, when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered, and the reasons why the request was not made earlier.  (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.1324(b)(3)-(4).)   Plaintiff’s motion is supported by the declaration of counsel, Gustav Alexander Von Schulz.  That declaration states only that counsel became aware of the additional alleged symptoms and limitations “within the past week,” but does not offer any explanation for the failure to raise or disclose these facts more promptly.  (Mot., Schulz Decl., ¶4.)  The nature and duration of Plaintiff’s injuries were clearly within her knowledge. No declaration from Plaintiff herself is included, nor is the Motion supported by any evidence, such as medical records, to show the dates, frequency, or severity of the symptoms or when they arose. The sudden discovery of these undocumented symptoms and life impacts three weeks before the August 17, 2026 trial date is not sufficiently supported to justify leave to amend.  (Miles v. City of Los Angeles (2020) 56 Cal. App. 5th 728, 739 (“unwarranted delay justifies denial of leave to amend”); Leader v. Health Industries of America, Inc. (2001) 89 Cal.App.4th 603, 613 (it “is well settled that a long deferred presentation of the proposed amendment without a showing of excuse for the delay is itself a significant factor to uphold the trial court's denial of the amendment”).)

 

Further, “the liberal policy favoring leave to amend applies only where no prejudice is shown to the adverse party.”  (Miles, 56 Cal. App. 5th at 739 (cleaned up).)  “Prejudice exists where the proposed amendment would require delaying the trial, resulting in added costs of preparation and increased discovery burdens.”  (Id.)  So here.  Discovery was complete as to Plaintiff’s injuries.  She had the opportunity to testify to them, and did, at her deposition, which was held more than a year ago and which was more than two years after the collision at issue.  Waiting until three weeks before trial to raise alleged additional, late-arising injuries entirely within the Plaintiff’s knowledge, is unduly prejudicial to the defendant.  The Court agrees with Defendant that simply continuing the trial date over Defendant’s objection does not cure the prejudice; it compounds the negative effects of Plaintiff’s unjustified delay.

 

Motion for Reassignment

 

The Court also denies the motion for reclassification to unlimited civil court.  The motion to reclassify is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 403.040, which allows a plaintiff to file a motion for reclassification of an action within the time allowed for that party to amend the initial pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 403.040, subd. (a).) If the motion is made after the time for the plaintiff to amend the pleading, the motion may only be granted if (1) the case is incorrectly classified; and (2) the plaintiff shows good cause for not seeking reclassification earlier. (Code Civ. Proc., § 403.040, subd. (b).) In Walker v. Superior Court (1991) 53 Cal.3d 257, the California Supreme Court held that a matter may be reclassified from unlimited to limited only if it appears to a legal certainty that the plaintiff’s damages will necessarily be less than the jurisdictional limit. (Walker v. Superior Court (1991) 53 Cal.3d 257, 262.) The jurisdictional limit, effective January 1, 2024, is $35,000.00. (Code Civ. Proc., § 85, subd. (a).)

 

In Ytuarte v. Superior Court (2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 266, 278, the Court of Appeals examined the principles set forth in Walker and held that to reclassify in the opposite direction – from limited to unlimited – “the court should reject the [movant]’s effort to reclassify the action as unlimited only when the lack of jurisdiction as an ‘unlimited’ case is certain and clear.” (Id. at 279.) The moving party “must present evidence to demonstrate a possibility that the damages will exceed [the jurisdictional limit]. If a jurisdictionally appropriate verdict may result, (i.e., if such a verdict is not virtually unobtainable) the court should grant the motion to reclassify the case as “unlimited.” (Ibid.)

 

This burden is not met here.  The request to reclassify the action relies on the “previously undiscovered symptoms” and their impacts on Plaintiff’s life discussed above.  But, the supporting declaration, which the Court identified as insufficient to support amendment, makes no mention of the damages Plaintiff has incurred as a result of her injuries and general pain and suffering. A conclusory statement that “these symptoms and functional limitations have substantially affected Plaintiff’s quality of life and ability to work” without any supporting medical records or invoices is not evidence that demonstrates a possibility that Plaintiff’s damages will exceed $35,000.00. Accordingly, the request to reclassify the action is also denied.

 

Motion to Continue Trial Date

 

Plaintiff’s request to continue the trial date and related motion and discovery cut-offs is premised on amending the Complaint and reclassifying the action. Plaintiff moves pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court rule 3.1332, which provides “[a]lthough continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The Court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (c).) Grounds for a continuance include the unavailability of trial counsel because of death, illness, or other excusable circumstances. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (c)(3).)

 

The court may look to the following factors in determining whether a trial continuance is warranted: (1) proximity of the trial date; (2) whether there was any previous continuance of trial due to any party; (3) the length of the continuance requested; (4) the availability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion; (5) the prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; and (6) whether trial counsel is engaged in another trial.  (See generally, Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (d)(1)-(11).) Additional factors for the court to consider include: a party’s excused inability to obtain essential testimony, documents, or other material evidence despite diligent efforts; whether all parties have stipulated to a continuance; and any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subds. (c), (d).)

 

Trial in this action has been continued three times already and a continuance is now opposed by Defendant. Also, in light of the Court’s denial of the Motion to Amend and Reclassify, the Court finds no good cause to continue the trial or related dates.

 

Motion for Relief from Waiver of Jury Trial

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 631, which states in relevant part that “[a] party waives trial by jury in any of the following ways: . . . (5) By failing to timely pay the fee described in subdivision (b), unless another party on the same side of the case has paid that fee.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 631, subd. (f).)

 

In this case, Defendant demanded a jury trial and paid jury fees, but Plaintiff did not pay jury fees, which is waiver under § 631(f).  Defendant no longer wishes to proceed by jury trial.  Plaintiff asks the Court to exercise its discretion to allow a trial by jury despite Plaintiff’s waiver, stating that the waiver was “inadvertent.”  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 631(g).)   

 

As the Court of Appeals has held, “[t]he court abuses its discretion in denying relief where there has been no prejudice to the other party or to the court from an inadvertent waiver.”   Gann v. Williams Bros. Realty, Inc. (1991) 231 Cal. App. 3d 1698, 1704 (internal citations omitted).  Relevant prejudice may include “delay in rescheduling jury trial, lack of funds, timeliness of the request and prejudice to the litigants.”  (Id.) 

 

The Court will grant relief.  Defendant’s opposition does not identify any particular prejudice from the granting of relief, other than to state that “Defendant has prepared the matter in its existing procedural posture.”  (Opp., p. 12.)  Defendant’s other arguments in opposition all concern the combined effect of granting relief from waiver in addition to the other relief sought by Plaintiff, i.e. “amendment, reclassification, continuance, and restoration of Plaintiff’s jury right.” (Id. (emphasis added).  This is not sufficient.  The other relief sought has not been granted, negating much of the basis for the claim of prejudice.  Trial is scheduled for November 20, 2026.  The parties have sufficient time to modify their trial preparation and prepare jury instructions and other documents necessary for a jury trial.  As set forth below, however, Plaintiff must pay the jury fees within five days of this Order.  Failure to timely pay the fees will result in waiver that will not be excused.

  

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Mary Diaz Alvarado’s Motion For (1) Leave To File First Amended Complaint; (2) Reclassification Of Action From Limited To Unlimited Jurisdiction; (3) Continuance Of Trial And All Related Dates; and (4) Relief From Jury Waiver, is denied as to leave to amend, reclassification, and continuance of the trial and related dates. The Court grants the request for relief from waiver of a jury trial.  Plaintiff is ordered to post jury fees within five days of the date of this Order. Failure to post fees within that time will result in waiver that will not be excused.

Case Number: 24STLC07577    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Loan Asset Issuer LLC Series 2021 M 2’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On October 18, 2024, Plaintiff Loan Asset Issuer LLC Series 2021 M 2 (“Plaintiff”) filed this action for breach of loan agreement against Defendant Verta Gil (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint on November 27, 2024. On November 25, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Monetary Sanctions against Defendant. (Minute Order, 11/25/25.)

 

Plaintiff filed and served the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings against Defendant on July 1, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

 

Discussion

 

The standard for ruling on a motion for judgment on the pleadings is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer, that is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.  (Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.)  Where the moving party is the plaintiff, judgment on the pleadings may be granted on the basis “that the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause or causes of action against the defendant and the answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense to the complaint.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(1)(A).) Matters which are subject to mandatory judicial notice may be treated as part of the complaint and may be considered without notice to the parties. Matters which are subject to permissive judicial notice must be specified in the notice of motion, the supporting points and authorities, or as the court otherwise permits. (Id.) The motion may not be supported by extrinsic evidence. (Barker v. Hull (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 221, 236.)

 

While a statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, et seq. must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration, there is no such requirement for a motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to the common law. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 439 [moving party must file declaration demonstrating an attempt to meet and confer in person or by telephone, at least five days before the date a motion for judgment on the pleadings is filed].) The instant Motion is supported by a meet and confer declaration. (Motion, D-Anna Decl., ¶¶3-6 and Exhibit 3.)

 

The instant Motion is accompanied by a request for judicial notice of (1) Plaintiff’s Request for Admissions propounded to Defendant; and (2) the Court Order Deeming Plaintiff’s Request for Admissions to Defendant. (RJN, Exhs. 3-4.) The Court takes judicial notice of the admissions pursuant to Cal. Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d). (Cal. Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d); see also Cloud v. Northrop Grumman Corp. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 995, 999; Evans v. California Trailer Court, Inc. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 540, 549) [holding that the court may take judicial notice of matters that cannot be reasonably controverted, including “admissions and concessions.”].)

 

“[I]n order for judicial notice to support a motion for judgment on the pleadings by negating an express allegation of the pleading, the notice must be of something that cannot reasonably be controverted…The same is true of evidentiary admissions or concessions.” (Columbia Casualty Co. v. Northwestern Nat. Ins. Co. (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 457, 468.)  This condition is not met here.  First, the admissions are internally contradictory.  As alleged in the Complaint, Plaintiff purchased the loan; Plaintiff was not the original party to the Promissory Note.  (See Compl., ¶10.)  Yet, the deemed admission that Plaintiff asks this Court to credit states that Defendant entered into the Loan Agreement with Plaintiff.  (RJN, Exh. 3, RFA No. 4.)  That is clearly not accurate.  The Loan Agreement and Promissory Note identifies “Cross River Bank” as the initial lender. (Compl., Exh. B.)  In a further issue, the deemed admissions, as written, leave ambiguity as to the amount remaining due on the loan.  RFA No. 12, asks Defendant to admit that “the principal amount due of $10,058.45 as set forth in the Complaint filed in this matter is correct”, while RFA No. 9 asks Defendant to admit, without specifying any date or amount, that “All payments YOU have submitted have been credited ot[sic] the subject loan.”  (RJN, Exh. 3.)  The RFAs were deemed admitted months after the Complaint was filed.  Taken together, these two deemed admissions do not clearly admit that $10,058.45 is due and owing now, such that the amount due could not “reasonably be controverted”, because Defendant may have made payments after the Complaint was filed.  (Columbia Casualty Co., 231 Cal.App.3d at 468.) 

 

Plaintiff’s proffered omnibus admission that “Every statement or allegation contained in Plaintiffs Complaint is true and correct” does not cure these issues for at least two reasons.  (See RJN, Exh. 3, RFA 13.)  First, even admitting the allegations in the Complaint wholesale does not correct these issues.  As indicated, the Complaint identifies Plaintiff as a purchaser of the loan, not a party to the Borrower Agreement or Promissory Note, creating a conflict with the deemed admissions.  (Compl., ¶10.)  And, the Complaint’s allegation that $10,058.45 was due at the time the Complaint was filed does not address whether any subsequent payments have been made and credited to the account.  Second, the omnibus admission is not a proper admission or matter for judicial notice in any event. (See Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 2033.010 (admissions must pertain to “the truth of specified matters of fact, opinion relating to fact, or application of law to fact”)(emphasis added); Cal. Evid. Code, §452(h) (notice may be taken of “facts and propositions not reasonably subject to dispute”). 

 

Thus, Plaintiff is not entitled to Judgment on the Pleadings based on the deemed admissions. 

 

This result is consistent with the appropriate use of deemed admissions more generally. “[A]fter a matter is deemed admitted, the scope and effect of the admission must be determined by the trial court.” (Fredericks v. Filbert Co. (1987) 189 Cal. App. 3d 272, 278) (citation omitted).)   The Court’s discretion encompasses the determination whether the opportunity to present additional evidence may provide relevant context as to the meaning and scope of the deemed admissions.  (See Columbia Casualty Co. v. Northwestern Nat. Ins. Co.,  231 Cal.App.3d at 468.)  The concern is grounded in due process: “The notice of motion for judgment on the pleadings is thus notice to be prepared to argue legal sufficiency of allegations; it is not notice to come forward with factual support for what is alleged. This process recognizes that there may in fact be evidence which challenges the truth of the allegations of the pleading under attack but defers the challenge until a motion for summary judgment or a trial.” (Id. at 469.)  Similarly in Fredericks, the court observed, “[t]he court must have discretion to admit evidence to elucidate and explain an admission, because the admission of a fact does not always reflect the party’s reasonable understanding of that fact.” (189 Cal. App. 3d at 278.)  Use of deemed admissions in a motion for judgment on the pleadings – in contrast to at trial or summary judgment – deprives a defendant of the opportunity to attempt to proffer evidence to elucidate and explain the meaning of requests for admission. As indicated above, that opportunity is appropriately afforded in this case given the ambiguities in the deemed admissions. 

 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Loan Asset Issuer LLC Series 2021 M 2’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC04327    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Defendant Mohammad S. Sadeq aka Mohammad Sabit Sadeq aka Mohammad Sadeq aka Mohammad Sabit is ordered to serve responses to the Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s requests for sanctions are denied.

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) served Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, on Defendant Mohammad S. Sadeq aka Mohammad Sabit Sadeq aka Mohammad Sadeq aka Mohammad Sabit (“Defendant”) on October 23, 2025. (Motions, Frischer Decl., Exh. 1.) Despite a meet and confer effort, to date, Defendant has not served verified responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶¶4-5 and Exh. 2) Plaintiff filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions on July 17, 2026. The Motions were served on Defendant by electronic and first-class mail the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Further, the motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Based on Defendant’s failure to timely respond to the propounded discovery, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling their service of verified responses to the discovery requests without objections.

 

Plaintiff moves for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030. In City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, the Supreme Court observed:

 

It is already well-established that a court may not rely on [Code of Civil Procedure] section 2023.030 to override the limitations prescribed by any other applicable sanctions provision in the [Civil Discovery] Act. A court may invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 only when confronted with an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision. And where it invokes that authority, it is constrained by the long-settled rules generally governing the imposition of discovery sanctions under the Act.

 

(City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 74-75.) The moving statutes, Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300, only make sanctions available when a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); § 2031.300, subd. (c).) Defendant filed no opposition to the instant Motions and cannot be sanctioned under sections 2030.290 or 2031.310.

 

Nor has Plaintiff shown that Defendant’s failure to serve discovery responses is “an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision.” Therefore, the Court declines to invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditor Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Defendant Mohammad S. Sadeq aka Mohammad Sabit Sadeq aka Mohammad Sadeq aka Mohammad Sabit is ordered to serve responses to the Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s requests for sanctions are denied.

Petitioner Maria Gutierrez’s Petitions to Approve Compromise of Disputed Claim on behalf of Minor Claimants Merida Bucio and Sahara Bucio are granted. Analysis:

 

Minor Plaintiffs Merida Bucio and Sahara Bucio (“Claimants”), by and through Plaintiff Maria Gutierrez (“Petitioner”), filed the instant action for motor vehicle negligence against Defendants Susan Zurita and Norma Carrillo (“Defendants”) on January 28, 2026.

 

On March 23, 2026, Petitioner filed Petitions for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Minor or Person with a Disability.
