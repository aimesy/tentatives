Case Number: 20STCV10085    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 513

Wright v. Carroll, 20STCV10085

Ex Parte Application  8/14/26

Plaintiffs are correct in stating in their ex parte application that, as of December 3, 2025, the discovery cutoff was December 29, 2025 (i.e., 30 days before the trial date then set for January 28, 2026).  (Stipulation and order, filed February 24, 2025, p. 4.)  For purposes of the discovery cutoff, “discovery is considered completed on the day a response is due or on the day a deposition begins.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.010.)  Thus, the parties were permitted to begin taking any depositions on or before December 29, 2025.

          In the Order on Ex Parte Application, filed December 3, 2025, the court ordered that the discovery cutoff was “extended and shall be based on the new trial date” (October 21, 2026) “only for discovery requests that have already been served . . . .”  Thus, in that order, the court extended the discovery cutoff date to September 21, 2026 only for discovery requests that had already been served as of December 3, 2025.

          In the stipulation and order filed August 3, 2026, the court ordered, in relevant part, that the deadline to complete discovery proceedings for depositions is extended and shall be based on the new trial date (June 9, 2027) “only as to depositions for which the notice of deposition was served before 12/3/25.”  Thus, the court’s August 3, 2026 order did not change the discovery cutoff dates for depositions that were established by the court’s previous orders.  In other words, the parties (1) had the right to begin taking any depositions on or before December 29, 2025, and (2) now have the right to begin taking depositions on or before May 10, 2027 “only as to depositions for which the notice of deposition was served before 12/3/25.”  (Stipulation and order, filed August 3, 2026, p. 4.)           

          The court therefore denies plaintiffs’ ex parte application for order correcting and/or modifying order entered on August 3, 2026. 

          The court orders plaintiffs to give notice of this order.

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Defendant Encore Group (USA) LLC

(1) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories

(2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production

(3) Motion for Order Establishing Admissions

The court considered the moving and opposition papers filed in connection with these motions.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for orders (1) compelling defendant Encore Group (USA) LLC (“Defendant”) to serve responses to Plaintiff’s first set of Special Interrogatories and first set of Requests for Production; (2) deeming admitted the genuineness of the documents and the truth of the matters specified in the Requests for Admission served on Defendant; and (3) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant.

The court denies the motions because Defendant has presented evidence that it served verified responses which are substantially code-compliant to all three sets of outstanding discovery on July 31, 2026 by electronic mail. (McClintock Decl. ¶ 2; Exs. A-C.)

          The court grants Plaintiff’s request for sanctions in part. “The court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed . . . or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.”  (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 3.1348(a).) The court finds that a reasonable award of sanctions on these three motions is $1,980.00 (4 hours of attorney time x $495.00 per hour.) (Walton Decl. ¶ 26.)

ORDER

The court DENIES plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc.’s motion to compel responses to special interrogatories.

The court DENIES plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc.’s motion to compel responses to requests for production.

The court DENIES plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc.’s motion for order establishing admissions.

The court orders defendant Encore Group (USA) LLC to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc. in the amount of $1,980.00 within 30 days of the date of this order.

 

 

The court orders plaintiff Video Tech Services, Inc. to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Petition for approval of compromise of claim for minor claimant

 

MOVING PARTY:            Petitioner Anlly Marquez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   No response

Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim for Minor Claimant 

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition papers were filed.

DISCUSSION

Petitioner Annly Marquez seeks court approval of the settlement made on behalf of plaintiff minor claimant Fatima Marquez (“Minor Claimant”) in this action.

The compromise of a minor’s disputed claim for damages is valid only after it has been approved, upon the filing of a petition, by the court.¿ (Prob. Code, § 3500.)¿ The petition must be verified by the petitioner, must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise, and must be prepared on Judicial Council form MC-350.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

Defendant Chedraui USA, Inc. has agreed to settle this action for a total of $250,000.00, to Minor Claimant.  (Pet., MC-350, ¶¶ 10-11.)  Of the $250,000.00 settlement, $87,500 will be paid to counsel for attorney’s fees and $30,000 will be paid for medical expenses, and $7,626.43 will be paid for costs.  (Pet., MC-350, ¶¶ 13, 16.)  The remaining $124,873.57 will be deposited into an insured account with a financial institution in this state, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the court.  (Pet., MC-350, ¶¶ 16, 18, subd. (b)(2).) 

The court has reviewed the petition and finds the settlement to be fair and reasonable, and in the best interests of Minor Claimant. The court further finds that the declaration of Susana Abrahamian is sufficient to support the request for attorney’s fees in the amount of $87,500 (representing 35 percent of the gross $500,000.00 settlement).  (Abrahamian Decl., ¶¶ 10-11 .)   

          The court therefore grants the petition.

ORDER

The court GRANTS petitioner Annly Marquez’s petition to approve the compromise of claim on behalf of minor claimant Fatima Marquez.   

The court sets for hearing an Order to Show Cause re filing Receipt and Acknowledgement of Order for the Deposit of Money into Blocked Account (MC-356) on November 13, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Department 513.  

The court orders petitioner Annly Marquez to give notice of this ruling. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant Ford Motor Company

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Stacey K. Smith

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant Ford Motor Company (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order granting judgment on the pleadings as to the sixth cause of action for fraudulent concealment alleged by plaintiff Stacey K. Smith (“Plaintiff”) in her complaint.

The court grant’s Defendant’s motion as to the sixth cause of action for fraudulent inducement – concealment because it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiff has not alleged that Defendant had a duty to disclose the facts regarding the subject defects in the vehicle, including because Plaintiff has not alleged facts establishing that Plaintiff purchased the vehicle directly from Defendant or its agent and that, during the course of those direct dealings, Defendant concealed facts regarding the defects from Plaintiff.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(1)(B)(ii); Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 40-41 [circumstances imposing a duty to disclose “presuppose a preexisting relationship between the parties” which “must necessarily arise from direct dealings between the plaintiff and the defendant; it cannot arise between the defendant and the public at large”]; Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 844, review dism. Dec. 18, 2024 [finding complaint sufficient and declining to hold claim barred on the ground that there was no relationship requiring Nissan to disclose known defects where plaintiffs alleged they bought the car from a Nissan dealership, that Nissan backed the car with its warranty, and Nissan’s authorized dealerships are its agents for purposes of the sale of Nissan vehicles to consumers].) 

The burden is on the plaintiff “to articulate how it could amend its pleading to render it sufficient.”¿ (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿ To satisfy that burden, a plaintiff “must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.”¿ (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)¿  The court finds that Plaintiff has not articulated the facts that she could allege to render the sixth cause of action against Ford sufficient and therefore grants the motion without leave to amend. 

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendant Ford Motor Company’s motion for judgment on the pleadings as to the sixth cause of action for fraudulent concealment without leave to amend.

 

The court orders defendant Ford Motor Company to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
