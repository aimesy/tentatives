CASE:                          Security National Insurance Company v. Modern Floor Specialists Inc., et al.

 

CASE NO.:                   24STCV15891

 

MOTION TO STRIKE PLEADINGS

 

 

MOVING PARTY/PARTIES: Plaintiff and Cross-Defendant Security National Insurance Company

 

RESPONDING PARTY/PARTIES: None

 

PROOF OF SERVICE:

·        Correct Address: Yes

·        Served electronically on March 30, 2026 for September 16, 2026 hearing date

 

CASE HISTORY:

·        June 25, 2024: Complaint filed.

·        April 28, 2025: Defendant’s operative first amended answer filed.

·        September 12, 2025: Defendant’s cross-complaint filed.

 

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

            Plaintiff and Cross-Defendant Security National Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) moves to strike the operative first amended answer and cross-complaint of Defendant Modern Floor Specialists, Inc. (“Defendant”).  Defendant has not opposed the motion.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Plaintiff’s motion to strike Defendant’s pleadings is GRANTED.

Moving party to give notice. 

 

DISCUSSION:

Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading, may serve and file a motion to strike the whole pleading or any part thereof.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1322, subd. (b).)  On a motion to strike, the Court may: (1) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the Court.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (a)-(b); Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782.)

Defendant is a suspended corporation.  “The suspension of the corporate powers, rights, and privileges means a suspended corporation cannot sue or defend a lawsuit while its taxes remain unpaid.” (Kaufman & Broad Communities, Inc. v. Performance Plastering, Inc. (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 212, 217–218.)  As such, Defendant cannot defend this action, nor can it maintain its cross-claims. 

As such, Plaintiff’s motion to strike Defendant’s pleadings is GRANTED. 

Moving party to give notice. 

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 24STCV34108    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 415

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Rolf M. Treu, Department 415

 

 

HEARING DATE:         September 16, 2026                                 TRIAL DATE:  Not set.

                                                                  

CASE:                             Spiva v. Maker Learning Network

 

CASE NO.:                     24STCV34108

 

             

SPECIAL MOTION TO STRIKE

 

MOVING PARTY/PARTIES: Defendants Robert Davis, Mario Bates, and Calvin Howard (“Defendants”)

 

RESPONDING PARTY/PARTIES: Plaintiff Derrick Spiva (“Plaintiff”)

 

PROOF OF SERVICE:

·       The Court continued the matter to this date.

 

CASE HISTORY:

·       December 23, 2024: Complaint filed.

·       June 5, 2025: First amended complaint filed.

 

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

              Plaintiff Derrick Spiva (“Plaintiff”) sued Defendants Robert Davis, Mario Bates, and Calvin Howard (“Defendants”) for employment claims.  Defendants filed a special motion to strike the eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth, and fourteenth causes of action in Plaintiff’s first amended complaint.  Plaintiff opposes the motion.

In its order of June 3, 2026, the Court directed Plaintiff to file an amended complaint within 20 days of notice of the order.  The Court deferred ruling on Defendants’ special motion to strike until resolution of the pending challenges to Plaintiff’s pleading.  (See Oakland Bulk & Oversized Terminal, LLC v. City of Oakland (2020) 54 Cal.App.5th 738, 751.)  Defendants gave notice of the order on June 8, 2026 by email.  As such, Plaintiff had until June 30, 2026 to file the second amended complaint.  Plaintiff failed to do so.   Accordingly, the Court rules on the special motion to strike.  The Court does not consider the motion as to the Plaintiff’s thirteenth cause of action, to which the Court sustained Defendants’ demurrer without leave to amend. Likewise, the Court does not consider the motion as to the fourteenth cause of action for fraud, to which the Court sustained Defendants’ demurrer with leave to amend.  As Plaintiff failed to amend, the fourteenth cause of action is subject to dismissal.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 581, subd. (f)(2).)  The Court considers the motion as to the eleventh and twelfth causes of action.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendants’ special motion to strike is DENIED.

Moving party to give notice. 

 

DISCUSSION:

In ruling on a defendant's special motion to strike, the trial court uses a two-step process.  First, the defendant must show that the act or acts of which the plaintiff complains were taken in furtherance of the defendant's right of petition or free speech under the United States or California Constitutions in connection with a public issue.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (b)(1).)  If the defendant carries that burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff to demonstrate a probability of prevailing on the claim.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (b)(3).) 

Plaintiff’s eleventh cause of action is for defamation and twelfth cause of action is for false light.  Plaintiff’s claims for defamation and false light are based on Plaintiff’s allegations that Defendants falsely stated “that Plaintiff engaged in financial mismanagement, fraud, or other unlawful conduct in his role as Executive Director of Synergy School of the Arts and Technology.”  (First Amended Complaint, ¶¶ 241-242.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants made these statements “in communications submitted to the Department of Justice and other third parties . . . .”  (First Amended Complaint, ¶ 241, see also ¶ 247.)  Reporting a person’s unlawful conduct to authorities may constitute a petition to an official body for purposes of a special motion to strike.  (See Wang v. Hartunian (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 744, 749.)  However, “[n]either the federal nor the state constitutional right of petition or free speech encompass a right to file a false crime report.”  (Lefebvre v. Lefebvre (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 696, 703, emphasis original.)  As such, if, as Plaintiff alleges, Defendants falsely reported Plaintiff to the authorities, Plaintiff’s claims do not fall within the special motion to strike framework.  Defendants advance no evidence to show that Defendants’ reports were truthful.  Accordingly, Defendants fail to meet their burden to show that Plaintiff’s claims fall within the special motion to strike framework.

As such, Defendants’ special motion to strike is DENIED.

Moving party to give notice. 

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV20390    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 415

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Rolf M. Treu, Department 415

 

 

HEARING DATE:         September 16, 2026                                 TRIAL DATE:  November 2, 2027

                                                                  

CASE:                             Jesse Klick v. TRB Melrose, LLC

 

CASE NO.:                     25STCV20390

             

MOTION FOR SANCTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY/PARTIES: Plaintiff Jesse Klick

 

RESPONDING PARTY/PARTIES: Defendant TRB Melrose, LLC

 

PROOF OF SERVICE:

·       Defendant opposes the motion on the merits, and thereby waived any procedural objections.  (See In re Marriage of Falcone (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 814, 826.)  

 

CASE HISTORY:

·       July 10, 2025: Complaint filed.

 

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

              Plaintiff Jesse Klick (“Plaintiff”) moves for sanctions against Defendant TRB Melrose, LLC (“Defendant”) and Defendant’s counsel of record, Jose-Manuel A. de Castro, based on Defendant’s filing of its motion for sanctions in this action.  Defendant opposes the motion.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Plaintiff’s motion for sanctions is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Moving party to give notice. 

 

DISCUSSION:

Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 requires an attorney to sign all pleadings, petitions, notices of motions and other similar papers.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (a).)  By the signature, the attorney certifies that the “legal contentions therein are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law . . . ,” and that the “allegations and other factual contentions have evidentiary support . . . .”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subds. (b)(2), (b)(3).)  If the court determines that an attorney has violated this requirement, the court may impose sanctions against the attorney or party responsible for the violation.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c).)  A court may impose sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 only if the challenged pleading “is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected” within 21 days after service of the motion.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c)(1).) 

In its order of March 3, 2026, the Court denied Defendant’s motion for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7.  Now, Plaintiff moves for sanctions under Section 128.7 based on Defendant’s filing of its motion for sanctions.  “A motion for sanctions brought by a party or a party's attorney primarily for an improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in the cost of litigation, shall itself be subject to a motion for sanctions.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (h).)  However, Plaintiff did not serve this motion on Defendant until after this Court denied Defendant’s motion.  As such, Plaintiff cannot satisfy the safe-harbor provision, which requires that Defendant be able to withdraw the motion.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c)(1).) 

In reply, Plaintiff argues that Plaintiff need not comply with the safe-harbor provision because Plaintiff seeks sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7, subdivision (c)(1), which provides, “If warranted, the court may award to the party prevailing on the motion the reasonable expenses and attorney's fees incurred in presenting or opposing the motion.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c)(1).)  Plaintiff is correct that Plaintiff may seek Plaintiff’s fees for opposing Defendant’s motion for sanctions if Defendant’s motion for sanctions was frivolous.  (See Musaelian v. Adams (2011) 197 Cal.App.4th 1251, 1257.)  However, to recover such fees, Plaintiff must file a motion for attorney’s fees and expenses, not a new motion for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7, which requires Plaintiff to comply with the safe-harbor provision.  Additionally, Plaintiff must show that Defendant’s motion was frivolous, which Plaintiff fails to address in this motion.

Likewise, Plaintiff’s argument that the Court directed Plaintiff to file this motion is incorrect.  In its order on Defendant’s motion for sanctions, this Court noted that Plaintiff could not seek sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 in opposition to a motion.  (March 3, 2026 Minute Order.)  The Court did not direct Plaintiff to file this motion. 

As such, Plaintiff’s motion for sanctions is DENIED without prejudice.

Moving party to give notice. 

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
