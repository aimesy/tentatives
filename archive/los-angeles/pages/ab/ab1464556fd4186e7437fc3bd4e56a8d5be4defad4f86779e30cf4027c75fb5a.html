Case Number: 24STCV33275    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 11, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Volort, LLC, et al. v. Robert Yepremian

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV33275

 

MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiffs Volort, LLC, Shin Ho Kim, and Gena Soon Yoon

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendant Robert Yepremian

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order granting summary judgment on all causes of action in the SAC in Plaintiffs’ favor.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for summary judgment is DENIED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On December 17, 2024, Plaintiffs Volort, LLC (Volort), Shin Ho Kim (Kim), and Gena Soon Yoon (Yoon) (sometimes collectively Plaintiffs) filed a complaint against Defendant Robert Yepremian (Defendant) with three causes of action for: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing; and (3) unfair business practices.

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiffs leased commercial property located at 3620 Foothill Blvd., Glendale, CA 91214 (the Premises) to Defendant to operate a dry-cleaning and tailoring business. Plaintiffs allege that Defendant was repeatedly in default of the lease and remain in possession of the Premises without paying back rent or current rent.

 

On January 28, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a first amended complaint with the same causes of action.

 

On March 18, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the operative second amended complaint (SAC) pursuant to stipulation with the same causes of action.

 

On April 17, 2025, Defendant filed a motion to strike portions of the SAC which the court GRANTED as to the punitive damages allegations and prayer for relief only.

 

On August 27, 2025, Defendant filed an answer.

 

On June 2, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion for summary judgment. On August 20, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. On August 28, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

“[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears the burden of persuasion that there is no triable issue of material fact and that he is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850, fn. omitted (Aguilar).)

 

“A plaintiff . . . has met [their] burden of showing that there is no defense to a cause of action if that party has proven each element of the cause of action entitling [them] to judgment on the cause of action.” (Code Civ. Proc. (CCP) § 437c subd. (p)(1).) “Once the plaintiff . . . has met that burden, the burden shifts to the defendant . . . to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to the cause of action or a defense thereto.” (Ibid.) They must “set forth the specific facts showing that a triable issue of material fact exists as to the cause of action or defense thereto.” (Ibid.)

 

A triable issue of material fact exists where “the evidence would allow a reasonable trier of fact to find the underlying fact in favor of the party opposing the motion in accordance with the applicable standard of proof.” (Aguilar, supra, at p. 850.)

 

The opposing party on a motion for summary judgment is under no evidentiary burden to produce rebuttal evidence until the moving party meets his or her initial movant’s burden.¿ (Binder v. Aetna Life Insurance Company (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 832, 840.)¿ Summary judgment must be granted “if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”¿ (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7¿Cal.App.4th¿1110, 1119.)¿¿¿ 

 

The pleadings dictate the scope of a motion for summary judgment. “The materiality of a disputed fact is measured by the pleadings [citations], which ‘set the boundaries of the issues to be resolved at summary judgment.’ [Citations.]” (Hutton v. Fid. Nat'l Title Co. (2013) 213 Cal. App. 4th 486, 493.)

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Plaintiffs contend summary judgment is proper because Defendant admitted non-payment and late payment of rent in a separate unlawful detainer action which conclusively establishes the elements of Plaintiffs’ claims in this case.[1] They add that withholding rent, promising to pay and not paying constitute bad faith and unlawful/unfair business practices.

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiffs’ defective separate statement warrants denial, Plaintiffs fail to meet their initial burden, the unlawful detainer judgments do not have res judicata/collateral estoppel effect, triable issues of material fact exist on all claims, and triable issues as to defenses exist.

 

Plaintiffs reply that the unlawful detainer judgments are final rulings, Defendant should be sanctioned for representing otherwise, and they established their claims.

 

As a threshold matter, the court addresses the separate statement issue.

 

“The supporting papers [to a summary judgment motion] shall include a separate statement setting forth plainly and concisely all material facts that the moving party contends are undisputed.” (CCP § 437c(b)(1).) “Each of the material facts stated shall be followed by a reference to the supporting evidence.” (Ibid.) “The failure to comply with this requirement of a separate statement may in the court’s discretion constitute a sufficient ground for denying the motion.” (Ibid.)

 

The separate statement must separately identify: “[e]ach cause of action, claim for damages, issue of duty, or affirmative defense that is the subject of the motion;” and “[e]ach supporting material fact claimed to be without dispute with respect to the cause of action, claim for damages, issue of duty, or affirmative defense that is the subject of the motion.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(d)(1)(A)-(B).) “The statement must state in numerical sequence the undisputed material in the first column followed by the evidence that establishes those undisputed facts in the same column.” (Id. at subd. (d)(3).) “Citation to the evidence in support of each fact must include reference to the exhibit, title, page, and line numbers.” (Ibid.)

 

Here, neither party presented a compliant separate statement. Plaintiffs’ separate statement failed to cite to evidence, is not separated by claim or issue, and includes argument. Defendant’s opposing separate statement includes argument and evidentiary objections that do not belong there. However, Plaintiffs’ separate statement is more problematic since they, as the moving party, bear the initial burden to show no triable issues of material fact.

 

Even considering the separate statement, Plaintiffs still fail to meet their initial burden. Critically, Plaintiffs lack evidence supporting their material facts to their claims. As an example, Plaintiffs state that Defendant “repeated[ly] promise[d] to pay rent and cure arrears while continuing to occupy the Premises”  - a material fact.[2] (Plaintiff’s Separate Statement No. 46.) Plaintiffs cite no evidence in support. Without evidence, Plaintiffs cannot meet their burden. (See CCP § 437c(b)(1) [“Each of the material facts stated shall be followed by a reference to the supporting evidence.”]) Summary judgment is therefore inappropriate.[3]

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES Plaintiffs’ motion for summary judgment.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for summary judgment is DENIED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 26STCV11533    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 11, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Keith Sidlo v. Mercury Insurance dba California Automobile Insurance Company

 

CASE NO.:                26STCV11533

 

DEMURRER

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant California Automobile Insurance Company

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Keith Sidlo

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      Demurrer to the third cause of action for failing to state sufficient facts and for uncertainty.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Demurrer to the third cause of action is OVERRULED;

2.      Defendant to file their answer within 14 days of this ruling.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On April 9, 2026, Plaintiff Keith Sidlo (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendant Mercury Insurance dba California Automobile Insurance Company (Defendant) with causes of action for: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing; and (3) violation of Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200.

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff was the named insured under Homeowner Insurance Policy No. CAHP0001162151 issued by Defendant. Plaintiff alleges the policy was effective from June 22, 2022 to June 22, 2023 and that he suffered a covered loss during this period. Plaintiff further alleges that Defendant unduly delayed in processing the claim, made only partial payments on the claim, unilaterally closed the claim, and other tactics.

 

On June 1, 2026, Defendant (appearing as California Automobile Insurance Company, erroneously sued as Mercury Insurance dba California Automobile Insurance Company) filed the instant demurrer. On August 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On September 3, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Meet and Confer¿ 

¿ 

Prior to filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to satisfy their meet and confer obligations pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41, and demonstrate that they so satisfied their meet and confer obligation by submitting a declaration pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41(a)(2) & (3). 

 

Here, the parties met and conferred on May 11, 2026 via Zoom call. (Declaration of Tami Kay Lee ¶ 2.)  This requirement is therefore met. 

 

Demurrer¿ 

¿ 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿(Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.¿In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice.¿(Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)¿“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. …. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”¿(Hahn¿147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true. (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.) Courts also consider exhibits attached to the complaint and incorporated by reference. (See Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94.)¿¿ 

 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Third Cause of Action: Violation of Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200

 

Defendant contends this claim fails because Plaintiff failed to establish the unlawful prong by failing to plead violations of each subdivision of Ins. Code § 790.03(h). In particular, they contend Plaintiff’s mere recitation of statutory language is insufficient. Defendant additionally contends the unfair prong fails because it is based on conclusory language, does not identify a legislatively declared public policy, and it limited to Plaintiff’s specific alleged contractual breaches. Next, Defendant contends this claim fails the fraudulent prong because there is no public-facing conduct likely to deceive, no duty to disclose.[1] Finally, Defendant contends this claim is derivative of the first two causes of action and fails to allege a distinct economic injury.[2]

 

Plaintiff argues that they sufficiently stated this claim. The unlawful prong is sufficient because they have a common law bad faith claim in addition to statutory violations and Defendant imposes a nonexistent heightened pleading standard as well as an incorrect liability context. As to the unfair prong, Ins. Code § 790.03(h) is itself legislatively declared policy as well as Ins. Code §§ 790-790.10 and Civ. Code § 3289. Plaintiff alleged conduct likely to deceive to support the fraudulent prong, they alleged affirmative misrepresentation that a partial remediation payment satisfied the entire property damage claim, and there is no requirement for the conduct to be public-facing. The claim is not duplicative since it seeks separate remedies and he has standing since he has sustained economic injury.[3]

 

Defendant replies that Plaintiff’s claims are barred as a prohibited end-run around Ins. Code § 790.03(h)’s bar against private rights of action and reiterate their arguments about insufficiency of the unlawful, unfair, and fraudulent prongs. They add that Plaintiff’s alleged unpaid policy benefits do not independently establish an actionable UCL injury to confer standing.[4]

 

California Business and Professions Code section 17200 prohibits “any¿unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business act or practice.” (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200;¿see Clark v. Superior Court¿(2010) 50 Cal.4th 605, 610.) A business practice is unfair when it offends an established public policy or when the practice is immoral, unethical, oppressive, unscrupulous, or substantially injurious to consumers." (Community Assisting Recovery, Inc. v. Aegis Security Ins. Co.¿(2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 886, 894.) A business practice is¿unlawful if it violates another law. (Berryman v. Merit Property Management, Inc.¿(2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 1544, 1554.) A business practice is fraudulent if “members of the public are likely to be deceived." (See Wang v. Massey Chevrolet¿(2002) 97 Cal. App. 4th 856, 871.) “A plaintiff alleging unfair business practices . . . must state with reasonable particularity the facts supporting the statutory elements of the violation.” (Khoury v. Maly’s of California, Inc.¿(1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 619.) 

 

“[F]irst party UCL actions based on grounds independent from [Ins. Code] section 790.03,” are not precluded “[e]ven when the insurer’s conduct also violates section 790.03.” (Zhang v. Superior Court (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364, 369(Zhang).) Put differently, a plaintiff may bring a UCL claim for independent conduct like false advertising and insurance bad faith. (Ibid.)

 

After reviewing the complaint, Plaintiff sufficiently stated a UCL claim. First, to the extent Plaintiff’s claim is based on Ins. Code § 790.03(h), it is prohibited. (Zhang, supra, 57 Cal.4th at p. 384 [“[A] litigant may not rely on the proscriptions of section 790.03 as the basis for a UCL claim.”]) On the one hand, Plaintiff appears to do that. (Compl. ¶¶ 28.) On the other hand, Plaintiff incorporated by reference prior allegations that allege bad faith conduct (i.e., breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing). (Compl. ¶¶ 11.) Such conduct consisted of: ceasing communications with Plaintiff, representing partial payment for remediation work consisted of full payment, unilaterally closing the claim file repeatedly, and involving a 12-month suit limitation clause despite causing the delays. (Ibid.) This conduct, while it may also violate Ins. Code § 790.03, also supports a common law bad faith claim. This, in turn, supports a UCL claim. (Zhang, supra, 57 Cal.4th at p. 369.) Thus, for pleading purposes, Plaintiff sufficiently stated this claim.

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES Defendant’s demurrer.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Demurrer to the third cause of action is OVERRULED;

2.      Defendant to file their answer within 14 days of this ruling.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[4] The court rejects this argument. UCL standing is confined to “those actually injured by a defendant’s business practices.” (Schwartz v. Provident Life & Accident Ins. Co. (2013) 216 Cal.App.4th 607, 611.) Proof of lost money or property “may be shown in many ways” such as when they are “deprived of money or property to which he or she has a cognizable claim. . . .” (Ibid. [internal citations omitted].) In Schwartz, the plaintiff never made an insurance claim. (Id. at p. 611-612.) Thus, the Court held that he did not lose any money or property. (Ibid.) Here, Plaintiff did make a claim and alleges he did not receive the full benefits of that claim. (Compl. ¶¶ 10, 11, 12.) This is sufficient economic injury to confer UCL standing.
