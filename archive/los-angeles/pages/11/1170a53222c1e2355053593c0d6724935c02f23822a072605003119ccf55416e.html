MOTION TO QUASH NOTICES TO APPEAR AT TRIAL

 

Motion filed on April 6, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Robert George McLellan, Jr., Sunrise Dressingrooms, Inc., Amazon Content Services, LLC, and Picrow, Inc. dba Pictures in a Row

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Christine Tanaka

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Defendants move to quash Plaintiff Christine Tanaka’s notices to appear at trial directed to David E. Kelley and Jonathan Shapiro, and request monetary sanctions of $2,323.41.

RULING: Denied without prejudice. Sanctions denied.

 

BACKGROUND

Defendants filed this motion on April 6, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 15, 2026. Defendants filed a reply on June 22, 2026.

 

DISCUSSION

 

This action arises from a motor vehicle collision. Defendant McLellan was “hired by PICROW, INC., a production company, to drive a production truck for the Amazon television show ‘Goliath’ Season 4, when his truck made contact with a vehicle driven by Plaintiff.” (Motion, p. 3.) On March 31, 2026, Plaintiff served notices under Code of Civil Procedure section 1987 requiring David E. Kelley and Jonathan Shapiro, the credited creators and writers of “Goliath,” to appear at trial.

 

Defendants move to quash on the ground that Kelley and Shapiro “have no knowledge or information whatsoever regarding this litigation,” that they “were no longer working on the show during Season 4 when this incident occurred,” and that requiring their attendance “is harassing and unduly burdensome.” (Motion, pp. 2-3.) Defendants contend the testimony Plaintiff seeks is available from a less intrusive source, having “offered a Picrow, Inc. PMK who can testify regarding these matters, which Plaintiff has outright refused.” (Motion, p. 3.)

 

Plaintiff opposes, contending she “seeks the trial testimony of David E. Kelley and Jonathan Shapiro to establish facts that Defendants have wrongfully withheld in discovery; i.e. that Defendant Amazon is vicariously liable for the truck that rear-ended Plaintiff.” (Opposition, p. 2.) Plaintiff contends Defendants offer no evidence of burden, that “notably missing from Defendants’ Motion is any actual evidence or reasoned argument as to why testifying at trial would be unduly burdensome and harassing,” and that Defendants submitted no declaration from either witness. (Opposition, p. 4.) Plaintiff further contends the motion is premature under the April 8 order, citing the Court’s ruling that “the issues raised are more appropriately resolved as evidentiary matters at or near the time of trial.” (Opposition, p. 6.)

 

The Court denied Defendants’ ex parte application to quash these notices on April 8, 2026. The Court found no immediate or irreparable harm. The Court ruled that “issues of relevance, burden, and alleged harassment” were “more appropriately resolved as evidentiary matters at or near the time of trial,” and stated it “will address any challenges to the testimony of these witnesses by motion in limine, Evidence Code section 402 hearing, or other appropriate trial procedure.” The Court ordered that Kelley and Shapiro “are not required to appear on the first day of trial absent further order of the Court.” (4/8/26 Minute Order, p. 1.)

 

The March 31, 2026 notices required Kelley and Shapiro to appear for the April 20, 2026 trial. On April 10, 2026, the Court vacated that trial date and continued the matter to July 26, 2027, after finding that “the required FSC materials were not submitted in compliance with the Court’s orders” and that “the matter is not trial-ready.” (4/10/26 Minute Order, p. 1.) The notices therefore do not present a current trial attendance issue. The reply’s statement that trial is set for August 4, 2026 is incorrect. (Reply, caption.)

 

This motion raises the same relevance, burden, and harassment grounds the April 8 order assigned to trial procedure, and asks the Court to decide them now, more than one year before trial. The Court declines to decide whether Kelley or Shapiro may offer admissible testimony at a trial set that far out. That issue is premature, and the April 8 order already directs that it be resolved by motion in limine, Evidence Code section 402 hearing, or other appropriate trial procedure. If Plaintiff serves renewed notices or subpoenas tied to the July 26, 2027 trial date, Defendants may raise any proper objection at that time.

 

Given the trial continuance, the Court encourages the parties to meet and confer regarding the testimony Plaintiff seeks and the means of obtaining it before further motion practice.

 

Sanctions are denied. Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.2, subdivision (a), permits the Court, in its discretion, to award reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing a motion under section 1987.1 if the Court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification, or that one or more requirements of the subpoena was oppressive. Plaintiff’s opposition was substantially justified by the trial continuance and the April 8 order, and the Court makes no finding that the March 31, 2026 notices were oppressive. Sanctions are denied.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion to quash is denied without prejudice as moot and premature. David E. Kelley and Jonathan Shapiro are not required to appear on the first day of trial absent further order of the Court, and Plaintiff shall provide reasonable advance notice to all parties before calling either witness. Defendants’ request for monetary sanctions is denied.

DEFENDANTS’ MOTIONS FOR PROTECTIVE ORDER REGARDING PLAINTIFF’S WRITTEN DISCOVERY

 

Motions filed on March 27, 2026 and April 7, 2026. Joinder filed on June 15, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Doron Shauly, Alon West Hills, LLC, Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Elly-Joy Shalomé Kaushansky

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Defendants seek protective orders excusing them from responding to Plaintiff’s written discovery, or requiring Plaintiff to reduce the number of requests.

RULING: Continued. Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia for protective order relief via joinder is denied.  

 

BACKGROUND

Plaintiff Elly-Joy Shalomé Kaushansky filed the operative First Amended Complaint on November 17, 2025 against Defendants Alon West Hills, LLC, Alon Investments, LLC, Iris Shapiro, Doron Shauly, Walter Floridia, and Does 1 through 50, asserting causes of action for (1) breach of contract, (2) breach of implied warranty of habitability and tenantability, (3) collection of rent on substandard dwelling, (4) breach of implied warranty of quiet enjoyment, (5) negligence based on habitability, (6) private nuisance, (7) retaliatory acts, (8) constructive eviction, (9) assault, (10) battery, (11) negligence based on personal injury, (12) intentional infliction of emotional distress, (13) negligent infliction of emotional distress, (14) harassment under Los Angeles Municipal Code section 45.33, and (15) equitable estoppel and constructive fraud.

 

Defendant Shauly filed a motion for protective order on March 27, 2026, supported by the declaration of Nhutruc H. Do and exhibits. Plaintiff filed a corrected opposition on May 15, 2026, supported by her declaration and exhibits. Defendant Shauly filed a reply on May 19, 2026.

 

Defendant Alon West Hills, LLC filed a motion for protective order on April 7, 2026, supported by the declaration of Tiffanie Q. Spivey and exhibits. Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 15, 2026, supported by her declaration and exhibits.

 

Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia filed a joinder on June 15, 2026, supported by the declaration of Michael J. Early and exhibits. Plaintiff filed an opposition to the joinder on June 22, 2026, supported by her declaration and exhibits.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party seeking a protective order must move promptly and must support the motion with a meet-and-confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.090, subd. (a), 2031.060, subd. (a), 2033.080, subd. (a).) In general, the moving party bears the burden of showing good cause for the protective order sought. (Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255.) Good cause requires specific facts showing undue burden, oppression, or other grounds for protection. (Goodman v. Citizens Life & Casualty Ins. Co. (1967) 253 Cal.App.2d 807, 819-820.)

 

A party may propound more than 35 specially prepared interrogatories if the party attaches a declaration under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.050 and the greater number is warranted by the complexity or quantity of the issues, the financial burden of oral deposition, or the expedience of written discovery to allow inquiry, investigation, or review of files or records. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.040, subd. (a)(1)-(3).) If the responding party seeks a protective order on the ground that the number is unwarranted, “the propounding party shall have the burden of justifying the number of these interrogatories.” (Id., subd. (b), emphasis added.)

 

The same burden applies to requests for admission exceeding 35 matters that do not relate to the genuineness of documents. A party may request a greater number if the party attaches a declaration under Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.050 and the greater number is warranted by the complexity or quantity of the issues. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.040, subd. (a).) If the responding party seeks a protective order on the ground that the number is unwarranted, “the propounding party shall have the burden of justifying the number of requests for admission.” (Id., subd. (b).)

 

For inspection demands, the Court may also restrict the frequency or extent of discovery if “[t]he discovery sought is unreasonably cumulative or duplicative,” or if “[t]he selected method of discovery is unduly burdensome or expensive, taking into account the needs of the case, the amount in controversy, and the importance of the issues at stake in the litigation.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2019.030, subd. (a)(1)-(2).)

 

The Court may order that the set or particular requests need not be answered, that the number of interrogatories or admission requests is unwarranted, or that the time to respond is extended. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.090, subd. (b)(1)-(3), 2033.080, subd. (b)(1)-(3).) For inspection demands, the Court may make any order that justice requires to protect a party from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden and expense. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.060, subd. (b).)

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant Shauly and Defendant Alon West Hills, LLC

 

Defendant Shauly and Defendant Alon West Hills timely moved for protective orders. Defendant Shauly filed his motion on March 27, 2026, and Defendant Alon West Hills filed its motion on April 7, 2026. Both motions were filed before the asserted April 15, 2026 response deadline.

 

Plaintiff previously served Defendant Shauly with Form Interrogatories, 35 Special Interrogatories, 47 Requests for Production, and 35 Requests for Admission. Defendant Shauly states he responded in July 2025. (Do Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) Plaintiff then served Defendant Shauly with 382 Special Interrogatories, 269 Requests for Production, and 280 Requests for Admission, for a total of 931 additional requests. (Do Decl., ¶ 4.)

 

Plaintiff previously served Defendant Alon West Hills with Form Interrogatories, 35 Special Interrogatories, 50 Requests for Production, and 35 Requests for Admission. Defendant Alon West Hills states it responded on June 18, 2025. (Spivey Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) Plaintiff then served Defendant Alon West Hills with 382 Special Interrogatories, 269 Requests for Production, and 127 Requests for Admission, for a total of 778 additional requests. (Spivey Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

For the Special Interrogatories and Requests for Admission exceeding 35, Plaintiff served declarations under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.050 and 2033.050. Plaintiff relies on the complexity of the case, the number of Defendants and causes of action, the volume of prior defense discovery, and alleged deficiencies in prior responses. That showing may support targeted follow-up discovery. On the current record, it does not explain why the March 13, 2026 discovery should be answered as served.

 

The record also reflects overlap and burden concerns. Defendant Shauly identifies overlap in requests concerning physical contact, striking, whether Plaintiff fell or was knocked down, rent notices, three-day notices, law enforcement communications, insurance information, and water-service interruptions. (Mot., pp. 5-7; Do Decl., ¶ 8.) Plaintiff declined to reduce the number of requests or agree to an extension, and maintained that the discovery was not repetitive or duplicative. (Do Decl., ¶¶ 9-10, Ex. C; Kaushansky Decl., ¶¶ 22-25.) Defendant Alon West Hills similarly attempted to meet and confer regarding the volume and burden of the March 13, 2026 discovery, but Plaintiff did not agree to reduce the requests. (Spivey Decl., ¶¶ 9-11.)

 

The motions were filed when trial was set for July 20, 2026 and the nonexpert discovery cutoff was June 20, 2026. Defendants contend, in part, that the volume of discovery requested by Plaintiff could not reasonably be answered within the statutory response period and before the then-existing discovery cutoff. Trial has now been continued. The continuance gives the parties an opportunity to narrow the discovery before the Court decides whether protective relief is necessary.

 

The Court will therefore continue Defendant Shauly’s and Defendant Alon West Hills’ motions for one further direct meet and confer. Pending the continued hearing, Defendant Shauly and Defendant Alon West Hills are not required to respond to the March 13, 2026 discovery as served.

 

In light of the large volume of discovery served, the Court expects Plaintiff to cooperate meaningfully in reducing, consolidating, or withdrawing requests where appropriate. The Court also expects Defendants to meaningfully meet and confer, and to identify the remaining requests or categories they contend are cumulative, duplicative, overbroad, or unduly burdensome. The parties must file a joint statement narrowing any remaining dispute, as ordered below.

 

Joinder by Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia

 

The joinder by Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia is denied.

 

Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia each seek affirmative protective-order relief. Early identifies 382 Special Interrogatories, 269 Requests for Production, and 308 Requests for Admission to Alon Investment LLC; 382 Special Interrogatories, 269 Requests for Production, and 172 Requests for Admission to Shapiro; and 382 Special Interrogatories, 269 Requests for Production, and 243 Requests for Admission to Floridia. (Early Decl., ¶ 3.)

 

Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia joinder was not brought as a timely noticed motion for protective order by those responding parties. It also is not supported by a declaration showing a reasonable and good-faith attempt to meet and confer regarding the discovery directed to those parties. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2016.040, 2030.090, subd. (a), 2031.060, subd. (a), 2033.080, subd. (a).)

 

Accordingly, the protected order relief requested as to Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE. The Court will permit Defendants to renew these arguments on a properly noticed motion, and meet and confer declaration.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motions for protective order filed by Defendant Doron Shauly and Defendant Alon West Hills, LLC are CONTINUED to September 15, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. in Department F-46.

 

Pending the continued hearing, Defendant Shauly and Defendant Alon West Hills are not required to respond to the March 13, 2026, discovery as presently propounded.

 

No later than August 14, 2026, Plaintiff shall serve upon Defendant Shauly and Defendant Alon West Hills, LLC a written identification of the discovery requests Plaintiff elects to withdraw, consolidate, narrow, or maintain.

 

No later than August 28, 2026, Plaintiff and Defendants Shauly and Alon West Hills, LLC, through counsel as applicable, shall personally meet and confer in good faith by telephone, videoconference, or in person regarding the remaining disputed discovery. The exchange of emails or correspondence alone shall not satisfy this requirement.

 

No later than five (5) court days before the September 15, 2026 hearing, the parties shall file a joint statement, not to exceed seven (7) pages, setting forth:

 

(1) the discovery requests Plaintiff withdraws, consolidates, narrows, or maintains;

(2) the discovery requests or categories on which the parties have reached agreement;

(3) for each remaining disputed request or category, Defendants' specific basis for contending the request remains cumulative, duplicative, overbroad, disproportionate, unduly burdensome, or otherwise objectionable;

(4) the parties' agreed response deadline for any discovery remaining after the meet-and-confer process or, if no agreement is reached, each side's proposed response deadline; and

(5) a certification stating the date, manner (telephone, videoconference, or in person), and participants in the meet-and-confer conference, and confirming that the parties and/or their counsel personally met and conferred in good faith in an effort to resolve or narrow the disputes. The exchange of emails or correspondence alone shall not satisfy this requirement. No new legal arguments shall be included except as necessary to explain the disputes remaining after the meet-and-confer process.

 

The joinder filed by Defendants Iris Shapiro, Alon Investment LLC, and Walter Floridia is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

 

The Court reserves the requests for sanctions relating to Defendant Shauly’s and Defendant Alon West Hills’ motions until the continued hearing.

 

The moving parties are ordered to give notice.

APPLICATION TO BE ADMITTED PRO HAC VICE

 

Application filed May 13, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Petitioner Employbridge Holding Company dba Select Staffing

RESPONDING PARTY: N/A

NOTICE: OK

RELIEF REQUESTED: Admission of Lars-Eric L. Hedberg, Esq. to appear as counsel pro hac vice.

RULING: Continued.

 

BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION

 

On May 13, 2026, Petitioner Employbridge Holding Company dba Select Staffing filed an application to admit Lars-Eric L. Hedberg, Esq. to appear pro hac vice in this action.

 

A non-licensee of the State Bar may appear as counsel pro hac vice in the court's discretion. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40, subd. (a).) An active State Bar licensee must be associated as attorney of record. (Id., subd. (a).) The applicant must file a verified application together with proof of service of the application and notice of hearing on all appearing parties and on the State Bar at its San Francisco office. (Id., subd. (c)(1).) The application must contain the recitals set forth in subdivision (d). (Id., subd. (d).) The applicant must pay the application fee set by the State Bar. (Id., subd. (e).)

 

Here, Hedberg is a non-licensee in good standing in Virginia and the District of Columbia. (Id., subd. (a).) He is not a California resident, regularly employed in California, nor regularly engaged in substantial business, professional, or other activities in California. (Id., subd. (a)(1)–(3).) Active California licensee Nicole A. Naleway (SBN 300701) is associated as attorney of record. (Id., subd. (a).) The application states Hedberg's residence and office addresses. (Id., subd. (d)(1).) It states the courts and dates of his admission. (Id., subd. (d)(2).) It states his good standing in those courts. (Id., subd. (d)(3).) It states that he is not suspended or disbarred in any court. (Id., subd. (d)(4).) It identifies California counsel of record. (Id., subd. (d)(6).)

 

However, the application does not state the title, date, or disposition of any pro hac vice application Hedberg has filed in California in the preceding two years. (Id., subd. (d)(5).) No proof of service on the State Bar at its San Francisco office accompanies it. (Id., subd. (c)(1).) The record contains no proof that the application fee has been paid. (Id., subd. (e).) Hedberg may not appear until each is supplied.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The application is CONTINUED to August 19, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Before the continued hearing, Hedberg must file the statement required by subdivision (d)(5), proof of service on the State Bar, and proof of payment of the application fee. Petitioner's counsel is to give notice.
