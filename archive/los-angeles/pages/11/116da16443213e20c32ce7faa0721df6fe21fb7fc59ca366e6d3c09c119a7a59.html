Case Number: 22STCV23823    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 14, 2026                           TRIAL DATE:  September 28, 2026

                                                          

CASE:                         Monique Cooper, et al. v. B. Ruth Allen, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 22STCV23823

 

 

PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION OF DEFENDANT B. RUTH

ALLEN AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS; REOPEN AND/OR EXTEND THE DISCOVERY CUTOFF FOR LIMITED PURPOSE OF TAKING THE DEPOSITION

                       

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiffs Monique Cooper, et al.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant B. Ruth Allen

 

 

I.          INTRODUCTION

 

Plaintiffs Monique Cooper and Jayon Levon (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) bring this habitability action arising from their tenancy at 5416 3rd Ave, Los Angeles, California (the “Property”).  The Property was owned by defendants B. Ruth Allen (“Allen” or “Defendant”), B. Ruth Allen Trust, Charles Clark, and Leonard F. Delpit at the relevant time period.  

 

On August 26, 2026, the parties stipulated to extend the discovery deadline to 15 days before the trial.  Accordingly, the deadline to complete discovery is September 14, 2026.

 

On September 1, 2026, Plaintiffs filed this motion to compel Allen to appear for deposition and to reopen or extend discovery for the limited purpose of deposing Allen.  Plaintiffs request sanctions against Allen.

 

On September 8, 2026, Allen filed an opposition. 

 

At the time of this writing, no reply had been filed.

 

II.        JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

            Defendant requests judicial notice of her filings on August 31, 2026.  The unopposed request for judicial notice is GRANTED.  The court takes judicial notice of those filings but not the truth of the matters asserted therein.

III.       LEGAL STANDARD

 

Any party may obtain discovery by taking in California the oral deposition of any person.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.)¿ “If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action…without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

“If a motion under [Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450] subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).)¿¿¿ 

 

III.       DISCUSSION

 

The court must address the following issues: (1) whether the court should compel Allen to appear for deposition; (2) whether good cause exists to extend discovery for that limited purpose; (3) whether good cause exists to delay the taking of Allen’s deposition because of Allen’s claimed cognitive impairments and related medical testing of the same; (4) whether the deposition may proceed subject to certain modifications; and (5) whether sanctions should be imposed against Allen. 

 

After reviewing the briefing, the court will order Defendant to appear for deposition within 30 days of this order and allow her son, Marcus Allen, to accompany Defendant at the deposition, but not to testify on behalf of or otherwise “coach” Defendant.[1]  Defendant is a party to this action.  As such, Plaintiffs are entitled to take her deposition.  Defendant establishes to the satisfaction of the court that she has cognitive impairments which warrant modifications to the deposition—specifically, support from her son, Marcus Allen.  Defendant, however, does not establish that she cannot sit for deposition and provide testimony on the noticed topics.  For the foregoing reasons, the court also finds good cause exists to extend the discovery deadline for the limited purpose of completing Defendant’s deposition and to continue the trial date.

 

As to Plaintiffs’ request for sanctions, the request is not well taken.  This case was filed over four years ago.  Yet, Plaintiffs waited until July 2026 to begin discussing the taking of Defendant’s deposition.  (See Johnson Decl., ¶ 3.)  Plaintiffs have not demonstrated diligence in the prosecution of this action.  The court issues the order compelling Defendant’s deposition, extending discovery, and continuing the trial date despite Plaintiffs lack of diligence.

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

            Based on the foregoing, the motion is GRANTED.  The court makes the following orders:

 

            1.  Defendant B. Ruth Allen is ordered to appear for deposition within 15 days of this order.  

 

            2.  Marcus Allen may accompany Defendant at the deposition, if the deposition is to be taken in-person.  If the deposition is to be taken remotely, Marcus Allen may not attend.  Defendant’s other requested relief is Denied.

 

            3.   Discovery is extended for the limited purpose of taking Defendant’s deposition and any related matters.

 

            4.  The court anticipates continuing the FSC to September 28, 2026 and trial to October 12, 2026. Discovery dates and FSC filing requirements are not reset or extended pursuant to these new dates.

 

            5.  Plaintiffs’ request for monetary sanctions is Denied. 

 

 

Plaintiffs to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 24STCV08288    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 14, 2026                           TRIAL DATE:  August 2, 2027

                                                          

CASE:                            Mike Tehrani, et al. v. Mehrdad Adjari

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV08288

 

 

PURE ECO, INC.’S DEMURRER TO SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant Pure Eco, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Plaintiffs Mike Tehrani and Eskan Builders Corp.

 

 

I.          BACKGROUND

 

            This case concerns the property located at 12428 Laurel Terrace Drive, Studio City, California 91604 (the Property).  Defendant Mehrdad Adjari (Adjari) is the owner of the Property.  In 2021, Adjari and plaintiffs Mike Tehrani and Eskan Builders, Corp. (Plaintiffs) entered into a written contract wherein Plaintiff agreed to provide labor, services, equipment, or materials for a construction project on the Property.  Plaintiffs completed the work.  Adjari failed to pay Plaintiffs for the amount due. 

 

            On April 16, 2026, Adjari filed the operative First Amended Cross-Complaint against Plaintiffs for breach of contract and negligence.

 

On February 20, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the operative Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”) alleging twenty-one causes of action.  For the first time, Plaintiffs name Pure Eco, Inc. (“Pure Eco”) in the SAC as a defendant.  Pure Eco performed construction work on a portion of the Property.

 

            On June 17, 2026, Pure Eco filed a demurrer to the 14th-20th causes of action in the SAC.

 

            On July 15, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition.

 

            On July 21, 2026, Pure Eco filed a reply.

 

            The demurrer was heard on July 28, 2026.  After oral argument, the court adopted the tentative ruling finding that Pure Eco did not meet and confer prior to filing this demurrer.  The court continued the hearing and directed the parties to meet and confer.  The court also directed the parties to file a supplemental opposition or supplemental reply pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1005.  Alternatively, Pure Eco was to file a status report no later than 5 court days before the hearing indicating whether the court should rule on the demurrer based on the current briefing.

 

            On August 24, 2026, counsel for Pure Eco filed a declaration explaining that, after meeting and conferring, Plaintiffs have agreed to dismiss the 18th and 19th causes of action.  The parties were unable to resolve informally the demurrers to the remaining causes of action.  The court proceeds to consider those demurrers.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿ (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)¿ When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.¿ (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)¿ “Because a demurrer challenges defects on the face of the complaint, it can only refer to matters outside the pleading that are subject to judicial notice.”¿ (Arce ex rel. Arce v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 556.)¿¿ 

 

III.       DISCUSSION

 

            Pure Eco demurs to the fourteenth cause of action for implied indemnity, fifteenth cause of action for equitable indemnity, sixteenth cause of action for contribution, seventeenth cause of action for comparative negligence, and twentieth cause of action for declaratory relief.  The court addresses these causes of action in turn.

 

            A.  Implied Indemnity

 

            Pure Eco argues the fourteenth cause of action or implied indemnity[1] fails because the SAC improperly groups “multiple subcontractors without specific facts identifying Defendant's work, acts, omissions, or causal role in any alleged loss.”  (Dem., p. 10:20-21.)  The court disagrees.  The SAC alleges “If Plaintiffs are held liable on any Cross-Complaints, such liability will be based not on any act of wrongdoing on the part of the Plaintiffs, but solely on the acts or omissions of those Defendants named herein. In particular, Plaintiffs are informed and believe and thereon allege that the damages, if any, sustained by Defendant MEHRDAD AJDARI or any party that may file a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiffs were primarily, actively, or ultimately caused by the negligent or wrongful acts or omissions of those Defendants named herein, who failed to perform their respective duties related to the Property, whereas any liability of the Plaintiffs is secondary, passive, or derivative in nature only.”  (SAC, ¶ 125.)  The court finds this is sufficient detail to apprise Pure Eco of Plaintiffs’ claims.  Any perceived deficiencies may be addressed by discovery.[2]

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the fourteenth cause of action is OVERRULED.

 

            B.  Equitable Indemnity

 

Pure Eco argues the fifteenth cause of action for equitable indemnity fails because it does not plead facts specific to Pure Eco.

 

 The elements of a cause of action for equitable indemnity are (1) a showing of fault on the part of the indemnitor and (2) resulting damages to the indemnitee for which the indemnitor is equitably responsible. (C.W. Howe Partners Inc. v. Mooradian (2019) 43 Cal.App.5th 688, 700.)

 

The court disagrees.  As discussed above, the SAC alleges that “the damages, if any, sustained by Defendant MEHRDAD AJDARI or any party that may file a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiffs were primarily, actively, or ultimately caused by the negligent or wrongful acts or omissions of those Defendants named herein, who failed to perform their respective duties related to the Property….”  (SAC, ¶ 125.)  The court found this allegation was sufficient to support the fourteenth cause of action.  It is incorporated into the fifteenth cause of action.  (SAC, ¶ 129.)  The allegation is sufficient to apprise Pure Eco of the nature of Plaintiffs’ claim.  Any perceived deficiencies may be addressed by discovery.

 

            C.  Contribution

 

The sixteenth cause of action for contribution is premature because it has not accrued. Code of Civil Procedure section 875 provides for contribution where a money judgment has been rendered jointly against two or more defendants in a tort action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 875.) “Under the California statute, the right of contribution among joint tortfeasors is contingent upon the entry of a judgment against them, and the right may be enforced only after one tortfeasor, by payment, has discharged the joint judgment or has paid more than his pro rata share thereof ….” (E. B. Wills Co. v. Superior Court (1976) 56 Cal.App.3d 650, 653.)

 

Here, Plaintiffs seek contribution from Pure Eco “[i]n the event Defendant MEHRDAD ADJARI or any other party recovers damages against Plaintiffs in this action….”  (SAC, ¶ 133.)  As the allegations make clear, there is neither a judgment nor any payment made.  The cause of action is not ripe and subject to demurrer.

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the sixteenth cause of action is SUSTAINED.  Leave to amend is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

 

            D.  Comparative Negligence

 

            Comparative negligence is not a cause of action. Rather, it is a doctrine “under which a jury properly may consider and evaluate the relative responsibility of various parties for an injury ...in order to arrive at an ‘equitable apportionment or allocation of loss.” (Agustin v. Golden Empire Transit Dist. (2025) 116 Cal.App.5th 426, 450 (cleaned up); see also Yale v. Bowne (2017) 9 Cal.App.5th 649, 659 [explaining that the system of comparative faulty is a defense which asks a different question than negligence liability].)  For this reason, this cause of action fails.

 

            Accordingly, the demurrer to the seventeenth cause of action is SUSTAINED.  Leave to amend is DENIED.

 

            E.  Declaratory Relief

 

“To qualify for declaratory relief, a party would have to demonstrate its action presented two essential elements: (1) a proper subject of declaratory relief, and (2) an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to the party’s rights or obligations.” (Jolley v. Chase Home Finance, LLC (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 872, 909, quotation marks and brackets omitted.)¿

 

A cause of action for declaratory relief should not be used as a duplicate cause of action for the determination of identical issues raised in another cause of action. (General of America Insurance Co. v. Lilly (1968) 258 Cal.App.2d 465, 470.)¿Further, “there is no basis for declaratory relief where only past wrongs are involved.”¿ (Osseous Technologies of America, Inc. v. DiscoveryOrtho Partners LLC (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 357, 366, quotation marks omitted.) Declaratory relief is “to be used in the interests of preventive justice, to declare rights rather than execute them.” (Cordoba Corp. v. City of Industry (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 145, 157, quoting Babb v. Superior Court (1971) 3 Cal.3d 841, 848.)¿ 

 

Here, the SAC alleges that “[a]n actual controversy exists between the parties concerning their respective rights, duties and obligations in that Plaintiffs contend that they are entitled to the defense and/or indemnity, contractually or equitably, totally or partially, from Defendants, and each of them, including any costs, fees, expenses or judgments, paid or incurred by these Plaintiffs on Defendants or any other party to this litigation. In addition, Plaintiffs contend that the Defendants were negligent and are subject to contribution due to the performance of the duties they undertook when they entered into to perform the services, contracts and subcontracts described in the Cross-Complaint and this Complaint. Defendants, and each of them, deny these contentions and contend the opposite. ”  (SAC, ¶ 152.) These allegations are duplicative of Plaintiffs’ other causes of action and/or otherwise describe past wrongs.  Plaintiffs do not plead a viable cause of action for declaratory relief. 

 

            Accordingly, the demurrer to the twentieth cause of action is SUSTAINED.  Leave to amend is DENIED.

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

Pure Eco’s demurrers are Overruled In Part, and Sustained In Part, as indicated herein.

 

            Defendant Pure Eco, Inc. is ordered to serve and file its Answer to the SAC within 10 days of this order.

 

Defendant to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 24STCV24999    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 14, 2026                           TRIAL DATE:  September 16, 2026

                                                          

CASE:                         Brayan Romero v. Roberto Menjivar, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV24999

 

 

DEFENDANTS’ MOTION IN LIMINE #1 TO EXCLUDE PLAINTIFF’S EXPERTS

 

I.          INTRODUCTION

           

            This is a motor vehicle negligence action.  On August 31, 2026, Defendants, Roberto Menjivar, Jennifer Cammack, and AJ Portables, Inc., filed this motion in limine to exclude Plaintiffs’ experts, Brandon Bourdage and Jonathan Eskenazi, M.D., under Code of Civil

Procedure section 2034.300 for failure to appear for a deposition prior to trial.

 

            At the time of this writing no opposition or reply had been filed.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

A motion in limine must present the specific evidence sought to be excluded. ¿“Until the evidence is actually offered, and the Court is aware of its relevance in context, its probative value, and its potential for prejudice, matters related to the state of the evidence at the time an objection is made, the Court cannot intelligently rule on admissibility.” ¿(Kelly v. New West Federal Savings (1996) 49 Cal.App.4th 659, 671.) ¿Instead of a motion in limine, the proper procedure is to make an objection at the time the evidence is offered, which serves to focus the issue and to protect the record. ¿(Id.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Accordingly, a motion in limine must specify the evidence, clearly state the exclusion, and explain why it applies.¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Further, Local Rule 3.57(a) requires that motions made for the purpose of precluding the mention or display of inadmissible and prejudicial matter in the presence of the jury must be accompanied by a declaration that includes the following:¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 
1. Specific identification of the matter alleged to be inadmissible and prejudicial;¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 
2. A representation to the court that the subject of the motion has been discussed¿¿with opposing counsel, and that opposing counsel has either indicated that such matter will be mentioned or displayed in the presence of the jury before it is admitted in evidence or that counsel has refused to stipulate that such matter will not be mentioned or displayed in the presence of the jury unless and until it is admitted in evidence;¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

3. A statement of the specific prejudice that will be suffered by the moving party if the motion is not granted; and¿¿¿ 

   
4. If the motion seeks to make binding an answer given in response to discovery,  the declaration must set forth the question and the answer and state why the use of the answer for impeachment will not adequately protect the moving party against prejudice in the event that evidence inconsistent with the answer is offered.    

 

III.       DISCUSSION

           

            Here, the parties made timely expert designation exchanges on August 10, 2026.  (Grandy Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. B.) As relevant here, Plaintiff designated neurologist Jonathan Eskenazi, M.D., and biomechanical engineer Brandon Bourdage.  (Grandy Decl., Ex. C.)  On August 13, 2026, Defendants noticed the depositions of Plaintiff’s experts for September 1 and 2, 2026.  Plaintiff served timely written objections to the depositions notices and stated, without providing alternative deposition dates, that his experts would not be produced for deposition on the noticed dates.  Defense counsel requested deposition dates.  After Plaintiff’s counsel did not respond, Defendants re-set the depositions for September 4, 2026 and sent notice of the same on August 26, 2026. 

 

“Any party may demand a mutual and simultaneous exchange by all parties of a list containing the name and address of any natural person, including one who is a party, whose oral or deposition testimony in the form of an expert opinion any party expects to offer in evidence at the trial.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.210(a).)¿“The specified date of exchange shall be 50 days before the initial trial date, or 20 days after service of the demand, whichever is closer to the trial date, unless the court, on motion and a showing of good cause, orders an earlier or later date of exchange.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.230(b).)  The exchange of expert witness information shall include either of the following: (1) a list setting forth the name and address of a person whose expert opinion that party expects to offer in evidence at the trial, or (2) a statement that the party does not presently intend to offer the testimony of an expert witness.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.260(b).)¿  

 

On objection of any party who has made a complete and timely compliance with Section 2034.260, the trial court shall exclude from evidence the expert opinion of any witness that is offered by any party who has unreasonably failed to make that expert available for a deposition, among other things.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.300(d).)¿ “Failure to comply with expert designation rules may be found to be ‘unreasonable’ when a party’s conduct gives the appearance of gamesmanship.”  (Staub v. Kiley (2014) 226 Cal.App.4th 1437, 1447.)¿ 

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

            Based on the foregoing, the motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is precluded from presenting at trial the testimony of his experts Jonathan Eskenazi, M.D., and Brandon Bourdage.

 

Moving party to give notice.
