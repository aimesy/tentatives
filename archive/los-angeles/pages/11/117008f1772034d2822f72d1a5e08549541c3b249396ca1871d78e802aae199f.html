DEPARTMENT SED LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22STCV29966    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: SED

DONALD JONES V. CITY OF LOS ANGELES, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  22STCV29966

HEARING:  07/10/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#12

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ unopposed motion to deem requests for admission admitted against Plaintiff Donald Jones is GRANTED.

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ unopposed motions to compel Plaintiff Donald Jones' responses is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Donald Jones is ordered to submit verified responses, without objections, within fifteen (15) days of notice of this Order.

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ request for sanctions is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles (Defendant) move for an order (1) deeming requests for admission admitted against Plaintiff Donald Jones (Plaintiff); and (2) compelling Plaintiff’s responses to Defendant’s Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Special Interrogatories, and Form Interrogatories.  Defendant also seeks monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,250.00.

 

Legal Standard

 

          Requests for Admission

 

“Any party may obtain discovery . . . by a written request that any other party to the action admit the genuineness of specified documents, or the truth of specified matters of fact, opinion relating to fact, or application of law to fact.  A request for admission may relate to a matter that is in controversy between the parties.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.010.)  “Within 30 days after service of requests for admission, the party to whom the requests are directed shall serve the original of the response to them on the requesting party, and a copy of the response on all other parties who have appeared . . . .”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250(a).)

 

If a party to whom request for admissions are served fails to provide a timely response, the party to whom the request was directed waives any objections, including based on privilege or the work product doctrine.   (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(a).)  The requesting party can move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the request be deemed admitted, as well as for monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(b).)  The court shall issue this order unless the party to whom the request was made serves a response in substantial compliance prior to the hearing on the motion.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(c).)

 

Requests for Production of Documents

 

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).)  If a party to whom requests for production of documents is directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling response to the discovery.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)  The party also waives the right to make any objections, including ones based on privilege or work-product protection.  (Code Civ. Proc., §¿2031.300, subd. (a).) 

 

          Interrogatories

 

When a party fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories, the party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling a response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).)  A party who fails to provide a timely response waives any objection, including one based on privilege or work product. (Id., § 2030.290, subd. (a).)

 

          Sanctions

 

If the motion to compel is granted and the moving party properly requests monetary sanctions, the court “shall” impose sanctions against the party to whom the discovery was directed, unless it finds that the sanctioned party acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (c) [interrogatories]; 2031.300, subd. (c) [demands for inspection].)

 

In contrast, with respect to requests for admissions, monetary sanctions are mandatory against the party, the attorney, or both whose failure to serve a timely response necessitated the motion to deem the matters admitted, and the statute does not provide an exception for substantial justification.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1348, subd. (a).)

 

Analysis

 

On April 1, 2025, Defendant served Plaintiff with Set One of Requests for Admissions, Requests for Production of Documents, Special Interrogatories, and Form Interrogatories.  (Valadez Decls., ¶ 2.)  Defendant represents that, as of the filing of the instant motions, Plaintiff had failed to serve verified, code-compliant responses.  (Id. at ¶ 3.)

 

Plaintiff's responses were due on or before May 16, 2025.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.260, subd. (a), 2031.260, subd. (a), & 2033.250, subd. (a).)  Plaintiff failed to timely serve verified responses, thereby waiving any objections to the discovery requests.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (a), 2031.300, subd. (a), & 2033.280, subd. (a).)

 

Plaintiff has not filed an opposition or otherwise disputed Defendant's showing that no timely verified responses were served.

 

Accordingly, Defendant's motions are GRANTED.

 

          Sanctions

 

Defendant requests monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and his counsel, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,250.00, based on 6.0 hours of attorney time at an hourly rate of $250.00.  Counsel represents that this includes 2.0 hours preparing each of the three motions, and an additional 3.0 hours to prepare replies and appear at the hearing (1.0 hour per motion).  (Valadez Decls., ¶ 4.)

 

Here, as discussed above, the Court grants Defendant's motions to compel.  Accordingly, monetary sanctions are warranted.  In addition, with respect to the requests for admissions, monetary sanctions are mandatory where a party's failure to serve timely responses necessitates a motion to deem the matters admitted, absent a showing of substantial justification or other circumstances making the imposition of sanctions unjust.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

 

However, given the straightforward nature of motions to compel initial responses and to deem requests for admissions admitted, the Court finds that 6.0 total hours (consisting of 1.0 hour to prepare each of the three motions and 1.0 hour to appear at the hearing on each motion) constitutes a reasonable amount of attorney time.  The Court excludes the requested 3.0 hours for preparing replies because no replies were filed.

 

Accordingly, Defendant's request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,500.00.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant City of Los Angeles’ unopposed motion to deem requests for admission admitted against Plaintiff Donald Jones is GRANTED.

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ unopposed motions to compel Plaintiff Donald Jones’ responses is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff Donald Jones is ordered to submit verified responses, without objections, within fifteen (15) days of notice of this Order.

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ request for sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,500.00.

 

 


Case Number: 25NWCV02837    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: SED

ANTHONY DAVILA CARRION, ET AL. V. J&P PLUMBING, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV02837

HEARING:  07/10/2026 @ 9:30 a.m.

 

#14

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Mark David Phillips’s motion for leave to file cross-complaint is GRANTED.

 

Defendant Mark David Phillips to separately file their proposed cross-complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Mark David Phillips (Phillips) moves for an order granting leave to file a cross-complaint against Defendants J&P Plumbing, Inc. and Yasmin Jovel Martinez Escobar (collectively, Defendants). 

 

Background

 

On August 7, 2025, Plaintiffs Anthony Davila Carrion and Yareli Yepe (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed the operative complaint against Phillips, Defendants, and Does 1 through 50, inclusive, asserting two causes of action: (1) Motor Vehicle Negligence and (2) General Negligence.

 

On August 29, 2025, Phillips filed his answer.

 

On October 9, 2025, Defendants filed their answer.

 

On June 9, 2026, Phillips filed the instant motion for leave to file a cross-complaint.

 

On June 26, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a notice of non-opposition.

 

On June 29, 2026, Defendants filed an untimely opposition. 

 

On July 2, 2026, Phillips filed his reply.

 

Timeliness

 

Defendants’ opposition was due on or before June 26, 2026, but was not filed until June 29, 2026.  Accordingly, the opposition is untimely.  However, Phillips does not object to the untimely filing or otherwise raise the issue in the reply.  Under these circumstances, the Court exercises its discretion to consider the opposition in the interests of resolving the matter on the merits.  Defendants are admonished to comply with applicable filing deadlines in the future.

 

Legal Standard

 

The court may grant leave to file a cross-complaint if the failure to plead a cause of action was the result of oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50.)  Where the proposed cross-complaint arises out of the same transaction as plaintiff’s claim, the court must grant leave to file the cross-complaint as long as defendant is acting in good faith.  (Ibid.; see Silver Organizations Ltd. v. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 99 (Silver Organizations) [the Court may not “exercise discretion” in the denial of a motion to file a compulsory cross-complaint].)  Judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters between the parties in the same lawsuit and discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendment of the pleadings.  (See Nestle v. Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 929.)¿

 

“‘Bad faith,’ is defined as ‘[t]he opposite of “good faith,” generally implying or involving actual or constructive fraud, or a design to mislead or deceive another, or a neglect or refusal to fulfill some duty or some contractual obligation, not prompted by an honest mistake ..., but by some interested or sinister motive[,] ... not simply bad judgment or negligence, but rather ... the conscious doing of a wrong because of dishonest purpose or moral obliquity; ... it contemplates a state of mind affirmatively operating with furtive design or ill will.  [Citation.]’  [Citations.]”  (Silver Organizations, supra, 217 Cal.App.3d at 100.) 

 

A party may obtain leave of court to file a cross-complaint at any time during the course of a lawsuit, and leave may be granted in the interest of justice.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 426.50, 428.50; see also Sidney v. Superior Court (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 710, 718.)  “To the extent practicable, all supporting memorandums and declarations must be attached to the notice of motion.”  (Cal. Rules of Court Rule 3.1113(j).)¿

 

Analysis

 

Phillips moves for leave to file a Cross-Complaint against Defendants asserting causes of action for (1) Comparative Indemnity, (2) Contribution, and (3) Declaratory Relief.

 

The proposed cross-complaint is compulsory because it arises out of the same transaction or occurrence as Plaintiffs' complaint.  Specifically, the November 19, 2024 motor vehicle accident underlying this action.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 426.10, 426.30.)  As the court explained in Sattinger v. Newbauer (1954) 123 Cal.App.2d 365, 369, "[t]he fundamental reason for allowing cross-complaints is to have a complete determination of a controversy among the parties in the one action, thus avoiding circuity of action."

 

Phillips contends that, during Plaintiff Yareli Yepez Maximi's May 1, 2026 deposition, Plaintiff Yepez testified for the first time that she experienced two separate impacts, attributing the first collision to Defendant Yasmin Jovel Martinez Escobar.  (Sadaka Decl., ¶ 10.)  According to Phillips, this testimony provided a factual basis to allege that Defendant Escobar may bear responsibility for some or all of Plaintiffs' alleged damages.  (Motion p. 3.)  Accordingly, the proposed cross claims arise from the same occurrence as Plaintiffs' claims and are logically related thereto.

 

Phillips further represents that his failure to file the cross-complaint earlier resulted from a lack of sufficient information regarding the allocation of fault at the time he filed his Answer on August 29, 2025.  (Sadaka Decl., ¶¶ 8-11.)  Following Plaintiff Yepez's deposition, Phillips states that he promptly prepared the proposed cross-complaint and the instant motion.  (Id. at ¶ 11.)

 

In opposition, Defendants argue that Phillips was never ignorant of the underlying facts because the traffic collision report and Plaintiffs' Complaint identified the parties involved in the accident.  (Opp., p. 2; Grandy Decl., ¶¶ 2-3, Exs. A, B.)  Defendants therefore contend that Phillips cannot rely on mistake or inadvertence to justify the delayed filing.  (Opp., p. 3.) 

 

In reply, Phillips argues that Defendants have failed to make the required strong showing of bad faith or demonstrate any resulting prejudice.  (Reply, p. 3.)  Phillips also notes that neither the Complaint nor the CHP report distinguished between the separate impacts or identified which collision allegedly caused Plaintiffs' injuries.  (Ibid.)  Rather, Plaintiff Yepez's deposition testimony allegedly provided new information regarding the sequence and force of the impacts, including differing body movement and force associated with each collision.  (Ibid.)  Phillips further argues that permitting the cross-complaint will not prejudice Defendants because it arises out of the same accident and will not affect the April 26, 2027 trial date.  (Id. at p. 4.)

 

The Court agrees.  Nothing before the Court demonstrates that Phillips acted in bad faith, with fraud, deceit, or an improper motive in seeking leave to file the proposed compulsory cross-complaint.  (See Silver Organizations, supra, 217 Cal.App.3d at 99-100.)  Although Defendants dispute whether Plaintiff Yepez's deposition revealed any new information, the Court is not persuaded that Phillips' explanation reflects bad faith.  Rather, it appears that Phillips did not believe there was a sufficient factual basis to assert comparative fault claims until after Plaintiff Yepez's deposition.  Moreover, Defendants have not demonstrated any prejudice resulting from the requested amendment, particularly where trial is not scheduled until April 26, 2027.

 

Accordingly, Phillips' motion for leave to file the proposed Cross-Complaint is GRANTED.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Mark David Phillips’s motion for leave to file cross-complaint is GRANTED.

 

Defendant Mark David Phillips to separately file their proposed cross-complaint within 7 days of this ruling.
