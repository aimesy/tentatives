24STCP02382

QUINN EMANUEL URAUHART & SULLIVAN. LLP vs USTOCKTRADE SECURITIES, INC.

 

July 6, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: MOTION BY PETITIONER TO FILE UNDER SEAL.

 

RULING:

The Court orders the Motion off calendar, due to substantial notice and procedure defects specified herein.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On July 29, 2024, QUINN EMANUEL URQUHART & SULLIVAN, LLP (Petitioner) filed a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award, against USTOCKTRADE SECURITIES, INC. (Respondent).

On June 18, 2025, Petitioner filed another Petition.

On January 22, 2026, the Clerk provided a notice of rejection of Entry of Default.

On May 28, 2026, Petitioner filed a First Amended Petition.

Petitioner alleges that on May 29, 2024, an Arbitration Award was made requiring Respondent to pay $792,635.33 for Petitioner’s fees and costs for legal services incurred in underlying cases. Petitioner also seeks attorneys’ fees in the sum of $18,447.00, $435 in costs, and ten percent post-judgment interest.

On May 28, 2026, Petitioner filed the unopposed Motion to Seal redacted portions of the Declaration of Richard H. Doss, Esq. filed in support of the First Amended Petition to Confirm Contractual Arbitration Award and Exhibits A and B, reportedly lodged conditionally under seal on May 28, 2026.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

“A document which is protected by the lawyer-client privilege is not subject to the rule 243.1 et seq. findings requirements.” (Huffy Corp. v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 97, 108 [citing, e.g., Cal. Rules of Court, rule 243.1(a)(2), since renumbered 2.550(a)(2)], abrogation on other grounds recognized by Curtis v. Superior Court (2021) 62 Cal.App.5th 453, 471.)

“[A] confidentiality provision in an arbitration agreement is not per se unconscionable when it is based on a legitimate commercial need (such as to protect trade secrets or proprietary information).” (Hasty v. Am. Auto. Assn. etc. (2023) 98 Cal.App.5th 1041, 1061–1062.)

Generally, courts must find a compelling, overriding interest, prejudice absent sealing, and the lack of less-restrictive means, before ordering filed documents sealed. (McGuan v. Endovascular Technologies, Inc. (2010) 182 Cal.App.4th 974, 988 [“the protection of trade secrets is an interest that can support sealing records ….”]; Hurvitz v. Hoefflin (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 1232, 1246; NBC Subsidiary (KNBC-TV), Inc. v. Superior Court (1999) 20 Cal.4th 1178, 1208-1209, fn. 25; Champion v. Superior Court (1988) 201 Cal.App.3d 777, 787; In re Providian Credit Card Cases (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 292, 310 [trial court decision that defendants had showed no overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access, was not abuse of discretion]; Universal City Studios v. Superior Court (2003) 110 Cal.App.4th 1273, 1283-1284 [a confidential settlement or other confidentiality agreement cannot qualify for sealing absent a showing of a substantial probability that the party would be prejudiced such that it constitutes an overriding interest].)

“All electronic records must be made reasonably available to the public in some form, whether in electronic or in paper form, except those that are sealed by court order or made confidential by law. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.503.) (Underscoring added.) “(1) If the sealed record is in electronic form, the clerk must file the court's order, maintain the record ordered sealed in a secure manner, and clearly identify the record as sealed by court order on a specified date. [¶] (2)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

The Motion is made on the grounds that confidential documents contain information the parties designated as “Confidential,” including attorney client, privileged material and arbitration content, and the request satisfied requirements including of California Rule of Court 2.550.

 

A.    Notice

“A copy of the motion or application must be served on all parties that have appeared in the case. (CRC, rule 2.551(b)(2).) A proof of service of moving documents must be filed no later than five court days before the hearing. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(c).) Where a moving party failed to provide sufficient notice of a hearing in accordance with California Code of Civil Procedure section 1005, the resultant order is void, absent an order shortening time. (Svistunoff v. Svistunoff (1952) 108 Cal.App.2d 638, 641.) Due-process principles require notice and an opportunity to be heard as to those having an interest in a matter. (Monarch Healthcare v. Superior Court (2000) 78 Cal.App.4th 1282, 1286.) “If a judge receives an unauthorized ex parte communication bearing upon the substance of a matter, the judge shall make provision promptly to notify the parties of the substance of the communication and provide the parties with an opportunity to respond.” (Code of Judicial Ethics, Canon 3B(7)(d).)

“‘The clerk's entry of default cuts off the defendant's right to take further affirmative steps such as filing a pleading or motion, and the defendant is not entitled to notices or service of pleadings or papers.’ ” (Sporn v. Home Depot USA, Inc. (2005) 126 Cal. App. 4th 1294, 1301. Accord, Dakota Payphone, LLC v. Alcaraz (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 493, 502 (“upon entry of default subsequent filing of pleadings by defendant is unauthorized and void….”); Sass v. Cohen (2020) 10 Cal.5th 861, 882.)

“The deadline for filing a petition to confirm an arbitration award is four years (from the date the petitioner was served with the award). (§ 1288.)…. A party may seek an order vacating or correcting an award in a standalone petition (§ 1285), and the default deadline for filing such a petition is 100 days (from the date the petitioner was served with the award). (§§ 1288, 1288.2.) A party may also seek an order vacating or correcting an award in its response to a prior-filed petition to confirm that award (§ 1285.2), and the default deadline for filing that response is 10 days (from the date the responding party is served with the petition to confirm). (§ 1290.6.).” (Darby v. Sisyphian, LLC (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 1100, 1110, disapproved on other grounds by Law Finance Group, LLC v. Key (2023) 14 Cal.5th 932, 952, fn.3.)

The Proposed Order, received May 28, 2026, states: “all parties having been given notice and an opportunity to be heard….”

Here, the Court can find no Proof of Service of notice of the Motion hearing given to Respondent. Because default has not been entered yet, such notice is required.

 

B.     Unredacted Filing

One prescribed procedure is for parties (1) to file a public redacted version of a document, and (2) to lodge a complete version conditionally under seal. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551(b)(5). Cf. Garcia v. Superior Court (2007) 4 Cal.4th 63, 73.) “[W]hen the court denies or denies in part the request to seal, and the submitting party elects not to have its provisionally sealed documents become a part of the record, the court must return the lodged documents as required by rule 2.551(b)(6).” (Regents of the Univ. of Cal. v. Superior Court (2013) 222 Cal.App.4th 383, 409.)

Petitioner has filed redacted documentation to be sealed and offered to make a true and correct copy available for an in camera review. (Richard H. Doss Declaration, filed 5/28/26, Exhibit 1.)

But the same documents have been separately filed unredacted and unsealed, the same date. Such documents should have been lodged provisionally under seal.

Moreover, filing such unredacted documentation has happened before, and already resulted in prior sealing Orders. (See Richard H. Doss Declaration, filed 8/6/24, ¶ 7 (“the unredacted confidential version of the Doss Decl. ISO Petition was inadvertently electronically filed with the motion.”); Minutes entered 11/20/24 (granting Motion to Seal); Minutes entered 8/9/24 (“Ex Parte Application For An Order To Remove Confidential Information From The Public Record is GRANTED.”).)

 

C.     Proposed Order

The Proposed Order received on May 28, 2026, incorrectly states the documents were lodged provisionally under seal, and lacks elements required. The order must state whether--in addition to the sealed records--the order itself, the register of actions, any other court records, or any other records relating to the case are to be sealed. [¶] (3) The order must state whether any person other than the court is authorized to inspect the sealed record.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551(e)(1)-(3).)

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court orders the matter off calendar, due to notice and procedural defects set forth above.

LADWAN THOMPSON vs MENS HEALTH FOUNDATION, A CALIFORNIA CORPORATION, et al.

July 6, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: MOTION OF LIPELES LAW GROUP, APC TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL-CIVIL.

 

RULING:

The Court continues the Motion hearing to July 10, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Department 311, for a Declaration completed as to confirming the client’s address.

 

ANALYSIS:

By the Motion, filed June 8, 2026, counsel moves to be relieved as attorney of record for Plaintiff LADWAN THOMPSON, by declaring, in the form Declaration, at paragraph 2, that counsel has lost contact with the client.

Here, the form notice, proposed order, and proof of service sufficiently comply with the requirements for a motion to be relieved as counsel. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)

Moving counsel’s declaration does show cognizable grounds for withdrawal:

  • A breakdown in the attorney-client relationship (or personality clash). (Estate of Falco v. Decker (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004, 1014 (Falco).)
  • The client by conduct renders it unreasonably difficult for the member to carry out the employment effectively (e.g., noncooperation or noncommunication). (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4). Cf. Falco, supra, 188 Cal App.3d at p. 1020 [“We find no abuse of discretion in the trial court's implicit finding rejecting the contention that respondents' lack of cooperation justified appellants' withdrawal.”].)

No opposing document is filed in order to show any prejudice caused by attorney withdrawal. (See Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(d); Vann v. Shilleh (1975) 54 Cal.App.3d 192, 197.)

However, the Declaration, at paragraph 3(b)(1)(d), fails to specify how counsel confirmed the client’s last known address.

The Court continues the Motion for an amended Declaration specifying how Plaintiff’s address was confirmed, such that there is an indicator that Plaintiff would receive important mailings.

If the client’s address remains unknown, then Code of Civil Procedure section 1011, subdivision (b) requirements can be satisfied by serving the Court Clerk. (CRC Rules 3.1362(d) and 3.252(a).)

At this time, the Court does not complete, sign and file the Form Order received-stamped on June 8, 2026.

25STCV22236

CENTURY CITY MALL, LLC, A DELAWARE LIMITED LIABILITY COMPANY vs DIGITAL BRANDS GROUP, INC., A DELAWARE CORPORATION, et al.

July 6, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH DEFENDANT’S AGREEMENT TO PRODUCE DOCUMENTS TO FIRST SET OF REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS FROM DEFENDANT AND WITH CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE SECTION 2031.280.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the unopposed Motion.

On or before July 10, 2026, Defendant DIGITAL BRANDS GROUP, INC. shall produce documents, without objections, and in full compliance with Defendant’s agreement to produce documents to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents numbers 1–11, 13–25, 27–35, and 38–41, and pursuant to the California Discovery Act (Code Civ. Proc. § 2016.010 et seq.). “Any documents or category of documents produced in response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling shall be identified with the specific request number to which the documents respond.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.280, subd. (a).)

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On July 29, 2025, CENTURY CITY MALL, LLC (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against DIGITAL BRANDS GROUP, INC (Defendant), “FOR ALTER EGO ACTION ON JUDGMENT.”

Plaintiff alleges that it seeks to add Defendant as an alter ego to a Judgment against Plaintiff’s former tenant Bailey 44, LLC, for over $1.4 million.

On June 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion to compel Defendant to produce all documents as agreed, and Plaintiff reports that Defendant has not produced all documents it agreed to.

Defendant has filed no Opposition to this matter.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

A motion to compel compliance as agreed with a request for production of documents is based upon a showing that the respondents failed “to permit an inspection ‘in accordance with that party's statement of compliance.’” (Standon Co. v. Superior Court (1990) 225 Cal.App.3d 898, 903. See also Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.320, subd. (a).) A separate motion to compel production of documents as agreed in responses, cannot be substituted for a motion to compel further responses. (Ibid.)

Where respondents served untimely discovery responses after parties have filed motions to compel initial responses, courts have broad discretion as to ruling, including: 1) denying the motion as moot, in whole or part, where valid responses without objections have resolved the motion; 2) awarding requested sanctions; 3) allowing moving party to take the motion off calendar; 4) considering the motion as voluntarily narrowed in scope; 5) compelling responses without objection, where no legally valid responses have been provided, as to some, or all, interrogatories; 6) treating the motion as one to compel further responses, and ruling accordingly, with, or without, a separate statement; 7) ordering the parties to meet and confer; 8) ordering moving party to file a separate statement; or, 9) ordering the motion off calendar while requiring the propounding party to file a motion to compel further responses. (Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Klugman (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 409; County of San Benito v. Superior Court (2023) 96 Cal.App.5th 243, 256 [citing Sinaiko regarding court considering supplemental responses served after motion].) Under some circumstances, it may be an abuse of discretion for a court to treat a motion as being one to compel further responses without requiring a separate motion. (St. Mary v. Superior Court (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 762, 778 [addressing motion to deem admissions admitted].)

The failure to file a proper and timely opposition in trial court creates a waiver of the issues on any appeal. (Bell v. Am. Title Ins. Co. (1991) 226 Cal.App.3d 1589, 1602; Cabrini Villas Homeowners Assn. v. Haghverdian (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 683, 693 [appellate court will not consider any erroneous rulings where an objection could have been made].)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiff states that Defendant has produced additional documents after the Motion’s filing, but nevertheless documents including the following have not been produced:

·         Bailey’s entity formation documents (RFP 5), corporate minute books (RFP 9), meeting minutes (RFP 10), and continuing resolutions or official actions (RFP 11);

·         Agreements between Bailey and Defendant (RFP 6);

·         Bailey’s audited financial statements from 2020 to the present (RFP 13);1

·         Balance sheets that reflect the judgment against Bailey in the Bailey action (RFP 18);

·         Documents related to the transfer of any assets valued at $500 or more from March 2020 to the present or cash over $500 from July 29, 2024 to the present to or from Bailey (RFP 24 & 25);

·         Negotiations of any lease agreements for Bailey retail stores from September 2023 (RFP 30);

·         Communications between Bailey and Defendant about the Bailey action (RFP 32); and

·         Communications about the Bailey judgment (RFP 33 & 34).

(Reply, p. 3.)

“If the party fails to make a production in accordance with its statement of compliance, the opposing party may file another motion to compel seeking compliance.” (Bd. of Registered Nursing v. Superior Court (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 1011, 1033 [citing Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.320].)

Notwithstanding supplemental productions, this Motion is not moot, given Defendant’s failure to produce some documents as was agreed in discovery responses.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court grants the unopposed Motion, as set forth above.
