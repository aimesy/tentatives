Michelle Espinosa v. FCA US, LLC, et al.

 

Tuesday, July 7, 2026

 

 

 

CASE NUMBER: 24STCV28928

 

UNOPPOSED

___________________________________________________________________

 

Defendant FCA US, LLC’s Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production from Plaintiffs Michelle Espinosa and Andres Espinosa ___________________________________________________________________

 

Facts: This is a lemon law action. The Complaint alleges as follows. Plaintiffs Michelle Espinosa and Andres Espinosa (“Plaintiffs”) purchased a 2020 Dodge Durango in December 2019, with express written warranties. (Complaint ¶ 10.) The vehicle contained or developed defects that the manufacturer has been unable to repair. (Complaint ¶¶ 15–31.)

Procedural History: Plaintiff filed the Complaint on November 4, 2024, alleging six causses of action:

1.      Civ. Code § 1793.2, subd. (d)

2.      Civ. Code § 1793.2, subd. (b)

3.      Civ. Code § 1793.2, subd. (a)(3)

4.      Breach of Implied Warranty of Merchantability

5.      Negligent Repair

6.      Fraudulent Concealment

Defendant FCA US filed the present motion to compel on May 21, 2026. No opposition has been filed.

Analysis

 

I.                   MOTION TO COMPEL

A propounding party may demand a responding party to produce documents that are in their possession, custody or control. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010.) A party may likewise conduct discovery by propounding interrogatories to another party to be answered under oath. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.010, subd. (a).) The responding party must respond to the production demand either by complying, by representing that the party lacks the ability to comply, or by objecting to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210.) The responding party must respond to the interrogatories by answering or objecting. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.210, subd. (a).) If the responding party fails to serve timely responses, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the production demand and interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.)

 

A party who fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories or a demand for inspection waives any objection to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.)

Defendant FCA US, LLC (“Defendant”) moves to compel responses to requests for production from Plaintiffs Michelle Espinosa and Andres Espinosa (“Plaintiffs”). The requests were served on November 12, 2025, and Plaintiffs served responses without verification on December 29, 2025. (Hughes Decl. ¶¶ 4–5.) Plaintiffs have not served verifications despite the efforts of Defendant’s counsel to meet and confer. (Hughes Decl. ¶¶ 6–8, Exh. C.)

Responses without verification are tantamount to no responses at all. (Motion at p. 6, citing Appleton v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 632, 635–36.) Defendant has served discovery, and Plaintiffs’ responses consist of more than mere objections, but attestations of compliance that remain unverified. (Hughes Decl. Exh. B.) Plaintiffs have filed no opposition to the present motion.

The motion is therefore GRANTED. Verifications shall be served within 20 days of this order.

1.      Sanctions

The prevailing party on a motion to compel is generally entitled to monetary sanctions, unless the court “finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Sanctions are also mandatory against a party whose failure to serve responses to requests for admission makes the motion necessary. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

 

Defendant seeks $2,260.00 in sanctions representing 5.5 hours of attorney work at $400 per hour. (Hughes Decl. ¶¶ 9–10.) This includes one hour of work responding to an opposition that was never filed, and $400 are thus properly deducted from the sanctions lodestar.

 

Thus sanctions are awarded against Plaintiffs and their counsel in the amount of $1,860.00, to be paid within 20 days of this order.

Itria Ventures, LLC v. Helen Anne residential Care LLC, et al.

 

Tuesday, July 7, 2026

 

 

 

CASE NUMBER: 25STCV21622

 

UNOPPOSED

___________________________________________________________________

 

Plaintiff Itria Investments LLC’s Motion to Enforce Settlement and Enter Judgment against Defendants Helen-Anne Residential Care, LLC, Fort Faith Home Care, LLC, Forth Faith LLC, Fort Faith SLS, LLC, and Nisha Fort ___________________________________________________________________

 

Facts: This is an action for breach of contract. The Complaint alleges as follows. Plaintiff Itria Investments, LLC (“Plaintiff”) signed agreements with Defendants Helen-Anne Residential Care, LLC, Fort Faith Home Care, LLC, Forth Faith LLC, Fort Faith SLS, LLC, guaranteed by Defendant Nisha Fort (“Defendants”) to pay Defendants two lump sums of $50,000 and $15,000 in exchange for a portion of Defendants’ future accounts receivable, a lien on their assets, and regular weekly payments. (Complaint ¶¶ 13–23.) Defendants have failed to make the payments required. (Complaint ¶¶ 24–39.)

Procedural History:  Plaintiff filed the Complaint on July 23, 2025, alleging

1.      Appointment of a Receiver

2.      Preliminary Injunction

3.      Breach of Written Contract

4.      Breach of Written Contract

5.      Breach of Written Contract

6.      Breach of Written Contract

A notice of settlement was filed on October 1, 2025.

The matter was dismissed on October 15, 2025.

Plaintiff filed the present motion to enforce settlement on May 7, 2026. No opposition has been filed.

Analysis:

I.       MOTION TO ENFORCE SETTLEMENT

Code Civ. Proc. section 664.6 states that:

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

“Section 664.6 permits the trial court judge to enter judgment on a settlement agreement without the need for a new lawsuit. [Citation.] It is for the trial court to determine in the first instance whether the parties have entered into an enforceable settlement. [Citation.] In making that determination, ‘the trial court acts as the trier of fact, determining whether the parties entered into a valid and binding settlement. [Citation.] Trial judges may consider oral testimony or may determine the motion upon declarations alone. [Citation.] When the same judge hears the settlement and the motion to enter judgment on the settlement, he or she may consult his [or her] memory. [Citation.]’ [Citation.]” (Osumi v. Sutton (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 1355, 1359–1360.)

Plaintiff Itria Ventures, LLC (“Plaintiff”) moves to enforce settlement and enter judgment against Defendants Helen-Anne Residential Care, LLC, Fort Faith Home Care, LLC, Forth Faith LLC, Fort Faith SLS, LLC, and Nisha Fort (“Defendant”). Based on a settlement agreement executed in August 2025. (Smalbach Decl. ¶ 9, Exh. 1.) Per this agreement, Defendants agreed to pay $74,901.87, comprised of biweekly payments of $2,728.86 beginning on August 22, 2025. (Smalbach Decl. ¶ 10, Exh. 1.) In September 2025 the parties agreed to modify the payment schedule to monthly payments of $5,457.72. (Smalbach Decl. Exh. 4.) Defendants have since then defaulted on these payment obligations, leaving a balance owed of $50,262.02.(Smalbach Decl. ¶¶ 15–17.) 

The settlement agreement contains a clause permitting entry of stipulated judgment for amounts owed in the event of default pursuant to Code of Civil procedure § 664.6, as well as attorney fees and prejudgment interest at a rate of 10%. (Smalbach Decl. Exh. 1, ¶¶ 2, 8, 9.) This matter was dismissed without prejudice with an express proviso for retained jurisdiction to enforce the settlement. (See 10/13/2025 Request for Dismissal.)

Plaintiff has demonstrated entitlement to entry of judgment in the amount of $50,262.02, plus $2,781.54 in prejudgment interest dating from a December 18, 2025, attorney fees in the amount of $6,220.00 (for 23.1 hours of attorney and paralegal work associated with the present motion at rates of $200, $275, and $300), plus $60 in costs, for a total judgment of $59,323.56. (Richards Decl. ¶¶ 2–4.)  No opposition to the motion has been filed.

The motion is therefore GRANTED.

Mayra C. Morales et al. v. Henry Aldubi Mendez, et al.

 

Tuesday, July 7, 2026

 

 

 

CASE NUMBER: 25STCV27696

 

UNOPPOSED

___________________________________________________________________

 

Defendant Henry Aldubi Mendez’s Motions to Compel Responses to Form and Special Interrogatories and Requests for Production from Plaintiff Mayra C. Morales ___________________________________________________________________

 

Facts: This is an action for motor vehicle negligence. The Complaint alleges as follows. Plaintiffs Mayra C. Morales, Michael T. Alvarez, Uriel G. Ramirez, and Emily E. Alvarez (“Plaintiffs”0 were injured in a motor vehicle collision on September 23, 2023, at the intersection of Avenue 21 and N. Broadway in Los Angeles, by the negligent operation of a motor vehicle by Defendant Henry Aldubi Mendez (“Defendant”). (Complaint ¶¶ 7–9.)

Procedural History: Plaintiffs filed the Complaint on September 22, 2025, alleging one cause of action for motor vehicle negligence.

Defendant filed a cross-complaint on January 5, 2026, alleging four causes of action:

1.      Indemnification

2.      Declaratory Relief – Apportionment of Fault

3.      Declaratory Relief – Full Indemnity

4.      Contribution

Defendant filed the present motions to compel on June 2, 2026. No opposition has been filed.

Analysis

 

I.                   MOTION TO COMPEL

A propounding party may demand a responding party to produce documents that are in their possession, custody or control. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010.) A party may likewise conduct discovery by propounding interrogatories to another party to be answered under oath. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.010, subd. (a).) The responding party must respond to the production demand either by complying, by representing that the party lacks the ability to comply, or by objecting to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210.) The responding party must respond to the interrogatories by answering or objecting. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.210, subd. (a).) If the responding party fails to serve timely responses, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the production demand and interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.)

 

A party who fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories or a demand for inspection waives any objection to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.)

Defendant Henry Aldubi Mendez (“Defendant”) moves to compel responses to form and special interrogatories and requests for production from Plaintiff Mayra C. Morales (“Plaintiff”). Defendant served the discovery at issue on January 8, 2026, and re-served the same discovery on February 13, 2026, following conference with Plaintiff’s counsel. (Jensen Decl. ¶¶ 2–7.) Responses were due by March 20, 2026. (Jensen Decl. ¶ 11.) No responses have been served despite efforts to reach out to Plaintiff’s counsel by Defendant’s counsel. (Jensen Decl. ¶¶ 14–18.)

Defendant has shown that Plaintiff has not served timely responses to discovery. No opposition to the motions has been filed.

The motions are therefore GRANTED. Responses without objection shall be served within 20 days of this order.

1.      Sanctions

The prevailing party on a motion to compel is generally entitled to monetary sanctions, unless the court “finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Sanctions are also mandatory against a party whose failure to serve responses to requests for admission makes the motion necessary. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

 

Defendant seeks a total of $2,556.00 in sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel, representing 8.4 hours of attorney work at $290 per hour., plus two $60 filing fees. (Jensen Decl. ¶ 20.)

 

Sanctions are awarded against Plaintiff and her counsel in the amount of $2,556.00, to be paid within 20 days of this order.
