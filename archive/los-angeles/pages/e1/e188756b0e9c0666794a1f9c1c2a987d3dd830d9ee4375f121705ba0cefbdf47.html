After thoroughly reviewing the Petition and supporting documents, the Court identifies the following discrepancies:

 

There is a discrepancy in the total amounts payable for expenses and fees and available to Couger-Costain between the Petition and the proposed Order.

 

The Petition proposes funding a special needs trust for Couger-Costain’s benefit but does not meet all requirements under the Probate Code and local rules.

 

First, the Petition does not include proof of service on relevant state agencies as required. (Prob. Code, § 3602, subd. (f) [“Notice of the time and place of hearing on a petition under subdivision (d), and a copy of the petition, shall be delivered pursuant to Section 1215 to the State Director of Health Care Services, the Director of State Hospitals, and the Director of Developmental Services at the office of each director in Sacramento at least 15 days before the hearing.”].) Notice is therefore incomplete.

 

Next, no briefing has been provided regarding trust issues, including factual information specifically intended to support the findings the Court must make pursuant to Probate Code section 3604, subdivision (b), and briefing regarding the necessity of certain provisions in the trust instrument, discussed more fully below.

 

The trust instrument contains an incomplete statement pertaining the sale of a personal residence pursuant to LASC local rule 4.116(b)(3). (MC-350, Attach. 18b(4), Exh. 1, § 7.02.) Language is required stating that any sales of a personal residence of the beneficiary “must be returned to the court for confirmation” pursuant to Probate Code sections 10300 et seq.

 

The trust instrument includes the following provisions that are beyond what is fundamental to the approval and funding of a trust, and the inclusion of these additional provisions has not been briefed: (1) section 7.01(h), allowing the trustee to take fees on an interim periodic basis on account; (2) section 7.01(i), allowing the attorney for the trustee to take fees on an interim periodic basis on account; and (3) section 7.01(j), allowing the trust advisory committee to take fees on an interim periodic basis on account. Moreover, no other mention is made in the trust instrument of an “advisory committee.”

 

The trust instrument refers at section 7.02 to “If … the Court has approved investments in mutual funds according to Probate Code § 2574(c),” but does not directly request that the Court approve such investments. Reference to mutual funds should be removed unless Petitioner intends to request—and brief—their inclusion in the trust instrument.

 

The Petition does not request or calculate a specific bond amount. Bond is required pursuant to Probate Code sections 2320 and 15602 and Court Rule 7.903(c)(5). Further, counsel’s declaration states the intention that bond not be required, which is improper unless the trustee is a corporate fiduciary or trust company. (MC-350, Attach. 18b(4).)

 

The proposed Order is missing the following general trust orders:

 

(1)   The trust terms are not included as required by LASC rule 4.115(c)(2).

(2)   The required Probate Code section 3604, subdivision (b) findings are not made in the Order.

(3)   The Order does not provide for the Court’s continuing jurisdiction and supervision over the trust.

(4)   The Order does not address the request that the trustee, attorney, and trust advisory committee take fees on an interim periodic basis on account, if applicable.

(5)   The Order does not address the bond.

(6)   The Order does not provide a deadline for the filing of a Notice of Commencement of Proceedings for a Court Supervised Trust (LASC Form PRO 044).

(7)   The Order does not provide a deadline for the trustee’s first accounting.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court CONTINUES the Petition to allow Petitioner an opportunity to address the noted discrepancies.

CONCLUSION 

The Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim, filed by Claimant Atticus Couger-Costain, by and through his Guardian ad Litem, Sara Graham-Costain, is CONTINUED.

Case Number: 25CHCV04612    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 9/23/26

Case Name: Vladimir Loganov vs. Walmart Inc.; Wal-Mart Real Estate Business Trust; PRTC, L.P.; Athena Property Management, LLC; Enterprise Rent-A-Car of Los Angeles, LLC; Ferdinand Padilla; and Does 1-100

Case No. 25CHCV04612

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

SEPTEMBER 23, 2026

 

APPLICATION FOR ADMISSION PRO HAC VICE

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25CHCV04612

 

Motion filed: 6/3/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Walmart, Inc. and Walmart Real Estate Business Trust

RESPONDING PARTY: None

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court admitting Daniel Simon of Susman Godfrey LLP to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this matter.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The motion is GRANTED.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises from personal injuries allegedly sustained by Plaintiff Vladimir Loganov (“Plaintiff”) when he was struck by a motor vehicle while crossing an access road on property controlled and operated by Walmart, Inc. (“Walmart”), Walmart Real Estate Business Trust (“Walmart Trust”), and Athena Property Management, LLC (“Athena”). On December 23, 2025, Plaintiff filed the Complaint against Defendants Walmart, Walmart Trust, PRTC, L.P. (“PRTC”), Athena, Enterprise Rent-A-Car of Los Angeles, LLC, Ferdinand Padilla, and Does 1 through 100, alleging the following causes of action: (1) motor vehicle negligence, (2) general negligence, and (3) premises liability.

 

On February 17, 2026, Walmart and Walmart Trust (collectively, “Moving Defendants”) filed their joint Answer.

 

On February 18, 2026, PRTC concurrently filed its Answer and a Cross-Complaint against Roes 1 through 25 for equitable/implied indemnity, apportionment and contribution, and declaratory relief.

 

On June 3, 2026, Moving Defendants filed the instant Application to Admit Daniel Simon Pro Hac Vice. On July 8, 2026, the Court continued the hearing to allow Moving Defendants to resolve service and other deficiencies. On August 25, 2026, Moving Defendants filed the instant motion for an order granting their application, attaching supplemental declarations.

 

No objection or opposition to the Application has been filed.

 

ANALYSIS

 

“A person who is not a licensee of the State Bar but who is an attorney in good standing and eligible to practice before the bar of any United States court or the highest court in any state … and who has been retained to appear in a particular cause pending in a court of this state, may in the discretion of such court be permitted upon written application to appear as counsel pro hac vice, provided that an active licensee of the State Bar is associated as attorney of record.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).)

 

A person who is a resident of the State of California or is regularly employed or regularly engaged in substantial business, professional, or other activities in the State of California is ineligible for admission pro hac vice. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).) “Absent special circumstances, repeated appearances by any person under this rule is a cause for denial of an application.” (Id., rule 9.40(b).)

 

A.    Application for Admission Pro Hac Vice

 

Moving Defendants seek admission of their counsel, Daniel Simon (“Simon”), pro hac vice in this matter.

 

(1)   Contents of Application

 

An application to appear as counsel pro hac vice must include:

 

(1) The applicant's residence and office address;

(2) The courts to which the applicant has been admitted to practice and the dates of admission;

(3) That the applicant is a licensee in good standing in those courts;

(4) That the applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court;

(5) The title of each court and cause in which the applicant has filed an application to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this state in the preceding two years, the date of each application, and whether or not it was granted; and

(6) The name, address, and telephone number of the active licensee of the State Bar who is attorney of record.

 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(d).)

 

Here, Simon’s application meets the foregoing content requirements. He lists his residence and office addresses, the courts in which he has been admitted to practice, with dates of admission and current standing, and all instances in the past two years in which he has both applied and been admitted pro hac vice to appear in courts within the state. (Simon Decl., ¶¶ 1-7.) The application also states the name, address, and telephone number of local counsel with whom Simon is associated in this matter. (Id., ¶ 8.) Associated local counsel, Michael Madigan (“Madigan”), also provides a declaration in support.

 

(2)   Eligibility

 

Simon is a member in good standing of the following courts: (1) the State Bar of Texas, (2) the State Bar of New York, (3) the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, (4) the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, (5) the United States District Court for the Southern District of Texas, (6) the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, (7) the United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York, and (8) the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York. (Simon Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) He attests that is not currently suspended or disbarred by any court. (Id., ¶ 5.)

 

Simon further attests that he is a New York resident and does not regularly engage in the practice of law or any other business in the State of California. (Simon Decl., ¶¶ 1, 6.) Simon indicates that, within the past two years, he has applied twice for pro hac vice admission in state and federal courts within the State of California, and has been admitted once. (Id., ¶ 7.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that Simon is eligible for admission pro hac vice pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a) and (b).

 

(3)   Verification and Service of Application

 

A verified application to the Superior Court to appear as counsel pro hac vice must be filed “together with proof of service by mail in accordance with Code of Civil Procedure section 1013a of a copy of the application and of the notice of hearing of the application on all parties who have appeared in the cause and on the State Bar at its San Francisco office.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(c)(1).) “[N]otice of [the] hearing must be given at the time prescribed in Code of Civil Procedure section 1005 unless the court has prescribed a shorter period.” (Ibid.)

 

Madigan attests that he served the application and supporting declarations by mail to all parties who have appeared and to the San Francisco offices of the State Bar of California, and that he has paid the necessary fee via the State Bar of California’s website. (Madigan Decl., ¶ 6.) The proof of service attached to the Application confirms that it was served by mail on Plaintiff’s counsel, counsel for PRTC, and the State Bar of California on August 25, 2026. The service requirement has accordingly been met.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the Application.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The Court GRANTS Defendant’s Walmart, Inc. and Walmart Real Estate Business Trust’s Application to Admit Daniel Simon Pro Hac Vice.

Case Number: 26CHCP00288    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: F49

Dept. F49

Date: 9/23/26

Case Name: In the Matter of: Pamela Guillen

Case No. 26CHCP00288

 

LOS ANGELES SUPERIOR COURT

NORTH VALLEY DISTRICT

DEPARTMENT F49

 

SEPTEMBER 23, 2026

 

PETITION FOR ORDER PERMITTING PRE-LITIGATION INSPECTION AND PRESERVATION OF EVIDENCE

Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 26CHCP00288

 

Motion filed: 7/29/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Petitioner Pamela Guillen

RESPONDING PARTY: Respondents Six Flags Entertainment Corporation and Magic Mountain, LLC

NOTICE: Incomplete

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order from this Court permitting the immediate inspection, testing, photographing, and preservation of evidence relating to the X2 rollercoaster located at Six Flags Magic Mountain.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The Petition is DENIED.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This matter arises from injuries allegedly sustained by Petitioner Pamela Guillen (“Petitioner”) while riding the X2 rollercoaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain on July 5, 2026. Petitioner alleges that the ride was unusually rough and that she lost consciousness shortly after the ride ended. (Pet., at p. 3.) She states that she underwent surgery for a subdural hematoma at Henry Mayo Newhall Hospital and has suffered severe, permanent brain damage. (Ibid.) On July 29, 2026, Petitioner filed the instant verified petition for an order permitting pre-litigation inspection and preservation of evidence (the “Petition”).

 

On September 11, 2026, Respondents Six Flags Entertainment Corporation and Magic Mountain, LLC (collectively, “Respondents”) filed an Opposition to the Petition.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2035.010, subdivision (a), “[o]ne who expects to be a party or expects a successor in interest to be a party to an action that may be cognizable in a court of the state, whether as a plaintiff, or as a defendant, or in any other capacity, may obtain discovery within the scope delimited by Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 2017.010), and subject to the restrictions set forth in Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 2019.010), for the purpose of perpetuating that person’s own testimony or that of another natural person or organization, or of preserving evidence for use in the event an action is subsequently filed.”

 

“The methods available for discovery conducted for the purposes set forth in Section 2035.010 are all of the following: (a) Oral and written depositions. (b) Inspections of documents, things, and places. (c) Physical and mental examinations.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.020.)

 

A.    Procedural Requirements

 

(1)   Content of Petition

 

A person who desires to preserve evidence pursuant to section 2035.010 must file a verified petition “in the superior court of the county of the residence of at least one expected adverse party.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.030, subd. (a).) The petition must set forth all of the following:

 

(1) The expectation that the petitioner or the petitioner’s successor in interest will be a party to an action cognizable in a court of the State of California.

(2) The present inability of the petitioner and, if applicable, the petitioner’s successor in interest either to bring that action or to cause it to be brought.

(3) The subject matter of the expected action and the petitioner’s involvement. A copy of any written instrument the validity or construction of which may be called into question, or which is connected with the subject matter of the proposed discovery, shall be attached to the petition.

(4) The particular discovery methods described in Section 2035.020 that the petitioner desires to employ.

(5) The facts that the petitioner desires to establish by the proposed discovery.

(6) The reasons for desiring to perpetuate or preserve these facts before an action has been filed.

(7) The name or a description of those whom the petitioner expects to be adverse parties so far as known.

(8) The name and address of those from whom the discovery is to be sought.

(9) The substance of the information expected to be elicited from each of those from whom discovery is being sought.

 

(§ 2035.030, subd. (b).)

 

Here, the Petition has been verified by Petitioner and meets the foregoing content requirements. Petitioner states that she expects to be the plaintiff in a civil action arising out of the injuries she suffered on the X2 ride. (Pet., at p. 4.) The Petition further avers that the evidence sought by the Petition—specifically, the identity and mechanical condition of the specific train, car, and seat involved in the injury and their associated maintenance history—is required “to properly plead the claims before a complaint can reasonably be filed.” (Ibid.) The Petition describes the subject matter of the action and expected theories of liability, identifies the discovery methods Petitioner wishes to employ, and states the facts Petitioner wishes to establish and the reasons for preserving those facts. (Id., at pp. 4-5.) The Petition also names the expected adverse parties, identifies the parties from whom the discovery is being sought, and describes the substance of the information expected to be discovered. (Id., at pp. 5-6.) The requirements of section 2035.030 are accordingly met.

 

(2)   Service of Petition

 

The petitioner must serve notice of the petition and a copy of the petition “on each natural person or organization named in the petition as an expected adverse party.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.040, subd. (a).) “This service shall be made in the same manner provided for the service of a summons.” (Ibid.) “[S]ervice shall be effectuated at least 20 days prior to the date specified in the notice for the hearing on the petition.” (§ 2035.040, subd. (c).)

 

Here, Petitioner identifies the following entities as expected adverse parties: (1) Six Flags Magic Mountain, (2) Six Flags Entertainment Corporation, (3) S&S Worldwide, Inc., (4) S&S Power, Inc., and (5) S&S Arrow, Inc. (Pet., at p. 5.) The Petition also describes “related entities responsible for the design, manufacture, ownership, operation, and/or maintenance of the X2 roller coaster” as expected adverse parties. (Ibid.)

 

The proof of service filed by Petitioner, however, indicates that the notice of hearing and the Petition were only served on Magic Mountain LLC. As Respondents note in their Opposition, no proof of service has otherwise been submitted establishing service on S&S Worldwide, Inc., S&S Power, Inc., or S&S Arrow, Inc. Service is therefore insufficient under section 2035.040, which requires service on all expected adverse parties.

 

B.     Grounds for Petition

 

Even if service were proper, the Court finds that Petitioner fails to make the required showing under sections 2035.010 et seq.

 

“If the court determines that all or part of the discovery requested under this chapter may prevent a failure or delay of justice, it shall make an order authorizing that discovery.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.050, subd. (a).) A petitioner under sections 2035.010 et seq. must make a sufficient showing that perpetuation will prevent a “failure or delay of justice.” (Block v. Super. Ct. (1963) 219 Cal.App.2d 469, 478.)

 

“One shall not employ the procedures of this chapter for purposes of either ascertaining the possible existence of a cause of action or a defense to it, or of identifying those who might be made parties to an action not yet filed.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.010, subd. (b).) The perpetuation statute is meant to be used for the preservation of evidence. (§ 2035.010, subd. (a); Hunt-Wesson Foods, Inc. v. Stanislaus County (1969) 273 Cal.App.2d 92, 98.) “[I]t was not designed for purely discovery purposes,” and “use of the section for obtaining collateral information only should be denied.” (Id., at pp. 94, 98.)

 

Petitioner seeks an order permitting inspection, testing, photographing, and preservation of evidence related to the X2 rollercoaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California. Petitioner contends that there is a risk evidence will be destroyed due to the amusement park’s routine maintenance and operation of the rollercoaster. (Pet., at p. 6.) Petitioner specifically wishes to: (1) identify the specific train, car, and seat involved in the injury, (2) preserve that train and any components already removed from it, in their current condition, since the date of the incident, (3) compel production of maintenance records for that train since the date of the incident, and (4) inspect the ride. (Id., at pp. 6-7.)

 

Petitioner expects the discovery to identify the specific train, car, and seat that was occupied by Petitioner when she rode the X2, reveal the mechanical condition of that train, car, and seat on the date of the incident, reveal the inspection and maintenance history of that train, and indicate whether Six Flags had notice that a mechanical defect or condition was developing that affected the ride’s safety. (Pet., at p. 5.)

 

 Respondents contend that the Petition violates the statute’s prohibition against utilizing its procedures for investigative purposes, is based on the speculative declaration of Petitioner’s counsel, and fails to establish good cause, omitting facts that undermine Petitioner’s assertion of necessity. (Opp’n, at p. 3-7.)

 

As to the purpose of the Petition, Petitioner states that her counsel “requires the evidence sought by th[e] Petition … to properly plead the claims before a complaint can reasonably be filed.” (Pet., at p. 4.) Petitioner thus indicates that she cannot presently bring the action because she lacks the evidence to appropriately plead her claims. As Respondents note, this suggests that the evidence is sought for the purpose of investigating the existence of possible causes of action, which is expressly prohibited by section 2035.010.

 

Further, even while Petitioner frames the discovery as necessary to plead her claims, she does not adequately explain why she is unable to sue presently and proceed with ordinary discovery. The Petition indicates only that “Counsel requires the evidence,” but does not state why the information specifically requested—the identity and maintenance history of the train, car, and seat involved in the incident—is necessary to bring Petitioner’s claim.

 

Additionally, Petitioner’s rationale for conducting the discovery is unsupported. Petitioner states that the evidence is sought now, prior to commencement of a civil action, because “it is uniquely susceptible to alteration or loss through Six Flags’ ordinary maintenance operations.” (Pet., at p. 5.) The only evidence offered in support, however, is the declaration of Petitioner’s counsel, Christopher V. Bulone (“Bulone”). Bulone attests based on “information and belief” that “operators of major amusement rides such as X2 perform frequently, often daily, mechanical inspection and maintenance, which routinely includes inspecting trains, adjusting wheel assemblies, lubricating bearings, replacing wheels and bushings, tightening hardware, replacing restraint components, rotating trains into and out of service, and, when warranted, removing a train from service and sending it to maintenance for repair.” (Bulone Decl., ¶ 4.) This is, as Respondents note, a generalized and speculative attestation that does not competently establish that X2’s current status is operational and evidence is at risk of loss. It is particularly weak given Respondents’ counsel’s declaration that the ride was closed at the time the Petition was filed and remains closed. (Cherazaie Decl., ¶ 3.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that Petitioner fails to establish that conducting this discovery prior to litigation will prevent a “failure or delay of justice.”

 

CONCLUSION

 

Petitioner Pamela Guillens’ petition for an order permitting pre-litigation inspection and preservation of evidence is DENIED.
