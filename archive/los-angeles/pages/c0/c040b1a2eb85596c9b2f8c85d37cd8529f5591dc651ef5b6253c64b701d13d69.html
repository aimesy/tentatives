DEPARTMENT 529 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV30656    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 529

KHOURY v. AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR, CO., INC. 

 

demurrer to the FIRST AMENDED complaint 

 

Date of Hearing:   7/21/2026 Trial Date:  None set 

Department: 45 Case No.:  25STCV30656 

 

Moving Party:  Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.  

Responding Party:   Plaintiff Natalie Khoury 

 

BACKGROUND 

 

This is a lemon law action arising out of Plaintiff Natalie Khoury’s (“Plaintiff”) purchase of a 2020 Honda Pilot, VIN 5FNYF5H97LB025517 (the “Subject Vehicle”), which was manufactured and/or distributed by American Honda Motor Co., Inc. (“AHM”).  

 

On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against AHM and DOES 1 through 10 (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for: (1) Violation of Subdivision (d) of Civil Code Section 1793.2; (2) Violation of Subdivision (b) of Civil Code Section 1793.2; (3) Violation of Subdivision (a)(3) of Civil Code Section 1793.2; and (4) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability – Civil Code Section 1791.1, Section 1794. 

 

On May 26, 2026, AHM filed the instant Demurrer to the FAC. On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition. On July 13, 2026, AHM filed a Reply.  

 

[Tentative] Ruling 

 

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED with 30 days leave to amend. 

 

LEGAL STANDARD 

 

“The primary function of a pleading is to give the other party notice so that it may prepare its case [citation], and a defect in a pleading that otherwise properly notifies a party cannot be said to affect substantial rights.” (Harris v. City of Santa Monica (2013) 56 Cal.4th 203, 240.)¿¿ 

¿ 

“A¿demurrer¿tests the legal sufficiency of the factual allegations in a complaint.” (Ivanoff v. Bank of America, N.A.¿(2017) 9 Cal.App.5th 719, 725.) The Court looks to whether “the complaint alleges facts sufficient to state a cause of action or discloses a complete defense.” (Id.) The Court does not “read passages from a complaint in isolation; in reviewing a ruling on a demurrer, we read the complaint ‘as a whole and its parts in their context.’ [Citation.]” (West v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 780, 804.) The Court “assume[s] the truth of the properly pleaded factual allegations, facts that reasonably can be inferred from those expressly pleaded and matters of which judicial notice has been taken.” (Harris, supra, 56 Cal.4th p. 240.) “The court does not, however, assume the truth of contentions, deductions or conclusions of law. [Citation.]” (Durell v. Sharp Healthcare (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 1350, 1358.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

A general demurrer may be brought under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e) if insufficient facts are stated to support the cause of action asserted or under section 430.10, subdivision (a), where the court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the pleading. All other grounds listed in Section 430.10, including uncertainty under subdivision (f), are special demurrers. Special demurrers are not allowed in limited jurisdiction courts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 92, subd. (c).)¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.)¿¿¿ 

 

ANALYSIS 

 

AHM demurs the entire FAC on the grounds that it is barred by the four-year statute of limitations period set forth in Commercial Code Section 2725, subdivision (2).  

 

“[T]he California courts have consistently held that a demurrer which raises the defense that a statute of limitation bars the action cannot be sustained unless the facts alleged in the complaint show that the action is barred. If all that appears from the facts alleged is that the action¿might¿be barred, the defense cannot be raised by demurrer.”¿(Anderson v. McNally¿(1957) 150 Cal.App.2d 778, 783; see also¿Pike v. Zadig¿(1915) 171 Cal. 273, 277,¿disapproved of¿on other grounds¿by¿Decesare¿v. Lembert¿(1983) 144 Cal.App.3d 20, stating [“a demurrer on the ground of the bar of the statute of limitations does not lie, where the complaint merely shows that the action may have been barred. It must appear affirmatively that, upon the facts¿stated, the right of action is necessarily barred.”].)¿ 

 

“The statute of limitations for a violation of warranty is four years.” (Flat v. Ford Motor Company (N.D. Cal. 2023) 678 F.Supp.3d 1138, 1140 citing to Mexia v. Rinker Boat Co. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1297, 1305-06 Krieger v. Nick Alexander Imports, Inc. (1991) 234 Cal.App.3d 205, 215 [“Section 2725 of the California Uniform Commercial Code provides that an action for breach of warranty accrues on tender of delivery. An exception is made where a warranty ‘explicitly extends to future performance of the goods and discovery of the breach must await the time of such performance.’ ”].) “Typically, a breach of the implied warranty of merchantability occurs at the date of sale.” (Id. at 1141.) “However, the Song-Beverly Act provides that the ‘duration of the implied warranty of merchantability and where present the implied warranty of fitness shall be coextensive in duration with an express warranty ... but in no event ... have a duration of less than 60 days nor more than one year following the sale of new consumer goods to a retail buyer.’” Nonetheless, the California Court of Appeal determined that the durational limit set forth in Civil Code Section 1791.1, subdivision (c) extends the implied warranty to future performance. (Id.) Thus, “the implied warranty of merchantability may be breached by a latent defect undiscoverable at the time of sale but must occur no later than the durational limit set forth in Section 1791.1(c).” (Id.) 

 

The FAC alleges, inter alia, that on or about May 12, 2021, Plaintiff purchased a brand new 2020 Honda Pilot, VIN 5FNYF5H97LB025517 (the “Subject Vehicle”), which was manufactured and/or distributed by American Honda Motor Co., Inc. (“AHM”). (FAC, ¶¶6, 17.) Plaintiff received express written warranties, including but not limited to: “a 3 year/36,000 mile New Vehicle Limited Warranty, and a 5 year/60,000 mile Powertrain Limited Warranty, in which [AHM] undertook to preserve or maintain the utility or performance of Subject Vehicle or to provide compensation if there is a failure in utility or performance for a specified period of time.” (Id., ¶19.) During Plaintiff’s ownership of the Subject Vehicle, it manifested multipurpose camera defects, electrical system defects, infotainment system defects, and radio defects (“Subject Vehicle defects”). (Id., ¶20.) The Subject Vehicle defects substantially impaired the use, value, and/or safety if the Subject Vehicle. (Id.)  

 

Taking the allegations as true, the earliest date the express and implied warranties regarding the defects alleged could have accrued was the date of purchase, May 12, 2021. This action was originally filed on October 20, 2025, which is four (4) years and five (5) months after the purchase date. As such, absent properly pleaded facts showing tolling provisions apply, this lawsuit was filed outside of the four-year statute of limitations period.  

 

Here,¿Plaintiff fails¿to¿plead any facts that support the application of Class Action Tolling as¿set forth in¿American Pipe & Construction Co. v. Utah¿(1974) 414 U.S. 538 (American Pipe). “Underlying the tolling rule of¿American Pipe¿were two major policy considerations. The first was the protection of the class action device…The second consideration involved the effectuation of the purposes of the statute of limitations.” (Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co.¿(1988) 44 Cal.3d 1103, 1121.)¿Essentially, the¿Court is tasked with protecting judicial efficiency and economy of litigation and protecting the defendant from unfair claims, i.e., claims it did not have sufficient notice of¿regarding¿substance and nature. (Id.¿at 1122-1123.)¿The¿FAC merely alleges: “To the extent there are any statutes of limitation applicable to Plaintiff’s claims, including, without limitation, the California Commercial Code’s express warranty, implied warranty of merchantability, implied warranty of fitness for a particular purpose, and Song-Beverly Warranty Act, the running of the limitation periods have been tolled by various tolling doctrines, including but not limited to, the discovery rule, the repair rule, and equitable tolling.” (FAC, ¶8.) There are no allegations that Plaintiff was a participant in a prior class action lawsuit against¿AHM¿related to the alleged Subject Vehicle defects herein that was denied such that¿AHM¿had prior notice of the substance and nature of her claims now alleged in the FAC.  

 

Likewise, the tolling provisions the Song-Beverly Act under Section 1795.6 are restricted to repair-related scenarios as it pertains to the express warranty period. (See generally,¿Nunez¿v. FCA US LLC¿(2021) 61 Cal.App.5th 385, 394.)¿Specifically,¿“the warranty period is tolled or extended under only three circumstances: (1) the car is in the shop for warranty repairs (§ 1795.6, subd. (a)(1)), (2) there are delays in warranty repairs beyond the control of the buyer, and (3) upon notice from the buyer, given within 60 days of completion of warranty repairs, that those repairs did not solve the problem (§ 1795.6, subd. (b)). The statute provides no other circumstances under which the warranty is tolled or extended.” (Id.)¿The FAC does allege that the Subject Vehicle was presented to AHM’s authorized repair facility on two separate occasions, i.e., February 8, 2022 and February 15, 2022 for warranty repairs. (FAC, ¶22.) The FAC also alleges that technicians at AHM’s authorized repair facility inspected the Subject Vehicle, verified Plaintiff’s complaints, and advised at pick up that the Subject Vehicle had been repaired and was working as designed. (Id.) However, the FAC is devoid of any allegations that there were delays in the warranty repairs let alone delays beyond Plaintiff’s control. Thus, Plaintiff cannot avail herself to the tolling provision under Section 1795.6 under the Song-Beverly Act.  

 

As to Plaintiff’s brief argument that the FAC pleads facts to support delayed discovery, thus tolls the statute of limitations period (Opp. at 8:11-18), it is not supported by legal authority. (See¿Goldstein v. General Motors LLC¿(S.D. Cal. 2021) 517 F.Supp.3d 1076, 1094 [“Song-Beverly's statute of limitations is four years, and the discovery rule does not apply to toll the statute of limitations”]; see also¿Mexia v. Rinker Boat Co., Inc.¿(2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1297, 1305 [“the warranty of merchantability is breached, by the existence of the unseen defect, not by its subsequent discovery.”].)¿ 

 

On the other hand, a defendant may be equitably estopped from asserting the statute of limitations as a defense “(1) if one potentially liable for a construction defect represents, while the limitations period is still running, that all actionable damage has been or will be repaired, thus making it unnecessary to sue, (2) the plaintiff reasonably relies on this representation to refrain from bringing a timely action, (3) the representation proves false after the limitations period has expired, and (4) the plaintiff proceeds diligently once the truth is discovered.” (Lantzy v. Centex Homes (2003) 31 Cal.4th 363, 384.) “Tolling during a period of repairs generally rests upon the same legal basis as does an estoppel to assert the statute of limitations, i.e., reliance by the plaintiff on the words or actions of the defendant that repairs will be made.” (Cardinal Health 301, Inc. v. Tyco Electronics Corp. (2008) 169 Cal.App.4th 116, 133–134.)  

 

As discussed above, the FAC does allege that AHM’s technicians at their authorized repair facility advised the Subject Vehicle defects, which were actionable damage that manifested during the warranty period, had been repaired. (FAC, ¶22b.) The FAC further alleges that the statements, representations of repairs made assurances by AHM’s technicians at their authorized repair facility led Plaintiff to believe that the recurring systems were not the result of defects, misleading and delaying Plaintiff’s discovery of the Subject Vehicle defects. (Id., ¶15.) Taking these allegations as true AHM’s authorized repair facility technicians represented to Plaintiff that the Subject Vehicle defects had been repaired.  

 

Contrarily, the FAC fails to show that Plaintiff reasonably relied on this representation. (See generally Mills v. Forestex Co. (2003) 108 Cal.App.4th 625, 655; Peregrine Funding, Inc. v. Sheppard Mullin Richter & Hampton LLP (2005) 133 Cal.App.4th 658, 686.) Specifically, the FAC alleges that  on or about February 15, 2022, AHM’s authorized repair facility technicians represented to Plaintiff that the Subject Vehicle defects had been repaired. (FAC, ¶22b.) The FAC further alleges that, thereafter, Plaintiff continued to experience the Subject Vehicle defects. (Id., ¶22c.) Moreover, Plaintiff waited nearly three years after the last alleged repair attempt, wherein the Subject Vehicle continued to experience the Subject Vehicle defects prior to filing the present action. Thus, the FAC fails to allege facts showing that Plaintiff acted with diligence. (See e.g., Gundogdu v. King Mai, Inc. (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 310, 317.) 

 

Accordingly, the FAC fails to plead sufficient facts to either toll the statute of limitations period and/or estop AHM from raising such defense.  

 

CONCLUSION 

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED with 30 days leave to amend. 

Case Number: 26STCV11797    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 529

PEEPLES v. VARLEY CLOTHING INC., et al. 

 

motion to strike PORTIONS OF THE COMPLAINT 

 

Date of Hearing:   7/21/2026 Trial Date:  None set 

Department: 45 Case No.:  26STCV11797 

 

Moving Party:  Defendant Varley Clothing Inc. 

Responding Party:   Plaintiff Brianna Peeples 

 

BACKGROUND 

 

This action arises out of the employment relationship between Plaintiff Brianna Peeples (“Plaintiff”) and Defendant Varley Clothing Inc. (“Defendant”). On April 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative Complaint against Defendant, asserting a sole cause of action for Waiting Time Penalties under Labor Code Section 203.  

 

On June 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion to Strike. On July 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition. On July 14, 2026, Defendant filed a Reply.  

 

[Tentative] Ruling 

 

Defendant Varley Clothing Inc.’s Motion to Strike is DENIED. Defendant is ordered to file an Answer to the Complaint within 15 days of this order.  

 

LEGAL STANDARD 

 

“Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof, but this time limitation shall not apply to motions specified in subdivision (e).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1).)¿¿¿¿ 

 

“The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (a); see also Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782 [“Matter in a pleading which is not essential to the claim is surplusage; probative facts are surplusage and may be stricken out or disregarded”.].)¿ 

 

The court may also strike all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with California law, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (b).) An immaterial or irrelevant allegation is one that is not essential to the statement of a claim or defense; is neither pertinent to nor supported by an otherwise sufficient claim or defense; or a demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 431.10, subd. (b).) The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437.)¿¿¿ 

 

ANALYSIS 

 

Defendant moves to strike Plaintiff’s request for attorney’s fees on the grounds that the Complaint does not allege any cause of action for nonpayment of wages, fringe benefits, health and welfare or pension fund contributions pursuant to Labor Code Section 218.5. 

 

Prayer for Relief, ¶2 at 5:3: “For reasonable attorneys’ fees pursuant to Labor Code section 218.5.” 

 

Here, Plaintiff brings this action against Defendant for waiting time penalties under Labor Code Section 203, which expressly states: “If an employer willfully fails to pay, without abatement or reduction, in accordance with Sections 201, 201.3, 201.5, 201.6, 201.8, 201.9, 202, and 205.5, any wages of an employee who is discharged or who quits, the wages of the employee shall continue as a penalty from the due date thereof at the same rate until paid or until an action therefor is commenced; but the wages shall not continue for more than 30 days.” (Lab. Code, § 203, subd. (a) [emphasis added]; Compl., ¶12.) 

 

Specifically, the Complaint alleges that on or about August 5, 2024, Defendant employed Plaintiff as a model for a still image shoot conducted for the production of still images for use in print, digital, or internet media. (Compl., ¶7.) Defendant agreed to Plaintiff wages at the rate of $4,000.00 for the one day of employment. (Id., ¶8.) Pursuant to Labor Code Section 201.6, Plaintiff was entitled to be paid her wages by Defendant’s next regular payday as defined in Labor Code Section 201.6, subdivision (a)(2) following Plaintiff’s termination from her employment. (Id., ¶9.) Defendant’s next regular payday was required to occur on or before August 26, 2024. (Id.) Defendant failed to make payment of Plaintiff’s wages on or before Defendant’s next regular payday and did not make payment until more than 30 days after the next regular payday. (Id., ¶¶10-11.)  

 

At issue is whether an action brought under Labor Code Section 203 for waiting time penalties constitutes “an action for nonpayment of wages” for purposes of recovering attorney’s fees under Labor Code Section 218.5, subdivision (a), which explicitly states: “In any action brought for the nonpayment of wages, fringe benefits, or health and welfare or pension fund contributions, the court shall award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to the prevailing party if any party to the action requests attorney's fees and costs upon the initiation of the action.” (Lab. Code, § 218.5, subd. (a) [emphasis added].) For the following reasons below, the Court finds that it does.  

 

It is undisputed that Labor Code Section 203 was enacted to promote full and prompt payment of an employee’s earned wages, as well as punish employers for failing to do, thus has been technically classified as “penalties.” (See generally Pineda v. Bank of America, N.A. (2010) 50 Cal.4th 1389, 1400–1401.) However, California courts have held that, “[u]nlike an employee suing under section 226.7, an employee suing for failure to pay wages by the deadline established in [Labor Code sections 201 and 202] is suing for nonpayment of wages for purposes of an attorney fee award under Labor Code section 218.5.” (Naranjo v. Spectrum Security Services, Inc. (2022) 13 Cal.5th 93, 112; see also Kirby v. Immoos Fire Protection, Inc. (2012) 53 Cal.4th 1244, 1256 [holding a lawsuit based on violation of Labor Code Sections 201 and 202 are claims for nonpayment of wages].) Labor Code Sections 201 and 202 are explicitly mentioned in Labor Code Section 203. Although the Complaint does not explicitly set forth a separate cause of action for violation Labor Code Section 201.6, Plaintiff is suing Defendant, i.e., her employer for failure to pay her wages by the deadline established in Labor Code Section 201.6, which like Labor Code Sections 201 and 202 is mentioned in Labor Code Section 203. As such, the gravamen of Plaintiff’ suit is one for nonpayment of wages.  

 

Accordingly, the Complaint sufficiently alleges a claim for nonpayment of wages against Defendant, thus entitlement to attorney’s fees under Labor Code Section 218.5.  

 

CONCLUSION 

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Varley Clothing Inc.’s Motion to Strike is DENIED. Defendant is ordered to file an Answer to the Complaint within 15 days of this order.
