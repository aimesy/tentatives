25STCV20313

GABRIEL M DE LA ROSA vs FCA US LLC, et al.

July 22, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL DISCLOSURES PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 871.26 AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS.

 

RULING:

The Court denies the Motion.

Defendant to give notice.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On  July 1, 2025, GABRIEL M DE LA ROSA (Plaintiff) filed a Lemon Law Complaint against FCA US LLC and MOSS BROS. CHRYSLER DODGE JEEP RAM SAN BERNARDINO (Defendants), alleging Causes of Action under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (Civ. Code §§ 1790-1795.8), along with claims for Negligent Repair and Fraudulent Inducement - Concealment.

Plaintiff alleges purchasing, with warranty provisions, a 2023 Dodge Challenger that Defendant FCA US LLC manufactured, which had defects, including engine, transmission, and electrical. Plaintiff further alleges that Defendant failed to repair the vehicle within a reasonable number of attempts and did not promptly replace it or make restitution.

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures that are verified, pursuant to section 871.26, and to obtain sanctions.

FCA US LLC (Defendant) filed an Opposition, reporting statutory compliance and objecting to requests exceeding the statutory scope.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 provides that, unless the party failing to comply with discovery requirements shows good cause, judges shall impose sanctions, including $1,500.00 against a plaintiff's attorney, or $2,500.00 against a defendant's attorney, to be paid within 15 business days, for the failure to comply with initial document production. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j)).

 

As to Lemon Law cases, Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, effective January 1, 2025, provides in material part, the following:

 

“(h) The defendant or manufacturer shall provide the following documents to all other parties pursuant to the timelines prescribed in subdivision (b):

 

(1) Copy of or access to a version of the owner's manual for a motor vehicle of the same make, model, and year.

 

(2) Any warranties issued in conjunction with the sale of the motor vehicle.

 

(3) Sample brochures published for the motor vehicle.

 

(4) The motor vehicle's original invoice, if any, to the selling dealer.

 

(5) Sales or lease agreement, if the manufacturer is in possession.

 

(6) Motor vehicle information reports, including build documentation, component information, and delivery details.

 

(7) Entire warranty transaction history for the motor vehicle.

 

(8) Listing of required field actions applicable to the motor vehicle.

 

(9) Published technical service bulletins (”TSBs”)1 for the same make, model, and year reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle.

 

(10) Published information service bulletins (“ISBs”) for the same make, model, and year reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle.

 

(11) Records relating to communications between the manufacturer or dealership and the owner or lessee of the motor vehicle, including those related to repair orders or claims involving the motor vehicle.

 

(12) Warranty policies and procedure manuals.

 

(13) Service manuals reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle.

 

(14) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, all call recordings of pre-suit communications with the consumer available at the time of service of the complaint.

 

(15) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, the manufacturer's written statement of policies and procedures used to evaluate customer requests for restitution or replacement pursuant to “Lemon Law” claims.

 

(16) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, any nonprivileged, prelitigation evaluation.

 

(17) Any warranty extensions or modifications issued by the manufacturer on the motor vehicle.”

 

(Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 871.26, subd. (h).)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiff identifies many areas of content that Defendant generally has electronic and other access to but did not include in its initial disclosures. (Motion, pp. 7-15.)

Defendant counterargues that Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 does not provide for all of the discovery that Plaintiff seeks, which instead must be sought pursuant to the California Discovery Act.  Defendant further maintains that the section “does not require verified written responses, search-methodology descriptions, an unfiltered production, or every VIN-searchable record in FCA’s internal systems.” (Opposition, 2:12-14.) Defendant further asserts that, “Plaintiff relies on attorney argument and general references to FCA materials to infer that additional documents must exist.” (Ibid, 4:13-14.)

The Court observes that there is a dearth of law about section 871.26 and nothing governing about legislative interpretation as to the intended scope of the statute.

Legislative interpretation begins by examination of the language of the statute, giving the words their ordinary meaning and considering them in the context of the statutory framework. (Barnes v. Dept. of Corrections (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 126, 131.) Judges should not disregard express statutory language, or add language that is not there. (Yao v. Superior Court (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 327, 333.) Only when statutory language is susceptible to more than one reasonable construction is it appropriate to consider extrinsic aids, including certain limited types of legislative history, to ascertain the meaning. (E.g., Esberg v. Union Oil Co. (2002) 28 Cal.4th 262, 269.)

According to legislative history, section 871.26 “would stay all discovery, except a limited set of disclosures….” (CA LEGIS 938 (2024), 2024 Cal. Legis. Serv. Ch. 938 (A.B. 1755) (WEST).) (Underscoring added.) Legislative history and the wording of the statute indicate a legislative intent to require much less disclosure than the great extent of information and searching for discovery responses that plaintiffs sometimes seek as part of initial disclosures in Lemon Law cases.

The Court does not order disclosure and production falling outside the scope of section 871.26, as to which the Discovery Act would serve as the prescribed procedure. Analogously, where a specialized Pitchess motion does not apply to the information sought, the proper procedure involves demanding an inspection of the information pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.030. (Ibarra v. Superior Court (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 695, 706.)

Additionally, the Motion is accompanied by a Declaration that contains no proof to support a finding that Defendant withheld existing and available documents, required by section 871.26. And statements in briefs filed are not supportive evidence. (Turrieta v. Lyft, Inc. (2024) 16 Cal.5th 664, 697.)

Finally, the Court finds that the Opposition shows good cause for not further providing documents pursuant to section 871.26, based upon reasonable contentions about legislative interpretation. So, the Court does not impose sanctions.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court denies the Motion, as analyzed herein.

25STCV21646

JESSICA IBARRA, et al. vs 3621 PACIFIC AVE SAN PEDRO, LLC, et al.

 

July 22, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PETITIONER/PARENT/GUARDIAN AD LITEM JESSICA IBARRA’S PETITION FOR APPROVAL OF COMPROMISE OF CLAIM OR ACTION OR DISPOSITION OF PROCEEDS OF JUDGMENT FOR MINOR.

 

RULING:

The Court denies the Petition, without prejudice to petition for at least a $6,000.00 balance of the proceeds of the proposed settlement for the Claimant after payment of all fees and expenses.

Additionally, the proposed form Order Approving Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Minor, received June 4, 2026, at paragraph 1, incorrectly shows the box checked for expedited approval without a hearing, whereas one has been reserved for this date and the Petition states the date.

Petitioner to give notice.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On April 2, 2026, JESSICA IBARRA and AALIYAH TOUTAR (Plaintiffs) filed a First Amended Complaint (FAC) against 3621 PACIFIC AVE SAN PEDRO, LLC and BEACH FRONT PROPERTY MANAGEMENT, INC. (Defendants), listing Causes of Action for:

1. BREACH OF CONTRACT

2. BREACH OF IMPLIED WARRANTY OF HABITABILITY

3. NUISANCE

4. NEGLIGENCE

5. WRONGFUL EVICTION

6. VIOLATION OF THE LOS ANGELES TENANT ANTI-HARASSMENT ORDINANCE “TAHO”.

Plaintiffs allege that, after renting in February 201, in August 2023, they noticed uninhabitable living conditions including dangerous levels of toxic mold from a rain leakage, throughout 3621 South Pacific Avenue #10, San Pedro. They also allege that they vacated the property in December 2024, because Defendants, as owners/managers, would not act after Plaintiffs’ repeatedly complained.

On June 4, 2026, Petitioner filed the Petition for Approval of a Minor’s Compromise for Claimant AALIYAH TOUTAR, age 13, who suffered living in uninhabitable conditions but was not injured and has completely recovered.

Petitioner seeks approval of $2,500.00 to the minor, less $625.00 attorneys’ fees, leaving a balance of $1,875.00. The total settlement amount is $79,500.00.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Petitions for approval of minors’ compromises shall fully disclose all information that has any bearing on the compromises’ reasonableness. (Chui v. Chui (2022) 75 Cal.App.5th 873, 899 [citing Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950].)

A petition for court approval of a compromise under Code of Civil Procedure section 372 must comply with California Rules of Court rules 7.950, 7.951, and 7.952. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1384(a).) Some of the Probate Code and the Code of Civil Procedure apply to compromises as to minors’ claims. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) In large part, the Local Rules incorporate by reference the California Rules of Court and statutory provisions. (See Super. Ct. LA County, Local Rules, rule 4.115 et seq.)

Notice must be given to all appearing defendants in the action.  (Cal. C. Civ. Pro. §1014). Alternatively, as to an expedited petition: Unless a hearing is requested or ordered, an expedited petition must be determined without a hearing, not more than 35 days after filing, subject to extension for good cause.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.5(b), (c)).

Regarding disbursements, courts may order that:

1.      Those funds shall not be disbursed without further order of the courts (id.); or,

2.      The depository shall permit the withdrawal of funds by minors at ages of majority, without further order of the courts, based on findings that minors will attain the age of majority on a definite date.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.953(b).)

Money or other property to be paid or delivered pursuant to order or judgment for the benefit of a minor, incompetent person, or person for whom a conservator has been appointed, shall be paid and delivered as provided in Chapter 4 of Part 8 of Division 4 of the Probate Code §3600 et seq.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 372, subd. (a).)

Courts may consider evidence presented and determine reasonable amounts to be paid to attorneys representing minors.  (Curtis v. Fagan (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 270, 273; Padilla v. McClellan (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 1100, 1106.) In cases involving the compromise of a pending action of a minor, the court must determine an award of attorney fees under California Rules of Court, Rule 7.955.  (Gonzalez v. Chen (2011) 197 Cal.App.4th 881, 888.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Most importantly, courts must decide whether to approve a compromise by determining if petitioners, such as guardians, are acting in the best interests of minors or incompetent persons. (E.g., CCP §372(a); Scruton v. Korean Air Lines Co. (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 1596, 1603-07.) “[T]he protective role the court generally assumes in cases involving minors, [is] a role to assure that whatever is done is in the minor's best interests….  [I]ts primary concern is whether the compromise is sufficient to provide for the minor's injuries, care and treatment.”  (Goldberg v. Sup. Ct. (1994) 23 Cal.App.4th 1378, 1382.) Judicial discretion in approving petitions for minors’ compromises, and ordering distributions, and local court policies, must be applied in the best interests of minors, in a case-by-case method, tailored to the circumstances.  (Christensen v. Sup. Ct. (1987) 193 Cal.App.3d 139, 142-44.)

Here, the Court finds that the proposed compromise is not in the best interests of the Claimant, because many other like cases, involving uninhabitable living conditions without any lingering injuries, have settled for a balance for the minors of approximately a $6,000.00 minimum, and sometimes up to around $70,000.00. The balance here of $1,875.00 is unacceptably low for no good reason, especially considering that it is a very small percentage of the total settlement amount of $79,500.00 that would largely go to the parent.

 

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court denies the Petition, without prejudice, as specified herein.

25STCV29323

7-ELEVEN, INC., A TEXAS CORPORATION vs AIRPORT PLAZA, A CALIFORNIA GENERAL PARTNERSHIP

July 22, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF 7-ELEVEN, INC.’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH DEMAND FOR INSPECTION AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the Motion as to Ordering the response and inspection.

The Court denies Plaintiff’s and Defendant’s sanctions requests, it finding substantial justification for some positions of each side.

On or before August 14, 2026, Defendant VASILE PROPERTIES AIRPORT PLAZA, LLC shall serve initial responses, without waived objections, in full compliance with the California Discovery Act (Code Civ. Proc. § 2016.010 et seq.), as to Plaintiff 7-ELEVEN, INC.’s Demand for Inspection.

Commencing at 7:00 a.m., on August 24, 2026, Defendant VASILE PROPERTIES AIRPORT PLAZA, LLC and Plaintiff 7-ELEVEN, INC. shall conduct the requested inspection of the subject sewer line.

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On October 7, 2025, 7-ELEVEN, INC. (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against VASILE PROPERTIES AIRPORT PLAZA, LLC (Defendant or VPAP).

On March 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC), listing Causes of Action for:

(1) DECLARATORY RELIEF

(2) BREACH OF CONTRACT

(3) BREACH OF THE COVENANT OF GOOD FAITH AND FAIR DEALING.

(4) UNJUST ENRICHMENT.

Plaintiff alleges that a commercial lease with Plaintiff requires Defendant, the landlord, to maintain common areas, but a corroded sewer line has been causing plumbing problems, thereby precluding Defendant from having reasonable use or enjoyment of the premises, including because of lacking a functioning restroom.

On June 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion to Compel Compliance with Plaintiff's Demand for Inspection and for Monetary Sanctions.

Defendant opposes in part, and advocates a proposed Order, received on July 9, 2026, partly granting the Motion, which Order contains many provisions appearing to be a thinly disguised Protective Order, as follows:

5. Defendant Vasile Properties Airport Plaza, LLC shall permit a non-destructive plumbing inspection of the property under the following protocol:

a. The inspection shall be non-destructive.

b. The inspection shall occur on a mutually agreeable date and time, with at least five court days’ advance notice to allow Defendant to coordinate access and personnel.

c. The inspection shall not exceed four hours absent further written agreement of the parties or further order of the Court.

d. Attendees for Plaintiff shall be limited to counsel for Plaintiff, Plumbing Masters personnel reasonably necessary to conduct the inspection, and any other person agreed to by Defendant in advance or authorized by further order of the Court.

e. Attendees for Defendant may include Defendant’s counsel, a property-management representative, and any person reasonably necessary to provide access or monitor the inspection.

f. The inspection shall be limited to non-destructive inspection, observation, photographing, video recording, measurement, and plumbing/sewer-line testing reasonably necessary to trace and inspect the sewer line serving Plaintiff’s leased premises.

g. The inspection may include Plaintiff’s leased premises, common areas, and vacant adjacent tenant spaces within the structure identified by Plaintiff, but only to the extent reasonably necessary to trace and inspect the sewer line serving Plaintiff’s leased premises.

h. The inspection shall not include destructive testing, drilling, excavation, cutting, opening walls, opening floors, opening ceilings, removal of fixtures, or any sampling or activity that alters or damages the property absent further written agreement of the parties or further order of the Court.

i. The inspection shall not include review, photography, copying, or recording of tenant files, business records, confidential materials, personal property unrelated to the plumbing/sewer-line issue, or areas unrelated to the sewer-line inspection.

j. Plaintiff’s representatives shall take reasonable steps to avoid disruption to the property and shall leave all inspected areas in the same condition in which they were found, except for ordinary movement reasonably necessary for non-destructive access.

k. If a dispute arises during the inspection regarding scope, access, or method, the parties shall meet and confer in good faith before seeking further relief from the Court.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

A motion to compel initial discovery responses need only show that discovery was properly served on the opposing party, the time to respond expired, and no response was served. (Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906; Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300 [compelling inspection demand].) A motion to compel initial discovery responses need not show good cause, meeting and conferring, or timely filing, and need not be accompanied by a separate statement. (Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pac. Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 404.)

“If the party seeking a monetary sanction meets its burden of proof, the burden shifts to the opposing party attempting to avoid a monetary sanction to show that it acted with ‘substantial justification.’” (Doe v. U.S. Swimming, Inc. (2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1424, 1435.) Courts may determine substantial justification based on the “whole picture” of the discovery dispute, including where parties partially prevailed as to some discovery items in dispute, or where discovery concessions occurred only after improperly asserted objections. (In re Marriage of Moore (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 1275, 1297.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiff states that Defendant never served a discovery response to the subject demand such that objections are statutorily deemed waived, has not proposed an inspection date, and now improperly advocates a protective order in opposition. Plaintiff adds that an objection to destructive testing is unjustified where Plaintiff’s demand only sought a non-destructive inspection.

Defendant requests an Order rejecting Plaintiff’s requested relief as being overbroad, monetary sanctions, unilateral-inspection control, and untethered access and destructive testing. Defendant also reports that, on April 21, 2026, Plaintiff’s plumbing consultant arrived late after the noticed start time for the inspection. And Defendant explains that the parties’ meeting and conferring entailed disputes about coordination, timing, attendees, duration, scope, access to external spaces, and appropriate on-site personnel. Further, Defendant objects to monetary sanctions based upon several arguments, including insufficient evidentiary support and merely estimated future reply expense.

Parties may demand, “[i]nspections of documents, things, and places.” (Civ. Proc. Code § 2019.010, subd. (c).) The response is due within 30 days after the demand is served. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) Parties failing to serve timely responses to discovery requests waive any objections thereto. (Pelton v. Delta Packaging Prods. (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 1658, 1577, fn.13; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pac. Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 404.)

Also, a noticed motion is required to request a protective order. (Titmas v. Superior Court (2001) 87 Cal.App.4th 738, 743; St. Paul Fire & Marine Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 82, 85.)

Here, the Court finds that Plaintiff satisfactorily evidenced Defendant’s failure to serve a timely response to the subject inspection request and to propose a mutually agreeable time for the inspection.

Further, Defendant is not authorized in the Opposition to insist upon conditions of a protective order for compelling the inspection, especially after causing a statutory waiver of discovery objections by never serving any timely response.

The Court further finds mutual substantial justification in denying both sanctions requests. For instance, Plaintiff’s first inspection attempt ran late through no fault of Defendant. (E.g., Reply, 9:3-5.) And Defendant nonsensically advocates a condition of an Order precluding destructive testing that the Plaintiff’s inspection demand expressly has not requested. Furthermore, discovery motions, such as for compelling depositions, frequently involve attorneys’ inability to simply arrive at a mutually agreeable time, typically leaving it up to a judge to order a mandated time.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court grants the Motion as specified herein and denies both sanctions requests.

Further, the Court gives advance notice that if the parties are unsuccessful in accomplishing the ordered plumbing inspection, the Court may consider appointing a Discovery Referee, at the equal expense of each party, to attend any future inspection and to supervise and direct it. (E.g., Code Civ. Proc., § 639.)

Finally, the Court observes that Motions to Compel Inspection Compliance, as to places and tangible things, appear to be exceedingly rare in this Court and around the state, as is evident by a dearth of California judicial opinions on the subject. Additionally, it appears that counsel are extra contentious in this case, as to a matter that should be readily resolved.
