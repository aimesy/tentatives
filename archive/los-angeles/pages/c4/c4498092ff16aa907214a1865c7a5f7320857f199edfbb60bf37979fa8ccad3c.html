Discussion[2]

 

Moving Defendant CSCDA demurs to the 3rd-6th and 8-9th COAs on the grounds that all fail to state facts sufficient to constitute the COA and are otherwise uncertain. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 430.10(e), (f).)

 

For reasons to be discussed, and absent a timely opposition, the court sustains the demurrer without leave to amend and grants the MTS without leave to amend.

 

As for the 3rd, 4th, and 9th COAs, CSCDA avers the claims are duplicative as they all sound in basic negligence, and Plaintiffs have already stated a cause of action for negligence and two separate causes of action for breach of the warranty of habitability. Thus, based on these facts, the SAC thus alleges only one cause of action. (Demurrer p. 7, citing Van Zyl v. Spiegelberg (1969) 2 Cal.App.3d 367-372 [“Accordingly, what plaintiff seeks is to recover in damages under the same state of facts but under different theories of recovery, that is, private nuisance and negligence. In such a case there is but one cause of action.”].) Thus, the court sustains the demurrer as to the foregoing COA.

 

As for the 5th COA for Breach of Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment, that also fails to plead sufficient facts. As stated in the demurrer, this covenant of quiet enjoyment is not broken until there has been an actual or constructive eviction. (Demurrer p. 7, citing Clark v. Spiegel (1971) 22 Cal.App.3d 74, 80; Slater v. Conti (1959) 171 Cal.App.2d 582, 585.)  Here, however, moving Defendant argues certain facts are lacking regarding specifying the manner or length of dispossession.[3] Thus, the court sustains the demurrer as to this COA.

 

As for the 6th COA for negligent violation of statutory duty to maintain habitable condition, moving Defendant avers that COA does not exist. In the alternative, they argue that this COA is duplicative of the Tortious Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability, Statutory Breach of Warranty of Habitability, and for negligence. For reasons iterated above, thus, the court sustains the demurrer as to this COA.

 

As for the 9th COA for public nuisance, moving Defendant argues that Plaintiffs allege that they were bitten by bedbugs in an apartment unit. But a public nuisance is “one which affects at the same time an entire community or neighborhood, or any considerable number of persons, although the extent of the annoyance or damage inflicted upon individuals may be unequal.” (Civ. Code § 3480). Here, Defendant alleges that Plaintiffs do not allege a public nuisance which affects an entire community or neighborhood.[4] Thus, the court sustains the demurrer as to this COA.

 

As for leave to amend, “In our adversarial system of adjudication, we follow the principle of party presentation. [] The parties frame the issues for decision while the court serves as ‘neutral arbiter of matters the parties present. [] In other words, courts call balls and strikes’; they don't get a turn at bat. [].” (Clark v. Sweeney, 607 U.S. ___ (2025).) This means that while the court has reviewed the cited authority to ensure correct statements of law and considered the moving party’s general arguments, absent an opposition, this court would transgress the party-presentation principle by denying relief on claims never advanced by Plaintiffs. That canon guides this court’s decision to deny leave to amend. But not only is the court denying leave to amend as Plaintiffs have (again) failed to file an opposition to show what amendments could cure the defects, but the court has previously given leave to amend yet Plaintiffs failed to make meaningful amendments. To the extent that Plaintiffs will attempt to make oral argument, the court directs them to the court’s 6/22/26 final ruling. 

 

As for the MTS, the motion is made on the grounds that Plaintiffs have failed to state facts sufficient to support a claim for punitive damages and failed to state entitlement to attorneys’ fees, and irrelevant references to inapplicable code sections. Here, as Plaintiffs’ SAC is couched in boilerplate language and no clear and concise allegations in the SAC of an intent by any specific moving Defendant to injure Plaintiffs during their one-week stay at the subject property, the court strikes any references for punitive damages. As for attorney fees, Plaintiffs have failed to pled any statutory or contractual basis for recovering attorneys’ fees.[5] Thus, the court strikes the MTS and leave to amend is denied for reasons stated above.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer is sustained without leave to amend and the MTS is granted without leave to amend.
