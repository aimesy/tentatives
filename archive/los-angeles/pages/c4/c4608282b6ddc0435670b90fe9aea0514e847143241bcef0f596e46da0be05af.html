DEPARTMENT 1 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS



Case Number: 23STCV06419    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: 1

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Jose Concepcion Rivera (Kick Law Firm, APC)

Responding Party:      Defendant Overhill Farms (CDF Labor Law LLP)

Ruling:                        Motion GRANTED on the condition that Plaintiff add an allegation to the FAC that “Plaintiffs have agreed to limit the scope of any motion for class certification to one class, which will be based on Covid-19 pre-shift screenings for non-exempt workers at Overhill Farms during the period from April 13, 2020 to May 2022.” 

 

This is a wage and hour putative class action which is related to two other actions, including Golden v. Jobsource, Inc. (Case No. 20STCV14536, Golden).  Plaintiff Jose Concepcion Rivera (Plaintiff) alleges that he and others were employed by defendants Overhill Farms, Inc., Jobsource Huntington Park, and other defendants, during which employment those defendants committed various violations of labor law.  Plaintiff moves for leave to file a first amended complaint (FAC).  Defendant Overhill Farms, Inc. (Defendant) opposes. 

 

This action was filed on March 23, 2023.  On December 19, 2025, the Court entered an order on Plaintiff and Defendant’s stipulation to continue the class certification deadline which recited that the parties were bound for mediation and that if the matter did not settle, Plaintiff could file a class certification, but that Plaintiff agreed to limit the motion to “one class, which will be based on Covid-19 pre-shift screenings for non-exempt workers at Overhill Farms during the period from April 13, 2020 to May 2022.”  (Court’s December 19, 2025 order, pp. 2-3.) 

 

In February 2026, Plaintiff filed doe amendments naming numerous additional defendants, including Precise Staffing Solutions LLC and Precise Personnel LLC (collectively, Precise Personnel).  Plaintiff now seeks leave to amend his complaint to add Martin Ramirez as a named plaintiff, who was allegedly employed by Precise Personnel (while Jose Concepcion Rivera was not). 

 

LEGAL STANDARDS

 

Although trial courts have broad discretion in allowing the amendment of any pleading before them¿(Huff v. Wilkins (2006) 138 Cal.App.4th 732, 746), “[t]he policy favoring amendment is so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified.” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1423 (Howard).)¿Indeed, when “no prejudice is shown to the adverse party, the liberal rule of allowance prevails.” (Higgins v. Del Faro (1981)¿123 Cal.App.3d 558, 564.) Thus, leave to amend may only properly be denied when: (1) the proposed amendment is futile; (2) the amending party has not exercised due diligence and has inexplicably delayed bringing the proposed amendment; or (3) the opposing party would otherwise be unfairly prejudiced by the amendment.¿(See¿Howard, supra, 184 Cal.App.4th at 1423;¿Huff v. Wilkins, supra,¿138 Cal.App.4th¿at¿746;¿Roemer v. Retail Credit Co. (1975) 44 Cal.App.3d 926, 939¿(1975).)

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

Defendant requests judicial notice of various records from this action, as well as a judgment in Zamora v. Overhill Farms, Inc., Case No. 19STCV10854.  These are all court records subject to judicial notice under Evid. Code § 452(d), and the request is GRANTED in full.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant contends that Plaintiff’s request for amendment is untimely, that the proposed complaint violates the terms of the stipulation, which the Court ordered, and that both Plaintiff and Mr. Ramirez lack standing to represent the class.

 

The Court rejects Defendant’s timeliness argument.  As set forth in the motion and reply, Plaintiff’s counsel deposed Defendant’s person most knowledgeable (PMK) in December 2025, at which the witness was asked about other staffing agencies that had supplied employees at Defendant’s worksite and the witness was improperly instructed not to answer.  When he was deposed in December 2025, Mr. Ramirez himself could not recall the agency other than JobSource for which he had worked, and Plaintiff promptly filed doe amendments naming Precise Personnel in February 2026 upon discovering their identities.  Plaintiff requested that Defendant stipulate to the addition of Mr. Ramirez shortly thereafter, and it is only because Defendant did not accept the stipulation that this motion became necessary.  Despite Defendant’s contention, there is no actual evidence that Plaintiff “has known since the inception of this case that Overhill used temp agencies – other than JobSource[.]”  While the motion is coming quite far into the case, it is nonetheless timely.

 

Defendant next contends that the class description in the FAC exceeds the scope of the class certification motion Plaintiff agreed to bring.  The FAC provides:

 

35. Proposed Class: “All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations.”

 

36. Proposed Sub-classes:

A. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations, who worked one or more shifts in excess of five (5) hours.

 

B. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations, who worked one or more shifts in excess of six (6) hours.

 

C. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations, who worked one or more shifts in excess of eight (8) hours.

 

 

D. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations, who worked one or more shifts in excess of three and one-half hours (3 ½), but less than or equal to six (6) hours.

 

E. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations, who worked one or more shifts in excess of six (6) hours, but less than or equal to ten (10) hours.

 

F. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the relevant statute of limitations, who separated their employment from Defendants.

 

G. All persons who are employed or have been employed by Defendants in the

State of California as hourly, non-exempt workers during the period of the

relevant statute of limitations, who received pay for one or more pay periods.

 

(FAC, ¶¶ 35-36.)  As Defendant notes, the parties stipulated and the Court ordered that “Plaintiff agrees to limit the scope of his Motion to one class, which will be based on Covid-19 pre-shift screenings for non-exempt workers at Overhill Farms during the period from April 13, 2020 to May 2022.”  (Court’s December 19, 2025 Order on Stipulation, pp. 2-3.)  The Court agrees that an inconsistency between the class description and the stipulation could needlessly confuse the record, but that is not reason to deny the motion or set Plaintiff to the task of redrafting the complaint.  Rather, Plaintiff should simply add an allegation that “Plaintiffs [i.e. Plaintiff and Mr. Ramirez] have agreed to limit the scope of any motion for class certification to one class, which will be based on Covid-19 pre-shift screenings for non-exempt workers at Overhill Farms during the period from April 13, 2020 to May 2022.”  That should eliminate any cause for concern. 

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiff and Mr. Ramirez each lack standing to represent a class of Defendant’s employees, because Plaintiff was only employed by JobSource and Mr. Ramirez was only employed by JobSource and Precise Personnel.  This is a merits argument, essentially arguing that the allegations of the FAC that Defendant, Precise Personnel, and JobSource exercised control over the putative class’s wages, hours, and working conditions are unmeritorious.  (See FAC, ¶¶ 12-15.)  A motion for leave to amend is not the venue for testing the merits of disputed allegations.  (See Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1048 [even where proposed amendment states novel legal theory, preferable practice is to permit amendment and test the sufficiency of the allegations by further proceedings].)  Whether Defendant employed Plaintiff or Mr. Ramirez is an issue for another day.  Likewise, Defendant’s single-sentence contention that the claims are barred by a class settlement in the Zamora case is not germane to this motion.  If indeed the Zamora settlement forecloses some or all of this action, that is a matter for a motion for summary judgment or adjudication, not for the Court to wade into now.

 

CONCLUSION

 

For the foregoing reasons, the Court will GRANT the motion, on the condition that Plaintiff adds an allegation to the FAC that “Plaintiffs have agreed to limit the scope of any motion for class certification to one class, which will be based on Covid-19 pre-shift screenings for non-exempt workers at Overhill Farms during the period from April 13, 2020 to May 2022.”  Plaintiff to give notice. 


Case Number: 25STCV22603    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: 1

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Amanda McGough (Employees First Labor Law P.C.)

Responding Party:      Defendants S&J Towing Service, Inc. and William Schlick (O’Hagan Meyer, LLP)

Ruling:                        Motion to approve Belaire-West notice is GRANTED.  No sanctions are awarded.

 

This is a wage-and-hour putative class action.  Plaintiff Amanda McGough (Plaintiff) alleges that she and others were employed by defendants S&J Towing Service, Inc., Jackqueline J. Schlick, William Schlick, and Howard Schlick, during which employment the defendants committed various violations of labor law.  Plaintiff moves the Court to approve a Belaire-West notice to the putative class to allow class discovery to continue.  Defendants S&J Towing Service, Inc. and William Schlick (Defendants) oppose.

 

Plaintiff contends that Defendants have been “radio-silent” about the Belaire-West notice, despite Plaintiff’s attempts to confer with them about it, and seeks the Court’s intervention.  Defendants respond that Plaintiff failed to properly meet and confer, that the motion is premature, that no discovery is outstanding to which the Belaire-West process could pertain, and that the proposals by Plaintiff are unjustified.

 

The record discloses the following.  On August 12, 2025, less than two weeks after the case was filed and shortly after Defendants had been sent a notice and acknowledgment of receipt (so, before formal service), Plaintiff’s counsel e-mailed Defendants’ counsel to meet and confer with respect to the proposed Belaire-West notice.  (LaCour Decl., Ex. D.)  Plaintiff’s counsel followed up on August 21, 2025, followed by a “final reminder” on August 25, 2025.  (Ibid.)  That day, Defendants’ counsel responded that the Belaire-West procedure “seems premature” as the case was not at issue and “there has been no class-related discovery upon which the Belaire-West process would serve as the informal compromise between parties.”  (Ibid.) 

 

On January 15, 2026, the parties filed a joint status conference statement which made no mention of Belaire-West and indeed, Plaintiff represented that “Discovery has not yet begun.”  (January 15, 2026 Joint Status Conference Statement, p. 4.)  On March 19, 2026, the Court held the initial status conference and ordered that the parties meet and confer on the Belaire-West notice to be sent to the class, and for Defendants to respond to the complaint.  (Court’s March 19, 2026 Minute Order.)  Defendants filed their answer on April 17, 2026. 

 

On April 15, 2026, nearly a month after the initial status conference, Plaintiff’s counsel resumed discussion with Defendants’ counsel about the Belaire-West notice.  (LaCour Decl., Ex. F.)  Defendants’ counsel did not respond.  (Ibid.)  Plaintiff’s counsel sent another e-mail on May 7, 2026, three weeks later, following up and stating that “[i]f we do not hear back from Defendants regarding the above documents by COB tomorrow, May 8, 2026, we will move for approval of the Belaire-West notice and seek sanctions.”  (Ibid.)  No response was forthcoming, and this motion followed on May 21, 2026.  That same day, Plaintiff propounded her first set of special interrogatories.  (Spiewak Decl., ¶ 9.)  Defendant’s counsel resumed responding to e-mails on or about June 4 (per Christopher Cho’s e-mail).  (Id., Ex. A, p. 7 [“Hi Lisa, I saw you emailed John on 6/16 and may have missed my 6/4 email below.”])

 

Defendant’s counsel attempted to confer with Plaintiff’s counsel by phone concerning the discovery before responses were due but did not receive a response.  (Id., ¶ 10.)  Defendants eventually served responses objecting on privacy grounds, among others.  (Id., ¶ 11; Evans Decl., ¶ 6.)  The parties finally connected by phone on July 1, 2026, during which Defendants’ counsel contended that the class definition was overly broad and that Defendants would not proceed with the Belaire-West process unless the class definition was amended.  (Evans Decl., ¶ 10.) 

 

Frankly, this motion could have been, and should have been, resolved in an informal discovery conference.  Neither party’s conduct is commendable.  Plaintiff’s counsel leapt prematurely into the Belaire-West process, demanding cooperation and threatening motion practice before discovery had opened, and indeed, before Defendants had even appeared.  Defendants responded that a Belaire-West notice would be premature before any discovery had been served, a position Plaintiff seemingly accepted by abandoning the issue until the initial status conference seven months later and not raising it in the pre-conference statement. Once the Court ordered Belaire-West to proceed, Defendants failed to respond to Plaintiff’s counsel for over a month.  While Defendants’ original position that Belaire-West requires outstanding discovery was a reasonable one, it is not reasonable to fail to make any response to requests to begin the process after the Court ordered it.  Had Defendants reiterated their prior position, the parties could have discussed the matter informally with the Court and resolved it.

 

The Court agrees with Defendants’ initial position that Belaire-West notice requires some outstanding discovery.  After all, the Belaire-West process is intended to balance the privacy rights of nonparty putative class members against the need of plaintiff class representatives to obtain disclosure of their contact information.  (See Belaire-West Landscape, Inc. v. Superior Court (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 554, 561.)  Where the obligation to disclose that contact information has not arisen (because no discovery has been served), Belaire-West is not required, although it is advisable to prepare the ground by finalizing the terms of a notice before the inevitable request for class contact information is made. 

 

This motion was filed before Plaintiff’s discovery was served, and those requests are not really at issue at the moment.  In the interest of putting this dispute to rest rather than preserving it for another day, the Court observes the following.  Although Defendants did not challenge Plaintiff’s class definition and instead answered the complaint, the class definition is indeed very confusing.  It is:

 

A. Definition of the CLASS PLAINTIFFS

 

49. The CLASS consists of: all persons who were employed by Defendants, within the Relevant Time Period as a member of Defendants’ CLASS PLAINTIFFS or with any similar title who were non-exempt or misclassified exempt employees and are owed unpaid minimum wages, unpaid overtime wages, penalties for noncompliant and/or missed meal and rest breaks, were provided inaccurate wage statements, and were not timely paid during and/or following their employment with Defendants.

 

(Complaint, p. 10.)

 

This class definition is circular both in its self-referential use of “CLASS PLAINTIFFS” and in its inclusion of employees who “are owed unpaid…wages,” which makes it difficult to ascertain who is in the class and who is not.  Plaintiff’s discovery turns on this definition.  (See Evans Decl., Ex. 1, p. 5.)  This is a legitimate issue.  However, the Belaire-West notice can still notify all employees who could potentially be in a class that their contact information might be turned over, and once the parties resolve their differences over the class definition, the process can continue apace.  There is no need to hold up the entire process based on a pleadings issue that Defendants failed to raise until now. 

 

Defendants contend that there is “no evidence or basis to support the demand for third-party information regarding employees who worked in entirely different positions and who never worked remotely or from the location Plaintiff has worked.”  This appears to be a preemptive class certification argument, i.e., that Plaintiff is not similarly situated to any employees who worked in different positions or locations than Plaintiff worked.  This is not a valid basis to oppose a Belaire-West procedure as to all employees.  Belaire-West is not a final determination of the size or validity of the class; it is a mechanism for resolving privacy objections as to the disclosure of potential, not official or undeniable, class members.  It cannot be conclusively known whether and to what extent Plaintiff is similarly situated to other employees unless Plaintiff can conduct discovery about those employees, which may properly include their contact information.  While the Court notes Defendants’ position that Plaintiff is not similarly situated, that is not a ground to resist the production of employee contact information. 

 

For the foregoing reasons, the Court is inclined to order the proposed Belaire-West notice be sent to all current and former employees of Defendants for whom Defendants have contact information.  The parties ought to split the cost of the notice, which is another standard term that should not require the Court’s involvement.  The notices should be amended to state that they are not postage-paid, or if any party would prefer them postage-paid, that party should bear the cost for the postage.  Defendants have not articulated any real issue with Plaintiff’s proposed third-party administrator, CPT Group, but if Defendants have an actual objection to that administrator, the parties should solicit competing bids and choose a mutually-agreeable administrator. 

 

Both parties request sanctions.  Neither is entitled to them.  Plaintiff filed this motion without having served any discovery to which a Belaire-West notice could pertain, despite Defendants raising that issue over a year ago.  Defendants did not promptly respond to Plaintiff and once they did, took the position that discovery need not be complied with because the class definition was overbroad, despite never having challenged the class definition and instead simply answering the complaint.  Neither party attempted to contact the Court for an informal discovery conference, instead forcing the Court to take up time in its motion calendar to address what is, in essence, a routine issue. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

For the foregoing reasons, the motion is GRANTED as discussed above.  No sanctions are awarded.  Plaintiff to give notice.
