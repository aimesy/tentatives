Liebregt v. World Courier Inc., et al.

Case No.: 24STCV05865

TENTATIVE RULING

Grant Final Approval and award/approve the following: (1) $316,666.367 for attorney fees to Class Counsel; (2) $23,275.94 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) $7,500 for an enhancement awards to the class representative; (4) $7,050 for claims administration costs; and (5) $32,500 (65% of $50,000 PAGA penalty) to the LWDA.

The Court orders class counsel to file a final report summarizing all distributions made pursuant to the approved settlement, supported by declaration. The Court will set a non-appearance date for submission of a final report for _________ ,2026. 

 

BACKGROUND

This is a wage and hour class action. Defendants operate a drug wholesale and distribution company that operates in the State of California, including the county of Los Angeles, where Plaintiff worked. Plaintiff worked for Defendants in California from approximately September 2013 to November 2022 as a non-exempt employee, paid on an hourly basis.

On March 8, 2024, Plaintiff commenced this Action by filing a Complaint alleging eight (8) causes of action against Defendants World Courier Inc., Cencora, Inc., AmerisourceBergen Drug Corporation, AmerisourceBergen Services Corporation, and AmerisourceBergen Specialty Group, LLC for: (1) Unfair Competition in Violation of Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200 et seq.; (2) Failure to Pay Minimum Wages in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 1194, 1197 & 1197.1; (3) Failure to Pay Overtime Wages in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 510, et seq.; (4) Failure to Provide Required Meal Periods in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code §§226.7 & 512 and the Applicable IWC Wage Order; (5) Failure to Provide Required Rest Periods in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 226.7 & 512 and the Applicable IWC Wage Order; (6) Failure to Reimburse Employees for Required Expenses in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code § 2802; (7) Failure to Provide Accurate Itemized Statements in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code § 226; and (8) Failure to Provide Wages When Due in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 201, 202, and 203.

On April 15, 2024, Defendants removed the Action to the United States District Court Central District of California, Case Number 2:24-cv-03051-JAK-SK.

On October 10, 2024, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Violations with the LWDA and served the same on Defendants. On December 18, 2024, Plaintiff filed an Amended Notice of Violations, adding Defendant World Customs Brokerage, Inc., and served the same on Defendants.

On January 21, 2025, this Action was remanded from the United States District Court Central District of California to the Superior Court of California, County of Los Angeles.

On February 7, 2025, Defendants Cencora, Inc., AmerisourceBergen Drug Corporation, AmerisourceBergen Services Corporation, and Amerisource Specialty Group, LLC were dismissed from the Action without prejudice.

On February 27, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint, adding Defendant World Customs Brokerage, Inc. as a defendant and adding a ninth cause of action for alleged Violations of the California Labor Code’s Private Attorneys Geneal Act [Labor Code §§ 2698, et seq.].

Counsel represents that prior to mediation, Plaintiff obtained, through informal discovery, Defendants’ pertinent wage and hour policies, including but not limited to, meal and rest period policies, overtime policies and timekeeping policies, Defendant’s Employee Handbook, and 100% of the time and payroll records for Class, which was reviewed by the statisticians and economists at Berger Consulting Group (“BCG”),

On November 18, 2024, the Parties participated in an all-day mediation presided over by Monique Ngo-Bonnici, Esq. which led to this Agreement to settle the Action.

A copy of the fully  executed Settlement Agreement  was filed with the Court on May 28, 2025, attached to the Declaration of Jean-Claude Lapuyade (“Lapuyade Decl.”) as Exhibit 1 ISO Preliminary Approval as Exhibit 1. 

On July 23, 2024, the court issued a checklist of items for counsel to address and continued preliminary approval. In response, on February 14, 2024, counsel filed a fully executed Settlement Agreement attached to the Supplemental Declaration of Jean-Claude Lapuyade (Lapuyade Supp. Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval as Exhibit 1. 

Preliminary Approval was granted on December 2, 2025. Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered. (See Declaration of Norma Ayala (“Ayala Decl.”).)

 Now before the Court is the motion for final approval of the settlement agreement.

ANALYSIS OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

A.     Does a presumption of fairness exist? 

The Court preliminarily found in its Orders on December 2, 2025, that the presumption of fairness should be applied.  No facts have come to the Court’s attention that would alter that preliminary conclusion.  Accordingly, the settlement is entitled to a presumption of fairness as set forth in the preliminary approval order.

The settlement was preliminarily found to be fair, adequate and reasonable.  Notice has now been given to the Class and the LWDA.

C.     Attorney Fees and Costs

Class Counsel request $316,666.67 in fees and litigation costs and expenses in the amount of $23,275.94. (Motion ISO Fees, 15:4-8; 13:12-15.)

The Settlement provides for attorney's fees up to $316,666.67 and costs of $30,000 (Settlement Agreement, ¶3.2.2); the class was provided notice of the requested awards and none objected. (Ayala Decl., ¶12 and Exhibit A thereto.)

The following fee split has been agreed to herein: 50% to JCL Law Firm, APC, and 50% to Zakay Law Group, APLC.  (Lapuyade Decl. ISO Preliminary Approval, ¶45.)

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba at 254.)  Here, class counsel requests attorney fees using the percentage method as cross-checked by loadstar. (Motion ISO Fees, pgs. 2-13.)

 The fee request represents 33% of the gross settlement amount which is the average generally awarded in class actions.  See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 (“Empirical studies show that, regardless of whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”).

            Class Counsel is requesting $23,275.94 in costs which is less than the settlement cap of $30,000. (Ibid.) The costs seem reasonable and necessary to litigation.

Based on the above, the recommendation is to award $316,666.67 for fees and $23,275.94 for litigation costs.

 

D.    Incentive Award to Class Representative

The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $10,000 for an incentive award to the class representative. (Settlement Agreement, ¶3.2.1)

Plaintiff Liebregt’s contributions to this litigation include, and are not limited to, spending 35 to 70 hours  on the following: obtaining counsel, gathering documents, having numerous conversations with counsel, remaining telephonically available for mediation, and reviewing the settlement. (Liebregt’ Decl., ¶¶5, 11-12.)

The court notes that the above actions are commendable, yet not exceptional. Based on the above, the recommendation is to award an enhancement award in the amount of $7,500.

 

E.     Claims Administration Costs

The claims administrator requests $7,050 for the costs of administering the settlement. (Ayala Decl., ¶19.)

The $7,050 request is equal to the $7,050 maximum amount estimated at preliminary approval and disclosed in the notice to class members, to which there were no objections. (Id. at ¶12 and Exhibit A thereto.)

Based on all the work performed by the Claims Administrator, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $7,050.
