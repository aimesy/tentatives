I.            INTRODUCTION

On July 6, 2026, defendant Anna Grace (“Defendant”) filed this motion to quash service of summons. Defendant argues that Plaintiff failed to demonstrate that she exercised “reasonable diligence” in attempting service of process by other means before filing an application for publication.

II.          LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 415.50 states, in relevant part:

(a) A summons may be served by publication if upon affidavit it appears to the satisfaction of the court in which the action is pending that the party to be served cannot with reasonable diligence be served in another manner specified in this article and that []:

(1) A cause of action exists against the party upon whom service is to be made or he or she is a necessary or proper party to the action.

 

When a defendant moves to quash service of summons, the burden of proving the existence of jurisdiction by a preponderance of the admissible evidence rests on the plaintiff. (Summers v. McClanahan (2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 403, 413; Floveyor Int’l, Ltd. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 789, 793.) Compliance with the statutory procedures for service of process is essential to establish personal jurisdiction, and defects in service render any resulting default or judgment void, not merely voidable. (Dill v. Berquist Construction Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1441.)

III.        DISCUSSION

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s application for publication, which was approved on August 3, 2022 by the Honorable Colin P. Leis, should not have been approved because it was based on an identical application that Judge Leis previously denied on June 27, 2022, and there was no “renewed judicial evaluation of whether reasonable diligence had, by then, actually been exercised.” (Motion, p. 7.) Defendant contends that the declaration executed by process server Gregory Lacap on March 18, 2022, is insufficient because it merely states that he “conducted a records search on the Name [sic] ‘Anna Grace’” and “crossed referenced [sic] the last known address and [sic] yielded no results on subject found.” Defendant further argues that no reasonable diligence was used to serve her by means other than personal service because Plaintiff only attempted to serve her by mail in June of 2022 at addresses that she already knew were inaccurate.

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that several attempts to effect personal service, combined with a records search and service by mailing to two different addresses is sufficient to demonstrate “reasonable diligence.” Plaintiff points out that Defendant’s sudden availability “years later on the eve of trial” is suspect and should be viewed as evidence that Defendant was evading service, necessitating the use of service by publication. (Opp., p. 10.) Plaintiff also suggests that the filing of the Second Amended Complaint was dispositive in Judge Leis’ decision to grant the second application for publication because it “provided the Court with more of a complete basis to assess the necessity of [Defendant] as a party.” (Opp., p. 9.) This statement is incorrect because the Court has reviewed the complaints operative at the time each application was submitted and the allegations against Defendant did not change to deem Defendant more or less of a “necessary party.” The Court also notes that Plaintiff incorrectly states that the applications were reviewed by two different judicial officers. (Opp., p. 10.)  

Defendant did not file a reply brief and the Court notes that Defendant offered no declaration in support of her moving papers explaining where she was located or whether and how she could have been located during the period of service using means other than those employed by Plaintiff. Defendant also does not explain how she first learned of the lawsuit or how she was contacted by defense counsel. There is no evidence in the record showing why the first application was denied by Judge Leis and the Court declines to speculate as to the reasoning behind the decisions of the previous judicial officer assigned to this case.

IV.        CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the motion to quash is DENIED.

I.            PLAINTIFF’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL AND DEEM ADMITTED

On May 28, 2026, plaintiff Layla Leng (“Plaintiff”) filed discovery motions for orders deeming admitted the Requests for Admissions, Set One, served on defendants New Royal Inn, Inc., Jung Chen, and Helen Chen (collectively, “Defendants”) and compelling Defendants to serve verified responses, without objections, to Form Interrogatories (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), and Request for Production of Documents (Set One) within 30 calendar days.

On July 10, 2026, Defendants filed opposition briefs stating that the motions are moot because responses were served the same day and that the delay was caused by “an attorney vacancy in [counsel’s] office.” (Motion, Green Decl., ¶ 3.)

Plaintiff did not file a reply brief disputing that responses were served.

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motions are DENIED as moot.

II.          DEFENDANTS’ MOTION FOR RELIEF FROM WAIVER OF OBJECTIONS RE: REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION

Defendants filed a motion on July 10, 2026, requesting relief from their waiver of objections with respect to their responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production (Set One) pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300(b). Defendants contend that the failure to serve verified responses on time was due to a staffing vacancy. (Motion, pp. 2-3.) The document requests were served on July 23, 2025, and Defendants argue that the delay in serving timely responses was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect because “[a]n attorney vacancy affected the timing of verification and finalization of responses.” (Motion, p. 5.) This vacancy has since purportedly been filled with the retention of new lead counsel and Defendants claim they acted diligently once new counsel assumed responsibility. (Motion, pp. 5-6.)

Defense counsel Michael Green submits a declaration that is devoid of any dates and fails to identify any attorney whose departure caused the purported “vacancy” that caused Plaintiff’s discovery responses to go unanswered for nearly a year. Defendants therefore fail to show that their failure to serve responses were the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect. Also, as Plaintiff notes, Defendants did not assert any objections when they eventually served their responses. Furthermore, the Court disagrees that Plaintiff has not been prejudiced by the delay. Defendants state in their discovery responses that responsive documents “no longer exist” without stating when they existed or were destroyed, or if any other copies of the records exist elsewhere.  

III.        CONCLUSION

Plaintiff’s three discovery motions are DENIED as moot. No sanctions are imposed because the notice of motion does not identify the party, attorney, or person to be sanctioned or the type of sanction sought.  

Defendants’ motion for relief from the waiver of objections is DENIED.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT CITY OF ALHAMBRA’S MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S FURTHER RESPONSES TO FIRST SET OF REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION

 

Dept. 3

         Defendant City of Alhambra (“Defendant”) moves for an order compelling plaintiff Anne Cho (“Plaintiff”) to serve further responses to Requests for Production Nos. 30-34, 37, and 38. This action arises from a slip and fall that occurred on the stairs of a parking structure.

Defendant’s document requests seek: (1) text messages that Plaintiff sent and/or received in the hour before and the hour after the alleged incident as well as Plaintiff’s cell phone records for the day of the incident, (2) all communications she has had with any person regarding the incident, and (3) all documents that relate to all other accidents Plaintiff has ben involved in since the incident.

On July 24, 2026, Plaintiff served amended responses, but Defendant contends that the motion is not moot because her responses to Requests Nos. 37 and 38 are deficient and a further response should be compelled. (Reply, p. 10.)

Request No. 37 seeks Plaintiff’s communications with any person regarding the incident at any time. Plaintiff’s most recently-served response contains a Code-compliant statement of her inability to comply with the document request; Plaintiff specifies that she cannot comply with the request because she does not believe any non-privileged responsive document has ever existed.

Request No. 38 seeks documents relating to any and all accidents she has been involved in since the incident on February 7, 2024, including motor vehicle accidents and slip-and-falls. Defendant argues that Plaintiff is being evasive because she states that no responsive documents exist, other than those she already produced regarding a dog bite and an accident with a fitness pole, since “there has been no other injury accident since the [incident].”

Defendant argues that the request for production seeks documents regarding all accidents, not only those accidents in which she was injured. Defense counsel also states in his declaration that Defendant has obtained a report showing that Plaintiff was involved in two motor vehicle collisions in April and December 2024, which were not disclosed in Plaintiff’s discovery responses and demonstrate a need to obtain information about those collisions.

The Court agrees that Plaintiff’s response improperly limits the scope of Defendant’s request to accidents she claims she was not injured in, however, the request for “all documents” and “all accidents” is too broad. Therefore, the Court orders Plaintiff to serve a further response, within 20 days, agreeing to produce all documents relating to the two motor vehicle accidents that took place on April 9 and December 25, 2024, which are relevant because Plaintiff is claiming cervical and lumbar spine injuries and these accidents took place after the incident and before she underwent surgery.

The Court also agrees that sanctions are warranted. Plaintiff’s initial responses to Requests Nos. 30-34 were evasive and her attempts to meet and confer were not in good faith. Plaintiff objected to requests on the grounds that they invaded her right to privacy, but now she claims that the documents those requests sought never even existed. Had Plaintiff simply conducted a reasonable search and diligent inquiry at the start and stated that those documents did not exist and could not be produced, motion practice could have been avoided and Defendant would not have had to incur the costs of meeting and conferring and bringing this motion.

Accordingly, sanctions are imposed on Plaintiff and counsel of record, jointly and severally, in the reduced amount of $1,260, consisting of 4.2 hours at defense counsel’s hourly rate of $300, payable within 20 days.
