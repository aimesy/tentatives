Plaintiffs’ Motion to Set Aside Dismissal is DENIED.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

On April 8, 2019, Plaintiffs Ofelia Fernandez and Carlos Fernandez (“Plaintiffs”) purchased a 2018 Ford F-150 (the “Vehicle”). (Complaint ¶ 8.) The Vehicle was defective. (Complaint ¶ 10.)

On May 10, 2023, Plaintiffs filed a complaint against Defendants Ford Motor Company (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 10, alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of Song- Beverly Act - breach of express warranty; (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act - breach of implied warranty; and (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act section 1793.2.

On May 23, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a notice of settlement of entire case.

On July 21, 2025, the court dismissed the case without prejudice and retained jurisdiction under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. (07/21/25 Minute Order.)

On August 12, 2026, Plaintiffs filed this motion to set aside the dismissal.

 

On August 19, 2026, Plaintiffs’ motion for attorney fees (filed on June 9, 2026) was denied as untimely.

 

On August 18, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition to the motion to set aside the dismissal.

 

On September 9, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a reply.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD¿

 

Code of Civil Procedure (CCP), section 473, subdivision (b) provides for two distinct types of relief—commonly differentiated as “discretionary” and “mandatory”—from certain prior actions or proceedings in the trial court.  (Luri v. Greenwald (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 1119, 1124 (Luri).)   

 

The discretionary portion of section 473, subdivision (b) provides: “The court may, upon terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Under the discretionary standard specific facts must be demonstrating “mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect[,]” (CCP § 473(b) [italics added]), and “must show a satisfactory excuse for his default, and he must show diligence in making the motion after discovery of the default.”  (Hopkins & Carley v. Gens¿(2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1410.) 

 

The mandatory portion of section 473 requires the court to, “whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against his or her client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

 

III.           DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiffs move under CCP section 473(b) to set aside the July 21, 2025 dismissal to the extent it forecloses Plaintiffs’ motion for attorneys’ fees.

This motion is untimely. This case was dismissed on July 21, 2025. This motion was filed August 12, 2026, more than a year after the dismissal.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subdivision (b).) The Court does not have authority under CCP section 473(b) to excuse Plaintiff’s noncompliance with the six-month time limit. (See Arambula v. Union Carbide Corp. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 333, 345.) Thus, the motion under CCP section 473(b) cannot be considered.

In reply, Plaintiffs attempt to recharacterize the order they are seeking to set aside. Now, they seek to set aside the Court’s minute order denying the motion for attorney fees. But that order was made on August 19, 2026, a week before the instant motion was filed. Thus, Plaintiffs could not have sought to set aside that order when this motion was filed. Further, CCP section 473(b) is not a vehicle to relitigate a ruling. (Cradduck v. Hilton Domestic Operating Co. (2025) 112 Cal. App. 5th 284.)

 

CRC Rule 3.1702(d)- Good Cause

Plaintiffs also argue that the Court should extend the time to file the fee motion for good cause under CRC Rule 3.1702(d).

“For good cause, the trial judge may extend the time for filing a motion for attorney’s fees in the absence of a stipulation or for a longer period than allowed by stipulation.” (CRC Rule 3.1702(d).)

“A court may grant a request for extension of time to file a motion for attorney’s fees even if the motion is not filed until after the deadline for filing an attorney’s fees motion under Rule 3.1702.” (Robinson v. U-Haul Co. of California (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 304, 326.) Good cause is “equated to a good reason for a party’s failure to perform that specific requirement [of the statute] from which he seeks to be excused.” (Id. at 327.) The “good cause” standard of Rule 3.1702(d) gives trial courts “considerably more latitude in ruling on an extension of time to file an attorney’s fees motion” than granting relief from a judgment, dismissal, or order under CCP section 473(b). (Ibid.)

Plaintiffs argue that the law was unsettled as to when the time to move for attorneys’ fees is triggered in a CCP sections 998 and section 664.6 settlement that defers dismissal and retains jurisdiction pending performance. Plaintiffs argue that no published decision had resolved that precise question until Hatlevig v. General Motors LLC (2026) 118 Cal.App.5th 644 was certified for publication on February 17, 2026.

 

However, the motion for attorney fees has already been denied. Thus, the request to extend the time to file the motion is now moot.

 

Further, the court already denied Plaintiffs’ request to extend the deadline to file the motion for good cause in the ruling for the motion for attorney fees. Plaintiffs have not sought reconsideration of that order and merely move for the same relief again, which is improper. They were required to bring a timely motion for reconsideration and show new or different facts or circumstances or law, and a valid reason for why they were not offered earlier. A court acts in excess of jurisdiction when it grants a motion to reconsider that is not based upon “new or different facts, circumstances or law.” (Gilberd v. AC Transit (1995) 32 Cal.App.4th 1494, 1499.)¿Motions for reconsideration are restricted to circumstances where a party offers the Court some fact or circumstance not previously considered, and some valid reason for not offering it earlier.¿ (Id.) Plaintiffs do not show any new or different facts, or circumstances or law, or any valid reason for not offering it earlier.¿¿¿¿

 

In any event, Plaintiffs’ argument for good cause rests on the incorrect premise that the law was unsettled at the time. CRC Rule 3.1702 existed when the Court dismissed this case. As the Court of Appeal stated in Hatlevig, supra,118 Cal. App. 5th at 649, “Rule 3.1702 of the California Rules of Court governs motions for attorney fees in civil cases.” (Id., rule 3.1702(a); Carpenter v. Jack in the Box Corp. (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 454.) Hatlevig simply affirmed existing case law applying the Rule. (Hatlevig, supra, 118 Cal.App.5th at 649 [citing Sanabria v. Embry (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 422; Catlin Ins. Co., Inc. v. Danko Meredith Law Firm, Inc. (2022) 73 Cal.App.5th 764.) The issue in Hatlevig was whether the date specified in the notice or the date of the minute order qualified as the dismissal date, not whether the time for a fee motion starts running from the date of dismissal. (See Hatlevig, supra, 118 Cal.App.5th at 649 [“The main issue in dispute in this appeal is what event started the clock running.”].) Thus, there is no good cause shown.

 

Plaintiffs argue that neither Sanabria or Catlin involved a dismissal without prejudice with retained jurisdiction and an agreement that fees would be set by later motion. However, Hatlevig also did not involve those circumstances. Regardless, “Rule 3.1702 of the California Rules of Court governs motions for attorney fees in civil cases.” (Hatlevig, supra,118 Cal. App. 5th at 649; Id., rule 3.1702(a); Carpenter v. Jack in the Box Corp. (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 454, 462.) Thus, Rule 3.1702 applied in this circumstance as well, even absent Hatlevig.

 

IV.           CONCLUSION 

¿ 

Plaintiffs’ Motion is DENIED in full.

COURT’S [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures pursuant to

Code of Civil Procedure §871.26 and Request for

Monetary Sanctions

 

[THE FOLLOWING IS A TENTATIVE RULING IN THE ABOVE CASE]:  

Granted in part and Denied in part

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

Action for violations of Song Beverly Warranty Act.  Plaintiffs filed a complaint on January 24, 2025 against FCA US, LLC and Rydell Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram.  The operative complaint is the First Amended Complaint filed on January 27, 2026 alleging (1) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2(d); (2) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2(b); (3) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2(a)(3); (4) breach of the implied warranty of merchantability; and (5) fraudulent inducement. 

 

On June 15, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Monetary Sanctions. On September Defendant filed and served an opposition on September 1, 2026.   Despite this, on September 8, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Non-Opposition. The court also was under the mistaken belief that there was no opposition, but upon being informed of the existence of the opposition the matter was trailed one day from September 15, 2026 to September 16, 2026.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD¿

“Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).”  (CCP §871.26(b).)

“The defendant or manufacturer shall provide the following documents to all other parties pursuant to the timelines prescribed in subdivision (b): (1) Copy of or access to a version of the owner's manual for a motor vehicle of the same make, model, and year. (2) Any warranties issued in conjunction with the sale of the motor vehicle. (3) Sample brochures published for the motor vehicle. (4) The motor vehicle's original invoice, if any, to the selling dealer. (5) Sales or lease agreement, if the manufacturer is in possession. (6) Motor vehicle information reports, including build documentation, component information, and delivery details. (7) Entire warranty transaction history for the motor vehicle. (8) Listing of required field actions applicable to the motor vehicle. (9) Published technical service bulletins (”TSBs”)1 for the same make, model, and year reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle. (10) Published information service bulletins (“ISBs”) for the same make, model, and year reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle. (11) Records relating to communications between the manufacturer or dealership and the owner or lessee of the motor vehicle, including those related to repair orders or claims involving the motor vehicle. (12) Warranty policies and procedure manuals. (13) Service manuals reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle. (14) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, all call recordings of pre-suit communications with the consumer available at the time of service of the complaint. (15) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, the manufacturer's written statement of policies and procedures used to evaluate customer requests for restitution or replacement pursuant to “Lemon Law” claims. (16) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, any nonprivileged, prelitigation evaluation. (17) Any warranty extensions or modifications issued by the manufacturer on the motor vehicle.”  (CCP §871.26(h).) 

“Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: (1) A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff's attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).”  (CCP §871.26(j).) 

 

III.           DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiffs ask that the Court order Defendant FCA US LLC to comply with its mandatory initial disclosure obligations under CCP §871.26.  Plaintiffs ask that Defendant produce all documents required under CCP §871.26(h)(6)-(10), (12) and (13) with verifications.  Plaintiffs also request sanctions in the amount of $2,500 against Defendant FCA US LLC and its counsel.

 

Defendant filed a demurrer to the original complaint on April 14, 2025, a demurrer to the FAC on February 26, 2026 and an answer on April 22, 2026.  Although Defendant has produced some documents, Plaintiff claims Defendant has not yet served initial disclosures with verifications required by section 871.26(h)(6)-(10), (12) and (13).  (Rucker Dec., ¶4.) 

 

Although Plaintiff filed a notice of non-opposition on September 8, 2026, Defendant filed and served an opposition on September 1, 2026.  Defendant’s opposition objects to Plaintiff’s attempt to obtain verified responses pursuant to CCP §871.26 and additional remedies wholly outside the scope of the statute.  Defendant argues sanctions and relief under section 871.26 are unwarranted because it is undisputed that Defendant produced documents pursuant to section 871.26.  Defendant argues section 871.26 does not require verified responses, nor are sanctions warranted merely because Plaintiff believes there are more responsive documents that were not produced in the initial disclosure.

 

Plaintiff fails to cite any authority authorizing the Court to compel initial disclosures under section 871.26 or any relief other than sanctions.  Plaintiff also fails to cite any applicable authority requiring verification of the initial disclosure under section 871.26.  Plaintiff cites to CCP §2016.090, which applies to initial disclosures in civil discovery as detailed in that section.  Section 2016.090 does not apply to the initial disclosures under section 871.26 on its face, nor does Plaintiff cite to any case law applying section 2016.090 to disclosures under section 871.26.  Plaintiff’s motion to compel verified initial disclosures is therefore denied. 

 

However, Plaintiff also requests sanctions in the amount of $2,500.  Section 871.26 subdivision (j) specifically authorizes mandatory sanctions for failure to provide the required disclosures, unless the non-complying party shows good cause.  Pursuant to subdivision (j)(1), a defense attorney who fails to comply with section 871.26 must be sanctioned in the amount of $2,500.  Plaintiff establishes non-compliance and is entitled to an order imposing sanctions against defense counsel.

 

Section 871.26(h) provides, “[t]he defendant or manufacturer shall provide the following documents to all other parties pursuant to the timelines prescribed in subdivision (b)….”  Defendant was therefore required to provide all the documents in subdivisions (6)-(17) with its initial disclosure under section 871.26.  Plaintiff claims Defendant did not produce responsive documents to categories (h)(6)-(10), (12) and (13).  Plaintiff details in its briefing the categories of documents required under subdivision (h) that were not included in the initial disclosure.  (Rucker, Dec., ¶4; Motion, pp. 8-14.) 

 

In order to avoid sanctions, Defendant was required to establish either that it produced documents in response to subsections (h)(6)-(10), (12) or (13) or good cause failing doing so.  (CCP §871.26(j).)  Defendant argues any disclosure at all is sufficient to avoid sanctions.  Defendant is incorrect.  Sanctions must be awarded if the Defendant did not comply with the disclosure requirements under subdivision (h).  Plaintiff has established that Defendant did not comply. 

 

Upon this showing, the burden shifted to Defendant to present some evidence that it complied or that good cause existed for its noncompliance.  Defendant fails to submit evidence of either.  Defendant only argues that Plaintiff’s requested relief exceeded the scope of section 871.26 and its partial, unverified disclosures were sufficient to avoid sanctions.  Defendant argues it acted in good faith and engaged in meet and confer efforts with Plaintiff.  Defendant fails to cite any authority holding that these facts constitute good cause for failure to provide full disclosures pursuant to subdivision (h), nor does Defendant provide a declaration attesting to the meet and confer efforts, the completeness of its initial disclosure and the reasons for any failure to include documents responsive to subdivision (h)(6)-(10), (12) and (13).  Based on the language of the statute, Defendant was required to produce all documents required under subdivision (h) and its failure to do so requires imposition of sanctions absent a showing of good cause.    

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Monetary Sanctions is granted as to the request for sanctions and denied as to all other requested orders, including an order compelling initial disclosure. 

 

IV.           CONCLUSION 

¿ 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Monetary Sanctions is granted as to the request for sanctions and denied as to all other requested orders, including an order compelling initial disclosure.

Granted

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiffs filed a complaint on January 24, 2025 against FCA US, LLC and Rydell Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram.  The operative complaint is the First Amended Complaint filed on January 27, 2026 alleging: (1) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2(d); (2) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2(b); (3) violation of Civil Code section 1793.2(a)(3); (4) breach of the implied warranty of merchantability; and (5) fraudulent inducement. 

 

On June 12, 2026, Defendants filed this Motion for Sanctions. 

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD¿

“Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).”  (CCP §871.26(b).)

“Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: (1) A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff's attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).”  (CCP §871.26(j).) 

 

III.           DISCUSSION

 

Defendants contend that under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (b), Plaintiff’s mandatory disclosures were due on or before June 13, 2025, as Defendants filed their responsive pleading on April 14, 2025. On July 28, 2025, Plaintiff served its initial disclosures and produced one single document. (Cho Decl., ¶ 4; Exh A.) Plaintiff failed to fully comply with the mandatory disclosures. (Id., ¶ 5.)

 

As Plaintiff has failed to comply with the mandatory disclosure obligations, Defendants seek sanctions in the amount of $1,500. Plaintiff has failed to file an opposition to show good cause. Thus, Defendants’ request for sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel is granted.

 

 

IV.           CONCLUSION 

¿ 

Defendants’ Motion for Sanctions is granted. Plaintiff’s counsel is ordered to pay Defendant $1,500.
