DEPARTMENT 400 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV21662    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: 400

Tentative Rulings on MotIons in Limine in Mendoza v. Ford 23STCV21662

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #1  Seeks to preclude evidence re settlement discussions.

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANTED.  There shall be no mention of settlement discussions absent prior approval obtained from the Court.

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #2:  Seeks to preclude evidence that Defendant proposed arbitration as an alternative to this litigation

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANTED.  The availability of a certified arbitration program is a defense to any claim for civil penalties based simply on a violation of the law. CC section 1794(e).   Plaintiffs do not seek civil penalties under that provision, but rather under CC section 1794(c), which requires a showing of willfulness.  The arbitration program is not relevant to that provision.  To the extent the parties are concerned that there may be references to the arbitration program in documents admitted into evidence, the Court will consider a proposed instruction to the jury not to consider whether an arbitration program was available. 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #3:  Seeks to preclude evidence relating to attorney’s fees incurred by counsel in this case.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.  The Court will give CACI instruction 3964, requested by both parties, that instructs jurors not to consider attorneys fees in their deliberations and verdict.

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #4:  Seeks to preclude evidence relating to Plaintiff’s financing  or financial condition.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANTED  Plaintiff’s financial condition or his terms of financing are not relevant to the claims in this case.  To the extent there may be an incidental reference in admitted evidence to the terms of purchase or Plaintiff’s credit application, the Court will consider an instruction that those issues are not relevant to the deliberations or verdict.

 

PLAINTIFF’S  MIL #5    Seeking order prohibiting reference to attorney advertisements and marketing.

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANTED.  These matters are not relevant to any claim or defense, they might be prejudicial, and they will be excluded under Evidence Code section 352. All counsel and parties will be introduced to prospective jurors who will be asked by the Court if they have any knowledge of the parties or counsel, and, if so, how they came to learn of them.  If further inquiry is warranted, that may occur outside the presence of the other prospective jurors.

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #6  Seeks to preclude argument that timely revocation of acceptance was required.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED   This issue appears to relate to the implied warranty of merchantability, which is not at issue in this case following the Court’s summary adjudication  of this issue on May 6, 2025. 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #7  Seeks an order that statements by dealers are admissions of the manufacturer.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE   These issues are much better addressed on a case by case  basis, and will require foundational proof of the relationship between the dealer and the manufacturer.

 

PLAINTIFF’S MIL #8:  Seeks to preclude evidence of Ford’s 998 offer to compromise.

 

TENTAIVE RULING:  GRANTED  The 998 offer to compromise is not admissible to prove liability. If it becomes relevant because Ps argue that Ford ignored them, the Court will consider whether this evidence might be proper in response.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #1:   Seeks to preclude evidence of P’s “subjective expectations” about the vehicle.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANT.   The standard as to whether the value of the vehicle was “substantially impaired” is an objective one, such that unique expectations the P may have had are not relevant.  That said, testimony about the intended uses of the vehicle could be relevant to whether its value was substantially impaired due to a defect.  The objective standard is to be applied to the specific circumstances of the buyer. Lundy v. Ford Motor Co. (2001) 87 Cal App 4th 472, 478.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL#2   Seeks to preclude evidence of non-recoverable damages

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANT, but the parties are ordered to meet and confer and develop a list of those incidental damages which are recoverable and those which are not.  The list must be presented to the Court at the time of jury selection.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #3:  Seeks to preclude references to the dealer as Ford’s agent.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED   While it appears that the dealership does act as agent of the manufacturer for some purposes, it is also clear they are not agents for other purposes.  The Court cannot determine whether certain activities are done on an agency basis until evidence is proffered as to (1) the nature of the agency relationship and (2) specific evidence that P wants admitted on that basis.  See Civil Code sections 2296 et seq. as it relates to agency.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #4  Seeks to prelude evidence of customer repair requests not covered by warranty

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANT  The relevant requests which are covered by warranty is what would constitute a potential breach, not those which are not.

 

DEFENDANTS MIL #5  Seeks to preclude evidence of repair requests presented only once.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENY WITHOUT PREJUDICE  Whie it is true that Defendant must be given a reasonable opportunity to repair and that means at least two chances, this ruling may be hard to enforce without addressing specifics because there may be a dispute about whether a particular repair request is related to one or more other requests. 

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #6  Seeks to preclude argument that Ford has a duty to repurchase the car without first receiving the customers request to do so.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED  This appears to misstate the law as the duty arises upon material violation of the warranty.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #7  Precludes questioning of Ford’s employees of the legal requirements of Song Beverly.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANT  Asking lay witnesses about legal requirements is irrelevant and unfair.  The employees may be asked what they did, but not be examined on legal issues.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #8   Would limit Plaintiff to one expert witness.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED  The Court will sustain objections where evidence is unreasonably cumulative, but not rule in advance that only one expert may testify.  In any event, Plaintiff states they will use only one expert at trial. 

 

DEFENDAT’S MIL #9  Seeks to preclude Plaintiff’s expert from testifying because he was not made available for deposition.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  ARGUE

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #10:  Seeks to preclude evidence of technical service bulletins which do not relate to the vehicle at issue.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  GRANT  It is not apparent to the Court that evidence of issues with parts which were not defective in Ps vehicle is relevant to prove breach of warranty.

 

DEFENDANT’S MIL #11:  Seeks to preclude evidence of complaints by other customers concerning vehicles other than the subject vehicle.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE   Complaints about other vehicles of the same model and year could be relevant to issues such as Ford’s knowledge of a problem.  They are not necessarily hearsay  as they may be in business records and/or relevant to Ford’s state of mind.  Ford may reserve all rights to object when such evidence is offered.

Case Number: 24STCV12553    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: 400

MOTION TO REQUIRE PLAINTIFF TO POST OUT OF STATE BOND

 

The Court tenders the following tentative ruling in the matter Joshua Martin Pannepacker v. John Ernest Papadakis, Los Angeles County Superior Court case number 24STCV12553, set for hearing on June 26, 2026. John Ernest Papadakis (Defendant) moves for an order requiring Joshua Martin Pannepacker (Plaintiff) to post an undertaking in the amount of $173,654.52 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1030. Plaintiff opposes the motion. The motion is denied.

 

A.    Legal Standard


Where a plaintiff in an action resides out of the state, the defendant may, at any time, apply to the court for an order requiring the plaintiff to file an undertaking to secure costs and attorney’s fees which may be awarded in the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1030, subd. (a).) The motion shall be made on grounds that the plaintiff resides out of the state and there is a reasonable possibility that the moving defendant will obtain judgment in their favor. (Id., § 1030, subd. (b).) The motion shall be accompanied by an affidavit in support of the grounds for the motion and setting forth the nature and amount of costs and attorney’s fees the defendant has incurred and expects to incur. (Ibid.) 

 

“The purpose of [Section 1030] is to enable a California resident sued by an out-of-state resident to secure costs in light of the difficulty of enforcing a judgment for costs against a person who is not within the court’s jurisdiction.” (Alshafie v. Lallande (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 421, 428.) The moving party is not required to show there is no possibility that an out-of-state plaintiff will win at trial, but rather that it is reasonably possible that the moving party will win. (Baltayan v. Estate of Getemyan (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 1427, 1432 (Baltayan).) 

 

If the motion is granted, the plaintiff shall file the undertaking no later than 30 days after service of the court’s order. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1030, subd. (d).) If plaintiff fails to file the undertaking within the time allowed, the plaintiff’s action shall be dismissed as to the moving defendant. (Ibid.)


B.     Discussion

 

Defendant moves for an order requiring Plaintiff to post an undertaking in the amount of $173,654.52. In doing so, Defendant asserts that Plaintiff resides in Pennsylvania and that there is a reasonable probability Defendant will obtain judgment in his favor. (Mendoza Decl., Ex. F.) Plaintiff contests Defendant’s motion on both grounds.

 

First, Defendant argues that Plaintiff resides out of state. Section 1030 requires a determination that the Plaintiff “resides out of the state.” (Id., § 1030, subd. (b).) This refers to the plaintiff’s actual dwelling, rather than technical legal domicile. (Myers v. Carter (1960) 178 Cal.App.2d 622, 626.) The Court’s determination of residence is based on the facts at the time the motion is considered. (Ibid.)

 

Here, Defendant offers an alleged declaration from Plaintiff stating that he moved to Pennsylvania after filing this lawsuit on May 17, 2024. However, Exhibit F, referred to by Defendant, is Defendant’s own offer to compromise pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 998 and includes no mention of Plaintiff’s relocation. Additionally, Plaintiff offers evidence that he renewed his California driver’s license in May 2026. (Barkhordar Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. A.) Plaintiff also offers testimony from his sister that he never fully recovered from his injuries and travelled to Pennsylvania to be close to family during his recovery. (Id. ¶ 4, Ex. B.) Thus, Defendant has not met its burden of demonstrating that Plaintiff resides out of the state. Accordingly, Defendant’s motion is denied.

             

Conclusion:


            The Court denies the motion without prejudice.

Case Number: 25STCV17124    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: 400

MOTIONS TO COMPEL

 

            The Court tenders the following tentative decision in the matter Leslyn Windsor v. Mark 1 Restoration Co., et al., Los Angeles County Superior Court case number 25STCV17124, set for hearing on June 26, 2026. Eastern Columbia Lofts Homeowners Association (Defendant) moves for orders compelling Leslyn Windsor (Plaintiff) to serve responses to Defendant’s form interrogatories, set one (FROGs), special interrogatories, set one (SPROGs), and requests for production of documents, set one (RFPs). Defendant also collectively requests $3,630 in sanctions. Defendant’s motions to compel are granted but the Court does not award sanctions.

 

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, that party waives any objection, the party making the demand may move for an order compelling response, and the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party who unsuccessfully opposes such a motion unless they acted with substantial justification. (Id., § 2030.290.) And if a party to whom an inspection demand is directed fails to serve a timely response, that party waives any objection, the party making the demand may move for an order compelling response, and the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against any party who unsuccessfully opposes such a motion unless they acted with substantial justification. (Id., § 2031.300.)

 

            Here, Defendant served all relevant written discovery requests via email on December 19, 2025. (Balderas Decl. ¶ 2.) Responses were due on or before January 21, 2026. (Ibid.) Plaintiff failed to timely serve responses and has thus waived all objections. (Id. ¶¶ 3–5.) Indeed, Plaintiff had still yet to respond as of Defendant’s filing these motions on May 18, 2026. (Id. ¶ 6–7.) Plaintiff provided late responses on June 1, 2026, to all discovery requests after the present motions were filed. (Mobassernia Decl. ¶ 11.) Because of Plaintiff’s failure to respond to the relevant discovery requests within the allotted timeframe, Defendant’s motions to compel discovery pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300 were proper. But Plaintiff provided responses which he contends are code-complaint.  Because these responses were submitted mid-briefing on this motion, the Court is not in a position to assess the claim.  In any event, the motions are granted.  If in fact the late responses are adequate, the entry of this Order granting the motions will not matter.  Plaintiff may separately seek relief from waiver of objections by filing a motion pursuant Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290, subdivision (a) and 2031.300, subdivision (a).

 

            The Court need not impose sanctions with respect to a motion to compel if the Court finds that circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (c), 2031.300, subd. (c).) Here, Plaintiff’s counsel testifies that Plaintiff’s failure to respond was due to their own inadvertence. (Mobassernia Decl. ¶¶ 5–10.) The Court finds this explanation satisfactory and declines to award sanctions. Plaintiff also requests sanctions, but these are also inappropriate because Plaintiff’s initial failure to respond to discovery necessitated the present motions to compel.

Conclusion:

 

            The Court grants Defendant’s motions and does not impose sanctions on either party.
