MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Interinsurance Exchange of the Auto Club

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

MOTION TO DEEM REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION ADMITTED; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

(CCP § 2033.280)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Court GRANTS Interinsurance Exchange of the Auto Club’s motion to deem matters admitted.  The contents of Interinsurance’s requests for admission are deemed admitted. 

 

Okechi Uluocha must pay Interinsurance $585.00 within 30 days of this order.

 

Interinsurance is to give notice.

REPLY:                     None filed as of July 28, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Interinsurance Exchange of the Auto Club filed this subrogation action against Okechi Uluocha.  Uluocha answered.  Interinsurance then propounded four requests for admission on Uluocha.  Uluocha failed to timely respond, so Interinsurance now moves to have those requests deemed admitted.

 

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)  If a party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted.  (Id., § 2033.280, subds. (a), (b).) 

 

Interinsurance served Uluocha with the requests for admission in December 2025.  To date, no responses have been served.  Uluocha has not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Interinsurance’s motion and the contents of Interinsurance’s requests for admission are deemed admitted.

 

Uluocha must also pay sanctions for causing Interinsurance to move to deem the requests admitted.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)  Interinsurance requests $585.00 from Uluocha, reflecting 3.5 hours of counsel’s work at $150.00 per hour plus $60.00 in filing fees.  The Court finds this request reasonable.  Uluocha must pay Interinsurance $585.00 within 30 days of this order.

 

Interinsurance is to give notice.  


Case Number: 25STLC06553    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Mon., August 3, 2026                                     JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           Lee v. ABI Construction Inc., et al.               COMPL. FILED:             08-27-25

CASE NUMBER:      25STLC06553                       

NOTICE:                  OK                                                                 

 

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND THE COMPLAINT AND RECLASSIFY THE ACTION FROM LIMITED TO UNLIMITED JURISDICTION           

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Insang Lee

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

MOTION FOR RECLASSIFICATION

(CCP § 403.040)

                                                              

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Court DENIES Insang Lee’s motion for leave to amend and for reclassification as an unlimited jurisdiction case.

 

Lee is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          None filed as of July 29, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

REPLY:                     None filed as of July 29, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Insang Lee brought a limited civil action against ABI Construction Inc., Dustin Charles Hughes, Evan Duong, and Hoan Duong over a motor vehicle collision.  Hoan and Evan answered and filed cross-complaints, and ABI Construction Inc. also answered.  Lee now moves to reclassify this case to the Court’s unlimited jurisdiction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 403.040, subdivision (b).[1]

 

            To reclassify an action at this point, a plaintiff must show (1) that the case is incorrectly classified and (2) that they had good cause for not seeking reclassification earlier.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 403.040, subd. (b).)  To show that a case is incorrectly classified, the plaintiff “must present evidence to demonstrate a possibility the damages will exceed $[35],000.”  (Ytuarte v. Superior Court (2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 266, 279, emphasis altered.)

 

            Lee presents no evidence to support reclassification.  Lee’s counsel argues in the motion that Lee “sustained over $43,000 in medical expenses” as a result of the collision.  Counsel also contends that before the complaint was filed, he was “unsure whether Defendant Hughes was in the course and scope of his employment with Defendant ABI Construction, Inc.,” but counsel filed the complaint to preserve the statute of limitations.  And after discovery was conducted, Lee’s counsel argues that Evan Duong supposedly produced a text message from Hughes that he was using his vehicle as part of his employment, indicating the availability of liability insurance.  While these facts may justify reclassification, a lawyer’s unsworn statements are not evidence.  (In re Marriage of Pasco (2019) 42 Cal.App.5th 585, 592.)  The motion is not accompanied by anything that is evidence.

 

            The Court DENIES Lee’s motion.

 

            Lee is to give notice.


[1] Lee’s motion also requests leave to amend, but no argument is made regarding amendment.  The Court thus rules on this motion as one for reclassification.


Case Number: 25STLC07134    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Mon., August 3, 2026                                     JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           General Commercial Properties, LLC v. Melamed, et al.

COMP. FILED:         09-17-25

CASE NUMBER:      25STLC07134                       

NOTICE:                   OK                                                                 

                                               

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION TO VACATE ENTRY OF DEFAULT

 

MOVING PARTY:   Defendant Houman David Melamed

RESP. PARTY:         Plaintiff General Commercial Properties, LLC

 

MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 473(d))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court DENIES Houman David Melamed’s motion to set aside the default entered against him.

 

            General Commercial Properties, LLC is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          Filed June 29, 2026                                        [   ] Late                      [   ] None

REPLY:                     Filed July 27, 2026                                         [   ] Late                      [   ] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            General Commercial Properties, LLC sued Houman David Melamed, Youssef Melamed, Mitra Melamed, and Magnolia Print Studio Inc.  Youssef and Mitra demurred, both demurrers were overruled, and both Youssef and Mitra answered.  Houman moved successfully to quash the October 11, 2025 service of process on himself.  General Commercial then served Houman again, and filed proof of that service.  Houman failed to respond, so General Commercial obtained a default against him in April 2026.  Houman now moves to have the default vacated under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), claiming that he was again never validly served with process and that the default is therefore void.  The Court DENIES the motion.

 

A registered California process server declared that  he personally served Houman with copies of the summons, complaint, and various other case-opening materials at “13940 NW Passage Apt. 102, Marina del Rey, California 90292.”  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 415.10.)  The process server’s declaration is prima facie evidence that General Commercial served Houman.  (Evid. Code, § 647; Lee v. Yan (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 975, 978.)  That places the burden on Houman to offer evidence to the contrary. (Palm Property Investments, LLC v. Yadegar (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 1419, 1428.)

 

            As an initial matter, Houman attempts to argue that the Court has already decided the January 2026 proof of service is invalid.  Not so.  In ruling on Houman’s motion to quash the October 2025 service, the Court noted that General Commercial represented that it had re-served Houman, but that “there is no new proof of service in the record,” General Commercial had “failed to demonstrate service . . . was done in conformity with the statutory requirements.”  The Court only struck the October 2025 proof of service that was before it.  Houman’s argument on this point fails.

 

            Houman also references the supplemental declaration he filed with his previous motion to quash and which purportedly rebuts the January 2026 service.  That supplemental filing includes a declaration from Houman’s friend, Robert Yager, declaring that Yager was present at the time of service.  Now that General Commercial has provided a video taken of the service of process in January 2026, however, Houman attests that the Yager interaction happened at a different time.

 

            Video taken by General Commercial’s process server and still images taken from the video show that General Commercial personally served Houman in January.  General Commercial’s server, Christopher Demirdjian, declared that he “personally delivered the service packet to” Houman, “recorded the service encounter contemporaneously with a body camera,” that the video and images attached to the opposition are true and correct, and he has not “altered, edited, or manipulated the substance of the video.”

 

            Houman argues that the video does not show him extending his hand for documents, taking any documents, contains no audio of what was said, and therefore does not demonstrate personal service on him.  But Houman does not dispute that the video depicts him, and refusing to accept service does not render personal service invalid when the papers are left as close as possible to the defendant in “some appropriate place where it would be most likely to come to the attention of the person being served.”  (Crescendo Corp. v. Shelted, Inc. (1968) 267 Cal.App.2d 209, 212–213; see Trujillo v. Trujillo (1945) 71 Cal.App.2d 257, 260.)  The video demonstrates that the process server attempted personal service.  Whether or not Houman immediately received the documents when the process server tried to hand them to him, the process server’s hand was later seen to be empty.  The server either personally served Houman when Houman opened the door, or he left the documents in an appropriate place where Houman would find them.  Either way, the Court finds that Houman has failed to rebut the presumption of valid personal service.

 

            The Court DENIES Houman’s motion to vacate the default entered against him.

 

            General Commercial is to give notice.

(CCP § 685.040)

(a), (b).)  Service must be made in accordance with Code of Civil Procedure section 684.110 for personal service or section 684.120 for mail service.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1005.)
