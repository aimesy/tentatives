Case Number: 25STCV04231    Hearing Date: September 8, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 10 - William F. Best v. Consolidated Fire Protection District of Los Angeles County, et al.

          Defendants’ Demurrer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint

          Defendants Consolidated Fire Protection District of Los Angeles County and Anthony C. Marrone demur to Plaintiff William F. Best’s first amended complaint (“FAC”) for declaratory relief.

Request for Judicial Notice

          Defendants request the Court take judicial notice of Title 32 of the Los Angeles County Code (“Fire Code”). This is subject to judicial notice under Evidence Code section 452(b). Defendants’ request for judicial notice is granted.

Demurrer

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s claim for declaratory relief must fail because (1) Plaintiff did not exhaust his administrative remedies before filing suit and (2) Plaintiff improperly seeks declaratory relief to review an administrative decision made by the Los Angeles County Fire Department. The Court will address each argument in turn.

A.      Exhaustion of Administrative Remedies

The Court is limited to the contents of the complaint and any judicially noticeable documents when ruling on a demurrer. General demurrers can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable; evidence or extrinsic matters are not considered. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 430.30, 430.70; Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

Defendants request the Court consider extrinsic evidence to assess whether Plaintiff exhausted administrative remedies—namely, that Plaintiff filed a permit application that was denied, and did not seek an appeal of the decision as required under the Fire Code. The Court cannot consider this extrinsic evidence. In ruling on a demurrer, courts “assume that the complaint’s properly pleaded material allegations are true and give the complaint a reasonable interpretation by reading it as a whole and all its parts in their context” but do not “assume the truth of contentions, deductions, or conclusions of fact or law.” (Moore v. Regents of University of California (1990) 51 Cal.3d 120, 125.) The complaint must be “liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.) “This rule of liberal construction means that the reviewing court draws inferences favorable to the plaintiff, not the defendant.” (Perez v. Golden Empire Transit Dist. (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1238.)

Here, the FAC (and original complaint) does not refer to Plaintiff’s failure to appeal any permit application denial. Plaintiff alleges he exhausted all administrative remedies. (FAC, ¶ 4.) This is sufficient at the pleading stage.

B.       Declaratory Relief

Defendants also argue that Plaintiff’s request for declaratory relief is improper because he is seeking review of an administrative decision made by the Los Angeles County Fire Department. While the FAC does not explicitly reference any permit application, Defendants direct the Court to consider Plaintiff’s prior allegations in his original complaint, which affirm that he did in fact file a permit application that was denied.

“Under the sham pleading doctrine, a plaintiff cannot avoid allegations that are determinative to a cause of action simply by filing an amended complaint which omits the problematic facts or pleads facts inconsistent with those alleged in the original complaint. . . . Instead, the plaintiff must satisfactorily explain such an omission. Failure to provide such an explanation allows the court to disregard the inconsistent allegations and read into the amended complaint the allegations of the superseded complaint.” (Tindell v. Murphy (2018) 22 Cal.App.5th 1239, 1248.)

Here, Plaintiff does not provide any explanation why these allegations were omitted from the FAC. When considering these prior allegations and those in the FAC, it is clear Plaintiff’s declaratory relief claim seeks review of an administrative decision by the Los Angeles County Fire Department. However, an administrative writ, not a declaratory relief claim, is the proper route to challenge an administrative decision. (See State of California v. Superior Court (1974) 12 Cal.3d 237, 249 [“It is settled that an action for declaratory relief is not appropriate to review an administrative decision.”].)

The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend on this ground.

Defendant Marrone

Defendants also argue that Plaintiff’s naming of Defendant Marrone in his official capacity as Fire Chief is redundant because Plaintiff already named Consolidated Fire Protection District of Los Angeles County aka Los Angeles County Fire Department as a Defendant. The Court agrees. The FAC contains no allegations regarding the fire chief; thus, there is no basis for any relief against him.

The demurrer is also sustained with leave to amend on this ground.

Disposition

          Defendants Consolidated Fire Protection District of Los Angeles County and Anthony C. Marrone’s demurrer is sustained with 21 days’ leave to amend.

Case Number: 26STCV05906    Hearing Date: September 8, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 7 – Midway HFCA LLC v. Arman Melkonyan

Plaintiff Midway HFCA LLC’s Request for Default Judgment

Plaintiff Midway HFCA LLC requests court judgment by default against Defendant Arman Melkonyan. Plaintiff’s application for default judgment suffers one defect.

Plaintiff seeks excessive attorney fees. Attorney fees for a default judgment are generally limited to a specified schedule based on the amount of damages. (Local Rules 3.207(a), 3.214(a).) A plaintiff may recover “a fee greater than listed in the [default] schedule because of extraordinary services.” (Rule 3.214(d).) Plaintiff’s proposed judgment includes a request for $5,772 in attorney fees. That is greater than the schedule, which provides for attorney fees of “$2,890 plus 1% of the excess over $100,000” for judgments over $100,000. (Rule 3.214(a).) Per the schedule, Plaintiff is only entitled to $3,141 [$2,890 + ($25,099.86 * 0.01)]. Plaintiff does not show extraordinary services justifying a greater fee.

Disposition

Plaintiff Midway HFCA LLC’s request for default judgment is denied without prejudice. If Plaintiff seeks only the $3,141 permitted under the fee schedule, the Court will instead grant the request and modify the proposed judgment.
