CASE:                         Gloria Hernandez Juarez, et al. v. Norman Harold Peeples, IV

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV22897

 

 

APPLICATION FOR ORDER FOR PUBLICATION

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiffs Gloria Hernandez Juarez, Estate of Michael Juarez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      None

 

CASE HISTORY:

  • 09/05/2024: Complaint filed.
  • 10/15/2025: First Amended Complaint filed.
  • 01/13/2026: Second Amended Complaint filed.

 

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

           

            This is an action for personal injury arising out of an automobile vs pedestrian collision.

 

            Plaintiffs filed an application for Order for Publication

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiffs’ application for Order for Publication is DENIED without prejudice.

 

           

 

DISCUSSION:

 

Unlike other service methods, a plaintiff must obtain a court order prior to serving a defendant via publication.  Strict compliance with the statutory requirements is required.¿ (County of Riverside v. Sup. Ct. (1997) 54 Cal.App.4th 443, 450.)¿ To obtain an order for publication, the plaintiff must show reasonable diligence in serving the defendant by some other manner.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.50, subd. (a).)  The plaintiff must establish reasonable diligence via the testimony of an individual with personal knowledge of the efforts to serve the defendant.  (Olvera v. Olvera (1991) 232 Cal.App.3d 32, 42.)  Likewise, the plaintiff must establish that a cause of action exists against the defendant via the testimony of an individual with personal knowledge of the underlying facts.  (Harris v. Cavasso¿(1977) 68 Cal.App.3d 723, 726.) A verified complaint is “no substitute for the required affidavit, and the existence of a cause of action was a jurisdictional prerequisite to the issuance of the order for publication.” (Olvera, supra 232 Cal.App.3d at 42 fn. 9.) However, if the form for application for service by publication contains a verification, that is sufficient if signed by the plaintiffs. (Id.) 

First, Plaintiffs have attached declarations of reasonable diligence, which shows a few process servers attempted to Defendant at 323 N Soto St, Apt 15 in Los Angeles on nine different occasions. (Brooks Decl.; Garcia Decl.; and Arellano Decl.) The process servers state that the property is located behind a locked security gate with no intercom, doorbell, or other means of notifying the residents of his presence. (Id.) They waited outside for 15 minutes on one occasion 10 minutes on another occasion, and 7 minutes on another occasion and no one answered. (Id.) Moreover, on one occasion one process server gained access to the building when someone walked out, and knocked on the door but no one answered. (Id.)

The Court finds it is questionable whether Plaintiffs have shown reasonable diligence in attempting to serve Defendant. “The term ‘reasonable diligence’ denotes a thorough, systematic investigation and inquiry conducted in good faith by the party or his agent or attorney.¿ A number of honest attempts to learn defendant's whereabouts or his address by inquiry of relatives, and by investigation of appropriate city and telephone directories, [voter registries, and assessor's office property indices situated near the defendant's last known location], generally are sufficient. These are the likely sources of information, and consequently must be searched before resorting to service by publication.¿ Before allowing a plaintiff to resort to service by publication, the courts necessarily require him to show exhaustive attempts to locate the defendant, for it is generally recognized that service by publication rarely results in actual notice.”¿ (Watts v. Crawford (1995) 10 Cal.4th 743, 749, fn. 5.) It appears Plaintiffs did not conduct a thorough, systemic investigation and inquiry, as Plaintiffs attempted to serve Defendant at one location, and there is no information as to whether Plaintiffs made attempts to look up another address for Defendant through likely sources of information.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiffs’ application for Order for Publication is DENIED without prejudice.

 

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

CASE:                         Glenda Patricia Larios, et al. v. Seonghyeon Lee

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV20003

 

 

MOTION TO SUBSTITUTE SUCCESSOR IN INTEREST FOR DECEDENT MARIA DELMA TORRES, OR IN THE ALTERNATIVE, TO DISMISS DECEDENT’S CLAIMS

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiffs Glenda Patricia Larios, Maria Delma Torres, and Clara Del Carmen Cruz

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      None

 

CASE HISTORY:

  • 07/07/2025: Complaint filed.

 

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

           

            This is an action for personal injury arising out of an automobile collision. 

 

            Plaintiffs move to substitute successor in interest for Decedent Maria Delma Torres.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff’s motion to substitute successor in interest is MOOT.

 

 

 

DISCUSSION:

 

            Plaintiff Maria Delma Torres’ counsel states that she passed away in May 2024. Plaintiffs seek an order: (1) setting a date certain by which any person claiming to be the successor in interest of decedent Maria Delma Torres must appear and file a declaration meeting the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 377.32 in order to be substituted as plaintiff in this action in place of decedent; and (2) in the event no qualifying successor in interest appears and timely files the required declaration, dismissing without prejudice the individual claims asserted in the name of Maria Delma Torres, with the action to proceed as to the remaining Plaintiffs.

 

Motion to Dismiss

 

“The court may in its discretion dismiss an action for delay in prosecution pursuant to this article on its own motion or on motion of the defendant if to do so appears to the court appropriate under the circumstances of the case.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.410.) “The court may not dismiss an action pursuant to this article for delay in prosecution except after one of the following conditions”: (1) service is not made within two years or (2) the action is not brought to trial within three years. (Code Civ Proc., § 583.420.)

 

In ruling on a motion to dismiss, the court may consider the court file, diligence in seeking effective service of process, the extent to which parties engaged in any settlement negotiations, diligence in pursuing discovery, nature and complexity of the case, pendency of other litigation based on common facts, nature of any extensions of time or other delay, condition of the court’s calendar, whether the interests of justice are best served by dismissal, and any other facts or circumstances relevant to fair determination. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1342(e).)

 

Motion to Substitute

 

CCP section 377.31 provides that “[o]n motion after the death of a person who commenced an action or proceeding, the court shall allow a pending action or proceeding that does not abate to be continued by the decedent’s personal representative or, if none, by the decedent’s successor in interest.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.31.) 

 

CCP section 377.32 provides that a person who seeks to commence such an action as the decedent’s successor in interest must file an affidavit or declaration providing the following: 

 

1.               The decedent’s name 

2.               The date and place of the decedent’s death 

3.               “No proceeding is now pending in California for administration of the decedent’s estate.” 

4.               If the decedent’s estate was administered, a copy of the final order showing the distribution of the decedent’s cause of action to the successor in interest 

5.               Either of the following, as appropriate, with facts in support thereof: 

A.              “The affiant or declarant is the decedent’s successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) and succeeds to the decedent’s interest in the action or proceeding.” 

B.              “The affiant or declarant is authorized to act on behalf of the decedent’s successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) with respect to the decedent’s interest in the action or proceeding.” 

6.               “No other person has a superior right to commence the action or proceeding or to be substituted for the decedent in the pending action or proceeding.” 

7.               “The affiant or declarant affirms or declares under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct.” 

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 377.32(a).)  A certified copy of the decedent’s death certificate must also be attached to the affidavit or declaration.  (Id., § 377.32(c).) 

 

            Here, the Court notes that Plaintiffs have filed another motion on July 31, 2026, requesting Glenda Patricia Larios to be substituted as Decedent’s successor in interest. As such, the request sought in this motion is MOOT.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV20350    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 507

Tentative Ruling

CASE:                         Aundra Cunningham v. FCA US LLC, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV20350

 

MOTION FOR JUDGMENT ON THE PLEADINGS

 

MOVING PARTIES:           Defendants FCA US LLC and San Leandro Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Plaintiff Aundra Cunningham

 

CASE HISTORY:

  • 07/01/25: Complaint filed.

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendants’ Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is GRANTED with 20 days leave to amend.

Moving Party to give notice.

BACKGROUND

           

            This is a lemon law action.

 

DISCUSSION:

 

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

 

Legal Standard

 

A defendant may move for judgment on the pleadings where the court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the complaint or the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438 subd. (c)(1)(B).)  A non-statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings may be made any time before or during trial.  (Stoops v. Abbassi (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 644, 650.)  “Such motion may be made on the same ground as those supporting a general demurrer, i.e., that the pleading at issue fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a legally cognizable claim or defense.”  (Ibid.)   

 

“In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 452; see also Stevens v. Superior Court (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 594, 601.)  “When a court evaluates a complaint, the plaintiff is entitled to reasonable inferences from the facts pled.”  (Duval v. Board of Tr. (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 902, 906.)  “In deciding or reviewing a judgment on the pleadings, all properly pleaded material facts are deemed to be true, as well as all facts that may be implied or inferred from those expressly alleged.”  (Fire Ins. Exch. v. Superior Court (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 446, 452.)  A motion for judgment on the pleadings normally does not lie as to a portion of a cause of action.  (Ibid.)  “In the case of either a demurrer or a motion for judgment on the pleadings, leave to amend should be granted if there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action.”  (Gami v. Mullikin Medical Ctr. (1993) 18 Cal.App.4th 870, 876.)   

 

Fifth Cause of Action: Negligent Repair

 

            Defendant demurs to the fifth cause of action for negligent repair, arguing that it is barred by the economic loss rule and Plaintiff fails to plead the element of damages.

 

The elements for negligence are: (1) a legal duty owed to the plaintiff to use due care; (2) breach of duty; (3) causation; and (4) damage to the plaintiff. (County of Santa Clara v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2006) 137 Cal.App.4th 292, 318.)  In California, negligence may be pleaded in general terms. (Landeros v. Flood (1976) 17 Cal.3d 399, 407-408.) “One who undertakes repairs has a duty arising in tort to do them without negligence.” (Southwest Forest Industries, Inc. v. Westinghouse Elec. Corp.¿(9th Cir. 1970) 422 F.2d 1013, 1020.)¿¿¿

 

“[A] demurrer based on an affirmative defense will be sustained only where the face of the complaint discloses that the action is necessarily barred by the defense.” (McKenney v. Purepac Pharmaceutical Co. (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 72, 78-79; see Cross Talk Productions, Inc. v. Jacobson (1998) 65 Cal.App.4th 631, 635; see also Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 23 [“[T]he economic loss rule does not act as an absolute bar to tort recovery in every case in which the parties have a contractual relationship.”].)¿ Under California law, the economic loss rule does not bar all cases involving the negligent performance of services.¿ (North American Chemical Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 764, 777-781; J’Aire Corp. v. Gregory (1979) 24 Cal.3d 799.)¿ Plaintiff’s complaint does not affirmatively disclose that the damages alleged are only economic losses such as prospective economic advantage, and no other damages such as property damage (i.e., to the car itself).¿

 

            Thus, the motion for judgment on the pleadings is denied on this ground.

 

            However, Plaintiff has not adequately pled the element of damages. The complaint alleges that these causes of action arise out of the warranty obligations of FCA in connection with a motor vehicle for which FCA issued a written warranty. (Compl., ¶ 11.) Thus, it appears the repairs were covered by the warranty. While Plaintiff alleges she suffered damages from Defendants’ failure to properly store, prepare and repair the vehicle, if the repairs were covered by the warranty, it is unclear what damages she sustained.

Thus, the motion for judgment on the pleadings is GRANTED with leave to amend.

CONCLUSION:

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is GRANTED with 20 days leave to amend.

Moving Party to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV28383    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 507

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Rolf M. Treu, Department 507

 

 

HEARING DATE:     August 7, 2026           TRIAL DATE: August 24, 2027

                                                          

CASE:                         David Schwarzman v. Blue Shield of California

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV28383

 

DEMURRER TO FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant California Physicians’ Service dba Blue Shield of California

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Plaintiff David Schwarzman

 

CASE HISTORY:

  • 09/29/25: Complaint filed.
  • 02/27/26: First Amended Complaint filed.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 Defendant’s Demurrer to the FAC is SUSTAINED in part and OVERRULED in part.

It is overruled as to the first and sixth causes of action for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and breach of contract.

             It is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to the second, third, fourth, and fifth causes of action for intentional interference with prospective economic relationship, negligent interference with prospective economic relationship, negligence and conversion.

Moving Party to give notice.

BACKGROUND

           

            Plaintiff alleges that his wife Lori Harmon was a licensed broker and founder of Harmon Insurance Services. When she passed away, he succeeded to Harmon Insurance Services and supported documents were provided to Defendant.

 

            Plaintiff submitted Defendant’s Book of Business Transfer Request, requesting that Harmon’s client accounts and records be transferred to him. Defendant approved Plaintiff as a licensed broker and agent on behalf of Harmon, and thereby accepted his Form Request. Plaintiff alleges that from the moment Plaintiff’s Form Request was approved, he was properly inducted into the Blue Shield network as an agent broker for it, and with the same contractual obligations as those of his late spouse’s. Despite this approval, Defendant refused and failed to provide Plaintiff with access to the broker portal, and Harmon’s book of business, effectively preventing Plaintiff from servicing

Harmon’s clients. Plaintiff alleges that as a result, Harmon’s client relationships deteriorated, commissions were lost, and Plaintiff suffered substantial financial harm.

 

DISCUSSION:

 

Demurrer to FAC

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. (Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228.) In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “The ultimate facts alleged in the complaint must be deemed true, as well as all facts that may be implied or inferred from those expressly alleged. (Marshall v. Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1397, 1403; see also Shields v. County of San Diego (1984) 155 Cal.App.3d 103, 133 [stating, “[o]n demurrer, pleadings are read liberally and allegations contained therein are assumed to be true”].)

 

First and Sixth Causes of Action: Breach of the Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing and Breach of Contract

 

            Defendant demurs to the sixth cause of action for breach of contract, and first cause of action for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, arguing that Plaintiff does not allege a valid agreement between Plaintiff and Defendant, as there was no consideration, and the attached documents do not set forth the duties and obligations owed by the parties under the alleged contract.

 

To state a cause of action for breach of contract, a plaintiff must allege: “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) resulting damages to the plaintiff.” (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.) 

 

The essential elements of a contract are: “(1) Parties capable of contracting; (2) Their consent; (3) A lawful object; and (4) A sufficient cause or consideration.” (Civil Code, § 1550.) 

 

            Here, as to the first element of the existence of a contract, Plaintiff has pled that Defendant approved Plaintiff as a licensed broker and agent on behalf of Harmon, and thus he was inducted into the Blue Shield network as an agent broker for it, with the same contractual obligations as those of his late spouse’s.

 

            Defendant argues that a request to transfer of book of business does not create a contract between Plaintiff and Defendant because there was no additional consideration, as doing or promising to do what one is already legally bound to do cannot be consideration for a promise. However, Plaintiff’s FAC alleges that Plaintiff filled out a form for the approval. A contract is supported by sufficient consideration if there is some benefit to the promisor or detriment to the promisee regardless of the amount of the benefit or detriment.  (Harris v. Time, Inc. (1987)191 Cal. App. 3d 449, 456.)  For example, it may be said that all the law requires for sufficient consideration for a contract is the proverbial peppercorn or that a consideration of $1 is ordinarily sufficient to support a contract at law.  (San Diego City Firefighters, Local 145, AFL-CIO v. Board of Admin. of San Diego City Employees' Retirement System, (2012) 206 Cal.App.4th 594, 619; Abers v. Rounsavell (2010) 189 Cal.App.4th 348, 362.)  Thus, there is consideration as Plaintiff filled out the Form Request.

 

Defendant also contends that Plaintiff fails to allege the contractual terms that were allegedly breached. “A written contract may be pleaded by its terms—set out verbatim in the complaint or a copy of the contract attached to the complaint and incorporated therein by reference—or by its legal effect.” (McKell v. Washington Mutual, Inc. (2006) 142 Cal.App.4th 1457, 1489.) “In order to plead a contract by its legal effect, plaintiff must allege the substance of its relevant terms.” (Id.) Plaintiff has pled that Defendant breached the contract by refusing and failing to provide Plaintiff with access to the broker portal and Harmon’s book of business. These are the substance of the relevant terms; thus, Plaintiff has pled a contract by its legal effect.

 

Next, Defendant argues because the breach of contract claim fails, so does the bad faith claim. (Austero v. Nat'l Cas. Co. (1976) 62 Cal.App.3d 511, 517 [“Where no [contractual] relationship exists, no recovery for “bad faith” may be had.”].)

 

As noted above, Plaintiff has pled consideration and the essential terms of the contract; thus, the complaint sufficiently alleges the existence of a contract between Defendant and Plaintiff. As such, the claim for breach of implied covenant of bad faith and fair dealing also survives.

 

The demurrer is OVERRULED as to the first and sixth causes of action.

 

Second Cause of Action: Intentional Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage

 

            Defendant also demurs to the second cause of action for intentional interference with prospective economic relationship, arguing that Plaintiff does not allege independent wrongful conduct aside from the interference itself.

 

The elements of the tort of intentional interference with prospective economic relationship include the following:

 

(a) An economic relationship between the plaintiff and some third party, with the probability of future economic benefit to the plaintiff.

(b) The defendant's knowledge of the relationship.

(c) Intentional acts by the defendant designed to disrupt the relationship.

(d) Actual disruption of the relationship.

(e) Economic harm to the plaintiff proximately caused by the acts of the defendant.

(f) Conduct that was wrongful by some legal measure other than the fact of interference itself. 

 

(Youst v. Longo (1987) 43 Cal.3d 64, 71.)

 

“[W]hile intentionally interfering with an existing contract is a wrong in and of itself, intentionally interfering with a plaintiff's prospective economic advantage is not. To establish a claim for interference with prospective economic advantage, therefore, a plaintiff must plead that the defendant engaged in an independently wrongful act. An act is not independently wrongful merely because defendant acted with an improper motive…We conclude, therefore, that an act is independently wrongful if it is unlawful, that is, if it is proscribed by some constitutional, statutory, regulatory, common law, or other determinable legal standard.” (Korea Supply Co. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. (2003) 29 Cal.4th 1134, 1158–59, internal citations omitted.) 

 

The FAC alleges that Plaintiff had valid economic relationships with Harmon’s clients that Defendant knew of, but intentionally denied access to disrupt them. Plaintiff argues in opposition that in Stevenson Real Estate Services, Inc., v. CB Richard Ellis Real Estate Services, Inc., (2006) 138 Cal.App.4th 1215, 1221, the court held that violations of well-defined, established rules or standards of a trade, association or profession can constitute wrongful conduct supporting an interference claim. However, Plaintiff has not alleged any well-defined, established rules or standards of a trade in the FAC. As such, the demurrer is sustained with leave to amend on this ground.

 

In the opposition, Plaintiff argues that Defendant's denial of portal access after formal approval constitutes independently wrongful conduct in violation of determinable industry standards governing insurance broker relationships. However, the Court in Stevenson held that independently actionable conduct means “the rules or standards provide for, or give rise to, a sanction or means of enforcement for a violation of the particular rule or standard that allegedly makes the defendant's conduct wrongful. Seldom are the rules or standards of associations, trades and professions likely to give rise to legal causes of action.”  (Id., at 1223.) Thus, Plaintiff must keep this in mind in amending the pleading.

 

Defendant also argues that Plaintiff’s alleged economic relationship with Harmon’s clients is speculative.

 

A plaintiff must establish an existing relationship to establish a claim for intentional interference with prospective economic advantage.” (Salma v. Capon (2008) 161 Cal.App.4th 1275, 1291.)

 

Allegations regarding interference with potential customers are insufficient to state a cause of action for intentional interference with prospective economic advantage. (See Korea Supply Co., supra, 29 Cal.4th at 1164 (stating that the tort of intentional interference with prospective economic advantage “‘protects the expectation that the relationship eventually will yield the desired benefit, not necessarily the more speculative expectation that a potentially beneficial relationship will arise” (quoting Westside Center Associates v. Safeway Stores 23, Inc. (1996) 42 Cal.App.4th 507, 524); Roth v. Rhodes (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 530, 546 (finding that an allegation that the defendant interfered with relations with future customers is insufficient to show an existing relationship).)  

 

However, as Plaintiff points out, he was the surviving spouse and sole heir to Harmon Insurance Services. Thus, Plaintiff inherited all existing client relationships maintained by his late wife's brokerage. (FAC ¶¶ 7-9, Exs. A and B.)

 

Defendant argues in Reply that whether those clients still exist or Harmon continued to service any remaining clients following the passing of Plaintiff’s wife remains unknown from the allegations in the FAC. But this argument is speculative. As Plaintiff inherited the business, the clients currently exist. To argue that they will leave in the future is speculative. The demurrer is overruled on this ground.

 

However, as noted above, based on the failure to plead an independently wrongful act, the demurrer to this cause of action is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

 

Third Cause of Action: Negligent Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage

 

Negligent interference shares the elements of intentional interference, except that “the acts are deemed tortious only where there was an existing duty of care owed by the defendant to the plaintiff. [Citation.] (Golden Eagle Land Investment, L.P. v. Rancho Santa Fe Association (2018) 19 Cal.App.5th 399, 430.)

 

As Plaintiff fails to plead an independently wrongful act, the third cause of action is also SUSTAINED on this ground with leave to amend.

 

Fourth Cause of Action: Negligence

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s cause of action for negligence fails because Blue Shield does not owe Plaintiff any legal duty in tort.

 

The elements for negligence are: (1) a legal duty owed to the plaintiff to use due care; (2) breach of duty; (3) causation; and (4) damage to the plaintiff. (County of Santa Clara v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2006) 137 Cal.App.4th 292, 318.)  In California, negligence may be pleaded in general terms. (Landeros v. Flood (1976) 17 Cal.3d 399, 407-408.)

 

“[W]hile negligence is ordinarily a question of fact, the existence of a duty is generally a question of law that may be addressed by demurrer. (Paul v. Patton (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 1088, 1096.)

 

            Plaintiff has alleged in the FAC that Defendant owed him a duty to reasonably process succession and transfer requests. Defendant contends that an insurer has no duty “to process applications within a particular time period” absent special circumstances not alleged here. (Lunardi v. Great-West Life Assurance Co. (1995) 37 Cal. App. 4th 807.)

 

Negligence is not among the theories of recovery generally available against insurers. (Adelman v. Associated Intern. Ins. Co. (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 352, 369-370; see also Everett Associates, Inc. v. Transcontinental Ins. Co. (N.D. Cal. 2001) 159 F.Supp.2d 1196, 1294 (applying California law).)¿

 

            However, Plaintiff argues that a party who undertakes to render services assumes duty to do so with reasonable care. (Paz v. State of California (2000) 22 Cal.4th 550.) Plaintiff argues that by taking an affirmative step — formally approving Plaintiff's appointment— Blue Shield assumed a duty to complete that process with reasonable care. “To establish a duty of care to a third person based on the negligent undertaking doctrine, a plaintiff must show: (1) the defendant undertook to render services to another; (2) the services were of the kind the defendant should have recognized as necessary for the protection of third persons; and (3) either (a) the defendant's failure to exercise reasonable care increased the risk of harm beyond what existed without the undertaking, (b) the undertaking was to perform a duty owed by the other to the third persons, or (c) a harm was suffered because the other or third persons relied on the undertaking.”  (Barenborg v. Sigma Alpha Epsilon Fraternity (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 70, 83-84.)  Defendant argues that Paz involved whether the defendant’s alleged failure to exercise reasonable care increased the risk of physical harm. (Element 3(a).) But Defendant has not shown that the theory of negligent undertaking cannot also be applied in a professional or commercial setting such as this one.

 

            However, because Plaintiff’s FAC does not plead the undertaking theory, the demurrer to the cause of action for negligence is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

 

Fifth Cause of Action: Conversion

 

“Conversion is the wrongful exercise of dominion over the property of another.  The elements of a conversion are: (1) the plaintiff's ownership or right to possession of the property at the time of the conversion; (2) the defendant’s conversion by a wrongful act or disposition of property rights; and (3) damages.  It is not necessary that there be a manual taking of the property; it is only necessary to show an assumption of control or ownership over the property, or that the alleged converter has applied the property to his own use.”  (Farmers Ins. Exchange v. Zerin (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 445, 451-452.) “To prove a cause of action for conversion, the plaintiff must show the defendant acted intentionally to wrongfully dispose of the property of another.” (Duke v. Sup. Ct. (2017) 18 Cal.App.5th 490, 508.)

 

The FAC alleges that Defendant wrongfully withheld possession and control of Plaintiff’s book of business and client records. Defendant contends there are no allegations showing Defendant acted intentionally to wrongfully dispose of the property as required in Duke.

 

Plaintiff argues in opposition that in Sanowicz v. Bacal (2015) 234 Cal.App.4th 1027, the court held that allegations that a real estate agent’s conversion of commissions due under an agreement by receiving funds, but refusing to pay the plaintiff's share, were sufficient to establish conversion. (Id., at 1042.) However, the court in Sanowicz was considering the first element, whether Sanowicz established an actual interference with his ownership or right of possession of property. Here, the question is whether Defendant acted intentionally to wrongfully dispose of the property, the second element. Thus, Sanowicz does not help Plaintiff.

 

Because Plaintiff has not alleged how Defendant acted intentionally to wrongfully dispose of the property, the demurrer is SUSTAINED with leave to amend as to the fifth cause of action for conversion.

 

CONCLUSION:

Accordingly, Defendant’s Demurrer to the FAC is SUSTAINED in part and OVERRULED in part.

It is overruled as to the first and sixth causes of action.

             It is sustained with 20 days leave to amend as to the second, third, fourth, and fifth causes of action.
