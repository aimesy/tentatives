Case Number: 24LBCV02189    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff, Bertha C. De La Cruz filed this action against Defendant, General Motors LLC for violation of Song-Beverly, breach of implied warranty, and fraud (fraudulent inducement and concealment).    

 

2.     Prior Hearing on Motion to Compel Production of Purchase Contract

The Court heard Defendant’s motion to compel production of the purchase contract on 6/09/26.  Prior to the hearing, the Court issued a detailed tentative ruling granting the unopposed motion to compel production of the purchase contract and imposing sanctions.  The Court’s tentative ruling also included the following discussion re: a further deposition:

The Court notes that the notice of motion and points and authorities indicates Defendant seeks an order compelling production of the purchase contract only.  Nothing in the notice of motion indicates Defendant seeks an order compelling a further deposition.  The notice of motion indicates Defendant was not able to examine Plaintiff on issues relating to the purchase contract, but does not indicate a further deposition is sought.  Similarly, the points and authorities, at page 3, lines 18-20, indicate Defendant seeks an order compelling production of the purchase contract and awarding monetary sanctions, but there is no mention of a further deposition.  See also page 6, lines 2-3, page 6, lines 19-20, page 7, lines 10-12, and the conclusion, page 7, lines 5-12, seeking only an order compelling production and imposing sanctions.  Defense Counsel’s declaration, however, at ¶12, seeks an order compelling Plaintiff to sit for a further deposition.  The Court cannot grant relief that is not requested in the notice of motion or the points and authorities and is mentioned only in the supporting declaration.  To the extent a further deposition is necessary, the Court asks the parties to resolve the issues without further court intervention; the Court is not ordering the deposition at this time.

 

The parties appeared and argued, and the Court’s final minute order is internally inconsistent.  It continues to include the above language from the tentative ruling, but then, at the end of the minute order, it states, “Further deposition of Plaintiff is ordered to take place by June 18, 2026.” 

 

3.     Motion to Compel Compliance with 6/09/26 Order

On 7/02/26, Defendant filed a motion to compel compliance with the 6/09/26 order.  Defendant alternatively seeks monetary, terminating, issue, and/or evidentiary sanctions.  Defendant contends Plaintiff has neither produced the purchase contract nor sat for the continued session of her deposition. 

 

Plaintiff, in opposition, contends she was never ordered to sit for a second session of her deposition, citing the portion of the tentative ruling quoted above that ultimately made its way into the final minute order after hearing.  She fails, however, to explain why she has not obeyed the order compelling her to produce the purchase contract.  Indeed, her opposition is entirely silent in this regard. 

 

Plaintiff filed her opposition papers timely, but Defendant did not file reply papers until four court days prior to the hearing, which is one day late per CCP §1005(b).  The Court briefly reviewed the reply papers, but notes that they were not necessary to the ruling. 

 

The Court wishes to hear from the parties at the hearing concerning why Plaintiff has not produced the purchase contract and whether she plans to do so.  The Court clarifies that the portion of the 6/09/26 order declining to order a second session of deposition appears to have been included in the final order by mistake, and clarifies that Plaintiff must sit for a second session of her deposition.  Counsel must meet and confer forthwith to schedule the second session of the deposition.

 

The Court will not impose sanctions of any kind based on the failure to appear for a second session of the deposition, as the prior minute order was internally inconsistent in this regard.  The Court will determine, after hearing from the parties at the hearing, whether to impose sanctions for Plaintiff’s failure to produce the purchase contract.  The Court is inclined to impose monetary sanctions in this regard, but not to impose extremely harsh issue, evidentiary, and/or terminating sanctions so long as Plaintiff agrees to produce the contract forthwith. 

 

If the parties submit on the tentative, Plaintiff will be ordered to produce the purchase contract within two days, work with Defense Counsel to schedule a second session of her deposition immediately (with the deposition to go forward within ten days), and monetary sanctions will be imposed in the amount of $2100 against Plaintiff and her attorney of record, jointly and severally. 

 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV00201    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: S27

 

a.     Relief Sought

Defendant moves to bifurcate the liability phase of the trial from the damages phase of the trial.  While not entirely clear from the notice of motion, which states only “liability” and “damages,” it appears Defendant also seek to bifurcate the punitive damages phase of the trial from the liability phase of the trial.

 

b.     Law Governing Bifurcation

Bifurcation is the procedure whereby the court may order separate trials of issues of parties joined in a single action.  The objective of bifurcation is to avoid wasting time and money on the trial of damages issues if the liability issue is resolved against the plaintiff.  Also, the procedure is not limited to separate trials of liability and damages; nor is it limited to dividing a case into only two parts.  A party seeking bifurcation should request such relief as soon as the need becomes apparent.  Delay may be a factor affecting the court’s exercise of discretion.  Generally, the court has the inherent power to regulate the order of trial and, therefore, can entertain a motion to bifurcate at any time, even during the trial.  However, where bifurcation is sought pursuant to CCP §598 (e.g., to obtain bifurcation of liability issue), the order must be made no later than 30 days before trial.  (I.e., the motion must be heard and order made more than 30 days before trial.)

 

Pursuant to CCP §1048(b), “The court, in furtherance of convenience or to avoid prejudice, or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy, may order a separate trial of any cause of action, including a cause of action asserted in a cross-complaint, or of any separate issue or of any number of causes of action or issues, preserving the right of trial by jury required by the Constitution or a statute of the state or of the United States.”

 

c.     Analysis

CCP §3295(d) mandates bifurcation of the issue of punitive damages in the event the defendant requests such relief.  Despite the fact that the notice of motion is not clear in this regard, the Court finds it is in the interest of justice to hear the punitive damages phase of the trial, if necessary, after the liability and compensatory damages phase of the trial.  The Court notes that Defendant fully briefed the issue in the memorandum of points and authorities, and Plaintiff did not oppose the motion.   

 

To the extent Defendant moves to bifurcate the issue of liability from the issue of compensatory damages, the motion is denied.  It is not clear whether Defendant seeks bifurcation in this regard, as Defendant’s notice of motion and points and authorities seem to use the words “damages,” “non-economic damages,” and “punitive damages” interchangeably.  Defendant did not meaningfully show how or why liability should be bifurcated from economic/compensatory damages. 

 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV00638    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff, Denise Guesno (“Denise”) filed this action against Defendants, Lawrence E. Guesno (“Lawrence”)  and Entourage Property Management, Inc. (“EPM”) (together “Defendants”) on March 7, 2025, asserting claims for (1) involuntary dissolution of corporation, (2) breach of fiduciary duty, (3) corporate waste, and (4) removal of a corporate officer/director.

 

Denise and Lawrence are or were engaged in dissolution of marriage proceedings. They jointly own EPM, the entity subject to Denise’s various corporate misgovernance claims.  Specifically, Denise alleges she owns at least 41% of EPM, and her interest is at risk of being lost or materially injured because Lawrence, EPM’s current Director, CEO, and majority shareholder has (1) distributed at least $1.374 million from EPM to himself, (2) caused EPM to loan $1.7 million to third parties so he can purchase real estate in his individual capacity at reduced prices, (3) “is threatening” to lend himself more than $1 million of EPM’s funds, (4) uses EPM’s assets as his own, and (5) refuses to allow Denise to inspect EPM’s records.

 

2.     Motion to Stay Action

Defendant moves to stay this action, contending there is a risk of inconsistent rulings if this action goes forward while the family law proceeding remains ongoing.

 

There is a hearing scheduled in the divorce court for 10/13/26.  The hearing is on Plaintiff’s request for a DVPA Restraining Order.  The DVPA is the Domestic Violence Protection Act.  It permits the family law court to issue injunctive relief to prevent a party from exercising coercive control over the other party, and permits the family law court to issue an order granting injunctive relief over the opposing party’s community, quasi-community, and/or separate property.  Plaintiff, in connection with this hearing, is seeking an order restraining Defendant from engaging in many of the acts at issue in this case – looting EPM’s assets, transferring monies, and operating competing businesses.  Plaintiff also seeks an order, in the family law court, appointing a referee over EPM and the competing businesses to preserve the assets while the parties’ family law and civil law proceedings are ongoing.

 

Defendant spends much of his opening brief citing portions of Plaintiff’s complaint and various documents filed in this action wherein Plaintiff seeks injunctive relief, restraining orders, and/or appointment of a referee over those same properties.  Those requests, however, have been denied, with this Court finding Plaintiff’s remedy in the civil action is damages. 

 

The purpose of the proceedings in the family law court is to preserve the assets that are at issue in this civil court while this civil action is ongoing.  While the Court finds the procedural posture extremely complicated, the Court is inclined to find, at this time, that there is no risk of inconsistent rulings between the family proceedings and the civil proceedings.  The family court may impose various remedies pending the outcome of the civil proceedings, but ultimately the issue in the civil proceedings will be whether Defendant has adequately protected Plaintiff’s minority interest in EPM and to what extent Plaintiff is entitled to monetary damages if he has not done so. 

 

Defendant also argues at length that Plaintiff has taken the position, in the family law court, that EPM is community property.  In May of 2025, the family law court held it is not.  Defendant, in reply, quotes portions of Plaintiff’s papers, filed in August of 2026, wherein she refers to funds and assets belonging to EPM, “and therefore to the community estate…”  The Court wishes to hear from the parties at the hearing concerning the implications of these statements, but it appears there has been a final order that EPM is NOT community property, and that each spouse has a separate property interest in his or her percentage share of EPM.

 

Finally, Defendant argues the family law court recently issued an extremely large attorneys’ fees award in Plaintiff’s favor, and contends this could conflict with any ruling issued in the civil court, where fees are also at issue.  The Court will not permit double recovery of the same fees.  While this could become complicated at some time, the Court finds there is no risk that Defendant will have to pay the same fees twice.

 

Ultimately, the issue in this civil action is whether Defendant, as majority shareholder in EPM, has devalued Plaintiff’s minority shareholder interest in the company and, if so, the amount of Plaintiff’s damages as a result of the devaluation.  The pending issue in the family law action is whether Defendant is engaged in financial acts that are designed to deprive Plaintiff of her rights to property and/or money, in which the family law court can take any acts, including restraining Defendant’s separate property, to protect Plaintiff from the abusive financial acts taken by Defendant.  While the issues are similar, they are ultimately distinct, and the motion for a stay is denied.

 

3.     Motion for Leave to Amend

a.     Relief Sought

Plaintiff seeks leave to file an amended complaint.  The scope of the proposed amendment is vast, and much of Plaintiff’s proposed amendment is simply adding allegations, clarifying prior allegations, etc.  The crux of her amendment, however, is the addition of six additional defendants, all of whom she seeks to allege aided and abetted the individual defendant in looting the assets of Entourage to deprive Plaintiff of her interest in the entity.  She also seeks leave to add clarifying allegations about the six defendants, their relationships to the individual defendant, and the transactions that she alleges were fraudulent in nature. 

 

b.     Opposition

Defendant opposes the motion.  He argues the motion should be denied because the proposed amendment is in bad faith and because the proposed amendment is a sham designed to drive up his legal costs while seeking access to property under the jurisdiction of the divorce court. 

 

c.     Law Governing Leave to Amend

The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on such terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.  CCP §§473 and 576.  Judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters between the parties and, therefore, leave to amend is generally liberally granted.  Ordinarily, the court will not consider the validity of the proposed amended pleading in ruling on a motion for leave since grounds for a demurrer or motion to strike are premature.  However, the court does have discretion to deny leave to amend where a proposed amendment fails to state a valid cause of action as a matter of law and the defect cannot be cured by further amendment.  California Casualty General Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1985) 173 Cal.App.3d 274, 281. 

 

The application for leave to amend should be made as soon as the need to amend is discovered.  The closer the trial date, the stronger the showing required for leave to amend.  If the party seeking the amendment has been dilatory, and the delay has prejudiced the opposing party, the Court has the discretion to deny leave to amend.  (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.)  Prejudice exists where the amendment would require delaying the trial, resulting in loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation such as an increased burden of discovery.  (Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 486-488.)

 

d.     Analysis

            Defendant’s first argument is that Plaintiff is making the motion in bad faith and the proposed amendment is a sham.  Defendant does not meaningfully articulate how or why this is so.  Defendant contends Plaintiff has known about the proposed new defendants for a long time due to the ongoing divorce proceedings, but this does not render the proposed amendment a sham; this would, at most, trigger the delay/prejudice test detailed above.

 

Even if the Court were to find delay, the Court cannot find prejudice.  Defendant complains that, if the motion is granted, he will have to expend additional attorneys’ fees defending the added claims.  This is not, however, the type of prejudice contemplated when determining whether or not to grant leave to amend. 

 

Defendant also argues the proposed claims violate the jurisdiction of the divorce court.  It appears Defendant’s argument is that the new defendants are his separate property, and if this court were to rule otherwise, it would violate the divorce court’s jurisdiction.  Plaintiff, in reply, however, concedes the companies are Defendant’s separate property, and explains that the purpose of the amendment is to add claims against the entities for wrongfully assisting Defendant in devaluing the parties’ community property company, Entourage.  The Court finds it cannot refuse to grant leave to amend on this ground. 

 

e.     Conclusion

In light of the liberal standard in favor of leave to amend, the motion is granted.  Plaintiff is ordered to file a separate copy of her amended complaint within ten days. 

 

4.     Objections to Referee’s Report and Recommendations

EPM inadvertently produced various documents during discovery and then attempted to claw them back.  The parties presented the issue to the referee, who held that the documents that form the subject of this motion should not be clawed back.  EPM and Mr. Guesno object to the recommendations.

 

The subject documents are communications by and between Mr. Guesno, his own attorney(s), and EPM’s attorney.  The crux of the referee’s ruling is that, because this is a derivative lawsuit by Ms. Guesno against EPM, EPM is only a nominal defendant and is, in reality, a plaintiff in the action.  The referee therefore concluded that EPM and Mr. Guesno do not share a “common interest” in the litigation and therefore any documents that are shared by and between them and their attorneys are not attorney-client privileged due to the third party communication exception to the privilege. 

 

The referee relied on various cases decided in the context of attorney disqualification motions to reach his conclusion.  The referee expressly conceded, in his report, that these cases were not directly on point, but found them compelling and determined the documents need not be returned to EPM.

 

EMP and Mr. Guesno have each filed objections to the report.  Ms. Guesno did not file anything in support of the report or its conclusions.  The Court has read and considered the objections and finds they must be sustained.  The Court read and considered Hoiles v. Superior Court (1984) 157 Cal.App.3d 1192, 1195, which both EPM and Mr. Guesno relied on in their papers.  In Hoiles, the plaintiff was a minority shareholder of a closely held corporation.  The plaintiff was alleging breach of fiduciary duty and seeking dissolution of the corporation.  Corporate counsel raised attorney-client privilege objections at a deposition as to questions concerning prelitigation strategy and requests.  The trial court sustained the objections, and the court of appeals affirmed.  The crux of the ruling was that the existence of other family members and corporate counsel at the meetings did not defeat the attorney-client privilege. 

 

While Hoiles is not directly on point, the Court is inclined to find that Mr. Guesno’s sharing of his thoughts between his own attorney and EPM’s attorney, similar to the conversations in Hoiles, did not defeat the privilege.  As Mr. Guesno and EPM both note in their papers, Mr. Guesno was the sole manager of EPM and the only person actively doing work for EPM.  It is therefore difficult to imagine whom EPM’s attorney would communicate with to address legal concerns during ongoing proceedings.

 

The objections are therefore sustained.  Plaintiff must return the subject documents to EPM.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV02502    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: S27

Plaintiff filed this lawsuit against Defendant for damages arising out of a trip and fall.  Defendant propounded FROGs and RPDs, and Plaintiff served responses.  After meeting and conferring, the parties were unable to resolve issues relating to production of Plaintiff’s VA medical records, and this motion was filed.

 

At issue are RPDs 12, 13, and 14 and FROGs 6.4 and 6.7.  The subject RPDs seek production of Plaintiff’s medical records.  Plaintiff responded to the RPDs, stating, “After a diligent search and reasonable inquiry, Plaintiff is not in possession or control of any responsive documents. Plaintiff’s treatment has primarily been through the Veterans Administration medical system. However, as a result of locating, Plaintiff is unable to produce responsive documents at this time. Investigation and discovery are continuing.” 

 

The subject FROGs seek information about Plaintiff’s care and treatment.  Plaintiff responded to the subject FROGs, stating, “Plaintiff received medical treatment through the Long Beach Veterans Administration and obtained a second opinion from UGI, with treatment involving multiple rotating physicians through the VA system. Plaintiff's primary VA facility is located in Prescott, which may refer Plaintiff to a facility in Lake Havasu that, in turn, may refer Plaintiff to private care providers as necessary.”

 

The relevant timeline is as follows: Plaintiff made a request for his records from the VA on 7/15/25.  He filed suit on 9/08/25.  Defendant propounded discovery on 3/26/26.  Plaintiff responded to discovery, with the responses detailed above, on 6/04/26.   Plaintiff made a second VA records request on 7/27/26. 

 

Defendant makes much of this timeline, arguing Plaintiff’s failure to search for records between receiving discovery on 3/26/26 and responding on 6/04/26 renders his responses deficient, as he was required to conduct the search in response to receiving discovery.  The Court finds Plaintiff’s request, on 7/15/25, for his own medical records was sufficient to satisfy his obligation to “search for” his records.  That request remained outstanding at the time he served his responses, such that it satisfies his obligation to verify he has made a reasonable search and exercised reasonable diligence in obtaining his records.

 

Plaintiff explains, in opposition to the motion, that the VA is “notoriously slow” in responding to medical record requests.  Defendant explains, in the moving papers, that it cannot subpoena the records because the VA is a federal facility, and only Plaintiff can obtain the records.  The situation creates a quandary, as Plaintiff needs to obtain the records, but the VA is making that difficult. 

 

The Court wishes to hear from the parties, at the hearing, about available avenues for obtaining the records and what Plaintiff is doing to obtain the records.  Has Plaintiff physically gone to a VA facility to ask for the records?  Has Plaintiff made follow-up phone calls to obtain the records?  Plaintiff cannot merely send off the records requests and then hope and wait for the VA to respond, knowing it is loathe to do so.  The Court also wishes to know whether there is an app, similar to the MyChart app most medical providers use, where Plaintiff can access his own medical records at any time without the need to authorize their release. 

 

The Court’s tentative ruling is to not impose sanctions, as Plaintiff has attempted to obtain the records and been unable to do so.  The Court will consider this issue at the hearing after hearing from Plaintiff concerning available methods for obtaining the records and what reasonable and diligent attempts he has made to obtain same. 

 

If the parties submit on the tentative ruling, the Court will continue the hearing for two months for Plaintiff to continue to make diligent attempts to obtain his records and the parties will be ordered to file status reports re: the discovery at least two weeks prior to the continued hearing.

 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV02545    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: S27

Plaintiff moves to compel responses to FROGs, RPDs, and RFAs from Defendant, IO Environmental & Infrastructure, Inc. and FROGs, RPDs, and SROGs from Defendant, Cesar Perez.  Plaintiff propounded all discovery on 3/02/26, and Defendants have not served responses. 

 

The motion to compel IO to respond to FROGs and RPDs is granted.  The motion to compel Perez to respond to FROGs, RPDs, and SROGs is granted.  Defendants are ordered to serve verified responses to the outstanding discovery, without objections, within ten days.  CCP §§2030.290(a),(b), 2031.300(a),(b). 

 

The motion to compel responses to RFAs is denied.  CCP §2033.280, which Plaintiff cites as authority for the request, permits the Court to deem RFAs admitted, but not to order a party to respond to RFAs.  Plaintiff does not seek an order deeming the RFAs admitted, and therefore no relief is awarded in connection with RFAs. 

 

Sanctions are mandatory.  §§2030.290(c), 2031.300(c).  Plaintiff seeks imposition of sanctions against Defendants and their attorney of record in the total amount of $1110, which amount is both reasonable and fully supported by Plaintiff’s attorney’s declaration.  Defendants and their attorney of record, jointly and severally, are ordered to pay sanctions to Plaintiff, by and through his attorney of record, in the total amount of $1110, within twenty days. 

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 26LBCV00742    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: S27

Plaintiff seeks an order consolidating two actions that arise out of the same automobile accident.  The moving party is suing (the plaintiff) in one action and is being sued (the defendant) in the other.  The motion is denied without prejudice because the cases have not been deemed related and are not pending in the same department.  25LBCV01938, the earlier filed case, is currently pending in Department S28.  The motion to consolidate is therefore premature.  Local Rule 3.3(g)(1). 

 

If Plaintiff renews the motion, Plaintiff is asked to closely read and consider all Rules of Court and Local Rules governing such relief, including rules making clear that relief must be sought in the earliest-filed case. 

 

The parties are reminded that there is a Case Management Conference scheduled concurrently with the hearing on the above motion.  The Court asks the parties to make arrangements to appear remotely at the CMC and hearing on the motion.
