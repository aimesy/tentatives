Case Number: 20SMCV00385    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Kim Kanner’s Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 by Appointment of the Independent Referee Contemplated by Sections 2.3(c) and 2.5 of the Settlement Agreement is GRANTED. 

The Court appoints the Honorable Terry Green (Ret.) as a referee for this action.

Plaintiff Kim Kanner to give notice. 


REASONING

Plaintiff Kim Kranner (“Plaintiff”) moves the Court for an order enforcing the parties’ March 11, 2024 Settlement Agreement by appointing the independent referee contemplated by Sections 2.3(c) and 2.5 of the Settlement Agreement pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 638 and 640.

“If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If requested by the parties, the court may retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6.)  

Here, Plaintiff has shown that the parties requested that the Court retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement. (Johnson Decl., Ex. A – Release and Settlement Agreement § 2.8; March 27, 2024 Order.) Thus, the Court has jurisdiction.

Plaintiff argues that Sections 2.3(c) and 2.5 provide that disputes concerning the sale of the Property and the purchase of the parties’ respective interests in Kanan Development shall be determined by an independent referee.

Section 2.3(c) provides that “[i]f a party declines an offer and does not make an offer to purchase, the referee shall determine if the offer being made is reasonable under the circumstances and may order the sale for the Property or interest on the terms set forth in the offer.” (Johnson Decl., Ex. A – Release and Settlement Agreement § 2.3(c).) Section 2.5 provides that “[a]ny conflict with respect to the sale of the Property or the purchase of the interest in Kanan Development as set forth above shall be conclusively resolved by the independent referee appointed by Judge Gross. The matter shall be submitted to the referee by written application for determination and shall be determined after a single hearing in which oral argument may be made by counsel for each party.” (Johnson Decl., Ex. A – Release and Settlement Agreement § 2.5.)

Here, Plaintiff has shown that the Settlement Agreement includes an agreement in writing that any or all issues shall be heard by referee and thus, the appointment of a referee is proper. (Code Civ. Proc., § 638, subd. (a) [“A referee may be appointed upon the agreement of the parties filed with the clerk, or judge, or entered in the minutes, or upon the motion of a party to a written contract or lease that provides that any controversy arising therefrom shall be heard by a referee if the court finds a reference agreement exists between the parties . . . [t]o hear and determine any or all of the issues in an action or proceeding, whether of fact or of law, and to report a statement of decision”].)

Code of Civil Procedure section 640 provides the procedure for appointing a referee. “The court shall appoint as referee or referees the person or persons, not exceeding three, agreed upon by the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 640, subd. (a).)

Plaintiff has provided three nominees for appointment as referee whom Plaintiff argues possess substantial experience in complex civil litigation and service as a judicial neutral. Defendants Richard Thurman and Kanan Development, L.P. (formerly Kanan Development, LLC) (collectively, “Defendants”) in their notice of non-opposition state that they do not oppose Plaintiff’s motion to appoint an independent referee and that they specifically agree and consent to the appointment of Hon. Terry Green (Ret.), who was proposed as a referee for this action by Plaintiff. Defendants request the appointment of Hon. Terry Green (Ret.) in this action.

Accordingly, the Court appoints the Honorable Terry Green (Ret.) as a referee for this action.

Case Number: 24SMCV06171    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Gabriel St. Angel’s Motion to Compel the Deposition of Defendant David Powers and Request for Monetary Sanctions is GRANTED. The parties shall meet and confer regarding the deposition of Defendant David Powers to occur within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.

Plaintiff Gabriel St. Angel’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,860.00 payable by Defendant David Powers and his counsel of record, jointly and severally, within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.  

Plaintiff Gabriel St. Angel to give notice.

REASONING

Request for Judicial Notice
Defendant David Powers (“Defendant”) seeks judicial notice of the Assembly Committee on Judiciary Report of Assembly Bill A.B. 1197 (2015-2016 Session) as amended on April 6, 2025 (analyzing current Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.220). Defendant’s request for judicial notice is GRANTED pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (c).

Motion to Compel Deposition
Plaintiff Gabriel St. Angel (“Plaintiff”) moves the Court for an order overruling Defendant’s objection to the use of Steno Agency, Inc. (“Steno”) as the court reporter and compelling the deposition of Defendant to take place within ten days.

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.230, without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent's attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

On April 13, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel served a Second Amended Notice of Deposition on Defendant scheduled for May 20, 2026. (Declaration of Robert J. Ounjian (“Ounjian Decl.”), ¶ 2; Ex. 1.) On May 8, 2026, Defendant served an objection on the grounds that “Steno is a self-proclaimed litigation financing service that has an improper financial interest in the case” because Steno’s “Delay Pay” arrangement constitutes improper litigation financing and Plaintiff failed to disclose this financing pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.220(a)(8)(A) and Defendant’s counsel was willing to produce Defendant for deposition if Plaintiff did not use Steno. (Ounjian Decl., ¶ 3; Ex. 2.)

“A party desiring to take the oral deposition of any person shall give notice in writing. The deposition notice shall state all of the following, in at least 12-point type . . . A statement disclosing the existence of a contract, if any is known to the noticing party, between the noticing party or a third party who is financing all or part of the action and either of the following for any service beyond the noticed deposition: (i) The deposition officer. (ii) The entity providing the services of the deposition officer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.220. subd. (a)(8)(A).)

The Court overrules Defendant’s objection to Steno because Steno is not providing any service beyond the noticed deposition. Steno is providing a service to conduct a deposition through a CSR and Steno’s Delay Pay simply allows Plaintiff, or any of its clients, to pay for the noticed deposition at the conclusion of the litigation. (Declaration of Dylan Ruga (“Ruga Decl.”), ¶ 1.) Plaintiff’s counsel is obligated to pay Steno for all services rendered regardless of the outcome of the litigation. (Ruga Decl., ¶ 5.) Because Steno is not providing a service beyond conducting a deposition through a CSR, the Court grants the motion to compel deposition of Defendant.

Monetary Sanctions
Plaintiff requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,460.00 against Defendant and his counsel, jointly and severally. 

“If a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).)

Because Defendant’s objection lacks merit, the Court finds that sanctions are warranted. Accordingly, the Court grants sanctions in the amount of $1,860.00 (4.5 hours at $400/hour plus $60.00 filing fee) against Defendant and his counsel of record, jointly and severally.


Case Number: 25SMCV03661    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

J. Raza Lawrence of Zuber Lawler LLP’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendants Rene’s Cultivar LLC and Nicholas Jammal is CONTINUED to TBD for counsel to provide sufficient proof of service of all documents associated with this motion to the new address provided in the notice of errata.

Counsel has asserted a valid reason for withdrawal, i.e., the clients have breached material obligations under the parties’ retention agreement, and the attorney-client relationship has broken down. (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4), (b)(5).) Counsel has provided the required forms under rule 3.1362 of the California Rules of Court, but he failed to provide proof of service of the moving papers at the new address identified as required by rule 3.1362, subdivisions (d) and (e), of the California Rules of Court. While the notice of errata states that true copies were mailed to the corrected address provided by Defendants, Mr. Lawrence has not filed a proof of service showing Defendants were served at the new address. Thus, J. Raza Lawrence of Zuber Lawler LLP’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendants Rene’s Cultivar LLC and Nicholas Jammal is CONTINUED toTBD for counsel to provide sufficient proof of service of all documents associated with this motion.

Case Number: 25SMCV04823    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

James Raza Lawrence of Zuber Lawler LLP’s Motions to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendants Rene’s Cultivar, LLC & Nicholas Jammal are GRANTED.

Counsel has asserted a valid reason for withdrawal, i.e., the clients have breached material obligations under the parties’ engagement agreement and have not rectified the issues despite being given the opportunity to do so. (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4).) Counsel has provided the required forms under rule 3.1362 of the California Rules of Court and served the clients and opposing parties with the same and has provided proposed orders and served the same as required by rule 3.1362, subdivisions (d) and (e), of the California Rules of Court. There being good cause to grant counsel’s request to withdraw, James Raza Lawrence of Zuber Lawler LLP’s Motions to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendants Rene’s Cultivar, LLC & Nicholas Jammal are GRANTED. The ruling granting counsel’s motions to withdraw shall become effective upon filing of proof of service of the ruling upon Defendants Rene’s Cultivar, LLC & Nicholas Jammal.

Case Number: 26SMCV00320    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Michael Lewis’s Motion to Compel Initial Responses to First Set of Demands for Production of Documents is GRANTED. The Court orders Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections, within 10 days of service of the Court’s order.

The Court also grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,071.76, to be paid by Defendant within 30 days of service of the Court’s order.

Plaintiff to give notice.


REASONING

According to Plaintiff, on May 8, 2026, Defendant Global was served with Demand for Production of Documents (Set One).  Plaintiff states that he never received responses, objections, or requests for an extension.  Thus, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel Defendant’s responses to the discovery request.  Plaintiff also requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,071.76 against Defendant: $1,000 under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.050, subdivision (a), $60 for motion filing fees, and $11.76 for electronic filing fees.

In opposition, Defendant states that it has fully responded to the Demand for Production of Documents.

In its reply, Plaintiff does not dispute that responses have been served.  However, Plaintiff requests that the Court order all objections to be waived and order Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections, and produce all responsive documents, within 10 days, given that the responses were not timely.  Plaintiff also requests sanctions in the amount of $1,000 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.050, subdivision (a), plus $71.76 in filing and electronic-filing fees, to be paid by Defendant within 30 days of the Court’s order.

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., §2031.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom requests for production of documents is directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling response to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., §2031.300, subd. (b).) The responding party also waives the right to make any objections, including ones based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., §2031.300, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to production of documents other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., §§2024.020, subd. (a), 2031.300.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411.)

Code of Civil Procedure, section 2023.030, subdivision (a) provides, in pertinent part, that the court may impose a monetary sanction on a party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone because of that conduct. Misuse of discovery includes “failing to respond or submit to an authorized method of discovery.” (Code Civ. Proc., §2023.010, subd. (d)). Furthermore, pursuant to section 2031.300, subdivision (c), the court shall impose monetary sanctions “against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  Finally, the court shall impose a $1,000 sanction if the court finds that “[t]he party, person, or attorney did not respond in good faith to a request for production of documents,” produced the requested documents within seven days before the hearing on the motion to compel production of documents, or failed to confer in a reasonable and good faith attempt to informally resolve the dispute.  (Code Civ. Proc., §2023.050, subd. (a).)

Having reviewed the moving papers, Opposition, and Reply, the Court makes the following findings. Although Defendant served responses to the discovery request, given that these responses were untimely, Defendant has waived all objections and has not sought relief from the waiver. The service of an untimely response does not divest the trial court of its authority to hear and grant a motion to compel responses. (Sinaiko, 148 Cal.App.4th at 405.) Accordingly, the Court orders Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections within 10 days of service of the Court’s order.

The Court also finds that monetary sanctions are mandatory because Defendant unsuccessfully opposed the motion and has not shown substantial justification or other circumstances making imposition of sanctions unjust.

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff Michael Lewis’s Motion to Compel Initial Responses to First Set of Demands for Production of Documents is GRANTED. The Court orders Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections, within 10 days of service of the Court’s order.

The Court also grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,071.76, to be paid by Defendant within 30 days of service of the Court’s order.
