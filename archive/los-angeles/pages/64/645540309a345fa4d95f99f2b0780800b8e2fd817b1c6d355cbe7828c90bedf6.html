DEFENDANT JASWINDER DHINSA’S DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

 

Motion filed on April 22, 2026.

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Jaswinder Dhinsa

RESPONDING PARTY: None

NOTICE: OK.

RELIEF REQUESTED: Order sustaining demurrer to Plaintiff Amir Edwards’s Complaint.

RULING: Overruled as moot.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On June 9, 2025, Amir Edwards (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against Defendants Jaswinder Dhinsa (Defendant Dhinsa), Clearview Real Estate Holdings LLC, and Does 1 through 10, asserting causes of action for intentional misrepresentation, negligent misrepresentation, abuse of process, civil conspiracy, and intentional infliction of emotional distress. Defendant Dhinsa filed this demurrer on April 22, 2026, supported by the declaration of Bradley T. Weeks. Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint on May 6, 2026. No timely opposition nor reply was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

“A party may amend its pleading once without leave of the court at any time before the answer, demurrer, or motion to strike is filed, or after a demurrer or motion to strike is filed but before the demurrer or motion to strike is heard if the amended pleading is filed and served no later than the date for filing an opposition to the demurrer or motion to strike.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 472, subd. (a).) When plaintiff files an amended complaint after a demurrer is filed, but before it is decided, the demurrer must be overruled as moot. (JKC3H8 v. Colton (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468, 477.)

 

Here, Defendant Dhinsa demurs to Plaintiff’s original Complaint. Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint on May 6, 2026, after the demurrer was filed and before the July 13, 2026 hearing. The First Amended Complaint supersedes the original Complaint and is now the operative pleading. Defendant Dhinsa’s demurrer no longer challenges the operative pleading. The demurrer is moot.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Defendant Dhinsa’s demurrer to the Complaint is overruled as moot.

DEFENDANT LOS ANGELES COUNTY METROPOLITAN TRANSPORTATION AUTHORITY’S MOTION TO DISMISS PLAINTIFF’S COMPLAINT

 

Motion filed on April 21, 2026.
MOVING PARTY: Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, erroneously sued as Los Angeles Metropolitan Transportation Authority
RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Leticia Rodriguez
NOTICE: OK.
RELIEF REQUESTED: Order dismissing Plaintiff’s complaint for failure to comply with the Court’s February 25, 2026 discovery order.
RULING: Granted in part.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On June 6, 2025, Leticia Rodriguez (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against Los Angeles Metropolitan Transportation Authority (Defendant). Defendant answered on July 15, 2025. On February 25, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s motions to compel Plaintiff’s responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One, and awarded monetary sanctions.

 

Defendant filed this motion on April 21, 2026, supported by the declaration of Gabriel H. Wainfeld and Exhibit A. No timely opposition was filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Disobeying a discovery order constitutes a misuse of the discovery process, for which the court may impose monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, subd. (g); 2023.030; 2030.290, subd. (c); 2031.300, subd. (c); Van Sickle v. Gilbert (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1495, 1516.) Terminating sanctions under section 2023.030, subdivision (d), include staying further proceedings until the party obeys a discovery order, dismissing the action, and entering a default judgment.

 

Because terminating sanctions are drastic, they must be used sparingly, tailored to the harm, and generally imposed in an incremental approach, with dismissal as a last resort. (Dep’t of Forestry & Fire Prot. v. Howell (2017) 18 Cal.App.5th 154, 191.) A court should generally not impose dismissal as a first sanction unless it finds lesser sanctions would be ineffective. (Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Soc’y of New York, Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604 (Lopez).) Where a stay is imposed, the court should specify a deadline for compliance and set a hearing to consider further sanctions if the party fails to comply. (See Duggan v. Moss (1979) 98 Cal.App.3d 735, 742–744.) The Court considers the totality of the circumstances, including whether the conduct was willful, the detriment to the propounding party, and the number of unsuccessful attempts to obtain the discovery. (Los Defensores, Inc. v. Gomez (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 377, 390.)

 

Here, Plaintiff disobeyed the Court’s February 25, 2026 discovery order. The Court ordered Plaintiff to serve verified responses, without objections, to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days. The Court also ordered Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel to pay $1,050.00 in monetary sanctions within 30 days. Defendant’s counsel declares Plaintiff has served no responses. (Wainfeld Decl. ¶ 4.) Plaintiff filed no opposition and offers no justification for the failure to comply. On this record, the Court finds Plaintiff’s noncompliance willful. Plaintiff’s noncompliance prejudices Defendant because Defendant still lacks verified responses to basic written discovery served in July 2025.

 

Still, dismissal remains premature. The Court has imposed only monetary sanctions. It has not yet imposed a stay, issue sanction, or evidence sanction, and the record does not show that lesser sanctions would be ineffective. Defendant’s request for dismissal is denied without prejudice, and the Court imposes the lesser terminating sanction of a stay.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Defendant’s request for dismissal is DENIED without prejudice.

 

The Court GRANTS a lesser terminating sanction. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 2023.030, subd. (d)(2), all proceedings as to Plaintiff Leticia Rodriguez are hereby STAYED.
