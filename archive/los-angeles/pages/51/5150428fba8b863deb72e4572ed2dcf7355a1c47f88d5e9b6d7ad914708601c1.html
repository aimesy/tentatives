DEPARTMENT I LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25VECV06273    Hearing Date: August 10, 2026    Dept: I

25VECV06273 ENEKA FRANKLIN-CUMMINGS vs BMW OF NORTH AMERICA, LLC, A DELAWARE LIMITED LIABILITY COMPANY, et al.

August 10, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling – Motion to Compel and Sanctions

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On October 31, 2025, Plaintiff Eneka Franklin-Cummings (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against BMW of North America, LLC (“BMW”), Sherman Oaks-B, Inc. dba BMW of Sherman Oaks (“BMW Sherman Oaks”), and Does 1 to 10 alleging four causes of action: (1) breach of express warranty under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; (2) breach of implied warranty under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act Section 1793.2; and (4) negligent repair.

 

On June 24, 2026, BMW filed the instant motion to compel the deposition of Plaintiff. BMW also requests sanctions.

 

On July 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed her opposition.

 

On July 30, 2026, BMW filed its reply.

 

II.     Legal Standard

 

CCP § 2025.450 provides in pertinent part:

 

“(a) If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director,

managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible described in the deposition notice.

 

(b) A motion under subdivision (a) shall comply with both of the following:

 

(1) The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.

 

(2) The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040 or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”

 

III.  Discussion

 

On December 11, 2025, BMW served its notice of deposition on Plaintiff scheduled for January 26, 2026. (Moore Decl. ¶ 4; Exh. A [notice].) On January 19, 2026, Plaintiff served objections stating that Plaintiff was unavailable but did not provide alternative dates for deposition. (Ibid.) BMW subsequently attempted to meet and confer with Plaintiff’s counsel. (Id. at ¶ 5; Exh. B [meet and confer correspondences].) As of the date of this motion, Plaintiff failed to respond to BMW’s meet and confer efforts or provide alternative dates for deposition. (Id. at ¶ 6.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that, on July 7, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel sent an email to opposing counsel with alternative dates of August 21, 25, and 28, 2026. (Opposition at 2:21-24; Exh. 2 [correspondence].) Notably, these dates were only provided after the initial motion was filed.

 

In reply, BMW argues that sanctions are still warranted since Plaintiff only provided alternative dates after this motion was filed. (Reply at 5:24-6:9.) The Court agrees.

 

BMW requests $1,600.00 in sanctions, representing four hours of work at an hourly rate of $400.00. (Moore Decl. at ¶ 8.) The Court finds the hourly rate and this amount reasonable.

 

IV.  Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, BMW’s motion is GRANTED. Plaintiff is to appear for deposition within 20 days of this order. Additionally, Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel, jointly and severally, are to pay defense counsel sanctions in the amount of $1,600.00 within 30 days of this order.
