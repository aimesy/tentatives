Case Number: 25NWLC44172    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:  

           

Plaintiff Belinda Maturo dba Perfect Pointe School of Dance’s Motion to Compel Further Responses and for Production of Documents in Response to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; for Monetary Sanctions is granted.

 

Defendant Raquel K. Hernandez is ordered to serve supplemental responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 1-5, without objections, within 20 days’ notice of this order. Defendant Raquel K. Hernandez is also ordered to pay sanctions of $2,060.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ notice of this order.

 

Analysis:

 

On August 7, 2025, Plaintiff Belinda Maturo dba Perfect Pointe School of Dance (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Raquel K. Hernandez (“Defendant”). The Complaint alleges causes of action for breach of contract and common counts. Defendant filed an Answer in propria persona on October 3, 2025.

 

On January 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses and for Production of Documents in Response to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; for Monetary Sanctions. Plaintiff served Defendant with the Motion on the same day by first-class mail. Following re-assignment of this action to the Limited Civil Non-Collections Hub, the hearing on the instant Motion was set for September 24, 2026 in Department 26 of the Spring Street Courthouse. (Minute Order, 07/08/26.) Plaintiff gave notice of the ruling on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Procedural Requirements

 

Notice of the motion to compel further must be given “within 45 days of service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or any specific later date to which the requesting party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (c).) This time is extended based on the manner of service; service by mail extends the deadline by five days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (a)(3)(B).) Defendant served a response to the Request for Production of Documents, Set One, on December 4, 2025. (Motion, Spielfogel Decl., ¶4 and Exh. 2.) 45 days after December 4, 2025 was January 18, 2026. An additional five calendar days made the deadline to file the instant Motion January 23, 2026. The instant Motion was timely served on Defendant on January 22, 2026. (Motion, p. 47.)

 

Second, California Rules of Court Rule 3.1345 requires all motions or responses involving further discovery to include a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345, subd. (a).) Alternatively, “the court may allow the moving party to submit a concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(3).) The Motion is accompanied by a separately filed separate statement.

 

Finally, the motion must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1).) Plaintiff’s counsel sent a meet and confer letter to Defendant on December 16, 2025, to which Defendant responded that she would “send what I have by the end of the week.” (Motion, Spielfogel Decl., ¶¶5-6 and Exhs. 3-4.) On January 19, 2026, Defendant emailed Plaintiff to say that she did not know how to respond. (Id. at ¶7 and Exh. 5.) Plaintiff’s counsel communicated to Defendant that unless supplemental responses were served, they would have to file a motion to compel further responses. (Id. at ¶8 and Exh. 6.) Defendant did not respond to that email but did respond to Special Interrogatories, Set One, by identifying documents and communications she had not produced. (Id. at ¶9 and Exhs. 7-8.) This exchange demonstrates that Plaintiff’s counsel sought to communicate the deficiencies in Defendant’s responses, but was unable to reach an agreement with Defendant regarding production of further responses. The meet and confer requirement is satisfied.

 

Request for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 1-5

 

A motion requesting an order compelling further responses to a demand for production of documents must set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).) If good cause is shown, the burden shifts to the responding party to justify any objections made to document disclosure. (Kirkland v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 98.) “[T]he party who seeks to compel production has met his burden of showing good cause simply by a fact-specific showing of relevance.” (Ibid. [citing Weil & Brown, Cal. Practice Guide: Civil Procedure Before Trial (The Rutter Group 2001) Discovery, ¶¶ 8:1495 to 8:1495.10, pp. 8H 24 to 8H 26]. Upon a fact-specific showing of relevance, the burden shifts to the responding party to justify its objections. (Ibid.)

 

The Motion argues that good cause exists with respect to the requests for production of documents relating to the obligation at issue and Defendant’s payments thereunder. It also argues that the requests seek communications between the parties that might lead to the discovery of admissible evidence. A response to a request for production of documents must either be:

 

(1) A statement that the party will comply with the particular demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling by the date set for the inspection, copying, testing, or sampling pursuant to paragraph (2) of subdivision (c) of Section 2031.030 and any related activities.

(2) A representation that the party lacks the ability to comply with the demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling of a particular item or category of item.

(3) An objection to the particular demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).) Plaintiff moves for further responses on the grounds that Defendant’s statements do not comply with the first two types of responses, and that any objection asserted is without merit. (Citing Code of Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

 

Defendant asserted general objections to all the requests for production, which the Court agrees are improper because they do not refer to the specific requests to which they apply and generically refer to privacy, ambiguity, relevance, and misleading facts. (Motion, Spielfogel Decl., Exh. 2, p. 1.) Why any particular request is subject to these objections is not identified in Defendant’s response. (Ibid.)  

 

Request No.1 asks Defendant to “produce all DOCUMENTS identified in YOUR response to Special Interrogatories, Set One, served concurrently herewith.” Request No. 2 similarly asks Defendant to produce all such communications. In response, Defendant denied that any documents or communications were identified. However, Defendant did identify documents in response to Special Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 3 and 6. (Motion, Spielfogel Decl., ¶9 and Exhs. 7-8.) Plaintiff, therefore, is entitled to production of the identified documents, as her representation that she lacks the ability to comply is clearly contradicted.

 

Requests Nos. 3 and 4 ask Defendant to “produce all DOCUMENTS that in any way reflect payments YOU made to PLAINTIFF from July 24, 2023, to the date of this request for production” and all related documents. Defendant responded by citing the general objections and that Plaintiff has all the documents. Finally, Request No. 5 asks Defendant to produce all documents that relate to the contract at issue. Defendant again responded by objecting generally and stating that Plaintiff has the documents. As explained above, this is not a proper response under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.210 because it does not state that Defendant will comply with the demands, cannot comply with the demands, nor assert a proper objection. Plaintiff is entitled to a further response to these requests.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff is entitled to further responses to Request Nos. 1-5, without objections. Plaintiff is also entitled to an award of sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.310, subdivision (h). The Court finds the amount sought based on time invested is excessive given the lack of any opposition and counsel’s ability to appear for the hearing remotely. Sanctions are awarded in the amount of $1,560.00 based on four hours of attorney time billed at $500.00 per hour, plus $60.00 in costs. (Motion, Spielfogel Decl., ¶11.)


Conclusion


Plaintiff Belinda Maturo dba Perfect Pointe School of Dance’s Motion to Compel Further Responses and for Production of Documents in Response to Request for Production of Documents, Set One; for Monetary Sanctions is granted.

 

Defendant Raquel K. Hernandez is ordered to serve supplemental responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One, Nos. 1-5, without objections, within 20 days’ notice of this order. Defendant Raquel K. Hernandez is also ordered to pay sanctions of $2,060.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ notice of this order.

Case Number: 25STLC01954    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:  

           

Plaintiff Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club’s Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions, is granted in part, and denied in part.

 

Defendant Gabriela Julia Gomez is to serve verified responses to the interrogatories without objections, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s request for an award of sanctions is denied.

 

Analysis:

 

On June 25, 2026, Plaintiff Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club (“Plaintiff”) served Form Interrogatories, Set One, on Defendant Gabriela Julia Gomez (“Defendant”). (Motion, Tapper Decl., ¶3 and Exh. A.) As of Plaintiff’s filing of the instant Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions on August 21, 2026, Defendant had not served responses. (Id. at ¶¶4-6 and Exh. C-D.) Plaintiff now moves to compel Defendant’s responses to the interrogatories pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290. There is no requirement for a prior meet-and-confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Based on Defendant’s failure to respond, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling code-compliant responses without objections.

 

Plaintiff also moves for an award of sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, subdivision (c), which provides for sanctions if a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses without reasonable justification. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c) [“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”].)

 

Plaintiff mischaracterizes the statute as mandating sanctions when a party fails to respond to interrogatories, unless the Court finds the failure was substantially justified or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust, without reference to whether an unsuccessful motion or opposition were filed. (See Motion, p. 4:6-9.) To be clear, Section 2030.290 does not authorize sanctions because Defendant did not file an opposition to the instant Motion.  The citation should be corrected in any future filings.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Interinsurance Exchange of the Automobile Club’s Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One; Request for Monetary Sanctions, is granted in part, and denied in part.

 

Defendant Gabriela Julia Gomez is to serve verified responses to the interrogatories without objections, within 20 days’ service of this order. Plaintiff’s request for an award of sanctions is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC03491    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Strategic Funding Source, Inc. dba Kapitus’ Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication Against Defendant Abrahan Bravo is granted. Plaintiff is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.

Order to Show Cause Re: Request for Default Judgment Against Defendant BClean Services Professional Cleaning, Inc is scheduled for 10/15/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On April 30, 2025, Plaintiff Strategic Funding Source, Inc. dba Kapitus (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for breach of contract against Defendants BClean Services Professional Cleaning, Inc. (“Defendant BClean”) and Abrahan Bravo (“Defendant Bravo”). On May 30, 2025, Defendants filed an Answer in propria persona. The Court ordered the Answer stricken as to Defendant BClean on October 12, 2025, due to its lack of proper legal representation. (Minute Order, 10/21/25.) The Court entered Defendant BClean’s default on January 15, 2026. On March 18, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted against Defendant Bravo. (Minute Order, 03/18/26.)

 

On June 25, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication Against Defendant Abrahan Bravo. The Motion was served on Defendant Bravo by first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for summary judgment, or adjudication, on the cause of action for breach of contract (guaranty) pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 437c. On a motion for summary judgment or adjudication of a particular cause of action, a moving plaintiff must show that there is no defense by proving each element of the cause of action entitling the party to judgment on that cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).) Then the burden shifts to the defendant to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action or a defense. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).) Additionally, in ruling on the Motion, the Court must view the “evidence [citations] and such inferences [citations], in the light most favorable to the opposing party.” (Intrieri v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 72, 81 [citing Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843].)

 

Allegations in the Complaint

 

The Complaint alleges a cause of action for breach of contract against Defendant BClean, and a cause of action for breach of contract (guaranty) against Defendant Bravo, based on the following facts. On or about May 29, 2024, Plaintiff and Defendant BClean entered into a written Loan Agreement. (Compl., ¶6 and Exh. 1.) Defendant Bravo executed the Agreement on behalf of Defendant BClean, and in their individual capacity as personal guarantor of Defendant BClean’s obligations under the Agreement. (Ibid.) In consideration of funds provided by Plaintiff, Defendant BClean promised to repay Plaintiff $40,200 (“Repayment Amount”), plus fees and costs. (Ibid.) The Repayment Amount was to be paid by Defendant BClean through weekly payments of $774 (“Payment”). (Ibid.) Defendant BClean defaulted under the Agreement. (Id.) As part of the Agreement, Defendant Bravo executed a written guaranty, unconditionally guaranteeing Defendant BClean’s payment and performance. (Id. at ¶17.) Defendant Bravo failed, and continues to fail, to pay the sums due and owing to Plaintiff, in breach of the Guaranty. (Id. at ¶19.) As a result of Defendant Bravo’s breach of the guaranty, Plaintiff has been damaged in the amount of $24,556, plus other fees, costs and charges, as well as attorneys’ fees and costs of suit, pursuant to the terms of the Agreement. (Id. at ¶21.)

 

 

 

Cause of Action for Breach of Contract (Guaranty)

 

The elements of a cause of action for breach of contract are (1) the existence of contract; (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance; (3) defendant’s breach (or anticipatory breach); and (4) resulting damage. (Wall Street Network, Ltd. v. N. Y. Times Co. (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1171, 1178.)

 

In support of the motion, Plaintiff presents the following facts as undisputed. The parties entered into a Loan Agreement on or about May 29, 2024, pursuant to which Defendant BClean received a business loan of $30,000.00 that it promised to repay through weekly payments. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact Nos. 1-2, Carlson Decl., Exh. 1; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A, Nos. 1-12.) Under the Agreement, Defendant Bravo executed a personal guaranty (“Guaranty”) unconditionally guaranteeing Defendant BClean’s payment and performance. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 6; Carlson Decl., Exh. 1, p. 2; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A, p. 7.) The guarantor’s obligations were due at the time of any breach of the Agreement by the borrower. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 7; Carlson Decl., Exh. 1, §3.2; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A at Exh. 1, §3.2.) Plaintiff performed its obligations under the Agreement by transferring the loan funds to Defendant BClean. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 8; Carlson Decl., Exh. 2; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A, Exh. 2, Nos. 11, 14-15.) Since about December 26, 2024, and thereafter, Plaintiff has been unable to debit the required payments from Defendant BClean’s account. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 9; Carlson Decl., Exh. 2; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A, Exh. 2, Nos. 16-23.) Defendant Bravo has not paid all amounts owed under the Agreement and is in breach of the Guaranty. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 10; Carlson Decl., Exh. 2; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A, Exh. 2, Nos. 25-35.) Plaintiff incurred principal damages of $24,556.00 as a result of the breach, and is entitled to an award of attorney’s fees and costs. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 12; Carlson Decl., Exh. 2; Gibbs Decl., Exh. A, Exh. 1, § 3.3.)

 

This evidence carries Plaintiff’s initial burden of proof with respect to the cause of action for breach of the guarantee. The burden now shifts to Defendant Bravo to demonstrate the existence of a triable issue of material fact. However, no opposition has been filed to date.

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Plaintiff Strategic Funding Source, Inc. dba Kapitus’ Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication Against Defendant Abrahan Bravo is granted. Plaintiff is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days.


Order to Show Cause Re: Request for Default Judgment Against Defendant BClean Services Professional Cleaning, Inc is scheduled for 10/15/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 26STLC01250    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff French Quarter, LLC’s Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Discovery, to Deem Requests For Admission Admitted, and for Monetary Sanctions is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff French Quarters, LLC (“Plaintiff”) propounded Special Interrogatories, Set One, Form Interrogatories, Set One, Request for Production of Documents, Set One, and Requests for Admission, Set One, on Defendant Ghazal Safdari (“Plaintiff”) on May 11, 2026. (Motion, Snyder Decl., Exhs. 1-4.) Despite a meet-and-confer effort, Defendant has not served responses as of the filing of the instant Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Discovery, to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted, and for Monetary Sanctions. (Id. at ¶¶5-8.) Plaintiff filed the instant Motion and concurrently served it on Defendant by first-class mail on August 19, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Initially, the Court notes that Plaintiff filed combined motions to compel responses with respect to four separate sets of discovery: (1) Form Interrogatories, Set One; (2) Special Interrogatories, Set One; (3) Request for Production of Documents, Set One; and (4) Requests for Admission, Set One. Filing a single motion to compel responses and/or deem admissions regarding four separate sets of discovery negatively impacts the Court’s calendar by placing more motions on the calendar than slots have been provided by the online reservation system. Further, it results in confusion in the motion papers regarding the arguments and evidence with respect to each request. Finally, combining multiple discovery sets into a single motion allows the moving party to avoid paying the requisite filing fees. Statutorily required filing fees are jurisdictional and “it is mandatory for the court clerks to demand and receive statutorily required filing fees.” (See Duran v. St. Luke’s Hospital (2003) 114 Cal.App.4th 457, 460.) Plaintiff must make separate motion reservations to address each set of discovery and pay each filing fee.

 

Additionally, the discovery requests served by Plaintiff do not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 94, which allows only 35 written discovery requests in the Limited Civil Court unless otherwise authorized by court order or by stipulation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 94, subd. (a); § 95.) Plaintiff served Defendant with more than 35 interrogatories alone. (Motion, Snyder Decl., Exhs. 1-2.) Based on the foregoing, the instant discovery Motion is denied. The Court will not grant a motion to compel discovery propounded in violation of the Code of Civil Procedure and filed in violation of the statutory requirement to pay filing fees.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff French Quarter, LLC’s Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Discovery, to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted, and for Monetary Sanctions is denied.
