Case Number: 26STCV00017    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 11 - Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. v. Coastal Charm LLC

          Defendant Coastal Charm LLC’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment

Defendant Coastal Charm LLC moves to set aside the default judgment entered against it on May 18, 2026. Defendant moves for relief under Code of Civil Procedure sections 473.5 and 473(d).

CCP § 473.5

Defendant is entitled to relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5. “When service of a summons has not resulted in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action and a default or default judgment has been entered against the party in the action,” the defendant may move to set aside the default or default judgment. (CCP § 473.5(a).) “Upon a finding by the court that the motion was [timely] and that [defendant’s] lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party’s avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect, it may set aside the default or default judgment.” (CCP § 473.5(c).) “The moving party has the burden of showing good cause for relief from a default or a default judgment.” (Tunis v. Barrow (1986) 184 Cal.App.3d 1069, 1080.) “ ‘[W]ith respect to setting aside a default judgment, it is the policy of the law to favor, whenever possible, a hearing on the merits.’ ” (Luxury Asset Lending, LLC v. Philadelphia Television Network, Inc. (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 894, 907 [CCP § 473.5].)

Here, Defendant shows it lacked actual notice of this lawsuit. It is undisputed that Plaintiff personally served the summons on VCorp Agent Service, Inc. (“VCorp”), which was the registered agent for service of process listed for Defendant on the California Secretary of State website at the time of service. (Aposhian Decl., ¶ 7.) However, Defendant’s manager Haim Zrihen attests, “nobody, including myself, at Coastal Charm received the Summons and Complaint from Vcorp before default was entered.” (Zrihen Decl., ¶ 3.) Therefore, Defendant did not have actual notice of the lawsuit.

Plaintiff attempts to rely on Pulte Homes Corp. v. Williams Mechanical, Inc. (2016) 2 Cal.App.5th 267 (Pulte) for the proposition that notice to an entity’s registered agent constitutes actual notice to that entity under CCP § 473.5. The Court finds that Pulte is inapposite here. The Pulte court’s analysis applied to service upon a designated agent for service of process of a dissolved corporation, where there was no other officer to whom notice could have been provided. (Pulte, supra, 2 Cal.App.5th at p. 274.) There is no defunct corporate defendant involved in this action. Therefore, service on VCorp does not impute actual notice to Defendant.

Plaintiff requests the Court allow it to present 15 minutes of testimony from VCorp’s person most qualified at the September 24, 2026, hearing under California Rules of Court rule 3.1306(b). Plaintiff argues that this testimony will show that (1) Defendant did receive actual notice, and (2) any lack of notice was a result of its inexcusable neglect. Plaintiff further declares that VCorp told it “Coastal Charm had not communicated with VCorp concerning its designation, that no service agreement existed, that VCorp used the principal address shown in the California Secretary of State's records, that those records contained no telephone number or email address, and that VCorp sent the Summons and Complaint and Request for Entry of Default by regular mail.” (Aposhian Decl., ¶ 19.)

The Court declines to allow this testimony at the hearing. Relevant testimony could have been submitted with the opposition in a declaration by the pertinent person at VCorp. Importantly, the fact that VCorp sent the summons and complaint via regular mail does not prove that Defendant received actual notice of the lawsuit. In addition, the fact that Defendant may have had no prior service agreement with VCorp is not dispositive on the issue of inexcusable neglect. As confirmed in Defendant’s own emails with VCorp, “[p]eople can register entities and appoint [Vcorp] as agent without informing [Vcorp] prior to or after registration was completed.” (Aposhian Decl., Ex. 8, at p. 6.) Plaintiff’s further arguments as to whether Defendant engaged in inexcusable neglect, on this record, are speculative. “Unless inexcusable neglect is clear, the policy favoring trial on the merits prevails. Doubts are resolved in favor of the application for relief from default.” (Elston v. City of Turlock (1985) 38 Cal.3d 227, 235, overruled by statute on other grounds as stated in Wilcox v. Birtwhistle (1999) 21 Cal.4th 973.)

Because the Court finds that Defendant is entitled to relief under CCP § 473.5, it need not and does not address whether Defendant is also entitled to relief under CCP § 473(d).

Disposition

Defendant Coastal Charm LLC’s motion to set aside default judgment is granted. The Court hereby vacates the judgment entered on May 18, 2026. Defendant shall file its proposed answer, attached as Exhibit A to the Declaration of Haim Zrihen, forthwith.


Case Number: 26STCV01255    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 - Ken Shao Xu, et al. v. BMW of North America, LLC

          Plaintiffs’ Motion to Compel Defendant’s Person Most Qualified Deposition

Plaintiffs Ken Shao Xu and Kotec Corp. move to compel the deposition of Defendant BMW of North America, LLC’s person most qualified. Plaintiffs also seek an order compelling the production of documents in response to their 35 accompanying document requests.

“An oral deposition shall be scheduled for a date at least 10 days after service of the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.270(a).) “The service of a deposition notice under Section 2025.240 is effective to require any deponent who is a party to the action… to attend and to testify, as well as to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing for inspection and copying.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.280(a).) One may move to compel the deposition of a party who fails to appear at a deposition or proceed with it “without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450(a).)

Plaintiffs do not meet their burden of showing they made “a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion” (CCP § 2016.040) as required when moving to compel a deposition (CCP § 2025.450(b)(2)).  Plaintiffs’ counsel’s supporting declaration shows counsel only communicated via email regarding available dates for the deposition. (Enav Decl., ¶¶ 15-18, Ex. 5.) Plaintiffs’ counsel states, “On July 1, 2026, after receiving Defendant’s objections, Plaintiffs’ counsel emailed Defendant’s counsel requesting available dates for the deposition of Defendant’s PMK. . . .  On July 2, 2026, Defendant’s counsel offered available dates for the PMK deposition, the earliest of which was February 1, 2027, and the latest of which was April 2, 2027. That same day, Plaintiffs’ counsel responded that requiring Plaintiffs to wait approximately eight (8) months for the deposition was unreasonable and requested an earlier date.” (Id., ¶¶ 15-16.)

Plaintiffs have shown no attempt to meet and confer about Defendant’s objections to the 19 specific matters of examination and 35 categories of documents requested in the notice of deposition. Plaintiffs’ separate statement, meanwhile, spends 41 pages on those disputed issues.

Disposition

Plaintiffs Ken Shao Xu and Kotec Corp.’s motion to compel the deposition of Defendant’s person most qualified is continued to October 26, 2026 at 8:30 AM. The parties are ordered to submit a joint statement on or before October 19, 2026, regarding which specific discovery issues, if any, remain at issue for the Court to decide.
