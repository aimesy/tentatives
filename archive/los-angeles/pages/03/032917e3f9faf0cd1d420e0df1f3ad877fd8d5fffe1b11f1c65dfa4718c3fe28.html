 Case No.: 22STCV15781

Plaintiffs’ counsel Justin Lo, Jarrod Y. Nakano, and Work Lawyers PC’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED, effective upon filing proof of service of the court’s order (MC-053).

 

Legal Standard

 

Motions to withdraw are routinely granted if made in the proper form and presented with proper notice. The court may issue an order allowing an attorney to withdraw from representation, after notice to the client.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) 

 

Discussion

 

Justin Lo, Jarrod Y. Nakano, and Work Lawyers PC bring the instant motion for relief as counsel as to Plaintiff Nicolas Vecchio. 

 

            The following documents are properly submitted: Civil Form MC-051 “Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” Civil Form MC-052 “Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” and Civil Form MC-053 “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel.”

 

            The motion is GRANTED.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 24STCV16249    Hearing Date: August 10, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

NAYRIKA D. MASJEDI

 

         vs.

 

ALFA CLOTHING & FINISHING, INC., et al.

 

 Case No.: 24STCV16249

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 10, 2026

 

Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED, WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

On 6/28/2024, Plaintiff in pro per Nayrika D. Masjedi filed suit against Alfa Clothing & Finishing, Inc., Andria’s Packing & Finishing Inc., Rafi Clothing & Finishing, and Rafik Torosyan. On 9/5/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC). On 3/3/2026, Plaintiff filed a second amended complaint (SAC), alleging: (1) breach of contract; (2) negligence; (3) breach of bailment/duty of care.

 

On 4/6/2026, Defendant Alfa Clothing & Finishing, Inc. and Rafik Torosyan (collectively, Defendants) demurred to Plaintiff’s SAC.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s SAC fails to remedy the deficiencies previously identified in the original demurrer, namely the issue of standing. Defendant further argues that the claims are insufficiently pled and uncertain.

 

In the original Complaint, Plaintiff brought this suit on behalf of “plaintiff Nayrika D. Masjedi dba the Clean Out Group” (See Complaint ¶ 3.) However, Defendant submitted evidence that no such legal entity exists. Rather, there is a Clean Out Group Inc., which has been in suspended status since 4/2/2024. As such, at the time this action was filed, Clean Out Group Inc had no standing to participate in litigation. (See Cal. Corp. Code §§ 23301, 23301.5 [a suspended corporation cannot prosecute an action or defend itself in court].) Moreover, Plaintiff, as a non-attorney, cannot represent a corporate entity.

 

            Then, in the FAC, Plaintiff removed allegations that the claims are brought on behalf of the Clean Out Group, and asserts them in her personal capacity. The Court sustained the demurrer finding that to avoid running afoul of the sham pleadings doctrine, Plaintiff needed to allege facts which could explain this change.

 

            The Court also noted that Plaintiff was limited to the original causes of action asserted in her original Complaint, i.e., (1) breach of contract; (2) intentional interference with prospective business advantage; (3) breach of fiduciary duty; (4) conversion; (5) trespass to chattel; (5) breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing; and (6) negligence.

           

Despite this, Plaintiff’s SAC still asserts claims for breach of bailment. The Court disregards this cause of action, and looks only to the breach of contract and negligence causes of action.

 

            After review, the Court finds that Plaintiff’s SAC runs afoul of the sham pleadings doctrine. As Defendant explains:

 

In the SAC, the defects found in the original complaint and FAC still exist and have not been cured. Additionally, Ms. Masjedi fails to provide a proper explanation as to why she has removed The Clean Out Group, Inc. as a Plaintiff from the SAC and why she has removed all references to the company. The only "explanation" she now provides in response to the Court's order for an explanation with "facts" is that she "inadvertently included a reference to "The Clean Out Group." (emphasis added) (See SAC, page 3, lines 25-26).

 

Ms. Masjedi's "explanation" falls below the standard required to overcome the burden of a sham pleading. First, the original complaint did not just have "a" reference to The Clean Out Group, Inc. but several references including calling the entity Ms. Masjedi's "company." Second, her explanation regarding "inadvertence" is a sham in and of itself. How does one include an entity-separate and apart from oneself – and allege facts pertaining to this entity – in an "inadvertent" manner? This is not a proper and viable explanation as to why Ms. Masjedi removed all reference to the "The Clean Out Group" in the SAC when evidence previously provided in the demurrers to the original and First Amended Complaint illustrate that The Clean Out Group is the real party in interest, having invoices and checks issued in its name. 

           

            (SAC Demurrer , 7: 13-28.)

 

            In sum, the Court finds Plaintiff’s own original allegations, as well as judicially noticed invoices and checks indicate that the Clean Out Group, of which Plaintiff was the principal and agent, is the real party in interest. Accordingly, Plaintiff lacks standing to pursue this action in her individual capacity.

 

            Given that this was Plaintiff’s third opportunity to allege facts which could show standing, the Court finds additional leave to amend would be futile.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer is sustained, without leave to amend. 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV10018    Hearing Date: August 10, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

NATASHA KORMAN

 

         vs.

 

FARMERS GROUP PROPERTY AND CASUALTY INSURANCE COMPANY, et al. 

 

 Case No.: 25STCV10018

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 10, 2026

 

Defendants’ motion to seal is GRANTED.

 

On 4/2/2025, Plaintiff filed suit against Farmers Group Property, Casualty Insurance Company, and Adriana Vargas, alleging: (1) discrimination; (2) failure to accommodate; (3) failure to engage in interactive process; (4) failure to prevent discrimination; (5) retaliation; (6) harassment; and (7) wrongful termination.

 

On 6/17/2026, Defendants armers Group Property and Casualty Insurance Company and Farmers Insurance Exchange (collectively, Defendants) moved to seal records being filed in support of its summary judgment motion.

 

Plaintiff filed a non-opposition.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants seek to seal 27 exhibits (See Motion, 2:8-3:5), on the basis that the information in the identified exhibits contain Plaintiff’s private medical information.

 

California Rule of Court 2.551(b)(1) provides: A party requesting that a record be filed under seal must file a motion or an application for an order sealing the record. The motion or application must be accompanied by a memorandum of points and authorities and a declaration containing facts sufficient to justify the sealing.

 

California Rule of Court 2.550 requires that a court granting a motion to file a document under seal expressly find the following: 1) There exists an overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access to the record; (2) The overriding interest supports sealing the record; (3) A substantial probability exists that the overriding interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed; (4) The proposed sealing is narrowly tailored; and (5) No less restrictive means exist to achieve the overriding interest.

 

Here, Defendants seek to maintain the confidentiality of the foregoing documents, which contain confidential and sensitive information related to Plaintiff’s medical history. After review, the Court finds that he information at issue satisfies all of the elements of Rule 2.550(d).

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion to seal is granted in its entirety.

 

It is so ordered.
