MOTION

Motion for Summary Judgment

MOVING PARTIES

Defendant Dallas Martin

OPPOSING PARTY

Plaintiff Brooklyn Bridges

 

 

BACKGROUND

Plaintiff Brooklyn Bridges (“Plaintiff”) brought the instant action against Defendant Dallas Maritn (“Defendant”), alleging he sexually assaulted her.  Defendant moves for summary judgment on Plaintiff’s complaint.  Plaintiff opposes the motion.

LEGAL STANDARD – MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

“[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears the burden of persuasion that there is no triable issue of material fact and that he is entitled to judgment as a matter of law[.] There is a triable issue of material fact if, and only if, the evidence would allow a reasonable trier of fact to find the underlying fact in favor of the party opposing the motion in accordance with the applicable standard of proof.”  (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850.)  “[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears an initial burden of production to make a prima facie showing of the nonexistence of any triable issue of material fact; if he carries his burden of production, he causes a shift, and the opposing party is then subjected to a burden of production of his own to make a prima facie showing of the existence of a triable issue of material fact.”  (Ibid.)  

DISCUSSION

In support of his motion, Defendant has presented Plaintiff’s deposition testimony.  At deposition, Plaintiff testified Defendant sexually assaulted her on three occasions.  (Compendium of Evidence in Support of Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment, Exhibit 5, pp. 20-21, 24-26, 55-56.)  Defendant also provides a police report Plaintiff made to the Burbank Police Department, which reflects Plaintiff’s report of Defendant having raped her.  (Id., Exhibit 6.)  Ordinarily, the contents of a police report would be considered hearsay and possibly not admissible evidence.  (See Sambrano v. City of San Diego (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 225, 237 [police reports may properly be subject to objections, though in some cases admissibility can be established or an alternative official records theory of admissibility might apply].)  Neither party has objected on any grounds concerning the Court’s consideration of the subject police report.                    The evidence Defendant himself has presented, without more, raises triable issues of material fact as to whether Plaintiff can prevail on her claims against Defendant.  Defendant’s characterization of Plaintiff’s testimony as conclusory and evasive is immaterial. 

Simply put, Defendant cannot obtain summary judgment on the basis Defendant believes Plaintiff lacks credibility. “The motion must stand self-sufficient and cannot succeed because the opposition is weak. ¶ A court generally cannot resolve questions about a declarant's credibility in a summary judgment proceeding unless admissions against interest have been made which justify disregard of any dissimulation.”  (AARTS Productions, Inc. v. Crocker National Bank (1986) 179 Cal.App.3d 1061, 1064-1065.)  Moreover, “[t]he trial court may not weigh the evidence in the manner of a factfinder to determine whose version is more likely true. Nor may the trial court grant summary judgment based on the court's evaluation of credibility. Nor may the trial court grant summary judgment for a defendant based simply on its opinion that plaintiff's claims are ‘implausible,’ if a reasonable factfinder could find for plaintiff on the evidence presented.” Binder v. Aetna Life Ins. Co. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 832, 84, internal citations omitted.)

 Thus, Defendant cannot obtain summary judgment because Defendant contends Plaintiff’s evidence is weaker than evidence Defendant sets forth.  Even though that may be the case, Plaintiff is not precluded from having the trier of fact weigh evidence such as the “sworn testimony, medical records, police records, contemporaneous communications, discovery responses identifying witnesses and treating providers, electronic evidence, and other documentary evidence” noted by Plaintiff, as this Court cannot say Plaintiff’s case is not meritorious, and Defendant is entitled to judgment, as a matter of law.

As Plaintiff’s deposition testimony alone reflects there are triable issues of material fact which defeat Defendant’s motion, the Court need not consider the documents Defendant lodged provisionally under seal.  As such, the Court declines to rule on the motion to seal and returns the lodged documents to Defendant.  (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551.)

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

As Defendant’s own evidence reflects triable issues of material fact exist with respect to Plaintiff’s claims, Defendant’s motion is denied.

Defendant is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file a proof of service of same.


Case Number: 24STCV16441A    Hearing Date: August 17, 2026    Dept: 415

MOTION

Motion to Enforce Judgment

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Joel Alvarado Rojas

OPPOSING PARTY

Defendant General Motors

 

MOTION

            Plaintiff Elmer Joel Alvarado Rojas (“Plaintiff”) moves to enforce a settlement agreement with Defendant General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”).  Defendant opposes the motion.

ANALYSIS

As authority for this motion, Plaintiff cites Code of Civil Procedure sections 664.6 and 871.27.  Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 (section 664.6) provides, “[i]f parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”  In the instant case, the Court entered judgment pursuant to the terms of the parties’ settlement on March 25, 2026, before Plaintiff filed this motion, and that judgment expressly provided the Court would retain jurisdiction pursuant to section 664.6.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.27, in a civil action for restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle, the defendant must promptly process any agreed-upon motor vehicle restitution or replacement and complete the restitution or replacement within 30 days from the date of receipt of a signed release from the plaintiff’s counsel.  If the defendant does not do so, the defendant incurs a mandatory penalty of $50 per day until the settlement is completed, unless the parties stipulate otherwise.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.27, subd. (g)(2).)  

In this instance, however, the parties stipulated to a different timeline. The judgment to which the parties stipulated states, in Paragraphs 5 and 6, “[n]o less than 45 days, but no more than 60 days, after Plaintiff provide [sic] the Loan Payoff Information to GM’s counsel, Plaintiff will transfer possession of the Subject Vehicle to GM . . . ,” and “[i]n the event Plaintiff accepts this Offer, no later than 10 business days after the transfer of possession of the Subject Vehicle and execution of transfer documents referenced in Paragraph 5, GM will send via overnight mail the loan payoff check to the lender and a check to Plaintiff’s counsel of record for the amounts set forth in Paragraphs 1 and 2, above, minus any loan payoff as set forth above.”  (Declaration of Aaron Yadegari in Support of Plaintiff’s Motion, Exhibit 1, Judgment, filed March 25, 2026.)  The Court notes Paragraphs 1 and 2 in the Judgment reference the amount Plaintiff is to be paid, including “any loan payoff,” and Plaintiff’s reasonably incurred attorney fees, expenses, and costs, the latter of which are the subject of two separately filed motions. 

The record before the Court, which the parties do not appear to dispute, reflects Plaintiff provided the loan payoff information to Defendant on February 16, 2026.  Thus, pursuant to the parties’ agreement, Plaintiff had until April 17, 2026 to surrender the vehicle to Defendant, and Defendant then had until May 1, 2026 to provide payment to Plaintiff.  On April 17, 2026 via priority mail, Defendant submitted a settlement check dated April 13, 2026 in the amount of $54,129.40 to Plaintiff through Plaintiff’s counsel of record, which was sent to Chevrolet of Montebello.  (Declaration of Natalie Keshishianin Support of Opposition to Plaintiff’s Motion, Exhibit A.)  A cover letter for the remittance from Defendant’s counsel stated, “the settlement check” was in connection with this case, and “[o]nce all necessary surrender documents have been signed, the check can be provided to the customer.”  (Ibid.)

Plaintiff asserts in his reply brief he is entitled to the relief he seeks because Defendant did not pay the funds and take possession of the vehicle until April 24, 2026.  (Reply, pp. 1-2.) But, as detailed above, the record placed before the Court does not so indicate.  Whatever procedures counsel utilized in handling settlement matters in other instances is of no moment, as it appears, in the instant action, Defendant complied with the specified terms to which the parties had agreed. 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Accordingly, the motion is denied.

Plaintiff is to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.

MOTION

Motion to Tax Costs

MOVING PARTY

Defendant General Motors LLC

OPPOSING PARTY

Plaintiff Elmer Joel Alvarado Rojas

 

MOTION

Plaintiff Elmer Joel Alvarado Rojas (“Plaintiff”) settled his claims against Defendant General Motors LLC (“Defendant”).  Defendant moves to tax the costs on the memorandum of costs Plaintiff served.  Plaintiff opposes the motion.

ANALYSIS

As Plaintiff points out, the Court must consider his entitlement to costs under Civil Code section 1794, the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act’s cost-shifting provision.  That statute provides a prevailing plaintiff may recover costs and expenses, including attorney fees.  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).)  It further states, as the prevailing party, Plaintiff is entitled to “the aggregate amount of costs and expenses . . . determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred . . . in connection with the commencement and prosecution of” this case.  (Ibid.)  As such, Plaintiff is entitled to recover costs and expenses which are not otherwise recoverable costs, if the Court concludes the amounts “were reasonably incurred.”  (Jensen v. BMW of North America, Inc. (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 112, 138, overruled on other grounds in Rodriguez v. FCA US LLC (2024) 17 Cal.5th 189.)

Defendant moves to strike “filing fees related to [Plaintiff’s] unfiled attorneys fee motion.”  (Motion to Strike or Tax Memorandum of Costs, p. 4.)  Plaintiff has now filed a motion for attorney fees.  As such, this argument is moot.  Defendant also argues Plaintiff’s notice of acceptance of Defendant’s offer to compromise and notice of entry of judgment were “incurred unnecessarily” and are, therefore, not compensable.  (Perko's Enterprises, Inc. v. RRNS Enterprises (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 238, 245.)  The Court cannot conclude these filings were unnecessary, especially given such filings are commonplace and reasonable.  Thus, Plaintiff may recover these fees.

Defendant moves to strike $1,287.75 in deposition costs for the deposition of Luis Sandoval (“Sandoval”).  Defendant designated Sandoval as its person most qualified to testify on specified matters.  This deposition was plainly reasonable for Plaintiff’s prosecution of this case.  Therefore, Plaintiff may recover these costs.

Defendant also moves to strike $455.67 in costs for printing, postage, and service.  The Court concludes Plaintiff’s costs for photocopying and postage are overhead of Plaintiff’s counsel’s business and not recoverable as costs.  The Court, therefore, grants this part of the motion by striking $49.25 in costs representing photocopying from Plaintiff’s memorandum of costs. 

The Court accepts Plaintiff’s assertion he has incurred additional expenses of $86.57 related to the filing of an attorney fees motion and will incur a total of $49.24 in fees for filing a reply brief in support of that motion and a notice regarding dismissal of this action.  Therefore, the Court concludes the  reasonable expenses and costs Plaintiff will incur amounts to $3,572.46 ($3,485.90 plus $135.81 (in additional costs) minus $49.25 (deduction for photocopying costs)). 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            Defendant is to pay Plaintiff $3,572.46 in costs incurred, through Plaintiff’s Counsel, within 30 days of notice of the Court’s order.

Defendant is to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.

MOTION 

Motion to Strike

MOVING PARTY

Defendant Grace Church of the Nazarene

OPPOSING PARTY

Plaintiff Alexys Watson

 

MOTION

            Plaintiff Alexys Watson (“Plaintiff”) brought the instant action against Defendant Grace Church of the Nazarene (“Defendant”), alleging  housing discrimination.  Defendants move to strike the punitive damages allegations from Plaintiff’s complaint.  Plaintiff opposes the motion.

ANALYSIS

Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading, may serve and file a motion to strike the whole pleading or any part thereof.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1322(b).)  On a motion to strike, the court may: (1) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the court.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (a)-(b); Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782.)

In ruling on a motion to strike punitive damages, “judges read allegations of a pleading subject to a motion to strike as a whole, all parts in their context, and assume their truth.”  (Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255.)  To state a prima facie claim for punitive damages, a plaintiff must allege the elements set forth in the punitive damages statute, Civil Code section 3294.  (College Hospital, Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 721 (College Hospital).)  Pursuant to Civil Code section 3294, subdivision (a), a plaintiff must allege the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud or malice.   “Malice is defined in the statute as conduct intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.”  (College Hospital, supra, 8 Cal.4th at p. 725.)  “The mere allegation an intentional tort was committed is not sufficient to warrant an award of punitive damages.  Not only must there be circumstances of oppression, fraud or malice, but facts must be alleged in the pleading to support such a claim.”  (Grieves v. Superior Ct. (1984) 157 Cal.App.3d 159, 166, internal citations and footnotes omitted.)

Defendant is an entity.  “[T]he imposition of punitive damages upon a corporation is based upon its own fault.  It is not imposed vicariously by virtue of the fault of others.”  (City Products Corp. v. Globe Indemnity Co. (1979) 88 Cal. App. 3d 31, 36.)  “Corporations are legal entities which do not have minds capable of recklessness, wickedness, or intent to injure or deceive.  An award of punitive damages against a corporation therefore must rest on the malice of the corporation’s employees.  But the law does not impute every employee’s malice to the corporation.  Instead, the punitive damages statute requires proof of malice among corporate leaders:  the officers, directors, or managing agents.”  (Cruz v. Home Base (2000) 83 Cal. App. 4th 160, 167, internal quotations and citation omitted.) 

In the instant case, Plaintiff alleges Defendant is the legal owner of the residential property Plaintiff attempted to rent; an unidentified “staff member” told Plaintiff  Defendant would not accept vouchers from the federal Housing Choice Voucher Program, or Section 8, as payment for rent; and co-Defendant Jana Huntley (“Huntley”) was an officer and agent of co-Defendant Envoy Properties, Inc.  (Complaint, p. 1, ¶¶ 3, 11, 12.)  Plaintiff does not allege, however, Huntley was an officer of Defendant. 

Plaintiff does not allege any of Defendant’s officers, directors, or managing agents acted with malice in making the decision not to rent the premises to her.  As a consequence, Plaintiff does not allege facts to support a claim for punitive damages against Defendant.      

The Court will provide Plaintiff with an opportunity to amend her complaint to cure this deficiency.  To the extent, Plaintiff avails herself of that opportunity, the Court impresses upon Plaintiff, as discussed above, for her to prevail in a pleading challenge to her punitive damages claim, she must sufficiently allege Defendant is guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant’s motion to strike portions of the complaint is granted with leave to amend.  Plaintiff is to file an amended complaint within 20 days of notice of this order.

Defendant is ordered to provide notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.
