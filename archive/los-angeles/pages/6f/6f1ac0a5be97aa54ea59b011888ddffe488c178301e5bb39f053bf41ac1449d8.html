Matter: Motion to Compel Further Response

Moving Party: Plaintiff Antonyan Vardan

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On May 28, 2025, Plaintiff Antonyan Vardan filed the operative Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”) asserting 24 causes of action.  Plaintiff alleges that after he objected to an unpermitted gate, Defendant “Kazamel orchestrated a retaliatory and procedurally invalid vote to remove him [from the HOA board]. That marked the beginning of a targeted effort to silence and eliminate Plaintiff. Over the following years, he faced illegal vehicle tows, wage garnishment, perjured testimony in court, denial of HOA election rights, and mass defamatory communications—all designed to isolate him socially and professionally. Coordinated actions by HOA members, attorneys, and vendors—under Kazamel’s leadership— culminated in Plaintiff’s job loss, failed businesses, and personal bankruptcy in 2025.”

Plaintiff now seeks to compel further responses to his RFPs from Defendant Tarek Ibrahim Kazamel.

Because there is no opposition, the Motion is granted. Further responses are to be provided within 30 days.

Moving party to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV01127    Hearing Date: July 24, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 24, 2026

Case Name: Hartley, et al. v. Living Dead Media, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV01127

Matter: Default Judgment Application 

Ruling: The Default Judgment Application is denied without prejudice.


Cross-Complainant Living Dead Media seeks a default judgment against Dragon Studios and Richard Driscoll for damages in the amount of $1.8 million.

While Living Dead Media requests $1.8 million in damages, the Cross-Complaint does not make any specific reference to damages. This is problematic as “[t]he relief granted to the plaintiff, if there is no answer, cannot exceed that demanded in the complaint, in the statement required by Section 425.11, or in the statement provided for by Section 425.115” (Code Civ. Proc. § 580.)  “A demand for ‘damages according to proof’ is insufficient; such a demand does not provide adequate notice to sustain a default judgment.” (Yu v. Liberty Surplus Ins. Corp. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 1024, 1027.) Further, phrases such as “in an amount not less than” do not give notice for the purposes of Code Civ. Proc. § 580. (Electronic Funds Solutions, LLC v. Murphy (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1173-1174.) Code Civ. Proc. § 580 applies even when a defendant has defaulted after having filed an answer and having participated in discovery. (See Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 828; Elec. Funds Sols., LLC v. Murphy (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1175.)  

Thus, Cross-Complainant can either accept the jurisdictional minimum of $35,001 or else amend the Cross-complaint’s allegations as to damages, which would be a material change opening Cross-Defendants’ defaults.  (Cole v. Roebling Const. Co. (1909) 156 Cal. 443; Leo v. Dunlap (1968) 260 Cal.App.2d 24, 27-28.)

There is also no filing explaining the propriety of multiple judgments.

Accordingly, the Default Judgment Application is denied without prejudice.
