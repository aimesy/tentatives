DEPARTMENT 517 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCP04606    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: 517


The Court is granting Xingyu Jin's ("Jin") petition under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 for an order compelling arbitration of a dispute between Jin and Respondent Jason J. Emer M.D., a, Professional Corporation ("Emer"),  over agreements for Jin's purchase of cosmetic treatments and products from Emer.  The agreements call for arbitration of any disputes arising from the transaction. The agreements do not designate a particular arbitration forum or a particular arbitrator.  As requested in Jin's petition, the Court is designating JAMS to be the arbitration forum.  As for a particular JAMS arbitrator, the Court provides here a list of potential arbitrators, all of whom are with JAMS: Linda Burrow; Cynthia Cwik; Cassandra Franklin;  Rian James; and David Platt. The Court directs Jin and Emer to meet and confer and select an arbitrator from this list.  The Court is setting a status hearing for September 30, 2026 regarding the selection of the arbitrator.

The Court strongly encourages the parties to continue discussing settlement of their dispute, short of arbitration.   The Court's understanding is that, on April 22, 2026, Emer tendered to Jin $80,321.06 to resolve the dispute.  That strikes the Court as an eminently fair amount.  The Court does not quite grasp why Emer has refused to accept the tendered amount and is instead insisting on arbitration.  The Court does not discern precisely what Jin hopes to gain in the arbitration.

Also pending before the Court is Emer’s motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 572 and 573 for an order allowing deposit with the Court, as custodian, of the amount that he tendered to Jin.  (Notice of Motion, p. 1.)   The Court is denying the motion.

Section 572 provides as follows:

 

When it is admitted by the pleadings, or shown upon the examination of a party to the action, that he or she has in his or her possession, or under his or her control, any money or other thing capable of delivery, which, being the subject of litigation, is held by him or her as trustee for another party, or which belongs or which is due to another party or which should, under the circumstances of the case be held by the court pending final disposition of the action, the court may order the same, upon motion, to be deposited in court or delivered to such party, upon those conditions that may be just, subject to the further direction of the court.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 572.)  Under section 573, any money paid or deposited to the court shall be deposited with the court’s treasury. (Id., § 573.)

 

The language of section 572 does not warrant the order that Emer seeks.  For one, Emer is not a trustee for Jin.  Further, while Emer argues that the $80,321.06 he tendered to Jin “constitutes the total sum at issue in the underlying dispute” (Motion, p. 1), Jin disputes that proposition.  Emer’s argument that the deposit would protect him “by ensuring that it is understood by all parties, the Court, and any future arbitrator that Emer has already tendered the entire sum demanded by Jin” (Motion, p. 5) essentially asks the Court to affirm preemptively that the sum tendered was the sum demanded by Jin.  That is not the case.  There is a possibility that the arbitrator could determine that Emer owes more to Jin.



Case Number: 25STCV25858     Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: 517

These tentative rulings are revised slightly from  those that were posted on June 10.  The revisions correct  typos in the June 10 tentative rulings.

JOINT DEMURRER AND MOTION TO STRIKE OF DEFENDANTS WFC CONSTRUCTION AND WILFREDO FRANCO 

Franco demurred to all eight causes of action asserted against him in the Plaintiff's operative First Amended Complaint.  The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend as to each of those causes of action.  The allegations in the FAC are insufficient to establish that Franco is liable for the actions of the other individual Defendants and for the actions/inactions of WFC Construction.

 WFC Construction demurred to the following three causes of action asserted against it in the FAC: fourth cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress, the sixth cause of action for failure to prevent discrimination and harassment in the workplace, and the seventh cause of action for constructive termination.  The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend as to the fourth cause of action.  It is overruled as to the sixth and seventh causes of action. 

The joint motion to strike that WFC Construction and Franco filed is denied as moot as to Franco.  That is because, through the demurrer ruling, there are no causes of action against Franco remaining: the demurrer was sustained with leave as to every single cause of action against Franco. Thus, there is nothing at this time for Franco to strike.   The motion to strike is denied in part and granted in part with leave to amend as to WFC Construction.  The motion's objection to the addition through the FAC of Defendants Mario Antonio Garcia and Juan Ramon is misplaced because WFC Construction lacks standing to object to that.  That is for those two Defendants to take up.  The motion's objection to the prayer for an award of punitive damages and the allegations supporting the prayer is valid.  The FAC fails to plead a basis under Civil Code section 3294 on which a jury could award punitive damages against WFC Construction.

DEMURRER AND MOTION TO STRIKE OF DEFENDANT MARIO GARCIA 

Defendant Mario Garica, whom Plaintiff alleges was a supervisor of his, has demurred to the following causes of action asserted against him in the FAC: second cause of action for sexual battery; fourth cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress; fifth cause of action for sexual harassment in the workplace; sixth cause of action for failure to prevent discrimination and harassment in the workplace; seventh cause of action for constructive termination; and eighth cause of action for negligent hiring, supervision and retention.   The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend as to the sixth, and seventh causes of action. It is overruled as to the other causes of action to which Mario Garcia demurred.

Mario Garcia's motion to strike is denied in all respects.

The ruling here is the same as to the demurrer and motion to strike of Mario Garica.
