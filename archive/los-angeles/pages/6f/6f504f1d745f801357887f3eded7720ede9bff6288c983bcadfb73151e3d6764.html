[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION FOR TWO JURY PANELS

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

August 6, 2026

 

)

 

 

Defendants Joanna Esquivel, Juan Molina, and Maria De Jesus Meza Hernandez (collectively, “Defendants”) move for an order directing that each phase of trial shall have a separate jury, including separate panels and separate sessions of juror selection. Code of Civil Procedure section 598 provides the statutory basis for separate jury panels in bifurcated trials. The court may order that the trial of any issue precede the trial of any other issue when doing so would promote the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economic and efficiency of handling the litigation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 598.) Code of Procedure section 598 expressly addresses the jury composition for subsequent phases, providing that after the first phase, the remaining issues shall be tried “before the same or another jury, as ordered by the court either upon its own motion or upon the motion of any party.” (Id.)  

Defendants argue that two jury panels are necessary to preserve the integrity of the distinct factual issues presented in each phase of trial. The first phase involves determining whether Plaintiff maintained insurance at the time of the accident while the second phase assesses liability and damages. Defendants argue that a single jury creates a substantial risk that jurors will associate their determination of Plaintiff’s insured status with his ability to recover non-economic damages, which would unfairly prejudice Defendants.

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that two juries are unnecessary because insurance coverage is not a threshold issue that would shorten trial. Plaintiff claims that a limiting instruction is sufficient to address any issues of prejudice, but the Court agrees with Defendants that it is not realistic to expect jurors to disregard what they might have learned or surmised during voir dire about Plaintiff’s alleged pain, suffering, and other non-economic damages when ruling on a narrow issue of insurance coverage. Conversely, there is no practical way to voir dire a single venire for the selection of a jury expected to sit on both phases without alluding to the full scope of the trial. Furthermore, if Plaintiff is precluded from recovering non-economic damages as a result of the first phrase of trial, the second phase will be shortened because there will be no need to present evidence or argument regarding that category of damages.

In light of the foregoing, Defendants’ motion is GRANTED.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION FOR SANCTIONS

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

August 6, 2026

 

)

 

 

Plaintiff RSP Holdings, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) moves for an order imposing evidentiary sanctions against defendants Sterling Vet Corp. and Rebecca Swimmer (collectively, “Defendants”) and monetary sanctions against nonparty Jane’s Management Systems, LLC (“JMS”). Plaintiff also requests the Court issue an order to show cause re: contempt.

The motion arises from JMS’s failure to abide by the Court’s order issued on November 20, 2025, which ordered JMS to produce documents and overruled JMS’s claim that communications between JMS and Defendants were protected as work product. Plaintiff argues JMS should be sanctioned because JMS has continued to withhold documents on unsubstantiated claims of attorney-client privilege, attorney work product, and Swimmer’s privacy rights. Plaintiff also argues that Defendants should be precluded from relying on any documents from JMS or testimony from John Ko at trial.

As the Court previously stated in its order dated November 20, 2025, a party's consultations with an expert before hiring counsel are not protectible as “attorney work product” nor can an attorney later “by retroactive adoption, convert the independent work of another, already performed, into his own.” (Jasper Const., Inc. v. Foothill Junior College Dist. of Santa Clara County (1979) 91 Cal.App.3d 1, 16 [disapproved on other grounds by Los Angeles Unified School Dist. v. Great American Ins. Co. (2010) 49 Cal.4th 739, 753.)

JMS did not file a separate statement addressing any of the specific entries in its privilege log that Plaintiff challenges. JMS also failed to submit any evidence, such as a declaration from Mr. Ko or Defendants, that the documents identified in its privilege log and withheld were prepared at the direction of an attorney or are subject to a privacy right that is threatened with a serious intrusion. Accordingly, JMS fails to show that its failure to obey the Court’s order was not willful. The motion for monetary sanctions is GRANTED. The Court declines to issue an order to show cause re: contempt.  

Plaintiff’s request that the Court impose evidentiary sanctions upon Defendants for JMS’s failure to abide by the Court’s discovery order is DENIED without prejudice because Plaintiff fails to show that JMS is a “managing agent” of Defendants for purposes of the discovery statutes. (Lopez v. Watchtower Bible and Tract Society of New York, Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 602-603; Waters v. Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 885, 896.) In order to determine whether someone is a “managing agent”, the Court considers three factors set forth in Waters: (1) does the person exercise judgment and discretion in dealing with the party's matters; (2) can the person be expected to comply with the party's directive to appear; and (3) can the person be anticipated to identify himself or herself with the party's interests. (Waters, supra, 58 Cal.2d at p. 896.) The burden is normally on the party seeking discovery. (Lopez, supra, 246 Cal.App.4th at pp. 600-601.) Here, there is no evidence that JMS is Defendants’ managing agent, therefore, there is no basis for imposing sanctions upon Defendants for JMS’s discovery abuses.

In conclusion, monetary sanctions are imposed against JMS in the amount of $4,660, consisting of 5 hours at Plaintiff’s counsel’s hourly rate of $575 and a $60 filing fee, payable within 20 days. JMS is further ordered to produce, within 20 days, the following documents identified in its privilege log in response to Plaintiff’s subpoena: Entries Nos. 1-18, 20-21, 23, 25, 27-44, 46-47, 49-58, 60-68, 70-71, 73-95, 97-104, 106-119, 121-123, 125-132, 134-156, 158-173, 175-239, 241-242, 245-253, 255-260, 270, 272-276, 278, 281-282, 284-298, 301-311, and 313-325.

Defendants’ request for sanctions against Plaintiff is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,350, consisting of 3 hours at defense counsel’s hourly rate of $450, payable within 20 days.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO BE RELIEVED

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

August 6, 2026

 

 

 

 

Tyson & Mendes LLP (“Tyson & Mendes”) seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for defendants Lucky Star Logistics, Inc. (“Lucky Star”) and Gamal Mohamed Elsayed (“Mr. Elsayed”) (collectively, “Defendants”) on grounds that the insurer has withdrawn coverage. Lucky Star has not communicated with Tyson & Mendes to continue its representation and Mr. Elsayed refused counsel’s terms for continued retention. Absent a showing of resulting prejudice, an attorney’s request for withdrawal should be granted. (People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 406.)

The Court notes that trial in this matter is set for February 8, 2027, and no prejudice will result from granting this motion. While Plaintiffs claim that counsel’s motion to withdraw from representing Defendants is tactical and prejudicial, Plaintiffs cite to no case law allowing them any standing to determine who should represent Defendants and Defendants themselves have not opposed Tyson & Mendes’ motion. Moreover, Defendants’ obligations to participate in discovery remain the same regardless whether they are represented by Tyson & Mendes.

However, the proposed order submitted with this motion is incomplete. Therefore, the hearing on this motion is CONTINUED to August 20, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Department 3 of the Alhambra Courthouse so that Tyson & Mendes can submit a proposed order checking Item 5b, listing its clients’ addresses and phone numbers in Item 6, and identifying all upcoming hearings and proceedings in Items 7 through 9. The revised proposed order must be filed no later than 5 court days before the date of the hearing.

Plaintiff Jie Liu (“Plaintiff”) moves for an order compelling defendant Zhang Yanyan (“Defendant”) to provide further responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One.

On a motion to compel further responses, the objecting party has the burden of justifying its objections and showing why a further response is not needed. (Coy v. Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 210; Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 541.) Defendant did not oppose this motion and did not show why further responses should not be ordered.

Accordingly, the unopposed motion is GRANTED and the Court ORDERS Defendant to serve further verified responses, without objection, to Special Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 1, 6, 7, 12, 15, 23, 30, and 32 within 20 days of the date of this Order.

The request for sanctions in the notice of motion is DENIED as defective because it does not identify the person, party, or attorney against whom the sanction is sought. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANTS’ DEMURRER TO FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

I.      INTRODUCTION

On November 6, 2025, plaintiff Raymundo Alejandro Maciel (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against defendants Gregg A. Guenthard and Elizabeth A. Guenthard (collectively, “Defendants”). Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint on June 10, 2026, which asserts two causes of action: (1) negligence and (2) negligent security. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants were negligent in renting their home to their son, Ryan Sterling Guenthard (“Guenthard”), who physically attacked Plaintiff on May 3, 2025.  

Defendants filed this demurrer on July 9, 2026, on the ground that Plaintiff fails to allege sufficient facts to impose a duty of care.

Plaintiff filed an opposition brief on July 24, 2026.

Defendants filed a reply brief on July 30, 2026.

II.     LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer may be brought if insufficient facts are stated to support the cause of action asserted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) A demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the pleadings and will be sustained only where the pleading is defective on its face. (City of Atascadero v. Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 445, 459.) “We treat the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law. We accept the factual allegations of the complaint as true and also consider matters which may be judicially noticed. [Citation.]” (Mitchell v. California Department of Public Health (2016) 1 Cal.App.5th 1000, 1007; Del E. Webb Corp. v. Structural Materials Co. (1981) 123 Cal.App.3d 593, 604 [“the facts alleged in the pleading are deemed to be true, however improbable they may be”].) Allegations are to be liberally construed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.) In construing the allegations, the court is to give effect to specific factual allegations that may modify or limit inconsistent general or conclusory allegations. (Financial Corporation of America v. Wilburn (1987) 189 Cal.App.3rd 764, 769.)

III.    DISCUSSION

“[A] person has no legal duty to protect others from a third party’s conduct. [Citations.] Liability for breach of this duty would rest upon that person’s failure to take action to protect the plaintiff; as a result, any duty would be grounded in nonfeasance. [Citations.] [¶] This second, no-duty-to-protect rule is not without exception, although the exception pertinent to this case is a narrow one…. [A] legal duty not to engage in  nonfeasance is actionable only if the person being sued (the defendant) (1) has a ‘special relationship’ with a specific individual, and (2) that special relationship gives rise to a legal duty of care running to the plaintiff (or, more broadly, to the class of persons to which the plaintiff belongs).” (Shalghoun v. North Los Angeles County Regional Center, Inc. (2024) 99 Cal.App.5th 929, 944-945.)

In Brown v. USA Taekwondo (2021) 11 Cal.5th 204, the California Supreme Court in Brown held that in order to determine whether a duty to protect against the conduct of a third party, the court must first determine whether there exists a “special relationship” between the parties or some other set of circumstances giving rise to an affirmative duty to protect, such as a defendant’s own tortious conduct which creates or increases an unreasonable risk of harm. (Id., p. 209.) If no special relationship or misfeasance exists, no duty can exist; but even if a special relationship were found, the factors set forth in Rowland v. Christian (1968) 69 Cal.2d 108 must be analyzed to determine whether an exception to that duty should be made. (Brown, supra, 11 Cal.5th at p. 222.)

Plaintiff’s negligence claims require him to allege that Defendants owed and breached a duty of care, causing him to sustain damages. (Defendants argue that Plaintiff fails to allege any facts establishing that they owed him a duty to protect him from a third party because Plaintiff only alleges they rented a house they owned to their son when their son committed an assault off property. (Demurrer, p. 10.) Defendants cite to several cases holding that parents do not assume liability for their adult children’s behavior simply by allowing them to live in their home. Defendants further emphasize that there are no factual allegations showing that a special relationship exists between them and Plaintiff and, even if there were, Plaintiff alleges he was standing on nearby public property when he was attacked, and Defendants’ duty to protect did not extend beyond the boundaries of their private property. (Demurrer, p. 11.)

Defendants also argue that Plaintiff cannot allege that they have a duty to control their son’s conduct because there is no ability to control their adult son. (Demurrer, p. 12 [citing K.G. v. S.B. (2020 46 Cal.App.5th 625, 633.) In Sproul v. Vallee (2025) 116 Cal.App.4th 285, the court of appeal affirmed the trial court’s sustention of a defendant’s demurrer and held that a complaint did not show that a defendant had a duty to control her husband’s behavior or warn neighbors about her husband’s violent propensities. The Sproul court discussed many cases finding no special relationship “entailing an ability to control – and the resulting duty to do so – in other family relationships” and concluded that policy considerations regarding marriage did not support imposing a duty to warn one’s neighbors of a spouse’s violent tendencies.

Here too, there are no allegations showing that Defendants could control their son, thereby creating a special relationship. And even if there were, imposing a duty based on that special relationship would undermine public policy in preserving family relationships while doing little to prevent future harm. (Smith v. Freund (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 466, 475 [“To impose a duty of care on defendants could cause greater harm in future cases by encouraging parents to disassociate from their adult children with chronic serious problems”].

          In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Defendants’ cited cases are unavailing because they do not involve “actual knowledge of prior crimes” committed by their family member whereas here, in contrast, Plaintiff alleges that Defendants knew of their son’s criminal history. (Opp., p. 3.) However, Plaintiff fails to cite any case law contradicting the two-step framework set forth in Brown and does not address the existence of any special relationship between Defendants and their son, or between Defendants and Plaintiff, which would give rise to a duty to protect him from Guenthard. 

Plaintiff also argues that leave to amend should be granted so that he can add further factual details regarding the nature and extent of Defendants’ knowledge of their son’s history and specify the specific precautionary measures Defendants failed to undertake. (Opp., p. 5.) However, adding these allegations would not show that Defendants owed Plaintiff a duty to warn him of their son’s behavior. (See Sproul, supra, 116 Cal.App.5th at p. 302 [denying leave to amend where proposed allegations of past conduct and financial support insufficient to impose duty on wife].) Accordingly, the demurrer is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.  

IV.    CONCLUSION

Defendant’s demurrer to the FAC is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.
