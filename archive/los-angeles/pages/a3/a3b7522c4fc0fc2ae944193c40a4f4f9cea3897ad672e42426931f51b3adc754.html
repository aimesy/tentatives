DEPARTMENT 8 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 26IWUD01086    Hearing Date: August 17, 2026    Dept: 8


The demurrer is overruled. The Defendant argues the three-day notice to pay or quit attached to the complaint fails to comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 1161(2). The only basis cited for her argument is that the notice is defective for failing to include required information, but she does not identify what information is. Thus, the court overrules the demurrer for making only conclusory arguments without any support. The Defendant is ordered to file an answer only within five days' notice of this ruling.

Case Number: 26IWUD01097    Hearing Date: August 17, 2026    Dept: 8


In considering a demurrer, the court accepts the truth of material facts properly pleaded in the operative complaint, but not contentions, deductions, or conclusions of fact or law. (Yvanova v. New Century Mortgage Corp. (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919, 924.) The court may also consider matters subject to judicial notice. (Ibid.)

The complaint states a cause of action for unlawful detainer based upon nonpayment of rent, as the three-day notice to pay or quit appears on its face to comply with Code of Civil Procedure § 1161(2). The Defendant argues the notice is defective because the parties entered into an agreement regarding payment of the unpaid rent identified in the notice. That argument cannot be resolved via this demurrer, as it relies on facts beyond the face of the complaint of which the court cannot take judicial notice.  (See County of Santa Clara v. Superior Court (2023) 14 Cal.5th 1034, 1041.)

The Defendant also argues the complaint is defective because it fails to state the amount of overdue rent. The past due rent is alleged at paragraph 20(c) and identified in the three-day notice attached to the complaint.

The demurrer is overruled. The Defendant is ordered to file an answer only within five days' notice of this ruling.

Case Number: 26IWUD01124    Hearing Date: August 17, 2026    Dept: 8


The court sustains the demurrer with leave to amend. 

The Defendant argues the complaint is defective for five reasons. First, the Defendant argues the complaint is not verified, but it is. Second, the Defendant argues the complaint is vague and ambiguous, but she states no basis for this conclusory argument. Third, the Defendant argues the three-day notice attached to the complaint fails to identify a specific payment method, but it does. 

Fourth, the Defendant argues paragraph 7(a) of the complaint fails to identify why the property is allegedly exempt from the Tenant Protection Act. The Defendant is right about this, and the court grants the Plaintiff leave to amend the complaint to address that shortcoming. Finally, the Defendant argues the notice informing the Defendant the property is exempt from the Tenant Protection Act was not served on the Defendant. That argument cannot be resolved by demurrer, as it relies on facts beyond the face of the complaint of which the court cannot take judicial notice. (See County of Santa Clara v. Superior Court (2023) 14 Cal.5th 1034, 1041.)

The Plaintiff is ordered to file any amended complaint within five days' notice of this ruling. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1167(3).)
