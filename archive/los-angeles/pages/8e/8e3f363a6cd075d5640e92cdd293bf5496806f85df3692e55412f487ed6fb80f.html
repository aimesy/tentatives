DEPARTMENT 735 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV14880    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 735

Usman Shaikh v. Craft Design America et al.

Motion to Quash Service of Summons

 

On May 21, 2025, Plaintiff Usman Shaikh filed a complaint against, among others, Defendant Sevan Avedian. Plaintiff filed a proof of personal service, indicating that Defendant was served with the summons and complaint on January 21, 2026, at 11:30 am at 12818 Woodcock Avenue, in Sylmar, California. (1/22/26 Proof of Service.)

Defendant Avedian moves to quash service of the summons. Defendant argues that he does not reside at the address listed above, and he was not present at the property on the date service was allegedly made. (Avedian Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) He further avers that he was not the person who service was allegedly made upon. (Id., ¶4.) Defendant also submits an attachment, which he argues shows his location at the date and time service was made, which shows he was in North Hills, California.

Ordinarily Defendant’s mere denial that process occurred without more would not be sufficient to rebut the presumption of proper service. But Evidence Code § 647 establishes that the return of a registered process server establishes a presumption of service. Here, the proof of service indicates the process server was not registered. Thus, the rebuttable presumption that service was proper does not apply. Therefore, Defendant’s filing of this motion to quash places the burden on Plaintiff to show that the Court has jurisdiction over Defendant.

Plaintiff does not show that the service was proper. Plaintiff argues that since Avedian received actual notice of the Summons and Complaint, “the statutory provisions regarding service of process should be liberally construed to effectuate service and uphold the jurisdiction of the court if actual notice has been received by the defendant.” Defendant’s knowledge of the action does not dispense with statutory requirements for service of summons.  However, as long as the defendant receives actual notice of the lawsuit,¿substantial compliance¿with the Code provisions governing service of summons will generally be held sufficient. Here, there was no evidence of substantial compliance.

Plaintiff also argues that Defendant’s GPS information is questionable. In Reply, Defendant submits the declaration of Norvik Manookian Ghaghani to corroborate his argument that on January 21, 2026, at 11:30 am (the date and time in the proof of service), Avedian was not at the address in the proof of service. Ghaghani declares that on January 21, 2026, Avedian arrived at Arno Cars, Inc. located at 8846 Sepulveda Boulevard in North Hills, CA, at 10:20 am and stayed until 11:15 am. (Ghaghani Decl., ¶¶ 1, 3.) At approximately 11:15 am, Ghaghani personally watched Avedian leave the shop on foot to go to a nearby mechanic shop where his car was being repaired. (Id., ¶ 4.) Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion to quash is granted.

The court denies without prejudice Plaintiff’s request in Plaintiff’s opposition to recover costs under Code of Civil Procedure section 415.30(d). Plaintiff may seek, if Plaintiff so desires, their recovery by noticed motion.

CONCLUSION 

The court grants Defendant’s motion to quash service of summons.  The court denies without prejudice Plaintiff’s request for costs.

Plaintiff shall give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV19601    Hearing Date: August 14, 2026    Dept: 735

Alexander Yevelev v. FCA US LLC, et al.

Demurrer with Motion to Strike

 

On June 30, 2025, Plaintiff filed his complaint against Defendants FCA US LLC, Stewart Chrysler Dodge Jeep and Ram dba Stewart Dealerships. Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s sixth cause of action for fraudulent inducement-concealment on grounds that Plaintiff fails to allege facts establishing any duty to disclose and to provide the specificity and particularity necessary to sustain a fraud claim. The elements of fraudulent concealment are: (1) the defendant concealed or suppressed a material fact; (2) the defendant was under a duty to disclose the fact to the plaintiff; (3) the defendant intentionally concealed or suppressed the fact with the intent to defraud the plaintiff; (4) the plaintiff was unaware of the fact and would not have acted in the same way had the plaintiff known of the concealed fact; (5) causation; and (6) the plaintiff sustained damages.¿

            Defendant argues that under California law, a duty to disclose based on exclusive knowledge arises only when the defendant has actual knowledge of a material fact that the plaintiff could not reasonably discover and which is vital to the transaction. But Defendant overstates the fraudulent concealment requirements by arguing that Plaintiff must allege facts regarding misrepresentations. Less particularity is required where the defendant is presumed to have knowledge of the facts equal to or greater than Plaintiff’s knowledge.

Plaintiff alleges that defendants had knowledge of the defect before Plaintiff purchased the subject vehicle in 2020, knew that the defect had not been remedied when it sold Plaintiff the vehicle, and failed to disclose the defect.  (Complaint, ¶¶ 20, 25, 67, 68, 71.) Plaintiff would not have purchased the vehicle if Defendant disclosed the defect.  (Complaint, ¶ 69.)  Plaintiff suffered harm because of Defendant’s concealment.  (Complaint, ¶ 71.)  

Defendant also argues Plaintiff fails to allege with particularity the defect GM allegedly concealed. In Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 844, the Court found that Plaintiff’s allegation that the “CVT transmissions were defective in that they caused such problems as hesitation, shaking, jerking, and failure to function” to be sufficient. (Id.) Here, Plaintiff alleges that FAC committed fraud by allowing the Subject vehicle to be sold to Plaintiff without disclosing that, “the Subject Vehicle equipped with the 3.6L engine was defective, and which may result in loss of power, stalling, engine running rough, engine misfires, failure or replacement of the engine.” (Complaint ¶¶ 16, 65.)  In any event, Plaintiff pleads the subject vehicle contained an “Engine Defect.” The Court finds Plaintiff’s allegations to be sufficient to state a cause of action for fraudulent misrepresentation.   

Motion to Strike

            Defendant moves to strike from the complaint all references to punitive and/or exemplary damages, including but not limited to: Page 12, line 7 – “For punitive damages.”  The Court declines to strike Plaintiff’s cause of action for fraud.  Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to allege punitive damages.  The motion to strike is denied.

CONCLUSION 

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer. The motion to strike is denied.

Defendant shall file and serve its answer within 10 days.

Defendant shall give notice.
