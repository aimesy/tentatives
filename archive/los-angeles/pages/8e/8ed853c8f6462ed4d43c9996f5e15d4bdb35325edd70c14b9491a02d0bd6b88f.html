CASE NAME:           Parviz Okhovat, et al. v. Ashkan Naimian, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV10430

 

MOTION TO ENFORCE SETTLEMENT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendants Ashkan Naimian and Assal Naimian aka Assal Hakamjani

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiffs Parviz Okhovat and Nahid Okhovat

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order compelling Plaintiffs to perform the terms of the February 17, 2026 settlement agreement.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion to enforce settlement agreement is DENIED without prejudice.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On April 25, 2024, Plaintiffs Parviz Okhovat and Nahid Okhovat (Plaintiffs) filed a verified complaint against Defendants Ashkan Naimian, Assal Naimian aka Assal Hakamjani, Sprout Mortgage, LLC, and All Persons Unknown, claiming any legal or equitable right, title estate, lien, or interest in the property described in the complaint adverse to Plaintiffs’ title, or any cloud upon Plaintiffs’ title thereto (Defendants) for: (1) quiet title – extinguishment of easement; (2) unreasonable use of easement; (3) breach of easement agreement; (4) negligence; (5) private nuisance; (6) private nuisance per se; (7) injunctive relief; and (8) declaratory relief.

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiffs own real property located at 15475 Duomo Via Street, Los Angeles, California 90077 and Defendants Ashkan and Assal Naimian own property located at 2780 Casiano Road, Los Angeles, California 90077 which is adjacent to Plaintiffs’ property. Defendant Sprout Mortgage has an interest in Defendants’ property. Plaintiffs allege that their predecessor-in-interest provided the Naimian’s an express right of way easement on September 10, 1976 concerning a wall against the hillside. Plaintiffs allege that the Naimians’ failure to maintain the wall caused Plaintiffs’ property damage and inability to access their property.

 

On July 8, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a stipulation and proposed order to withdraw request for punitive damages and attorneys’ fees without prejudice which the court GRANTED.

 

On July 18, 2024, Ashkan and Assal Naimian filed an answer.

 

On July 24, 2024, Ashkan and Assal Naimian filed an amended answer.

 

On July 29, 2024, Plaintiffs filed a first amended complaint (FAC).

 

On September 30, 2024, Ashkan Naimian and Assal Hakamjani (erroneously sued as Assal Naimian) (the Naimians) filed an answer.

 

On October 14, 2025, the Naimians filed an ex parte application to continue trial and all related dates which the court GRANTED.

 

On February 17, 2026, the Naimians filed a notice of settlement agreement.

 

On July 30, 2026, the Naimians filed the instant motion to enforce settlement pursuant to CCP § 664.6. On September 4, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition.[1] On September 10, 2026, the Naimians filed a reply.

ANALYSIS:

 

The Naimians contend relief is proper because Plaintiffs do not have approval permits for the project and refuse to pay $3,500 for a sump pump which is contemplated by the settlement agreement as an additional cost for the project of rebuilding the retaining wall.

 

Plaintiffs argue that they have reasonably progressed through the permitted procedures required by the City, the City does not require a sump pump system, such system would not cost only $3,500, and the motion is premature as it bypasses the dispute-resolution procedure.

 

The Naimians reply that Section 13.2 does not apply to this disagreement because it did not arise during the construction or restoration of the wall, that section is permissive not mandatory, there is still not construction or timelines to complete the project, and request a status conference in one month to check on progress.

 

There is no dispute that a writing exists stipulating to the settlement and providing that the court retains jurisdiction under CCP § 664.6. (Declaration of Mark E. Capell, Exhibit 1 [Notice of Settlement Agreement and Settlement Agreement, Item 13.3(c).]) Thus, the court has jurisdiction to enter judgment as agreed by the parties.

 

The dispute turns on whether Plaintiffs are in breach. After reviewing the evidence submitted with the motion and the opposition, they are not.

 

Critically, the parties did not contemplate commencing construction until after Plaintiffs obtained all permits. The Agreement provides:

 

“Construction shall begin as soon as practicable and within 30 days of the payment provided for in Section 12.4 hereinbelow and within 45 days of all city approvals.” (Agreement, Section 11.1.)

 

“The Parties further recognize that the construction timeline may reasonably extend due to inspections, weather conditions, permitting delays, expert availability, differing site conditions, or other matters outside the control of Plaintiff or Plaintiffs’ contractor.” (Agreement, Section 11.2.)

 

“Copies of all permits and approvals shall be provided to Defendant before work begins.” (Agreement, Section 12.3.)

 

Read together, the parties agreed that Plaintiffs must obtain permits before commencing construction of the new wall. Next, Plaintiffs provided evidence they are diligently pursuing these permits. (Declaration of Alex Rahban ¶¶ 8, 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18, 20, 21, 25, 27, 29, 31, 32, 33.) This same evidence demonstrates Plaintiffs are not causing delay – the City permitting process is. What is more, the parties contemplated such delays in the “estimate” project timeline. (Agreement, Section 11.1 [“Construction shall begin as soon as practicable . . . .”]) As such, Plaintiffs are not in breach of the Agreement.[2]

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES the motion to enforce good faith settlement without prejudice.

 

The court will consider the alternative relief of setting status on progress to avoid unnecessary future motion practice.[3]

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

1.      Motion to enforce settlement agreement is DENIED without prejudice.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

CASE NO.:                25STCV26138

 

MOTION TO DECLARE VEXATIOUS LITIGANT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Sam J. Israels; Defendants Sierra Fino and Darren Burris

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff James Webb Hunter, III

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

Israels Motion:

1.      An order declaring Plaintiff as a vexatious litigant and entering a prefiling order that prohibits him from filing any new litigation in California in pro per without first obtaining permission from the presiding justice or presiding judge of the court where the filing is proposed.

Fino & Burris Motion:

1.      An order declaring Plaintiff as a vexatious litigant, barring him from filing any new action or proceeding with any existing actions, requiring him to furnish security in the amount of $50,000, and place him on the statewide vexatious litigant list.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Israels’ motion for an order declaring Plaintiff as a vexatious litigant is GRANTED;

2.      Plaintiff is hereafter required to obtain advance approval of the presiding judge prior to filing new litigation.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On September 8, 2025, Plaintiff James W. Hunter (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendants Sierra Fino (Fino), Darren Burris (Burris), and Sam J. Israels (Israels) (sometimes collectively Defendants) with four causes of action for: (1) independent equitable action for relief from judgment (extrinsic fraud); (2) civil conspiracy to commit fraud (fraud upon the court); (3) declaratory relief; and (4) injunctive relief.

 

According to the complaint, Defendants orchestrated a staged and deceitful presentation of false testimony to procure a restraining order against Plaintiff. He further alleges that the trial judge compounded this fraud by denying him his Fifth Amendment rights, displaying bias, and improperly converting the domestic violence restraining order into a civil harassment proceeding without notice, depriving Plaintiff of a fair opportunity to defend himself. As a result, Plaintiff alleges an unjust five-year restraining order.

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

A vexatious litigant is a person who “[i]n the immediately preceding seven-year period has commenced, prosecuted, or maintained in propria persona at least five litigations other than in a small claims court that have been (i) finally determined adversely to the person or (ii) unjustifiably permitted to remain pending at least two years without having been brought to trial or hearing.”  (Code Civ. Proc. (CCP) §391(b)(1), emphasis added.) 

 

The vexatious litigant statutes “do not provide for a single procedural mechanism for declaring a person a vexatious litigant” because the “statutes refer to a person’s vexatious litigant status solely in connection with each of the statutory remedies”—a prefiling order or security order.  (Bravo v. Ismaj (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 211, 223.)  In connection with a security motion, C.C.P. “[s]ections 391.1 and 391.2 provide that the court must hold a hearing on the issue of the litigant’s status” and “consider such evidence, written or oral, by witnesses or by affidavit, as may be material to the ground of the motion.”  (Id.)  In connection with a motion for a prefiling order, “section 391.7 . . . does not state the manner in which a plaintiff’s status as a vexatious litigant is determined.”  (Id. at pg. 224)  

 

For purposes of declaring a vexatious litigant under C.C.P. §391(b), “litigation” typically includes appeals and writs.  (McColm v. Westwood Park Assn. (1998) 62 Cal.App.4th 1211, 1216 [“Manifestly, ‘any civil action or proceeding’ includes any appeal or writ proceeding.”]; Garcia v. Lacey (2014) 231 Cal.App.4th 402, 406 [“A litigation includes an appeal or civil writ proceeding filed in an appellate court.”].) 

 

For purposes of C.C.P. §391(b)(1) and (b)(2), cases voluntarily dismissed by the plaintiff (absent settlement) are considered finally determined adversely to that plaintiff.  (Tokerud v. Capitolbank Sacramento (1995) 38 Cal.App.4th 775, 779 [“A party who repeatedly files baseless actions only to dismiss them is no less vexatious than the party who follows the actions through to completion. The difference is one of degree, not kind.”]; Garcia, 231 Cal.App.4th at pg. 406 [“A litigation is finally determined adversely to a plaintiff if he does not win the action or proceeding he began, including cases that are voluntarily dismissed by a plaintiff.”].)  However, the “summary denial of a writ petition that merely sought relief from pretrial rulings and did not constitute the exclusive means of obtaining appellate review would not qualify as a litigation finally determined adversely . . . [and] cannot count towards the five litigations required” by C.C.P. §391(b)(1).  (Fink v. Shemtov (2010) 180 Cal.App.4th 1160, 1173.) 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Evidentiary Objections

 

The court DENIES Plaintiff’s evidentiary objections as immaterial to this ruling.

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The court GRANTS Israels’ and Hunter’s requests for judicial notice of court files, records, register of actions and dockets

 

Vexatious Litigant

 

Israels contends Plaintiff is a vexatious litigation because: (1) he has filed, in pro per, more than five litigations in the past seven years which concluded against him; (2) he has repeatedly filed lawsuits, motions, and other pleadings to re-litigate the same cause of action, claim, controversy, or other issue of fact or law already decided against Plaintiff entered in Los Angeles Case No. 22SMR000115 (the Domestic Violence Restraining Order Case); (3) he repeatedly filed unmeritorious motions, pleadings, or other papers on at least 18 occasions; and (4) his litigation conduct as a whole demonstrates he is a vexatious litigant.

 

Fino and Burris contend Plaintiff is a vexatious litigant for similar reasons and he has filed actions against them despite their protected status in the domestic violence restraining order. They add that Plaintiff has also expressed his intent to bankrupt Ms. Fino and her attorneys.

 

Plaintiff argues this motion is improper because the court did not have authority to allow briefing on it after he dismissed this matter. On the merits, Plaintiff argues that all Defendants failed in their burden under any theory to show he is a vexatious litigant. As to Fino and Burris, Plaintiff challenges their opposition as untimely.[1]

 

Israels replies that Plaintiff adds requirements that do not exist in the statute, Plaintiff did not rebut the presumption that his voluntary dismissals are adverse determinations to him, and Plaintiff admits to filing more than five cases in a seven year period.

 

Fino and Burris reply that a prior denial of a vexatious litigant motion does not preclude this court from considering this motion, there is no collateral estoppel as to subsequent conduct, and this is not an improper motion for reconsideration.

 

i.                    Five Litigation within Seven Years With Outcomes Adverse to Plaintiff

Israels identified the following cases initiated by Plaintiff that were not decided in his favor. This includes cases he voluntarily dismissed. (Tokerud, supra, 38 Cal.App.4th at p. 779.)

 

The seven year lookback period is measured from the date the motion or OSC is filed. (In re Marriage of Kouvabina & Veltman (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 293, 298.)

 

After reviewing the papers and evidence supported therein, at the time of filing the motion, Plaintiff satisfies the numerical requirements of CCP § 391(b)(1).

 

To start, the look back period commenced, at the earliest, on October 20, 2025 when Israels filed his initial motion to deem Plaintiff a vexatious litigant. Israels filed an amended motion on April 29, 2026 (before Plaintiff’s dismissal) and another amended memorandum on July 13, 2026 (after Plaintiff’s dismissal). Fino and Burris filed their motion on July 17, 2026.[2] The latest look back date is therefore April 29, 2026 before Plaintiff filed his request for dismissal.

 

As of April 29, 2026, the following matters were filed by Plaintiff, in pro per, and finally decided adversely against him:

While it appears there are six actions, when considering the evidence presented, there are really five.

 

First, the United States Supreme Court Cases is likely insufficient under Fink as it was a summarily denied petition with no evidence the petitions were the exclusive means of appellate review. (Fink, supra, 180 Cal.App.4th at p. 1172-1173.)

 

Next, Plaintiff provided evidence, via declaration, that he initiated an additional appeal while represented by counsel. (Declaration of James Webb Hunter ¶¶ 3, 4.) This is for Case No. B324129. (Ibid.) However, the Court of Appeal case docket indicates that he filed the civil case information stated on November 10, 2022 in pro per. (Israels RJN, Exhibit M; Court of Appeal Second District docket for Case No. B324129.) The court need not resolve this dispute in the court files because it is undisputed that Hunter substituted Lewin out on or about March 6, 2025, and thereafter continued to prosecute and maintain the action by filing a petition for rehearing on March 11, 2025.  (September 14, 2026 Decl. Hunter ¶ 25.) [3]  As a result, this appeal counts towards finding Plaintiff a vexatious litigant.

 

The remaining cases identified in this list favor finding a vexatious litigant. First, three of them satisfy Tokerud. These are Case Nos.: 23SMCV02140, 25SMCV03753, and 25NNCV04928. Plaintiff filed voluntary dismissals for each case. While Plaintiff argues that one of the dismissals occurred before he served the defendants, the court is not aware of authority that this is sufficient to defeat the presumption that it is an adverse final determination. Second, one Court of Appeal petitions satisfies Fink in that it was the exclusive means for the Court of Appeal to exercise authority (Case No. B34972). As a result, these four cases also count towards finding Plaintiff a vexatious litigant.

 

Thus, there are five litigations commenced by Plaintiff as a pro se litigant within the last seven years that have a final adverse ruling against him.[4] A determination that he is a vexatious litigant is therefore proper.[5]

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Israels’ motion to determine Plaintiff as a vexatious litigant.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Israels’ motion for an order declaring Plaintiff as a vexatious litigant is GRANTED;[6]

2.      Plaintiff is hereafter required to obtain advance approval of the presiding judge prior to filing new litigation.

Defendant Israels is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
