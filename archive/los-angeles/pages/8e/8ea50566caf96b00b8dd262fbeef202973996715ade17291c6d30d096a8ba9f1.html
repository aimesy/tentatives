Case Number: 24STCV32071    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

SARAH DAKKAK

 

 

         vs.

 

SAADIA GROUP, LLC et al.

 

 Case No.: 24STCV32071  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 22, 2026

 

Defendants’ motion to dismiss is DENIED. However, Plaintiff is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,750, due within 10 days of entry of this order.

 

On 12/5/2024, Plaintiff Sarah Dakkak (Plaintiff) filed suit. On 1/31/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC) against Saadia Group, LLC, S3 Holding LLC, S3 Group Management, LLC, Olivia Miller, Inc., Nader Basilious, and Erick Klein, alleging: (1) hostile work environment; (2) quid pro quo; (3) discrimination; (4) retaliation; (5) failure to prevent; (6) sexual battery; (7) whistleblower retaliation; (8) negligent hiring, supervision, and retention; (9) violations of the Ralph Act; (10) failure to timely furnish personnel records; (11) failure to timely furnish payroll records; (12) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (13) constructive discharge; and (14) wrongful termination.

 

On 6/24/2026, Defendants Saadia Group, LLC, S3 Holding LLC, S3 Group Management, LLC, Jack Saadia, and Olivia Miller, Inc. (collectively, Defendants) moved for dismissal.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s claim should be dismissed, arguing:

 

Plaintiff: (1) bargained for leave to amend in lieu of a demurrer; (2) secured a court order fixing the deadline to file her Third Amended Complaint; and (3) thereafter, Plaintiff allowed that stipulated deadline to expire without Plaintiff filing anything at all. The consequence of that failure is dismissal. (Code Civ. Proc., § 581, subd. (f)(2).) Plaintiff’s failure was neither inadvertent nor excusable. Plaintiff had prepared her pleading well in advance of the stipulation, and Plaintiff did not need to engage in any effort beyond amending the proof of service and submitting the document for electronic filing—a five-to-ten-minute process. (Lesches Decl., ¶ 12.) There is no reasonable interpretation of the facts other than the conclusion that Plaintiff deliberately elected to breach her stipulation to engage in that filing—a five-minute effort—within seven days.

 

Moreover, Plaintiff knew that Defendants intended to file a special motion to strike under section 425.16 (“anti-SLAPP”) immediately after Plaintiff’s Third Amended Complaint was on file. Plaintiff learned of such facts by way of various communications out of court, in court, through the June 16, 2026 ex parte proceedings. By withholding the very pleading that she had stipulated to file, no later than January 22, 2026, Plaintiff manufactured a procedural posture that sought to preserve her pending discovery motions while postponing Defendants’ anti-SLAPP. That was a deliberate, tactical choice to engage in noncompliance with Plaintiff’s own stipulation; and, for such reasons, the consequences of Plaintiff’s noncompliance should attach.

 

            (Motion, 3:3-19.)

 

            Given that Plaintiff has since filed the third amended complaint, and Defendants’ special motion to strike is pending, the Court finds insufficient prejudice to justify the extreme sanction of dismissal of this action. However, the Court does find Plaintiff’s failure to abide by a stipulated deadline to warrant sanctions.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion to dismiss is denied. However, Plaintiff is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,750 ($350/hr x 5), due within 10 days of entry of this order.

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV15497    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

RON FRIEDMAN, et al

 

         vs.

 

ERAN PICK, et al.

 

     and

 

LIQUIDATE DIRECT, LLC

 

                      Nominal Defendant.

 

 Case No.: 25STCV15497

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 22, 2026

 

 

Plaintiffs’ motion to compel Defendant Channels Manager’s compliance with their RFPs (Set One) is DENIED.

 

            On 5/29/2025, Plaintiffs Ron Friedman and Roy Livyatan (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed suit against Eran Pick, Open Retail, Inc., and Channels Managers, Inc. (collectively, Defendants) and nominal Defendant Liquidate Direct, LLC.

 

On 2/18/2026, Plaintiffs filed a second amended complaint (SAC) alleging: (1) breach of fiduciary duty—derivative; (2) breach of fiduciary duty—direct; (3) aiding and abetting breach of fiduciary duty—derivative; (4) conversion—derivative; (5) aiding and abetting conversion—derivative; (6) civil theft; (7) intentional interference with prospective economic advantage—derivative; (8) negligent interference with prospective economic advantage—derivative; (9) unfair business practices; and (10) an accounting.

 

On 8/25/2026, Plaintiffs moved to compel Defendant Channels Manager (CM or Channels Manager) to comply with Plaintiffs’ Requests for Production (RFPs) (Set One).

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiffs seek to compel CM to produce documents responsive to their RFP Nos. 1-34.

 

            In opposition, Defendant explains that on 2/26/2026, Defendants served responses with objections. Defendant’s initial written responses to the Requests contained language that was inadvertently carried over from Defendant’s response to Special Interrogatories. Specifically, Defendant’s response to each category of documents in the Request contained the following language:

 

Responding Party objects to this interrogatory on the ground that it improperly requires Responding Party to make a compilation, abstract, audit, or summary of documents where the burden of deriving the answer is substantially the same for Propounding Party as for Responding Party. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.230.) Pursuant to section 2030.230, the records from which the answer may be derived or ascertained will be made available for inspection.

 

(See Patterson Dec., ¶ 4.)

 

On 6/17/2026, subsequent to the Court ruling on Defendants’ demurrer and motion for protective order, Defendants (including CM) supplemented their prior written responses to the discovery (including the Requests). These supplemental responses, including to the Requests, amended and corrected Defendants’ prior responses and were intended to “amend, supplement, and supersede in their entirety the responses previously served by [Defendants] on or about February 26, 2026.” (See Patterson Dec., ¶ 5, Ex. C; Opp., 5: 21-26.)

 

Defendant’s responses to the categories of documents included agreements to produce in whole (16 categories), in part (3 categories), that after a diligent search and reasonable inquiry it was unable to comply with the category (3 categories), and objections to producing any documents based on financial privacy, the prohibition regarding financial discovery relating to punitive damages as set forth in Civil Code, section 3295, or other objections (12 categories). (Patterson Dec., ¶ 7.)

 

Then on 7/13/2026, Defendants produced approximately 3,650 pages of documents. (See Patterson Dec., ¶ 9.)

 

As such, there appears to be a disagreement between the parties as to whether Defendant’s initial (and admittedly erroneous) production from February 2026 is controlling, or whether the supplemental responses served in June 2026 are.

 

After review, the Court finds the June 2026 responses to be operative. While Plaintiffs argue that Defendant had no right to supplement its response to the Request and is bound by its initial responses, the code clearly indicates that the service of supplemental responses to requests for production of documents was contemplated. (Cal. Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c)—“Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the demanding party and the responding party have agreed in writing, the demanding party waives any right to compel a further response to the demand.”)

 

Additional case law suggests that parties may serve supplemental responses to inspection demands. For example, courts have held that the service of a supplemental written response to an inspection demand does not divest the Court of authority to rule on a pending motion to compel. (See County of San Benito v. Superior Court (2023) 96 Cal.App.5th 243, 256.) The Court did not hold that a party must first obtain leave of court to serve the supplemental response, nor did the Court hold that a voluntary supplemental response is unauthorized. The Court only held that it had the power to decide the pending motion to compel regardless of the service of a supplemental response.

 

Nothing in the code forbids a party from serving a voluntary supplemental response to an inspection demand, and public policy generally does not favor “gotcha” maneuvers.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ motion to compel Defendant Channels Manager’s compliance with their RFPs (Set One) is denied.

 

It is so ordered.
