BACKGROUND



This is a breach of contract action. On
May 17, 2024, plaintiff Unite Welding Enterprises, Inc. (Plaintiff) filed a
Second Amended Complaint (SAC) alleging breach of contract and common counts
against individual defendants Agassi Halajyan and Michael Keheian (Defendants)
as well as E Roane Construction Management, Inc., Edward Dean Roane, Dillon
Construction, Inc., Christopher David Dillon, Dillon Roane, a Joint Venture,
Inc., and Canplan Industries, LLC.



On July 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the
instant motion for authorization to serve defendants Agassi Halajyan and
Michael Keheia (collectively Defendants) by publication. No opposition has been
filed as of September 8, 2026.



 



LEGAL
STANDARD



Code of Civil Procedure Section 415.50(a) states that a
summons may be served by publication if upon affidavit it appears to the
satisfaction of the court in which the action is pending that the party to be
served cannot with reasonable diligence be served in another manner specified
in this article and that either: (1) a cause of action exists against the
party upon whom service is to be made, or he or she is a necessary or
proper party to the action; or (2) the party to be served has or claims an
interest in real or personal property in this state that is subject to the
jurisdiction of the court or the relief demanded in the action consists
wholly or in part in excluding the party from any interest in the
property. (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.50(a).)



“An application for an order of publication must be accompanied by an
affidavit stating facts from which the trial court can draw the conclusion that
the plaintiff has a cause of action against the defendant.” (Rios v.
Singh (2021) 65 Cal.App.5th 871, 884.) “This is a jurisdictional
prerequisite to the issuance of an order of publication.” (Id.) 



To obtain service by publication, the supporting affidavit must
meet two requirements. First, a competent affiant with personal knowledge must
declare probative facts that the party used reasonable diligence to serve the
defendant in a statutorily authorized manner. (Olvera v. Olvera (1991)
232 Cal.App.3d 32, 42.) “The term ‘reasonable diligence’ ... denotes a
thorough, systematic investigation and inquiry conducted in good faith by the
party or his agent or attorney.” (Watts v. Crawford (1995) 10
Cal.4th 743, 749 n.5; see also Rios, supra, 65 Cal.App.5th at
880.) (“The question is whether the plaintiff took the steps a reasonable
person who truly desired to give notice of the action would have taken under
the circumstances.”) Second, the affiant must declare that Defendant is a
necessary party to the cause of action or has an interest in real property
subject to the jurisdiction of the Court. (See Olvera, supra,
232 Cal.App.3d at 42 n.9.)



If the Court finds that service by publication is authorized, “[t]he
court shall order the summons to be published in a named newspaper, published
in this state, that is most likely to give actual notice to the party to be
served.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.50(b).)



 



DISCUSSION                                                                                              



Plaintiff moves
the court for an order authorizing service of summons by publication to
Defendants. (Mot., p. 2.)



Plaintiffs state
that they have met with Defendant Michael Keheia and the cousin of Defendant
Agassi Halajyan and that Defendants are aware of the claims against them.
(Mot., p. 4.) Plaintiff argues that at this meeting, Defendant Michael and the
cousin of Defendant Agassi alluded to it not being economically worth defending
the action. (Ibid.) Plaintiff argues that after an unsuccessful petition
for entry of default, they attempted to serve Defendants on May 2, 6, and 9, 2025
at the addresses of their respective businesses. (Mot., pp. 5-6.) Further,
Plaintiffs argue they have attempted to serve Defendants on June 15, 17, 20,
and 24, 2025, at residential addresses associated with Defendants according to
“SmartLinx Person Report”. (Mot., pp. 6-7.) Plaintiff states that after a
previous motion for publication of summons was denied, they hired a private
investigator to attempt to locate Defendants. (Mot., p. 7.) Plaintiff argues
subsequent attempts to serve Defendants at additional residential addresses identified
via skip trace on March 5, 9, and 12, 2026 were unsuccessful. (Ibid.)



Moreover, Plaintiff argues that a cause of
action exists against Defendants because their entities received the benefit of
construction on their respective properties without providing payment to
Plaintiff who performed the construction. (Mot., p. 8.)



Here,
Plaintiff has been diligent and made reasonable efforts in trying
to locate and serve Defendants in a statutorily authorized manner. The
claims against Defendants arise out of non-payment for construction
services rendered by Plaintiff. (SAC, ¶¶ 19-36.) Plaintiff made a total of ten
renewed service attempts at several addresses associated with Defendants, evidenced by
several non-service reports. (Arvizu Decl., ¶¶ 10, 12, 14, 16, Exh.
10-11, 13-16.) Following those unsuccessful attempts, Plaintiff hired a private
investigator to conduct a skip trace, which revealed different Los Angeles
address’ for Defendants which also resulted in unsuccessful service
attempts. (Arvizu Decl., ¶ 17.)



Further,
Defendants are necessary to this action as all causes of action are asserted
against both Defendants and their associated entities. (See SAC.)



Defendants are
necessary parties to this action, and Plaintiff has engaged in diligent efforts
to serve them. The Court finds that the Los Angeles Times is a
newspaper most likely to give actual notice to the party to be served. 



 



CONCLUSION
AND ORDER



Plaintiff Unite
Welding Enterprises, Inc.’s motion for order for publication of summons is GRANTED.

BACKGROUND

This is a worker’s compensation action. On June 3, 2024, plaintiff Rafael Gomez Wilson (Plaintiff) was employed and working within the scope of his employment with Next Level Burger Company, Inc., dba Veggie Grill (Employer). Plaintiff was outside cleaning tables when a car crashed into the building, causing Plaintiff to hit a table and umbrella and sustain injuries (the Incident). On March 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against defendants Edward Olshansky, Galina Makovoz, 4300 Burbank, LLC, and DOES 1-100 (collectively, Defendants) alleging negligence.  

Technology Insurance Company, Inc. (Technology) is the workers compensation provider of Employer and has been paying out worker’s compensation insurance benefits to Plaintiff as a result of the Incident.

On July 24, 2026, Technology’s previous motion to intervene was denied for insufficient service.

On July 29, 2026, Technology filed a renewed motion for leave to intervene in the action. On September 2, 2026, Technology filed a declaration addressing service. No opposition has been filed as of September 8, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

An intervention takes place when a nonparty, an intervenor, seeks to become a party to an action or proceeding between other persons by joining a plaintiff in claiming what is sought by the complaint, joining a defendant in resisting the plaintiff’s claims, or demanding anything adverse to both a plaintiff and a defendant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (b).)

“A nonparty shall petition the court for leave to intervene by noticed motion or ex parte application. The petition shall include a copy of the proposed complaint in intervention or answer in intervention and set forth the grounds upon which intervention rests.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 387(c).) 

If the nonparty timely files its application, then the court shall permit the nonparty to intervene in the action as long as either of the following conditions is satisfied: (A) A provision of law confers an unconditional right to intervene; (B) The person seeking intervention claims an interest relating to the property or transaction that is the subject of the action and that person is so situated that the disposition of the action may impair or impede that person's ability to protect that interest, unless that person's interest is adequately represented by one or more of the existing parties. (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (d)(1).) 

Otherwise, the court may permit the nonparty to intervene in the action, as long as the motion is timely, if the person has an interest in the matter in litigation, or in the success of either of the parties, or an interest against both. (Code Civ. Proc., § 387(d)(2).) This section is construed liberally “in favor of intervention.” (Simpson Redwood Co. v. State of California (1987) 196 Cal.App.3d 1192, 1200.) “For permissive intervention three factors are paramount: the intervenor must have a direct interest in the lawsuit, the intervenor must not enlarge the issues raised by the original parties[,] and the intervenor must not tread on the rights of the original parties to conduct their own lawsuit.” (Kuperstein v. Superior Court (1988) 204 Cal.App.3d 598, 600.)

There is no statutory time limit for filing a motion to intervene. (Noya v. A.W. Coulter Trucking (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 838, 842.) “[T]he timeliness of a motion to intervene under section 387 should be determined based on the date the proposed interveners knew or should have known their interests in the litigation were not being adequately represented.” (Ziani Homeowners Assn. v. Brookfield Ziani LLC (2015) 243 Cal.App.4th 274, 282.) Intervention is possible at any time, even after judgment, if otherwise appropriate.  (Mallick v. Superior Court (1979) 89 Cal.App.3d 434, 437.)  

If intervention is granted, the intervenor must (1) separately file (a) a complaint-in-intervention, (b) answer-in-intervention, or (c) both and (2) serve all parties. (Code Civ. Proc., § 387, subd. (e).)   

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

As of June 3, 2026, Technology has allegedly paid out a total of $13,945.25 to Plaintiff. (Kirsch Decl., ¶ 5.) Technology intervenes in this action on the grounds that it has an interest in the matter in litigation, and in the outcome of the litigation, consisting of (a) a worker’s compensation subrogation lien over and against any settlement or judgment award to Plaintiff and (b) a credit against future worker’s compensation liability in the amount of Plaintiff’s recovery from Defendants.

Procedural Requirements

            The Court notes that Technology has complied with the statutory requirements of the Code of Civil Procedure, section 387. Technology has petitioned the Court with a noticed motion including a copy of the proposed Complaint in Intervention setting forth the ground upon which intervention rests. (Kirsch Decl., Exh. A.)

Right to Intervene

            Technology argues that they have an absolute right to intervene at any time before trial pursuant to California Labor Code section 3850. (Mot., p. 4.) Alternatively, Technology argues they have the permissive right to intervene pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 387(d)(1)(B). (Ibid.) Technology argues that the worker’s compensation subrogation lien interests arise from the Incident and the Incident is the subject of this litigation. (Ibid.) Moreover, Technology argues that no other party is representing their interests, thus the court should permit Technology to intervene in the action. (Mot., p. 5.)

            Here, Technology claims an interest in the form of a lien relating to the Incident that is the subject of the action and the disposition of the action may impair or impede Technology’s ability to protect that interest since no one currently involved in the action is representing their interests. (Kirsch Decl., ¶ 5.) Thus, Technology should be permitted to intervene pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 378(d)(1)(B).

Sufficient Service

            In civil cases involving both represented and self-represented parties, each self-represented party or other person is to file, serve, and be served with documents by non-electronic means unless the self-represented party or other person affirmatively agrees otherwise. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.253(b)(3).) A self-represented party may express consent to electronic service by either (1) serving a notice on all parties and filing the notice with the court, or (2) manifesting affirmative through electronic means with the court and concurrently providing the party’s electronic address. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6(c)(3).) The act of electronic filing shall not be construed as express consent. (Ibid.) Here, the Court finds no record of Plaintiff, who is a pro per litigant, consenting to electronic service.

            Here, Plaintiff has still not consented to electronic service, however, Technology has mailed physical copies of the noticed motion and accompanying documents to Plaintiff’s address (See Proof of Service filed 7/29/26 and 9/2/26.) Further, Technology declares having spoken to Plaintiff in order to confirm the provided address is current and accurate. (Kirsch Decl., ¶¶ 3-5.) Thus, Technology has cured defective service by providing service to pro per Plaintiff via non-electronic means.

Accordingly, the motion for leave to intervene is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Technology Insurance Company, Inc.’s Motion for Leave to Intervene is GRANTED.

MOVING PARTY shall provide notice and provide a proposed order consistent with this ruling within 10 days from this date.
