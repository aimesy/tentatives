No. 10 - Alla Anatolyevna Zorikova v. Victoria Murcia, et al.

          Defendants Victoria Murcia and Luis Hidalgo’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions Against Plaintiff

Defendants Victoria Murcia and Luis Hidalgo move for terminating sanctions against Plaintiff Alla Anatolyevna Zorikova for failure to comply with several court orders. In the alternative, Defendants move for issue and monetary sanctions.

Background

This is Defendants’ second motion for terminating sanctions. It addresses Plaintiff’s repeated noncompliance with several court rulings ordering her to provide witness contact information.

This specific discovery dispute began over a year ago. On May 7, 2025, the Court ordered Plaintiff to provide further responses to several of Defendant Murcia’s special interrogatories. (Miretsky Decl., ¶ 2, Ex. A.) Specifically, SPROG no. 26 requested witness contact information. The Court found: “This request seeks discoverable information. Plaintiff cannot refuse to provide the contact information of witnesses or require that certain witnesses be contacted only through her.” (Id., at p. 4.)

On July 7, 2025, the Court granted Defendant Murcia’s motion to compel Plaintiff’s attendance at her deposition. (Miretsky Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. B.) The Court ordered Plaintiff to answer the 18 deposition questions identified in the motion, which included four questions requesting contact information for Olivia Jeong. (Ibid.) Following this order, Plaintiff refused to provide accurate contact information for witnesses in her deposition. (Id., ¶ 4.) For example, during the deposition, Plaintiff provided only a shared business email address for witness Olivia Jeong and one telephone number. (Miretsky Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. C, Pl. Depo. 81:5-82:4) When Defense counsel called the phone number during a break, Plaintiff answered the phone. (Miretsky Decl., ¶ 4.) Plaintiff stated that she knew Jeong’s personal phone number but would “[a]bsolutely not” provide it because Jeong had not given her permission. (Miretsky Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. C, Pl. Depo. 102:24-104:6.) Further, Plaintiff admitted she knew witness Jocelyn Lew’s phone number, but refused to provide it because Lew had not given her permission. (Id., 87:14-23.)

The Court heard Defendants’ first motion for terminating sanctions on April 14, 2026, which addressed this discovery issue, among other things. The Court declined to issue terminating sanctions at that time, but ordered Plaintiff to provide contact information for witnesses Olivia Jeong and Jocelyn Lew and imposed monetary sanctions. Specifically, the Court found: “To the extent Plaintiff continues to withhold witness contact information, the Court hereby orders Plaintiff to give contact information for Olivia Jeong and Jocelyn Lew to Defendants immediately. Further failure to comply with the Court's orders regarding contact information could result in the exclusion of witnesses from trial and/or an appropriate jury instruction regarding presumptions to be afforded to testimony from those two witnesses.” (4/16/26 Minute Order, at p. 4.)

          Defendants attest that Plaintiff has still failed to provide the contact information. (Miretsky Decl., ¶ 11.) When asked to provide the information again via email, Plaintiff merely gave a previously identified mailing address, and stated that both witnesses must be contacted via mail only. (Id., Ex. I, at pp. 1-2.) Plaintiff repeats this in her opposition brief. (Opp., at pp. 3-4.)

Sanctions

CCP § 2023.030(d) permits courts to impose terminating sanctions against a party for misuse of the discovery process. Misuse of the process includes “[f]ailing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery” (CCP § 2023.010(d)) and “[d]isobeying a court order to provide discovery” (CCP § 2030.010(g)). 

Courts “may impose a terminating sanction” by “striking out” pleadings. (CCP § 2023.030(d)(1).) Discovery sanctions should be imposed incrementally, “starting with monetary sanctions and ending with the ultimate sanction of termination.” (Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604.)

The Court has reviewed the file and declines to award terminating sanctions. While the Court acknowledges that Plaintiff has improperly refused to provide relevant witness contact information for more than a year in willful disobedience of Court orders, awarding issue sanctions is the most appropriate form of sanctions at this stage. “[A] terminating sanction should generally not be imposed until the court has attempted less severe alternatives and found them to be unsuccessful and/or the record clearly shows lesser sanctions would be ineffective.” (Ibid.) Terminating sanctions are “ ‘ordinarily a drastic measure which should be employed with caution.’ ” (McArthur v. Bockman (1989) 208 Cal.App.3d 1076, 1080.)

The Court will impose the following issue sanctions, with the following facts deemed admitted and not subject to contest at trial:

1.  That, on the date of the incident, Plaintiff was a canine professional specializing in sale and placement of German Shepherds trained as protection dogs.

2.  That, on the date of the incident, Plaintiff had an ownership interest in Personal Protection Dogs, LLC which did business as Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies.

3.  That Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies was in the business of training and selling trained dogs.

4.  That Plaintiff’s encounter with Defendants and their dogs was in the course of her professional capacity for Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies.

5.  That Plaintiff has refused, despite Court orders, to provide telephone numbers and email addresses for potential witnesses Olivia Jeong and Jocelyn Lew, who are affiliated with Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies. As such, those witnesses will be precluded from testifying for Plaintiff at trial, and the Court will presume that their testimony was adverse and damaging to Plaintiff’s position.

The Court declines to award further monetary sanctions.

Disposition

          Defendants Victoria Murcia and Luis Hidalgo’s motion for terminating sanctions against Plaintiff is granted in part as to issue sanctions only. At trial, the Court will treat the following issues as having been established for purposes of trial:

1.  That, on the date of the incident, Plaintiff was a canine professional specializing in sale and placement of German Shepherds trained as protection dogs.

2.  That, on the date of the incident, Plaintiff had an ownership interest in Personal Protection Dogs, LLC which did business as Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies.

3.  That Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies was in the business of training and selling trained dogs.

4.  That Plaintiff’s encounter with Defendants and their dogs was in the course of her professional capacity for Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies.

5.  That Plaintiff has refused, despite Court orders, to provide telephone numbers and email addresses for potential witnesses Olivia Jeong and Jocelyn Lew, who are affiliated with Personal Protection Dogs and Puppies. As such, those witnesses will be precluded from testifying for Plaintiff at trial, and the Court will presume that their testimony was adverse and damaging to Plaintiff’s position.

          If Plaintiff elects to provide to Defendants in writing the Court-ordered telephone numbers and email addresses for Olivia Jeong and Jocelyn Lew within 30 days of this Order, and if Plaintiff does not interfere with efforts by Defendants to contact and/or depose Ms. Jeong and Ms. Lew, the Court may consider softening or eliminating these issue sanctions. The Court advises Plaintiff that further noncompliance with court orders may result in terminating sanctions.

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Set Aside Dismissal of the First Amended Complaint’s First and Second Causes of Action

Plaintiff Alla Anatolyevna Zorikova moves to set aside the Court’s dismissal of the first and second causes of action in her first amended complaint under CCP sections 473(b) and 437c(f)(2). Specifically, Plaintiff seemingly seeks to vacate the Court’s May 19, 2026 order granting Defendants’ motions for summary adjudication as to Plaintiff’s first and second causes of action for intentional tort and intentional infliction of emotional distress.

Request for Judicial Notice

As an initial matter, Plaintiff requests the Court take judicial notice of two court opinions. The “opinions” appear to have been cut and pasted from an unknown source. The first case, People v. Nealis (1991) 232 Cal.App.3d Supp. 1, involves a criminal conviction where a dog was found, under the circumstances of the case, to be a “deadly weapon” and supported a conviction for battery. While the Court can take judicial notice of this decision, it is irrelevant to deciding the instant motion. The second case, People v. Hanif (2007) First App. Dist., no. A115392, is an unpublished court of appeal decision. That decision cannot be cited or relied on for any purpose. (Cal. R. Ct., Rule 8.1115(a)) For these reasons, the Court declines to take judicial notice of these documents. The request for judicial notice is denied.

CCP § 473(b)

Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b) provides, “The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.”   

First, this motion is procedurally defective. A motion to vacate is typically employed when a party in default seeks to have the default set aside due to a mistake or excusable neglect. Significantly, the relief is intended where default judgment or dismissal is entered against the moving party. This is why the statute includes a requirement that a proposed pleading be filed with the motion. (See CCP § 473(b) [“Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted . . . .”].)

Second, the motion also fails on the merits. Plaintiff does not show that the Court’s order granting summary adjudication of her first and second causes of action resulted from her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Instead, Plaintiff argues that deciding the motion was premature because she did not have the opportunity to inspect Defendants’ dogs. She also argues that Defendants’ expert’s 2023 article about dog bites shows that her intentional tort claims have merit. Whether Plaintiff could have provided more or different evidence supporting her opposition to Defendants’ motion for summary adjudication does not warrant relitigating the issues on the merits now. Further, if Plaintiff felt the motion was premature due to the status of discovery, the proper remedy was to move to continue the hearing on the motion, not raise different arguments on the merits almost four months later.

CCP § 437c(f)

Plaintiff also moves for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 437c(f)(2). That statute is inapplicable here. It provides, “A motion for summary adjudication may be made by itself or as an alternative to a motion for summary judgment and shall proceed in all procedural respects as a motion for summary judgment. A party shall not move for summary judgment based on issues asserted in a prior motion for summary adjudication and denied by the court unless that party establishes, to the satisfaction of the court, newly discovered facts or circumstances or a change of law supporting the issues reasserted in the summary judgment motion.”

This statute contemplates a circumstance in which a moving party brings a second motion for summary adjudication. Here, Defendants, not Plaintiff, brought the earlier motion for summary adjudication. There is also no new motion for summary adjudication before the Court.

Disposition

Plaintiff Alla Anatolyevna Zorikova’s motion to set aside the Court’s dismissal of the first and second causes of action in her first amended complaint is denied.

No. 11 - Jeremy Frank v. Ford Motor Company

          Plaintiff Jeremy Frank’s Motion to Compel Defendant’s Person Most Qualified Deposition 

Plaintiff Jeremy Frank moves to compel Defendant Ford Motor Company to produce its person most qualified (“PMQ”) to testify about 10 topics with 10 accompanying document requests. The Court posted a tentative ruling and heard argument from the parties at the hearing. The Court adopts the following as its final decision on the motion. 

“An oral deposition shall be scheduled for a date at least 10 days after service of the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.270(a).) “The service of a deposition notice under Section 2025.240 is effective to require any deponent who is a party to the action… to attend and to testify, as well as to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing for inspection and copying.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.280(a).) One may move to compel the deposition of a party who fails to appear at a deposition or proceed with it “without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450(a).)

Here, Plaintiff served the operative notice of deposition on Defendant on December 4, 2025, for a scheduled deposition to occur on December 17, 2025. (Pascal Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. 1.) Defendant did not object to the deposition notice and failed to appear at the deposition. (Id., ¶ 4.) Plaintiff attempted to meet and confer with Defendant to resolve this discovery dispute from February to July 2026; however, Defendant has still failed to proceed with producing a PMQ witness. (Id., ¶¶ 6, 8.)

Defendant raises three arguments as to why it need not produce a PMQ witness for the deposition: (1) the Court’s November 14, 2025 order addressing Defendant’s prior notice of deposition already decided that several requests, which are “essentially identical” to the requests at issue here, were overbroad and burdensome (Opp., at p. 9); (2) Defendant already produced a PMQ witness for matter nos. 1 and 2; and (3) Plaintiff already had ample opportunity to seek the information in matter/request no. 3 or request documents in written discovery. The Court will address each argument in turn.

Defendant’s first argument has some merit, but the scope can be narrowed to address the overbreadth. Matter/request nos. 4 and 8 substantially mirror the content of Plaintiff’s prior requests that the Court found to be overbroad. For example, matter no. 8 seeks testimony regarding “E-mails, memorandum, electronically stored information, or other correspondence regarding Defendant’s concerns with the automatic ten-speed 10R80 transmission or automatic ten-speed 10R80 transmission-related components prior to its introduction.” (Pascal Decl., Ex. 1, Matter No. 8.) But, the issues regarding the 10R80 transmission are directly relevant to Defendant's pending motion for summary adjudication. Plaintiff agreed to limit the overbreadth the period two years before the June 25, 2022 purchase of the subject vehicle until the date of purchase. The revision, which the Court adopts, addresses any issue regarding overbreadth.

Plaintiff properly narrowed request nos. 5-7, and 9-10 from the overbroad requests at issue in the prior PMQ notice. For example, matter no. 14 in the prior deposition notice asked for “Any and all information regarding the failure rates of the 10R80 transmissions or 10R80 transmission-related components in the continental U.S.” The Court determined that this request was overbroad because it was not narrowly tailored to vehicles in California or Plaintiff’s vehicle’s specific make and model. (See 11/14/25 Minute Order, at p. 4.) Now, Plaintiff requests testimony regarding “The actual failure rate of the automatic ten-speed 10R80 transmission or automatic ten-speed 10R80 transmission-related components in vehicles of the same year, make, and model as the Subject Vehicle in California from 2020 to present.” (Pascal Decl., Ex. 1, Matter No. 6.) Plaintiff narrowed the request further at the hearing to the date of purchase rather than the "present." This is narrowly tailored and seeks discoverable information.

          Defendant’s second argument – that it already produced a PMQ witness to testify to matter nos. 1 and 2 – is without merit. Defendant cites no caselaw holding that Plaintiff cannot compel a PMQ to testify to matters that were not addressed in prior PMQ depositions even though they were included in the notice. In addition, matter/request nos. 1 and 2 seek discoverable information. “[A]ny party may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, that is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action or to the determination of any motion made in that action, if the matter either is itself admissible in evidence or appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.” (CCP § 2017.010.) At the hearing, Plaintiff confirmed that this is only a request for documents, not additional testimony. The Court adopts this narrowing.  

Defendant’s third argument is also without merit. Defendant contends that it need not produce a PMQ to address matter/request no. 3 because “Plaintiff could have included a request for testimony regarding advertising and promotional materials relating to the 10R80 transmission in his Fourth or Fifth Amended Notice of Deposition, in response to each of which Ford produced a PMQ witness, but he did not. Further, as to Request No. 3, Plaintiff included substantially the same Request in his Requests for Production, Set One, to Ford, and should not be permitted to get a second bite at the apple by re-serving the same Request for Production in a different set of discovery.” (Opp., at p. 14.)

The Court notes that Defendant provides no legal authority supporting its argument that failure to include certain categories of testimony in prior deposition notices waives that party’s ability to obtain that testimony in the future. Further, Defendant cites no legal authority holding it is improper to re-serve requests for certain documents in a deposition notice that was previously included in a request for production of documents. Rather, caselaw confirms that this is proper. (See Carter v. Superior Court (1990) 218 Cal.App.3d 994, 997.)

Disposition

Plaintiff Jeremy Frank’s motion to compel deposition of Defendant’s person most qualified is granted in part as to matter/request nos. 1-10, as narrowed and modified.

No. 4 – Ayse Cetinkaya v. Villanoink LLC

          Plaintiff Ayse Cetinkaya’s Request for Default Judgment

Plaintiff Ayse Cetinkaya requests court judgment by default against Defendant Villanoink LLC. Plaintiff’s application for default has several defects.

Excessive Damages

Plaintiff requests default judgment for damages of $120,000; this includes $45,000 in compensatory damages for loss of earnings and $75,000 for pain and suffering/emotional distress damages. This improperly exceeds the amount demanded in the complaint. “A default judgment greater than the amount specifically demanded in the complaint is void as beyond the court’s jurisdiction.” (Airs Aromatics, LLC v. CBL Data Recovery Technologies, Inc. (2018) 23 Cal.App.5th 1013, 1018 (Airs Aromatics).) “Where no amount of damages is demanded any amount awarded is by definition greater than the amount demanded.” (Falahati v. Kondo (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 823, 830-831.) The complaint’s prayer for relief does not specify any amount of damages. (See Compl., prayer, at p. 19.) Plaintiff’s service of a statement of damages in Defendant does not cure this defect. “[A] statement of damages does not satisfy section 580 if the case does not involve personal injury or wrongful death.” (Airs Aromatics, supra, 23 Cal.App.5th at p. 1013.) This is not a personal injury case.

To recover the damages Plaintiff requests, she must amend the complaint to specifically demand that amount. Doing so would vacate Defendant’s default. After a plaintiff “file[s] an amended complaint praying for a different amount of damages and/or other appropriate relief… she must serve her amended complaint on defendants, who will be entitled to file a new answer; all issues will then be at large, including liability.” (Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 830; accord Airs Aromatics, supra, 23 Cal.App.5th at p. 1025 [filing amended complaint vacates default].) Any amended complaint must be served in the manner of a summons. (Engebretson & Co. v. Harrison (1981) 125 Cal.App.3d 436, 444.)

Amount of Damages

Plaintiff presents insufficient evidence showing she suffered $75,000 in emotional distress/pain and suffering damages. It is also unclear why $45,000 was chosen as an amount to compensate Plaintiff for lost wages. There are no authenticated exhibits showing Plaintiff’s wage statements or declarations attesting to how she suffered this specific amount in lost wages.

Excessive Attorney Fees

Plaintiff seeks excessive attorney fees. Attorney fees for a default judgment are generally limited to a specified schedule based on the amount of damages. (Local Rules 3.207(a), 3.214(a).) A plaintiff may recover “a fee greater than listed in the [default] schedule because of extraordinary services.” (Rule 3.214(d).) Plaintiff’s proposed judgment includes a request for $25,671.76 in attorney fees. That is far greater than the schedule, which provides for attorney fees of “$2,890 plus 1% of the excess over $100,000” for judgments over $100,000. (Rule 3.214(a).) Plaintiff does not show extraordinary services justifying a greater fee.

Pre-Judgment Interest

Plaintiff requests prejudgment interest at the annual rate of 10%, but did not include interest computations to support her request. Plaintiff must provide “[i]nterest computations as necessary.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1800(a)(3).) Such computations require identifying the principal amount, beginning date, end date, and interest rate.

In addition, it is not clear to the Court how violations of the Labor Code would justify a 10% prejudgment interest rate. This 10% prejudgment interest rate is only available for claims based on breach of contract. All other claims are entitled to a 7% interest rate. (See Civil Code § 3287; Cal. Const. Art. 15 § 1.)

Disposition

Plaintiff’s request for default judgment is denied without prejudice.

The Court hereby vacates the default of Defendant Villanoink LLC.


Case Number: 26STCV02936    Hearing Date: September 11, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 - William Magana v. Svih Management LLC, et al.

Plaintiff William Magana's Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint

Plaintiff William Magana moves for leave to file his second amended complaint to add Jeffrey Klein and Carlo Brandon-Bordon as Defendants, add a cause of action for aiding and abetting harassment, remove a Labor Code claim, and consolidate other FEHA claims.

Evidentiary Objections

          Plaintiff makes 7 objections to the declaration of Aronson. Objection no. 1 is sustained based on mediation confidentiality. Objection no. 2 is sustained based on lack of foundation/personal knowledge. Objection no. 6 is sustained based on hearsay. All other objections are overruled.

Leave to Amend

Code of Civil Procedure section 473(a)(1) provides, “The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.” Courts exercise their discretion “liberally to permit amendment,” and “[t]he policy favoring amendment is so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified.” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428.) A motion for leave to amend will normally be granted unless (a) the party seeking to amend has delayed bringing the proposed amendment; and (b) the delay in seeking leave to amend will cause prejudice to an opposing party. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.)

Defendant Svih Management LLC argues that Plaintiff does not provide an adequate explanation in his declaration for the delay in amending the first amended complaint because the incident involving Defendant Klein happened in late 2024/early 2025. However, while Plaintiff arguably could have amended the first amended complaint sooner, that does not mean his delay caused any undue prejudice to Defendants. Plaintiff filed his initial complaint in January 2026, and first amended complaint in February 2026. The parties are still at the pleading stage and trial has not been set. The Court will therefore permit Plaintiff to file the second amended complaint.

Defendant also argues that the content of the proposed allegations against Defendant Klein arise from the parties’ recent mediation and, therefore, are protected by mediation privilege. However, Defendant provides no admissible evidence showing this speculative assertion is true.

Disposition

Plaintiff William Magana’s motion for leave to file a second amended complaint is granted. Plaintiff is ordered to file the proposed second amended complaint attached as Exhibit C to the Declaration of Artest forthwith.

No. 12 - Marlene Y. Martin Estrada v. Angel Estrada, et al.

Defendants Angel Estrada and Dynasty Employment Solutions, Inc.’s Demurrer and Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Complaint

Defendants Angel Estrada and Dynasty Employment Solutions, Inc. demur to Plaintiff Marlene Y. Martin Estrada’s complaint. Defendants also move to strike portions of Plaintiff’s complaint. Plaintiff did not file an opposition.

This matter was originally set for hearing on August 10, 2026. The Court found that Defendants failed to file a meet and confer declaration in support of their demurrer and motion to strike as required under the Code. (See CCP §§ 430.41(a), 435.5(a).) Due to this error, the Court continued the hearing on the motion to allow the parties time to meet and confer. The Court held: “Defendants should submit the required meet and confer declaration at least 16 court days before the next hearing as required under the Code.” (8/10/26 Minute Order.) No such declaration has been filed. Therefore, the Court will continue the matter again.

Disposition

Defendants Angel Estrada and Dynasty Employment Solutions, Inc.’s demurrer and motion to strike portions of Plaintiff Marlene Y. Martin Estrada’s complaint are continued to October 13, 2026, at 8:30 am. Defendants should submit the required meet and confer declaration at least 16 court days before the next hearing as required under the Code.
