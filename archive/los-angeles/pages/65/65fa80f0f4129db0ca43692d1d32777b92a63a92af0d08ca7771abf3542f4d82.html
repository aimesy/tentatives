Case Number: 23VECV03853    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: T

23VECV03853 Sierra v Whole Foods

[TENTATIVE] ORDER: Defendant/Doe 1 Mrs. Gooch’s Natural Food Markets, Inc.’s Demurrer to the Second Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND as to the first and eleventh causes of action and otherwise OVERRULED.

 Defendant/Doe 1 Mrs. Gooch’s Natural Food Markets, Inc.’s Request for Judicial Notice is GRANTED but not as to facts in dispute or hearsay.

Introduction

            Defendant/Doe 1 Mrs. Gooch’s Natural Food Markets, Inc. (Defendant) demurred to Plaintiff Orfelina Sierra’s (Plaintiff) Second Amended Complaint (SAC).  Defendant’s demurrer placed into issue the first cause of action (COA) for promissory fraud - deceit, the third COA for unpaid wages under the Labor Code, the fourth COA for sexual harassment, the fifth COA for discrimination under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), the sixth COA for failure to prevent under FEHA, the seventh COA for retaliation, the eighth COA for unfair business practices (UCL), and the eleventh COA for defamation.

AI Hallucinations or typos?

The court is unclear if two case citations in the demurrer & reply are hallucinations or if the incorrect citations were just typos.  Defendant’s Demurrer and Reply cited to “Kelly v. General Telephone Co. (1982) 136 Cal.3d 278”.  (See Demurrer pgs. 17:13-14 and 18:2 & Reply pgs. 8:25, 9:6, and 9:23.]  The correct citation is 136 Cal.App.3d 278.  Defendant’s Demurrer cited to “Wright v. FMC Corp (1978) 81 Cal.3d 777”.  (See Demurrer pg. 12:11.)  However, the correct citation is 81 Cal.App.3d 777.  Also, the opinions of the two cases are workers’ comp opinions.

It would be better to get an explanation from counsel.  Counsel are required to be personally responsible to check every citation in their papers.

Discussion

Defendant asserted that the first COA for promissory fraud was barred by the Workers Compensation Act (WCA) and failed to plead sufficient facts.  Defendant’s argument as to WCA exclusivity relied upon several cases: Apple v. Lee Swett Livestock Co. (1987) 192 Cal.App.3d 466, 473; Spratley v. Winchell Donut House, Inc. (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1408, 1417; and Wright v. FMC Corp. (1978) 81 Cal.App.3d 777, 779.  However, the Court does not find the case law authority to be applicable to the facts alleged in the instant action.  Plaintiff alleged that the fraud stems from misrepresentations related to “starting salary, raises, bonuses, promotions to higher level positions beyond a team member and various other benefits incidental to employment.”  (SAC par. 30.)  Nothing in the COA’s allegation regarding false promise relates to the alleged sexual assaults by coworkers and a supervisor and/or Defendant’s failure to address and/or prevent the sexual assaults.  Because the three cases cited by Defendant did not involve fraud claims stemming from false promises related to salary, bonuses, and promotions or even coworker/supervisor sexual assaults, the three cases are inapplicable to the instant action.  Further, Defendant’s argument was reviewed at the prior demurrer to the First Amended Complaint (FAC) and was found not to be persuasive for the same reason.

The Court further noted that, to the extent the false promise could be grounded upon the sexual assaults, a plain reading of the WCA’s exclusivity rule shows that physical assaults by the employer are exempted.  (Lab. Code, sec. 3602(b)(1).)  The sexual assault by the supervisor is grounds to show that the fraud claim is not covered under the WCA exclusivity rule.

Defendant then argued that the false promise COA failed to plead specific facts related to the false promises.  Plaintiff added facts to allege the persons making the false promises (Manager Mark Wesson or Adam Ramirez), the when (May 7, 1996), and to whom (Plaintiff).  However, the SAC failed, again, to plead the “how”, the “where” and “by what means” the false promises were made.  Further, the false promise COA failed to plead specific facts as to Defendant’s then-existing intent not to perform.  These are the same pleading defects the Court ruled upon at the prior demurrer to the FAC.  Plaintiff had an opportunity to cure the pleading defect but failed to do so.  Plaintiff’s reliance upon the allegations in the SAC to argue that the false promise COA being sufficiently plead with supportive facts is unpersuasive.  Because Plaintiff could not cure the pleading defect as to the first COA, the demurrer to the first COA is persuasive.

The demurrer to the first COA for false promise is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

Defendant argued that the fourth COA for sexual harassment, the fifth COA for discrimination under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), the sixth COA for failure to prevent under FEHA, and the seventh COA for retaliation failed to: (1) exhaust administrative remedies, (2) is time-barred, and (3) failed to plead sufficient supportive facts.

Defendant argued that Plaintiff did not file a complaint with the Civil Rights Department (CRD, formerly known as the Department of Fair Employment Housing) based upon Defendant’s search of the CRD records.  (RJN Exhs. C and D.)  However, this is a fact in dispute and Defendant’s RJN cannot be grounds to sustain the demurrer.  Plaintiff expressly alleged that they received a Right to Sue Notice from the CRD on May 9, 2023.  (SAC par. 28.)  For demurrer purposes, the fact allegation is deemed true.  Defendant’s argument disputing the veracity of this fact allegation is not proper grounds for a demurrer.  The argument is not persuasive.

Defendant argued that the claims are untimely per the three-year statute of limitations (SOL) based upon the grounds that Plaintiff failed to file a claim with CRD and any attempt at filing of a claim at this time would be time-barred.  (Gov. Code, sec. 12960(e).)  However, as reviewed above, the argument is not proper for a demurrer and is not persuasive because it disputes the veracity of the express allegation that a Right to Sue Notice was received by Plaintiff.

Defendant’s contention that the COAs do not plead sufficient facts to support the COAs lacks any argument.  Without any application of fact to law, Defendant failed to meet their initial burden to show that a dispute as to the sufficient fact pleading of the COAs exists.  To the extent that the last argument is again based upon the contention that Plaintiff did not obtain a Right to Sue Notice, the argument, as reviewed above, is unpersuasive.

Defendant’s demurrer to the fourth, fifth, sixth, and seventh COAs is OVERRULED.

As to the third COA for unpaid wages under the Labor Code and the eighth COA for unfair business practices (UCL), Defendant argued that the claims are barred by res judicata.  Defendant cited to a prior Private Attorney General Act (PAGA) class action lawsuit, Poledouris v. Mrs. Gooch’s Natural Food Markets, Inc. (Super. Ct. of L.A. County, December 18, 2025, 19STCV10444.) (Poledouris Action.)  (Request for Judicial Notice (RJN) Exhs. A and B.)  However, Defendant’s contention requires a review of facts that are not within the four corners of the pleading.  Defendant asserted that Plaintiff did not opt-out of the class action when notice was provided to Plaintiff.  However, there are no facts within the SAC that support the contention that Plaintiff received notice or that Plaintiff did not opt-out.  On this defect alone, the argument is not persuasive.  Further, Plaintiff alleged that she was hired on May 7, 1996, and terminated on March 13, 2023.  (SAC pars. 10 and 26.)  In the Poledouris Action, the Class members included non-exempt, hourly paid employees in the State of California at any time during the period from March 27, 2015, through February 2, 2025.  (RJN Exh. B, par. 10.)  Even if it was alleged within the SAC that Plaintiff received notice and did not opt out, the claims in the SAC go beyond the definition of the Class members in the Poledouris Action since Plaintiff’s term of employment is more than the dates of employment at issue in the Poledouris Action.  Because the dates of employment do not exactly match, the claim preclusion argument is insufficient to resolve the entire claim alleged in the SAC and cannot be grounds to sustain the demurrer.

The demurrer to the third and eighth COAs is OVERRULED.

Defendant argued that the eleventh COA for defamation failed to cure the prior pleading defect by failing to specifically identify the statements that were false/defamatory and/or who made the statements to determine if they are attributable to Defendant.  Plaintiff’s allegations only allege that Plaintiff would find store items in her personal bag.  The store items would be placed in Plaintiff’s personal bag by other staff members to get Plaintiff in trouble and/or terminated for suspicion of or actual theft.  Plaintiff alleged that she would complain to and report the misconduct of her coworkers to her managers and supervisors.  However, Plaintiff’s managers and supervisors would not address, reign in, or stop the misconduct of her coworkers.  (SAC par. 81.)  The pleading defect, as argued by Defendant, was raised at the prior demurrer to the FAC.  The allegations are still defective because there is no express allegation asserting that Defendant called her a “suspect” or a “thief”.  The misconduct of her coworkers to get the supervisors/managers to discipline/terminate Plaintiff as a “thief” is insufficient to allege a defamatory statement because there are no facts to support the contention that Defendant actually called her a “suspect” or “thief”.  Plaintiff failed to plead facts to allege that a defamatory statement was made against her.  The pleading defect is the same pleading defect found in the FAC.  Plaintiff failed to plead additional facts to cure the prior pleading defect.  The demurrer is persuasive.  The Court does not disagree with Plaintiff’s contention that false accusations of theft would be sufficient to support a defamation claim.  However, Plaintiff failed to allege facts to plead that Defendant called her a thief. 

The demurrer to the eleventh COA is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

IT IS SO ORDERED, CLERK TO GIVE NOTICE.

 


Case Number: 25VECV00441    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: T

25VECP00441 El Pollon v Salazar

[TENTATIVE] ORDER: Respondent Leonardo Salazar’s Motion to Dismiss Appeal is GRANTED as to the request for dismissal and entry of judgment in favor of Respondent Leonardo Salazar.  Respondent Leonardo Salazar’s request for attorneys’ fees is to be considered by way of noticed motion.

 Introduction

            Respondent Leonardo Salazar (Plaintiff) moved to: (1) dismiss Appellant El Pollon SM Inc., dba El Pollo Sonora; Samuel Monreal (Defendants) Appeal of the Labor Commissioner’s Order, Decision, or Award (ODA); (2) enter judgment in favor of Respondent; and (3) award Respondent’s reasonable attorney’s fees.  The Labor Commissioner awarded Respondent $156,859.93. 

 Discussion

            Respondent asserted that Appellant’s appeal of the ODA should be dismissed because Appellant failed to timely file the appeal within the 10-day time-limit and failed to post an undertaking.  The Notice of Appeal (NOA) is required to be “filed” 10 days after service of the ODA.  (Lab. Code, sec. 98.2(a).)  “The time for filing a notice of appeal from a decision of the Labor Commissioner is mandatory and jurisdictional. A late filing may not be excused on the grounds of mistake, inadvertence or excusable neglect.”  (Pressler v. Bren (1982) 32 Cal.3d 831, 837.)  The Proof of Service (POS) attached to the ODA shows that the ODA was served via mail on June 4, 2025.  (Jessenya Hernandez Decl., par. 2, Exh. 1.)  The Court record shows that the NOA was filed on July 3, 2025.  The deadline to “file” the NOA expired on June 19, 2025, which includes the additional five days for mailing.  Because the NOA was “filed” after the June 19, 2025[1], deadline, the NOA is untimely filed.  The Court lacks jurisdiction over the NOA.  Appellant argued that the NOA was timely “served” on June 20, 2026.  However, service of the NOA is not relevant because the statute expressly requires “filing”.  Further, Appellant fails to present any legal authority to support their contention that “service” of the NOA would make the NOA timely.  Because Appellant cannot show that the NOA was timely “filed”, the motion to dismiss based upon the first argument is persuasive.

            As to the second argument regarding the undertaking, the statute sets the posting of an undertaking as a “condition” to filing the appeal.  Because an undertaking is a “condition” to filing the appeal, the posting must occur before the notice of appeal can be filed or no later than 10 days after service of the ODA.  The posting requirement is jurisdictional.  (Lab. Code, sec. 98.2(b); Palagin v. Paniagua Construction, Inc. (2013) 222 Cal.App.4th 124, 131-132.)  The Court record does not reflect that Appellant posted an undertaking.  Without the undertaking, the Court does not have jurisdiction to hear the NOA.  Appellant argued that the posting is unconscionable and punitive.  However, Appellant failed to present any legal authority to support the contention that the posting requirement requires the Court to consider the amount of the undertaking.  Without any legal authority, the Court does not find Appellant’s argument to be persuasive.  The motion to dismiss on the secondary argument regarding Appellant’s failure to post any undertaking. 

            Appellant’s arguments in the opposition sought to attack the parties’ settlement and the resulting ODA.  However, arguments attacking the settlement/ODA are not relevant to the jurisdictional issue presented in the instant motion.  The Court did not consider the merits of the settlement/ODA. 

            The motion to dismiss is GRANTED.  Judgment of dismissal without prejudice shall be entered in favor of Respondent.

            Respondent’s request for attorneys’ fees shall be made by way of noticed motion. 

             IT IS SO ORDERED, ____________________ TO GIVE NOTICE.

[1] Respondent asserted that the deadline to file was June 20, 2025, but Respondent’s calculation of the date is incorrect.

Case Number: 25VECV01308    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: T

25VECV01308: MICHAEL YOAKUM, et al. vs ARROWHEAD FINANCIAL GROUP, A GENERAL PARTNERSHIP, et al.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER:  PLAINTIFF MICHAEL YOAKUM’S Motion for Protective Order

Introduction

Plaintiff moves the Court for a protective order on the discovery propounded by Defendant Arrowhead Financial Group (“Arrowhead”). More specifically, Plaintiff moves the Court for an order staying all discovery propounded by Arrowhead pending resolution of the standing issue, an order requiring Arrowhead to establish its standing and an order finding Arrowhead lacks standing to propound discovery because they failed to register as a foreign entity. In the alternative, Plaintiff moves for an order extending Plaintiff’s time to respond to Arrowhead’s discovery.

On June 25, 2026, Arrowhead filed an opposition. On June 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.  Later, the court allowed a sur-reply to new issues.

Procedure

Meet and Confer

A motion for a protective order under Code of Civil Procedure, section 2030.090 must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration.(Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.090, subd. (a).) The  Declaration must state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented in the motion.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040.)

Here, Plaintiff’s counsel, Levi Uku, declares on March 17, 2026, that he attempted to meet and confer with Arrowhead’s counsel, Jacob Reich. (Uku Decl., par. 10, Ex. A.) However, Mr. Reich declares, Mr. Uku never mentioned the issue of a protective order on the basis that Arrowhead is allegedly a foreign entity. (Reich Decl., pars. 42, 45.) Mr. Reich declares the March 17, 2026, email was about the other case between these parties (LASC Case No. 23VECV01606). (Reich Decl., par. 42, Exs. L and M.) Mr. Reich declares he sent the first meet and confer letter pertaining to this case (LASC Case No. 25VECV01308), on March 24, 2026. (Reich Decl., par. 44, Ex. E.)

The Court finds the meet and confer requirement has not been satisfied. Email communications are insufficient to satisfy the meet and confer requirement. However, the Court will exercise its discretion and hear the motion on the merits in the interest of judicial efficiency.

Discussion

A party on whom discovery has been propounded may seek a protective order when necessary to protect “from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.090.) “The Court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party or other natural person or organization from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.090, subd. (b) [interrogatories]; Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.060, subd. (b) [production of documents]; Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.080 [requests for admissions].)  Sections 2030.090, 2031.060, and 2033.080 provide a nonexclusive list of directions that may be included in a protective order. (Ibid.)

Promptness

The Civil Discovery Act imposes a promptness requirement for motions for protective order but does not define a fixed deadline. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.060 [production of documents]; Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.090 [interrogatories]; Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.080 [requests for admissions].)

Here, the discovery that is the subject of this Motion for Protective Order was served on February 7, 2026, and February 8, 2026. (Reich Decl., ¶ 5-8.) On March 2, 2026, Plaintiff served responses to the requested discovery.  (Reich Decl., ¶ 9.) Plaintiff filed the instant motion on April 13, 2026. The Court finds that the instant motion was brought promptly and will hear the motion on its merits.

Arrowhead’s Standing

Plaintiff argues Arrowhead does not have standing to participate in the litigation and propound discovery because Arrowhead is a foreign entity not registered in California and did not register as a fictitious business name. First, Plaintiff cites authority that foreign limited liability companies must register with the Secretary of State before conducting business in California. (Mot., p. 5, par. 25-28; see Corp. Code section 2105; see also Corp. Code Sections 17450-17457.) However, Plaintiff cites no authority requiring general partnerships to register with the Secretary of State. Even if Plaintiff could cite such authority, Arrowhead is registered to do business in California, attached as Exhibit P to Arrowhead’s opposition is the Certificate of Good Standing General Partnership. (Opp., Ex. P.) It shows that Arrowhead was recognized under the laws of the State of California on June 6, 2008. (Opp., Ex. P.) Plaintiff’s complaint also states, “Defendant Arrowhead Financial Group, a general partnership established under the laws of the State of California . . .” (FAC., par. 8.) Plaintiff’s current allegation that Arrowhead is not registered is both incorrect and contradictory. Therefore, the Court finds Arrowhead is registered in California.

Next, Plaintiff argues that Arrowhead Financial Group lacks legal capacity because it failed to register as a fictious business name under California Business and Professions Code section 17910. Plaintiff argues that Business and Professions Code section 17918 bars Arrowhead’s participation in this action. To demonstrate Arrowhead Financial Group has not been registered as a fictitious business name, Plaintiff’s counsel went ahead and registered the fictitious business name, Arrowhead Financial Group himself. (Uku par. 7, Exs. B, C.) Plaintiff now argues that any cure is impossible as a practical and legal matter. (Reply, pg. 4, ln. 11-13.)

California Business and Professions Code section 17918 prohibits a non-compliant party from maintaining an action on a contract made, not from propounding discovery in an action in which they have been named as a defendant. The statute’s underlying rationale is that “noncompliance merely prevents a fictitiously named business from enforcing obligations owed to it until it places on record its true nature and ownership.” (Villareal v. LAD-T, LLC (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 446, 457 (quoting Hydrotech Systems, Ltd. v. Oasis Waterpark (1991) 52 Cal.3d 988, 1001, fn. 8) (emphasis in original).)

The Court finds that Arrowhead’s failure to file a fictitious business name statement with the County Recorder does not bar them from propounding discovery in this action. Arrowhead is a named defendant and is not maintaining an action based on contract. Plaintiff cannot now claim Arrowhead is barred from propounding discovery after Plaintiff chose to name Arrowhead as a defendant under their fictitious business name. (FAC., par. 8.) Therefore, California Business and Professions Code section 17918 does not bar Arrowhead from propounding discovery.

In reply, Plaintiff argues, for the first time, the federal court order (United States v. Michael Henschel, et al., Case No. CR17-00350 VAP (C.D. Cal. 2017)) bars Michael Henschel, Carlos Velasquez, and Camerino Islas, the individuals who constitute Arrowhead’s partnership, from associating with or communicating with each other, and thus the partnership has been dissolved by operation of law. (Reply, pg. 7-8, Ex. A, Clauses 5, 6, 8, 9.)

“The general rule of motion practice, which applies here, is that new evidence is not permitted with reply papers.” (Jay v. Mahaffey (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1522, 1537.) Here, Plaintiff introduces new evidence in his reply which was not provided in his motion for protective order. Namely, Plaintiff submits evidence that the federal criminal case prevents the partners from communicating with one another. (Reply, Ex. A.) Plaintiff argues the court order has made the partnership legally impossible to carry on and thus Arrowhead has been dissolved.

The Courts have recognized exceptions to the general rule. The gap filler exception permits a moving party to submit reply evidence that fills gaps in the original evidence created by the opposition. (Jay, supra, 218 Cal.App.4th at p. 1538.)  This exception does not apply here because Plaintiff’s reply introduces a new factual basis for the motion, rather than supplementing or clarifying evidence already in the record. If the Court permits the use of additional evidence in the reply, “the other party should be given the opportunity to respond.” (Jay, supra, 218 Cal.App.4th at p. 1537-1538.)  Therefore, a sur-reply was allowed and considered.

Under Corporation Code section 16802, subdivision (a), a partnership continues after dissolution only for the purpose of winding up its business, and the partnership is not terminated until the winding up of its business is completed. Corporation Code section 16803, subdivision (c) provides that a person winding up a partnership's business may prosecute and defend actions and proceedings, whether civil, criminal, or administrative, settle and close the partnership's business, discharge the partnership's liabilities, and perform other necessary acts. Plaintiff has not shown that winding up has been completed, thus Arrowhead has not ceased to exist as a legal entity. Furthermore, Plaintiff has provided no legal authority that states a dissolved partnership cannot defend themselves in litigation and propound discovery. Again, Plaintiff named the partnership as a defendant in this action, he cannot now claim the partnership doesn’t exist to prevent Arrowhead from participating in discovery.

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for a protective order is DENIED. Plaintiff’s request for an order extending time to respond to Defendant Arrowhead’s discovery requests is DENIED.

IT IS SO ORDERED, PLAINTIFF TO GIVE NOTICE.

26VECV03039 DANIELS V UNION HOME LOAN

Tentative order: Motion for Preliminary Injunction is DENIED.

The court takes judicial notice of the documents requested but not hearsay in them.  The court takes notice that Ari Daniels made certain statements.

Plaintiffs seek a Preliminary Injunction halting the foreclosure sale of real property that plaintiff Ari Daniels now states under oath is his primary residence despite having denied that under oath in his loan application. 

In order to determine whether the preliminary injunction should issue, plaintiffs must show “irreparable injury” if the injunction is not issued.  The court also will consider the likelihood of the moving party’s success on the merits, and the extent to which the balance of hardships favors the respective parties.

Plaintiffs have not shown irreparable injury.  Mr. Daniels does not state that he has no other place to live, that he owns no other homes (whether here in California which he now states in his home or in Florida which he also stated was his home).  He does not say that he would not be able to provide a home for himself and his daughter.  Irreparable harm has not been shown.  Moreover, he has not shown that he is current on the first in place mortgage.

This action derives from loans obtained by plaintiffs to which the Corbin property located was pledged as collateral.  Reviewing the verified complaint, plaintiffs do not deny that money was borrowed from UHL to the tune of the unpaid principal balance of $340,000.  This is fundamentally the basis of this entire lawsuit.  But for the loan, there would be no lawsuit.

Therefore, in reviewing the case, the court finds that plaintiffs have not shown the likelihood of success of not having to repay the loan.  True, there are other issues relating to fraud, etc., but this preliminary injunction seeks to bar the sale of property which was secured by a loan, which plaintiff does not deny receiving, and has not paid back.  For purposes of this hearing, it is irrelevant what the loan was for.  It is irrelevant as to the terms.  It is only relevant that plaintiffs received the money and loan was secured by the property.  There are no grounds stated in the complaint which would support that plaintiffs do not have to repay the principal amount.

Even if there was fraud, plaintiffs have failed to establish that there is a likelihood that they will not have to repay a loan obtained by them, so the court denies the preliminary injunction.  The remainder of the claims are merely monetary damages claims which do not involve the property.

26VECV03416 Shirley Capital v Dean

Demurrer: OVERRULED.  Defendant to answer within 10 days.  Defendant argues in the demurrer that on the face of the complaint, rent was demanded for a period which is barred by Civil Code section 1942.4.  None of the facts in the complaint support this.  Defendant asks the court to take Judicial Notice of Code Violations dated May 4, 2026.  

Under Civil Code section 1942.4, there are specific requirements all of which must be met.  Between the Code Violation notice and the face of the complaint do not satisfy all of the requirements such that the court can say as a matter of law that rent could not have been collected:

(1)The dwelling has to be substantially uninhabitable under Section 1941.1 or violate Section 17920.10 of the Health and Safety Code, or is deemed and declared substandard as set forth in Section 17920.3 of the Health and Safety Code because conditions listed in that section exist to an extent that endangers the life, limb, health, property, safety, or welfare of the public or the occupants of the dwelling.  This does not appear in the Code Violation notice nor in the complaint.

(2)A public officer or employee who is responsible for the enforcement of any housing law, after inspecting the premises, has notified the landlord or the landlord's agent in writing of his or her obligations to abate the nuisance or repair the substandard conditions.  No notice to repair conditions listed in (1) above are shown.

(3)The conditions have existed and have not been abated 35 days beyond the date of service of the notice specified in paragraph (2) and the delay is without good cause. There is no evidence of this on the face of the complaint.

(4)The conditions were not caused by an act or omission of the tenant or lessee in violation of Section 1929 or 1941.2.  This also does not appear on the face of the complaint.

For these reasons, the court overrules the demurrer.  These issues are more properly for when the court can review evidence outside the face of the complaint and Judicial Notice.

The court admonishes counsel for defendant for submitting the declaration of defendant to the court on 7/29/2026.  As a licensed attorney, he knows it is improper to argue facts outside the face of the complaint on a demurrer.  Moreover, he attached videos to the reply which have nothing to do with the demurrer.  He should know better. 

 Ex parte application: Granted.  As the matter has been fully briefed, the court advances the motion to compel answers to interrogatories and deem request for admissions admitted to 7/31/2026 and DENIES the motions.  The evidence establishes that both the interrogatories and the request for admission responses were timely served on 7/27/2026.  For insufficient responses, a different motion needs to be filed to compel further responses.  Sanctions denied.
