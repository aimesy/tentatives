Case Number: 26VECP00348    Hearing Date: August 31, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Ocheret v. City of Los Angeles, Case no. 26VECP00348

Hearing date August 31, 2026

Petitioner Ocheret’s Petition for Order to File Late Tort Claim Against Government Entity

Petitioner Ocheret petitions for an order allowing her to file a late tort claim against a government entity, respondent City of Los Angeles, pursuant to Gov. Code §946.6. Respondent opposes.

Per the Government Claim Act’s claim-presentation requirements (Gov. Code §901, et seq.), a plaintiff cannot state a cause of action for damages against a public entity unless plaintiff presents a timely pre-litigation claim for damages or is excused from doing so. Gov. Code, §§945.4, 950.2; State of California v. Superior Court (Bodde) (2004) 32 Cal.4th 1234, 1243. The deadline for presenting a claim for damages for personal injury is six months from accrual. Gov. Code §911.2(a). Under statutorily prescribed circumstances, a court may relieve a claimant of failure to timely present a claim. Gov. Code, §946.6. Petitioner has the burden of proving one or more of the grounds for relief. N.G. v. County of San Diego (2020) 59 Cal.App.5th 63, 72.

Petitioner alleges she tripped and fell on a “pothole and/or cracked, uneven, misleveled, and deteriorated roadway surface” at the intersection of Welby Way and Sales Avenue in West Hills on 6/16/25. Decl. L. Ocheret, para. 2. Petitioner sustained fractures of the left hand and elbow, requiring surgeries in September 2025, January 2026 and May 2026. Id., paras. 2-4.

The six-month deadline to file a pre-litigation governmental tort claim ran 12/16/25. Petitioner filed a claim and an application for leave to present a late claim on 5/22/26, almost a year after her claim accrued. Decl. Downey, exh. 1. Respondent City denied the application on 6/5/26. Id. at exh. 2.

Petitioner argues she was preoccupied with medical treatment and recovery, which caused her to delay consulting with a legal representative until May 2026. See Decl. L. Orcheret, para. 11. Petitioner argues she was reliant on her adult daughter’s support to manage day-to-day activities and was functionally physically and mentally incapacitated for purposes of Gov. Code §911.6(b)(4). Petitioner argues her circumstances, including her injuries and limited ability to communicate in English, establish mistake, inadvertence or excusable neglect, such that relief is warranted.

Respondent City argues petitioner unreasonably delayed in applying for leave to present a late claim, delaying nearly a year. Courts have found unreasonable delay even where an application for leave to present a late claim was made within the one-year limit. See Drummond v. County of Fresno (1987) 193 Cal.App.3d 1406, 1411. Respondent argues the nearly one-year delay from claim accrual to application is unreasonable and asserts the five-month delay from 12/16/25 to 5/22/26 constitutes further unreasonable delay.

Petitioner cites Barragan v. County of Los Angeles (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1373 for the premise that, where the claimant’s physical and/or mental condition sufficiently limits her ability to function and seek counsel such that the failure to do so could itself be considered the act of a reasonably prudent person under the same or similar circumstances, excusable neglect is established. Id. at 1385. Per Barragan, evidence or affidavits by petitioner may be sufficient to establish adequate cause for relief. Id. at 1382.

Petitioner offered her declaration and the declaration of her adult daughter/primary caregiver, B. Ocheret. Petitioner experienced persistent pain and impaired sleep, took pain medications that affected her concentration, clarity, and energy, suffered limitations in performing ordinary activities of daily living, underwent unsuccessful conservative treatment followed by multiple surgical procedures and depended upon her daughter and family members for assistance with daily needs and medical care. See generally Decls. L. Ocheret, B. Ocheret.

Respondent City does not dispute the severity of petitioner’s injuries nor scope of her medical treatment and recovery. Rather, respondent City argues the severity of petitioner’s condition does not constitute a sufficient basis to establish excusable neglect. While Barragan, supra, found an appellant who was rendered quadriplegic and had to relearn ADLs was entitled to relief from the tort claim time requirements, petitioner here fails to substantiate an analogous basis for relief.

Petitioner failed to proffer affidavits from medical professionals as to her physical condition or limitations. Petitioner failed to offer details regarding the severity of her medical procedures or details regarding subsequent inpatient or outpatient treatment. Assertions of incapacity in a self-serving declaration are insufficient to find excusable neglect in this case.

Petitioner must provide specific facts to establish the bases for the relief asserted. Dep’t. of Water & Power v. Superior Court (Dzhibinyan) (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 1288, 1293. “A superior court may properly deny relief under Government Code section 496.6 where there is simply no competent evidence before the trial court upon which it could exercise its discretion.” Munoz v. State of California (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1767, 1783.

While public policy generally favors resolution of claims on the merits, that policy cannot be applied indiscriminately to render the specific statutory time limits imposed by the Government Claims Act ineffective. Barragan, supra, at 1382.

The issues faced by petitioner– significant injury, medical treatment, including surgery, and recovery allegedly preventing her from filing a claim within the statutory timeframe – are not unique. Under the facts presented, this does not establish excusable neglect nor a sufficient basis for relief from the requirements of filing a timely tort claim per Gov. Code 901, et seq. There is not sufficient evidence for the court to exercise its discretion in granting relief. DENIED.


Case Number: 26VECV01589    Hearing Date: August 31, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Ward v. Community Creative Network, Case no. 26VECV01589

Hearing date August 31, 2026

Plaintiff Ward’s Motion to Deem RFAs Admitted - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Ward, trustee of the Ward Family Trust and the Raeleene K. Darling Trust, sues defendant Community Creative Network for unlawful detainer. Plaintiff moves to deem RFAs admitted. The motion is unopposed.

If a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response, propounding party may move to deem the matters admitted. Code Civ. Proc. §2033.280(2)(b).

Plaintiff served RFAs, set one, on defendant 6/18/26. Decl. Szkolnik, para. 5, exh. 1. Despite multiple extensions, defendant failed to serve responses. Id. at paras. 7-9. At the case management conference on 7/16/26, defense counsel represented that verified discovery responses would be provided within 48 hours. See 7/16/26 Min. Order. No responses were provided. Decl. Szkolnik, para. 7. The motion is unopposed. An order deeming RFAs admitted is warranted.

Sanctions are mandated. Code Civ. Proc. §2033. Plaintiff requests $1,730 in sanctions. Decl. Szkolnik para. 12. Counsel requests an hourly rate of $550/hour, based upon his 40 years of experience and emphasis on unlawful detainer matters. Id. This is reasonable in light of counsel’s expertise and is granted. Plaintiff requests 3 hours for motion drafting and appearance at hearing, $550 x 3 = $1,650. Id. This is reasonable. The court awards $80 in filing fees.

GRANTED; sanctions of $1,730 payable by defendant only within 30 days.
