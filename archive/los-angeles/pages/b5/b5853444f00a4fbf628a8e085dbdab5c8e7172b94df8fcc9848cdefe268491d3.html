Case Number: 23STCV26193    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 224

Villagomez v. Futura Truck Line, Inc.,

TENTATIVE RULING

The Motion to Enforce Settlement is GRANTED as to Defendant Futura Truck Line Inc., and judgment shall be entered against Futura Truck Line Inc. in the amount of $142,500.00. The motion is DENIED as to Juan Ortega, without prejudice to Plaintiff’s enforcement of the Personal Guaranty by separate action.

Introduction

On October 26, 2023, Plaintiff Fernando Villagomez filed the Complaint in this action.

On August 4, 2025, following the first day of trial, the parties attended private mediation, reached a settlement, and executed a Memorandum of Understanding

under which Defendant agreed to pay Plaintiff $142,500.00 in exchange for a dismissal with prejudice and a full release.

On October 30, 2025, the Court entered an order retaining jurisdiction to enforce the settlement pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6.

On December 31, 2025, the first settlement installment of $75,000.00 came due, and Defendant failed to make payment.

On January 5, 2026, Plaintiff served Defendant with written notice of default, and Defendant did not cure within the two-day period provided by the parties' stipulation.

On March 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Enforce the Parties' Settlement Under Code of Civil Procedure Section 664.6 and for Entry of Judgment Against Defendant Futura Truck Line Inc. and Juan Ortega.

No opposition or reply has been filed.

Legal Standard

“If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If requested by the parties, the court may retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6.) “Section 664.6 empowers a court to enforce a settlement agreement by way of a summary procedure if certain requirements are satisfied. [citations] In order to take advantage of the statute’s expedited procedure, a party must first establish the agreement at issue was set forth ‘in a writing signed by the parties’ (section 664.6) or was made orally before the court [citation].” (Harris v. Rudin, Richman & Appel (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 299, 304.)

Discussion

Plaintiff moves to enforce the Memorandum of Understanding executed by the parties following mediation on August 4, 2025. (Kim Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. A.) The MOU was signed by Plaintiff Fernando Villagomez and by Juan Ortega as President of Futura Truck Line Inc. (Id., Ex. A.) In exchange for payment of $142,500.00,

Plaintiff agreed to dismiss the operative complaint with prejudice and grant a full release. (Id.) The MOU states that it constitutes a written settlement agreement within the meaning of section 664.6, waives mediation confidentiality under Evidence Code sections 1122 and 1123, and authorizes the Court to enforce it. (Id.)

The Court finds the MOU is signed by the parties as section 664.6 requires.

Per the MOU’s terms, Defendant was to pay $75,000.00 by December 31, 2025, $32,750.00 by February 15, 2026, and $32,750.00 by March 15, 2026. (Kim Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. A.) The parties separately executed a Stipulation for Entry of Judgment in which Defendant admits liability in the sum of $142,500.00, waives its right to trial and to appeal, and consents to entry of judgment for $142,500.00, less any payments made, upon an uncured default. (Kim Decl. ¶ 5, Ex. C.)

Plaintiff establishes that Defendant defaulted. Defendant did not pay the first installment due December 31, 2025. (Kim Decl. ¶¶ 3, 6.) On January 5, 2026, Plaintiff served written notice of default on defense counsel as the Stipulation requires, affording Defendant until January 7, 2026, to cure. (Kim Decl. ¶ 6, Ex. E.) Defendant did not cure and has paid nothing toward the settlement. (Kim Decl. ¶ 3.)

The Court retained jurisdiction to enforce the settlement under section 664.6 by stipulation and order dated October 30, 2025, filed before any dismissal. (Kim Decl. ¶ 7, Ex. D.) Defendant has filed no opposition. On this record, the settlement is enforceable against Defendant Futura Truck Line Inc., which is a party to the action, signed the settlement, admitted liability, and defaulted without curing. Judgment is properly entered against Futura Truck Line Inc. in the amount of $142,500.00, less any payments made under the Payment Plan. No payments having been made, the judgment amount is $142,500.00.

Plaintiff also seeks judgment against Juan Ortega individually. The Court cannot grant that relief on this motion. Section 664.6 authorizes entry of judgment only against parties to the pending litigation, and Juan Ortega is not a named defendant in this action. The sole named Defendant is Futura Truck Line Inc. Ortega signed the MOU and the Stipulation for Entry of Judgment in his representative capacity as Futura’s president, and the Stipulation by its terms provides for judgment only against “Defendant.” Judgment against Ortega individually therefore may not be entered under section 664.6.

Accordingly, the Motion to Enforce Settlement is GRANTED as to Defendant Futura Truck Line Inc., and judgment shall be entered against Futura Truck Line

Inc. in the amount of $142,500.00. The motion is DENIED as to Juan Ortega, without prejudice to Plaintiff’s enforcement of the Personal Guaranty by separate action.

Case Number: 24STCP01657    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 224

N444SS, LLC v. Avire, LLC

TENTATIVE RULING

The Court hereby CONTINUES the hearing on the instant Motion for Attorney’s Fees for Avire to submit supplemental evidence demonstrating the amount of time incurred on this matter. The hearing is continued to September 28, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. Supplemental filings demonstrating admissible billing and time entries, hourly rates, etc. are due no later than August 31, 2026.

Introduction

On May 22, 2024, Plaintiff filed the Petition in this action.

On March 26, 2025, Avire filed its Notice of Appeal.

On November 24, 2025, the Court of Appeal issued its opinion, awarding Avire its costs on appeal.

On January 28, 2026, the remittitur issued.

On March 9, 2026, Avire filed the instant Motion for Attorney's Fees and Costs Incurred on Appeal, seeking a total of $81,970.89, consisting of: (1) $80,134.50 in attorney's fees; (2) $514.42 in filing fees; and (3) $1,321.97 in computerized legal research costs.

No opposition has been filed.

Legal Standard

A party may claim attorney’s fees on appeal as required under a statute or contract but must also make such claims within 40 days after the remittitur. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1702(c)(1).)¿ Failure to follow the procedure results in loss of the right to costs, although the court has the power pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 473 to relieve the delay if failure to timely file was due to mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.¿ (Russell v. Trans Pacific Group (1993) 19 Cal.App.4th 1717, 1729; see also Robinson v. U-Haul Company of California (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 304, 328.)¿However, the parties are permitted to extend the time for filing a motion for attorneys’ fees by stipulation for an additional 60 days before the time has expired to file such a motion. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1702(c)(2).)

As in the trial court, attorney fees on appeal are recoverable when authorized by statute or contract.¿ (See Code Civ. Proc. § 1021, Civil Code § 1717, Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1702; see also Serrano v. Unruh (1982) 32 Cal.3d 621.)¿ Even if a statute does not expressly authorize attorney fees on appeal, a successful party may recover fees incurred on appeal when the statute expressly grants such an award in the trial court, unless the statute specifically provides otherwise.¿ (Morcos v. Board of Retirement (1990) 51 Cal.3d 924.)¿ However, where a statute does not permit recovery of attorney fees as costs in the trial court, they necessarily are not awardable on appeal.¿ (Imperial Bank v. Pim Electric, Inc. (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 540.)

The lodestar method of calculation applies to a statutory award of attorney fees unless the statute provides another method. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1135.) “The determination of what constitutes a reasonable fee generally ‘begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate....’” “[T]he lodestar is the basic fee for comparable legal services in the community; it may be adjusted by the court based on factors including, as relevant herein, (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award....” (Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 154.)

“In determining the lodestar amount, a prevailing party generally may not recover for work on causes of action on which the party was unsuccessful” except in some cases where are amounts are so intertwined that it would be impracticable to separate them out. (Mann v. Quality Old Time Service, Inc. (2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 328, 342; Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 157.) " ‘Attorneys fees need not be apportioned between distinct causes of action where plaintiff's various claims involve a common core of facts or are based on related legal theories.'"" Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 159.) “[A]ttorney fees need not be reduced for work on unsuccessful claims if the claims ‘are so intertwined that it would be impracticable, if not impossible, to separate the attorney's time into compensable and noncompensable units.’" (Mann v. Quality Old Time Service, Inc. (2006) 139 Cal. App. 4th 328, 342.)

In setting the hourly rate for an attorney fees award, courts are entitled to consider the rate of “‘fees customarily charged by that attorney and others in the community for similar work.’" (Bihun v. AT&T Information Systems, Inc. (1993) 13 Cal. App. 4th 976, 997, overruled on other grounds by Lakin v. Watkins Associated Indus. (1993) 6 Cal. 4th 644, 664.) “‘A fee request that appears unreasonably inflated is a special circumstance permitting the trial court to reduce the award or deny one altogether.” (Chavez v. City of L.A. (2010) 47 Cal.4th 970, 990.)

Timeliness

A motion to claim contractual attorney's fees on appeal must be served and filed within the time for filing the memorandum of costs on appeal – i.e., within 40 days after issuance of the remittitur. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1702(c)(1); Rule 8.278(c)(1).)

The remittitur issued January 28, 2026. Excluding the date of issuance, 40 days from that date is March 9, 2026 (February 2026 had 28 days). Avire served and filed both this Motion and its Memorandum of Costs on Appeal on March 9, 2026.

The Motion is therefore timely, and no relief under Code of Civil Procedure § 473 is required.

Lodestar

Avire requests a lodestar in the amount of $80,134.50 in attorney fees.

The hourly rates for the timekeepers who billed on this matter range from $330 to $675 per hour. Amy Nashon, a partner, billed at $595 in 2024 and $645 in 2025. Daniel Rygorsky, a partner, billed at $675 in 2025. Chinelo Ikem, an associate, billed at $485 in 2024 and $535 in 2025. Carol Tenney, a paralegal, billed at $330 in 2024. (Nashon Decl. ¶ 4.) The Nashon Declaration attests that these are the normal and customary rates for attorneys in California with commensurate levels of education, experience, and expertise. (Id.)

The Court finds the hourly rates to be reasonable.

However, Avire fails to provide, and the Court cannot deduce, approximately how much time has been incurred on the matter such that the amount of requested fees is “reasonable.” Key to the lodestar inquiry is whether the amount of time spent is reasonable. While Avire is not required to submit itemized, contemporaneous billing records to recover fees, it is still required to provide some evidence of the hours worked from which the Court can assess reasonableness. As the court explained in Mardirossian & Associates, Inc. v. Ersoff (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 257, 269, a mere request for a flat fee for "services rendered," without any attempt to explain the extent of the services in more than general terms, is insufficient to support an award. A fee applicant must furnish the court with enough information, whether through declarations describing the hours expended or other competent evidence, to permit the court to make an informed evaluation of the time reasonably spent.

Therefore, the Court hereby CONTINUES the hearing on the instant Motion for Attorney’s Fees for Avire to submit supplemental evidence demonstrating the amount of time incurred on this matter.

Conclusion

The Court hereby CONTINUES the hearing on the instant Motion for Attorney’s Fees for Avire to submit supplemental evidence demonstrating the amount of time incurred on this matter. The hearing is continued to September 28, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. Supplemental filings demonstrating admissible billing and time entries, hourly rates, etc. are due no later than August 31, 2026.
