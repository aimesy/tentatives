BACKGROUND

            On December 6, 2023, plaintiff Mahin Golchin filed a complaint against LAX Physical Medicine, LAX Physical Therapy, and Matthew Araghi for general negligence; and against DJO Global, Inc. for product liability. 

            On January 26, 2024, plaintiff filed an amendment designating Doe 1 as Matthew Szkalak.

On March 14, 2024, the Court sustained defendants LAX Physical Medicine and Matthew Araghi’s demurrer with leave to amend based on uncertainty because there were no allegations against LAX Physical Medicine.

On March 14, 2024, plaintiff filed a FAC.

On April 30, 2024, the Court overruled LAX Physical Medicine and Araghi’s demurrer to the first cause of action in the FAC.

On December 30, 2025, the Court granted motion to continue trial, from March 16, 2026 to May 4, 2026.

On February 2, 2026, plaintiff filed an amendment designating DJO, LLC as Doe 1.

On March 10, 2026, the Court denied defendant DJO Global Inc.’s motion for summary judgment.

On April 23, 2026, the Court denied DJO Global’s motion for relief from untimely expert witness designation.

On April 24, 2026, the Court granted plaintiff’s oral request to dismiss DJO, LLC.  The Court continued the trial to June 1, 2026.  Defendants Szkalak and LAX Physical Therapy indicated that they reached a settlement.

On April 30, 2026, the Court granted plaintiff’s oral request to dismiss DJO Global, Inc.  The Court denied DJO Global’s motion for terminating and monetary sanctions.

On May 6, 2026, based on the agreement of the existing parties, the Court vacated the trial date.

On May 8, 2026, plaintiff filed an amendment stating that the true name for DJO Global Inc. is DJO LLC.

On May 14, 2026, DJO Global, Inc. filed a memorandum of costs for $77,641.80.

On June 1, 2026, plaintiff filed a request for dismissal as to defendants LAX Physical Therapy and Matthew Szkalak.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

Demurrer

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.  Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal. App. 4th 1216, 1228.  “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters.  Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.”  SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal. App. 3d 902, 905.  “The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”  Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal. App. 4th 740, 747.  Sufficient facts are the essential facts of the case “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficiently specific to acquaint the defendant with the nature, source, and extent of his cause of action.” Gressley v. Williams (1961) 193 Cal. App. 2d 636, 643-44.

Strike

“The court may, upon a motion . . . , or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:  (a) Strike any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.  (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”  CCP §436(b).

            CCP §431.10 states:  “(a) A material allegation in a pleading is one essential to the claim or defense and which could not be stricken from the pleading without leaving it insufficient as to that claim or defense. 

(b) An immaterial allegation in a pleading is any of the following:  (1) An allegation that is not essential to the statement of a claim or defense.  (2) An allegation that is neither pertinent to nor supported by an otherwise sufficient claim or defense.  (3) A demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint or cross-complaint. 

(c) An ‘immaterial allegation’ means ‘irrelevant matter’ as that term is used in Section 436.”

The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice.  CCP §437.

CCP §474 and relation back

Under CCP §474, “When the plaintiff is ignorant of the name of a defendant, he must state that fact in the complaint, or the affidavit if the action is commenced by affidavit, and such defendant may be designated in any pleading or proceeding by any name, and when his true name is discovered, the pleading or proceeding must be amended accordingly . . . .”  Where a complaint is amended after the statute of limitations has run to identify a fictitiously-named defendant, the amended complaint will “relate back” to and be deemed filed as of the date of the original complaint, if: (1) the original complaint stated a valid cause of action against the now-identified Doe defendant; (2) plaintiff was “genuinely ignorant” of the defendant’s identity; and (3) the amended complaint is based on the same general set of facts as the original.  Austin v. Massachusetts Bonding & Ins. Co. (1961) 56 Cal.2d 596, 600-601.

Amendment

“As a general rule, ‘an amended complaint that adds a new defendant does not relate back to the date of filing the original complaint and the statute of limitations is applied as of the date the amended complaint is filed, not the date the original complaint is filed.’  But where an amendment does not add a ‘new’ defendant, but simply corrects a misnomer by which an ‘old’ defendant was sued, case law recognizes an exception to the general rule of no relation back.”  Hawkins v. Pacific Coast Bldg. Products, Inc. (2004) 124 Cal. App. 4th 1497, 1503 (citations omitted).  “Whether a plaintiff may amend the complaint to change a party’s description or characterization ‘after the statute of limitations has run depends on whether the misdescription or mischaracterization is merely a misnomer or defect in the description or characterization, or whether it is a substitution or entire change of parties.  In the former case, an amendment will be allowed; in the latter, it will not be allowed.’”  Id. at 1504 (citations omitted).

DISCUSSION

            Demurrer

Defendant DJO, LLC demurs to the entire FAC on the ground that it is barred by the applicable statute of limitations under CCP §335.1.

The Court sets forth the following chronology:

On December 6, 2023, plaintiff filed a complaint against DJO Global, Inc. for products liability. 

On January 19, 2024, “Defendant DJO, LLC (erroneously named and sued as DJO Global, Inc.) (‘Defendant’) for itself only and on behalf of no other entity” filed an answer to the complaint.

On March 14, 2024, plaintiff filed an FAC against “DJO Global, Inc.,” which is listed in the caption and as defendant at paras. 1, and 5.b.  Under the second cause of action for products liability, it states against “defendant DJO LLC only,” and alleges that DJO Global, Inc., owner and parent of the Chatanooga M2 manufacturer serial number 22565 “subject heating pad,” and Does 1 through 50 were engaged in the business of manufacturing, designing, assembling, compounding, testing, inspecting, packaging, labeling, fabricating, constructing, analyzing, distributing, servicing, merchandising, recommending, advertising, promoting, marketing, and selling the subject electric heating pad and its component parts and constituents, for resale to and use by chiropractors and other medical services providers including defendants LAX Physical Therapy and Matthew Araghi.  Prior to the time that the subject electric heating pad was used by plaintiff, defendants DJO Global and Does 1 through 50 made implied warranties to plaintiff and the general public that said subject electric heating pad was of merchantable quality and safe and fit for the use for which it was intended.  The subject heating pad is defectively designed, manufactured, in that it heated to such a high temperature that it caused significant burns to plaintiff’s skin when it was used in a reasonably foreseeable manner.  The subject heating pad was dangerous and failed to carry sufficient warnings.  Plaintiff alleges that she suffered a burn to her leg on September 5, 2023.

On November 7, 2025, DJO Global, Inc. filed an answer to the FAC on behalf of itself and no other entity.

On December 17, 2025, DJO Global Inc. filed a motion for summary judgment.

On February 2, 2026, plaintiff filed an amendment designating “DJO, LLC” as Doe 1, but never served the operative complaint on DJO, LLC.

On April 8, 2026, defendant DJO Global, Inc. filed a motion for terminating sanctions.

On April 24, 2026, at the FSC, pursuant to an oral request made by plaintiff, DJO, LLC was dismissed without prejudice.

On April 30, 2026, at the hearing on the motion for sanctions, pursuant to an oral request made by plaintiff, DJO Global, Inc. was dismissed without prejudice.

On May 4, 2026, the Court denied plaintiff’s ex parte application to vacate “inadvertent and mistaken dismissal” of DJO, LLC.

On May 8, 2026, plaintiff filed an amendment to complaint, replacing the incorrect name of DJO Global, Inc. with the true name of DJO, LLC.

Defendant DJO, LLC argues that the May 8, 2026 amendment is time barred.  As stated above, defendant DJO, LLC appeared in this matter on January 19, 2024 when it filed an answer.  Defendant argues that with the knowledge that DJO, LLC had appeared as a proper defendant, plaintiff nonetheless filed her FAC on March 14, 2024 neither naming DJO, LLC as a defendant nor serving it, and thus DJO, LLC was dismissed from the FAC.

Defendant further argues that the cause of action for products liability accrued on September 5, 2023 and is subject to a two-year statute of limitations under CCP §335.1.  Defendant further contends that the amendment is time barred because DJO Global, Inc. and DJO, LLC were previously dismissed after the statute of limitations had run.  Defendant asserts that plaintiff’s attempt to bring in DJO, LLC at this juncture is time barred because the applicable statute of limitations had run before plaintiff was in a position to renew the dismissed cause of action whether as DJO, LLC erroneously named as DJO Global, Inc., as DJO Global, Inc., or as DJO, LLC.  Also, defendant argues, DJO Global, Inc. was dismissed from the FAC on April 30, 2026 and is no longer an active defendant whose name can be corrected.  Moreover, DJO, LLC cannot be brought in under the pretext they are a “Doe” defendant so as to avoid the statute of limitations under CCP §474 because DJO, LLC was not unknown.  Further, defendant asserts, both former defendants are separate legal entities as evidenced by the suffix in their names.  Defendant also argues that plaintiff unreasonably delayed in filing an amendment and that defendant will be severely prejudiced.

In opposition, plaintiff argues that DJO, LLC is equitably and judicially estopped from asserting that it was never properly before the Court and that it cannot simultaneously participate in litigation as a defendant—producing witnesses, conducting an inspection, and exchanging discovery—and then disclaim defendant status.  Plaintiff asserts that DJO (in the form of DJO Global, Inc.), as the manufacturer named in the FAC, appeared in and litigated this action and cannot now assert a statute of limitations defense.  Plaintiff states that the second cause of action in the FAC was explicitly “against defendant DJO LLC only” even as the caption of the FAC used the “Global, Inc.” moniker.  Plaintiff notes that DJO’s own PMK presented specifically as the authorized representative of the company to testify on categories, including the design and temperature settings of the M-2, and identified his employer as “DJO, LLC.”  Plaintiff argues that the amendment properly corrects a misnomer, and is not a substitution of parties, by correcting the legal suffix attached to the one entity plaintiff always intended to sue.  Also, plaintiff argues, DJO’s extensive participation in discovery precludes a finding of prejudice.

The Court rules as follows:  The Court finds that the amendment filed on May 8, 2026 (as well as the February 2, 2026 amendment) is barred by the two-year statute of limitations and does not relate back to the filing of the complaint or the FAC under CCP §474.  Plaintiff was not genuinely ignorant of the identity of DJO, LLC; plaintiff was aware of  DJO, LLC being the proper entity by at least January 19, 2024.  Although DJO, LLC became a party to the action when it filed an answer, it was effectively dismissed without prejudice when plaintiff filed a first amended complaint and did not name DJO, LLC as a defendant.  See Troche v. Daley (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 403, 412.  While plaintiff was free to again name DJO, LLC as a defendant, she was required to do so before the statute of limitations ran in September 2025.  She did not.  Instead, she sought to add DJO, LLC as a defendant in February 2026 (then again requested dismissal of DJO, LLC without prejudice) and again in May 2026.  Both attempts were beyond expiration of the statute of limitations.  Because plaintiff was not ignorant of the true name of the proper defendant, DJO, LLC need not show prejudice from the delay in adding it as a party.  See Woo v. Superior Court (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 169, 177 (“[I]f the identity ignorance requirement of section 474 is not met, a new defendant may not be added after the statute of limitations has expired even if the new defendant cannot establish prejudice resulting from the delay.”). 

Moreover, the May 8, 2026 amendment is not allowed under CCP §473 as it is a substitution of parties rather than a misnomer correction.  DJO, LLC and DJO Global, Inc. are two different entities.  DJO, LLC is not the same as DJO Global, Inc. where plaintiff mischaracterized “Inc.” for “LLC.”  As such, the amendment is not allowed and does not relate back.  Even if viewed as a misnomer, plaintiff seeks to substitute DJO, LLC in place of a dismissed party.  When the Court dismissed DJO Global, Inc. at plaintiff’s request, it was as if DJO Global, Inc. was no longer named in the FAC.  There is thus no named DJO entity to correct in the FAC.  DJO, LLC cannot take the place of a party that no longer exists in the action.

 Finally, plaintiff argues that DJO Global, Inc. submitted to the Court’s jurisdiction when it filed an answer to the FAC on its own behalf and should be estopped from asserting that it was never properly before the Court.  First, DJO Global, Inc. is not asserting anything at this point.  The demurrer and motion to strike were filed by DJO, LLC.  Second, it is undisputed that, at different points in time, DJO, LLC and DJO Global, Inc., were properly before this Court in that they had submitted to the Court’s jurisdiction by filing answers.  Plaintiff, however, dismissed DJO, LLC when she filed her FAC and did not name DJO, LLC as a defendant.  Plaintiff twice sought to correct this oversight, but only after the statute of limitations had passed.

The demurrer is thus SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

            Motion to Strike

            Defendant requests that the Court strike the FAC as to DJO, LLC as moot because plaintiff’s amendment designating DJO Global, Inc., a dismissed defendant in the underlying action, to be the incorrect name of defendant DJO, LLC is irrelevant as both parties were previously dismissed and the statute of limitations has run.  In the alternative, DJO, LLC requests that the Court strike the “false allegations” in the amendment that DJO Global, Inc. was a misnomer for DJO, LLC.

            Defendant argues that plaintiff has known since at least January 19, 2024 that DJO, LLC and DJO Global, Inc. are separate legal entities, when DJO, LLC answered as DJO, LLC (erroneously named and sued as DJO, Global, Inc.).

            In opposition, plaintiff argues that the motion to strike is an improper vehicle for defendant’s statute of limitations argument.  Further, plaintiff contends, “DJO, LLC” was the intended and proper defendant although plaintiff using “DJO Global, Inc.” as the manufacturer was not “false.”  Plaintiff also asserts that the prior dismissals were without prejudice.

            See the Court’s ruling above under demurrer.  The Court finds that the May 8, 2026 amendment is improper and thus it is STRICKEN.  The motion is GRANTED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

ORDER

The Court rules in the alternative. 

The demurrer to the FAC is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.      

            The motion to strike is GRANTED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.  The May 8, 2026 amendment is STRICKEN.

Moving defendant is ordered to give notice of ruling.

Motion to Strike Punitive Damages

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

            The motion to strike is DENIED.

            Defendant is ordered to file an answer within twenty days.

BACKGROUND

            On August 4, 2025, Jose Luis Merino and Andres Merino Alvarado filed a complaint against Kenneth ONeil for motor vehicle negligence, general negligence, and intentional tort based on an incident that occurred on March 16, 2024 at S. Pacific Coast Highway and Prospect Avenue, Redondo Beach.

            On April 16, 2026, the Court granted with leave to amend defendant’s motion to strike punitive damages.

            On May 6, 2026, plaintiffs filed a FAC for (1) motor vehicle negligence, (2) general negligence and (3) intentional tort.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

“The court may, upon a motion . . . , or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:  (a) Strike any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.  (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”  CCP §436(b).

CCP §431.10 states:  “(a) A material allegation in a pleading is one essential to the claim or defense and which could not be stricken from the pleading without leaving it insufficient as to that claim or defense. 

(b) An immaterial allegation in a pleading is any of the following:  (1) An allegation that is not essential to the statement of a claim or defense.  (2) An allegation that is neither pertinent to nor supported by an otherwise sufficient claim or defense.  (3) A demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint or cross-complaint. 

(c) An ‘immaterial allegation’ means ‘irrelevant matter’ as that term is used in Section 436.”

The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice.  CCP §437.

DISCUSSION

            Defendant Kenneth O’Neil requests that the Court strike the FAC at page 3, section 10 “exemplary damages,” page 3, section 14 “punitive damages,” and page 10 exemplary damages attachment.

Under the causes of action for general negligence and intentional tort, the FAC alleges that on March 16, 2024 at S. Pacific Coast Hwy and Prospect Ave., Redondo Beach, defendant was driving an Audi Q7 on a public roadway and encountered plaintiffs that were operating minibikes/ATVs on the same public roadway.  Defendant made several attempts to strike other individuals but ultimately only struck plaintiffs.  Defendant’s actions were grossly negligent and extremely reckless with no regard to the possibility of loss of human life.  On information and belief, plaintiffs assert that defendant was maliciously attempting to run them down due to defendant’s frustration with plaintiffs being on the roadway.  Defendant made several passes nearly hitting other individuals, executed a U-turn and aimed his car directly at plaintiffs before eventually hitting them.  Defendant’s actions were not only extremely negligent and reckless they were intentional with the express purpose being to harm plaintiffs. 

            Defendant argues that the allegations are conclusory and insufficient to support an award for punitive damages and that there are no specific facts alleged to demonstrate malice.  Defendant contends that there are no allegations detailing specific facts, such as the speed and manner in which defendant was driving, that defendant engaged in outrageously hazardous driving or allegations of threats, verbal statements of intent, prior disputes with plaintiffs, or any other aggravating facts demonstrating intentional conduct.

            In opposition, plaintiffs argue that the allegations are adequate.  They assert that defendant “tried to run Plaintiffs down and kill them.”

            In reply, defendant reiterates that the allegations are conclusory and merely describe plaintiffs’ characterization of defendant’s driving and that they fail to rise to the level of being “despicable.”

            The Court rules as follows: 

            Civil Code §3294 authorizes the recovery of punitive damages in non-contract cases where “the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice . . . .”  To state a claim for punitive damages under Civil Code §3294, a plaintiff must allege specific facts showing that the defendant has been guilty of malice, oppression or fraud.  Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal. App. 4th 1033, 1042.  The basis for punitive damages must be pled with specificity; conclusory allegations devoid of any factual assertions are insufficient.  Id.  A motion to strike may lie where the facts alleged, if proven, would not support a finding that the defendant acted with malice, fraud or oppression.  Turman v. Turning Point of Central California (2010) 191 Cal. App. 4th 53, 63.

The Court in Taylor v. Superior Court (1979) 24 Cal.3d 890, 894-95, found that “[s]omething more than the mere commission of a tort is always required for punitive damages.  There must be circumstances of aggravation or outrage, such as spite or ‘malice,’ or a fraudulent or evil motive on the part of the defendant, or such a conscious and deliberate disregard of the interests of others that his conduct may be called willful or wanton.”

“’Malice’ means conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.”  Civil Code §3294(c)(1).  

As the Court noted in College Hospital v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 713, Section 3294 was amended in 1987 to require that, where malice is based on a defendant’s conscious disregard of a plaintiff’s rights, the conduct must be both despicable and willful.  The Court in College Hospital held further that “despicable conduct refers to circumstances that are base, vile, or contemptible.”  Id. at 725 (citation omitted).

The Court finds that the allegations are sufficient to show malice as plaintiffs allege that defendant intended to injure them with his vehicle as he made several passes nearly hitting other individuals, executed a U-turn and aimed his car directly at plaintiffs.

            The motion is DENIED.

ORDER

The motion to strike is DENIED.

Defendant is ordered to file an answer within twenty days.

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice of ruling.
