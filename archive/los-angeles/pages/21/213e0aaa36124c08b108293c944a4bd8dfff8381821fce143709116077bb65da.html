DEPARTMENT 735 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22STCV18163    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 735

Anabi Oil Corporation v. Envision WC Toy RE, LLC, et al.

 

Cross-Defendant Envision WC Toy RE, LLC’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production (Set Two) from Cross-Complainants Huy Quoc Dang dba West Covina Shell, West Covina Shell Auto Care, Inc., and D.S. Dang LLC, and Requests for Sanctions

 

Cross-Defendant Envision WC Toy RE, LLC moves to compel further responses to RPDs, set two, nos. 31-120 from Cross-Complainants Huy Quoc Dang dba West Covina Shell, West Covina Shell Auto Care, Inc., and D.S. Dang LLC. The parties appeared for argument on June 16, 2026. The Court consolidated the motions related to each Cross-Complainant and continued the motions to this date for hearing. The Court ordered the parties to meet and confer telephonically and for Cross-Defendant to file a supplemental declaration identifying remaining disputed issues. The motions now come on for hearing a second time.

DISCUSSION

Threshold Matters

As a threshold matter: most or all of Cross-Complainants’ responses are preceded by a host of objections, then responded to subject to those objections. Cross-Complainants did not specify documents withheld subject to objection as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.240 [partial response must “[i]dentify with particularity any document ... to which an objection is made”].) The responses are therefore not Code-compliant, whether or not documents have been withheld.

The Court sustains or overrules the objections and grants the motion on this basis in many cases, recognizing that once the objections have been overruled, responsive documents still may not exist, etc.. After the Court’s ruling on Cross-Complainants’ objections, Cross-Complainants must produce any documents withheld subject to an overruled objection. If no documents have been withheld, Cross-Complainants must confirm as much via supplemental response, since their initial responses were not Code-compliant.

Cross-Complainants also purport to reserve their right to supplement their responses at a later date because “discovery is ongoing”. Cross-Complainants cite no provision of the Civil Discovery Act permitting this reservation of rights, and the purported reservation of rights does not relieve Cross-Complainants of their obligation to conduct a diligent search and fully respond. Documents that were not produced in initial responses may be subject to exclusion at trial or similar sanction, notwithstanding Cross-Complainants’ “reservation of rights”.

Third: Cross-Defendant repeatedly employed the vague term “DOCUMENTS RELATING TO” or “DOCUMENTS RELATED TO” in each of its requests. In this sense, Cross-Complainants’ vagueness objection is well-taken even where it is overruled. Where the Court overrules vagueness objections and grants the motion, in every instance the Court construes the term “DOCUMENTS” to mean “communications and/or agreements”.

Finally: notwithstanding the rulings below, no objection based on attorney-client privilege or attorney work product has been overruled. If any documents have been withheld on this basis, however, Cross-Complainants are ordered either (1) to provide a privilege log or (2) state via supplemental response that production of a privilege log would impose an undue burden.

Requests in Dispute

Subject to the foregoing, the Court rules as follows on the disputed Requests and various objections.

Ruling

RFP Nos.

Objections OVERRULED and motion GRANTED.

31, 42-44, 50-52, 53 (as narrowed), 54-60, 66-68, 70, 73, 77-80, 83-93, 96-98, 101-119 (with “DOCUMENTS” construed to include records of payments).

Vagueness/overbreadth objection SUSTAINED and motion DENIED.

36-41, 45-49, 61, 62-65, 69, 71-72, 74-76, 81-82, 94-95, 99-100, 120.

SANCTIONS

            The Court finds Cross-Complainants were substantially justified to object to Cross-Defendant’s requests. The numerous requests were in many cases vague or overbroad and extended over long periods of time. The Court disapproves of Cross-Complainants’ apparent failure to participate meaningfully in meeting-and-conference efforts. That does not change the result of the motion, which was only partly successful and required the Court to narrow the definition of the overbroad term “DOCUMENTS RELATED TO” throughout.

            Cross-Defendant’s requests for sanctions are denied.

CONCLUSION

            The motion is granted in part and denied in part on the foregoing terms, subject to the qualifications set forth at the outset of this ruling.

Cross-Defendant’s requests for sanctions are denied.

Cross-Defendant is ordered to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV30038    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 735

Mardirossian Akaragian LLP v. KDA Construction Inc., et al.

Special Motion to Strike (Anti-SLAPP)

 

BACKGROUND

Plaintiff Mardirossian Akaragian LLP (the “Law Firm”) filed this action on October 14, 2025, asserting one cause of action for defamation against KDA Construction Inc. (“KDA”), Dead Presidents, LLC (“DP”).  The Law Firm’s complaint alleges that KDA hired DP, a professional protesting company, to protest and shame the Law Firm even though the Law Firm had no relationship with KDA. KDA and DP move under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 to strike the Complaint. The court grants Plaintiff’s request for judicial notice of various court records but not for the truth of hearsay matters asserted within those records.  (Request for Judicial Notice, filed July 16, 2026, pp. 2:2-4:18.)

DISCUSSION

KDA’s Lack of Standing

KDA lacks standing to bring the instant motion because it was defaulted on December 8, 2025, and the default has not been set aside. Therefore, the Court deems this motion as being brought only by DP.

SLAPP’s Prong One: Protected Activity

The parties dispute whether the allegedly defamatory statements were made in connection with a public issue or an issue of public interest within the meaning of the anti-SLAPP statute for which those statements contributed to or otherwise advanced that public discussion. DP argues that its statements that the Law Firm was unethical and untrustworthy were made in connection with a public issue or constituted an issue of public interest because (1) the Law Firm is a prominent California law firm that may impact the lives of many individuals, and (2) attorneys and law firms are required by law to be ethical.  In opposition, the Law Firm argues that the statements concerned a dispute that was private, not a matter of public interest or significance. The Law Firm correctly observes, “The topic of the speech at issue is not about the legal profession in general or lawyers, but rather is about a specific law firm … supposedly being unethical because it refuses to pay funds claimed owed by a builder.” Because DP’s statements do not contribute to a public discussion of a matter of public interest, DP does not satisfy the first prong; its motion to strike fails for that reason alone.

SLAPP’s Prong Two: Probability of Prevailing

However, if one assumes DP did in fact satisfy the first prong by contributing to a public discussion of a matter of public interest, the burden shifts to the Law Firm to establish the Law Firm’s probability of prevailing on the merits.  To establish the likelihood of prevailing, the Law Firm must demonstrate that its complaint is both legally sufficient and supported by a sufficient prima facie showing of facts to sustain a favorable judgment if the evidence the Law Firm submits is credited. The court shall consider the pleadings and supporting and opposing affidavits stating the facts upon which the liability or defense is based. The court does not, however, weigh the defendants’ evidence against the Law Firm, in terms of either credibility or persuasiveness.  Rather, the defendants’ evidence is considered with a view toward whether it defeats the Law Firm’s evidence as a matter of law, such as by establishing a defense or the absence of a necessary element.

The court finds that the Law Firm has met its burden of establishing the likelihood of prevailing on its defamation claim.  Defamation involves (a) a publication that is (b) false, (c) defamatory, and (d) unprivileged, and that (e) has a natural tendency to injure or that causes special damage.  Here, the Law Firm’s witness, Garo Mardirossian, attests to the following facts in support of the motion.  He founded the Law Firm more than 40 years ago and the firm specializes in various legal areas including catastrophic personal injury cases.  (Mardirossian Decl., ¶ 2.)  “Separate from, and wholly unrelated to [his] legal practice, [he is] also the manager of a real estate investment company called Ararat Holdings LLC (‘Ararat’),” which is a title owner of three properties in Pacific Palisades.  (Id. ¶ 3.)  In his sole capacity as Ararat’s manager, Mardirossian retained KDA to undertake certain construction services in the three properties owned by Ararat.  (Id. ¶ 4.)  Subsequently, after Mardirossian informed KDA’s counsel that KDA was barred from seeking compensation for its services because it was unlicensed, Mardirossian observed DP on social media, protesters standing in front of the Law Firm’s premises, and posters displaying signs implying that the firm owed KDA $106,482.77.  (Id. ¶¶ 8-15.)  However, the Law Firm has never had any dealings with or hired KDA to provide any goods or services or ever been retained by KDA.  (Id. ¶ 5.)

DP has not offered any evidence disproving the Law Firm’s defamation claim. Therefore, the court finds that the Law Firm has met its burden under the second prong.

CONCLUSION 

The court denies the defendants’ special motion to strike.

Defendants shall give notice.
