Case Number: 23STCV11134    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

Hearing Date:  August 7, 2026

Lucy Broadbent et al. v. 3585 Multiview, LLC, et al., Case No. 23STCV11134

Ruling:            Plaintiffs’ Motion for Order Appointing Receiver is denied without prejudice. 

 

Ruling on Defendants’ Evidentiary Objections: Objections to Collins Decl. filed with Motion: sustained as to paras. 3, 4, 5, 6 (except for Exh. 2), 8; otherwise overruled;

Objections to Feffer Declarations filed with Motion and with Reply: overruled; goes to weight.

Objections to Collins Decl. filed with Reply: Sustained.

Plaintiffs are requesting appointment of a receiver pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 564(b)(9), which provides:  “A receiver may be appointed by the court in which an action or proceeding is pending, or by a judge of that court, in the following cases: . . . (9) In all other cases where necessary to preserve the property or rights of any party.” 

Plaintiffs contend that Defendants have failed to comply with requirements of the City of Los Angeles with regard to construction on their property, including permits, erosion remediation, and structural issues, and that their failure to comply has resulted in mudslides that have damaged their property.  The are concerned that continued failure to comply will cause further damage to their property from mudslides because of the upcoming Super El Nino weather pattern, which is often accompanied by heavy rains and flooding, which is predicted for winter 2026-2027.  Much of Plaintiffs’ evidence is based on hearsay.  Although they have presented evidence of two Orders to Comply, Defendant have presented evidence of compliance with the OTC to the City’s satisfaction. 

Defendants have also offered evidence that at least one of the mudslides was caused by erosion on property other than theirs.  They have also presented evidence that the City is not intending to pursue the criminal matter for which Plaintiff presented evidence of a referral to the City Attorney’s office.  

Appointment of a receiver is an extraordinary remedy; it causes great expense to one or both parties as well as disruption of a business operation or, as here, construction that may be occurring on a property. 

The Court can understand Plaintiffs’ concerns about protecting their properties from further mudslide damage in the upcoming expected heavy rains this winter.  While Defendants stated that they submitted erosion mediation plans to the City for winter 2026-2027, which were approved by the City, the evidence did not support that assertion.  The latest plans appear to have been for winter 2025-2026, and approval appears to have expired in April 2026. 

If evidence is presented that Defendants are not taking reasonable measures to assure downhill properties are protected from mudslides or erosion due to any instability of their property or soils on their property because of the construction on their property, including but not limited to appropriate approvals from the City for such measures, the Court may entertain future requests for equitable relief by Plaintiffs.


Case Number: 24STCV03642    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

Hearing Date:  August 7, 2026

Antonia Rmoero v. General Motors, LLC, Case No. 24STCV03642

Ruling:            Plaintiff Antonio Romero (“Plaintiff”)’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses (CRS # 407926234489) is granted as modified.

Plaintiff is ordered to submit a proposed order that adheres to the Court’s reductions of attorneys’ fees by August __, 2026.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Submission of Attorney Fees Order for ___________, 2026, at 8:30AM. 

 

Plaintiff Antonio Romero (“Plaintiff”)’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses is called for hearing.

Plaintiff’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses is heard and argued.

The motion includes costs and expenses pursuant to the settlement agreement by the parties.

Plaintiff has filed a reply that includes a request for judicial notice and a supplemental declaration in support of the motion. The general rule in regard to motions precludes consideration of new evidence submitted with a reply.  (Jay v. Mahaffey (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1522, 1537.) “This rule is based on the same solid logic applied in the appellate courts, specifically, that ‘[p]oints raised for the first time in a reply brief will ordinarily not be considered, because such consideration would deprive the respondent of an opportunity to counter the argument.’ ” (Id. at 1538 [citing American Drug Stores, Inc. v. Stroh (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1446, 1453; Browne v. County of Tehama (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 704, 720, fn. 10].) The Court does not consider the request for judicial notice and a supplemental declaration in support of the motion filed with the Reply.

I.                Attorneys’ Fees

A prevailing party in entitled to recover costs, including attorneys’ fees where there is a statutory or contractual basis. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1032(a)(4), 1032(b), 1033.5.)  In Song-Beverly claims, if the buyer of a consumer good who is damaged by a failure to comply with the Song-Beverly Act prevails in an action under this section, the buyer may recover as part of the judgment the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, which were reasonably incurred by the buyer in the action. (Civ. Code § 1794(d).)

The correct measure for attorneys’ fees is the market value of the services, and “[t]his standard applies regardless of whether the attorneys claiming fees charge nothing for their services, charge at below-market or discounted rates, represent the client on a straight contingent fee basis, or are in-house counsel.” (Nemecek & Cole v. Horn (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 641, 651.)  “Absent an arbitrary ruling, case law gives a trial judge great discretion in using their knowledge and experience to assess the value of services performed by attorneys in the case their court even without introduction of expert testimony.  (Marshall v. Webster (2020) 54 Cal.App.5th 275, 287 [internal citations omitted]; Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1132 [“The ‘ “experienced trial judge is the best judge of the value of professional services rendered in his court, and while his judgment is of course subject to review, it will not be disturbed unless the appellate court is convinced that it is clearly wrong.” ’ ”].)

Plaintiff requests a total award for attorney fees, costs, and expenses in the amount of: $65,743.81 [$46,441.00 (111.1 hours of billable time) + 1.3 multiplier ($5,370.51) + $2,400.00 (estimated time on this motion) + 1.3 multiplier for estimated time ($720.00) + $2,250.51 (costs and expenses)].

The Court makes the following changes:

·       A loadstar multiplier is not included as, although this case was taken on a contingency basis, it is neither novel nor complex for a Song-Beverly case.

·       Jennifer Romo, Clarence Serrano, Katelyn Gilkey, Walter Orellana, Oliver Thomas, Virgina Walls, and Caleth Ibarra are presented to be paralegals, with rates between $200.00 to $270.00 per hour (see Mtn. at Decl. Laura E. Goolsby at Exh. A, Opp. 7:5-12). The Court reduces the paralegal rates to $200.00 per hour for all of them.

·       The Court is aware of the extensive use of templates in Song-Beverly motor vehicle actions. Because of this, the following reductions are made:

o   The time billed for the Complaint is reduced from 2.0 hours to 1.5 hours; and

o   The Motion for Leave to Amend is reduced from 4.5 hours to 4 hours;

·       The time spent on this motion is presented as 3.8 hours in connection with the moving papers and an addition 4.0 hours in anticipated time for a total of 7.8 hours. The Court reduces the time spent on this motion to 5.8 hours.

·       Goolsby billed 1.7 hours to draft an “[e]mail in/out: re client dec iso opp to MSJ and next steps re same.” (Decl. Goolsby at Exh. A., p. 15.) The Court reduces this amount to 1.0 hour.

 

II.             Costs and Expenses

GM asks for a reduction of the following costs: filing fees in the amount of $151.47 for Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to Amend, $60.00 in courtesy copy fees, and $32.39 in connection with convenience fees for credit card payments.

Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5(c)(1) provides: “Costs are allowable if incurred, whether or not paid.” Here, the filing fees are an allowable cost. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 1033.5(a)(1).) The Court does not tax the filing fee amount of $151.47 for Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to Amend. The cost is reasonable as, only after such a motion, settlement was reached.

Regarding the $60.00 in courtesy copy fees and $32.39 in connection with convenience fees for credit card payments, the Court agrees with Plaintiff’s reasoning: the “courtesy copy” and nominal processing fees are routine litigation expenses incidental to motion practice, and GM cites no authority otherwise. Such costs “may be allowed or denied in the court's discretion.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1033.5(c)(4).) The amounts requested for these costs do not appear unreasonable.

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s requested costs and expenses are granted without reduction.

Plaintiff Antonio Romero (“Plaintiff”)’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses (CRS # 407926234489) is granted as modified.

Plaintiff is ordered to submit a proposed order that adheres to the Court’s reductions of attorneys’ fees by August __, 2026.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Submission of Attorney Fees Order for ___________, 2026, at 8:30AM. 

 


Case Number: 25STCV06110    Hearing Date: August 7, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

Hearing Date:  August 7, 2026

Melissa Ragan v. General Motors, LLC, Case No. 25STCV06110

Defendant’s  Motion to Transfer Venue

Ruling:            Defendant’s Motion to Transfer Venue to the County of El Dorado is granted.  The Court orders venue to be transferred upon payment by Defendant of all required transfer fees.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Venue Transfer and Payment of Transfer Fees for ___________, 2026.  All future hearing dates except for the October 19, 2026, hearing on Plaintiff’s counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel are vacated. If the transfer occurs before October 19, 2026, that hearing date will be deemed vacated as well.

 

Defendant filed Motion to Transfer Venue of this Song-Beverly Act (lemon law) case on April 23, 2026, seeking an order to transfer venue of this case to the Superior Court of California, County of El Dorado.  Plaintiff filed no opposition to the motion, and, according to Defendant’s counsel, Plaintiff’s counsel informed him during a meet and confer regarding the motion that he would not oppose the motion. 

The motion is brought under sections 396b and 397(c) of the Code of Civil Procedure on the grounds that Plaintiff lives in Oregon, and, as alleged by Plaintiff, the warranty repairs were performed in El Dorado County.  Plaintiff has presented no evidence since the case was filed on March 5, 2025, that any party or material witness is located in Los Angeles County, and, since all  repairs were performed in El Dorado County, the material witnesses reside there. 

Considering the evidence presented with the motion, the Court determines that the interests of justice and convenience of the witnesses and partes support transfer of the venue of this case to El Dorado County.
