DEPARTMENT P LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25TRCV03476    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: P

Demurrer to Plaintiff’s Complaint

Moving Party: Defendant Manuel Corrales, Jr. 
Responding Party: Plaintiff Rockpoint Funding, LLC

RULING

The court considered the moving papers, opposition, and reply. As Defendant’s demurrer is procedurally improper, the court declines to consider Defendant’s demurrer.
 

PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND

On October 10, 2025, Plaintiff Rockpoint Funding, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendant Manuel Corrales, Jr. (“Defendant”) and DOES 1 through 20, inclusive, alleging five causes of action for: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Conversion; (3) Fraud; (4) Common Counts; and (5) Declaratory Judgment.

On November 18, 2025, Defendant filed his first demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint. On February 18, 2026, the court overruled the demurrer as to Plaintiff’s first, second, fourth, and fifth causes of action and sustained the demurrer as to Plaintiff’s third cause of action for Fraud with leave to amend. 

On March 26, 2026, Defendant filed the instant demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint. On September 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On September 11, 2026, Defendant filed a reply. 

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant, in his ordinary course of business as an attorney, will request funding advances based on non-recourse purchase agreements. (Complaint, ¶ 10.) Plaintiff alleges that as a result, attorneys enter into written agreements establishing a lien in favor of Plaintiff against the funds recovered by attorneys in the specific client’s case. (Complaint, ¶ 10.) Plaintiff alleges that on May 22, 2024, Plaintiff entered into an agreement with Defendant entitled “Nonrecourse Purchase Agreement,” regarding a nonrecourse purchase for a litigated matter for Jane Doe. (Complaint, ¶ 20.) Next, Plaintiff alleges that on September 13, 2024, Plaintiff entered into a second agreement with Defendant entitled “Nonrecourse Purchase Agreement,” regarding a nonrecourse purchase for a litigated matter for Jane Doe. (Complaint, ¶ 22.) Plaintiff alleges that the underlying lawsuit was settled in August, 2025. (Complaint, ¶ 17.) Plaintiff alleges that Defendant has failed to pay the sums due, constituting a material breach of the agreement. (Complaint, ¶¶ 21, 23.)
LEGAL STANDARD

A party may demur to a complaint on the grounds that it “does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.” (Code Civ. Proc. Section 430.10(e).) A party may also demur to a pleading on the grounds that the pleading is uncertain. (Code Civ. Proc. Section 430.10(f).) A demurrer tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) A special demurrer for uncertainty under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10(f) is disfavored and will only be sustained where the pleading is so bad that defendant or plaintiff cannot reasonably respond—i.e., cannot reasonably determine what issues must be admitted or denied, or what counts or claims are directed against him or her.(Khoury v. Maly’s of Calif., Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.)

When considering demurrers, courts accept all well pleaded facts as true. (Fox v. JAMDAT Mobile, Inc. (2010) 185 Cal.App.4th 1068, 1078.) “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.” (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) “The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.” (Hahn, supra, 147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)On demurrer, a court does “not accept contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Simonyan v. Nationwide Insurance Company of America (2022) 78 Cal.App.5th 889, 895.)

Although courts construe pleadings liberally, sufficient facts must be alleged to support the allegations pled to survive a demurrer. (Rakestraw v. California Physicians' Serv. (2000) 81 Cal.App.4th 39, 43.) Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) The burden is on the party who filed the pleading subject to demurrer to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Ibid.)

DISCUSSION  

Moving Party’s Argument

Defendant attempts to demur to all five of the causes of action in Plaintiff’s complaint on the grounds that the alleged agreement constitutes an illegal fee-sharing agreement between a law office and a non-lawyer such that it is void and unenforceable, and because Defendant’s client did not give written consent to the alleged funding agreement. 

Opposing Party’s Argument

Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s instant demurrer is made on the same grounds as Defendant’s previous demurrer and argues that a motion for reconsideration is required in order to revisit issues previously ruled on by the court. Plaintiff acknowledges that it chose not to amend its causes of action for Fraud. 

Reply Argument

Defendant reiterates his arguments that the parties’ alleged funding agreement is an illegal fee-sharing agreement and is void and unenforceable because the client’s written consent was never obtained. Defendant argues that his instant demurrer is not an improper motion for reconsideration because the demurrer “addresses the illegality of the subject funding agreement which this Court did not rule upon in the first demurrer.” (Reply, p. 5:24-25.) 

Merits of the Motion

A. Meet and Confer Requirements 

Before filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the demurrer for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc. Section 430.41(a).)

Defendant declares that on November 6, 2025, Defendant emailed Plaintiff’s counsel a letter requesting to meet and confer telephonically. (Corrales Decl., ¶ 5.) Defendant declares that on November 12, 2025, Defendant and Plaintiff’s counsel met and conferred over the phone and the parties were unable to reach an agreement. (Corrales Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) 

Therefore, the court finds that the meet and confer requirements have been satisfied.

B. Procedural Issues

Code of Civil Procedure Section 1008 “essentially forbids trial courts from reconsidering orders previously rendered in the action—either their own or those made by other judges—‘unless made according to this section.’ A motion made in accordance with section 1008 must include reference to new or different facts, circumstances, or law before the earlier order may be reconsidered.” (Bennett v. Suncloud (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 91, 96; see Code of Civ. Proc. Section 1008.) 

In Bennett, the court stated that the court was “foreclosed from rendering a new determination on the viability of those claims” to which a demurrer had previously been overruled “unless some new facts or circumstances were brought to [the court’s] attention.”  (Bennett, supra, 56 Cal.App.4th at 96-97.)  

Here, Defendant filed his first demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint on November 18, 2025. The court sustained Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s third cause of action for Fraud with leave to amend and overruled Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s first, second, fourth, and fifth causes of action. Plaintiff has not since filed an amended complaint. In ruling on Defendant’s original demurrer, the court expressly acknowledged Defendant’s arguments that “the alleged agreement is an illegal fee-sharing agreement, and thus void” and that “Defendant is not a part of the alleged agreement or contract.” (Minute Order, dated February 18, 2026, p. 4.) In his moving papers, Defendant repeats identical arguments already addressed by the court, being that “the alleged agreement or contract is an illegal fee-sharing agreement…and thus void and unenforceable” and “the client of the attorney did not give written consent to the funding agreement.” (Dem. Notice, p. 1:22-28.) The court also notes that Defendant now attempts to demur to Plaintiff’s Fraud cause of action, to which the court previously sustained Defendant’s original demurrer. 

Therefore, the court finds that Defendant is improperly treating the instant demurrer as a motion for reconsideration, which must be made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure 1008.

As Defendant’s instant demurrer is procedurally improper, the court declines to consider it. Defendant is ordered to file and serve an answer within 30 days. 

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the court declines to consider Defendant’s demurrer. 

Defendant is ordered to file and serve an answer within 30 days.

Case Number: 26TRCV01474    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: P

Motion to Compel Arbitration and Stay Proceedings

Moving Party: Defendant Farmers Insurance Exchange
Responding Party: None

RULING

The court considered the moving papers. Defendant’s Motion to Compel Arbitration is GRANTED to the extent that issues remain unresolved. The court STAYS the proceedings pending conclusion of the arbitration.
 

PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND

On April 22, 2026, Plaintiff Precious Evans (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendant Farmers Insurance Exchange (“Defendant”), alleging a single cause of action for Motor Vehicle.

On June 1, 2026, Defendant filed a demurrer and motion to strike. On July 6, 2026, the court sustained the demurrer without leave to amend and granted Defendant’s motion to strike in its entirety without leave to amend. 

On June 1, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion. 

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Plaintiff alleges that on July 6, 2025, an incident occurred at the intersection of Alameda Street and Town Center Drive in Los Angeles, California. (Complaint, p. 4.) Plaintiff alleges that Defendant is liable as the “insurance carriers for Plaintiff’s uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage, and have not made offers to make Plaintiff whole.” (Complaint, p. 4.)

LEGAL STANDARD

Parties may be compelled to arbitrate a dispute upon the court finding that: (1) there was a valid agreement to arbitrate between the parties; and (2) said agreement covers the controversy or controversies in the parties’ dispute.(Code Civ. Proc. Section 1281.2.) California law favors enforcement of valid arbitration agreements. (Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services, Inc. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 83, 97.) A party petitioning to compel arbitration has the burden of establishing the existence of a valid agreement to arbitrate and the party opposing the petition has the burden of proving, by a preponderance of the evidence, any fact necessary to its defense. (Banner Entertainment, Inc. v. Superior Court(1998) 62 Cal.App.4th 348, 356-357.)

The Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”) applies to any contract evidencing a transaction involving interstate commerce which contains an arbitration clause. (Wolls v. Superior Court (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 197, 211.) Section 2 of the FAA provides that arbitration provisions shall be enforced, save upon grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract. (Ibid.) A state court may refuse to enforce an arbitration clause on the basis of generally applicable contract defenses, such as fraud, duress, or unconscionability. (Ibid.) A state court, however, may not defeat an arbitration clause by applying state laws applicable only to arbitration provisions. (Ibid.)

For the FAA to apply, a contract must involve interstate commerce. (Ibid.) When it applies, the FAA preempts any state law rule that stands as an obstacle to the accomplishment of the FAA’s objectives. (Carbajal v. CWPSC, Inc. (2016) 245 Cal.App.4th 227, 238.) A party asserting FAA preemption bears the burden to present evidence establishing a contract with the arbitration provision affects interstate commerce, and the failure to do so renders the FAA inapplicable. (Ibid.) Evidence must be presented, in the form of declarations or other evidence, that establishes that the contract affects interstate commerce. (Ibid.) “[S]ince arbitration is a matter of contract, the FAA also applies if it is so stated in the agreement.” (Davis v. Shiekh Shoes, LLC (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 956, 963.)

“On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party thereto refuses to arbitrate such controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists, unless it determines that: (a) the right to compel arbitration has been waived by the petitioner; or (b) grounds exist for the revocation of the agreement.” (Code Civ. Proc. Section 1281.2(a-b).) Thus, in assessing a motion to compel arbitration, the moving party “bears the burden of proving the existence of a valid arbitration agreement by a preponderance of the evidence, and a party opposing the [motion] bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence any fact necessary to its defense.” (Giuliano v. Inland Empire Personnel, Inc. (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 1276, 1284, citation omitted.)

“If a court of competent jurisdiction . . . has ordered arbitration of a controversy which is an issue involved in an action or proceeding pending before a court of this State, the court in which such action or proceeding is pending shall, upon motion of a party to such action or proceeding, stay the action or proceeding until an arbitration is had in accordance with the order to arbitrate or until such earlier time as the court specifies.” (Code Civ. Proc. Section 1281.4.)

DISCUSSION  

Moving Party’s Argument

Defendant argues that Plaintiff has been covered by an automobile insurance policy issued by Defendant that includes uninsured motorist coverage, and the policy contains a binding arbitration provision. Defendant argues that both California law and the parties’ insurance policy require that the instant dispute is resolved through arbitration. 

Merits of the Motion

At the outset, the court notes that it previously sustained Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint without leave to amend, and granted Defendant’s motion to strike in its entirety without leave to amend.

To the extent that issues remain unresolved, the court finds that arbitration is mandatory pursuant to Insurance Code Section 11580.2(f), which provides:

“The policy…shall provide that the determination as to whether the insured shall be legally entitled to recover damages, and if so entitled, the amount thereof, shall be made by agreement between the insured and the insurer or, in the event of disagreement, by arbitration.”

(Ins. Code Section 11580.2(f).)

Defendant correctly notes that Plaintiff alleges damages arising from a motor vehicle collision involving an uninsured driver. (Complaint, p. 4.) Additionally, Defendant’s counsel declares that at all relevant times, Plaintiff was covered under any automobile insurance policy issued by Defendant that includes uninsured motorist coverage, with the policy containing a binding arbitration provision (the “Auto Policy”). (Thomas Decl., ¶ 5; Exh. C.)

Section I of “Part II – Uninsured Motorist Coverage” of Plaintiff’s Auto Policy provides that “[i]f an insured person and we do not agree (1) that the person is legally entitled to recover damages from the owner or operator of an uninsured motor vehicle, or (2) as to the amount of payment under this part, either that person or we may demand that the issue be determined by arbitration.” (Thomas Decl., Exh. C, p. 13.) The court finds that in addition to the applicability of Insurance Code Section 11580.2(f) to this matter, Defendant has provided evidence of an arbitration agreement between the parties.

Therefore, pursuant to Insurance Code Section 11580.2(f) and the binding arbitration provision contained in Plaintiff’s Auto Policy, Defendant’s Motion to Compel Arbitration is GRANTED to the extent that issues remain unresolved. 

Code of Civil Procedure Section 1281.4 provides that if the court has ordered the arbitration of a controversy, it “shall, upon motion of a party to such action or proceeding, stay the action or proceeding until an arbitration is had in accordance with the order to arbitrate or until such earlier time as the court specifies.”

Therefore, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure Section 1281.4, the court STAYS this action pending conclusion of the arbitration proceedings.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s Motion to Compel Arbitration is GRANTED to the extent that issues remain unresolved. The court STAYS the proceedings pending conclusion of the arbitration. 

Moving party is ordered to give notice of ruling.
