TENTATIVE RULING

 

HEARING DATE:                 08/11/2026

CASE NUMBER:                   19STCV42431

CASE NAME:                        RICHARD HARRIS vs PARK LA BREA LLC, et al.

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Richard Harris

OPPOSING PARTY:             Specially appearing Defendant Universal Protection Service LP

PROCEEDING:                      Motion to Reopen case and vacate dismissal

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff Richard Harris’ Motion to Reopen and Vacate Dismissal is denied.

                                                                                                                                               

 

Background

 

On November 26, 2019, Plaintiff Richard Harris (“Plaintiff”) filed his Complaint against Defendants Park La Brea LLC and Allied Universal, alleging one cause of action for Premises Liability.

 

Plaintiff alleges that, on November 30, 2017 at Park La Brea’s premises, he was forcibly handcuffed by Allied Universal.

 

Allied Universal responded as Universal Protection Service, LP (“UPS”).

 

On January 31, 2024, UPS’ Motion for Terminating Sanctions was granted. The Court ordered UPS to file the Request for Dismissal within 10 court days.  On March 07, 2024, the Court dismissed UPS with prejudice.

 

On March 12, 2027, the Court amended the request for dismissal to reflect a file stamp date of March 07, 2024.

 

As of May 21, 2024, the case was dismissed without prejudice under Code of Civil Procedure section 581(b)(3) (dismissal by the court, without prejudice, when no party appears for trial following 30 days' notice of time and place of trial) as there were no appearances for either side nor any communication with the Court as to why there are no appearances on the date of the jury trial.

 

On May 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion to Reopen and Vacate Dismissal (the “Motion”).  On July 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed an amended Motion.

 

On July 29, 2026, UPS filed its Opposition.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff seeks to reopen this action and vacate the dismissal of UPS. Plaintiff brings this motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d). (See Mtn. at header.)  Section 437(d) provides as follows:

 

The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.

 

Section 437(d) does not authorize the Court to reopen and vacate the dismissal (1) against UPS after a dismissal with prejudice stemming from a Motion for Terminating Sanctions; and (2) of the case as a whole due to failure to prosecute.  

 

Plaintiff relies in part on Rule 11(b) and 11(c) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure to support the motion, but those provision are not binding on this Court. (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 239–240 [“As a general rule, California courts are not bound by the federal rules of procedure . . .”].)

 

The California authorities on which Plaintiff relies are not more availing. The Court has discussed Code Civ. Proc. § 473(d), above.  Section 581 of the Code of Civil Procedure, cited by Plaintiff, concerns dismissal of the action or complaint by parties or the courts.  Section 1008 of the Code of Civil Procedure authorizes a party to file a motion for reconsideration of a court ruling, but the motion must be filed no more than 10 days after written entry of the order.  Plaintiff’s motion was filed far beyond that deadline. 

No authority has been cited by Plaintiff for the Court to reopen this case and vacate UPS’ dismissal.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Richard Harris’ Motion to Reopen and Vacate Dismissal is denied.

CASE NAME:                        JEROME GARDNER, AN INDIVIDUAL vs SOCAL AMERICAN HOME CENTER,...

MOVING PARTY:                Plaintiff Jerome Gardner

OPPOSING PARTY:             There are two Defendants in this action – Socal American Home Center which ECourt says defaulted, but they have filed a cross-complaint; and Jennier Goodie. No party filed an Opposition.

PROCEEDING:                      Motion for reconsideration

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff Jerome Gardner’s Motion for Reconsideration is denied.

                                                                                                                                               

Background

 

On May 8, 2024, plaintiff Jerome Gardner (“Plaintiff”) filed a Complaint against defendants SoCal Home Center, Inc. (“SHC”) and Jennifer Goodie (“Goodie”), alleging two causes of action for (1) Breach of Contract against SHC and Goodie; and (2) Mechanic’s Lien against Goodie.

 

Plaintiff alleged that on or about April 3, 2023, he entered into a part written, part oral agreement with Defendants to furnish labor, services, equipment, and materials for a work of improvement to the real property known as 4055 Brighton Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90047 (the “Property”) for a contract price of $55,000.00 plus additional sums to be determined; Plaintiff has performed all terms and conditions to be performed on his part under the contract; and Defendants failed to pay and refuse to pay Plaintiff.

 

On August 12, 2024, SHC filed a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, and Eco Construction Management, Inc. dba Jerome’s OCL Home Improvement, Inc. (“Eco Construction”), alleging three causes of action for (1) Misappropriation of Trade Name and Likeness; (2) Intentional Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage; and (3) Unfair Competition (Bus. & Proc. Code § 17200).

 

Plaintiff filed a Fourth Amended Complaint (“4AC”) on November 10, 2025, alleging two causes of action for (1) Quantum Meruit against SoCal and Goodie and (2) Mechanic’s Lien Foreclosure against Goodie.

 

The Court held a hearing on Goodie’s Demurrer to on May 7, 2026. The Court found that the failure of Plaintiff to include pertinent previous allegations triggered the sham pleading doctrine. The Court sustained Goodie’s Demurrer without leave to amend and ordered Goodie dismissed from this action.

 

On May 13, 2026, judgment in the action was entered as to Goodie. Goodie was dismissed from the action with prejudice.  

 

On May 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion for Reconsideration.  No Opposition has been filed.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Section 1008(a) of the Code of Civil Procedure provides:¿¿¿¿

 

When an application for an order has been made to a judge, or to a court, and refused in whole or in part, or granted, or granted conditionally, or on terms, any party affected by the order may, within 10 days after service upon the party of written notice of entry of the order and based upon new or different facts, circumstances, or law, make application to the same judge or court that made the order, to reconsider the matter and modify, amend, or revoke the prior order.¿ The party making the application shall state by affidavit what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order or decisions were made, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown.¿¿

¿¿

A court acts in excess of jurisdiction when it grants a motion to reconsider that is not based upon “new or different facts, circumstances or law.” (Gilberd v. AC Transit (1995) 32 Cal.App.4th 1494, 1499.)¿Motions for reconsideration are restricted to circumstances where a party offers the Court some fact or circumstance not previously considered, and some valid reason for not offering it earlier.¿ (Id.)¿¿¿¿¿¿

 

Moreover, there is a strict requirement of diligence, which means that the moving party must present a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence or different facts earlier.¿ (Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 690.)¿ The burden under section 1008 is comparable to that of a party seeking a new trial on the ground of newly discovered evidence: the information must be such that the moving party could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered or produced it at trial.¿ (New York Times Co. v. Superior Court (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 206, 212-213; Mink v. Superior Court (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 1338 [“ ‘[T]he party seeking reconsideration must provide not only new evidence but also a satisfactory explanation for the failure to produce that evidence at an earlier time.’ ”]; Glade v. Glade (1995) 38 Cal.App.4th 1441 [“The party seeking reconsideration must provide not just new evidence or different facts, but a satisfactory explanation for the failure to produce it at an earlier time.”].)¿¿¿¿

 

New circumstances can be shown by evidence that the court failed to consider a timely-filed memorandum of points and authorities in its prior ruling.¿ (Johnston v. Corrigan (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 553, 556.)¿¿¿¿¿¿

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff asks the Court (1) to reconsider its previous ruling on Goodie’s Demurrer; (2) to grant Plaintiff leave to file a Fifth Amended Complaint (“5AC”); (3) to vacate or modify Goodie’s dismissal; and (4) to grant such other relief as the Court deems just and proper.

 

Plaintiff bases his motion on his proposed 5AC, attached to the motion as Exhibit A. Plaintiff argues that the proposed 5AC materially differs from prior pleadings, cures the defects in the previous pleadings, does not trigger the sham pleading doctrine, and the interests of justice requires filing of the 5AC.

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff asks the Court (1) to reconsider its previous ruling on Goodie’s Demurrer; (2) to grant Plaintiff leave to file a Fifth Amended Complaint (“5AC”); (3) to vacate or modify Goodie’s dismissal; and (4) to grant such other relief as the Court deems just and proper.

 

Plaintiff bases his motion on his proposed 5AC, attached to the motion as Exhibit A. Plaintiff argues that the proposed 5AC materially differs from prior pleadings, cures the defects in the previous pleadings, does not trigger the sham pleading doctrine, and the interests of justice requires filing of the 5AC.

 

Plaintiff does not base his motion for reconsideration on new or different facts, circumstances, or law as required by section 1008. Rather, Plaintiff asks the Court to apply the demurrer standard to his proposed 5AC through this motion and allow the 5AC to be filed. This is not a proper basis for a motion for reconsideration.  Plaintiff has failed to present any grounds under section 1008 of the Code of Civil Procedure to reconsider the Court’s prior ruling. 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Jerome Gardner’s Motion for Reconsideration is denied.
