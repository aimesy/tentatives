TENTATIVE RULING

 

GET AFTER IT MEDIA, INC.

 

         vs.

 

MEDIA CULTURE, et al.

 

 Case No.: 25STCV24354

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 28, 2026

 

 

Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant Christena Garduno to provide responses to its Form Interrogatories (Set One) is GRANTED. Defendant is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $700.00.  Responses and payment is due within 15 days of entry of this order.

 

Plaintiff’s motion to have its Requests for Admission deemed admitted as to Defendant Christena Garduno is GRANTED. Defendant is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $700.00.  Payment is due within 15 days of entry of this order.

 

On 8/19/2025, Plaintiff Get After It Media, Inc. (Plaintiff) filed suit against Media Culture, Christena Garduno, and Rachel Ford, alleging: (1) fraud; (2) negligent misrepresentation; (3) breach of contract; and (4) common count.

 

On 5/28/2026, Plaintiff moved to have its Requests for Admission (RFAs) deemed admitted as to Defendant Christena Garduno (Defendant).

 

            On 6/1/2026, Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant Garduno to provide responses to its Form Interrogatories (Set One).

 

            The motions are unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

            On 4/16/2026, Plaintiff served Defendant with Form Interrogatories (Set One) and RFAs. To date, no responses have been received and Defendant did not oppose these motions.

 

 

 

            The Court takes Defendant’s non-opposition to be a concession to the motions on the merits.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant to provide responses to its Form Interrogatories (Set One) is granted. Defendant is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $700.00 ($350/hr x 2 hr). Responses and payment is due within 15 days of entry of this order.

 

Plaintiff’s motion to have its Requests for Admission deemed admitted as to Defendant Christena Garduno is GRANTED. Defendant is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $700.00.  Payment is due within 15 days of entry of this order.

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV26351    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

LILIA HANSBROUGH

                          

         vs.

 

WELLS FARGO NA

                                         

 Case No.: 25STCV26351

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 28, 2026

 

Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED, WITH 15 DAYS LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

            On 9/9/2025, in propria person plaintiff Lilia Hansbrough (Plaintiff) filed suit against Wells Fargo NA (Defendant). On 3/17/2026, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC) alleging: (1) breach of implied contract; and (2) promissory estoppel.[1]

 

            On 5/22/2026, Defendant demurred to Plaintiff’s first and second causes of action.

 

Factual Background

 

On 12/6/2024, Plaintiff executed a California General Durable Power of Attorney (POA) form appointing herself as her attorney-in-fact. (See FAC, Exh. A.) In July 2025, Plaintiff applied for a $25,000 personal loan from Wells Fargo using the POA. (FAC ¶ 6.) Plaintiff was told Wells Fargo’s underwriters were reviewing her application. (Complaint ¶ 8.) Wells Fargo denied Plaintiff’s application for a loan. (See FAC, Exh. C.) Wells Fargo advised Plaintiff that the Bank’s “policy does not allow a customer to be listed as their own power of attorney …. [to obtain] a loan without completing the application process.” (Id.) The Bank invited her to re-apply for a personal loan. (Id.)

 

Discussion

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff has not alleged sufficient facts to support her claims.

 

            After review, the Court agrees.

 

As for the first cause of action for breach of implied contract, the California Civil Code defines an implied contract as one in which “the existence and terms of which are manifested by conduct.” (Cal. Civ. Code § 1621.) California courts take this a step farther to set out that an implied contract is of “no less degree than an express contract, must be founded upon an ascertained agreement of the parties to perform it.” (Silva v. Providence Hospital of Oakland (1939) 14 Cal.2d 762, 773.) Thus, courts view a true implied contract as existing of “obligations arising from a mutual agreement and intent to promise where the agreement and promise have not been expressed in words.” (Dunham, Carrigan & Hayden Co. v. Rubber Co. (1927) 84 Cal.App. 669; Williston on Contracts, vol. 1, sec. 3.)

 

Here, Plaintiff alleges that she attempted to obtain a loan in her name from Wells Fargo by using a POA form in which she appointed herself as her attorney-in-fact. It is undisputed that Wells Fargo refused to accept her POA and explained that its policy “does not allow a customer to be listed as their own power of attorney” and invited her to reapply for a loan without the power of attorney. (See FAC, Ex. C.) But rather than re-apply in her individual name, Plaintiff sued. She alleges that as a result of being unable to obtain the loan, she suffered a financial loss and loss of opportunities. (See FAC, ¶ 11.)

 

As such, by Plaintiff’s own allegations, Defendant did not enter into a contract with her, but rather reviewed and denied her application because she applied as her own attorney-in-fact, which is not allowed by the Bank.

 

As for the second cause of action, even accepting all allegations as true, the FAC does not allege a specific, enforceable promise that Wells Fargo made that Plaintiff relied on to her detriment. Wells Fargo agreed to review her application for a personal loan. It did. It denied the application because she attempted to obtain the loan as her own attorney-in-fact, which the Bank does not allow. That was explained to her and she was invited to apply again, but did not.

 

Leave to amend will be afforded to Plaintiff to allege facts which could resolve these deficiencies and state a claim.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer is sustained with 15 days leave to amend.

 

It is so ordered.
