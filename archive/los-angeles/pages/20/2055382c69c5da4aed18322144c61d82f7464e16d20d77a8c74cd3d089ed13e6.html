ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On October 6, 2025, Plaintiff Israel Deras filed a verified complaint for partition of 3016 Isabel Drive, Los Angeles, CA 90065. Defendant Maria Deras in pro per answered the complaint on November 5, 2025.

 

II.              Legal Standard & Discussion

 

The pleadings frame the issues for motions, “since it is those allegations to which the motion must respond. (Citation.)” (Scolinos v. Kolts (1995) 37 Cal. App. 4th 635, 640-641; FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367, 382-383; Jordan-Lyon Prods., LTD. v. Cineplex Odeon Corp. (1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 1459, 1472.) The purpose of a motion for summary judgment or summary adjudication “is to provide courts with a mechanism to cut through the parties’ pleadings in order to determine whether, despite their allegations, trial is in fact necessary to resolve their dispute.” (Aguilar v. Atl. Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843.)  “Code of Civil Procedure section 437c, subdivision (c), requires the trial judge to grant summary judgment if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”  (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1110, 1119.)

 

“On a motion for summary judgment, the initial burden is always on the moving party to make a prima facie showing that there are no triable issues of material fact.” (Scalf v. D.B. Log Homes, Inc. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1510, 1519.) A defendant moving for summary judgment “has met his or her burden of showing that a cause of action has no merit if the party has shown that one or more elements of the cause of action . . . cannot be established.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(2).) “Once the defendant . . . has met that burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff . . . to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to the cause of action or a defense thereto.” (Ibid.)

 

“When deciding whether to grant summary judgment, the court must consider all of the evidence set forth in the papers (except evidence to which the court has sustained an objection), as well as all reasonable inference that may be drawn form that evidence, in the light most favorable to the party opposing summary judgment.” (Avivi v. Centro Medico Urgente Medical Center (2008) 159 Cal.App.4th 463, 467; see also Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (c).) “An issue of fact can only be created by a conflict in the evidence.  It is not created by speculation, conjecture, imagination or guesswork.”  (Lyons v. Security Pacific National Bank (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 1001, 1014 (citation omitted).)

 

Defendant answered in pro per and filed no opposition to the motion. “Although self-represented litigants are not entitled to special treatment, they are entitled to the same treatment as a represented party. ... ‘Trial judges must acknowledge that in propria persona litigants often do not have an attorney's level of knowledge about the legal system and are more prone to misunderstanding the court's requirements.’ (Citation). When one party has counsel and the other does not, the trial court ‘should monitor to ensure the in propria persona litigant is not inadvertently misled, either by the represented party or by the court... [S]pecial care should be used to make sure that verbal instructions given in court and written notices are clear and understandable by a layperson.’” (Petrosyan v. Prince Corp. (2013) 223 Cal.App.4th 587, 594.)

 

            Plaintiff moves for summary judgment on the partition action based on a representation that Defendant admitted Requests for Admissions numbers 1-5. The admitted requests contain the following:

 

REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 1: Admit that YOU took title to the PROPERTY from the Grant Deed recorded on December 13, 2006 (a copy of which is attached hereto as Exhibit A).

 

REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 2: Admit that YOU took 1/2 title to the PROPERTY from the Grant Deed recorded on December 13, 2006 (a copy of which is attached hereto as Exhibit A).

 

REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 3: Admit that the individual named Jose Israel Deras was YOUR husband on December 6, 2006.

 

REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 4: Admit that Plaintiff, ISRAEL E. DERAS currently holds 1/2 title to the PROPERTY.

 

REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 5: Admit that once the PROPERTY is sold that Plaintiff, ISRAEL E. DERAS is entitled to a minimum of 1/2 of the net proceeds from the sale of the PROPERTY.

 

The grant deed shows a conveyance by Maria Deras a married woman transferring a one-half interest to Israel Deras, a single man in November 2006. The admissions establish the parties were married at the time notwithstanding the language in the grant deed.

 

Matters admitted or deemed admitted through an order deeming request for admissions admitted conclusively establish the content of the admissions in the litigation and are not subject to contest through contradictory evidence. (St. Mary v. Superior Court (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 762, 775 accord Murillo v. Superior Court (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 730, 736.) The responses were served under penalty of perjury. [Declaration of Alana Anaya, Ex. A-B.] (Code Civ. Proc., § 2015.5.)

 

“Under California law, the term ‘partition’ signifies ‘the procedure for segregating and terminating common interests in the same parcel of property.’” (14859 Moorpark Homeowner's Ass'n v. VRT Corp. (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 1396, 1404–1405.)

 

(a) A partition action may be commenced and maintained by any of the following persons:

(1) A coowner of personal property.

(2) An owner of an estate of inheritance, an estate for life, or an estate for years in real property where such property or estate therein is owned by several persons concurrently or in successive estates. …

 

Code Civ. Proc., § 872.210

 

(a) A description of the property that is the subject of the action. In the case of tangible personal property, the description shall include its usual location. In the case of real property, the description shall include both its legal description and its street address or common designation, if any.

(b) All interests the plaintiff has or claims in the property.

(c) All interests of record or actually known to the plaintiff that persons other than the plaintiff have or claim in the property and that the plaintiff reasonably believes will be materially affected by the action, whether the names of such persons are known or unknown to the plaintiff.

(d) The estate as to which partition is sought and a prayer for partition of the interests therein.

(e) Where the plaintiff seeks sale of the property, an allegation of the facts justifying such relief in ordinary and concise language.

 

Code Civ. Proc., § 872.230

 

The complaint seeks the sale of the property. The motion remains unopposed.

 

Notwithstanding the lack of opposition from the pro per defendant, however, the basis of the partition remains uncertain. Specifically, Plaintiff fails to address the state of the parties’ marriage. A partition action is proper following a divorce decree. (Romanchek v. Romanchek (1967) 248 Cal.App.2d 337, 341.) The equitable nature of a partition action requires consideration of any potential marital estate and terms of any divorce, if applicable. (Porter v. Superior Court (1977) 73 Cal.App.3d 793, 803 [Court holds unquestioned authority to address community property disputes, as well the ability to determine the existence of separate property properly part of a civil partition action]; see In re Marriage of Leversee (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 891, 897.)

 

In other words, while a partition may be proper post-divorce, the lack of any information as to the parties’ marriage and any potential assignment of property rights within the former marital estate requires address. If the parties remain married, Plaintiff needs to establish the propriety of proceeding with a civil action, as opposed to a family law court.

 

Furthermore, even if the court found a basis for partition, Plaintiff fails to request an interlocutory judgment and potential appointment of a referee.

 

“(a) If the court finds that the plaintiff is entitled to partition, it shall make an interlocutory judgment that determines the interests of the parties in the property and orders the partition of the property and, unless it is to be later determined, the manner of partition.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.720, subd. (a).) “The court shall order that the property be divided among the parties in accordance with their interests in the property as determined in the interlocutory judgment.” (Code Civ. Proc., §872.810.) “Notwithstanding Section 872.810 , the court shall order that the property be sold and the proceeds be divided among the parties in accordance with their interests in the property as determined in the interlocutory judgment in the following situations: (a) The parties agree to such relief, by their pleadings or otherwise. (b) The court determines that, under the circumstances, sale and division of the proceeds would be more equitable than division of the property. For the purpose of making the determination, the court may appoint a referee and take into account his report.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.820.)

 

 

III.            Conclusion & Order

 

The motion for summary judgment is DENIED. Plaintiff fails to shift the burden establishing a right to judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).) Plaintiff must account for the state of the parties’ marriage, and any potential disposition of any potential community property estate addressed in a divorce decree. If the parties remain married, Plaintiff needs to submit legal support for the right to proceed with a partition in the civil court rather than proceeding in family law.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On October 16, 2017, The Jaeger Family Trust and third party EYM King of Missouri, LLC entered into a lease for premises located at 4001 Blue Ridge Cutoff, Kansas City, Missouri. [First Amend. Comp., ¶ 17, Ex. A.] On June 22, 2018, the lease was assigned to third party Tasty King of Missouri, LLC. [Id.]

 

On October 11, 2024, The Jaeger Family Trust obtained a judgment against Tasty King of Missouri in Jackson County, Missouri against tasty King of Missouri, LLC for $408,485.07. [First Amend. Comp., ¶ 21, Ex. B.] On October 15, 2024, a court in Clay County Missouri entered judgment of $657,532.35 in favor of Plaintiff McCleary Road, LLC against Tasty King of Missouri, Tasty King of Kansas, and Tasty King of Iowa, on a breach of lease claim. [First Amend. Comp., ¶ 26, Ex. C.] Both judgments remain outstanding.

 

Plaintiffs allege Tasty King of Missouri and Defendant TK Midwest South share common ownership through third party Thomas Scott, chief executive of third party TK PropCo, LLC. All entities operate in El Segundo, CA. 

 

On January 28, and May 6, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their complaint for Alter Ego Liability for Sister State Judgments and Breach of Lease. The breach of lease claim alleges a failure to pay rent on the Jaeger Family Trust lease from the period of August 2024 through December 2024, thereby leaving an outstanding balance of $58,509.19.

 

II.              Legal Standard & Discussion

 

Demurrer

 

Defendant TK Midwest South, LLC (TK) submits a demurrer to the second cause of action for breach of lease in the first amended complaint. TK presents voluminous argument for the primary position that the lease was found terminated in 2023, thereby barring any claim for rent damages in 2024. The position relies on extensive citation to pleadings filed in the Missouri action, whereby Plaintiffs allegedly made certain “judicial admissions,” thereby barring the subject action. TK relies on the doctrine of “sham pleading” in that Plaintiffs filed an invalid, factually contradictory complaint. TK next contends the breach of lease claim is barred by judicial estoppel, and constitutes claim splitting (e.g. Plaintiff was required to bring all claims as to the lease in the prior action), thereby violating the “one judgment rule.” On the actual factual allegations, TK challenges the legal sufficiency of the cause of action.

 

 Plaintiffs in opposition challenge the demurrer as seeking a factual determination at the pleading stage. Plaintiffs deny any proof of lease termination. Plaintiffs deny any “judicial admission” basis for the challenge, or application of the one judgment bar. Plaintiffs challenge any showing of judicial estoppel or claim splitting. Plaintiffs also deny any violation of the sham pleading standard.

 

TK in reply reiterates the content of the pleadings as creating the basis for a barred action. The judicial admissions and subject action confirm the operative complaint constitutes a “sham” pleading.” Judicial estoppel established in the Missouri judgment precludes any further litigation. The one judgment rule further bars any claim splitting. TK presents a position challenging the “rent survivor clause” position raised in a footnote in the opposition as a basis for ongoing damages (Opposition, 2:22 (footnote 1), and also addresses the alter ego positions summarized in the opposition. (The position was raised in opposition as part of the summary of the dispute, but not substantively addressed as part of any material defense to the demurrer.)

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

“A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly's of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616; Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139 [“[U]nder our liberal pleading rules, where the complaint contains substantive factual allegations sufficiently apprising defendant of the issues it is being asked to meet, a demurrer for uncertainty should be overruled or plaintiff given leave to amend.]

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

K

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

On Defendants request for judicial notice, the court can take judicial notice of the existence of pleadings and claims, but not for the content of any pleading for the truth of the matter asserted. The court cannot take judicial of the court docket for determination of any dispositive factual finding in reviewing the subject operative complaint. The court can acknowledge the judgment in The Jaegar Family Trust breach of lease action, but cannot accept the judgment as a superseding finding thereby undermining the requirement that the court consider the operative pleading as truthful under the demurrer standard.  (Sosinsky v. Grant (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1548, 1568-1569.)

 

To the extent Defendant presents a position based on claim preclusion, consideration of any preclusive effect, however, the court may consider the prior judgment. “Taking judicial notice of a finding in determining its preclusive effect does not involve a determination of the finding's truth. ...  In other words, even though a factual finding in a prior judicial decision may not establish the truth of that fact for purposes of judicial notice, the finding itself may be a proper subject of judicial notice if it has a res judicata or collateral estoppel effect in a subsequent action.” (Rincon EV Realty LLC v. CP III Rincon Towers, Inc. (2019) 43 Cal.App.5th 988, 1005–1006.)

 

For Plaintiffs’ request for judicial notice, as addressed with Defendant’s request, the court cannot take notice of the content of any leases, pleadings, court dockets, petitions, affidavits, and transcripts, except for any operative lease incorporated into the challenged complaint. To the extent the lease and assignment is incorporated into the first amended complaint, the terms are reviewable for purposes of the demurrer. (Avery v. Integrated Healthcare Holdings, Inc. (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 50, 69; Shaw v. Regents of University of California (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 44, 54.) The court will also consider the judgment if presented for determination of any preclusive effect. (Rincon EV Realty LLC v. CP III Rincon Towers, Inc., supra, 43 Cal.App.5th at pp. 1005–1006.)

 

Venue

 

            The court assumes jurisdiction was selected in Los Angeles County due to the location of TK in Los Angeles notwithstanding performance of the contract and presumably execution of the agreement occurred in Missouri. (Code Civ. Proc., § 395.5.) TK makes no motion for a change of venue, and potentially waived any such challenges upon the filing the demurrer thereby making a general appearance in the action. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§396b, 397; Walt Disney Parks & Resorts U.S., Inc. v. Superior Court (2018) 21 Cal.App.5th 872, 879-880.)

 

Claim Preclusion

 

The doctrine of res judicata gives certain conclusive effect to a former judgment in subsequent litigation involving the same controversy.  It seeks to curtail multiple litigation causing vexation and expense to the parties and wasted effort and expense in judicial administration.  Res judicata, or claim preclusion, prevents relitigation of the same cause of action in a second suit between the same parties or parties in privity with them. Under the doctrine of res judicata, if a plaintiff prevails in an action, the cause is merged into the judgment and may not be asserted in a subsequent lawsuit. All claims based on the same cause of action must be decided in a single suit; if not brought initially, they may not be raised at a later date. Res judicata precludes piecemeal litigation by splitting a single cause of action or relitigation of the same cause of action on a different legal theory or for different relief. (Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co. (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888, 897.) “Res Judicata is not a bar to claims arising after the filing of the initial complaint.” A party may assert new claims in an amended pleading, “but if no such pleading is filed, a plaintiff is not foreclosed. [Citation.] The general rule that a judgment is conclusive as to matters that could have been litigated ‘does not apply to new rights acquired pending the action which might have been, but which were not, required to be litigated [Citation]’.” (Allied Fire Protection v. Diede Const., Inc. (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 150, 155; Planning & Conservation League v. Castaic Lake Water Agency (2009) 180 Cal.App.4th 210, 226-227.)

 

“In general, collateral estoppel precludes a party from relitigating issues litigated and decided in a prior proceeding.  (Citations.) ‘Traditionally, we have applied the doctrine only if several threshold requirements are fulfilled. First, the issue sought to be precluded from relitigation must be identical to that decided in a former proceeding. Second, this issue must have been actually litigated in the former proceeding. Third, it must have been necessarily decided in the former proceeding. Fourth, the decision in the former proceeding must be final and on the merits. Finally, the party against whom preclusion is sought must be the same as, or in privity with, the party to the former proceeding. (Citation.)’” (Gikas v. Zolin (1993) 6 Cal.4th 841, 848–849.)

 

“‘Judicial estoppel is an equitable doctrine aimed at preventing fraud on the courts.’ (Citation.) It prohibits a party from taking inconsistent positions in the same or different judicial proceedings. (Citation.)” (M. Perez Co., Inc. v. Base Camp Condominiums Assn. No. One (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 456, 463.)

 

“Where a party files an amended complaint and seeks to avoid the defects of a prior complaint either by omitting the facts that rendered the complaint defective or by pleading facts inconsistent with the allegations of prior pleadings” the pleading is considered a sham pleading. (Owens v. Kings Supermarket (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 379, 383-384; Berman v. Bromberg (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 936, 946 [“A pleader may not attempt to breathe life into a complaint by omitting relevant facts which made his previous complaint defective”].) A pleader is entitled to explain any inconsistencies. (Ibid; Vallejo Development Co. v. Beck Development Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 929, 946.)

 

The October 11, 2024 judgement relied upon in the subject action is incorporated into the first amended complaint, and therefore reviewable. [First Amend. Comp., Ex B.] The parties also submit  copies of the same judgment in their respective requests for judicial notice. As addressed in the judicial notice standard, the court can review the judgment itself for its effect on the judgment. The actual judgment however only awards damages and contract fees. Lacking is any attendant order or findings by the Missouri court regarding the basis for the judgment. The paucity of contextual information prevents the court from reviewing the parameters of any relief thereby allowing the court to potentially determine certain legal findings. Without such information, the court cannot conclude in the subject demurrer, whether the subject action should be barred under the doctrines of claim preclusion, claim splitting, collateral estoppel, res judicata, or the “one judgment rule.” (Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co., supra, 28 Cal.4th at p. 897; Allied Fire Protection v. Diede Const., Inc., supra, 127 Cal.App.4th at p. 155; Planning & Conservation League v. Castaic Lake Water Agency, supra, 180 Cal.App.4th at pp. 226-227.)

 

Because the court also lacks any discussion regarding the actual prior operative pleading submitted to this Court in this action, the Court also finds no basis for consideration of any “judicial admission” and application of the sham pleading doctrine. (Owens v. Kings Supermarket, supra, 198 Cal.App.3d at pp. 383-384; Berman v. Bromberg, supra, 56 Cal.App.4th at p. 946.)

 

            As cited above, the law in California allows for new action based on new right acquired, separate and apart from any prior claims to proceed. TK presents no support for any position as to the requirement that Plaintiffs bring all potential claims in the prior action, thereby precluding later action and potential claim splitting. (Allied Fire Protection v. Diede Const., Inc., supra, 127 Cal.App.4th at p. 155; Planning & Conservation League v. Castaic Lake Water Agency, supra, 180 Cal.App.4th at pp. 226-227.) Plaintiffs may also additionally and alternatively plead new causes of action potentially impacted by the first cause of action as well. (Third Eye Blind, Inc. v. Near North Entertainment Insurance Services, LLC (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 1311, 1323; Rader Co. v. Stone (1986) 178 Cal.App.3d 10, 29.)

 

            The first amended complaint, as presented, therefore sufficiently alleges breach of lease. Any challenges arising from the actual plain language of the lease was not raised. Any challenges under Missouri law regarding accelerated damages and required mitigation measures requires actual support for binding application of Missouri law, rather than California law. (See Frontier Oil Corp. v. RLI Ins. Co. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1436, 1460; see also WEA Crestwood Plaza, L.L.C. v. Flamers Charburgers, Inc. (Mo. Ct. App. 2000) 24 S.W.3d 1, 7.)

 

III.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, the demurrer to the first amended complaint is overruled.

 

TK is ordered to give notice.
