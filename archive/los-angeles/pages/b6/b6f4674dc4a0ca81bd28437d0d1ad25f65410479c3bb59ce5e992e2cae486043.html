Case Number: 26VECV00267    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: T


26VECV00267 Sosa v Arbel

[TENTATIVE] ORDER: Defendant ADB Group Inc.’s Motion to Transfer Venue is DENIED.

Introduction

            Defendant ADB Group Inc. (Defendant) moved to transfer the venue of Plaintiff Jacob Thomas Sosa’s (Plaintiff) Complaint to Ventura County. 

[Note: there is a typo in the motion.  Defendant cited to “Hennigan v. Boren (1966) 243 Cal.App.3d 810”.  (Mtn. pg. 3:21.)  However, the correct citation is “Cal.App.2d”.  Please be careful so that the court will not need to investigate whether AI was used.]

Discussion

            Defendant asserted that the action should be transferred to Ventura County because of the convenience of witnesses and the ends of justice would be promoted.  Defendant provided that all parties to this action, except for Defendant, a Los Angeles County resident, reside in Ventura County, and the accident occurred in Ventura County.  For the fact that Defendant admittedly resides in Los Angeles County, the action is properly venued in this Court. 

            Even if filed in a proper county, on appropriate motion, the court has discretionary power to transfer the case to any other county “[w]hen the convenience of witnesses and the ends of justice would be promoted by the change.”  (Code Civ. Proc. Sec. 397(c); Rycz v. Superior Court (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 824, 836.)  However, the Court only considers the convenience of nonparty witnesses.  Absent extraordinary circumstances, the parties’ conveniences are not considered.  (Dillman v. Superior Court (1962) 205 Cal.App.2d 769, 773.)  Further, the moving party has the burden to show three elements:  the names of the witnesses, the nature of the testimony expected from each, and the reasons why the attendance of each would be inconvenient.  (Id.)  Defendant’s motion is only based upon the parties’ convenience and failed to identify any nonparty witnesses that would be inconvenienced.  Further, Defendant failed to present any extraordinary circumstances.  Because the motion failed to identify the convenience/inconvenience of any nonparty witnesses to support the motion, the motion is not persuasive.

            Defendant further argued that Plaintiff is reneging on their prior agreement to transfer the action.  However, Counsels’ prior agreement is not a statutory ground to transfer venue.

            The fact that Defendant filed their Answer only provides grounds to allow the Court to entertain the motion for change of venue on the ground of witness convenience.  (Johnson v. Superior Court (1965) 232 Cal.App.2d 212, 214.)  The opinion does not state that the Court is required to transfer the action because Defendant filed an answer.  Filing of an answer only provides grounds to show that the motion is appropriately filed.  The Court does not dispute that the motion was appropriately filed.  The Court considered the appropriately filed motion and did not find the substance of the motion to be persuasive. 

            The motion to transfer venue is DENIED.    

            IT IS SO ORDERED, CLERK TO GIVE NOTICE.

Case Number: 26VECV01609    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: T


26VECV01609 Park v City of LA

[TENTATIVE] ORDER: Plaintiff-in-Intervention California Contractors Network’s Motion for Leave to Intervene is GRANTED. 

Introduction

            Plaintiff-in-Intervention California Contractors Network (CCN) moved for leave to intervene in the instant action. 

Discussion

            CCN asserted that they are the workers’ compensation insurance carrier for Plaintiff David Park’s (Plaintiff) employer, Alpha Structural Inc.  CCN provided that Plaintiff’s solo motorcycle accident due to the dangerous condition on the roadway occurred while Plaintiff was working in the course and scope of his employment.  CCN provided that money damages were paid under the policy and CCN seeks subrogation reimbursement. The Court finds that CCN sufficiently presented good cause for intervention because the provided facts show that CCN  has an interest in the litigation.  (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 387.)  Further, the motion is not opposed and the failure to oppose shows that the parties submit to the motion.  The motion is persuasive.

            The motion to intervene is GRANTED.  Complaint in intervention shall be served and filed no later than 10/1/2026.

            IT IS SO ORDERED, CLERK TO GIVE NOTICE.
