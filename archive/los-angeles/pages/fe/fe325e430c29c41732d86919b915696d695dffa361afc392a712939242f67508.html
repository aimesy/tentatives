Case Number: 23CHCV00750    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-14-26

Case # 23CHCV00750, Leyva, et al. v. Sylmar Health and Rehabilitation Center, et al.

Trial Date: 09-28-26

 

MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITIONS

 

MOVING PARTIES:  Plaintiffs Richard Leyva, through his successor-in-interest Daleen Leyva, and Daleen Leyva

RESPONDING PARTY:  No opposition was filed.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order compelling defendant Sylmar Health and Rehabilitation Center, Inc. and defendant Golden State Health Centers, Inc. to produce Martha Vega, Mildren de Castro, RN, and a person most knowledgeable for Management Agreement and Cost Report for depositions.  Order imposing $2,535 in monetary sanctions against defendant Sylmar Health and its counsel of record Hinshaw & Culbertson, LLP, jointly and severally.

 

RULING:  Motion is continued.  The parties must meet and confer directly and file a joint statement.

 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

This elder abuse action arises out of the care and treatment of plaintiff Richard Leyva at defendant Sylmar Health and Rehabilitation Center, Inc.’s 24-hour nursing facility.  Defendant Sylmar Health is a locked psychiatric facility whose residents were incarcerated, many of whom are under the care of court-appointed conservators.  On March 14, 2023, Mr. Levya filed a complaint alleging elder abuse, negligence, violation of California Health and Safety Code section 1430, subdivision (b), and willful misconduct.  Mr. Leyva passed away on August 11, 2025.  On November 7, 2025, Mr. Leyva, through his mother and successor-in-interest, Daleen Leyva, filed a First Amended Complaint against defendants Sylmar Health and Rehabilitation Center, Inc. and Golden State Health Centers, Inc. (Defendants), asserting an additional wrongful death claim.  Ms. Leyva alleges that defendant Sylmar Health and the owner of the facility failed to protect Mr. Leyva from multiple falls, the final of which resulted in a traumatic brain injury.

 

Martha Vega, RN

 

On July 28, 2023, Plaintiffs served Defendants with a deposition notice for Martha Vega, RN.  (Declaration of David E. Ramirez ¶ 2, Exh. 1 [noticed for August 10, 2023 at 1:00 p.m.].)  At some point defense counsel responded, stating August 2023 was too busy and requested October dates.  (Id., Exh. 2.)  Plaintiffs provided October dates, but defense counsel never responded.  On November 2, 2023, plaintiffs’ counsel requested November 2023 dates from defense counsel.  (Id., Exh. 2.)  Plaintiffs followed up again on November 17 and November 28.  (Id. ¶ 3.)

 

On December 12, 2023, Plaintiffs served a second deposition notice for Ms. Vega, noticing the deposition for January 24, 2024 at 1:00 p.m.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 3, Exh. 3.)  Defendants canceled the deposition, and on September 16, 2024, plaintiffs’ counsel sent an email attempting to reschedule the deposition.  (Id. ¶ 4, Exh. 4.)

 

On November 19, 2024, Plaintiffs served a third deposition notice for Ms. Vega, noticing the deposition for December 3, 2024 at 1:00 p.m.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 5, Exh. 5.)  At some point, Defendant objected to the deposition notice, and on the day of the deposition, plaintiffs’ counsel requested alternative deposition dates for Ms. Vega.  (Id. ¶ 6, Exh. 6.)  On February 11, 2025, Plaintiffs’ counsel and defense counsel attended the Mandatory Settlement Conference.  (2/11/25 Minute Order.)  Later that day, Plaintiffs served a fourth deposition notice for Ms. Vega, noticing the deposition for February 26, 2025 at 2:00 p.m.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 7, Exh. 7.)  Defendant objected to the deposition.  (Id. ¶ 8.)  On February 27, 2025, Plaintiffs served a fifth deposition notice for Ms. Vega, noticing the deposition for April 8, 2025 at 1:00 p.m.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 8, Exh. 8.)  This deposition did not occur.

 

Over one year later on April 9, 2026, Plaintiffs served a sixth and final deposition notice for April 22, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 9, Exh. 9.)  This deposition did not go forward, and defense did not send alternative dates.  (Id. ¶ 10.)

 

            Mildred de Castro, RN

 

On April 9, 2026, Plaintiffs served Defendants with a deposition notice for defendant Golden State Health Centers, Inc.’s nurse consultant, Mildred de Castro.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 13, Exh. 11 [noting the deposition for April 22, 2026 at 11:00 a.m.].)  In response to plaintiffs’ counsel’s April 16, 2026, follow-up email, defense counsel apologized for the delay in responding, stated the date did not work, and that he would provide new dates on April 16, 2026.  (Id. ¶ 14, Exh. 12.)  After receiving no response, plaintiffs’ counsel sent a follow-up email on May 20, 2026 requesting alternative dates by the end of the week, May 23, 2026.  (Id. ¶ 15, Exh. 13.)  Defense counsel responded that same day, apologizing for the delay.  (Id. ¶ 15, Exh. 13.)  Defendants have yet to produce Ms. De Castro for a deposition.  (Id. ¶ 16.)

 

            Person Most Knowledgeable Deposition

 

On April 9, 2026, Plaintiffs served Defendants with a deposition notice for person most knowledgeable (PMK) on the Management Agreement and Cost Reports.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 17.)  Plaintiffs’ counsel followed up with defense counsel on April 16, 2026 and May 20, 2026, but defense counsel never provided alternative dates or produced a PMK.  (Id. ¶¶ 18-20.)

 

On May 20, 2026, plaintiffs’ counsel emailed defense counsel requesting alternate deposition dates for Ms. Vega, Ms. De Castro, and the PMK re: Management Agreement and Cost Reports by the end of the week.  (Ramirez Decl. ¶ 10, 15-16, 18-20, Exh. 10.)  Although defense counsel has responded to Plaintiffs’ other emails, defense counsel never provided alternative dates for the depositions or to produce the individuals for the depositions.  However, on May 21, 2026, defense counsel offered May 29, 2026, as a date for Ms. Vega’s deposition.  (Id. ¶ 12.)

 

The next day, On May 22, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion to compel the three depositions and to impose $2,535 in monetary sanctions against defendant Sylmar Health and its counsel of record, jointly and severally.  Plaintiff did not provide the court with an update concerning whether the Vega deposition has occurred.  No opposition was filed.

 

 

ANALYSIS  and MEET AND CONFER

 

A motion to compel a deposition must include either a meet and confer declaration or, if the deponent fails to attend the deposition and to produce the items described in the deposition notice, “a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2025.450, subd. (b), 2016.040.)  In Department F43, meet and confer means in person or via phone.  A letter or email does not suffice.  (Department F43 Courtroom Information, p. 2.)

 

After reviewing the moving papers, the court finds that the parties never met and conferred in person, by phone, or by video conference concerning the depositions.  Nor did Plaintiffs take any certificates of nonappearance at the scheduled depositions.  The extent of plaintiffs’ counsel’s “meet and confer” efforts was sending emails, with defense counsel responding by email.  Because the moving papers do not state that Plaintiff’s counsel ever attempted to call or meet directly with defense counsel, the “meet and confer” requirement is not met.

 

Therefore, the court continues the hearing on the motion.  The parties are ordered to meet and confer concerning scheduling the remaining depositions.  The parties must meet directly by phone, or by video conference.  The parties are also ordered to file a joint statement of remaining issues by a date to be set by the court.  The joint statement should briefly describe the matters in dispute, followed by Plaintiffs’ arguments and then Defendants’ arguments.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

1.  The motion is continued to _____________.

 

2.  The parties are ordered to conduct a meaningful “meet and confer” in person, by telephone, or by video conference as described above.

 

3.  The parties shall submit a joint statement of the remaining issues as described above.  The format should be as follows:  The parties should recite the specific depositions at issue, followed by the moving party’s statement of why it should be compelled, followed by the opposing party’s statement of why it should not be compelled.  To the extent that an argument is repeated for the other depositions, the party shall simply refer to the section where the argument was previously made.

 

4. The deadline for the joint statement is _____________.

Case Number: 25CHCV02497    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-14-26

Case # 25CHCV02497, Sendra, Jr. v. Senda

Trial Date: 02-13-29

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO RECORD A SUBSEQUENT NOTICE OF PENDENCY OF ACTION (LIS PENDENS)

 

MOVING PARTIES:  Plaintiffs Margarito Senda, Jr. and Armando Senda, in pro per

RESPONDING PARTY:  Defendant Lucia Senda, in pro per

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order granting plaintiffs leave to record a subsequent notice of pendency of action (lis pendens) against real property commonly known as 12022 Pierce Street, Sylmar, California 91342.

 

RULING:  Motion is denied without prejudice.

 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On July 15, 2025, plaintiffs Margarito Senda, Jr. and Armando Senda (Plaintiffs) filed this quiet title action seeking cancellation of a quitclaim deed recorded against real property located at 12022 Pierce Street, Sylmar, CA 91342 (the Property).  Plaintiffs allege the Property was held in the Margarito Senda, Sr. Family Trust, created by Plaintiffs’ father Margarito Senda, Sr., for the benefit of all his children. (Compl. ¶¶ 4-5.)  Plaintiffs further claim they are named beneficiaries of the trust and are successors to the trust.  (Id. ¶ 6; see also id. at p. 14 [listing beneficiaries, including plaintiffs].)  Margarito Senda, Sr. was 89 years old when this action was filed.  On February 26, 2024, a quitclaim deed was recorded, indicating title to the Property was transferred from the trust to defendant Lucia Senda (Defendant).  (Id. ¶ 7.)  According to Plaintiffs, this transfer was executed without proper notice, consent, or authority, without the knowledge of the beneficiaries or successor trustees, that Margarito Senda, Sr. lacked capacity and was unduly influenced to sign the transfer, and that Defendant acted in bad faith.  (Id. ¶¶ 8-10.)  Defendant has refused to return and restore the Property to the trust.  (Id. ¶ 10.)  The Complaint asserts cancellation of instrument and quiet title causes of action.

 

Plaintiffs are self-represented.

 

On July 16, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a notice of a proposed lis pendens on this action, which the court (Hon. Judge Gary Micon) approved and signed on July 23, 2025.  At some point, Plaintiffs recorded the lis pendens against the Property.  On October 28, 2025, Plaintiffs personally served Defendant with the lis pendens, the summons, and Complaint.

 

Defendant filed an answer on November 17, 2025 and is self-represented in this matter.

 

On May 11, 2026, Defendant moved to expunge the lis pendens because Plaintiffs failed to comply with the mandatory service and recording requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 405.22.  The court granted the motion.

 

On June 17, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a motion for leave to record a new notice of pendency of action (lis pendens).  Defendant opposed on June 23, 2026.  No reply was filed.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Granting leave to file a Notice of Pendency of Action (Lis Pendens) after the initial notice is expunged

 

“Once a notice of pending action has been expunged, the claimant may not record another notice of pending action as to the affected property without leave of the court in which the action is pending.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.36.)  A party whose lis pendens has been expunged is not precluded from filing another notice of lis pendens that complies with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure sections 405.20 and 405.22.  (McKnight v. Superior Court (1985) 170 Cal.App.3d 291, 303.)  Where the original expungement was based solely on a technical service defect and there is no evidence of bad faith or improper purpose in the underlying action, the expungement does not preclude the plaintiff from refiling a procedurally compliant lis pendens.  (Id.)

 

Plaintiffs seek to file a new lis pendens to prevent third parties from acquiring interests in the Property during the pendency of this action.  According to Plaintiffs, such interests would create additional complications and impair the court’s ability to grant complete relief.  Plaintiff Margarito Senda, Jr. presents a supporting declaration, demonstrating the need for a lis pendens:  Plaintiffs seek court action concerning ownership of the Property which was originally owned by the Margarito Senda, Sr. Revocable Trust; issues concerning Margarito Senda, Sr. executing a quitclaim deed transferring ownership of the Property solely to Defendant; and whether the quitclaim deed was executed due to undue influence, lack of capacity, and in a manner inconsistent with the intent of the Trust and rights of the beneficiaries.  (Declaration of Margarito Senda, Jr. ¶¶ 2-11.)

 

In opposition, Defendant claims Plaintiffs cannot assert a real property claim because their complaint is unverified and does not satisfy the statutory requirements for a quiet title claim.  Defendant also claims the prior violations (no pre-recordation service and not recording the proof of service with the lis pendens) were substantive rather than technical, that her actual notice of this matter does not cure the statutory noncompliance, and Plaintiffs’ claimed risk of transfer does not override the statutory requirements or hardship to the property owner.

 

First, Defendant’s motion to expunge addressed only the service and recordation defects, not the substance of Plaintiffs’ claims in this matter and whether Plaintiffs filed this matter in bad faith.  In granting the motion, the court found the previous lis pendens was void for technical violations and did not make any findings on whether Plaintiffs assert a real property claim and whether Plaintiffs filed this action in bad faith.  Moreover, the service and recordation of a proof of service are technical requirements for lis pendens.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.22;  see generally McKnight, supra, 170 Cal.App.3d 291 [discussing predecessor statute Section 409, subdivisions (c) and (d)].)  The prior lis pendens was expunged based solely on technical violations (failure to serve Plaintiff by mail and record the proof of service with the lis pendens).  (6/16/26 Minute Order; 5/11/26 Motion to Expunge Lis Pendens.)  Thus, Plaintiffs are entitled to seek leave of the court to file a new lis pendens.

 

Although actual notice does not cure statutory noncompliance and Plaintiffs are still required to serve the notice of lis pendens by mail, Plaintiffs’ failure to do so with the prior lis pendens does not affect a subsequent lis pendens.  The court agrees that compliance with statutory requirements – “service by certified mail” and “recordation of the proof of service” – is necessary regardless of hardship to the property owners.  (Carr v. Rosien (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 845, 853 [purpose of the mailing requirement is to ensure “all owners of the property, and all adverse parties claiming an interest in the property, are aware of the lis pendens.”].)  The court notes that in granting the motion to expunge, Plaintiffs failed to present evidence demonstrating they attempted to mail the prior lis pendens to Defendant or that Defendant was actively evading service.

 

“Under California law, a claimant recording a lis pendens must mail a copy of the notice by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, to all known addresses of adverse parties and record owners before recordation.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 405.22, 1013a.)  When a defendant deliberately refuses to sign for or accept certified mail, California courts have consistently held that the defendant cannot defeat the statutory service requirement through willful evasion.   (Hankla v. Governing Board (1975) 46 Cal.App.3d 644, 655; Bear Creek Master Assn. v. Edwards (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 1470, 1485-1488 [finding willful evasion where defense counsel refused to sign for certified letters, which were returned by the post office, while the concurrent first-class mail copies were not returned].)  Proper mailing to the correct address — even if the defendant refuses to claim or sign for the mail — constitutes valid service.”

 

Accordingly, if the court grants Plaintiffs’ motion for leave, Plaintiffs’ must comply with all statutory requirements for a lis pendens, including the pre-recordation certified mail service requirement and the recording of the new lis pendens with the proof of service attached.

 

However, the court does find that Plaintiffs did not properly verified their Complaint.  Complaints asserting quiet title must be verified.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 761.020.)  When the complaint is verified by a person, the person “need not swear to the truth or his or her belief in the truth of the matters stated therein but may, instead, assert the truth or his or her belief in the truth of those matters ‘under penalty of perjury.’”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 446, subd. (a).)  The verification must state that the matters in the complaint are true of the verifier’s own knowledge, except as to matters stated on information and belief, and as to those matters the verifier believes them to be true.  (Id.)  Moreover, Code of Civil Procedure section 2015.5 provides that whenever any matter is required or permitted to be supported by a sworn statement, affidavit, or verification under California law, it may be supported with equal force and effect by an unsworn written statement that recites it is certified or declared “to be true under penalty of perjury, is subscribed by the declarant, and either (1) if executed within California, states the date and place of execution, or (2) if executed anywhere, states the date of execution and that it is certified under California law.  Although both Plaintiffs signed the Complaint on July 14, 2025 (Compl. at p. 3.), Plaintiffs failed to do so under penalty of perjury and failed to state the place where the execution occurred.

 

Plaintiffs’ failure to properly verify the Complaint renders the Complaint insufficient to support their real property claim.  However, Plaintiffs may cure the defect by seeking leave of the court to file an amendment.  (United Farm Workers of America, AFL-CIO v. Agricultural Labor Relations Bd. (1985) 37 Cal.3d 912, 915;  Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Jr. Univ. v. Superior Court (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 1154, 1167; Code Civ. Proc., §§ 472, 473, subd. (a)(1).)  Without a verified complaint, Plaintiffs cannot demonstrate a valid real property claim which supports a notice of lis pendens.

 

Therefore, the court denies Plaintiffs’ motion to for leave to file a new notice of pendency (lis pendens) without prejudice.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Motion is denied without prejudice.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.


Case Number: 26CHUD00501    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-14-26

Case # 26CHUD00501, Equity Residential Management, LLC v. Carr, et al.

Trial Date: None set.

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE A DEMURRER

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant LaTonya Carr, in pro per

RESPONDING PARTY:  Plaintiff Equity Residential Management, LLC

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order granting defendant LaTonya Carr leave to file a demurrer.

 

RULING:  Motion is denied.

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

Plaintiff Equity Residential Management, LLC (Plaintiff) filed this unlawful detainer action against defendants Trevor Carr, LaKeithia Terry, LaTonya Carr, Cesar Javier Sanchez Alonso, and Lisa Smith on April 30, 2026.  Plaintiff is a lessor as agent for the owner of property located at 18420 Oak Canyon Road, Apartment 510 in Santa Clarita, CA 91387 (the Premises).  Defendant tenants have failed to pay rent due under a lease agreement for the Premises.  Plaintiff seeks forfeiture of a residential lease agreement, reasonable attorneys’ fees, and damages of past-due rent ($3,085) and holdover rent at a daily rate of $103.80.

 

On May 21, 2026, defendant LaTonya Carr specially appeared and filed a motion to quash service of the summons and complaint.  The court denied the motion on June 3, 2026 and gave Plaintiff five days from that date (June 8, 2026) to file an answer.  (6/03/26 Minute Order.)

 

Ms. Carr did not file an answer or any other responsive pleading by June 8, 2026.

 

On June 10, 2026, Ms. filed the instant motion for leave to file a demurrer instead of an answer.  Carr filed an amended motion on June 11, 2026. 

 

On July 6, 2026, defendant Cesar Javier Sanchez Alonso filed a notice of stay of proceedings, indicating he is currently a debtor in an active Chapter 13 bankruptcy case.  (7/06/26 Notice of Stay of Proceedings.)  This matter was placed in a stay that same day.

 

On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to Ms. Carr’s motion for leave to file a demurrer.

 

No reply was filed.

 

 

 

 

 

SUMMARY OF ARGUMENTS

 

Defendant LaTonya Carr (Carr) contends the court had no right to restrict her right to a responsive pleading to an answer because in unlawful detainer cases, a demurrer is an equally permissible responsive pleading.  The deadline for Carr to file a responsive pleading is five days from written notice of the entry of the order plus five additional days if notice is served by mail.  Any default or default judgment entered before the expiration of Carr’s time to file a responsive pleading is void and subject to reversal.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff claims the bankruptcy stay precludes further proceedings in this matter.  If the stay is lifted, Plaintiff contends the court should deny Ms. Carr’s motion because her cited authorities address whether a default entered before expiration of a defendant’s time to respond is valid.  According to Plaintiff, no such circumstances exist here.  Carr has filed neither an answer nor a demurrer.  Plaintiff agrees that Carr has a statutory right to demur.  The issue is whether the court should exercise its discretion to grant Carr leave to file a demurrer where Ms. Carr has filed no responsive pleading and instead seeks “a prospective order confirming her right to demur.”  Ms. Carr’s cited authorities addresses do not address this question. 

 

 

REQUESTS FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

Defendant Carr asks the court to take judicial notice of the following documents pursuant to Evidence Code sections 452 and 453:

 

·         Ruling entered on October 16, 2017 by the Appellate Division of the Los Angeles County Superior Court in Van Butenschoen v. Cynthia Flaker (2017) 16 Cal.App.5th Supp. 10, Case No. BV031862; and

·         This court’s Minute Order denying defendant LaTonya Carr’s Motion to Quash Service of the Summons and Complaint, entered on June 3, 2026.

 

The court grants the request pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivisions (c) and (d).

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

            A.  Defendant Sanchez’s Bankruptcy Stay applies only to claims pertaining to him.

 

When a bankruptcy petition is filed, an automatic stay arises immediately and stays the commencement or continuation of any judicial action or proceeding against the bankruptcy debtor.  (11 U.S.C. § 362(a)(1).)  The critical language is the bankruptcy code’s reference to “the debtor,” meaning the “person or municipality concerning which a [bankruptcy] case [has been commenced].”  (11 U.S.C. 101(13).)  California courts and federal courts applying California law have uniformly interpreted this statutory language to mean that the automatic stay protects only the debtor and does not extend to non-debtor co-defendants.  (Higgins v. Superior Court (2017) 15 Cal.App.5th 973, 981-983; In re Miller (9th Cir. BAP 2001) 262 B.R. 499, 505-506.)  “[T]he bankruptcy of one defendant does not normally stay the case as to non-debtor defendants absent unusual circumstances,” and only the bankruptcy court may extend the automatic stay to a non-debtor party.  (Totten v. Kellogg Brown & Root, LLC (2016) 152 F.Supp.3d 1243, 1267-1268.)

 

Because Plaintiff does not present an order from the bankruptcy court extending the automatic stay to the other defendants in this matter, Plaintiff’s “bankruptcy stay” argument fails.

 

 

B.  Although the court’s order limited Ms. Carr’s responsive pleading to an “answer,” Ms. Carr’s instant motion was filed without cite a proper procedural basis and is untimely because it was filed two days after the June 8, 2026 deadline.

 

As an initial matter, Plaintiff fails to cite a valid procedural basis for her motion.  The court still addresses the merits of the moving papers.

 

“On or before the day fixed for their appearance, the defendant may appear and answer, demur, or move to strike any portion of the complaint.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1170, subd. (a).)  “[F]ollowing the denial of a motion to quash, a court cannot order a defendant to file only an answer.  Such an order is improper because the statutes do not limit the defendant’s responsive pleading to an answer, and a demurrer is an acceptable responsive pleading.”  (Butenschoen v. Flaker (2017) 16 Cal.App.5th Supp. 10, 15.)

 

The language of the court’s June 3, 2026, Minute Order limits Ms. Carr’s responsive pleading to an answer.  (6/03/26 Minute Order at p. 5.)  As such, Ms. Carr was entitled to file an acceptable responsive pleading whether an answer or demurrer. 

 

“Notwithstanding any other provision of law, in any action under this chapter: . . . The service and filing of a notice of motion under subdivision (a) shall extend the defendant’s time to plead until five days after service upon him of the written notice of entry of an order denying his motion, except that for good cause shown the court may extend the defendant’s time to plead for an additional period not exceeding 15 days.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1167.4, subd. (b).)

 

Ms. Carr contends the responsive deadline was extended by five days pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1013’s five-day extension for mail.  However, Section 1167.4’s inclusion of the phrase “Notwithstanding any other provision of law,” signals a legislative intent to displace general procedural rules — including section 1013 — within the summary unlawful detainer context.  (Highland Plastics, Inc. v. Enders (1980) 109 Cal.App.3d Supp. 1, 7-12 [reasoning legislature intended to maintain the summary nature of unlawful detainer actions].)

 

Furthermore, Ms. Carr was required to file a responsive pleading five calendar days from service of notice of the ruling not five court days.  California authority establishes that unqualified references to “days” in statutory language means calendar days, rather than “court days.”  (Iverson v. Superior Court (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 544, 548.) 

 

The Clerk’s Certificate of Mailing indicates the notice of the Minute Order was mailed to defendant Carr the day it was entered, June 3, 2026.  (6/03/26 Certificate of Mailing.)  Therefore, Ms. Carr’s filing deadline was five days later:  June 8, 2026.

 

Ms. Carr failed to file a responsive pleading or the instant motion by the June 8, 2026, deadline.

 

Because Ms. Carr’s motion is untimely and she fails to provide a valid procedural basis for bringing this motion, the court denies the motion to grant leave to file a demurrer.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Motion is denied.
