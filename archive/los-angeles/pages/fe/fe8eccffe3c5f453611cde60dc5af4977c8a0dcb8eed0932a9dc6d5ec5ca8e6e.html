DEPARTMENT 8 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 26IWUD00315    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 8


Tentative:

The motion to set aside the dismissal which was entered under Code of Civil Procedure section 1167.1 is denied. That statute provides, "If proof of service of the summons has not been filed within 60 days of the complaint's filing, the court may dismiss the action without prejudice." As opposed to a case dismissed for failure to file a proof of service or to appear at an order to show cause regarding proof of service where the plaintiff had failed to file a proof of service, in this action the Plaintiff appears never to have effected service. Indeed, on June 1, 2026 this court denied an application to post and mail the summons and complaint for failure to show reasonable diligence. 

Section 1167.1 was enacted alongside section 1161.2, which provides that an unlawful detainer record is restricted where judgment is entered for the plaintiff after trial more than 60 days since the filing of the complaint. Granting the Plaintiff's motion to set aside the dismissal in this action would necessarily mean any judgment would be entered more than 60 days since filing the complaint and therefore frustrating the purpose of and policies supporting section 1161.2.  

Case Number: 26IWUD00696    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 8


Tentative:

The court denies the motion to set aside and vacate the dismissal of this action. The Plaintiff states she "mistakenly dismissed the case without realizing or fully understanding the consequences of such an action." But at the time the Plaintiff dismissed the action she was represented by counsel, and the Plaintiff does not explain whether and what discussions she and her counsel had about her request to vacate the action. Those omissions undermine her contentions that she did not understand the consequences of her actions and that her request to dismiss the action is excusable under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Based on the Plaintiff's declaration, which provides no explanation at all for the "mistakenly filed" request for dismissal, the court finds the Plaintiff has not shown "mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect" under section 473, subdivision (b).

The court notes the Plaintiff claims that vacating the dismissal would not cause the Defendant prejudice because he "already has a default judgment against him." The Defendant did answer the complaint, however, and a default was entered only against unnamed occupants. 

Case Number: 26IWUD00798    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 8


Tentative:

The court overrules the Defendants' demurrer. The court rejects the Defendants' argument the first amended complaint was untimely filed for two reasons. First, the amended complaint was filed before the hearing on the Defendants' demurrer and, as such, could be filed without leave to amend. (See Code Civ. Proc., ss 472, subd. (a), 1170, subd. (c).) Second, the amended complaint was likely filed after this court issued a tentative ruling sustaining the Defendants' demurrer to the original complaint with leave to amend, but one day before the court's tentative ruling became the ruling by operation of Rule of Court, rule 3.1308. Under Code of Civil Procedure section 128, subdivision (a)(8), this court has authority to "amend and control its process and orders so as to make them conform to law and justice." Pursuant to that authority, the court finds the Plaintiff had leave to amend the complaint for purposes of filing the first amended complaint. 

The first amended complaint states a cause of action for unlawful detainer based upon nonpayment of rent, as the complaint appears on its face to comply with Code of Civil Procedure § 1161(2). The complaint is not defective or internally inconsistent for failing to attach the alleged lease agreement with the prior owner. The complaint alleges the Defendants breached that agreement, which became a month-to-month agreement after the original lease term ended, by failing to pay rent.

The Defendant's argument that the Plaintiff's co-owner is an indispensable party cannot be resolved via this demurrer, as it relies upon facts beyond the face of the complaint of which the court cannot take judicial notice. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318; Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) The mere fact there is a co-owner of the property is not sufficient in and of itself to show the plaintiff is not the "landlord, or the successor in estate of the landlord," as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 1161(2). Nor would that fact show the plaintiff lacks a possessory interest in the property necessary to establish standing to bring this action. (See Friendly Village Community Assn., Inc. v. Silva & Hill Constr. Co. (1973) 31 Cal.App.3d 220, 224 [real party in interest must show "ownership, lawful possession, or right to possession, of the property"].)

With regard to the allegedly inconsistent allegations in the Plaintiff's complaint filed in Case No. 26IWUD00655, the Plaintiff voluntarily dismissed that action even before the Defendants filed a responsive pleading. Thus, the allegations in that complaint were never litigated and under the circumstances do not constitute binding judicial admissions in this action, even if they are inconsistent with the allegations in the first amended complaint. The complaint filed in this action effectively superseded the complaint filed in the previous action. (See Minish v. Hanuman Fellowship (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 437, 456.)

To the extent the Defendants claim they had the right to file a demurrer because their previous pleading titled "Motion to invalidate service of amended complaint" was essentially a motion to quash, the demurrer is overruled. The Defendants have already filed an answer. The Defendants' motion for reconsideration of this court's order denying the motion to invalidate service is now moot.
