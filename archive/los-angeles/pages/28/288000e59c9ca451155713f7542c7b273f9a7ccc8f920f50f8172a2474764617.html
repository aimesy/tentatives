I.               BACKGROUND 

This case arises out of habitability and harassment claims brought by two

groups of tenants, Carlos Gonzalez, et al. (“Gonzalez Plaintiffs”) and Maria Corona, et al. (“Corona Plaintiffs”), against their former landlord, A AND J Apartments, Inc. (“A AND J”), and property managers Maria Hernandez and Raul Zaragoza (collectively, “Original Defendants”). Plaintiffs allege they experienced uninhabitable and hazardous living conditions at 14662 W. Rayen Street, Panorama City, California (herein, “Subject Property”), including mold contamination, raw sewage exposure, and vermin infestations, many of which allegedly continue to the present day. (See Gonzalez First Amended Complaint (“Gonzalez FAC”), ¶¶ 16-21, 32-35; Corona First Amended Complaint (“Corona FAC”) (collectively, “FACs”).)

On March 7, 2025, A AND J filed Cross-Complaints in both actions, asserting causes of action for equitable indemnity, contribution, implied indemnity, declaratory relief, and apportionment of fault against 14662 Rayen Street LLC (“Rayen”’), the current owner of the subject property. A AND J has cross-complained, alleging any liability imposed upon it would be derivative of Rayen’s own wrongdoing, as Rayen has owned the property since September 8, 2023, and Plaintiffs allege that the habitability violations continue to the present day. (See A AND J Cross-Complaint ¶¶ 1-2, 6.)

Plaintiff now seeks leave of this Court to file a second amended complaint.

II.            PROCEDURAL HISTORY

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure § 473(a)[1] provides: “The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 473(a)(1); see also Code Civ. Proc. § 576.)¿¿¿¿ ¿¿ 

The court has broad discretion to permit amendments to pleadings, and “the court’s discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendment of the pleadings.” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428.)¿“The policy favoring amendment is so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified.”¿(Id.)¿“If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend.”¿(Morgan v. Superior Court (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.) Prejudice includes “delay in trial, loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation.” (Solit v. Tokai Bank, Ltd. New York Branch (1999) 68 Cal.App.4th 1435, 1448.)¿¿ 

A motion for leave to amend a pleading must comply with the procedural requirements of Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, under which the motion must include (1) a copy of the proposed and numbered amendment, (2) specifications by reference to pages and lines the allegations that would be deleted and added, and (3) a declaration specifying the effect, necessity and propriety of the amendments, date of discovery and reasons for delay. (See Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a), (b).)¿  

IV.          ANALYSIS

After reviewing Plaintiffs’ Motion, the Court finds the motion is in compliance with Rule of Court, rule 3.1324(a) and (b). Plaintiffs represent they moved for leave promptly after depositions completed in the spring of 2026 confirmed 14662 Rayen Street LLC's notice of and failure to remediate the alleged uninhabitable conditions during its ownership. (See Mot. at 11:2-4; Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b)(3)-(4).) Moreover, 14662 Rayen Street LLC has been a party to this action since March 7, 2025, has appeared through counsel, responded to the pleadings, and participated in discovery. With trial set for October 12, 2026, the Court finds the proposed amendment will not result in undue delay or prejudice, as 14662 Rayen Street LLC has long been aware of the underlying allegations and will have ample opportunity to prepare its defense before trial. Further, Plaintiffs’ counsel provides a declaration attesting to the above and attaches a proposed second amended complaint in compliance with Rules of Court, rule 3.1324.

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s unopposed Motion is GRANTED.

V.             CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Plaintiffs’ Motion for Leave to File a Second Amended Complaint. Plaintiffs are ordered to file and serve the proposed second amended complaint on or before July 16, 2026.
