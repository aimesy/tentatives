ANALYSIS:

 

            Richard Marusa guaranteed a contract between
Westlake Services, LLC and Richland Motors, Inc.  Richland breached the contract, so Westlake
sued Richland and Marusa.  Richland
defaulted, Marusa answered, and Westlake propounded discovery.  Marusa failed to provide satisfactory
responses to its discovery requests, so Westlake moved successfully to compel
those responses.  On February 5, 2026,
this Court granted Westlake’s motion and ordered Marusa to pay Westlake
$1,345.50 in sanctions.  The sanctions
were to be paid within 30 days, but the Court’s order omitted a deadline for
Marusa to provide the required discovery responses.  Marusa neither provided discovery responses
nor paid Westlake the $1,345.50 that the Court ordered. 

 

Westlake therefore
moved for terminating sanctions in April 2026. 
The Court granted the motion in part, ordering Marusa to respond to the
outstanding discovery requests within 20 days of the May 28, 2026 order, immediately
pay Westlake the $1,345.50 previously ordered, and pay additional sanctions in
the amount of $1,873.82 payable within 30 days of the order.  The Court warned Marusa if he failed to
comply that “he likely faces the imposition of terminating sanctions.”  The Court’s Judicial Assistant and Westlake provided
notice of the ruling to Marusa, and as of now, Marusa has neither provided
discovery responses nor paid Westlake the total of $3,219.32 that the Court
ordered. 

 

            Westlake therefore again moves to have Marusa’s answer
stricken and his default entered. 
Westlake also requests monetary sanctions for having to file this
motion.  Marusa has not opposed the
motion or given any indication of compliance with the previous order—or
interest in defending himself in this matter.

 

            A court has the power to impose monetary, evidence,
issue, or terminating sanctions on a party who fails to respond to discovery or
fails to obey a court order to provide discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010,
subds. (d), (g); id. § 2023.030, subds. (a)–(d).)  Terminating sanctions are often appropriate
only as a last resort, either when a party fails to respond to escalating
measures of coerced compliance or when its discovery misconduct is so bad that lesser
sanctions would have the Court and the party seeking discovery running a fool’s
errand.  (City of Los Angeles v. PricewaterhouseCoopers, LLP
(2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 63; Lopez v. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of N.Y.,
Inc. (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 566, 604.)

 

            Given
that Marusa has again failed to comply with the Court’s order, the Court GRANTS
Westlake’s motion for terminating sanctions. 
The Court STRIKES Marusa’s answer, and he is now in default.  The Court also awards Westlake an additional
$1,873.82 in monetary sanctions for causing Westlake to file this motion, to be
paid within 30 days of this order.  (Code
Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); id. § 2031.300, subd. (c).)  The Court VACATES the trial date in this
matter, and ORDERS Westlake to SHOW CAUSE, on Wednesday, October 28, 2026 at
9:30 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse, re:  the entry of a default judgment.

Case Number: 25STLC06914    Hearing Date: August 24, 2026    Dept: 25

FAC FILED:             09-12-25

CASE NUMBER:      25STLC06914                       

NOTICE:                   OK                                                                                                                                                                 

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION FOR ORDER CORRECTING MISNOMER IN PLAINTIFF’S NAME BY INTERLINEATION [CAL. CIV. PROC. CODE § 473(a)(1)]

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Golden Kaizer, Inc.

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

MOTION TO CORRECT COMPLAINT

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Court GRANTS Golden Kaizer, Inc.’s motion.  The first amended complaint and all prior pleadings and proceedings in this action are deemed amended, by interlineation, to state Golden Kaizer’s true name as “KAIZER GOLD, INC., a California corporation, doing business as GOLDEN KAIZER INC” wherever the name “GOLDEN KAIZER, INC.” appears.

 

The defaults entered against Blanca Jewelry, LLC and Blanca Alarcon remain in effect.

 

Golden Kaizer is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          None filed as of August 19, 2026                   [   ] Late                      [X] None

REPLY:                     None filed as of August 19, 2026                   [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Three days after filing its initial complaint, Golden Kaizer, Inc. filed its first amended complaint against Blanca Jewelry, LLC and Blanca Alarcon for various causes of action arising from Alarcon’s failure to pay for jewelry sold by Golden Kaizer.  Golden Kaizer obtained defaults against Blanca Jewelry and Alarcon but has not yet obtained a default judgment.  Golden Kaizer now moves for an order correcting a misnomer in its name in the first amended complaint and all prior pleadings and proceedings in this action, by interlineation, from “GOLDEN KAIZER, INC., a California corporation” to “KAIZER GOLD, INC., a California corporation, doing business as GOLDEN KAIZER INC.”

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 473 gives the Court authority to allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by correcting a mistake in the name of a party.  (Id., § 473, subd. (a)(1); e.g., Hawkins v. Pacific Coast Building Products, Inc. (2004) 124 Cal.App.4th 1497, 1503–1505.)  

 

            The invoice underlying this matter was issued under the business name “Golden Kaizer Inc.”  Counsel for Golden Kaizer attests that when preparing the default judgment papers, they confirmed Golden Kaizer’s true name of record with the California Secretary of State is “Kaizer Gold, Inc.”  Counsel attaches the fictitious business name statement evidencing this fact.  The Court finds that Golden Kaizer is Kaizer Gold, Inc.’s fictitious business name and that it represents a single business entity.  Thus, the Court GRANTS Golden Kaizer’s motion.

 

            This change does not constitute a substantive change requiring the amendment to be served on Blanca Jewelry and Alarcon, and does not open the defaults entered against them.  (Compare Paterra v. Hansen (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 507, 530 [substantive, material changes include an increase in damages sought, a new or different cause of action based on a different theory, or one that impacts a reasonable defendant’s consideration of whether to contest the claims or to default]; Engebretson & Co. v. Harrison (1981) 125 Cal.App.3d 436, 442–443 [an amended pleading containing substantive changes must be served on any defendant who has not yet appeared in the action].)  Golden Kaizer has previously served the first amended complaint on Blanca Jewelry and Alarcon; it does not need to re-serve any documents and the defaults remain entered against Blanca Jewelry and Alarcon.

 

            Golden Kaizer is to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Jennifer Absinthia Vermut entered into an independent contractor agreement with Center for Culinary Culture.  Center breached the agreement by failing to perform any of the services outlined in the agreement, such as producing podcast episodes and securing distribution deals.  Pursuant to an arbitration, Vermut was awarded $8,950.00, reflecting $6,000.00 in principal and $2,950.00 in arbitration fees, to accrue interest at the rate of 18 percent per annum.  Vermut now seeks to confirm that arbitration award.

 

            The Court finds that Vermut has complied with the relevant procedural prerequisites to confirm the award regarding filing requirements, service of the petition and notice of hearing, service of the award, and timeliness of the petition.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1283.6, 1285.4, 1288, 1288.4, 1290.4.)  Thus, the Court GRANTS the petition.
