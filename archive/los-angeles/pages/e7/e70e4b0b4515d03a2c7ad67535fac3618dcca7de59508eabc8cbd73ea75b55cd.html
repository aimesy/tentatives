MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER DISCOVERY RESPONSES

(Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set Two)

Los Angeles Superior Court Case # 24CHCV00833

 

Motion filed: 4/27/26

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant/Cross-Complainant Luisita Caberto Dela Rosa, Trustee of the Luisita Caberto Dela Rosa 2004 Revocable Trust dated August 2, 2024 (“Defendant”)

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Eldeodora Gregorio Cruz (“Plaintiff”)

NOTICE: OK

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: Orders compelling Plaintiff’s further responses to the following discovery requests:

·         Defendant’s Special Interrogatories, Set Two, Nos. 75–80; and

·         Defendant’s Requests for Production of Documents (“RFPs”), Set Two, Nos. 32–37.

 

TENTATIVE RULING: The motions are continued. The parties’ counsel are ordered to meet and further confer either telephonically or in-person within 60 days, to resolve and/or narrow all outstanding discovery issues. Defendant’s counsel is to file a declaration with the Court which confirms compliance with this order, or explains why no meaningful meet and confer occurred.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a fraud action in which Plaintiff alleges that pending divorce proceedings from her ex-husband, defaulted defendant/cross-defendant Pedro Cabral Cruz, Cruz purchased a property located at 20517 Martingale Place, Santa Clarita, CA 91350, taking title to the subject property as “a married man” on 1/19/21. (FAC ¶¶ 7–8.) In 2023, Cruz attempted to sell the subject property, and requested that Plaintiff sign an Interspousal Transfer Grant Deed, which Plaintiff refused. (Id. at ¶ 9.) Plaintiff alleges that Cruz proceeded to forge Plaintiff’s signature on the Interspousal Transfer Grant Deed, and sold the subject property to Defendant. (Id. at ¶¶ 10–11.)

 

On 3/13/24, Plaintiff filed her original complaint, alleging against three named Defendants the following causes of action: (1) Fraud; (2) Breach of Fiduciary Duty; (3) Unjust Enrichment; (4) Negligence; (5) Cancellation of Deed; (6) Cancellation of Deed; and (7) Quiet Title. On 6/10/24, Defendant filed her answer and cross-complaint, alleging against her two co-defendants a sole cause of action for Equitable Indemnity. On 2/4/26, Plaintiff amended her complaint to name Provident Title Company as previously unnamed Doe defendant 1. On 6/16/26, Plaintiff filed her first amended complaint (“FAC”), alleging the same seven causes of action.

 

On 2/10/26, Defendant served her second set of discovery requests on Plaintiff. (Decl. of Michaelle Lee ¶ 2.) On 3/9/26, Plaintiff served her responses thereto. (Id. at ¶ 3.)

 

On 4/27/26, Defendant filed the instant motions to compel further responses to the subject discovery requests. On 6/25/26, Plaintiff filed her oppositions. On 7/1/26, Defendant filed her replies.

 

ANALYSIS

 

“The party to whom interrogatories have been propounded shall respond in writing under oath separately to each interrogatory by any of the following: (1) An answer containing the information sought to be discovered; (2) An exercise of the party’s option to produce writings; or (3) An objection to the particular interrogatory.” (Code Civ Proc. § 2030.210, subd. (a).) “Each answer in a response to interrogatories shall be as complete and straightforward as the information reasonably available to the responding party permits.” (Code Civ Proc. § 2030.220.) “If an objection is made to an interrogatory or to a part of an interrogatory, the specific ground for the objection shall be set forth clearly in the response.” (Code Civ Proc. § 2030.240, subd. (b).)

 

A propounding party may move for an order compelling further responses to interrogatories if any of the following apply: “(1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete; (2) An exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate; or (3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.300, subd. (a).) Discovery is relevant if it is admissible as evidence, or “appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2017.010.) “Discovery may relate to the claim or defense of the party seeking discovery or of any other party to the action.” (Ibid.)

 

California law requires a responding party to respond to each request for production of documents with either a statement of compliance, a representation that the party lacks the ability to comply, or an objection to the demand. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.210, subd. (a).) If the response includes an objection to the demand in part, it must also include a statement of compliance or noncompliance as set forth above. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.240, subd. (a).) Additionally, the response must (1) identify the particular document that falls within the category of the request to which the objection is being made, and (2) expressly set forth the extent of, and specific ground for, the objection. (Id. at subd. (b).)¿A propounding party may move for an order compelling further response to a discovery request if it decides that “an objection in the response is without merit or too general.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

A.    Meet and Confer

 

A motion to compel further discovery responses must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration stating “facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.300, subd. (b)(1); 2031.310, subd. (b)(2); 2016.040.)

 

Here, Defendant’s counsel declares that beginning on 4/20/26, she emailed Plaintiff’s counsel attempting to meet and confer letter regarding the issues raised in the instant motions. (Lee Decl. ¶¶ 4–5.) On 4/30/26, counsel for the parties met and conferred telephonically, and on 5/15/26, Plaintiff served supplemental responses to the subject discovery requests. (Decl. of Ma. Rita S. Vesagas ¶¶ 7–9.) On 6/23/26, counsel for the parties met and conferred telephonically regarding Plaintiff’s supplemental responses, including Defendant’s request “that Plaintiff provide: (1) Bates-stamped copies of the documents produced; (2) verified supplemental responses identifying which Bates-numbered documents are responsive to each Request for Production; and (3) clarification confirming that no responsive documents were being withheld on privilege grounds.” (Suppl. Lee Decl. ¶¶ 2–7.) As of the date of Defendant’s reply, Plaintiff has not complied with Defendant’s request.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court orders counsel for the parties to meet and further confer either telephonically or in-person within 60 days, to resolve and/or narrow all outstanding discovery issues. Defendant’s counsel is to file a declaration with the Court which confirms compliance with this order or explains why no meaningful meet and confer occurred. The motions are therefore continued pending further meet and confer.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The unopposed motions are continued 10/13/2026. The parties’ counsel are ordered to meet and further confer either telephonically or in-person within 60 days, to resolve and/or narrow all outstanding discovery issues. Defendant’s counsel is to file a declaration with the Court which confirms compliance with this order, or explains why no meaningful meet and confer occurred.

MOTION TO EXPUNGE LIS PENDENS

Los Angeles Superior Court Case # 26CHCV00506

¿ 

Motion Filed: 4/24/26

¿ 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Val-Chris Investments, Inc.; The Glenn And Carrie Donahue Revocable Trust; Glenn Donahue, and Carrie Donahue (collectively, “Moving Defendants”)

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Domenique Chantel Thola, as Trustee of the Valley Ranch Family Living Trust, dated March 20, 2023 (“Plaintiff”)

NOTICE: OK¿ 

¿ 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order expunging the Notice of Pendency of Action (Lis Pendens) recorded by Plaintiff on 2/11/26.

¿ 

TENTATIVE RULING: The unopposed motion is granted. Moving Defendants are awarded $1477.50 in attorney fees and costs.

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE: Moving Defendants’ request for judicial notice is granted as to the existence, but not the contents, of Exhibits 2–7, 9–11, and 16. Moving Defendants’ request for judicial notice is denied as to Exhibits 1, 8, and 12–15.

 

BACKGROUND¿ 

 

“This case arises from an elaborate fraud and breach of trust orchestrated by now-indicted real estate agent Federico Jorge Triebel IV in collusion with mortgage lender Val Chris Investments, Inc. Triebel sold the valuable 16515 Valley Ranch Road property to Juan Carlos Thola for $2.6 million in 2021, intending it as a gift for JC Thola’s daughter (Triebel’s goddaughter) Domenique Thola. At the time, JC Thola requested that title be placed in a trust for Domenique’s benefit. Instead, Triebel deeded the property into a revocable trust under his control—naming himself as trustee and giving his own son a contingent benefit—while failing to pay off the existing mortgages on the property at the time of acquisition. Triebel and Val Chris conspired to misappropriate the $2.6 million purchase money rather than using it to clear the loans: Val Chris agreed to assign the old deeds of trust to Triebel’s self-serving trust instead of reconveying them, allowing both to pocket the sale proceeds and leave the prior liens intact. They also colluded on a $390,000 “construction loan” from Val Chris in late 2021 that was never invested into the property. Through these actions, Defendants saddled the property with fraudulent debt while diverting the funds meant to pay those debts, all at the expense of the true beneficiary, Domenique. Triebel, acting as trustee, then stonewalled Domenique—refusing to provide trust accountings or transfer title—and after being federally indicted for fraud, he attempted with Val Chris to inflate the loan payoffs and “short-sell” the property at a discount for their benefit.” (Compl. ¶ 1.)

 

Moving Defendant Val Chris Investments, Inc. (“VCI”) is a mortgage lender “being sued for its role in the fraudulent scheme and its continuing assertion of lien interests adverse to Plaintiff’s title.” (Id. at ¶ 4.) Moving Defendants “The Glenn and Carrie Donahue Trust, its trustees Glenn Donahue and Carrie Donahue, are trustees of a private trust (or related IRA accounts managed by Glenn and Carrie Donahue) that VCI used as an assignee for the $390,000 ‘construction’ loan.” (Id. at ¶ 6.)

 

On 12/18/24, Plaintiff filed her complaint, alleging against Triebel and Moving Defendants the following causes of action: (1) Fraud – Intentional Misrepresentation; (2) Fraud – Civil Conspiracy; (3) Breach of Fiduciary Duty; (4) Aiding and Abetting Breach of Fiduciary Duty; (5) Quiet Title; (6) Cancellation of Instruments; (7) Unjust Enrichment; (8) Constructive Trust; (9) Injunctive Relief; (10) Declaratory Relief; and (11) Accounting.

 

“The Department of Justice, Triebel (as the then trustee of the Valley Ranch Family Living Trust), and the Val-Chris Defendants entered into an agreement whereby the Department of Justice would sell the Property and the net proceeds would first go to pay off the senior and junior loans, then the balance would go to reimbursing the victims of Triebel and Juan Carlos Thola’s criminal activities.” (Defs.’ Mot. 5:17–21.) Thereafter, on 3/19/26, Moving Defendants foreclosed on the property, and “The Donahue Trust has obtained a buyer for the Property, willing to pay $2,600,000 for the Property as is, and was in the process of opening a 30 day escrow when it learned of the existence of the lis pendens for the first time on March 26, 2026 when Plaintiff’s counsel sent a face page for the recording to the real estate broker who had listed the Property for sale. Plaintiff never served the lis pendens on any of the Val-Chris Defendants—let alone prior to recording--and does not appear to have filed it with this Court, both violations of CCP § 405.22.” (Id. at 6:15–20.)

 

On 4/24/26, Moving Defendants filed the instant motion to expunge lis pendens and request for judicial notice. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Any party¿or a non-party having an interest in the property affected by a notice of lis pendens¿may move for expungement¿any time after the lis pendens is recorded.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.30.)¿ A lis pendens must be removed for being improper on account of (a) the¿pleading on which the notice is based¿not containing a real property claim, assuming the allegations to be true, or¿(b) the claimant not being able to establish by a preponderance of the evidence the probable validity of the real property claim. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 405.31, 405.32;¿Ziello v. Superior Court¿(1995) 36 Cal.App.4th¿321, 331–332; Urez Corp. v. Superior Court¿(1987) 190 Cal.App.3d 1141, 1149.)

 

 

Here, Moving Defendants argue that “the central premise of the claims against the Val-Chris Defendants is based on the knowingly false contentions that Val-Chris and the Donahue Trust had any knowledge of the putative intent/agreement of Triebel that the Property was to be acquired for the benefit of Plaintiff … made any misrepresentations to Plaintiff, or owed any duty to Plaintiff.” (Defs.’ Mot. 7:8–12.)

 ¿¿ 

A.    Nature of Claim

 

A real property claim lies where the causes of action, if proven, would affect either: (a) title to or the right to possession of specific real property, or (b) the use of an easement identified in the pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.4.) In determining whether a real property claim has been asserted, the Court engages in a demurrer-like analysis. (Park 100 Investment Group II, LLC v. Ryan¿(2009) 180 Cal.App.4th 795, 808.) “Review ‘involves only a review of the adequacy of the pleading and normally should not involve evidence from either side, other than possibly that which may be judicially noticed as on a demurrer.’” (Kirkeby v. Superior Court (2004) 33 Cal.4th 642, 648.)

 

Here, Moving Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s first, second, third, fourth, seventh, eighth, and eleventh causes of action “do not affect title to or possession of real property but rather seek monetary damages for wrongs supposedly committed against Plaintiff.” (Def.’s Mot. 7:22–24, citing BGJ Associates, LLC v. Superior Court (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 952, 970–972; Campbell v. Superior Court (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 904, 912–922.) Moving Defendants therefore assert that “the only potential real property claims even attempted to be alleged by the Complaint are the Fifth Cause of Action for Quiet Title, the Sixth for Cancellation of Instruments, and the Ninth for Declaratory Relief.” (Defs.’ Mot. 8:6–8.)

 

B.     Probable Validity of Claim

 

When questioning the evidentiary merit of a claim, the claimant who filed the lis pendens has the burden of proof. (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.30.) Thus, that claimant, in opposing the motion to expunge the lis pendens, must demonstrate the following: (1) the action affects title to or right of possession of the real property described in the notice; (2) insofar as the said notice is concerned, the party recording the notice has commenced the action for a proper purpose and in good faith; and (3) the probable validity of the real property claim by a preponderance of the evidence. (Hunting World, 22 Cal.App.4th at 70; Code Civ. Proc., § 405.30 et seq.)

 

“A claim has ‘probable validity’ where it is more likely than not that the plaintiff will obtain a judgment against the defendant on that claim.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 481.190.) Here, Moving Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s fifth, sixth, and ninth causes of action lack probable validity.

 

Here, Moving Defendants argue that “Plaintiff does not allege that she made any actual tender, or satisfied or discharged the debt. This omission is fatal.” (Defs.’ Mot. 8:14–16, citing Lueras v. BAC Homes Loans Servicing, LP (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 49, 86 [“A borrower may not, … quiet title against a secured lender without first paying the outstanding debt on which the mortgage or deed of trust is based.”].) “Here, Plaintiff just attacks the rights of the Val-Chris Defendants under the Senior and Junior deeds of trust against the Property by making wholly unsupported and unsupportable conclusions that their security interests are voidable as a result of supposed fraudulent conduct and breaches of fiduciary duty.” (Id. at 8:20–23.)

 

Moving Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s claims lack probable validity because she fails to allege her fraud claims with the requisite specificity. (Id. at 8:24–9:6.) Moving Defendants further argue that they “were not parties to any agreement between Triebel and Juan Carlos Thola as to the Property and did not know of any agreement between them concerning the Property nor of any agreement to place the Property into a trust at the time the Senior and Junior Loans were made and assigned.” (Id. at 9:17–20.) Furthermore, Moving Defendants assert that “the Complaint is bereft of any factual allegations as to any misappropriation or improper retention of any loan proceeds by any of the Val-Chris Defendants” (Id. at 10:4–6.)

 

The Court notes that Plaintiff has failed to file any opposition to the instant motion. Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that Plaintiff has failed to meet her burden to prove the probable validity of her claims against Moving Defendants. Accordingly, the motion is granted.

 

C.    Service

 

“Immediately following recordation, a copy of the notice [of lis pendens] shall also be filed with the court in which the action is pending. Service shall also be made immediately and in the same manner upon each adverse party later joined in the action.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 405.22.) “Any notice of pendency of action shall be void and invalid as to any adverse party or owner of record unless the requirements of Section 405.22 are met for that party or owner and a proof of service in the form and content specified in Section 1013a has been recorded with the notice of pendency of action.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 405.23.)

 

Here, the Court notes that Plaintiff failed to file any notice of lis pendens with the Court as required by statute. Accordingly, the motion is granted on this additional basis.

 

D.    Attorney Fees

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 405.38 provides that “the court shall direct that the party prevailing on any motion under this chapter be awarded the reasonable attorney’s fees and costs of making or opposing the motion unless the court finds that the other party acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of attorney’s fees and costs unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 405.38.)

 

Here, Moving Defendants seek to recover $10,880.25 in attorney fees and costs against Plaintiff and her attorney, encompassing: (1) 17.9 hours of Moving Defendants’ attorney’s time spent on meet and confer efforts and preparing the instant motion; and (2) an anticipated 5 hours reviewing the opposition, drafting a reply, and appearing at the hearing, at counsel’s hourly rate of $472.50 per hour. (Decl. of Jonathan D. Fink ¶ 5.) Moving Defendants also seek to recover $60.00 in filing fees. (Ibid.)

 

In granting the instant unopposed motion, the Court finds it reasonable to award Moving Defendants $1477.50 in attorney fees and costs.

 

CONCLUSION¿ 

 

The unopposed motion is granted. Moving Defendants are awarded $1477.50 in attorney fees and costs.
