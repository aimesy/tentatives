Case Number: 22AHCV00548    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: X

#22 - PREF PASADENA COLLECTION, LLC vs PASADENA COLLECTION WEST PROPERTY OWNERS' ASSOCIATION

The motion is granted.

In the Statement of Decision, the referee recommended entry of judgment for the Association on all causes of action, found the Association to be the prevailing party under Civil Code section 5975, subdivision (c), and awarded $328,933.10 in attorney fees and $127,019.78 in costs.

Plaintiff PREF Pasadena Collection, LLC opposes and objects to the referee’s statement of decision. The opposition argues the fee-and-cost award conflicts with CC&R section 14.5.1.12, that Civil Code section 5975(c) does not apply because the action was not brought to enforce the governing documents, and that the cost award is insufficiently itemized. (Objs., pp. 3; Opp., pp. 2-3.) Plaintiff requests corrections or clarification before judgment is entered.

This case proceeded as a section 638 general reference on all issues in dispute. According to Code of Civil Procedure section 644, the decision of the referee upon the whole issue must stand as the decision of the court, and upon filing of the statement of decision, judgment may be entered thereon in the same manner as if the action had been tried by the court.

Accordingly, the referee’s decision is not an advisory recommendation that this Court may reweigh or revise on the present motion. (See Michael S. Yu, a Law Corp. v. Superior Court (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 636, fn. 6 [disapproving dictum in In re Marriage of Demblewski (1994) 26 Cal.App.4th 232, 237 that motions may be made to the trial court before judgment is entered on the referee’s decision].)  Here, the Court’s task is to enter judgment consistent with the referee’s decision, not to conduct a review of the referee’s reasoning or the evidentiary basis for the fee and cost award.

Plaintiff’s arguments concerning the CC&Rs, Civil Code section 5975(c), and the sufficiency of the cost showing are preserved by its opposition and objections. But those arguments do not authorize the Court, at this pre-judgment stage, to refuse entry of judgment on a section 638 decision that must stand as the decision of the court. To the extent Plaintiff seeks review of asserted legal error in the referee’s decision, the statutory scheme provides post-judgment avenues for challenge rather than pre-judgment revision of the decision itself. (Yu, supra, 56 Cal.App.5th at p. 647 [“Logically, the language in section 645 about exception and review, namely that a general referee's decision ‘may be excepted to and reviewed in like manner as if made by the court’ (italics added), means that aggrieved parties have the option to challenge the general referee’s decision by bringing a postjudgment motion (see e.g., §§ 663a, 657), or by filing an appeal.”].)

Defendant is to submit a Proposed Judgment for review and entry by the Court on or before September 30, 2026. 

Moving Party is to give notice.

#23 - JAMES COLUCCI vs SABRINA KHAGGI, et al.

Plaintiff moves for further responses to Special Interrogatories, Set Two from Defendant Sabrina Khaggi.

Plaintiff served Special Interrogatories, Set Two, on April 8, 2026.  Plaintiff’s motion states the response deadline passed on May 13, 2026, Defendant later served responses on or about May 29, 2026, and those responses were initially unverified.  Plaintiff thereafter sent a detailed deficiency letter on June 3, 2026, and a further meet-and-confer letter on June 22, 2026, specifically identifying defects in the interrogatory responses and demanding amended responses.  The June 3 letter specifically identified Special Interrogatory Nos. 29, 31, 32, 33, 34, and 35 as requiring corrected amended responses because the answers did not respond to the interrogatories asked.

Defendant later transmitted verifications on June 4, 2026, and Defendant’s opposition contends later supplemental responses rendered the motion unnecessary. However, Plaintiff’s later correspondence states the supplemental responses still did not fully answer at least some of the interrogatories at issue.

The Court finds the meet-and-confer requirement satisfied. The record reflects a May 15 overdue-response letter, a detailed June 3 deficiency letter, a June 22 renewed letter, and follow-up communications by telephone. That showing is sufficient on this record.

The motion is not moot. Defendant’s opposition argues later supplemental responses made further relief unnecessary. However, Plaintiff’s later correspondence states the supplemental responses still failed to identify which previously produced documents were being referenced in response to Special Interrogatory No. 29 and still did not fully state what information Defendant provided to Jordan Midgley in response to Special Interrogatory No. 31.

The Court cannot conclude the motion has been fully resolved by supplemental service. The Court finds further responses are warranted as to Special Interrogatory Nos. 29, 31, 32, 33, 34, and 35.

Special Interrogatory No. 29 sought identification of documents relating to the March 25, 2024 water test, but the response instead discussed whether Defendant was aware of a plumbing leak from her unit.

Special Interrogatories Nos. 31 and 32 asked what information Defendant provided to Midgley, Kingery, and/or any American Leak Detection representative during the January 19, 2026 inspections, but the responses instead invoked lack of technical knowledge or referred generally to contractor work.

Special Interrogatories Nos. 33, 34, and 35 asked whether Defendant informed Midgley and/or Kingery about the March 25, 2024 water test, its findings, and HOA notices or directives, but the cited responses addressed work dates, contractor identities, or construction materials rather than the questions asked.

Those responses are evasive or incomplete responses within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.300(a)(1).  The Court is not persuaded by the objections reflected in the present record.  These interrogatories concern Defendant’s own knowledge, communications, disclosures, and contentions regarding the March 25, 2024 water test, the January 2026 inspections, HOA notices, and claimed causation defenses.

No particularized showing of undue burden sufficient to justify withholding answers appears in the record before the Court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2019.030(a)(2).) Further, the Court finds the initial responses were untimely, and no sufficient showing has been made on this record to relieve Defendant from waiver as to objections to the interrogatories identified above. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(a).)

Accordingly, the further responses to Special Interrogatory Nos. 29, 31, 32, 33, 34, and 35 shall be served without objections.

As to sanctions, Plaintiff seeks $9,647.50.  A monetary sanction is mandatory against a party or attorney who unsuccessfully opposes a motion to compel further responses, absent substantial justification or circumstances making sanctions unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300(d).)  The Court determines that sanctions are warranted. The record reflects untimely initial responses, repeated meet-and-confer efforts, and continuing deficiencies in the interrogatory responses even after supplemental service.  Therefore, the Court awards monetary sanctions in the reduced amount of $3,260.00, payable by Defendant Sabrina Khaggi and/or her counsel of record, jointly and severally, to Plaintiff’s counsel within 30 days of service of notice of ruling.

Moving Party is to give notice.

Case Number: 25NNCV05084    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: X

#27 - MATAN ATIASS vs STEVEN PALLINI, et al.

Defendant Verej Ghoukasian filed an application for determination of good faith settlement between him and Plaintiff. Then, Defendants/Cross-Complainants Steven Pallini and Northeast Graffiti Busters filed a motion contesting that application.

The motion is DENIED and the application is GRANTED.

The record shows a policy-limits settlement and a disputed liability picture. On one hand, the moving parties point to Plaintiff’s claimed damages and to evidence they say shows Ghoukasian was traveling at an excessive speed. On the other, Ghoukasian points to evidence that Pallini was driving the wrong way in the southbound lanes at the time of the collision. Thus, comparative fault is genuinely disputed.

Given the disputed liability evidence and the asserted policy-limits nature of the settlement, the Court cannot conclude that the $100,000 settlement falls so far outside a reasonable range as to establish bad faith.

The moving parties’ reliance on Plaintiff’s claimed damages does not compel a different result because claimed damages alone do not resolve Ghoukasian’s likely share of comparative fault. The papers also do not identify concrete evidence of collusion, fraud, or tortious conduct directed at prejudicing the nonsettling defendants.

In reply, the moving parties alternatively request a continuance to conduct discovery into Ghoukasian’s financial condition if the Court is not inclined to deny the application outright.

The record already includes the settlement amount, the asserted policy limits, the parties’ competing liability positions, and Plaintiff’s claimed damages. On this showing, further delay is not warranted.

In reply, the moving parties also ask the Court to disregard Plaintiff’s joinder as untimely. Because the Court reaches the merits based on the motion alone, it need not resolve that procedural issue.

Verej Ghoukasian’s application for determination of good faith settlement is GRANTED.

The motion by Steven Pallini and Northeast Graffiti Busters contesting Verej Ghoukasian’s application for determination of good faith settlement is DENIED.  The Court finds that the settlement between Plaintiff Matan Atiass and Defendant Verej Ghoukasian, in the amount of $100,000, was made in good faith within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure sections 877 and 877.6.  Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6, all claims against Ghoukasian for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault are barred.

Ghoukasian is to give notice.

Case Number: EC063728    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: X

#25 - SURJIT P. SONI VS CARTOGRAPH, INC.

This action arose from an attorney-client fee dispute that was arbitrated under the Mandatory Fee Arbitration Act through the Los Angeles County Bar Association. 

On August 16, 2021, the Court entered a final judgment awarding Defendants $334,458.41 in fees and costs against Judgment Debtor. Defendants move to amend the August 16, 2021 judgment to add “The Soni Family Trust” as an additional judgment debtor. The motion is supported through the Declaration of Jessica M. Weisel and exhibits that include the judgment, a recorded grant deed, selected pages of the Carmen Ascanio transcript, Morgan Stanley statement pages, a Bates-stamped trust copy, account applications, and a declaration by Weisel stating “I am informed and believe” as to the Morgan Stanley account being closed after a December 2025 transfer.

Soni opposes the motion, files separate evidentiary objections, and submits a certified copy of the full April 8, 2026 examination transcript of Carmen Ascanio. The opposition states that the judgment runs against Surjit P. Soni dba The Soni Law Firm, and that no trust or trustee was named in the operative pleadings or included in the judgment.

Evidentiary Rulings

1) Objection No. 1 challenges Weisel Declaration paragraph 1’s blanket assertion of personal knowledge and asks that the paragraph be limited to counsel’s identity and participation. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Paragraph 1 may establish counsel’s identity and participation, but it does not by itself supply foundation for paragraphs 4 through 10 or Exhibits C through G.

2) Objection No. 2 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 2 and Exhibit A, the August 16, 2021 judgment, and requests that any consideration be limited to proper judicial notice of the court record and its contents.
Ruling: Sustained in part. The Court considers Exhibit A only as a court record and for its existence, date, and contents, not for disputed inferences beyond the record.

3) Objection No. 3 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 3 and Exhibit B, the recorded grant deed, and challenges the phrase that it was signed “just 12 days” after the appellate decision.
Ruling: Sustained in part. Exhibit B may be considered for its face, execution and recording dates, and record-title effect, but not as proof of intent, concealment, or identity.

4) Objection No. 4 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 4 and challenges any use of the Ascanio PMK designation or production event as blanket authentication for the Morgan Stanley records. 
Soni’s declaration states that Ascanio testified she did not assist in producing the subpoenaed documents, did not know whether she had seen them before the examination, and did not know who searched for responsive documents. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Paragraph 4 is limited to the fact that the examination occurred and documents were produced; it does not furnish general authentication or business-record foundation.

5) Objection No. 5 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 5 and Exhibit C, the October 14, 2024 letter, and challenges signature attribution and any assertion that the letter accomplished a transfer. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Exhibit C may be treated only as a document produced by Morgan Stanley; it is not competent on this record to prove that it was signed by the Sonis or that it effected a transfer.

6) Objection No. 6 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 6 and Exhibit D, the selected Ascanio transcript pages, and invokes completeness under Evidence Code section 356 and limits based on personal knowledge and foundation. 
The full transcript submitted by Soni includes testimony that Ascanio had not reviewed key trust materials, did not know who selected the production, and could not authenticate the trust agreement or account-opening records. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Exhibit D will be considered for testimony within Ascanio’s personal knowledge, and the Court considers the connected portions of the complete transcript necessary for context.

7) Objection No. 7 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 7 and Exhibit E, monthly statement pages allegedly reflecting a $100,000 transfer in October 2025, and challenges authentication, hearsay, and business-record foundation. The objections state that Ascanio read the transaction entry but did not know whether the transfer occurred at the account holder’s request. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Exhibit E is not competent on this record to prove who directed the transfer, why it occurred, or what legal significance it carried.

8) Objection No. 8 challenges Weisel Declaration paragraph 8, which states on information and belief that one account closed and another remained frozen. The objections state that Ascanio testified she did not know whether the transferred account was closed.
Ruling: Sustained. 

9) Objection No. 9 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 9 and Exhibit F, described as a true and correct copy of the Soni Family Trust, and challenges any claim that the exhibit is the complete, current, operative trust instrument. The objections state that Ascanio testified she could not authenticate a trust agreement and had not reviewed the relevant trust pages before the examination.
Ruling: Sustained in part. Exhibit F may be considered as a copy produced by Morgan Stanley, not as competent proof that it is the complete, current, operative trust instrument or that no amendment, limitation, or later exercise exists.

10) Objection No. 10 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 10 and Exhibit G, two account applications with checked revocable boxes and electronic signatures, and challenges authentication, signature attribution, and business-record foundation. The objections state that Ascanio did not prepare the applications, did not know who prepared them, and did not know the signatures personally. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Exhibit G is not competent on this record to prove that either settlor executed the forms, that any electronic signature is attributable to either settlor, or that the forms establish the legal scope of any revocation power.

11) Objection No. 11 addresses Weisel Declaration paragraph 11 and the redactions to Exhibits C, F, and G, and seeks to prevent any inference that the redacted copies are complete. 
Ruling: Sustained in part. Sealing or confidentiality treatment is permitted, but the Court draws no completeness inference from the redacted copies.

Tentative Ruling

Defendants’ motion to amend judgment is DENIED.

Legal Authority and Analysis

“Section 187 grants every court the power and authority to carry its jurisdiction into effect. [Citation.] This includes the authority to amend a judgment to add an alter ego of an original judgment debtor, and thereby make the additional judgment debtor liable on the judgment. [Citation.]” (Highland Springs Conference & Training Center v. City of Banning (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 267, 280, internal citations omitted.)

“Amending a judgment to add an alter ego of an original judgment debtor ‘is an equitable procedure based on the theory that the court is not amending the judgment to add a new defendant but is merely inserting the correct name of the real defendant.’ [Citation.]”  (Id., internal citations omitted.)

“Section 187 contemplates amending a judgment by noticed motion. [Citations.]  The court is not required to hold an evidentiary hearing on a motion to amend a judgment, but may rule on the motion based solely on declarations and other written evidence. [Citation.]”  (Id., internal citations omitted.)

“A court may amend its judgment at any time so that the judgment will properly designate the real defendants. . . . Amendment of a judgment to add an alter ego is equitable procedure based on the theory that at the court is not amending the judgment to add new defendant but is merely inserting the correct name of the real defendants.”  (Greenspan v. LADT, LLC (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 486, 508; Hall, Goodhue, Haisley Barker, Inc. v. Marconi Conference Center Board (1996) 41 Cal.App.4th 1551, 1554-1555.) Amending a judgment to add a party as an alter ego “requires both (1) that the new party be the alter ego of the old party and (2) that the new party … controlled the litigation, thereby having had the opportunity to litigate, in order to satisfy due process concerns. The due process considerations are in addition to, not in lieu of, the threshold alter ego issues.” (Wolf Metals Inc. v. Rand Pacific Sales Inc. (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 698, 703.) 

Limited by the foregoing evidentiary rulings, the record establishes, at most, that:

1. a judgment exists against Surjit P. Soni dba The Soni Law Firm;
2. a grant deed transferred title from the Sonis individually to themselves as co-trustees;
3. Morgan Stanley produced certain documents at the Ascanio examination; and
4. certain trust-related and account-related materials were among that production. 

The opposition states that the trust was dated February 10, 2020, while the action began in March 2015, and argues that Defendants submitted no evidence that the Trust retained counsel, paid counsel, selected strategy, controlled settlement, supplied witnesses, or directed the appeal. (Opp., p. 3.) Plaintiff further states that Ascanio did not search for the documents, did not know who did, had never seen the October 14, 2024 letter before the examination, and could not authenticate a trust agreement or personally identify the signatures on the account applications. (Opp., p. 3; Ascanio Transcript, 11:10-17, 44:19-45:16, 15:4-9, 16:4-9.)

Plaintiff also argues that the requested amendment would affect trust-titled assets and potentially the interests of Milena Soni as co-settlor, co-trustee, beneficiary, spouse, and potential owner of community or separate property—although she is not a judgment debtor and was not joined or served in that capacity. (Opp., p. 14.)

Defendants’ showing is insufficient. The present record does not establish that “The Soni Family Trust” was the real defendant in the underlying action, that the trust controlled the litigation in the due process sense required for amendment under section 187, or that the proffered trust and brokerage materials establish the complete operative trust instrument and the extent of any revocation power. Instead, the motion attempts to merge distinct questions of identity, property reach, and post-judgment enforcement into a single amendment order.  Even if some trust property might be reachable in a properly framed enforcement proceeding, the present motion does not justify adding “The Soni Family Trust” itself as a new judgment debtor.

Defendants’ Motion to Amend the Judgment is DENIED without prejudice to any otherwise available, properly noticed post-judgment enforcement proceeding directed to specific property or parties.
