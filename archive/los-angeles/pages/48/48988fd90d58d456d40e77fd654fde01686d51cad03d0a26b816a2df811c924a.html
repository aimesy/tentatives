I.            INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff Xiao Fan Peng (“Plaintiff”) moves for an order permitting her to appear remotely at the trial scheduled for November 8, 2026. The motion is made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 367.75 and CRC 3.672. Plaintiff argues that a remote appearance is necessary because she is a resident of China, currently resides in China, does not possess a visa to enter the United States, and would be subjected to substantial hardship and expense if required to appear in person at trial.

On August 27, 2026, the Court continued the hearing on Plaintiff’s motion to allow for supplemental briefing. Plaintiff filed an additional declaration on September 11, 2026, while Defendant filed one on September 17, 2026.

 

II.          LEGAL STANDARD

Except as otherwise provided by law, upon any party’s motion, the court may conduct a trial through the use of remote technology, absent a showing by the opposing party as to why a remote appearance or testimony should not be allowed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 367.75, subd. (d)(10).) “The court may require a party to appear in person at a proceeding … (1) If the court determines on a hearing-by-hearing basis that an in-person appearance would materially assist in the determination of the proceeding or in the effective management or resolution of the case.” (CRC 3.672, subd. (d)(1); see Code Civ. Proc., § 367.75, subd. (b)(3).)

III.        DISCUSSION

Plaintiff wishes to testify remotely from China on the grounds that she does not possess a visa to enter the United States and that traveling to the United States for travel would cause substantial hardship and expense, including financial expenses and difficulties managing her personal and professional responsibilities.

In opposition, Defendant raises several legitimate questions regarding the legality of providing testimony through video under Article 277 of the Chinese Civil Law (“Article 277”). Article 227 provides that “no foreign authority or individual is allowed to serve process, conduct investigation, or obtain evidence within the borders of People’s Republic of China without permission” from the country’s Central Authority.

In a supplemental declaration filed on September 11, 2026, Plaintiff explains that her re-entry permit into the United States expired on July 26, 2026, she has abandoned her green card, and that her only way to gain entry into the United States is through a B1/B2 visa. Plaintiff states that she applied for a travel permit to Hong Kong and Macau on September 2, 2026. She also applied for a B1/B2 visa on September 4, 2026. Her B1/B2 visa application is currently pending and a consular interview is scheduled for November 12, 2026. Therefore, Plaintiff now requests permission to testify remotely from Hong Kong, specifically a private guest room at the Hong Kong Sky City Marriott Hotel, 1 Sky City Road East, Hong Kong International Airport, Lantau Island. (Supp. Peng Decl., ¶ 13.) She makes numerous representations regarding the technology that will be available and her conduct, while offering to comply with any request to position the camera (or cameras) to ensure that there will be no undisclosed person in the room and that no one will coach, signal, or communicate about the substance of her testimony, and that she will not review any notes, devices, or documents other than exhibits, documents, and materials authorized by the Court. (Id., ¶¶ 16-17.)

In opposition, Defendant argues that the motion should be denied because Plaintiff failed to act diligently. Defendant also argues that remote testimony would materially impair cross-examination because “the jury’s ability to observe Plaintiff’s her full demeanor, body language, and spontaneous reactions would be limited to what a single camera frame captures.” (Stoller Decl., ¶ 9.) Defendant additionally raises questions regarding the competency of Plaintiff’s supplemental declaration because Plaintiff’s declaration is entirely in English while Plaintiff requests a Mandarin-English interpreter and states that Mandarin is her primary language. (Id., ¶ 10.)

Having considered the moving, opposing, reply, and supplemental filings, the Court first emphasizes that the only reason Plaintiff applied for the visa and permit at this late stage appears to be because the Court pointed out at the last hearing, on August 27, 2026, that she stated that she did not have a visa, not that she had made any attempt to obtain one or had been denied one.

Plaintiff filed this case and availed herself of this Court’s jurisdiction nearly five years ago. That at some point along the way Plaintiff took steps that made it difficult for her to return to the United States to take care of her business affairs, and this litigation in particular, means that Plaintiff has placed herself in this position of unavailability. On the other hand, Defendant’s insistence that Plaintiff appear in person, while refusing to agree to continue the trial date, places the Court in an impossible position.

The examination of witnesses is more effective and compelling to the jury if the witnesses appear in person, particularly when the witness is a party, in this case the Plaintiff. Testimonial credibility appears important in this case inasmuch as it is not a predominantly “paper” case. Thus, since Plaintiff’s consular interview is scheduled for November 12, 2026, in Beijing, Plaintiff’s counsel can inform the Court as to the status of Plaintiff’s visa application on the same day given the time difference. The Court therefore continues the hearing on this motion to November 12, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. and will set the trial date on that date. The final status conference remains as currently scheduled and the Court expects that the parties will have complied with the jury trial preparation order to the letter so that trial may commence not later than November 16, 2026. The trial date related discovery deadlines are not continued and remain related to the November 2, 2026, trial date.

IV.        CONCLUSION

The hearing on this motion is CONTINUED to November 12, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

The November 2, 2026, trial date is vacated and the Court will set a new trial date on November 12, 2026.

Plaintiff to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO COMPEL PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS RESPONSIVE TO BUSINESS RECORDS SUBPOENA

 

Dept. 3

)

 

 

At issue in this motion are defendants County of Los Angeles and Deputy Van P. Lam’s (collectively, “Defendants”) subpoenas to the Department of Motor Vehicles (“DMV”), to Plaintiff’s prior employers, and to Mercury Insurance for her insurance records. Plaintiff, not the subpoenaed entities, object to these subpoenas. Defendants argue that DMV records are relevant because they concern the motor vehicle collision at issue, including statements made by Plaintiff, accident-related reporting, licensing information, and other information bearing on liability, causation, and damages such as prior accidents, claimed injuries, and prior license suspensions or restrictions. (Motion, p. 1.) Defendant’s further argue that Plaintiff's employment records are relevant for her wage loss, earning capacity, inability to work claims. (Motion, p. 1.) Last, as to her car insurance records, Defendants assert that these are relevant to prior injury claims, prior accidents, statements made regarding this incident, as well as causation and damages. (Motion, p. 1.) Plaintiff did not file a motion to quash but only filed a general objection. The subpoenas sought the production of records on May 4, 2026, and on April 22, 2026, Plaintiff served a general objection. Plaintiff did not file a motion to quash. After some discussion, Defendants agreed to limit the scope of their medical subpoenas, but the parties were unable to reach an agreement as to the subpoenas at issue in this motion.

On May 13 and 14, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel asserted that the deposition officer could no longer seek documents pursuant to the subpoenas because the date of production had passed. Plaintiff claimed (and still contends) that a new “notice” was required after the production date expired. Plaintiff threatened litigation against the deposition officer and the deposition officer stated that it would hold the disputed records pending an agreement of the parties or a court order.

Plaintiff fails to seriously advance any substantive arguments regarding the merits of the subpoenas, even failing to serve a response to Defendants’ separate statement. Instead, Plaintiff chooses to stand on its meritless procedural claims by requesting the Court deny the motion as untimely and require Defendants to serve another notice to consumer or notice to employee and issue an additional subpoena. (Opp., pp. 2, 5 [“Plaintiff’s biggest issue is with Defendant’s copy service, Ronsin, [who] continued to actively contact third-parties to obtain employment and personal records….]) However, Plaintiff cites to no authority holding that a subpoena’s validity is tethered to the stated date of production. Furthermore, Plaintiff’s threats of litigation against the deposition officer are improper because Plaintiff did not file a motion to quash but only filed a general objection. As a party to this action, it was Plaintiff’s burden to file a motion to quash pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1 before the production date.

Plaintiff also contends that this motion is untimely because it was not filed within 20 days under Code of Civil Procedure section 1985.3(g) and 1985.6(f). However, these statutes apply to motions that are brought in response to an objection raised by nonparty employees/consumers, which she is not.

Accordingly, the motion to compel compliance is GRANTED and Ronsin is ordered to release the records produced in response to Defendant’s subpoenas to the DMV, Mercury Insurance, LSC Communications MCL LLC and LSC Communications US LLC, Nu Way Builders, and D’S Hair Salon.

I.            INTRODUCTION

On September 18, 2025, plaintiff David I. Pankonin (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Anna Tan and Tim Lane. In brief, Plaintiff alleges that he was defrauded by Anna Tan and induced into depositing money into “EC Markets”, a fake trading platform.

On July 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a proof of service of summons stating that on July 7, 2026, a copy of the summons and complaint was left with Tony Zhang at 2040 Courtland Avenue in San Marino, California. The party served was “Anna Tan.”  

On August 13, 2026, Qingru She aka Shirley She (“Ms. She”) filed this motion to quash. Ms. She’s husband, Mr. Zhang, states that the process server specifically sought “Shirley” and referred to “Shirley AKA Anna.” (Motion, Zhang Decl., ¶ 6.) Ms. She argues that service was improper because, in effect, Ms. She is not “Anna Tan” and her photos and personal information have been misappropriated to create fraudulent identities, presumably such as “Anna Tan”, which she has never used in any capacity.  

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Ms. She and Anna Tan are the same individual and that a subpoena has been served on Wells Fargo to produce bank records. Plaintiff contends the bank records will show that Ms. She controlled the bank accounts that he transferred money into. Plaintiff requests the Court continue the hearing on this Motion for 90 days to bring a motion to compel Wells Fargo’s compliance with the subpoena as Ms. She objected to the subpoena. (Motion, p. 2.)

To the extent that Plaintiff intended to sue and serve Qingru She as having used the alias Anna Tan, service was improper because Ms. She’s name, either her formal legal name of Qingru She or English name of Shirley She, does not appear anywhere in the summons, Complaint, or the proof of service of summons. The summons must be “directed to the defendant” and must contain “[t]he names of the parties to the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., 412.20(a).) “The defendant should be sued in his or her true name, if known, even where a writing or other instrument on which the complaint is founded contains a different or erroneously spelled name. (4 Witkin, Cal. Proc. 6th Plead § 489 (2026).) Thus, whether Wells Fargo can produce documents relating to Anna Tan is not dispositive of this motion because Qingru She has not been named in this action.

Accordingly, the service of summons effected on July 7, 2026, is quashed as ineffective on Qingru She because no such party has been named in this action.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO COMPEL DISCOVERY RESPONSE

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

United Financial Casualty Company (“UFCC”) is the respondent in an uninsured motorist insurance arbitration initiated by claimants Arsamak Oziev, Iza Ozieva, Amalia Ozieva, Adeliya Ozieva, Salm Ozieva, Aila Ozieva, and Safina Ozieva (collectively, “Claimants”).

The superior court has exclusive jurisdiction over discovery disputes in uninsured motorist arbitrations. (Miranda v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 913, 927.) UFCC moves for an order compelling Claimants’ responses to Demand for Production of Documents (Set One), without objections, and produce responsive documents. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300(b).)

The motion is unopposed and it is undisputed that no responses have been served by any of the Ozieva Claimants. Accordingly, the motion to compel responses is GRANTED and each of the Ozieva Claimants are ordered to serve verified responses, without objections, to UFCC’s Demand for Production of Documents (Set One), and produce all responsive nonprivileged documents within 20 days of the date of this Order.

UFCC to give notice.
