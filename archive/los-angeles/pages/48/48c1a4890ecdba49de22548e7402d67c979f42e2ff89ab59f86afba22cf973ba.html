REVIEW OF DEFAULT JUDGMENT PACKAGE

AND RELATED CROSS-ACTION

           

            Background

Cross-Complainant Pai Sung Lin (“Lin”) requests default judgment against Cross-Defendants Chen Wang and Zoe Chow aka Zoe Chow Szwman (“Chow”) (jointly “Defendants”), in the total amount of $539,297.96, comprising $230,000.00 demanded in the complaint, $201,758.96 in interest, and $107,539.00 in attorney fees.

Lin explains that “[o]n February 29 and 29, 2024, trial was held on Plaintiff’s claims against Lin. The Court found that Plaintiff’s signature on Lin’s promissory note and Deed of Trust were forged and that Plaintiff had timely filed her lawsuit seeking to set aside Lin’s loan. Lin’s Deed of Trust was found invalid and unenforceable as against Plaintiff’s residence.” (Memo., 2:11-14; see Statement of Decision p. 2, ¶ 7 [“Wang and Defendant Zoe Chow a.k.a. Zoe Chow Sizeman (‘Chow’) forged Zhao’s name on the Note Secured by Deed of Trust.”].) Additionally, the Court stated, “[t]he parties have stipulated that Zhao’s signature on the Deed of Trust was forged. (Stipulated Facts Nos. 7 and 11.) Therefore, the document is void. … Consequently, the Court finds that Lin’s Deed of Trust is not valid or enforceable against the Property.” (Statement of Decision, 7:4-12.) The Court ordered Lin to “file default judgment packages as to the defaulted defendants and cross-defendants.” (Statement of Decision, 12:26-27.)[1]

Lin asserts that, “[i]n the present matter, Lin presents evidence that Wang and Chow knowingly entered into an agreement to forge Plaintiff’s signature on the Note Secured by Deed of Trust and Deed of Trust so that Wang could obtain a loan in the amount of $230,000.00 from Lin without Plaintiff’s knowledge or consent. Lin further presents evidence that he funded the loan in reliance on the executed Note Secured by Deed of Trust and Deed of Trust. Lin additionally presents evidence that he would not have funded the loan for $230,000.00 has [sic] he known that the documents were forged. Finally, Lin presents evidence that he is owed $431,758.96 on the loan and is owed $107,539.00 in attorney’s fees and costs because he was required to defend the validity and enforceability of the Note Secured by Deed of Trust and Deed of Trust.” (Memo., 3:24-4:5.)

Request for Judicial Notice

The Court grants Lin’s request for judicial notice of Exhibits 7 and 8.

Discussion

Previously, the Court denied the request for default judgment, in part, due to an insufficient declaration of non-military status. Lin’s counsel filed a declaration attesting to counsel’s inability to ascertain Chow’s military status. Lin’s counsel explains, inter alia, that “to determine Chow’s military status under the SCRA my office needs to posses [sic] either her Social Security Number or her date of birth. We have neither for several reasons.” (Beall Decl., ¶ 4.) “First, Lin has never met Chow and has no personal knowledge of her Social Security Number or a date of birth.” (Beall Decl., ¶ 5.) “Second, Chow failed to file an appearance in this litigation and, as such, Lin was unable to conduct discovery upon her seeking her Social Security Number or a date of birth.” (Beall Decl., ¶ 6.) “Third, my office has researched all of the documents obtained through discovery from Plaintiff in this case (escrow documents and loan documents) and was unable to locate any information for Chow including her Social Security Number or a date of birth.” (Beall Decl., ¶ 7.) “Fourth, my office has conducted research with First Amended Datea Tree (a real property database), Westlaw People Map (a public records database) and conducted index searches of the Los Angeles County Superior Court records and PACER records and was unable to locate any information for Chow including her Social Security Number or a date of birth.” (Beall Decl., ¶ 8.) “Fifth, my office has conducted internet research of LinkedIn and Google and was unable to locate any information for Chow including her Social Security Number or a date of birth.” (Beall Decl., ¶ 9.) The Court finds that Lin’s counsel’s declaration testifies to her diligence in attempting to confirm Chow’s nonmilitary status. Accordingly, this defect is cured.

Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that Lin sufficiently supported the request with the submitted evidence and declarations. Accordingly, the Court grants Lin’s request and will sign the judgment.

No appearance is necessary at the hearing on this matter.

ORDER RE:

 

PLAINTIFF SILVER VISION TEXTILES, INC.’S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT, OR IN THE ALTERNATIVE FOR SUMMARY ADJUDICATION

           

The motion for summary judgment or, in the alternative, summary adjudication brought by Plaintiff Silver Vision Textiles, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) against Defendant Newport Apparel Corporation d/b/a Green Envelope is continued as set forth below.

Plaintiff interposed a total of 11 evidentiary objections. Due to the number of objections, the hearing on the motion will be continued to a date that will be set at the Hearing on Objections discussed below.

The Court orders the parties to meet and confer by telephone or in person in a serious and good faith effort to resolve and eliminate the objections. The only objections that should remain are those that pertain to material evidence regarding material issues. Keeping the rules of evidence in mind, the parties should be able to reduce the objections to just a few or none. If any material objections remain unresolved, the parties are to set them forth in a joint statement with the text, the objection, and the argument of each side in favor of their respective positions regarding the remaining material objections, along with a space for a ruling.

The joint statement must be filed on or before _______ with a courtesy copy delivered to Department 508. The Court will review any remaining objections with the parties at a hearing on _______________, 2026 at 2:00 p.m. (the “Hearing on Objections”). The date for the hearing on the motion will be set at the Hearing on Objections.

If necessary, based upon the resolutions reached during the meet and confer process and/or at the Hearing on Objections, the parties may respectively file and serve revised briefing and evidence. The revised evidence may eliminate objectionable material; however, no new evidence or new argument is to be submitted unless it is as a result of compromises reached during the meet and confer process. In the event that revised briefing and evidence are necessary, the Court will discuss with the parties a briefing schedule for the revised briefing at the Hearing on Objections.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this Order.
