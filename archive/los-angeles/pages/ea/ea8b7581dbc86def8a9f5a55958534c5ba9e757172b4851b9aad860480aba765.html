Case Number: 20STCV47014    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: T

Ibrahim Mikhail, et al. vs Hanif Hirji

Motion to Compel Plaintiff to Post Appeal Bond

Moving Party:                         Defendant Hanif Hirji

Responding Party:                   Plaintiff Ibrahim Mikhail

Tentative Ruling:                    Deny

BACKGROUND

This is a motor vehicle and general negligence action brought by Plaintiffs Ibrahim Mikhail and Maria Mikhail against Defendant Hanif Hirji (“Defendant”).

On October 6, 2025, the case was called for a jury trial. On October 14, 2025, the jury rendered a special verdict finding that Defendant was negligent and that Defendant’s negligence was not a substantial factor in causing harm to Plaintiff Ibrahim Mikhail (“Plaintiff”). On November 12, 2025, by reason of this special verdict, the Court entered judgment in favor of Defendant.

On February 19, 2026, following the Court’s denial of Plaintiff’s motion for new trial and motion for judgment notwithstanding the verdict, Plaintiff filed a notice of appeal.

Defendant now moves for an order compelling Plaintiff to post an appeal bond.

Plaintiff opposes, and Defendant replies.

MOVING PARTY POSITION

            Defendant argues that Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1(a)(2) empowers the Court to require an appeal bond for costs awarded pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 998.  In addition, the Court has discretionary power to require the party seeking appeal to post a bond while an appeal is pending under Code of Civil Procedure section 917.9.

            Defendant further argues that the Court should compel Plaintiff to post an appeal bond in order to protect Defendant’s ability to collect the judgment from Plaintiff and that the motion should be granted to eliminate the substantial risk of the judgment becoming uncollectable.

OPPOSITION

            Plaintiff contends that Section 917.1 does not authorize the Court to compel Plaintiff to post a bond but instead governs only whether enforcement is stayed.  Plaintiff further contends that Section 917.9 provides no basis for the requested order because defendant offers no evidence of risk to collectability.  Plaintiff also contends that, even if the Court concludes that an undertaking is relevant to some portion of the cost award, Defendant failed to demonstrate what amount should be fixed for this undertaking.  In addition, Defendant’s authorities do not support compelling a bond where no stay is sought, and no evidence of dissipation is offered.

REPLY

            Defendant responds that because Defendant’s judgment includes the award of costs for Code of Civil Procedure section 998 expert witness fees, he is entitled to a bond that at least covers the expert witness fees under Gallardo v. Specialty Restaurants Corp. (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 463.  In addition, this Court has discretionary power to impose a bond for costs only judgments, which the Court should exercise to mandate the posting of an undertaking for Section 998 costs. 

            Defendant further responds that the Court should require a bond for the entire judgment, as the Court of Appeal in Quiles v. Parent (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 130 suggested in dicta that the Legislature should require a bond for any award of costs that exceed $50,000 or $100,000.  Plaintiff is also free to post a bond with a surety company and is not claiming any indigence as a basis for seeking relief from this bond requirement.

            Defendant finally responds that Plaintiff’s authority is not binding or applicable, as one of the cited cases does not appear to exist.

ANALYSIS

I.          Legal Standard

            A.        Automatic Stay

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 916(a), and “[e]xcept as provided in Sections 917.1 to 917.10, inclusive, and in Section 116.810, the perfecting of an appeal stays proceedings in the trial court upon the judgment or order appealed from or upon the matters embraced therein or affected thereby, including enforcement of the judgment or order, but the trial court may proceed upon any other matter embraced in the action and not affected by the judgment or order.” 

            In specifying some of the exceptions to this automatic stay rule, Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1(a) provides that, “[u]nless an undertaking is given, the perfecting of an appeal shall not stay enforcement of the judgment or order in the trial court if the judgment or order is for any of the following:

(1) Money or the payment of money, whether consisting of a special fund or not, and whether payable by the appellant or another party to the action.

(2) Costs awarded pursuant to Section 998 which otherwise would not have been awarded as costs pursuant to Section 1033.5.

(3) Costs awarded pursuant to Section 1141.21 which otherwise would not have been awarded as costs pursuant to Section 1033.5.”

            “The undertaking shall be for double the amount of the judgment or order unless given by an admitted surety insurer in which event it shall be for one and one-half times the amount of the judgment or order. The liability on the undertaking may be enforced if the party ordered to pay does not make the payment within 30 days after the filing of the remittitur from the reviewing court.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 917.1, subd. (b).)  

            “Costs awarded by the trial court under Chapter 6 (commencing with Section 1021) of Title 14 shall be included in the amount of the judgment or order for the purpose of applying paragraph (1) of subdivision (a) and subdivision (b). However, no undertaking shall be required pursuant to this section solely for costs awarded under Chapter 6 (commencing with Section 1021) of Title 14.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 917.1, subd. (d).)   “A cost is a cost, unless specifically excepted in section 917.1, subdivision (a).”  (Quiles v. Parent (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 130, 148.)  

B.        Discretionary Stay

            Under Code of Civil Procedure section 917.9(a), “[t]he perfecting of an appeal shall not stay enforcement of the judgment or order in cases not provided for in Sections 917.1 to 917.8, inclusive, if the trial court, in its discretion, requires an undertaking and the undertaking is not given, in any of the following cases:

(1) Appellant was found to possess money or other property belonging to respondent.

(2) Appellant is required to perform an act for respondent’s benefit pursuant to judgment or order under appeal.

(3) The judgment against appellant is solely for costs awarded to the respondent by the trial court pursuant to Chapter 6 (commencing with Section 1021) of Title 14.”

II.        Discussion

            At the outset, the Court notes that neither party directly addresses the threshold question of whether Plaintiff’s appeal of the underlying judgment for Defendant’s recovery of his costs under Code of Civil Procedure section 998 automatically stayed its enforcement.  On this question, the Court finds the Court of Appeal’s analysis in Quiles, supra, 10 Cal.App.5th at pp. 143–144, to be instructive: “But it is now clear that an expert witness fee award under section 998 is not, on its own, a money judgment under section 917.1, subdivision (a)(1). Instead, it resides in a separate category of judgments and orders that require a bond for a stay on appeal. [Citation.]” 

            Accordingly, as Defendant sought recovery of his expert witness fees under Section 998, and as the Court found that $210,718.00 of these fees were recoverable by Defendant in ruling on Plaintiff’s motion to tax costs, Plaintiff’s appeal of the underlying judgment did not automatically stay enforcement of these fees under Section 917.1(a)(2).  Rather, this section requires the making of an undertaking “for the portion of the judgment consisting of section 998 expert witness costs, but not for the section 1032 routine costs.” (Gallardo v. Specialty Restaurants Corp. (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 463, 468; accord. Quiles, supra, 10 Cal.App.5th at p. 137, fn. 3 [noting that the Court of Appeal in Gallardo correctly held that the undertaking amount should be calculated based solely on the expert witness fee awarded given Section 917.1(d)’s express application to only paragraph (1) of subdivision (a).].)  Given this statutory requirement, the Court finds that Plaintiff must make an undertaking to stay enforcement of the judgment awarding expert witness fees to Defendant.

            However, as Defendant does not appear to have taken any steps to enforce this portion of the judgment against Plaintiff, and as Plaintiff does not appear to have taken any affirmative steps to stay its enforcement, Defendant fails to provide any factual or legal basis for his requested order compelling Plaintiff to make this undertaking within 30 days of the hearing.  (Compare Bank of San Pedro v. Superior Court (1992) 3 Cal.4th 797, 799 [the real-party-interest opposed the bank’s motion for issuance of a writ of execution on “the ground that enforcement of the judgment was automatically stayed by his appeal of the order awarding costs under section 998”]; Quiles, supra, 10 Cal.App.5th at p. 135 [the defendant applied ex parte to stay enforcement of the judgment pending appeal of the attorney fee and cost awards in the amended judgment]; Gallardo, supra, 84 Cal.App.4th at p. 466 [the plaintiff filed a writ of supersedeas to stay the writ of execution for the awarded expert witness fees].)  Although Section 917.1(a) requires an undertaking, this section does not empower a trial court to compel the making of an undertaking where no stay has been sought.  Nor does this section empower a trial court to make this order based solely on the judgment debtor’s perfecting of an appeal.  (Compare Bank of San Pedro, supra, 3 Cal. 4th at p. 799 [“The sole issue in this case is whether enforcement of the award of expert witness fees is automatically stayed by operation of law while the judgment is being appealed by plaintiff. We hold such a judgment is not automatically stayed and that the appellant must post an appeal bond or other sufficient undertaking to obtain a stay pending appeal.”].) 

            Nor has either party provided the Court with sufficient evidence to fix the amount required for this undertaking under Section 917.1(b), as the proper calculation of this amount based on the awarded expert witness fees of $210,718.00 is either double or one and one-half times these fees, depending on whether the undertaking shall be given by an admitted surety insurer.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 917.1, subd. (b).)  

            Moreover, to the extent that Defendant asks the Court to exercise of its discretion under Section 917.9 to require that Plaintiff make an undertaking for the remaining Section 998 costs awarded in the amended judgment, the Court finds that Defendant has failed to demonstrate the probable risk of damage in the absence of a bond or undertaking pending appeal.  (Quiles, supra, 10 Cal.App.5th at p. 145 [“In a case where the costs judgment is large or the danger of asset dissipation is acute, a trial court can mitigate any injustices arising from the costs-only judgment rule.”].)  Because there is no evidenced basis for the provision of this discretionary relief as to the $32,655.72 in remaining costs awarded, and as a stay of enforcement of these awarded costs does not otherwise require an undertaking given Section 917.1(d)’s express language, the Court declines to impose an undertaking requirement for such costs at this time,

            Therefore, as Defendant seeks to compel the posting of a bond where no stay has been sought, and as no evidence has been submitted for the Court to fix the bond amount of the required undertaking for any stay of Section 998 expert witness fee award, nor for the Court to impose an undertaking requirement on the remaining Section 998 costs awarded to Defendant, the motion is denied without prejudice.    

II.        Errant Citation

Notedly, Plaintiff’s opposition contains an errant citation to Hein v. Highlands Insurance Co. (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 1510 for the following proposition: “The moving party bears the burden of establishing entitlement to the relief sought.”  Although a case bearing a similar title, Hein v. Highlands Ins. Co. (1976) 64 Cal.App.3d 627, does exist, the discussion therein does not appear to support this proposition.  Given the fact that well-established case law does, in fact, support this contention, the Court does not conclude that Plaintiff intended to mislead the Court.  That said, counsel is reminded that, “[s]imply stated, no brief, pleading, motion, or any other paper filed in any court should contain any citations— whether provided by generative AI or any other source—that the attorney responsible for submitting the pleading has not personally read and verified.” (Noland v. Land of the Free, L.P. (2025) 114 Cal.App.5th 426, 431.)

RULING

Defendant Hanif Hirji’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff to Post Appeal Bond is DENIED without prejudice.


Case Number: 26NNCV01868    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: T

Janik Tsaturyan vs. Tesla Motors, Inc., a Delaware Corporation

Motion for Attorney’s Fees and Costs

Moving Party:                         Plaintiff Janik Tsaturyan

Responding Party:                   Defendant Tesla Motors, Inc.

Tentative Ruling:                    Grant in part

 

BACKGROUND

On March 13, 2026, Plaintiff Janik Tsaturyan (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendant Tesla Motors, Inc. (“Defendant”), alleging various causes of action under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.

On June 11, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of settlement in which Plaintiff informed the Court of the conditional settlement of the entire case and stated that a request for dismissal will be filed no later than August 7, 2026.   

Plaintiff now moves to recover reasonable attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses in the total amount of $16,024.74.

Defendant opposes.  No reply was filed.

MOVING PARTY POSITION

            Plaintiff argues that he is entitled to recover his reasonable attorney’s fees and costs as the prevailing buyer in the action under Civil Code section 1794(d).  Plaintiff argues that the lodestar method should be applied in assessing the reasonableness of his counsel’s fees.  In applying the lodestar method, his counsel’s fees are reasonable because the hourly rates charged by attorneys Raffi Sarukhanyan and Sarkis Terterian are consistent with other attorneys litigating consumer matters in this area of law.  Plaintiff also argues that he is entitled to recover the fees and costs incurred in preparing the instant motion.

            Plaintiff finally argues that he is entitled to recover reasonable costs and litigation expenses under Section 1794(d).

            In sum, Plaintiff requests that the Court grant the motion and award $16,024.74 in total fees and costs, which are comprised of the following amounts: (1) $11,460.00 in attorney’s fees; (2) $564.74 in recoverable costs and expenses; and (3) $4,000.00 in anticipated fees for the instant motion.

OPPOSITION

            Defendant contends that Plaintiff’s fee demand is unreasonable based on the prevailing rate for noncontingent work in the community of lemon law litigation and given the relative lack of complexity in this practice area.  The hourly rates sought by counsel are not anchored to market reality, and the cited Laffey Matrix rates are inapplicable to lemon law litigation in California.  Defendant further contends that its counsel’s rate is the superior market evidence for noncontingent work and that recent court and arbitration decisions corroborate this lower market rate.  

            Defendant also contends that Plaintiff’s counsel’s total hours of work warrant reduction due to excessive or improper billing entries, including but not limited to the following proposed reductions: (1) 1.5 hours from hours of work attributed to preparation of a demand letter based on a template, amounting to $750.00; (2) 1.4 hours from hours of work attributed to complaint and supporting document preparation, amounting to $700.00; (3) 3.7 total hours of improperly billed clerical work by Plaintiff’s counsel and his paralegal before the contractual cut-off date, amounting to $630.50; (4) 7.95 total hours of work billed after the contractual cut-off date, amounting to $3,445.00; and (5) the entire $4,000.00 amount in anticipated fee motion fees sought by Plaintiff. 

            Defendant does not contest any of the costs.

REPLY

            No reply was filed.

ANALYSIS

I.          Legal Standard

Civil Code section 1794(d) provides that a buyer who prevails in an action under that section, “shall be allowed by the court to recover as a part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and the prosecution of such action.”

II.        Discussion

A.                Entitlement to Attorneys’ Fees

At the outset, the Court notes that no order or judgment of dismissal has been entered in this action.  “[A] dismissal or judgment in favor of the party seeking fees is a predicate to a fee award.”  (Hatlevig v. General Motors LLC (2026) 118 Cal.App.5th 644, 650; see, also, Berti v. Santa Barbara Beach Properties (2006) 145 Cal.App.4th 70, 75 [“It is well settled that statutory costs and attorney fees are incident to the judgment.”].) 

However, the parties do not dispute that a conditional settlement was reached, or that the settlement provided for Plaintiff’s recovery of attorney’s fees and costs.  Moreover, because the term “judgment” as used in Civil Code section 1794(d) embraces final dispositions “recognized in other contexts as tantamount or equivalent to a judgment for purposes of Code of Civil Procedure section 998 and/or for allowing the trial court to determine the prevailing party and to award costs and fees under fee-shifting statutes,” the undisputed existence of the settlement provides the necessary predicate for the determination of the prevailing buyer and for the award of reasonable fees and costs.  (Wohlgemuth v. Caterpillar Inc. (2012) 207 Cal.App.4th 1252, 1263; see, e.g., Goodstein v. Bank of San Pedro (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 899, 907 [reasoning that “a compromise agreement contemplating payment by defendant and dismissal of the action by plaintiff is the legal equivalent of a judgment in plaintiff's favor”].) 

            Thus, the Court finds that Plaintiff is the prevailing party and is entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees and costs pursuant to Civil Code section 1794(d), subject to the contractually agreed upon limitations set forth in the express terms of the settlement agreement.  Therefore, Plaintiff’s motion is proper.

            B.        Reasonableness of Fee Amount

            Plaintiff seeks to recover $15,460.00 in attorney’s fees.

“[A]bsent circumstances rendering the award unjust, an attorney fee award should ordinarily include compensation for all the hours reasonably spent, including those relating solely to the fee. We explained that the purpose behind statutory fee authorizations—i.e., encouraging attorneys to act as private attorneys general and to vindicate important rights affecting the public interest—will often be frustrated, sometimes nullified, if awards are diluted or dissipated by lengthy, uncompensated proceedings to fix or defend a rightful fee claim.”  (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1133-1124, emphasis in original.)  “The fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate. … The lodestar figure may then be adjusted, based on consideration of factors specific to the case, in order to fix the fee at the fair market value for the legal services provided.”  (Id. at p. 1134.) 

Therefore, the Court will consider the reasonableness of the $15,460.00 fee amount sought by Plaintiff through application of the “lodestar” method.

1.      Reasonableness of Hourly Rate 

In applying the lodestar method, the Court begins by determining the ‘reasonable hourly rate,’ i.e., the rate ‘prevailing in the community for similar work.’” (Marshall v. Webster (2020) 54 Cal.App.5th 275, 285 [quoting PLCM Group, Inc. v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095].) Under the general rule, the “relevant ‘community’ is that where the court is located.” (Marshall, supra, 54 Cal.App.5th at p. 285 [quoting Altavion, Inc. v. Konica Minolta Systems Laboratory, Inc. (2014) 226 Cal.App.4th 26, 71].) 

Here, Plaintiff’s counsel, attorneys Raffi Sarukhanyan and Sarkis Terterian of Sarukhanyan Law, APC, provided legal services on contingency for Plaintiff in the instant action at billable rates of $500.00 and $300.00 per hour, respectively.  (Sarukhanyan Decl. ¶¶ 1, 30–32; id. ¶ 32, Exh. A (“Billing Records”); Terterian Decl. ¶¶ 1, 30–32.)  In support of their respective rates, attorneys Sarukhanyan and Terterian described their experience and background in this area of consumer protection la.   (Sarukhanyan Decl. ¶¶ 28-30; Terterian Decl. ¶¶ 28-30.)  In addition, the attorneys cited the Laffey Matrix as an example of the reasonableness of these rates.  (Ibid.) 

Although Defendant contends that these rates are not commensurate with the market and the noncontingent rates charged by other attorneys in the area of lemon law litigation, including its counsel’s noncontingent rate and the market rates set forth in the exhibited court and arbitration decisions, the Court finds that, based on the evidence and given the contingent nature of the litigation, counsel’s hourly rates of $500.00 and $300.00 are reasonable and commensurate with rates charged by attorneys with comparable skills and experience in their community of practice.

Thus, the Court finds these hourly rates to be reasonable.

2.      Reasonableness of Hours Expended

Padding “in the form of inefficient or duplicative efforts is not subject to compensation.”  (Premier Medical Management Systems, Inc. v. California Insurance Guarantee Association (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 556.)  However, parties opposing motions for attorneys’ fees fail to establish an abuse of discretion where they merely contend that amounts of attorneys’ fees are excessive without providing an analysis or factual support.  (Maughan v. Google Technology, Inc. (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 1242, 1250; Avikian v. Wtc Fin. Corp. (2002) 98 Cal.App.4th 1108, 1119; see, also, Premier Medical, supra, 163 Cal.App.4th at p. 560 [emphasizing that opposing parties “submitted no evidence that the hours claimed by counsel were excessive,” and declining to “declare as a matter of law that the hours were unreasonable”]; see, e.g., Villanueva v. City of Colton (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 1188, 1204 [opposing party “offered no evidence of any kind which might have warranted a reduced fee award.”].)

As an initial matter, the Court finds that the attorney’s fees sought by Plaintiff for work conducted by his counsel after the settlement agreement was executed by the parties on May 22, 2026, are not recoverable.  Under the express terms of the agreement, Defendant agreed to provide, in relevant part, the following consideration to Plaintiff:

Tesla shall pay Releasing Parties’ attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses to date that have been reasonably incurred in an amount to be determined by the Court, by way of a single noticed motion, unless the Parties can agree on the amount of fees to be paid absent such a motion. For purposes of such motion only, Tesla agrees that Releasing Parties are the prevailing parties. Any Party may disclose the financial terms of this Settlement to the Court in connection with the motion for attorneys’ fees.

(Hooper Decl. ¶ 12, Exh. F, at p. 2 of 6.)  Under these terms, Defendant is only obligated to pay Plaintiff’s attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses that were accrued to date, which reflects the mutual intention of the parties to contractually limit the amount of recoverable attorneys’ fees to only those fees accrued on or before May 22, 2026.  (Wohlgemuth, supra, 207 Cal.App.4th at p. 1263, fn. 10 [“Of course, parties are free to expressly waive attorney fees and costs as part of a compromise agreement.”]; Civ. Code § 1636 [“A contract must be so interpreted as to give effect to the mutual intention of the parties as it existed at the time of contracting, so far as the same is ascertainable and lawful.”]; id. § 1641 [“The whole of a contract is to be taken together, so as to give effect to every part, if reasonably practicable, each clause helping to interpret the other.”].)  Thus, the Court strikes in their entirety the billed and anticipated hours of work accrued after May 22, 2026, resulting in a total reduction of $7,445.00.  (See Billing Records; Mot., at p. 8.) 

            As to the remaining categories of billed time challenged by Defendant in its opposition, including entries that fall within these categories and others not specified therein that are facially unreasonable, the Court finds that counsel’s and their office manager’s billed clerical work was not reasonably incurred and thereby strikes these fees, resulting in a total reduction of $645.00.  (See Billing Records)  The Court further finds that the exhibited similarities between the templated versions of the complaint and demand letter evidence an excessiveness in the time attributed by counsel for their preparation and thus warrant a reasonable reduction of 2 hours from the total time attributed by attorney Sarukhanyan to their preparation, resulting in a total reduction of $1,000.00.  (Ibid.)  The Court also finds that the hours attributed by Terterian to his settlement emails are facially excessive and not reasonably incurred, thus warranting a reasonable reduction of $240.00 based on 0.8 hours.  (Ibid.) 

            The Court otherwise finds that Plaintiff’s counsel’s billing entries reflect hours of work that were reasonably expended.

Thus, the request for attorneys’ fees is granted in the reduced amount of $6,130.00.

C.        Costs and Expenses

Plaintiff seeks to recover $564.74 in costs and expenses.  As Defendant does not challenge any of the costs and expenses sought by Plaintiff, the Court shall award the specified costs and expenses in Plaintiff’s memorandum of costs in full. 

Thus, the request for costs and expenses is granted in the amount of $564.74.

RULING

Plaintiff Janik Tsaturyan’s Motion for Attorneys’ Fees and Costs is GRANTED IN PART in the amounts of $6,130.00 in reduced attorney’s fees and $564.74 in recoverable costs and expenses, totaling $6,694.74.
