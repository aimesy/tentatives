ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On August 22, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants Shoreline Foods Inc., Amusement Foods Inc., Freeway Foods, Inc., Duquesne Properties, LLC, 730 Broadway, Inc., Waffle Plaza Properties, Inc., Diego Plate Properties, LLC, Gower Properties, LLC, La Breafo, Inc., La Brea Food, Inc., Roscoe’s Cool, Inc., Heirbel Management, Inc., Heirbella Drink Co, LLC, Roscoe’s  Intellectual Property, LLC, Herbert Hudson, and Sam White (“Defendants”) and Does 1-30, alleging Breach of Contract, Open Book Account, Account Stated, Intentional Misrepresentation, Negligent Misrepresentation, Promissory Estoppel, Quantum Meruit, and Unjust Enrichment causes of action. The underlying action involves Plaintiff supplying products to Defendants for use at their Roscoe’s House of Chicken n’ Waffles restaurants, but Defendants owe Plaintiff “over $1,304,850.76 for products, plus a ‘1.5% per month service charge’ for any amount due over 30 days. The total amount due to Defendants in service charges constitutes more than an additional $102,241.27.” (FAC., p. 2.) Defendants stopped making payments to Plaintiff on or around 2023. (FAC., pp. 2, 8.)

 

On April 20, 2026, the Court found the instant matter related to Case No. 25STCV24957. (4/20/26 Order.)

 

On June 1, 2026, Hudson and White filed the instant Motion to Consolidate Related Actions, Continue Trial, and Coordinate Case Management (the “Motion”).

 

On June 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

 

On July 7, 2026, Hudson and White filed a Reply.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1048 states as follows:

 

(a)   When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the court, it may order a joint hearing or trial of any or all the matters in issue in the actions; it may order all the actions consolidated and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.

 

(b)   The court, in furtherance of convenience or to avoid prejudice, or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy, may order a separate trial of any cause of action, including a cause of action asserted in a cross-complaint, or of any separate issue or of any number of causes of action or issues, preserving the right of trial by jury required by the Constitution or a statute of this state or of the United States.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1048.)

 

“Code of Civil Procedure section 1048 grants discretion to the trial courts to consolidate actions involving common questions of law or fact. The trial court’s decision will not be disturbed on appeal absent a clear showing of abuse of discretion.” (Todd-Stenberg v. Shield (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 976, 978-979.) In deciding whether to grant a motion to consolidate, the court should weigh whether there are common issues between the cases and “whether undue confusion or prejudice [is] likely to result from the consolidation.” (Id., at p. 978.)

 

The purpose of consolidation “ ‘is merely to promote trial convenience and economy by avoiding duplication of procedure, particularly in the proof of issues common to both actions.’ [Citation].” (See Estate of Baker (1982) 131 Cal.App.3d 471, 485.)

 

III.            Discussion

 

Parties’ Arguments

 

Hudson and White move to consolidate the instant case with Case Number 25STCV24957 for all purposes on the following grounds:

 

Both [cases] arise from Plaintiff Choice Foods, Inc.’s alleged supplier relationship with affiliated restaurant entities operating under the Roscoe’s House of Chicken N’ Waffles (“Roscoe’s”) trade name and, during the relevant period, under coordinated operational management. In both the First Action and Second Action, Plaintiff alleges substantially identical claims based in breach of contract and fraud and arising from the same alleged course of dealings, invoices, payment history, payment communications, and alleged representations concerning outstanding balances. Plaintiff further alleges overlapping operational structures, centralized accounts payable management, and coordinated financial operations among the entities. Indeed, the complaints attach an identical supplier invoice, both sent to ECF, as evidence to support their various causes of action.

 

(Motion, pp. 3-4.)    

 

In Opposition, Plaintiff states the Motion should be denied because “the cases are at different stages of preparedness and consolidation would prejudice the non-moving party by delaying trial.” (Opp., pp. 0, 2-3.) Plaintiff contends that any overlapping issues can be addressed through bifurcation at trial rather than consolidation. (Opp., pp. 0, 7-8.) Further, Plaintiff contends that the cases involved different defendants, claims, and facts, and the Motion fails to identify any “specific inconsistent ruling likely to occur absent consolidation, any concrete prejudice from separate proceedings, or any discovery that cannot be coordinated informally between the actions.” (Opp., pp. 3-4.) Plaintiff highlights that East Coast Foods from the other action has a distinct legal history compared to the defendants in the instant action; the Motion is untimely; none of the entity defendants joined the instant Motion; and Hudson and White’s claim that there is a risk of inconsistent rulings is speculative. (Opp., pp. 4-6.)

 

In Reply, Hudson and White emphasize that the cases here arise from the “same supplier relationship, the same Roscoe’s restaurant system, the same alleged ownership/control structure, the same alleged alter ego theory, and many of the same alleged misrepresentations and payment practice,” and “[c]onsolidation and a continuance of trial will avoid duplicative discovery, multiple juries, inconsistent determinations, and unfair prejudice from a rushed August 3 trial.” (Reply, p. 3.) Hudson and White state that the Opposition confirms there are common questions between the two cases that warrant consolidation, and the Opposition’s contentions regarding the different procedural postures of the cases supports a continuance of trial rather than a denial of consolidation. (Reply, pp. 3-5.)

 

California Rules of Court

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1) provides that a notice of motion to consolidate must:

(A) List all named parties in each case, the names of those who have appeared, and the names of their respective attorneys of record;

(B) Contain the captions of all the cases sought to be consolidated, with the lowest numbered case shown first; and

(C) Be filed in each case sought to be consolidated.

 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1).)

 

Here, the Notice of Motion fails to comply with California Rules of Court, rule 3.350, subdivision (a)(1) – namely, the Notice of Motion does not comply with California Rules of Court, rule 3.350, subdivision (a)(1)(A)-(B).

 

Analysis

 

In the instant matter, the FAC states that Plaintiff has supplied products to Defendants Shoreline Foods Inc., Amusement Foods Inc., Freeway Foods, Inc., Duquesne Properties, LLC, 730 Broadway, Inc., Waffle Plaza Properties, Inc., Diego Plate Properties, LLC, Gower Properties, LLC, La Breafo, Inc., La Brea Food, Inc., Roscoe’s Cool, Inc., Heirbel Management, Inc., Heirbella Drink Co, LLC, Roscoe’s  Intellectual Property, LLC, Herbert Hudson, and Sam White for use at Roscoe’s House of Chicken n’ Waffles restaurants. (FAC, ¶¶ 1-2, 17-18, 27-28.) Plaintiff has promptly filled Defendants’ orders and delivered fresh quality products every time Defendants asked; however, in 2023, Defendants stopped making payments on outstanding invoices due to Plaintiff. (FAC, ¶¶ 1-2.)

 

For Case Number 25STCV24957, the Court notes that the Motion fails to attach a copy of the complaint in that case; however, a court may take judicial notice on its own motion (see Evid. Code, § 455), and the Court finds it appropriate to take judicial notice of the Complaint filed in Case Number 25STCV24957 pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d). As such, the Court takes judicial notice of the Complaint in Case Number 25STCV24957 without taking judicial notice of the truth of its contents. The Complaint states that Plaintiff has supplied products to Defendants East Coast Foods, Inc., Hudson, and White for use at Roscoe’s House of Chicken n’ Waffles restaurants. (Compl., ¶¶ 1-2, 15.) Plaintiff has promptly filled Defendants’ orders and delivered fresh quality products every time Defendants asked; however, in early 2024, Defendants stopped making payments on outstanding invoices due to Plaintiff. (Compl., ¶¶ 1-2.)

 

Here, the two aforementioned cases have been found to be related, and the cases concern the same factual and legal issues because they all arise out of the same issues with payment for products supplied by Plaintiff for use at Roscoe’s House of Chicken n’ Waffles in a similar time period, for which Defendants are allegedly liable. The eight causes of action in Case No. 25STCV24957 are all included in the 14 causes of action in the instant action, and the parties to the actions are predominantly the same – the only party from Case No. 25STCV24957 not named in the instant matter is East Coast Foods, Inc. Based on the common questions of law and fact, the Court consolidation would significantly reduce the time and expense of trying the actions separately, promote judicial economy, and not cause undue confusion or prejudice to the parties. To the extent the proximity of the trial date in Case No. 24STCV33191 is of concern to Plaintiff, the Court finds a reasonable continuance of the trial date would not be prejudicial to Plaintiff or the other parties in the actions given the significant overlap between the two cases.

 

Thus, the Motion is GRANTED.

 

Further, in ordering consolidation, the Court “may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (a).)

 

As such, the Court finds that a trial continuance in the instant action is warranted in light of the consolidation.

Upon a properly noticed motion of a party made no later than the close of pretrial conference, or in other cases, no later than 30 days before the trial date, a court may bifurcate a trial into separate issues when the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economy and efficiency of handling the litigation would be promoted thereby.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 598.)  “The court, in furtherance of convenience or to avoid prejudice, or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy, may order a separate trial of any cause of action, including a cause of action asserted in a cross-complaint, or of any separate issue or of any number of causes of action or issues, preserving the right of trial by jury required by the Constitution or a statute of this state or of the United States.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (b), emphasis added.) The court has broad discretion to order a severance of trials. (See National Electric Supply Co. v Mt. Diablo Unified School Dist. (1960) 187 Cal.App.2d 418, 421; see also Grappo v. Coventry Fin. Corp. (1991) 235 Cal.App.3d 496, 504.)

 

The Court finds that a Motion to Bifurcate at this juncture is premature. Between now and the continued trial date to March 1, 2027 on the consolidated matters, the pleadings of the case, named parties, and the status of the case, including the cross-complaints, may change. Thus, based on the variable circumstances, the Court is not satisfied with the evidence that bifurcation would promote judicial efficiency at this time.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Choice Foods, Inc’s. Motion to Bifurcate is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

IV.            Legal Standard

 

“As a general matter, in a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Ibid.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

A general demurrer may be brought under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e) if insufficient facts are stated to support the cause of action asserted or under section 430.10, subdivision (a), where the court has no jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action alleged in the pleading. All other grounds listed in Section 430.10, including uncertainty under subdivision (f), are special demurrers.

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245).

V.              Discussion

 

A.    Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that “[b]efore filing a demurrer pursuant to this chapter, the demurring party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) The parties are to meet and confer at least five days before the date the responsive pleading is due. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(2).) Thereafter, the demurring party shall file and serve a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(3).) 

Here, the Demurrer is accompanied by a meet and confer declaration that states the parties’ counsel met and conferred telephonically regarding the issues raised in the Demurrer. (Demurrer, Carson Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) The parties were unable to reach an agreement regarding the FAC. (Demurrer, Carson Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

Thus, the Court finds the meet and confer requirement is satisfied.

 

B.    Demurrer

 

Hudson and White demur to all causes of action in the Complaint on the following grounds: (1) the FAC’s alter ego allegations regarding Hudson and White are insufficiently pled, and thus the Breach of Contract causes of action fail and (2) the Complaint’s allegations are insufficiently pled to satisfy the heightened pleading requirements for fraud and thus the Intentional Misrepresentation, Negligent Misrepresentation, Promissory Estoppel, and Quantum Meruit claims fail. (Demurrer, pp. 2-5.) Hudson and White request that any leave to amend the FAC be denied. (Demurrer, p. 6.)

 

            In Opposition, Plaintiff contends the FAC specifically and adequately alleges alter ego theory of Shoreline Foods, Amusement Foods, and Freeway Foods and Hudson and White as well as allegations of fraud and points to specific assertions in the FAC to demonstrate the FAC states sufficient facts to support each stated cause of action. (Opp., pp. 1-18.) Further, Plaintiff requests leave to amend should the Court find any of the causes of action are deficiently pled in the FAC. (Opp., p. 18.)

 

            In Reply, Hudson and White reiterate the FAC fails to plead adequate alter ego liability and the fraud causes of action. (Reply, pp. 1-4.) Hudson and White assert that the FAC’s fraud allegations rely on alleged statements by White, not Hudson, and therefore fraud is not pled with particularity as to Hudson in the FAC. (Reply, p. 3.) Further, Hudson and White contend the FAC’s alter ego allegations do not substitute for specific allegations regarding fraud as to Hudson. (Reply, pp. 3-4.) As to the equitable claims, Hudson and White state the Opposition mischaracterizes the Demurrer, and the Demurrer’s challenge to these claims is as follows: “to the extent those claims are based on the same alleged fraudulent promises and representations, the FAC still must plead a nonconclusory factual basis for imposing personal liability on Hudson and White.” (Reply, p. 4.)

                       

Alter Ego Allegations

 

The Court finds the Complaint satisfactorily states specific facts to allege alter ego liability for Hudson and White as to all causes of action asserted against them. (See FAC; Leek v. Cooper (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 399, 415.) For instance, the FAC states: “Defendants are alter egos of one another, as they have commingled funds, failed to observe corporate formalities, undercapitalized the Company Defendants, and used the Company Defendants to perpetrate fraud or injustice. Choice Foods alleges that there exists a unity of interest and ownership between Defendants such that adherence to the Company Defendants’ corporate fiction of separateness would result in an inequitable outcome. Choice Foods is also informed and believes, and based upon such information and belief alleges, that funds are routinely transferred without clear documentation, justification, or adherence to corporate formalities between and amongst Defendants and to the Individual Defendants. Accordingly, all Defendants are liable for the breach of contract as the alter egos of Freeway Foods, Inc.” (FAC, ¶ 44.) Thus, the Court finds the Complaint adequately alleges sufficient facts to show a unity of interest and ownership between Hudson and White and the corporate defendants and an unjust result if the corporate defendants are treated as the sole actors. (Leek, supra,194 Cal.App.4th at p. 415.)

 

Based on the foregoing, Hudson and White’s Demurrer on the grounds that Plaintiff failed to sufficiently allege alter ego allegations against them is OVERRRULED.

 

            Causes of Action 1-14

 

The Court finds the Demurrer’s challenges to a majority of the FAC’s causes of action were based solely on the alter ego theory addressed above and thus are meritless. The Demurrer also challenges the sufficiency of the FAC’s allegations for the “fraud claims” against Hudson and White separate and apart from the alter ego liability challenge.

 

Here, the Court finds the tenth and eleventh causes of action for intentional misrepresentation and negligent misrepresentation claims are specifically and sufficiently pled as to Hudson and White. Indeed, the FAC states the misrepresentations made by Hudson and White were made in writing, orally, and by text message and were that they would pay outstanding invoices issued by Plaintiff; the FAC includes specific verbatim and/or chronological examples of these misrepresentations made by both Hudson and White, including the dates the misrepresentations were made; the FAC alleges Hudson and White knew they had insufficient funds in their accounts to make the outstanding payments and thus intended to induce Plaintiff’s reliance without reasonable ground for believing they could make the outstanding payments; and Plaintiff was damaged as a result of Hudson and White’s misrepresentations. (FAC., ¶¶ 83-105; Charnay v. Cobert (2006) 145 Cal.App.4th 170, 184, citing Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 638 [providing the elements of intentional misrepresentation]; Small v. Fritz Companies, Inc. (2003) 30 Cal.4th 167, 184, citing Lazar, supra, 12 Cal.4th at p. 645; Hydro-Mill Co., Inc. v. Hayward, Tilton & Rolapp Ins. Associates, Inc. (2004) 115 Cal.App.4th 1145, 1154 [providing the elements of a cause of action for negligent misrepresentation]; Friedman v. Merck & Co. (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 454, 481 [“the ‘ordinary rule’ is that liability for negligent misrepresentation is imposed only on those who supply information for ‘ “business purposes in the course of a business or profession’ ”].)

 

Given the clarifications stated in the Reply, the Court finds it need not address the twelfth, thirteenth, and fourteenth causes of action separately from the aforementioned analysis.

 

For all these reasons, the Demurrer is OVERRULED.

 

IV.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants Herbert Hudson and Sam White’s Demurrer is OVERRULED.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice.

III.            Legal Standard

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 284 states that “the attorney in an action…may be changed at any time before or after judgment or final determination, as follows: (1) Upon the consent of both client and attorney…; (2) Upon the order of the court, upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 284; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)  “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133); see Lempert v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1173.)

 

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Counsel Forms MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion), MC-052 (Declaration), and MC-053 (Proposed Order). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (e).) 

 

In addition, California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 subsection (d) requires that the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case by personal service, electronic service, or mail.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On December 16, 2025, Plaintiff Dmoney Peters, by and through his Guardian Ad Litem, Shermick Peters (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Continuity Care Home Nurses, Inc. (“Continuity Care Home”); Continuity Care Staffing Services, Inc. (“Continuity Care Staffing”); Francisco J. Gallegos Loaiza (“Defendants”); and Does 1-50, alleging causes of action for Negligence and Negligent Hiring, Supervision, or Retention of Employee. (Compl., p. 1.)

 

On February 27, 2026, Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing filed an Answer and Motion to Strike.

 

On April 24, 2026, the Court granted Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing’s Motion to Strike and struck the claims for punitive damages in the Complaint for Plaintiff’s failure to comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 425.13. (4/24/26 Order.) The Court held that “Plaintiff may move for leave to assert punitive damages.” (4/24/26 Order.) 

 

On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Leave to File a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) (the “Motion”).

 

On June 30, 2026, Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing filed an Opposition.

 

On June 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

A party may amend its pleading once without leave of court at any time before the answer, demurrer, or motion to strike is filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 472.)

 

            Leave to amend is permitted under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a), which states: “The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by adding or striking out the name of any party, or by correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect; and may, upon like terms, enlarge the time for answer or demurrer. The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.”

 

“Although courts are bound to apply a policy of great liberality in permitting amendments to the complaint at any stage of the proceedings, up to and including trial [citations], this policy should be applied only ‘where no prejudice is shown to the adverse party . . .. [citation].  A different result is indicated ‘where inexcusable delay and probable prejudice to the opposing party’ is shown. [Citation].” (Magpali v. Farmers Group (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 487.)

 

            A motion for leave to amend a pleading must also comply with the procedural requirements of California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, which requires that a motion to amend must:  “[i]nclude a copy of the proposed… amended pleading… [and] state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be [deleted and/or added], if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the [deleted and/or additional] allegations are located.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a).)

 

A motion for leave to amend a pleading must also include “[a] separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify: (1) The effect of the amendment; (2) Why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) The reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

 

III.            Discussion

 

Plaintiff seeks leave of court to file a FAC that would “add claims for Dependent Adult Abuse pursuant to the Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act, Welfare and Institutions Code section 15600, et seq., Assault and Battery, Sexual Battery, and Fraud, as well as allegations and a prayer for punitive damages.” (Motion, pp. 1-2, Jahezian Decl., ¶¶ 2, 4, 5, Exhs. A-B.) Plaintiff files a proposed FAC – both a clean copy and a copy with redlines – in conjunction with the Motion. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a).)

 

In Opposition, Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing appear to concede that granting Plaintiff’s requested amendments is within the Court’s discretion except for the proposed claim for punitive damages. The Opposition states that “despite having the edification of the prior ruling, Plaintiff’s Motion does not even mention section 425.13, let alone comply with its requirements,” and thus the request for punitive damages violates the Court’s prior order. (Opp., p. 2.)

 

In Reply, Plaintiff states it is no longer necessary for Plaintiff to comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 425.13 as the proposed FAC raises other causes of action that allow for punitive damages besides a medical negligence by health care provider claim. (Reply, pp. 1-2, 5.)

 

The Court finds the Motion complies with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a). The Motion includes a copy of the proposed FAC (Motion, Jahezian Decl., Exhs. A-B) and sets forth the allegations to be added and/or deleted along with the corresponding paragraph numbers. (Motion, p. 2, 5, Jahezian Decl., ¶¶ 2, 4, 5, Exhs. A-B.)

 

Plaintiff’s Motion also includes a separate declaration from Plaintiff’s counsel that substantially satisfies California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b). Indeed, the declaration states that Plaintiff seeks to file a FAC to add claims for Dependent Adult Abuse pursuant to the Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act, Welfare and Institutions Code section 15600, et seq., Assault and Battery, Sexual Battery, and Fraud, as well as allegations and a prayer for punitive damages; the facts giving rise to the proposed amendment were discovered in May 2026 when Defendants responded to discovery and produced documents; the request for amendment was not made earlier “because the facts and information supporting the proposed amended allegations, including facts concerning the nature and timing of the alleged sexual abuse, Plaintiff’s dependent adult status and full dependency for care, Defendants’ custody, care, supervision, and control of Plaintiff, and Defendants’ relevant policies, procedures, training, supervision, hiring, screening, and reporting obligations, were obtained and/or clarified through Plaintiff’s continuing investigation and Defendants’ May 2026 discovery responses and document production”; and Defendants will not suffer prejudice as a result of the proposed amendments. (Motion, Jahezian Decl., ¶¶ 2-8.)

 

Thus, the Court finds Plaintiff has substantially met the requirements set forth in California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).

 

As to Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing’s arguments regarding the punitive damages allegations in the proposed FAC, the Court notes that there are new causes of action for Dependent Adult Abuse, Assault and Battery, Sexual Battery, and Fraud, above and beyond the Negligence cause of action enumerated in the Code of Civil Procedure section 425.13, thus the Court’s prior order does not bar the instant FAC. In particular the Code of Civil Procedure section 425.13 does not apply to an Elder Abuse Act or fraud claims. (See Covenant Care Inc. v. Sup. Ct. (2004) 32 Cal.App. 4th 771, 782-790; Country Villa Claremont Healthcare Ctr. Inc. v. Sup.Ct. (2004) 120 Cal.App. 4th 426, 431.) To the extent Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing argue the allegations in the FAC are insufficient to support a request for punitive damages, Continuity Care Home and Continuity Care Staffing may bring a responsive pleading to formally challenge these allegations should they choose.

 

Accordingly, the Motion is GRANTED.

 

IV.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff Dmoney Peters, by and through his Guardian Ad Litem, Shermick Peters’s Motion for Leave to File a First Amended Complaint is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff is to file the First Amended Complaint within 10 days from this order.
