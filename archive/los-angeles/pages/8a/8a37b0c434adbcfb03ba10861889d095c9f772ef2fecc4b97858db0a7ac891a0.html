Case Number: 24PSCV02144    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: H

Rreef CPIF 13668 Valley Boulevard, LLC v. SLCC Enterprise, Inc., Case No. 24PSCV02144

 

ORDER ON MOTION FOR ATTORNEY FEES

 

Plaintiff Rreef CPIF 13668 Valley Boulevard, LLC’s unopposed Motion for Attorney Fees is GRANTED, in the reduced amount of $49,035.00. The Court awards costs in the amount of $3,507.26. The total amount of attorney’s fees and costs awarded is $52,542.26.


Background

Plaintiff Rreef CPIF 13668 Valley Boulevard, LLC (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows:

Plaintiff is the owner of the commercial real property located at 13668 Valley Boulevard, Suites B and C1 in the City of Industry (“subject premises”). Plaintiff’s predecessor, as landlord, and SLCC Enterprise, Inc. (“Defendant”), as tenant, entered into a written lease agreement dated June 28, 2013 (the “Lease”) for Suite B of the subject premises. The Lease was amended twice: the first amendment, dated July 1, 2018, added Suite C1 to the subject property and the second amendment, dated June 29, 2022, extended the Lease term to December 31, 2023. Defendant vacated the subject premises and returned possession on or about January 5, 2024. Defendant owes, and has not paid, holdover and repair damages. 

On July 3, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action againstDefendant and Does 1-10 for: (1) Breach of Written Lease and (2) Common Count. 

On February 27, 2026, this matter proceeded to a court trial. On March 20, 2026, judgment was entered. 

On March 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed (and mail served) a “Notice of Entry of Judgment.”

 

Legal Standard 

“As used in this section, unless the context clearly requires otherwise: . . .
(4) ‘Prevailing party’ includes the party with a net monetary recovery. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4).) 

“The following items are allowable as costs under Section 1032:. . .
(10) Attorney’s fees, when authorized by any of the following:. . .
(A) Contract. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10(A).) 

“[I]n any action on a contract, where the contract specifically provides that attorney’s fees and costs, which are incurred to enforce that contract, shall be awarded either to one of the parties or to the prevailing party, then the party who is determined to be the party prevailing on the contract, whether he or she is the party specified in the contract or not, shall be entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees in addition to other costs…” (Civil Code § 1717(a).) 

“The court, upon notice and motion by a party, shall determine who is the party prevailing on the contract for purposes of this section, whether or not the suit proceeds to final judgment. Except as provided in paragraph (2), the party prevailing on the contract shall be the party who recovered a greater relief in the action on the contract. The court may also determine that there is no party prevailing on the contract for purposes of this section…” (Civil Code § 1717(b)(1).)

 

Discussion 

Plaintiff moves the Court, pursuant to California Rules of Court Rule 3.1702, Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5(a)(10)(A) and Civil Code § 1717, for an order awarding Plaintiff attorneys’ fees and costs against Defendant in the total amount of $52,942.26 (i.e., $45,715.00 in fees through trial, plus $3,507.26 in costs, plus $3,720.00 in motion related fees). 

1. Entitlement to Fees

Plaintiff asserts that it is entitled to attorney’s fees and costs pursuant to Paragraph 22.11 of the Standard Industrial Lease Agreement dated June 28, 2013 (“Lease Agreement”) entered into between Plaintiff’s predecessor in interest, as landlord, and Defendant, as tenant, which reads as follows:

“22.11 Attorneys’ Fees If either Landlord or Tenant commences or engages in , or threatens to commence or engage in, an action by or against the other party arising out of or in connection with this Lease or the Premises, including but not limited to any action for recovery of Rent due and unpaid, to recover possession or for damages for breach of this Lease, the prevailing party shall be entitled to have and recover from the losing party reasonable attorneys’ fees and other costs incurred in connection with the action, preparation for such action, any appeals relating thereto and enforcing any judgments rendered in connection therewith. If Landlord becomes involved in any action, threatened or actual, by or against anyone not a party to this Lease, but arising by reason of or related to any act or omission of Tenant or Tenant's Parties, Tenant agrees to pay Landlord's reasonable attorneys’ fees and other costs incurred in connection with the action, preparation for such action, any appeals relating thereto and enforcing any judgments rendered in connection therewith to the extent awarded by a court of competent jurisdiction.” 

(Kight Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. 1, p. 17, ¶ 22.11.) 

The Lease Agreement was amended twice in writing with Plaintiff listed as the landlord, most recently on June 29, 2023. (Id.) 

This case, which was filed on July 3, 2024, proceeded as a breach of lease action for damages outstanding to the real property at issue in this action in excess of the security deposit that was on hand. (Burton Decl., ¶ 8.) The case went to trial on February 27, 2026 and the Court issued its decision in favor of Plaintiff on March 2, 2026. (Id., ¶ 9.) On March 20, 2026, judgment was entered in the amount of $37,669.56. (Id.) 

The Court determines that Plaintiff is the prevailing party and is entitled to seek attorney’s fees. 

2. Reasonableness of Fees 

The Court turns to the issue of reasonableness of the fees sought. “[T]rial courts have broad discretion in determining the amount of a reasonable attorney's fee award. This determination is necessarily ad hoc and must be resolved on the particular circumstances of each case.” (Meister v. Regents of University of California (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 437, 452.) “Trial judges are entrusted with this discretionary determination because they are in the best position to assess the value of the professional services rendered in their courts.” (Christian Research Institute v. Alnor (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 1315, 1321.) 

“[O]nce a party has established he or she is entitled to fees, the lodestar method is generally presumed to be the starting point in analyzing the appropriate amount of attorney fees. Under this method, a court first calculates the number of hours reasonably spent multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate for each billing professional, and then may adjust the amount based on various relevant factors to ensure the fee reflects the fair market value [of the attorney services] for the particular action. (K.I. v. Wagner (2014) 225 Cal.App.4th 1412, 1425 [quotations and citations omitted].)

a.       Reasonableness of Hourly Rates 

“In determining hourly rates, the court must look to the prevailing market rates in the relevant community. The rates of comparable attorneys in the forum district are usually used. In making its calculation, the court should also consider the experience, skill, and reputation of the attorney requesting fees. The court may rely on its own knowledge and familiarity with the legal market in setting a reasonable hourly rate.” (Heritage Pacific Financial, LLC v. Monroy (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 972, 1009 [internal quotations and citations omitted].) 

Attorney Anthony W. Burton’s (“Burton”) hourly rate was initially $350.00 and increased to $400.00 as of August 1, 2025. (Burton Decl., ¶ 6.) Paralegal Yuri Gutierrez’s hourly rate is $225.00. (Id.) 

Based on the Court’s own experience and knowledge, the Court finds that the above requested hourly rates are appropriate.

b.       Reasonableness of Time Incurred 

“[A]n award of attorney fees may be based on counsel’s declarations, without production of detailed time records.” (Raining Data Corp. v. Barrenechea (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 1363, 1375.) “In challenging attorney fees as excessive because too many hours of work are claimed, it is the burden of the challenging party to point to the specific items challenged, with a sufficient argument and citations to the evidence. General arguments that fees claimed are excessive, duplicative, or unrelated do not suffice.” (Premier Medical Management Systems, Inc. v. California Ins. Guarantee Assn. (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 564.) 

Burton sets forth a summary of attorney’s fees incurred in this case through the Notice of Entry of Judgment, which total $45,715.00. (Burton Decl., ¶ 11.) Burton also provides the Court with a summary of attorney’s fees incurred in connection with this instant motion, which total $3,720.00. The Court will reduce this amount by one hour, inasmuch as the estimated one hour time of reviewing any opposition and preparing a reply was not incurred.

The Court will award attorney’s fees in the reduced amount of $49,035.00.

 

3. Costs

Plaintiff requests $3,507.26 in costs. Plaintiff filed a “Memorandum of Costs” on April 1, 2026. Defendant has not filed a motion to strike or to tax costs. The Court will award costs in the amount of $3,507.26.

 

Conclusion 

The Court grants the unopposed motion for attorney’s fees in the reduced amount of $49,035.00. The Court awards costs in the amount of $3,507.26. The total amount of attorney’s fees and costs awarded is $52,542.26.

Background 

Plaintiff Erik Samaniego (“Plaintiff”) alleges that he sustained injuries and damages in a December 13, 2022 motor vehicle accident. 

On December 12, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against Roy Hunley (“Defendant”) and Does 1-100 for: (1) Negligence, (2) Negligence Per Se, and (3) Vicarious Liability. 

A Post-Mediation Status Conference/Case Management Conference is set for July 7, 2026.

 

1. Motion to Compel Re: Form and Special Interrogatories, Sets No. 1

Legal Standard

A response to interrogatories is due within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260, subd. (a).) “If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, . . . [t]he party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling response to the interrogatories.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).)

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)

Discussion

Defendant moves the Court for an order compelling Plaintiff to provide responses to Defendant’s Form and Special Interrogatories, Sets No. 1. Defendant also seeks sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $810.00.

Defendant’s counsel Kim Haycraft (“Haycraft”) represents, and/or the accompanying exhibits reflect, as follows:

On October 23, 2025, Defendant served the subject discovery. (Haycraft Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.) On December 1, 2025, Haycraft sent a letter to Plaintiff’s counsel Andy Basseri, advising him that responses to the subject discovery were past due and requesting that verified responses be provided no later than December 11, 2025. (Id., ¶ 3, Exh. B.) No responses have been received, nor has Plaintiff’s counsel requested an extension. (Id., ¶ 4.)

The unopposed motion is granted. Plaintiff is ordered to provide verified responses, without objections, to Defendant’s Form and Special Interrogatories, Sets No. 1, within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling.

 

Sanctions

Defendant seeks sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $810.00 [calculated as follows: 2 hours preparing motion at $180.00, plus 2 hours attending hearing, plus $90.00 motion filing fee].

Sanctions are awarded in the reduced amount of $240.00 (i.e., 1 hour at $180.00/hour, plus $60.00 filing fee), payable within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling.

 

2. Motion to Compel Re: Demand for Production and Inspection of Documents and Things

Legal Standard 

A response to a request for production of documents is due within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) “If a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to serve a timely response to it, . . . [t]he party making the demand may move for an order compelling response to the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).)

“[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)

Sanctions

Sanctions are awarded in the reduced amount of $240.00 (i.e., 1 hour at $180.00/hour, plus $60.00 filing fee), payable within 20 days from the date of the notice of ruling.                

Case Number: 25PSCV03506    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: H

Jollevet v. Hildago-Wohlleben, Case No. 25PSCV03506

 

ORDER ON DEMURRER


Defendant Oliver Hidalgo-Wohlleben’s Demurrer to First Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED, without leave given to amend.

 

Background 

Plaintiff Dondi DeShawn Jollevet (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows:

Oliver Hildalgo-Wohlleben (“Defendant”) was, at all relevant times, acting as the campaign manager or senior representative of Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s political campaign. Plaintiff volunteered with the campaign. Defendant made statements and communications that portrayed Plaintiff as a safety concern or potential threat within the campaign organizing environment. 

On March 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), asserting causes of action against Defendant and Does 1-20 for: (1) Defamation (Libel/Defamation by Implication) [FN], (2) False Light Invasion of Privacy, and (3) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress. 

A Case Management Conference is set for July 7, 2026.

 

Legal Standard 

A demurrer may be made on the grounds that the pleading, inter alia, does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action and/or is uncertain. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subds. (e) and (f).) 

“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.” (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905 [citations omitted].) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) “[A] demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction placed on an instrument pleaded therein, or facts impossible in law, or allegations contrary to facts of which a court may take judicial knowledge.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732 [citations omitted].)

 

Discussion 

Defendant demurs, per Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, to the first through third causes of action in Plaintiff’s FAC, on the basis that they each fail to state facts sufficient to constitute causes of action and are uncertain.  

 

First Cause of Action (Defamation (Libel/Defamation by Implication)) 

Libel “is a false and unprivileged publication by writing, printing, picture, effigy, or other fixed representation to the eye, which exposes any person to hatred, contempt, ridicule, or obloquy, or which causes him to be shunned or avoided, or which has a tendency to injure him in his occupation.” (Civ. Code § 45.) “The general rule is that the words constituting an alleged libel must be specifically identified, if not pleaded verbatim, in the complaint.” (Kahn v. Bower (1991) 232 Cal.App.3d 1599, 1612, fn. 5.)

Plaintiff has alleged that he is “informed” and “believes” that unspecified “statements portraying [him] as a safety concern” were circulated to “affiliated progressive organizations and related political networks.” (FAC, ¶ 91.) “[A] pleading made on information and belief is insufficient if it merely asserts the facts so alleged without alleging such information that leads the plaintiff to believe that the allegations are true.” (Gomez v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc. (2011) 192 Cal.App.4th 1149, 1158-1159 [internal quotations and citation omitted].) As Defendant states, Plaintiff “does not provide specific instances, circumstances, or content of the purported statements made to a third party by any party, let alone by [Defendant] specifically. Nor does he provide facts that support his belief that such statements were made or facts to show he was informed that such statements occurred.” (Dem., 7:12-15.) Plaintiff also fails to factually allege a specific harm stemming from any of the allegedly defamatory statements.

Next, “[i]n a case in which a plaintiff seeks to maintain an action for defamation by implication, the plaintiff must demonstrate that (1) his or her interpretation of the statement is reasonable; (2) the implication or implications to be drawn convey defamatory facts, not opinions; (3) the challenged implications are not ‘”substantially true”’; and (4) the identified reasonable implications could also be reasonably deemed defamatory.” (Issa v. Applegate (2019) 31 Cal.App.5th 689, 707.) Plaintiff attempts to allege that unspecified statements could be interpreted to suggest that he “was a recent, unknown volunteer attempting to approach a private location without authorization.” (FAC, ¶ 93.) As Defendant states, “Plaintiff is assigning an interpretation without providing the content or context of any statement, let alone one specifically made by [Defendant].” (Dem., 8:7-8.) Additionally, even if general statements were made to others that Plaintiff was a “security threat,” such a statement would be an opinion based on an assessment of Plaintiff’s behavior towards the congresswoman. 

The Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the first cause of action, without leave given to amend.

 

Second Cause of Action (i.e., False Light Invasion of Privacy) 

“False light is a species of invasion of privacy, based on publicity that places a plaintiff before the public in a false light that would be highly offensive to a reasonable person, and where the defendant knew or acted in reckless disregard as to the falsity of the publicized matter and the false light in which the plaintiff would be placed.” (Jackson v. Mayweather (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 1240, 1264 [internal quotation and citation omitted].) Plaintiff fails to allege facts regarding the circumstances, content, or instances of alleged statements by Defendant. Plaintiff has not alleged any facts as to the element of knowledge or reckless disregard of the falsity of any statements by Defendant.

Additionally, “[w]hen a false light claim is coupled with a defamation claim, the false light claim is essentially superfluous, and stands or falls on whether it meets the same requirements as the defamation cause of action.” (Eisenberg v. Alameda Newspapers, Inc. (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 1359, 1385, fn. 13.)   

The Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the second cause of action, without leave given to amend.

   

Third Cause of Action (i.e., Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress) 

“[T]o state a cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress a plaintiff must show: (1) outrageous conduct by the defendant; (2) the defendant’s intention of causing or reckless disregard of the probability of causing emotional distress; (3) the plaintiff's suffering severe or extreme emotional distress; and (4) actual and proximate causation of the emotional distress by the defendant’s outrageous conduct.” (Yau v. Santa Margarita Ford, Inc. (2014) 229 Cal.App.4th 144, 160 [quotations and citation omitted].) 

For conduct to be considered “outrageous” it “must be so extreme as to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized society.” (Id. at 160.) Plaintiff has failed to allege any extreme and outrageous conduct by Defendant. 

Next, “[s]evere emotional distress means emotional distress of such substantial quality or enduring quality that no reasonable person in civilized society should be expected to endure it.” (Bock v. Hansen (2014) 225 Cal.App.4th 215, 233 [internal quotations and citations omitted].) The allegations pled in Paragraph 102 do not support the element of severe emotional distress. 

The Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the third cause of action, without leave given to amend.

   

[FN] Although the caption of the FAC identifies the first cause of action therein as “Defamation (Libel Per Se),” page 8, lines 9-10 therein identifies the first cause of action as “Defamation (Libel/Defamation by Implication).”

Case Number: 25PSCV03534    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: H

Citibank, N.A. v. MJCH Logistics, et al., Case No. 25PSCV03534

 

ORDER ON MOTIONS TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

 

1.       The hearing on Counsel for Defendant MJCH Logistics’ (i.e., Law Offices of Michael A. Peart) Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is CONTINUED to July 31, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. [see below]. 

2.       The hearing on Counsel for Defendant Mary Hui’s (i.e., Law Offices of Michael A. Peart) Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is CONTINUED to July 31, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. [see below].

 

Background

Plaintiff Citibank, N.A. (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows: On July 7, 2021, MJCH Logistics (“MJCH”) executed and delivered to Plaintiff a Commercial Credit Application/Agreement wherein MJCH requested a business credit account from Plaintiff in the amount of $100,000.00; this request was approved by Plaintiff on August 3, 2021. Mary Hui (“Hui”) executed a personal guaranty in connection with the above. On May 4, 2025, MJCH failed to make payments due and owing. 

On September 30, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against MJCH, Hui and Does 1-100 for: (1) Breach of Written Agreement, (2) Breach of Personal Guaranty, (3) Money Lent, and (4) Account Stated. 

On December 10, 2025, MJCH and Hui’s defaults were entered. 

On June 9, 2026, the Court granted MJCH and Hui’s Motion to Set Aside Default. 

A Case Management Conference is set for September 24, 2026.

 

Discussion

The Law Offices of Michael A. Peart seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for MJCH and Hui. 

The Court continues the hearing on the above motions, to July 31, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. Motion #1 was filed (and electronically served) on June 12, 2026; Motion #2 was filed (and electronically served) on June 16, 2026. 

Both motions reflect insufficient Code of Civil Procedure sections 1005, subdivision (b) and 1010.6, subdivision (a)(3)(B) notice (i.e., 16 court days, plus an additional 2 court days for electronic service). June 19, 2026 and July 3, 2026 are court holidays and are thus excluded for purposes of calculating notice. The motions should have been filed no later than June 11, 2026 and electronically served no later than June 9, 2026. 

Moving counsel is to provide notice of the continued hearing date in compliance with California Rules of Court rule 3.1362 and is to file a proof of service regarding same no later than 5 court days prior to the continued hearing date.
