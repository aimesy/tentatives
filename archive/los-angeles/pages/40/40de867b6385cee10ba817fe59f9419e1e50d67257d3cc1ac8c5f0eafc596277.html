JAMES DAVIS, et al.

 

         vs.

 

SCOTT EPPINGA, et al.

 

 Case No.: 23STCV25985  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 30, 2026

 

Defendants/Cross-Complainants Scott Eppinga, Benjamin Eppinga and Think Humble, Inc’s motion to dismiss with prejudice is GRANTED.

 

On 10/24/2023, Plaintiffs James Davis, Paranotek, LLC, and Astra Veda Corporation (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed suit against Scott Eppinga, Bradley M. Listermann, Benjamin Eppinga, and Think Humble, Inc (THI). On 4/24/2024, Plaintiffs filed a first amended complaint (FAC) alleging: (1) breach of fiduciary duties; (2) breach of the duty of good faith and fair dealing; (3) fraud; and (4) unjust enrichment.

 

On 4/15/2026, Defendants/Cross-Complainants Scott Eppinga, Benjamin Eppinga and Think Humble, Inc. (collectively, Defendants) filed a motion to dismiss the claims of Plaintiffs James Davis and Paranotek, LLC against Eppingas with prejudice.

 

The motion is unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants seek an order dismissing this action with prejudice based on the partial settlement the parties reported to the Court at the hearing set on April 6, 2026, as follows: 

 

The April 6, 2026 hearing was scheduled for the purpose, inter alia, of determining whether the Court should issue an order to show cause as to why Plaintiffs Davis and Paranotek, along with their attorney, should not be held in contempt for not complying with an earlier order imposing monetary sanctions. At the hearing on April 6, 2026, the parties advised the Court that they had reached an accommodation in which Eppingas agreed to withdraw and waive further proceedings related to the sanctions order and contempt motion, in return for Plaintiff Davis executing a Declaration and Davis and Paranotek dismissing their claims against Eppingas, with prejudice. See April 6, 2026 Minute Entry attached hereto as Ex. “A” and the Declaration of James Davis, attached as Ex. “B”.

 

Given that this motion seeks an order granting the agreed-upon dismissal, and is unopposed, the Court finds good cause to grant the relief sought.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendants/Cross-Complainants Scott Eppinga, Benjamin Eppinga and Think Humble, Inc’s motion to dismiss with prejudice is granted.

 

It is so ordered.

TENTATIVE RULING

 

MATTHEW MASSMAN, et al.

 

         vs.

 

6122 WILSHIRE BOULEVARD PROPERTY

 

 Case No.: 25STCV22910

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 30, 2026

 

Plaintiffs’ motion to compel Tanya Hakim’s deposition is GRANTED. Within 5 days of entry of this order, Defendant is to provide a list of 3 available dates for Ms. Hakim’s deposition to take place within 30 days. The Court declines to award sanctions at this time. 

 

On 8/4/2025, Plaintiffs Matthew and Marissa Long (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed suit against 6122 Wilshire Boulevard Property (6122 or Defendant), alleging: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of warranty of habitability; (3) breach of statutory warranty of habitability; (4) breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment; and (5) private nuisance.

 

On 4/8/2026, Plaintiffs moved to compel the deposition of Tanya Hakim.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiffs move to compel the in-person deposition of Tanya Hakim, agent for the managing partner of Defendant.

 

            In opposition, Defendant indicates that it “[h]as agreed to produce Ms. Hakim for deposition on numerous occasions, provided dates to Plaintiffs’ counsel for the deposition, and will produce Ms. Hakim in Irvine, as requested by Plaintiffs. There is no issue as to whether the deposition can be taken, where it is to be taken, or the scope of the deposition.” (Opp., 3: 9-13.)

 

            The Court finds that inadequate meet and confer efforts were undertaken by Plaintiffs to schedule Ms. Hakim’s deposition without Court interference. As a result, the Court declines to award sanctions at this time, but the grants the motion to ensure Ms. Hakim’s timely deposition.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ motion to compel Tanya Hakim’s deposition is granted. Within 5 days of entry of this order, Defendant is to provide a list of 3 available dates for Ms. Hakim’s deposition which is to take place within 30 days. The Court declines to award sanctions at this time. 

 

 

It is so ordered.

MATTHEW MASSMAN, et al.

 

         vs.

 

6122 WILSHIRE BOULEVARD PROPERTY

 

 Case No.: 25STCV22910

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 30, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses from Defendant to his Form Interrogatories (Set One) is DENIED. Accordingly, the Court declines to award sanctions at this time.

 

On 8/4/2025, Plaintiffs Matthew and Marissa Long (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed suit against 6122 Wilshire Boulevard Property (6122), alleging: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of warranty of habitability; (3) breach of statutory warranty of habitability; (4) breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment; and (5) private nuisance.

 

            Now, Plaintiff Matthew Massman (Plaintiff) moves to compel further responses from Defendant to his Form Interrogatories (Set One).

 

Discussion

 

On 1/8/2026, Plaintiff and his co-plaintiff Marissa Long (Ms. Long) (collectively Plaintiffs) each propounded discovery request on Defendant. (Illya Decl., ¶ 2.) The requests included a total of 226 Special Interrogatories, 40 Requests for Production, 118 Requests for Admission, as well as Form Interrogatories. (Illya Decl., ¶ 3.) Each of Plaintiffs’ requests were duplicative of the other’s. (Illya Decl., ¶ 4.) Defendant served responses to this discovery on 2/24/2026. (Illya Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

Plaintiffs’ counsel sent a meet and confer at the close of business on 3/30/2026, stating that Defendant had until 4/3/2026, to amend responses or else Plaintiffs would “have no choice but to file their respective motions...”. As such, Defendant was provided only four days to review and amend its responses.

 

            After review, the Court finds Plaintiff did not engage in a meaningful effort to resolve the dispute informally, and thus  has not met the meet and confer requirement. (See CCP § 2016.040.)

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to compel further discovery is denied.

 

 

It is so ordered.

         vs.

 

TRIXXI CLOTHING COMPANY, INC.

 

 Case No.: 26STCV02164

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 30, 2026

 

Defendant’s counsels Michell Ferber and Adam Arce’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED, effective upon filing proof of service of the court’s order (MC-053).

 

Legal Standard

 

Motions to withdraw are routinely granted if made in the proper form and presented with proper notice. The court may issue an order allowing an attorney to withdraw from representation, after notice to the client.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) 

 

Discussion

 

Michell Ferber and Adam Arce bring the instant motion for relief as counsel as to Defendant Trixxi Clothing Company, Inc.  

 

            The following documents are properly submitted: Civil Form MC-051 “Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” Civil Form MC-052 “Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” and Civil Form MC-053 “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel.”

 

            The motion is GRANTED.

 

 

It is so ordered.
