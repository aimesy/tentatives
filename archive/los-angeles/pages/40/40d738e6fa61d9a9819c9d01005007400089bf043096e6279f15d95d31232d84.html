APPROVAL OF PAGA SETTLEMENT

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiffs, Omar Sonda, Jose Trejo, and Ian Zaldana-Terauds

RESPONDING PARTY: Unopposed/Defendants, Mastro’s Restaurants, LLC et al.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion for Approval of PAGA Settlement

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiffs Omar Sonda and Jose Trejo and Ian Zaldana-Terauds, individually and on behalf of all other aggrieved employees, filed PAGA actions against Defendants Mastro’s Restaurants, LLC, Mastro’s OC LA, LLC, Mastro’s Mason SFO Restaurant, LLC, Landry’s Management, L.P., and Landry’s Payroll, Inc. The actions were consolidated on July 15, 2021. On October 28, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a “consolidated” PAGA complaint.  

 

RULING: Granted.

Plaintiffs seek approval of a settlement of the PAGA actions with Defendants. “The superior court shall review and approve any settlement of any civil action filed pursuant to [the Private Attorneys General Act]. The proposed settlement shall be submitted to the agency at the same time that it is submitted to the court.” (Lab. Code, § 2699, subd. (l)(2).) Plaintiffs present proof of submission of the settlement to the California Labor and Workforce Development Agency (LWDA). [Khalili Decl. ¶ 25, Ex. G: Email Acknowledgment of Receipt.]  

 

Labor Code section 2699, subdivision (i), requires that in an action brought under PAGA, “civil penalties recovered by aggrieved employees shall be distributed as follows: 75 percent to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency for enforcement of labor laws, . . . and 25 percent to the aggrieved employees.”

 

The parties settled all claims for a gross settlement amount of $1,355,000 for the period of February 10, 2019 to July 1, 2024, representing approximately 3,566 aggrieved employees working through 103,586 “Pay Periods.” [Khalili Decl. ¶ 23, Ex. E: Settlement Agreement.] The settlement agreement stipulates that counsel shall request up to 33 1/3% of the Gross Settlement Fund or $451,666.67 in fees, with 88% of awarded fees allocated to Matern Law Group, PC and 12% to Capstone Law APC. [Id. ¶¶ 23-24, Ex. E, Ex. F: Addendum to Settlement Agreement.] The agreement also provides for the recovery of costs, allocated among counsel based on counsel’s actual costs and expenses. [Id. ¶ 23, Ex. E.] Counsel seeks costs in the total amount of $37,318.26 ($28,782.74 incurred by Matern Law Group and $8,535.52 incurred by Capstone Law). [Id. ¶ 53, Ex. H; Perez Decl. ¶ 23.] The Court finds the requested fee amount and costs reasonable, and counsel are awarded the requested amounts in accordance with the allocations delineated in the settlement agreement. The settlement also provides for administration costs up to $24,500 and payment of 75% of Net Settlement Proceeds to LWDA and 25% to aggrieved employees. [Khalili Decl. ¶ 23, Ex. E.]

 

The administrator shall make payments not later than 30 days following receipt of the total amount of the Gross Settlement Fund from Defendants. [Khalili Decl. ¶ 23, Ex. E.] Payments to aggrieved employees will be mailed. [Ibid.]

 

The parties reached this agreement following mediation and negotiations. The release of claims occurs upon full funding of the settlement. [Khalili Decl. ¶ 23, Ex. E.] The Court acknowledges the request for retention of jurisdiction under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6.

 

The unopposed motion is granted.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.

PRO HAC VICE

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants, Medtronic, Inc., Medtronic USA, Inc., Medtronic Logistics, LLC, and Medtronic Puerto Rico Operations Co.

RESPONDING PARTY: Unopposed/Plaintiffs, Candy Bubar and Edward Bubar

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion for Pro Hac Vice Admission

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff Candy Bubar underwent a refill procedure for her Medtronic SynchroMed II model no. 8637-20 pain pump at the Cedars-Sinai Pain Center in Los Angeles. Defendant Dr. Howard L. Rosner performed the procedure. Immediately after the procedure, Candy became increasingly lethargic and confused, with her vital signs dropping. She then became bradycardic and non-responsive, and emergency medical services transported her to the emergency department. Candy’s husband, Edward Bubar, witnessed the entire incident.

 

Shortly after arriving at the emergency department, Candy went into cardiac arrest. After receiving CPR and epinephrine injections, her pulse returned approximately 10 minutes later. She was then intubated and admitted to the Neurocritical Intensive Care Unit for 8 days. During her hospitalization, she suffered acute hypoxemic respiratory failure, acute encephalopathy, post-arrest seizures, septic shock, a coma, and hypothermia. The primary cause of her injuries was determined to be baclofen toxicity, or drug overdose.

 

Candy now sues Defendants Medtronic, Inc., Medtronic USA, Inc., Medtronic Logistics, LLC, and Medtronic Puerto Rico Operations Co. (collectively, “Medtronic”) for: (1) strict product liability – manufacturing defect; (2) negligent manufacturing defect; (3) strict product liability – failure to warn; (4) negligent failure to warn; (5) negligence per se; (6) breach of express warranty; and (7) fraud. She also asserts a claim for medical negligence, among other claims, against Defendants Cedars-Sinai Health System, Cedars-Sinai Medical Center, and Dr. Rosner (collectively, “Cedars Defendants”). Edward brings claims against Defendants for negligent infliction of emotional distress and loss of consortium.

 

On May 29, 2026, the Court sustained the demurrer to Candy’s sixth cause of action for breach of express warranty.

 

RULING: Granted.

Medtronic moves for pro hac vice admission of attorney Ilana Eisenstein.

 

Pro hac vice admission in California is governed by California Rules of Court, rule 9.40. To be admitted pro hac vice, one must be “an attorney in good standing of and eligible to practice before the bar of any United States court or the highest court in any state, territory, or insular possession of the United States.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).) However, in no case shall an attorney appear pro hac vice if the attorney is a resident of California, regularly employed in California, or “regularly engaged in substantial business, professional, or other activities in the State of California.” (Ibid.)

 

An attorney seeking pro hac vice admission must file a verified application in court establishing:

 

(1) [t]he applicant’s residence and office address; (2)[t]he courts to which the applicant has been admitted to practice and the dates of admission; (3)[t]hat the applicant is a licensee in good standing in those courts; (4)[t]hat the applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court; (5)[t]he title of each court and cause in which the applicant has filed an application to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this state in the preceding two years, the date of each application, and whether or not it was granted; and (6)[t]he name, address, and telephone number of the active licensee of the State Bar who is attorney of record.

 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(d).)

 

The application complies with California Rules of Court, rule 9.40. Eisenstein lives and practices in Pennsylvania. She has applied for pro hac vice admission in California state court twice within the last two years. While the declaration of attorney Anna Yeung indicates the application was uploaded through the State Bar’s AIMS [Yeung Decl. ¶ 3], the application and notice of hearing must be served via mail. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(c)(1).) The proof of service also does not indicate service on Dr. Rosner. The application for pro hac vice admission is granted, with counsel to file the requisite proof of service.

 

Medtronic to give notice.
