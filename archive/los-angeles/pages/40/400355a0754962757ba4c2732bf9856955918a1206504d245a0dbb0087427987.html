DEPARTMENT 406 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV24810    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 406

 

ALEXYS WATSON,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

PEGAH KHOSRAVI,

                        Defendant.

 

  Case No.:  25STCV24810

  Hearing Date:  September 18, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

plaintiff’s motion for summary judgment or adjudication

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On August 25, 2025, Plaintiff Alexys Watson filed this action against Defendant Pegah Khosravi, asserting causes of action for violation of FEHA, violation of the Unruh Act, and negligence. The complaint stems from Defendant’s refusal to engage with Plaintiff as a housing applicant based on Plaintiff’s participation in Section 8.

            On June 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for summary judgment or adjudication. Defendant filed an opposition on September 3, 2026. Plaintiff filed a reply on September 4, 2026.

LEGAL STANDARD

The function of a motion for summary judgment or adjudication is to allow a determination as to whether an opposing party cannot show evidentiary support for a pleading or claim and to enable an order of summary dismissal without the need for trial. (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843.) Code of Civil Procedure section 437c(c) “requires the trial judge to grant summary judgment if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1110, 1119.)

“[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears an initial burden of production to make a prima facie showing of the nonexistence of any triable issue of material fact; if he carries his burden of production, he causes a shift, and the opposing party is then subjected to a burden of production of his own to make a prima facie showing of the existence of a triable issue of material fact.” (Leyva v. Garcia (2018) 20 Cal.App.5th 1095, 1101.) To establish a triable issue of material fact, the party opposing the motion must produce “substantial responsive evidence.” (Sangster v. Paetkau (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 151, 166.) Courts “liberally construe the evidence in support of the party opposing summary judgment and resolve doubts concerning the evidence in favor of that party.” (Dore v. Arnold Worldwide, Inc. (2006) 39 Cal.4th 384, 389.)

DISCUSSION

I. Undisputed Facts

            Plaintiff was the recipient of a Section 8 voucher and intended to use it to rent a portable housing unit. (Plntf.’s Stmt. of Undisputed Facts (UF) 1, 6.) When Plaintiff contacted Defendant to schedule a tour of Defendant’s property, Defendant responded, “We don’t accept Section 8.” (UF 7-10.) Defendant admitted that the sole reason for refusing to rent to Plaintiff was Plaintiff’s intended use of a Section 8 voucher. (UF 13.) Defendant acted on the advice of her real estate agent, who advised her not to rent to tenants using Section 8 vouchers. (UF 4-5.) Defendant did not consult an attorney or local housing authority, or conduct independent legal research. (UF 5.)

II. FEHA

FEHA makes it unlawful “[f]or the owner of any housing accommodation to discriminate against or harass any person because of the . . . source of income . . . of that person.” (Gov. Code, § 12955(a).) FEHA also prohibits the publication of any statement indicating a preference or limitation based on a protected characteristic, including source of income. (Id., § 12955(c).) “Source of income” includes “federal housing assistance vouchers issued under Section 8 of the United States Housing Act of 1937.” (Id., § 12955(p)(1).)

Here, the undisputed facts establish housing discrimination based on source of income as defined by FEHA. However, a plaintiff moving for summary judgment or adjudication must “prove[] each element of the cause of action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c(p)(1).) The plaintiff may not “seek summary adjudication of liability only, leaving the resolution of damages to a later trial.” (Paramount Petroleum Corp. v. Superior Court (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 226, 241.)

Plaintiff has no evidence of her damages. She simply avers in conclusory fashion that “Defendant Khosravi’s refusal harmed me. I was denied the opportunity to tour, apply for, or rent the Subject Property because of my lawful source of income.” (Watson Decl. ¶ 9.) Plaintiff’s evidence does not identify any harm nor establish any causal connection between Defendant’s actions and the harm. Thus, Plaintiff effectively “seek[s] summary adjudication of liability only, leaving the resolution of damages to a later trial.” (See Paramount, supra, 227 Cal.App.4th at p. 241.) This is impermissible, because a motion for summary adjudication must “completely dispose[] of a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for damages, or an issue of duty.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c(f)(1).)

Plaintiff argues that she is not leaving open the question of damages, because she only seeks the Unruh minimum of $4,000 and waives all other damages. (Reply 2:6-9; Civ. Code, § 52(a).) However, the Unruh damage provision does not apply to FEHA. Thus, the FEHA claims would still fail even if Plaintiff could establish entitlement to Unruh damages as a matter of law. Moreover, for the reasons discussed below, Plaintiff has not established her entitlement to recovery under Unruh.

In sum, Plaintiff is not entitled to summary adjudication of the FEHA claims because she has failed to establish all elements, specifically damages.

III. Unruh

Unruh provides that “[a]ll persons within the jurisdiction of this state are free and equal, and no matter what their sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language, or immigration status are entitled to the full and equal accommodations, advantages, facilities, privileges, or services in all business establishments of every kind whatsoever.” (Civ. Code, § 51(b).)

Source of income is not listed as a protected characteristic under Unruh. Plaintiff cites Marina Point, Ltd. v. Wolfson (1982) 30 Cal.3d 721 for the proposition that protection under Unruh is not limited to the enumerated characteristics. However, Marina Point pertained to discrimination against families with children. The case did not suggest that Unruh protects every conceivable characteristic, including source of income. To the contrary, the court in Harris v. Capital Growth Investors XIV (1991) 52 Cal.3d 1142, 1148 found that Unruh does not “encompass economic criteria.” Harris acknowledged that “decisions have occasionally recognized additional categories of prohibited discrimination.” (Ibid.) However, those cases pertained to “personal characteristics of individuals that bore little or no relationship to their abilities to be responsible consumers of public accommodations,” such as “physical appearance and family status.” (Ibid.) By contrast, permitting businesses to make decisions based on economic criteria “further[s] the legitimate interest of business establishments in controlling financial risk.” (Ibid.)

Ultimately, Plaintiff cites no authority suggesting that protection under Unruh extends to source of income. As one court noted, “decisions issued by the court of appeal during the post-Harris period reflected a new unwillingness to expand the number of protected characteristics under the Unruh Act.” (Butler v. Adoption Media, LLC (N.D.Cal. 2007) 486 F.Supp.2d 1022, 1032.) Therefore, Plaintiff is not entitled to summary adjudication of the Unruh claim.

IV. Negligence

The elements of negligence are: (1) a duty to exercise ordinary care; (2) breach of that duty; (3) causation; and (4) damages. (Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.)

For the reasons discussed above, Plaintiff has failed to establish the element of damages, and the claim is not saved by the Unruh damages provision.  

CONCLUSION

            Plaintiff’s motion for summary judgment or adjudication is DENIED.

 


Case Number: 25STCV29227    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 406

 

MELISSA PENUELA,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

FARHAD ARDESHIRPOUR, M.D.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  25STCV29227

  Hearing Date:  September 18, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

Defendants’ motion to compel further responses (CRS# 6696)

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On October 6, 2025, Plaintiff Melissa Penuela filed this action against Defendants Farhad Ardeshirpour, M.D. (Ardesh); Farhad Ardesh, M.D., Inc.; and Rodeo Wilshire Surgery Center LLC. Plaintiff filed the operative Second Amended Complaint (SAC) on July 28, 2026, asserting causes of action for (1) breach of written contract, (2) breach of express warranty, (3) constructive fraud, (4) fraudulent inducement, (5) fraudulent deceit, (6) false advertising, (7) unfair business practices, and (8) violation of the Consumer Legal Remedies Act.

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant Ardesh falsely promised her he could perform cosmetic surgery without visible scars. After the surgery, Plaintiff allegedly suffered from permanent, visible disfigurement.

On August 12, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion to compel Plaintiff’s further responses to interrogatories. Plaintiff filed an opposition on September 1, 2026. On September 4, 2026, Defendants filed an “Amended Motion” narrowing the discovery requests at issue.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

Upon receiving responses to its discovery requests, the propounding party may move for an order compelling further responses if the responses are incomplete or evasive, or objections are without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(a), 2031.310(a), 2033.290(a).)

A party “is entitled to demand answers to its interrogatories, as a matter of right . . . [and] the burden of justifying any objection and failure to respond remains at all times with the party resisting an interrogatory.” (Williams v. Sup. Ct. (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 541.) By contrast, the party seeking production of documents bears the initial burden of showing good cause through a fact-specific showing of relevance. (Kirkland v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 98.) Once this showing is made, the burden shifts to the responding party to justify any objections. (Ibid.)

MEET AND CONFER

A motion to compel further must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration demonstrating an attempt to resolve the matter informally. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(b)(1), 2031.310(b), 2033.290(b).) Here, Defendants’ meet and confer letter only addressed six interrogatories. Defendants concede this and have withdrawn the remaining interrogatories from their motion. Therefore, the Court only addresses the six interrogatories.

DISCUSSION

I. SROG E-1

            SROG E-1 asks Plaintiff to identify any collateral source that has reimbursed or is obligated to reimburse her for the damages claimed in the case. Plaintiff responded: “No COLLATERAL SOURCE or obligor has reimbursed, paid, or is obligated to pay or reimburse Responding Party, or anyone acting on her behalf, for any of the damages and losses claimed in this case.” This is a complete response. Defendants’ skepticism as to the truth of the response is not a reason to compel a different response.

II. SROG F-1 through F-3

            SROG F-1 through F-3 ask Plaintiff to identify every health care provider, and the reason for each visit, for the past 15 years. Plaintiff’s first supplemental response identified doctors who performed services related to Plaintiff’s face. Plaintiff’s second supplemental response added: “two physicians in France rendered elective cosmetic care to Plaintiff in France — one performed the two dissolvable cosmetic thread procedures, on two occasions approximately six months apart, in approximately 2015; the other administered facial dermal filler through approximately 2018, and Botox and lip filler through approximately 2020.” Plaintiff declined to provide information on health care providers for conditions unrelated to those at issue in this action.

            Plaintiff’s privacy objection is well-taken. The SROGs broadly seek every health care provider for the past 15 years, with no demonstrated relevance to the issues in litigation. Plaintiff did not open up her entire medical history by filing this lawsuit. Plaintiff otherwise provided a responsive, complete answer to the SROGs.

III. SROG F-4

            SROG F-4 seeks a description of all scars Plaintiff has on her body, regardless of origin or etiology. Plaintiff objected and then provided a description of the scars resulting from the surgery at issue in this lawsuit. However, information on other scars is relevant because Plaintiff alleges emotional distress from scarring caused by Defendants. Defendants are entitled to discover potential alternative causes of the scarring and of the alleged distress.

IV. SROG G-1

            SROG G-1 asks Plaintiff to list every social security number she has ever used. Plaintiff’s privacy objection is well-taken. Defendants have not demonstrated that the information is relevant or reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence.

 

CONCLUSION

            Defendants’ motion to compel further responses is GRANTED as to SROG F-4 and DENIED in all other respects. Sanctions are denied as the parties acted with substantial justification.

 


Case Number: 26STCV03602    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 406

 

SHIN P. YANG, 

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

KAZN AM 1300, et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  26STCV03602

  Hearing Date:  September 18, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

plaintiff’s motion to conduct discovery into financial condition (CRS# 7045)

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On February 3, 2026, Plaintiff Shin P. Yang filed this action against Defendants KAZN AM 1300 and Yvonne Liu, asserting (1) equitable enforcement of oral settlement agreement, (2) promissory estoppel, and (3) breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

            The dispute originates from an earlier litigation regarding breach of an advertising agreement, Shin Yang, et al. v. KAZN 1300, et al. (19STCV11399). During the trial in that earlier action, the parties allegedly reached an oral settlement before the court. The alleged agreement was for Defendants to pay Plaintiff $250,000 and provide Plaintiff with one year of free radio advertising in exchange for dismissal of the lawsuit. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants have failed to perform their obligations under the settlement agreement.

            On August 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for authorization to conduct discovery into Defendant Liu’s financial condition. Defendant filed an opposition on September 9, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“No pretrial discovery by the plaintiff shall be permitted with respect to the [defendant’s financial condition] unless the court enters an order permitting such discovery pursuant to this subdivision.” (Civ. Code, § 3295(c).) “Upon motion by the plaintiff supported by appropriate affidavits and after a hearing, if the court deems a hearing to be necessary, the court may at any time enter an order permitting the discovery otherwise prohibited by this subdivision if the court finds, on the basis of the supporting and opposing affidavits presented, that the plaintiff has established that there is a substantial probability that the plaintiff will prevail on the claim pursuant to Section 3294.” (Ibid.)

            “In this context, a ‘substantial probability’ of prevailing on a claim for punitive damages means that it is ‘very likely’ that the plaintiff will prevail on such a claim or there is ‘a strong likelihood’ that the plaintiff will prevail on such a claim.” (Kerner v. Superior Court (2012) 206 Cal.App.4th 84, 120.) “The purpose of this requirement is to protect defendants' financial privacy and prevent defendants from being pressured into settling nonmeritorious cases in order to avoid disclosure of their financial information.” (Ibid.)

DISCUSSION

            Plaintiff requests discovery under Civil Code section 3295(c) but does not even attempt the requisite showing. Plaintiff presents no argument or evidence whatsoever demonstrating a “substantial probability” of prevailing on his claim for punitive damages.

            Plaintiff only argues that he needs the information to pursue his claim for punitive damages. But section 3295(c) is clear in its mandate, and Plaintiff presents no authority to the contrary. Without the showing required under section 3295(c), Plaintiff cannot obtain pretrial discovery of Defendant’s financial information. The discovery will only be pertinent if and when the trier of fact finds malice, oppression, or fraud. (See Civ. Code, § 3295(d); Holdgrafer v. Unocal Corp. (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 907, 919.) The amount of punitive damages will then be litigated separately. (Ibid.)

CONCLUSION

            Plaintiff’s motion to conduct financial discovery is DENIED.

 











 


MARJAN JAHANSHAHI,


                        Plaintiff,


            v.


 


AFSHIN ASHER, et al.,


                        Defendants.

 


  Case No.:  26STCV13770


  Hearing Date:  September 18, 2026


 


     [TENTATIVE]
order RE:


defendants’ demurrer to first amended
complaint

 

 


BACKGROUND



            On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff Marjan
Jahanshahi filed this action against Defendants Afshin Asher and Asher Law
Group, APC. Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint on May 18,
2026, asserting (1) professional negligence, (2) breach of fiduciary duty, and
(3) fraudulent concealment.



            On August 6, 2026, Defendants filed
the instant demurrer to the FAC. Plaintiff filed an opposition on September 4,
2026. Defendants filed a reply on September 11, 2026.



LEGAL STANDARD



A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether
the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When
considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in
context. (Taylor v. City of Los
Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228.)
In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the
pleading or by proper judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30(a).) A
demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic
matters. (SKF Farms v. Superior Court
(1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) “California law emphasizes ultimate fact
pleading (with some exceptions, notably for fraud and related torts) ‘in
ordinary and concise language,’ and the test for adequacy is not absolute but ‘whether
the pleading as a whole apprises the adversary of the factual basis of the
claim.’” (Lim v. The.TV Corp. Internat. (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 684, 690.)



MEET AND CONFER



Before filing a demurrer or a motion to
strike, the demurring or moving party is required to meet and confer with the
party who filed the pleading demurred to or the pleading that is subject to the
motion to strike for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be
reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the
objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.41, 435.5.)
The Court finds that Defendants have satisfied the meet and confer requirement.
(See Parcells Decl.)



DISCUSSION



            According to the FAC, “[t]his action
arises from Defendants’ preparation, handling, transmission, and later
characterization of materials affecting Plaintiff’s property rights, financial
obligations, economic interests, and financial exposure associated with the
transaction and Property, without maintaining or producing a coherent,
contemporaneous documentary record sufficient to identify what materials, if
any, affected Plaintiff’s rights or obligations at any relevant time.” (FAC ¶
1.) “Plaintiff cannot determine what document, if any, affects her rights,
obligations, authority, economic interests, or financial exposure, while she
remains personally obligated on the mortgage and responsible for ongoing
liabilities related to the Property.” (Id., ¶ 2.)



            However, the FAC is uncertain. The
allegations vaguely describe the handling of unspecified documents, and it is
unclear what the transaction pertained to or what Defendants’ involvement was.
The FAC does not describe the relationship between Plaintiff and Defendants.
Plaintiff does not allege that she retained Defendants for legal services.
Thus, the FAC fails to allege any relationship giving rise to a professional
duty of care, fiduciary duties, or a duty to disclose. (See Fox v. Pollack (1986)
181 Cal.App.3d 954, 959 [“an attorney's duty to his or her client depends
on the existence of an attorney-client relationship. If that relationship does
not exist, the fiduciary duty to a client does not arise”].) Plaintiff’s
opposition provides no further clarity and relies on material in Plaintiff’s
declaration, which is external evidence that the Court cannot consider on a
demurrer.



CONCLUSION



            Defendants’ demurrer is SUSTAINED
with leave to amend.



 



 

Case Number: 26STCV03614    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 406

 

KIMBERLINA WHETTAM & ASSOCIATES, INC.,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

HOOMAN NISSANI, et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  26STCV03014

  Hearing Date:  September 18, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

plaintiff’s motion to stay

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff Kimberlina Whettam & Associates, Inc. filed this action against Defendants Hooman Nissani, HSK Properties, LLC, and HMN Corp for recovery of unpaid compensation under a retainer agreement.

            The matter is currently in arbitration. Plaintiff allegedly filed this action to toll the statute of limitations and to provide a forum for enforcing an arbitration award.

            On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to stay the matter pending arbitration. No opposition has been filed.

LEGAL STANDARD

A trial court has statutory authority to control the proceedings before it in the interests of justice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128(a).) Additionally, “[c]ourts have inherent authority to control their own calendars and dockets . . . .” (Walker v. Superior Court (1991) 53 Cal.3d 257, 267.) This includes “the inherent power to stay proceedings in the interests of justice and to promote judicial efficiency.” (Freiberg v. City of Mission Viejo (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1484, 1489.)  

DISCUSSION

            Here, the requested relief has no basis in law because this action was not necessary for Plaintiff to toll the statute of limitations or obtain a judgment on the arbitration award. Plaintiff may file a petition to confirm an arbitration award without filing an independent complaint. For these reasons, the Court finds that a stay is not warranted.

CONCLUSION

            Plaintiff’s motion to stay is DENIED.

            Defendants’ demurrer is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

 

 


Case Number: BC713870    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 406

 

CARLOUS PARKER, et al.,

                        Plaintiffs,

            v.

 

PARKER’S TOWING & SALVAGE, INC., et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  BC713870

  Hearing Date:  September 18, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

plaintiffs’ motion to enforce settlement

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On July 13, 2018, Plaintiffs Carlous Parker and Renee Parker filed this action against Defendants Parker’s Towing & Salvage, Inc.; Kenneth Parker; Yvonne Parker; Donald Parker; Margot Parker; Anthony Glenn; and June Glenn. The complaint asserts partition of real property, accounting, breach of fiduciary duty, conversion, conveyance of property, and involuntary dissolution of corporation.

            On August 24, 2018, Donald Parker and Paker’s Towing & Salvage, Inc. filed a cross-complaint against Carlous Parker and Renee Parker, asserting breach of fiduciary duty, conversion of corporate assets, and intentional interference with prospective economic advantage.

            On December 3, 2020, the Court held a hearing on Order to Show Cause Re: Dismissal (Settlement). There was no appearance by either side, and the Court ordered the action dismissed without prejudice.

            On June 5, 2026, Carlous Parker and Renee Parker (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed the instant motion to enforce settlement. Kenneth Parker and Donald Parker (collectively, Defendants) filed an opposition on September 10, 2026. Plaintiffs filed a reply on September 11, 2026.

LEGAL STANDARD

“If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6(a).)

DISCUSSION

            Section 664.6 provides that upon proper agreement, “the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6(a).) In order for the court to retain jurisdiction upon dismissal, the parties must present the requisite agreement to the court prior to dismissal. (Mesa RHF Partners, L.P. v. City of Los Angeles (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 913, 918; Wackeen v. Malis (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 429, 439-40.) The court cannot retain jurisdiction if the parties never inform the court of the agreement to do so prior to dismissal.

            In this case, no party appeared at the December 3, 2020 OSC re: Dismissal or presented the Court with an agreement to retain jurisdiction. Thus, the Court dismissed the action without prejudice without retaining jurisdiction under section 664.6. Because the Court was not informed of an agreement to retain jurisdiction prior to dismissal, it dismissed the action without retaining such jurisdiction, and cannot acquire jurisdiction for the first time after dismissal.

            Plaintiffs argue that the Court only dismissed “the Complaint filed by CARLOUS B PARKER ET AL on 07/13/2018,” but not the cross-complaint filed on August 24, 2018. (See 12/03/2020 Minute Order.) Plaintiffs argue that no reservation of jurisdiction was necessary because the Court never lost jurisdiction with a cross-complaint still pending. (See Eagle Fire & Water Restoration, Inc. v. City of Dinuba (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 448, 460.) However, this is a clerical error on the minute order. The Court in fact dismissed the entire action consistent with the settlement of the entire case. The Court hereby corrects its December 3, 2020 minute order nunc pro tunc to state the following: “The Court orders the Complaint filed by CARLOUS B PARKER ET AL on 07/13/2018 and the Cross-Complaint filed by DONALD PARKER ET AL on August 24, 2018 dismissed without prejudice.”

            Because the Court dismissed the action without retaining jurisdiction under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, the Court has no jurisdiction to grant the relief requested in the motion.  

CONCLUSION

            Plaintiffs’ motion to enforce settlement is DENIED.
