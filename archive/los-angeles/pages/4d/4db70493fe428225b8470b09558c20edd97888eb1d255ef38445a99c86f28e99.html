Tentative Ruling

Ruiz v. Saxon, Case no. 23VECV03831

Hearing date September 24, 2026

Plaintiff Ruiz’s Petition to Approve Minor’s Compromise - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiffs Ruiz, M. Hernandez, A. Hernandez and minor A. A. Hernandez sue defendant Saxon, trustee of the Lee Saxon Revocable Trust, for breach of lease. Plaintiffs alleged habitability defects and illegal lockout. A notice of settlement was filed. See 1/5/26 Notice. Plaintiff Ruiz petitions for approval of minor’s compromise for A. A. Hernandez. The petition is unopposed.

Minor plaintiff suffered emotional injuries as a result of the lockout and has fully recovered. Minor’s Comp., paras. 5-9. Minor plaintiff is to receive $15,000. Id. at para. 10. The total of proceeds to all plaintiffs is $145,000. Id. at para. 11(b).

Minor plaintiff received no treatment and incurred no medical expenses. Minor’s Comp., para. 12(a). No attorney’s fees are requested. Minor’s Comp., para. 13(a). No costs are requested. Id. at para. 13(b). The net balance of $15,000 is to be deposited into an insured account, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the court. Id. at para. 18(b)(2). The funds are to be deposited in a blocked, interest-bearing account at Chase Bank, PO Box 6185, Westerville, OH 43086. See Attch. 18b(2).

There are two errors on the proposed minor’s compromise. First, paragraph 15 erroneously states minor plaintiff is to receive $150,000, not $15,000. See Minor’s Comp., para. 15. Second, paragraph 18(b)(2) indicates the funds are to be deposited in a financial institution in this state, but attachment 18b(2) states only the out-of-state PO Box to which the deposit will be mailed. See Attch. 18b(2). The attachment must state the branch name and location where the deposit will be held. See Minor’s Comp., para. 18(b)(2).

The terms of the minor’s compromise are reasonable. The petition is unopposed. GRANTED contingent on the above errors being rectified.

Tentative Ruling

Simon v. Nissan North America, Case no. 25VECV05871

Hearing date September 24, 2026

Plaintiff Simon’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures – UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Simon sues defendant Nissan North America, alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Act and moves to compel initial required disclosures per Code Civ. Proc. §871.26 and for monetary sanctions. The motion is unopposed.

In September 2024 the California Legislature enacted Assembly Bill No. 1755, amending the Code of Civil Procedure to establish streamlined procedures for Song-Beverly Act cases. Code Civ. Proc., § 871.20, et seq. These reforms aim to reduce court congestion and facilitate early resolution by mandating initial disclosures and requiring early mediation. Code Civ. Proc. §871.26(c). The statute requires early exchange of documents, initial depositions within 120 days of an answer and mediation within 150 days. Code Civ. Proc. §871.26(b)-(e). All other discovery is stayed, aside from the depositions and required disclosures, pending mediation. Id.

This section applies to actions filed after 1/1/25. Code Civ. Proc. §871.26(l). This matter was filed 10/14/25, so sec. 871.26 applies. 10/14/25 Compl. Pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. §871.26(b), “[w]ithin 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).” Defendant Nissan answered on 1/7/26, so disclosures were due 3/8/26. See 1/7/26 Answer.

Plaintiff’s counsel emailed defense counsel regarding the disclosures due in this and 462 other matters. See Decl. Hill, para. 3, exh. 1. Defense counsel requested permission to produce disclosures on approximately 50 cases per month but failed to satisfy the proposed rate of production. Id., see also Mtn., 7:4-8. The motion is unopposed; defendant failed to show good cause or otherwise explain its failure. An order compelling production is mandatory.

“Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: … [a] two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney …, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).” Code Civ. Proc. §871.26(j)(1). The motion is unopposed; defendant cannot show good cause. Sanctions of $2,500 against defense counsel Sweeney are mandatory.

GRANTED; mandatory disclosures are to be served within 15 days. Sanctions of $2,500 to be paid within 15 court days.


Case Number: 26VECV02686    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Dhaliwal v. Rahlin, Case no. 26VECV02686

Hearing date September 24, 2026

Defendant MCM’s Demurrer to/Motion to Strike FAC

Plaintiff lessees Dhaliwal and Kapoor sue defendants Rahlin and defendant Modern Community Management (“MCM”), alleging water intrusion, moisture, mold contamination, plumbing and HVAC issues and other habitability issues. MCM demurs to the FAC and moves to strike punitive damages allegations. Plaintiffs oppose.

Defendant MCM’s Demurrer to the FAC

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

MCM demurs to the second cause of action for private nuisance. Where an alleged nuisance is based on defendant’s alleged failure to exercise due care, the nuisance claim is governed by the rules of negligence. City of San Diego v. U.S. Gypsum Co. (1990) 30 Cal.App.4th 575, 587. Where the alleged nuisance results from defendant’s alleged negligent conduct, rules of negligence are applied. Martinez v. Pacific Bell (1990) 225 Cal.App.3d 1557, 1565.

MCM argues the nuisance cause of action is duplicative of the negligence cause of action. Lussier v. San Lorenzo Valley Water Dist. (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 92, 104 (holding “[t]he torts of negligence and nuisance ... frequently are, coexisting and practically inseparable.... [a] nuisance in many, if not in most, instances, especially with respect to buildings or premises, presupposes negligence.”).

Plaintiffs allege defendants failed to inspect, maintain, repair and remediate water intrusion, mold growth, plumbing leaks, moisture intrusion and other habitability defects. FAC, paras. 20-25. These same allegations underpin the negligence cause of action. Id. at paras. 77-83. MCM does not dispute the sufficiency of the allegations but argues plaintiffs failed to allege an independent basis for a nuisance claim, separate from the alleged negligence. “Where negligence and nuisance causes of action rely on the same facts about lack of due care, the nuisance claim is a negligence claim.” El Escorial Owners’ Assn. v. DLC Plastering, Inc. (2007) 154 Cal.App.4th 1337, 1349.

Plaintiffs argue a residential tenant may pursue nuisance claims arising from unsafe and dilapidated rental conditions, including against managing agents, citing Stoiber v. Honeychuck (1980) 101 Cal.App.3d 903, 919-921, 929. While Stoiber held, under properly pleaded facts, a tenant could also pursue tort remedies arising from unsafe rental conditions, it does not contradict the later El Escorial, supra, ruling that negligence and nuisance claims arising from the same allegations are duplicative.

Under El Escorial, supra, where the nuisance claim arises from the same alleged negligent conduct and adds no independent theory of liability, the nuisance claim is duplicative of the negligence claim. Plaintiffs must allege with specificity not merely resultant nuisance but different underlying facts or circumstances giving rise to said nuisance. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

MCM demurs to the claim for breach of the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment. “The law is too well settled to require extensive comment or citation of authority that the covenant of quiet enjoyment is not breached until there has been an actual or constructive eviction.” Stockton Theatres, Inc. v. Palermo (1954) 124 Cal.App.2d 353, 360. An actual eviction occurs when the landlord ousts the tenant or allows the tenant to be ousted by one with paramount title. Giraud v. Milovich (1939) 29 Cal.App.2d 543, 547. There is no breach unless there is a disturbance of possession. Slater v. Conti (1959) 171 Cal.App.2d 582, 585.

MCM argues plaintiffs remain in possession, so cannot show “meaningful disturbance to their right to possession.” Mtn., 8:24-25. The FAC alleges plaintiffs signed a lease in May 2025 and renewed the lease until the present day. FAC, paras. 131, 133. The FAC alleges breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment because MCM’s “acts and omissions rendered substantial portions of the premises unusable and unsuitable for the residential purposes for which they were leased.” Id. at para. 134. The FAC does not allege relinquishment of possession or actual eviction.

Plaintiffs argue actual possession or surrender is not a required element of a breach of covenant claim. A tenant may treat sufficiently serious interference as constructive eviction and surrender possession or may remain in possession and sue for breach of contract damages. Andrews v. Mobile Aire Estates (2005) 125 Cal.App.4th 578, at 588-590. There, plaintiffs were not required to relinquish possession or allege actual eviction to sustain a claim for breach of the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment.

Plaintiffs alleged substantial interference with enjoyment of the property such that whether plaintiffs were constructively evicted is a question of fact not properly resolved on demurrer. See FAC, paras. 135-137.

In reply, MCM first argues that plaintiffs fail to allege facts showing MCM was party to the underlying lease or owed plaintiffs a contractual covenant of quiet enjoyment. Defendants’ meet and confer letter attached to demurrer, which states the bases for the demurrer, does not include this issue. See Decl. Poitras, para. 2, exh. A. A party cannot raise a new argument in reply not set forth in the moving papers and which opposing party did not have the opportunity to address. This is improper and cannot be considered. OVERRULED.

Defendant MCM’s Motion to Strike

As the court sustained MCM’s demurrer to the second cause of action with leave to amend, the motion to strike punitive damages allegations is MOOT.
