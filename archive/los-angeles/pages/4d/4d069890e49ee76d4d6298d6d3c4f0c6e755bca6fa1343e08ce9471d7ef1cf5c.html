CASE:                            Mike Tehrani, et al. v. Mehrdad Adjari

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV08288

 

 

PURE ECO, INC.’S DEMURRER TO SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant Pure Eco, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Plaintiffs Mike Tehrani and Eskan Builders Corp.

 

 

            This case concerns the property located at 12428 Laurel Terrace Drive, Studio City, California 91604 (the Property).  Defendant Mehrdad Adjari (Adjari) is the owner of the Property.  In 2021, Adjari and plaintiffs Mike Tehrani and Eskan Builders, Corp. (Plaintiffs) entered into a written contract wherein Plaintiff agreed to provide labor, services, equipment, or materials for a construction project on the Property.  Plaintiffs completed the work.  Adjari failed to pay Plaintiffs for the amount due. 

 

            On April 16, 2026, Adjari filed the operative First Amended Cross-Complaint against Plaintiffs for breach of contract and negligence.

 

On February 20, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the operative Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”) alleging twenty-one causes of action.  For the first time, Plaintiffs name Pure Eco, Inc. (“Pure Eco”) in the SAC as a defendant.  Pure Eco performed construction work on a portion of the Property.

 

            On June 17, 2026, Pure Eco filed a demurrer to the 14th-20th causes of action in the SAC.

 

            On July 15, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition.

 

            On July 21, 2026, Pure Eco filed a reply.

 

            The demurrer was heard on July 28, 2026.  After oral argument, the court adopted the tentative ruling finding that Pure Eco did not meet and confer prior to filing this demurrer.  The court continued the hearing and directed the parties to meet and confer.  The court also directed the parties to file a supplemental opposition or supplemental reply pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1005.  Alternatively, Pure Eco was to file a status report no later than 5 court days before the hearing indicating whether the court should rule on the demurrer based on the current briefing.

 

            At the time of this writing, no supplemental briefing or status report has been filed with the court.

 

            Based on the foregoing, the hearing for the demurrer is CONTINUED to September 14, 2026.  The parties are ordered to meet and confer within 5 court days of this order and to submit a status report no later than 5 court days before the hearing. 

 

The court also sets an OSC Re: Failure to Meet and Confer for September 14, 2026.  No less than 10 court days before the OSC, Pure Eco is ordered to file a response to the OSC explaining why they failed to meet and confer and/or failed to file a status report in violation of the court’s July 28, 2026 order.

 

            Demurring party to give notice.

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     August 17, 2026                                 TRIAL DATE:  November 15, 2027

                                                          

CASE:                         Samantha Brustin v. Petrol Advertising, Inc., et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV13689

 

ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: RE CONTEMPT FOR DEFENDANT’S WILLFUL DISOBEDIENCE OF THE PROTECTIVE ORDER ENTERED BY THE COURT ON JULY 28, 2025  

 

ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: RE MANDATORY INTERNET SWEEP

 

ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: RE WHETHER DEFENDANT’S COUNSEL MUST CREATE CLE HIPAA COMPLIANCE VIDEO

 

I.          BACKGROUND

 

            Plaintiff Samantha Brustin (“Brustin” or “Plaintiff”) brings this employment action against her former employers, defendants Petrol Advertising, Inc. (“Petrol”) and Enad Global 7 AB (PUBL) (“EG7”) (collectively, “Defendants”).  Brustin worked for Defendants from November 2022 until she was terminated in April 2024.  Brustin alleges that, during her employment, she was subjected to sexual harassment, a hostile work environment, and retaliation for taking CFRA leave.  Plaintiff was terminated a month after she returned from medical leave. 

 

On June 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an ex parte application for an order (1) sealing portions of the documents filed by Petrol on May 27 and 28, 2026; (2) issuing an Order to Show Cause (OSC) Re Contempt for Defendant’s willful disobedience of the protective order entered by the court on July 28, 2025; (3) issuing an OSC Re Mandatory Internet Sweep to immediately contact all online legal databases and docket aggregators (including but not limited to Trellis, Law360, Lexis, and Westlaw) and make reasonable, documented efforts to contact all  known online legal databases and docket aggregators, including Trellis, Law360, Lexis, and Westlaw, and request removal, suppression, or replacement of any scraped public versions of the confidential medical filings pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 128 at the sole expense of Defendant and their counsel of record; (4) issuing an OSC Re whether Jackson Lewis, P.C. and Henry Sanchez, Esq., within ninety days of the Court’s order, must create, produce, and have Mr. Sanchez star in a one-hour Continuing Legal Education video focused on HIPAA compliance and the protection of third-party privacy rights for this Court’s review and approval, and shall subsequently file a declaration under penalty of perjury within six months from the video’s completion date confirming that all attorneys employed by Jackson Lewis P.C. have watched the video in its entirety; and (5) for other such relief as the Court deems just, necessary, and proper. 

 

On June 4, 2026, the court granted in part the ex parte application and ordered stricken Petrol’s oppositions and supporting materials filed on May 27 and 28, 2026 and directed Petrol to re-file those documents consistent with the procedures outlined in the Protective Order.  The court also set the following OSCs:

 

1.  OSC Re Contempt for Defendant’s willful disobedience of the protective order entered by the court on July 28, 2025; 

 

2. OSC Re Mandatory Internet Sweep  

 

3.  OSC Re Whether Defendant’s Counsel Must Create CLE HIPAA Compliance Video. 

 

The court also ordered Defense Counsel to file a declaration addressing the above OSCs.

 

On June 12, 2026, Defense counsel filed declarations. 

 

On June 29, 2026, the parties appeared before the court.  In advance of the hearing, the court issued a tentative ruling stating it was not inclined to hold Defense Counsel in contempt nor require Defense Counsel to create a CLE HIPAA compliance video.  Defense counsel represented that they would hire a professional firm to conduct the internet sweep.  The court stated it was considering a monetary sanction but would wait to learn of the cost of the internet sweep.  The OSCs were continued.

 

On July 14, 2026, Defense counsel filed another declaration.

 

II.        DISCUSSION

 

            Defense counsel state they have hired Erase Technologies, LLC dba Guaranteed

Removals to conduct the internet sweep at the cost of $3,000.  (Steffan Decl., ¶5.)  The sweep will be completed in 60 days.  (Id.)  

 

Given Defense counsel’s efforts to remove Plaintiff’s information and the court’s previous findings that Defense counsel’s failure to observe the terms of the Protective Order was not willful, the court declines impose any monetary sanctions.

 

III.       CONCLUSION

           

Based on the foregoing, the OSCs are DISCHARGED.

 

            Defendant is ordered to give notice, unless waived.

CASE NO.:                   25STCV25221

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL DEFENDANT DEL TACO, LLC'S COMPLIANCE WITH PLAINTIFF'S DEMAND FOR PRODUCTION, SET ONE

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Priscilla Solis

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      Defendant Del Taco, LLC

 

 

I.          BACKGROUND

 

This is a FEHA and wrongful termination action.  On May 29, 2026, Plaintiff, Priscilla Solis, filed a motion to compel Defendant, Del Taco, LLC, to comply with Plaintiff’s Demand for Production, Set One.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendant and its counsel of record.

 

            On June 16, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.  Plaintiff did not file a reply.

 

            The motion was heard on July 21, 2026.  In advance of the hearing, the court issued a tentative ruling to continue the hearing to August 17, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.  The court ordered the parties to meet and confer regarding the sufficiency of Defendant’s late document production.  Plaintiff was further ordered to file a reply or status report no later than 7 court days before the continued hearing date. 

 

On August 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.         DISCUSSION

 

According to Plaintiff’s reply, Defendant has still not produced all requested documents. Given that Defendant has failed to produce all documents consistent with its statement of compliance, and only partially produced documents during the pendency of this motion, the court will grant the motion and impose sanctions against Defendant and its counsel of record in the requested sum of $900.

 

III.       CONCLUSION

 

The motion to compel compliance is GRANTED. Defendant is ordered to comply and produce documents in accordance with Defendant’s statements of compliance within 10 days of this order.

 

Defendant and its counsel of record are ordered to pay, jointly and severally, sanctions in the sum of $900 within 30 days of this order.

 

            Plaintiff is ordered to give notice, unless waived.

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     August 17, 2026                                             TRIAL DATE:   Not set

                                                          

CASE:                         Luis E. Valle, On Behalf of Himself and Tax Payers of the State of California v. Richard Camacho, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV37281

 

 

MOTION FOR SUMMARY ADJUDICATION FOR UNDISPUTED FUNDS

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Luis E. Valle, in pro per

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant Los Angeles Community College District

 

 

I.          INTRODUCTION

 

            On December 23, 2025, Plaintiff Luis E. Valle dba as Valle Grande Construction, on behalf of himself and tax payers of the State of California, commenced this action against defendants Richard Camacho, Vanir Construction Management, Inc., Hector Diaz, VIS Inspection Services, Inc., Janay Wilson, Cordoba Corporation, Marco Tarantino, Julio Benitez, Jacobs Engineering Group Inc., Los Angeles Community College District (“LACCD”).  Plaintiff brings causes of action for breach of contract, breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, statutory retaliation, civil extortion, civil conspiracy, declaratory and injunctive relief, and retaliatory breach of contract.   

 

            On May 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Motion for Summary Adjudication of Undisputed Funds.  Plaintiff seeks an order directing LACCD to immediately release and remit to Plaintiff the contractual sum of $350,000.

 

            On July 28, 2026, LACCD filed an opposition.

 

            On August 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.        DISCUSSION

 

            Plaintiff’s motion is procedurally defective.  A motion for summary adjudication “shall be granted only if it completely disposes of a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for damages, or an issue of duty.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (f)(1).)  “The purpose of the enactment of Code of Civil Procedure section 437c, subdivision (f) was to stop the practice of piecemeal adjudication of facts that did not completely dispose of a substantive area.” (Catalano v. Superior Court (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 91, 97.)

 

            Here, Plaintiff’s motion does not seek adjudication of any issue enumerated in section 437c.  Plaintiff does not identify a cause of action to be adjudicated, an affirmative defense to be resolved, a claim for damages, or an issue of duty.  Instead, Plaintiff’s motion seeks adjudication of one factual issue: whether certain contract funds in the total sum of $350,000 should be paid.  Adjudication of this narrow issue would still leave unsettled Plaintiff’s breach of contract claims, LACCD’s affirmative defenses, and the parties’ remaining contractual disputes. This is the very sort of piecemeal adjudication subdivision (f) of the summary judgment statute was designed to prevent.  On this ground alone, the motion fails.

 

III.       CONCLUSION

 

            The motion for summary adjudication is DENIED.

 

            Defendant is ordered to give notice, unless waived.
