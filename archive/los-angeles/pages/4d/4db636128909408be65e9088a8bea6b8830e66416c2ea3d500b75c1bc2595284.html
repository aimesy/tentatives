(1)   Plaintiff Eric Abbiss’ Motion for Attorney’s Fees is DENIED pursuant to Civil Code section 1780.

The Court considers the moving papers filed on June 1, 2026, the opposition brief filed on July 1, 2026, and the reply brief filed on July 8, 2026.

BACKGROUND

Factual Background

On July 28, 2023, plaintiff Eric Abbiss (“Plaintiff”) filed their Complaint against defendants Exo Energy, Inc. (“Defendant”) and DOES 1 through 100.  The Complaint includes the following causes of action: (1) Unlawful Business Practices and (2) Negligence.

On June 9, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Strike Defendant’s Answer, which allowed Plaintiff to request a default judgment.

On September 8, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed DOES 1 through 100.  On that same day, the Court entered a default judgment against Defendant, ordering judgment in the amount of $234,458.39.

On December 3, 2025, the Court granted Defendant’s Motion for Order to Set Aside Default Judgment and to Set Aside the Order Striking Defendant’s Answer and Issuing Terminating Sanctions.  (December 3, 2025, Minute Order, p. 6.)  Additionally, the Court ordered Defendant’s attorney, Benjamin Graff, to pay Plaintiff’s counsel $14,990.00 by December 31, 2025.  (December 3, 2025, Minute Order, p. 6.)

On May 18, 2026, Plaintiff allegedly accepted a Code of Civil Procedure section 998 Offer that did not include fees and costs.  (Motion, p. 2:3-4.)

On May 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed a memorandum of costs, seeking $1,719.55 in costs.

On June 5, 2026, the Court entered judgment against Defendant’s attorney, Benjamin, and in favor of Plaintiff, awarding $14,990.00.  (June 5, 2026, Judgment, p. 1:22-23.)

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

The Court takes Judicial Notice pursuant to Evidence Code Section 452 of November 27, 2024, Minute Order, Exhibit 1.  However, the Court will not take judicial notice of the truth of the matters presented in these documents since they are subject to interpretation.  (See L.B. Research & Education Foundation v. UCLA Foundation (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 171, 180, fn. 2, citing Comings v. State Bd. of Education (1972) 23 Cal.App.3d 94.)

LEGAL STANDARD

Attorneys’ fees are allowed as costs when authorized by contract, statute, or law.  (Code Civ. Proc, § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10)(B).)  “The court shall award court costs and attorney’s fees to a prevailing plaintiff in litigation filed pursuant to this section.  Reasonable attorney’s fees may be awarded to a prevailing defendant upon a finding by the court that the plaintiff’s prosecution of the action was not in good faith.”  (Civ. Code, § 1780, subd. (e).)

A plaintiff’s verified billing invoices are prima facie evidence that the costs, expenses, and services listed were necessarily incurred.  (See Hadley v. Krepel (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 677, 682.)  “In challenging attorney fees as excessive because too many hours of work are claimed, it is the burden of the challenging party to point to the specific items challenged, with a sufficient argument and citations to the evidence.  General arguments that fees claimed are excessive, duplicative, or unrelated do not suffice.”  (Lunada Biomedical v. Nunez (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 459, 488, quoting Premier Med. Mgmt. Sys., Inc. v. California Ins. Guarantee Ass’n (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 564.)  The Court has discretion to reduce fees that result from inefficient or duplicative use of time.  (Horsford v. Bd. of Trustees of California State Univ. (2005) 132 Cal. App. 4th 359, 395.)

In determining a reasonable attorney fee, the trial court begins with the lodestar, i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate.  (Warren v. Kia Motors America, Inc. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 24, 36.)  The lodestar may then be adjusted based on factors specific to the case in order to fix the fee at the fair market value of the legal services provided.  (Ibid.)  The factors that courts look at to determine if a multiplier is reasonable are: “(1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, and the skill displayed in presenting them; (2) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys; (3) the contingent nature of the fee award” and (4) the success achieved.  (Serrano v. Priest (1977) 20 Cal.3d 25, 49 (Serrano).)

No specific findings reflecting the court’s calculations are required.  The record only needs to show that the attorney’s fees were awarded according to the “lodestar” or “touchstone” approach.  The court’s focus in evaluating the facts should be to provide a fee award reasonably designed to completely compensate attorneys for the services provided.¿ The starting point for this determination is the attorney’s time records.  (Horsford v. Board of Trustees of Calif. State Univ. (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 395-397 [verified time records entitled to credence absent clear indication they are erroneous].)  However, California case law permits fee awards in the absence of detailed time sheets.  (Sommers v. Erb (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 1644, 1651; Dunk v. Ford Motor Co. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 1794, 1810; Nightingale v. Hyundai Motor America (1994) 31 Cal.App.4th 99, 103.)  An experienced trial judge is in a position to assess the value of the professional services rendered in his or her court.  (Serrano, supra, 20 Cal.3d 25 at p. 49.)

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff contends entitlement to $30,880.00 in attorney’s fees pursuant to Civil Code section 1780, subdivision (e).  (Motion, p. 2:5-11.)  “The court shall award court costs and attorney’s fees to a prevailing plaintiff in litigation filed pursuant to this section.  Reasonable attorney’s fees may be awarded to a prevailing defendant upon a finding by the court that the plaintiff’s prosecution of the action was not in good faith.”  (Civ. Code, § 1780, subd. (e).)  Here, Plaintiff has made no efforts to demonstrate that they prevailed in litigation filed pursuant to Civil Code section 1780.  Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED.

ORDERS

1)      Plaintiff Eric Abbiss’ Motion for Attorney’s Fees is DENIED.

2)      Plaintiff is ordered to provide notice of this Court’s ruling.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

(1)   PLAINTIFF MARISELA G. VARGAS’ MOTION TO CONSOLIDATE ACTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY:                Plaintiff, Marisela G. Vargas

RESPONDING PARTY:       Defendant, County of Los Angeles

 

(1)   Plaintiff Marisela G. Vargas’ Motion to Consolidate Actions is DENIED pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1048 and California Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1)(C).

The Court considers the moving papers filed on June 26, 2026, and the opposition brief filed on July 15, 2026.  To date, no reply brief has been filed.

BACKGROUND

Factual Background

On January 3, 2025, plaintiff Marisela G. Vargas (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint against defendants City of Inglewood, County of Los Angeles, State of California, Jimmy D’s Inc., and DOES 1 through 100.  The Complaint includes the following causes of action: (1) Dangerous Condition of Public Property; (2) Negligence; and (3) Premises Liability.

On March 12, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed City of Inglewood without prejudice.

On May 23, 2025, Jimmy D’s Inc. filed the Cross-Complaint against cross-defendants City of Inglewood, County of Los Angeles, State of California, and ROES 1 through 20.  The Cross-Complaint includes the following causes of action: (1) Equitable Indemnity; (2) Partial Equitable Indemnity; (3) Contribution and Equitable Apportionment; and (4) Declaratory Relief.

On June 6, 2025, Jimmy D’s Inc. dismissed City of Inglewood without prejudice.

On September 2, 2025, Jimmy D’s Inc. dismissed State of California without prejudice.

On September 4, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed State of California with prejudice.

On February 18, 2026, Plaintiff dismissed Jimmy D’s Inc. without prejudice.

On March 4, 2026, Jimmy D’s Inc. dismissed County of Los Angeles without prejudice.

On March 5, 2026, County of Los Angeles filed a Notice of Related Case, notifying the Court that Raquel Garcia v. County of Los Angeles, et al., Case No. 25TRCV00220, and Ruben Sanchez v. County of Los Angeles, et al., Case No. 25TRCV00999, are related cases.

On March 25, 2026, Jimmy D’s Inc. dismissed the Cross-Complaint without prejudice.

The plaintiffs in Case Nos. 25TRCV00017, 25TRCV00220, and 25TRCV00999 have drafted a Joint Stipulation to Consolidate Related Cases.  (Declaration of Mohammed Bashraheel (“Bashraheel Decl.”), ¶¶ 5-6, Exh. A.)

LEGAL STANDARD

California Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a) states:

(1) A notice of motion to consolidate must:

(A) List all named parties in each case, the names of those who have appeared, and the names of their respective attorneys of record;

(B) Contain the captions of all the cases sought to be consolidated, with the lowest-numbered case shown first; and

(C) Be filed in each case sought to be consolidated.

(2) The motion to consolidate:

(A) Is deemed a single motion for the purpose of determining the appropriate filing fee, but memorandums, declarations, and other supporting papers must be filed only in the lowest-numbered case;

(B) Must be served on all attorneys of record and all non-represented parties in all of the cases sought to be consolidated; and

(C) Must have a proof of service filed as part of the motion.

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a).)

Additionally, Code of Civil Procedure section 1048, subdivision (a), states: “When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the court, it may order a joint hearing or trial of any or all the matters in issue in the actions; it may order all the actions consolidated and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.”

The decision to grant or deny a motion to consolidate is within the trial court’s sound discretion and will only be reversed if there is a clear showing of abuse of that discretion.  (See Fellner v. Steinbaum (1955) 132 Cal.App.2d 509, 511.)

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves to consolidate this action with Case Nos. 25TRCV00220 and 25TRCV00999 on the grounds that “the actions arise out of the same or substantially similar allegations (i.e. dangerous condition of a public curb), involve common questions of law or fact, and thereby consolidation will avoid unnecessary costs or delay.”  (Motion, p. 4:7-9.) 

In accordance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1), Plaintiff has: (A) identified all named parties and their respective attorneys of record in both cases; and (B) provided the captions of both cases to be consolidated, listing the lowest-numbered case first.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1)(A)-(B).)

Furthermore, in accordance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(2), Plaintiff’s motion complied with the following conditions: (A) the memorandums, declarations, and other supporting documents were filed in the lowest-numbered case; (B) the motion has been served on all attorneys of record; and (C) a proof of service is attached and has been filed as part of the motion.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(2)(A)-(C).)

Plaintiff maintains that the incidents in question occurred at the “same exact unmarked dangerous curb” and states that “[t]he maintenance and condition of the curb was identical in all three instances.”  (Motion, p. 5:4-6.)  Furthermore, Plaintiff claims that “[t]he responsible parties are identical in all three” incidents and emphasizes that “the evidence that would be required to support and prove their allegations of negligence and a dangerous condition of Public Property are identical for all three [p]laintiffs.”  (Motion, p. 5:6-8.)  Plaintiff posits that the only variance lies in the damages asserted by each plaintiff, given the distinct injuries, medical histories, and pain and suffering involved.  (Motion, p. 5:8-11.)

However, the County of Los Angeles aptly notes that “the circumstances surrounding each alleged incident are different, including the date, [p]laintiff involved, manner in which the incident allegedly occurred, and the specific condition encountered.”  (Opp., p. 5:2-3.)  These distinctions necessitate individualized determinations regarding liability, encompassing considerations such as “whether a dangerous condition existed, whether the condition caused the alleged injury, whether the County had notice or sufficient time to respond, and whether the Plaintiff exercised reasonable care all require individualized determinations.”  (Opp., p. 5:6-8.)  Such differences will also result in “individualized discovery concerning each Plaintiff’s alleged incident, injuries, damages, and liability issues.”  (Opp., p. 6:20-21.)

Moreover, the Court observes that consolidating these cases may prejudice the County of Los Angeles by potentially confusing the jury.  The County of Los Angeles articulates that “[p]resenting three alleged incidents involving the same general location to a single jury creates a significant risk that the jury may improperly infer that, because multiple individuals allegedly experienced incidents at the same location, the condition must necessarily have been dangerous or that the County must necessarily be liable.”  (Opp., p. 5:16-19.)  Furthermore, consolidation could “create a risk that evidence concerning one [p]laintiff’s claim would improperly influence the evaluation of another [p]laintiff’s claim.”  (Opp., p.5:27-28.)

Moreover, Plaintiff failed to file the notice of this motion under Case Nos. 25TRCV00220 and 25TRCV00999.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1)(C).)

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED.

ORDERS

1)      Plaintiff Marisela G. Vargas’ Motion to Consolidate Actions is DENIED.

2)      Plaintiff is ordered to provide notice of this Court’s ruling in Case Nos. 25TRCV00017, 25TRCV00220, and 25TRCV00999.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
