Case Number: 18STLC08931    Hearing Date: September 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal and Enter Judgment is granted. Judgment is to be entered in Plaintiff’s favor and against Defendant Connor Kostman in the amount of $1,560.00 unpaid principal and costs of $494.50.

 

The proposed judgment filed on June 24, 2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On June 18, 2018, Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed this subrogation action against Defendant Connor Kostman (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint on August 15, 2018. On December 20, 2018, Plaintiff filed a Stipulation and Order for Dismissal. The Court entered the stipulation and dismissed the action on the same date. (Stip and Order, filed 12/20/18.)

 

On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Vacate Dismissal and Enter Judgment. The Motion was served on Defendant through counsel by first-class and electronic mail. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion is brought under Code of Civil Procedure, section 664.6, which states in relevant part:

 

If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. If the parties to the settlement agreement or their counsel stipulate in writing or orally before the court, the court may dismiss the case as to the settling parties without prejudice and retain jurisdiction over the parties to enforce the settlement until performance in full of the terms of the settlement.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (a).) “Parties” include “an attorney who represents the party” and an insurer’s agent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6, subd. (b).) The settlement must include the signatures of the parties seeking to enforce the agreement, and against whom enforcement is sought. (J.B.B. Investment Partners, Ltd. v. Fair (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 974, 985.) The settlement agreement complies with the statutory requirements set forth above because it was signed by both parties and their attorneys. (Motion, Espinoza Decl., Exh. A, p. 4.)

 

The settlement provided that Defendant pay Plaintiff $9,500.00, starting with an initial payment from Defendant’s insurer on November 30, 2018, followed by monthly payments. (Id. at Exh. A, ¶2.) The settlement agreement also provided that in the event of Defendant’s default, Plaintiff may seek judgment in the amount prayed for in the Complaint, less credit for all payments made prior to default, plus any court costs. (Id. at Exh. A, ¶3.) A total of $7,940.00 was paid towards the settlement, after which Defendant defaulted. (Id. at ¶4.) Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to judgment in the amount of $1,560.00 unpaid principal ($9,500.00 - $7,940.00) and costs of $494.50. (Id. at ¶7.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal and Enter Judgment is granted. Judgment is to be entered in Plaintiff’s favor and against Defendant Connor Kostman in the amount of $1,560.00 unpaid principal and costs of $494.50.

 

The proposed judgment filed on June 24, 2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 23VECV05342    Hearing Date: September 1, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Emeka Victor Okwudili Peter’s Motion for Discretionary Stay of Vexatious Litigant Prefiling Order Pending Appeal is denied.

 

Plaintiff’s Motion for Reconsideration of Order Declaring Plaintiff a Vexatious Litigant set for hearing on September 16, 2026 is taken off calendar.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On December 4, 2023, Plaintiff Emeka Victor Okwudili Peter (“Plaintiff”), in propria persona, filed the Complaint in this action against Defendants Cooperative Regions of Organic Producer Pools (“Defendant CROPP”), erroneously sued as Cooperative Regions of Organic Producer Pools d/b/a Organic Valley and CROPP Cooperative, The Kroger Co. (“Defendant Kroger”), and Ralphs Grocery Company dba Ralphs (“Defendant Ralphs”). The Complaint alleged causes of action for (1) general negligence; (2) negligence per se; (3) strict liability; and (4) negligent hiring, retention, and control of employees. Specifically, Plaintiff alleged that he became very ill after drinking spoiled milk purchased from Defendants. The action was originally assigned to the Unlimited Civil Court.

 

On January 2, 2024, Ralphs filed its Answer to Plaintiff’s Complaint. On January 12, 2024, Defendant CROPP filed a Demurrer to the Complaint and Motion to Strike Portions of the Complaint. On March 29, 2024, Defendant CROPP filed a Cross-Complaint against Defendants Kroger and Ralphs, to which Defendant Ralphs filed its Answer on April 23, 2024. The Court sustained Defendant CROPP’s Demurrer to the Complaint and ordered Plaintiff to file a First Amended Complaint within 30 days. (Minute Order, 05/07/24.)

 

On May 30, 2024, the Court granted Defendant Ralphs’ Motion to Reclassify the action to a Limited Civil Court. (Minute Order, 05/30/24.) Following Plaintiff’s failure to file a First Amended Complaint, the Court granted Defendant CROPP’s Motion re: For Judgment of Dismissal. (Minute Order, 08/01/24.) Trial in this action was initially called on September 12, 2024 and continued to October 15, 2024. (Minute Order, 09/12/24.) The Court also set an Order to Show Cause Re: Why Plaintiff Should Not Pay $500 Sanctions for Failure to Meet and Confer/File Trial Documents. (Ibid.)

 

After the first Order to Show Cause was discharged, the Court set a second Order to Show Cause Re: Sanctions of $100.00 for Plaintiff for Failure to Appear for Trial on June 11, 2025. (Minute Order, 06/11/25.) The action came for trial on July 18, 2025 and the jury came back with a defense verdict on July 21, 2025. (Minute Orders, 07/18/25 and 07/21/25.) The Court entered an order of dismissal on August 14, 2025.

 

Plaintiff filed a Motion for New Trial, which the Court denied on October 1, 2025. (Minute Order, 10/01/25.) Plaintiff then filed a Motion for Reconsideration of Order Denying Motion for New Trial, which the Court denied on October 28, 2025. (Ruling on Motion, 10/28/25.) Plaintiff also filed a Notice of Appeal on October 16, 2025. Plaintiff filed a Motion for Sanctions for Bad Faith Conduct on April 22, 2026. On June 3, 2026, the Court partially granted Defendant Ralphs’ Motion to Deem Plaintiff a Vexatious Litigant and entered a pre-filing order against Plaintiff. (Minute Order, 06/03/26.)

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Discretionary Stay of Vexatious Litigant Prefiling Order Pending Appeal on June 11, 2026. Plaintiff also filed a Motion for Reconsideration of Order Declaring Plaintiff a Vexatious Litigant on June 15, 2026, which is set for hearing on September 16, 2026. The Court denied the Sanctions Motion on July 28, 2026. (Minute Order, 07/28/26.)

 

 

 

On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Appeal with respect to the June 3, 2026 order declaring Plaintiff a vexatious litigant. Defendant Ralphs’ opposition to the instant Motion was filed on August 13, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

As an initial matter, the Court notes that Defendant Ralphs’ opposition to the instant Motion was served on Plaintiff by only electronic mail. (Opp., p. 53.) Unrepresented parties such as Plaintiff must affirmatively consent to electronic service. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.251(c)(3)(B).)  Papers in this action have been previously served on Plaintiff by mail. (See Opp. to Mot., 7/15/26, p. 11.)  The Court is not aware of any record demonstrating that Plaintiff has agreed to service by email.  Service of the opposition upon Plaintiff by electronic mail, therefore, does not meet the statutory requirements for notice. Failure to give notice of the papers and hearing date is a violation of due process and of the statutory notice requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.) Accordingly, the Court will not consider the opposition in its ruling on the instant Motion.  

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Stay is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 918, which states in relevant part:

 

(a) Subject to subdivision (b), the trial court may stay the enforcement of any judgment or order.

 

(b) If the enforcement of the judgment or order would be stayed on appeal only by the giving of an undertaking, a trial court shall not have power, without the consent of the adverse party, to stay the enforcement thereof pursuant to this section for a period which extends for more than 10 days beyond the last date on which a notice of appeal could be filed.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 918, subds. (a), (b).) Plaintiff argues that “[i]n balancing the harms, the Court must look to whether a stay will financially or legally prejudice the moving party,” (Motion, p. 3:21-22) and argues that Defendant Ralphs will suffer no financial prejudice if the prefiling order is stayed, and also argues that his appeal contesting the designation is brought in good faith. 

 

Before addressing the merits of the request, the Court clarifies the scope of the pre-filing order.  Plaintiff characterizes the pre-filing order as requiring him to “seek leave from the Presiding Judge for every single procedural step required to advance this case.” (Mot., p. 4.)  That is not accurate.  The pre-filing order does not require Plaintiff to seek leave to file documents in this case, or any case pending at the time it was entered.  It requires Presiding Judge permission only before filing additional lawsuits as a self-represented plaintiff.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 391.7.) As the Court explained in Shalant v. Girardi (2011) 51 Cal. 4th 1164:

 

[O]ur vexatious litigant statutes provide courts and nonvexatious litigants with two distinct and complementary sets of remedies. In pending litigation, a defendant may have the plaintiff declared a vexatious litigant and, if the plaintiff has no reasonable probability of prevailing, ordered to furnish security. If the plaintiff fails to furnish the security, the action will be dismissed. (§§ 391.1–391.6.) In addition, a potential defendant may prevent the vexatious litigant plaintiff from filing any new litigation in propria persona by obtaining a prefiling order and, if any new litigation is inadvertently permitted to be filed in propria persona without the presiding judge's permission, may then obtain its dismissal. (§ 391.7.)

 

(Id. at 1171 (bold and underlining added).)  Here, only the latter remedy applies.  The pre-filing order entered in this case on June 3, 2026 only operates to prevent Plaintiff “from filing any new litigation in propria persona” without “obtaining a prefiling order.”  (Id.) The Court notes that its order of June 3, 2026 may have created a contrary impression where it stated: “Self-represented Plaintiff must obtain an order from the Presiding Judge of Los Angeles Superior Court under CCP § 391.7(c) to proceed with this case.” (Minute Order, 06/03/2026 (underlining added).)  To be clear, Plaintiff does not need to obtain Presiding Judge permission to file documents in this case. 

 

This mis-impression appears to be the basis of Plaintiff’s request for stay.  (See Mot., p.4; Peter Decl., ¶5.)  The Motion does not refer to any intention to file new, separate litigation in propria persona as a justification for the requested stay. Accordingly, with the scope of the pre-filing order clarified, the Motion presents no basis to stay the pre-filing order pending determination of Plaintiff’s appeal.  The Court denies the Motion.

 

The Court notes that Plaintiff has pending a Motion for Reconsideration of the Court’s Order declaring Plaintiff vexatious, which is set for hearing in approximately two weeks, on September 16, 2026.  The Court lacks jurisdiction to hear that motion or to reconsider its Order deeming Plaintiff vexatious because Plaintiff has filed a Notice of Appeal from the Order.  Filing an appeal of the Order deprives this Court of jurisdiction to reconsider the Order while the appeal is pending.  (See Appeal-Notice of Appeal, 07/08/2026; see also Code Civ. Proc., § 916(a) (“the perfecting of an appeal stays proceedings in the trial court upon the judgment or order appealed from or upon the matters embraced therein or affected thereby”).)  Accordingly, the Court takes off calendar the Motion to Reconsider. 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Emeka Victor Okwudili Peter’s Motion for Discretionary Stay of Vexatious Litigant Prefiling Order Pending Appeal is denied.

 

Plaintiff’s Motion for Reconsideration of Order Declaring Plaintiff a Vexatious Litigant set for hearing on September 16, 2026 is taken off calendar.

Case Number: 26STLC00593    Hearing Date: September 1, 2026    Dept: 26

 

Defendant Osiel Escobar’s Motion for Order Setting Aside Request for Entry of Default is granted. The default entered on 04/01/2026 is hereby vacated. Defendant is to file and serve the proposed Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this ruling. Defense counsel Brian P. Smith is to pay Plaintiff’s counsel $750.00 within 20 days of this ruling.

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff Deric Situ (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Osiel Escobar (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, Plaintiff filed a request for entry of default on April 1, 2026, which the Court entered the same day.

 

Defendant filed a first Motion for Order Setting Aside Entry of Default on May 11, 2026, which the Court denied without prejudice on June 17, 2026. (Minute Order, 06/17/26.) Defendant filed the instant Amended Motion for Order Setting Aside Entry of Default on July 17, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on August 18, 2026 and Defendant replied on August 25, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to vacate the entry of default and default judgment pursuant to the mandatory provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Under this statute, an application for relief must be made within six months of default judgment and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the party or attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) The Motion must also attach a copy of the moving party’s response to the Complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Defendant timely filed and served the instant Motion three-and-a-half months after the entry of default and attached a copy of their proposed Answer to Complaint. (Motion, Smith Decl., Exh. A.) The Motion is also supported by a declaration of fault from defense counsel, who attests that they failed to seek an extension of time to respond to the Complaint before the responsive deadline expired. (Id. at ¶¶3-6.) Based on the neglect of Defendant’s attorney as set forth in the supporting declaration, the Court must vacate the entry of default and allow Defendant to respond to the action.

 

Plaintiff asks for an award of $1,000.00 for expenses incurred with respect to the request for entry of default and the oppositions to the first and second Motions to Vacate. Code of Civil Procedure section 473 states in relevant part: “The court shall, whenever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault, direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

 

In support of the request for sanctions, Plaintiff’s counsel attests that he bills at $350.00 per hour, and that he spent two hours to file and serve the Notice of Filing of Proof of Service, the Request for Entry of Default with the Court, to serve the Notice of Entry of Default, and to file the default judgment package. (Opp., Morris Decl., ¶10.) Plaintiff’s counsel states that he spent an additional 2.5 hours preparing oppositions to Defendant’s Motions for Relief. (Ibid.)  If all of the time were claimed in sanctions, the amount would total $1575; Plaintiff’s counsel asks the Court to order $1000, which correlates to approximately 2.85 hours’ work.

 

The Court will limit the award is limited to fees incurred in obtaining the default only, which is $750 for two hours work.  (See Rogalski v. Nabers Cadillac (1992) 11 Cal.App.4th 816, 822 (limiting compensatory fee award to the plaintiff’s “attorney fees and costs incurred in obtaining the defaults only”). Plaintiff is awarded reasonable compensatory legal fees of $750.00.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Osiel Escobar’s Motion for Order Setting Aside Request for Entry of Default is granted. The default entered on 04/01/2026 is hereby vacated. Defendant is to file and serve the proposed Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this ruling. Defense counsel Brian P. Smith is to pay Plaintiff’s counsel $750.00 within 20 days of this ruling.
