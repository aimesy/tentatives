Case Number: 24STCV34611    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 10 – Nishara J. Abeyguneratne, et al. v. Mercedes-Benz USA LLC, et al.

Plaintiff Nishara J. Abeyguneratne’s Motions for Orders Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted and Compelling Responses to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production

Plaintiff Nishara J. Abeyguneratne moves for an order deeming that Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA LLC has admitted the truth of the matters specified in Plaintiff’s requests for admission. Plaintiff also moves for orders compelling Defendant to respond to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production.

A party must respond to written discovery “[w]ithin 30 days after service.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.260(a) [interrogatories], 2031.260(a) [requests for production], 2033.250(a) [requests for admission].) When the responding party fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories or requests for production, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(b) [interrogatories], 2031.300(b) [requests for production].) Failing to serve a timely response results in waiving any objections. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a), 2031.300(a).) When a party fails to serve a timely response to requests for admission, “[t]he requesting party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(b).) 

Defendant failed to serve timely responses to Plaintiff’s discovery requests. Plaintiff served her requests for admission, form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production on Defendant on March 5, 2025. (Wong Decls., ¶ 3.) As of the date these motions were filed, Defendant had still not responded. (Id., ¶ 5.) Plaintiff is therefore entitled to an order deeming admitted the matters specified in its requests for admission and to orders compelling Defendant to respond to written discovery.

In opposition, Defendant makes two arguments.

First, Defendant argues that Plaintiff failed to serve former counsel at the proper e-service address. According to Sabrina Narain, former counsel on the case, Plaintiff served the discovery requests to FCAWarranty@ltlattorneys.com. (Narain Decl., ¶ 6.) However, Defendant’s answer listed ltl-e-service@ltlattorneys.com as the proper service address. (Id., ¶ 4.) Because of this, Narain declares, counsel “never received the written discovery requests.” (Id., ¶ 7.) Defendant argues that this improper service on former counsel never started the 30-day clock to serve discovery responses.

The Court disagrees. Defendant’s answer indicates four email addresses for counsel: sabrina.narain@ltlattorneys.com, cynthia.sun@ltlattorneys.com, jacky.wang@ltlattorneys.com, and ltl-e-service@ltlattorneys.com. (See Answer filed 2/26/25.) Plaintiff served her discovery requests to: sabrina.narain@ltlattorneys.com, jacky.wang@ltlattorneys.com, and FCAWarranty@ltlattorneys.com. (Wong Decls., Ex. 2.) Neither former counsel Narain nor current counsel explains why service via email to two former attorneys of record’s direct email addresses did not constitute proper service. Service was proper and started the 30-day clock to respond.

Second, Defendant argues that any failure to respond was based on mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290(a)(2). However, this statute references a party’s request for relief from waiver of objections, not relief from a motion to compel responses. Even if the statute were applicable, Defendant does not meet the first requirement cited in subdivision (a)(1): “The party has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance . . . .” Defendant does not show that it has since served discovery responses, nor has it attached any proof they were served on Plaintiff.

Sanctions

Plaintiff also requests monetary sanctions against Defendant. Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300(c) provides, “[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction … against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (See also Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(c) [interrogatories].) “It is mandatory that the court impose a monetary sanction . . . on the party or attorney, or both, whose failure to serve a timely response to requests for admission necessitated this motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(c).) Defendant failed to provide any responses by the deadline. There is no evidence that Defendant acted with substantial justification.

The Court finds sanctions are appropriate. The Court exercises its discretion to award Plaintiff the requested sanctions in full in the amount of $2,448.

Disposition

Plaintiff Nishara J. Abeyguneratne’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and request for production are granted. Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA LLC is ordered to serve verified responses without objections within 10 days. Plaintiff Nishara J. Abeyguneratne’s motion to deem matters admitted is granted. Plaintiff’s request for admissions as to Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA LLC are hereby deemed admitted.

Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA LLC and its counsel of record are ordered to pay Plaintiff Nishara J. Abeyguneratne $2,448 in sanctions within 21 days.


Case Number: 25STCV20957    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 11 - Daniel Pena v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority

Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s Motion for Relief from Waiver of Discovery Objections

          Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority moves for relief from its waiver of objections to Plaintiff Daniel Pena’s form interrogatories, special interrogatories, requests for production, and requests for admission.

As an initial matter, the Court notes that Defendant’s motion for relief from waiver of objections to four different sets of discovery constitutes an improper omnibus motion. Defendant should have filed four separate motions.

A responding party who does not serve a timely response waives all objections to discovery requests. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a), 2031.300(a), 2033.280(a).) The court may grant relief from that waiver when the responding party (1) “has subsequently served a response that is in substantial compliance with” the statutes governing that method of discovery and (2) “[t]he party’s failure to serve a timely response was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a)(1) & (2), 2031.300(a)(1) & (2), 2033.280(a)(1) & (2).) Defendant meets both criteria. 

Plaintiff served his discovery requests on Defendant via email on March 11, 2026. (Aleagha Decl., ¶¶ 4-6.) However, Defense counsel reports that the email containing the discovery requests was routed to a quarantine folder and did not appear in counsel’s inbox. (Ibid.) The firm’s IT consultant confirmed that the transmission was routed into quarantine due to security concerns. (Id., ¶ 9.) When Defense counsel discovered the error more than one month later, she asked Plaintiff’s counsel for a thirty-day extension, to which counsel agreed. (Id., ¶ 10.) However, Plaintiff’s counsel did not agree to waive Defendant’s objections. (Id., ¶¶ 12-13.) Defendant served verified responses on June 2, 2026. (Id., ¶ 10.) The Court has reviewed Defendant’s responses and finds them to be in substantial compliance.

In addition, Defendant’s failure to serve a timely response resulted from mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect. Defense counsel attests that the discovery requests were automatically routed to quarantine, and they were not discovered until April 15, 2026. (Aleagha Decl., ¶ 9.)

In opposition, Plaintiff largely disputes that Defendant did not receive the discovery requests in a timely fashion. Plaintiff argues that he sent a copy of the first amended complaint and the discovery requests in the same email; thus, it was impossible that one email was quarantined. (Galope Decl., ¶ 2.) In reply, Defendant concedes that the Exhibit Plaintiff submitted showing the routed email presents “what appears to be a single email with a combined subject line.” (Reply, at p. 2.) However, Defendant’s IT consultant confirmed that the transmission was indeed routed; even if the discovery requests were not routed, Defendant argues, it is possible that counsel accidentally did not review all attachments in the email. This is excusable neglect. Under the circumstances, the Court determines that any mistake made by Defense counsel was excusable. Denying this motion would prejudice Defendant in a way that does not serve the interests of justice.

Plaintiff also argues that the responses are not in substantial compliance because they include waived objections. However, inclusion of objections in these discovery responses does not make Defendant’s responses not Code-compliant under these circumstances. If this were true, the relief afforded from waiver of objections would be rendered meaningless.

The Court will grant relief from Defendant’s waiver of objections.

Disposition

Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s motion for relief from its waiver of objections to Plaintiff Daniel Pena’s form interrogatories, special interrogatories, requests for production, and requests for admission is granted.

Plaintiff Daniel Pena’s Motions for Orders Deeming Requests for Admission Admitted and Compelling Responses to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production

Plaintiff Daniel Pena moves for an order deeming that Defendant has admitted the truth of the matters specified in Plaintiff’s requests for admission. Plaintiff also moves for orders compelling Defendant to respond to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production.

Plaintiff’s discovery motions are moot. Plaintiff’s motions rest on the premise that Defendant was not allowed to serve discovery responses that included waived objections. The Court now finds that relief from this waiver is appropriate (see above); thus, no order compelling responses is necessary.

However, the Court will award Plaintiff mandatory sanctions against Defendant for its failure to timely serve responses to requests for admission. “It is mandatory that the court impose a monetary sanction . . . on the party or attorney, or both, whose failure to serve a timely response to requests for admission necessitated this motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(c).) Defendant failed to provide responses to Plaintiff’s request for admission by the response deadline.

However, Plaintiff’s counsel did not reasonably incur $1,875 for time spent drafting this simple discovery motion at a rate of $750 per hour. The Court exercises its discretion to reduce Plaintiff’s requested fees by 50%. The Court declines to award Plaintiff sanctions requested in the other three discovery motions.

Disposition

Plaintiff Daniel Pena’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production are denied as moot. Daniel Pena’s motion to deem matters admitted is granted in part as to sanctions only. Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority and its counsel of record are ordered to pay Plaintiff Daniel Pena $937.50 in sanctions within 21 days.


Case Number: 26STCV08900    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 12 – Eugen Gabriel Parhoti, et al. v. Heather Village Association, et al.

Defendants Heather Village Association and Icon Realty Services, Inc.’s Demurrer and Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ First Amended Complaint

Demurrer

Defendants Heather Village Association and Icon Realty Services, Inc. demur to Plaintiffs Eugen Gabriel and Lavinia Parhoti’s fourth cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress in their first amended complaint.

          Plaintiffs allege sufficient facts for this cause of action. The claim’s elements are “(1) extreme and outrageous conduct by the defendant with the intention of causing, or reckless disregard of the probability of causing, emotional distress; (2) the plaintiff’s suffering severe or extreme emotional distress; and (3) actual and proximate causation of the emotional distress by the defendant’s outrageous conduct.” (Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, 1050.) “Whether the defendant’s conduct was outrageous and whether the plaintiff’s emotional distress was severe are generally questions of fact.” (Murphy v. Allstate Ins. Co. (1978) 83 Cal.App.3d 38, 51.)

          Plaintiffs allege that their kitchen and living room ceiling started leaking and flooding their kitchen, living room, and dining room. (FAC, ¶ 10.) Despite asking their HOA, Defendant Heather Village Association, for immediate assistance with the emergency, Defendant “did not provide any assistance or direction whatsoever,” instead directing them to work it out with their upstairs neighbor. (Id., ¶ 13.) The next day, Plaintiff Lavinia Parhoti suffered a panic attack due to the circumstances. (Id., ¶ 14.) Three days after the leak started, Plaintiffs’ upstairs neighbors had a plumbing service check out the leak; they discovered that “the water had absorbed into the walls causing mold to develop in the ceiling in the kitchen and living room common area walls causing Category 3 ‘black water’ damage that is grossly contaminated containing toxins and pathogens.” (Id., ¶¶ 18-20.) Plaintiffs were forced to relocate and live in a hotel for 6 months due to the damage. (Id., ¶ 21.) Plaintiffs allege that Defendants caused “severe and extreme emotional distress of such a substantial quality that no reasonable man in civilized society should be expected to endure including stress, anxiety, panic, worry, horror, uncertainty, and suffering.” (Id., ¶ 115.) While it is a close call, the Court cannot conclude that, as a matter of law, Defendants’ conduct was not outrageous enough to support a cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress. These allegations are sufficient at the pleading stage.

Motion to Strike

Defendants Heather Village Association and Icon Realty Services, Inc. move to strike 15 references to punitive damages from the FAC.

Courts may strike allegations related to punitive damages where the facts alleged “do not rise to the level of malice, oppression or fraud necessary” to recover punitive damages under Civil Code section 3294. (Turman v. Turning Point of Central California, Inc. (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 53, 64.) Conclusory allegations are not enough. (Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1042.) The complaint must make “factual assertions supporting a conclusion [defendants] acted with oppression, fraud or malice.”  (Ibid.)

Here, the Court finds that Plaintiffs allege sufficient facts for punitive damages. The FAC alleges that Defendants “had knowledge of the leaks and water damage to the subject unit and were aware that the water damage and continue to mold [sic] in the unit but they consciously disregarded that likelihood including the likelihood that it would harm Plaintiffs.” (FAC, ¶ 62.) They allege that “the emergency request was intentionally ignored by Defendants and is willful, wanton and malicious behavior by its officers, directors and managing agent’s [sic] of Defendants.” (Id., ¶ 33.)

The Court cannot conclude that, as a matter of law, these allegations do not suffice to constitute malice as “despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others” (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(1)) or oppression as “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights” (id., subd. (c)(2)). 

The Court declines to strike references to punitive damages.

Disposition

Defendants Heather Village Association and Icon Realty Services, Inc.’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ first amended complaint is overruled. Defendants must answer the complaint within 21 days.

          Defendants Heather Village Association and Icon Realty Services, Inc.’s motion to strike portions of Plaintiffs’ first amended complaint is denied.
