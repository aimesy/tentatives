DEPARTMENT 516 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV00753    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             September 23, 2026   

Case Name:               Romero v. Dunn, et al.

Case No.:                   25STCV00753

Matter:                        Motion - Other Re Appointment of Receiver

Moving Party:             Plaintiff David Romero

Responding Party:       None

 

Tentative Ruling:      Plaintiff David Romero’s motion to appoint receiver is denied

 

 

            Plaintiff David Romero (“Plaintiff”) and Dos Portillos, LLC (“Dos Portillos”) filed this action against Defendants Gilbert Anthony Dunn (“Dunn”), an individual, Reginald Hobbs (“Hobbs”), an individual, and Does 1 through 50. The Complaint alleges four causes of action for: (1) conversion; (2) breach of contract; (3) breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing; and (4) declaratory relief.

 

            Plaintiff moves for a Court Order appointing a receiver to take control of the Subject Property, 2219 Pine Avenue, Long Beach, California 90806 (“Subject Property”). Dunn and Hobbs did not file an opposition.

 

Legal Standard

 

            The Court may appoint a receiver to enforce the judgment where the judgment creditor shows that, considering the interests of both the judgment creditor and the judgment debtor, the appointment of a receiver is a reasonable method to obtain the fair and orderly satisfaction of the judgment. (Civ. Code, § 708.620.) Code of Civil Procedure section 564, subdivision (b), states “[a] receiver may be appointed by the court in which an action or proceeding is pending, or by a judge of that court, in the following cases: . . .(3) [a]fter judgment, to carry the judgment into effect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 564, subd. (b).)

 

            “[T]he appointment of a receiver is a drastic remedy to be employed only in exceptional circumstances.” (City and County of San Francisco v. Daley (1993) 16 Cal.App.4th 734, 744 (Daley).) “[R]eceivers are often legal luxuries, frequently representing an extravagant cost to a losing litigant. When it appears that no reasonably certain benefit will result to one litigant, and a distinct disadvantage will result to another, courts should weigh carefully the propriety of appointing a receiver.” (Ibid.) A receiver will not be appointed if another adequate remedy exists to protect the interest of the party requesting a receiver. (McNeil v. Graner (1949) 92 Cal.App.2d 371, 373.) 

 

Analysis

 

            Plaintiff moves for a Court Order appointing a receiver because Hobbs and Dunn have exercised exclusive control over the Subject Property, and Hobbs has failed to distribute any proceeds to Plaintiff or provide any accounting regarding the Subject Property since 2019. In the alternative, Plaintiff moves to be appointed as interim manager instead of professional receivership.

 

            Plaintiff specifically requests that a receiver be authorized to:

 

a.       Collect rents and income;

b.      Pay ordinary and necessary operating expenses;

c.       Maintain appropriate insurance;

d.      Preserve and maintain the Property;

e.       Obtain and review Property-related books and records;

f.        Maintain complete financial records;

g.      Provide periodic reports to the Court and the parties;

h.      Preserve net income;

i.        Receive and preserve any net sale proceeds; and

j.        Perform such additional acts as are reasonably necessary to protect the Property and the parties’ interests.

 

(Mot., 11:15-26.)

 

            As an initial matter, Plaintiff cites Code of Civil Procedure section 564, subdivision (b)(3); however, no judgment has been entered; thus, subdivision (b)(3) is inapplicable. (See Daley, supra, 16 Cal.App.4th at p. 743.) Additionally, “'Where an injunction will protect all the rights to which the applicant for the appointment of a receiver appears to be entitled, a receiver will not be appointed.'” (Alhambra-Shumway Mines, Inc. v. Alhambra Gold Mine Corp. (1953) 116 Cal.App.2d 869, 873.)

 

            Plaintiff argues that the anticipated sale of the Subject Property requires that the Court appoint a receiver. However, the Court finds that other remedies are available to him that can address Plaintiff’s concern. Similarly, Hobb’s failure to comply with the June 4, 2026, Court Order is not a basis to appoint a receiver. Plaintiff has other remedies available for Hobb’s decision not to participate in discovery.

 

            Plaintiff has not presented legal authority to support his arguments that a receiver is warranted. Moreover, Plaintiff has not provided any legal authority to appoint him as an “interim manager.” (Mot., 8:8-11:10.)

 

            Thus, Plaintiff’s motion is denied in its entirety.

 

Conclusion

 

            Plaintiff David Romero’s motion to appoint a receiver is denied.

 

 

 


Case Number: 25STCV15142    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:           September 23, 2026   

Case Name:              Navarro v. FCA US, LLC

Case No.:                  25STCV15142

Matter:                       Motion to Enforce Settlement  

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Jane Navarro

Responding Party:    None

 

Tentative Ruling:      Plaintiff Jane Navarro’s  motion to enforce settlement is granted.

 

 

            Plaintiff Jane M. Navarro (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant FCA US LLC (“Defendant”) and Does 1 to 10.

 

            On June 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of conditional settlement.

 

            Plaintiff moves for the Court to enforce the parties' Code of Civil Procedure 988 Offer (“Agreement”). Defendant did not file an opposition.

 

Legal Standard

 

            The Court is authorized to enter judgment pursuant to the stipulated settlement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6.) In reviewing a motion to enforce a settlement, the Court determines “whether the parties entered into a valid and binding settlement.” (Hines v. Lukes (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 1174, 1182.) “A settlement is enforceable under section 664.6 only if the parties agreed to all material settlement terms. [Citations.] The court ruling on the motion may consider the parties’ declarations and other evidence in deciding what terms the parties agreed to, and the court’s factual findings in this regard are reviewed under the substantial evidence standard.  [Citations.] If the court determines that the parties entered into an enforceable settlement, it should grant the motion and enter a formal judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”  (Id. at pp. 1182-1183; see also Osumi v. Sutton (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 1355, 1357 [“Strong public policy in favor of the settlement of civil cases gives the trial court, which approves the settlement, the power to enforce it”].) 

 

            Strict compliance with the statutory requirements is necessary before a court can enforce a settlement agreement under section 664.6. (Sully-Miller Contracting Co. v. Gledson/Cashman Construction, Inc. (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 30, 37.) ““[M]utuality of obligation must exist where the exchange of promises between promisor and promisee is meant to represent the contract's consideration.” (Id. at p. 36.) The party seeking to enforce a settlement “must first establish the agreement at issue was set forth ‘in a writing signed by the parties’ (§ 664.6) or was made orally before the court.¿ [Citation.]”¿(Harris v. Rudin, Richman & Appel (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 299, 304.) 

 

Analysis

 

            Plaintiff moves for the Court to grant its motion to enforce settlement against Defendant.

 

            “Although a monetary settlement is in some ways like a private contract, a settlement is obtained as a means of resolving and terminating a lawsuit. Moreover, settlement agreements pursuant to section 664.6 or section 998 result not only in contractual agreements but also in judgments that conclusively resolve the issues between the parties.” (DeSaulles v. Community Hospital of Monterey Peninsula (2016) 62 Cal.4th 1140, 1153.)

 

               Plaintiff contends that Defendant has breached the Agreement because Defendant did not pay Plaintiff the $10,000 owed within 60 days. (Ter-Haroutunian Decl., ¶¶ 4-5, Ex. A.) The Court finds that the parties' Agreement is signed by Defendants and clearly defines Defendant’s obligation to pay within 60 days of execution. (Id. at Ex. A.) In the absence of any opposition by Defendant, the Court finds that Plaintiff has met her burden.

 

            Thus, the Court grants Plaintiff’s motion to enforce settlement.

 

Conclusion

 

            Plaintiff Jane Navarro’s  motion to enforce settlement is granted.
