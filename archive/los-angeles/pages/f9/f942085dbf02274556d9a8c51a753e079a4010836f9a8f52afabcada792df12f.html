Case Number: 25STCV04874    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 11 - Armita Amini v. Tesla, Inc.

Defendant Tesla, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Binding Arbitration

Defendant Tesla, Inc. moves to compel arbitration of this action against it by Plaintiff Armita Amini. Tesla, Inc. further moves to stay the action under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.4.    

Request for Judicial Notice

          Defendant requests that the Court take judicial notice of the complaint filed in this action. This pleading is subject to judicial notice under Evidence Code section 452(d) as a court record. Defendant’s request for judicial notice is granted.

Existence of Agreement

The party moving to compel arbitration must establish the existence of a written arbitration agreement between the parties. (CCP § 1281.2.) It can meet the “initial burden to show an agreement to arbitrate by attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement purportedly bearing the opposing party’s signature.” (Espejo v. Southern California Permanente Medical Group (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 1047, 1060.) “At this step, a movant need not ‘follow the normal procedures of document authentication’ and need only ‘allege the existence of an agreement and support the allegation as provided in’ ” California Rules of Court, rule 3.1330. (Iyere v. Wise Auto Group (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 747, 755.)

Here, Defendant meets its initial burden by attaching a copy of the retail installment sales contract. (Kim Decl., Ex. 1.) On the bottom of the first page, it provides: “Agreement to Arbitrate: By signing below, you agree that, pursuant to the Arbitration Provision on page 5 of this contract, you or we may elect to resolve any dispute by neutral, binding arbitration and not by a court action. See the Arbitration Provision for additional information concerning the agreement to arbitrate.” (Id., p. 1.) Underneath, Plaintiff’s name is electronically written in cursive font. (Ibid.) 

Plaintiff argues that the agreement was not properly authenticated by the declarant and that the signed agreement itself does not show that Plaintiff agreed to the arbitration provision. However, as mentioned above, Defendant need not authenticate the document to meet its initial burden of establishing the existence of a valid agreement. Even so, Raymond Kim, Manager of Business Resolution at Tesla, Inc., attests that he is “familiar with the service and sales of Tesla vehicles, including the related creation and record-keeping procedures.” (Kim Decl., ¶ 2.) Kim located a copy of Plaintiff’s retail installment sales contract by searching Tesla’s electronic records. (Id., ¶¶ 3, 6-8.)  He states, “Given my knowledge and understanding of Tesla's procedures, the signed RISC only ends up in the electronic file (as it did here) after a consumer has executed the RISC either in person or electronically, which Plaintiff did on or about October 13, 2023.” (Id., ¶ 8.) This is sufficient to authenticate the agreement and establish that Plaintiff agreed to arbitrate when she signed the retail installment sales contract. Plaintiff submits no declaration disputing the authenticity of her electronic signature.

The Court also finds that Plaintiff’s claims fall within the scope of the arbitration provision. The agreement states, “Any claim or dispute, whether in contract, tort, statute or otherwise . . . between you and us or our employees, agents, successors or assigns, which arises out of or relates to your credit application, purchase or condition of this Vehicle, this contract or any resulting transaction or relationship (including any such relationship with third parties who do not sign this contract) shall, at your or our election, be resolved by neutral, binding arbitration and not by a court action.” (Kim Decl., Ex. 1, at p. 5.)

Plaintiff argues that the arbitration provision in the retail installment sales contract does not cover warranty claims, citing Ford Motor Warranty Cases (2023) 89 Cal.App.5th 1324, aff'd (2025) 17 Cal.5th 1122. However, while Ford Motor Warranty established that “independent manufacturer warranties are not part of, but are independent from, retail sale contracts,” this case was decided in the context of applying equitable estoppel to permit a nonsignatory manufacturer to compel arbitration under a dealer’s sales agreement. (Id., at p. 1336.) Here, Tesla, Inc. is both the manufacturer and the seller of Plaintiff’s vehicle; thus, there is no equitable estoppel issue here that would compel a blanket exclusion of all warranty-based claims.

Waiver

          Plaintiff argues Defendant waived its right to compel arbitration. Waiver occurs “when the party seeking to enforce a known contractual right has intentionally relinquished or abandoned that right.” (Quach v. California Commerce Club, Inc. (2024) 16 Cal.5th 562, 569 (Quach).) “To establish waiver under generally applicable contract law, the party opposing enforcement of a contractual agreement must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the waiving party knew of the contractual right and intentionally relinquished or abandoned it.” (Id. at p. 584.) “Its intentional relinquishment or abandonment of the right may be proved by evidence of words expressing an intent to relinquish the right or of conduct that is so inconsistent with the intent to enforce the contractual right as to lead a reasonable factfinder to conclude that the party had abandoned it.” (Ibid.)

          The Court does not find that Defendant acted in a manner so inconsistent with an intent to arbitrate as to lead a reasonable factfinder to believe it abandoned its right. Defendant engaged in only minimal actions inconsistent with the right to arbitrate. Defendant filed a case management statement requesting a jury trial. However, in the same statement, it clearly stated that it “reserves the right to file . . . a motion to compel arbitration and stay action . . . prior to trial.” (See Def. Case Mgmt Stmt, ¶ 15.) Further, Defendant did not propound any discovery requests or respond to any discovery. (See Reply, at p. 2.) Therefore, Defendant did not substantially invoke the machinery of litigation. On this record, the Court finds Defendant did not waive its right to arbitrate.

Unconscionability

          Plaintiff argues the agreement is unconscionable. “Both procedural and substantive unconscionability must be shown for the defense to be established, but ‘they need not be present in the same degree.’ ” (OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho (2019) 8 Cal.5th 111, 125 (OTO).) “The burden of proving unconscionability rests upon the party asserting it.”  (Id. at p. 126.)

Plaintiff shows no procedural unconscionability. “ ‘The procedural element addresses the circumstances of contract negotiation and formation, focusing on oppression or surprise due to unequal bargaining power.’ ” (OTO, 8 Cal.5th at p. 125.) The “analysis ‘begins with an inquiry into whether the contract is one of adhesion.’  [Citation.] An adhesive contract is standardized, generally on a preprinted form, and offered by the party with superior bargaining power ‘on a take-it-or-leave-it basis.’ ” (Id. at p. 126.) For an adhesive contract, the court must consider “whether circumstances of the contract’s formation created such oppression or surprise that closer scrutiny of its overall fairness is required.” (Ibid.) “ ‘ “ ‘Oppression occurs where a contract involves lack of negotiation and meaningful choice, surprise where the allegedly unconscionable provision is hidden within a prolix printed form.’ ” ’ ” (Id. at p. 126.)  

In the opposition brief, Plaintiff argues the contract was adhesive because it was presented in a “take it or leave it” fashion and she had no meaningful opportunity to negotiate its terms. (See Opp., at pp. 14-15.) However, Plaintiff provides no personal declaration attesting to these facts signed under penalty of perjury. In addition, an arbitration agreement offered on a “take it or leave it basis” does not render the agreement unenforceable. (See Lagatree v. Luce, Forward, Hamilton & Scripps (1999) Cal.App.4th 1105, 1127.) There is no indication that Plaintiff did not have ample time to review and sign the agreement. Thus, Plaintiff’s arguments do not support a finding of procedural unconscionability.

Because Plaintiff does not meet her burden in showing the agreement is procedurally unconscionable via admissible evidence, the Court need not and does not address whether the agreement is substantively unconscionable. (See Crippen v. Central Valley RV Outlet (2004) 124 Cal.App.4th 1159, 1167 [finding no procedural unconscionability and therefore not addressing substantive unconscionability].)

Disposition

          Defendant Tesla, Inc.’s motion to compel binding arbitration is granted. Plaintiff Armita Amini is ordered to arbitrate this action against Defendant Tesla, Inc. The Court hereby stays the entire action pending resolution of the arbitration proceeding.

No. 8 - Jeri Schwebs v. David Azizi, et al.

Defendants’ Motion for Order Compelling Walmart, Inc.’s Compliance with Subpoena for Production of Business Records

Defendants David Azizi and Law Offices of David Azizi move to compel nonparty Walmart Inc. (“Walmart”) to comply with a deposition subpoena for production of business records. There was no opposition filed in response to the motion.

Under California law, a party may obtain discovery from a nonparty through a subpoena for documents or other materials. (CCP §§ 2020.010, 2020.020.) The court may compel compliance with a subpoena upon motion if the production sought is subject to discovery. (CCP §§1987.1, 2025.480(b).)

The Court cannot order the relief Defendants seek here. Defendants did not properly serve the moving papers on Walmart. “A written notice and all moving papers supporting a motion to compel an answer to a deposition question or to compel production of a document or tangible thing from a nonparty deponent must be personally served on the nonparty deponent unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail or electronic service at an address or electronic service address specified on the deposition record.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1346.) 

Defendants did not personally serve this motion on Walmart as required, and provide no evidence that Walmart agreed to accept service by mail or electronic service. 

Disposition

          Defendants David Azizi and Law Offices of David Azizi’s motion to compel nonparty Walmart Inc. to comply with a deposition subpoena for production of business records is denied without prejudice.

No. 9 - In the Matter of: Nelson Tucker

          Petitioner Nelson Tucker’s Petition for Adjudication of Newspaper Pursuant to California Government Code section 6000

Petitioner Nelson Tucker dba CalPublications.com moves for an order granting his petition to adjudicate CalPublications.com as an authorized newspaper for purposes of publishing legal notices and documents. This Court has authority to decide this petition under Los Angeles Superior Court Local Rule 2.8(e).

In certain circumstances, notice to a party in litigation can be provided through publication in a newspaper. (See, e.g., Code Civ. Proc. § 415.50(b) (where service of process by conventional means cannot be effected, “The court shall order the summons to be published in a named newspaper, published in this state, that is more likely to give actual notice to the party to be served.”)) As is apparent from the statute, the purpose of publishing a notice in a newspaper is to increase the prospects that a party receives notice of the pending litigation when other means for service have failed. In 1943, when the legislature adopted section 6000, it presumably assumed that most persons obtained their news from a regularly-published newspaper of general circulation and would see a notice that they had been sued. In short, they were doing something else when they happened to see a notice that they had been sued.

In contrast, the primary purpose of Petitioner’s website is to publish legal notices. The target audience – i.e., persons being sued – would have to go to the website not for news, but expressly to determine if they have been sued.

“Whenever a newspaper desires to have its standing as a newspaper of general circulation ascertained and established, it may, by its publisher, manager, editor or attorney, file a verified petition in the superior court of the county in which it is established, printed and published, setting forth the facts which justify such action.” (Gov. Code, § 6020.)

For both procedural and substantive reasons, the Court cannot grant the relief that Petitioner seeks. Procedurally, the petition is to be published both in the petitioning newspaper and “in some other newspaper of general circulation published in the same city as the petitioning newspaper if there is one.” (Gov. Code, § 6021(a), (b).) There is no indication that the Petition was published either on CalPublications.com or in a newspaper of general circulation in Los Angeles County. This omission alone is grounds to deny the Petition.

The Petition also fails substantively. Under California Government Code section 6000, “A ‘newspaper of general circulation’ is a newspaper published for the dissemination of local or telegraphic news and intelligence of a general character, which has a bona fide subscription list of paying subscribers, and has been established, printed and published at regular intervals in the State, county, or city where publication, notice by publication, or official advertising is to be given or made for at least one year preceding the date of the publication, notice or advertisement.” Government Code section 6008(a) details the requisite criteria for a “newspaper of general circulation”:

“1.      It is a newspaper published for the dissemination of local or telegraphic news and intelligence of a general character, which has a bona fide subscription list of paying subscribers and has been established and published at regular intervals of not less than weekly in the city, district, or public notice district for which it is seeking adjudication for at least three years preceding the date of adjudication.

“2.      It has a substantial distribution to paid subscribers in the city, district, or public notice district in which it is seeking adjudication.

“3.      It has maintained a minimum coverage of local or telegraphic news and intelligence of a general character of not less than 25 percent of its total inches during each year of the three-year period.

“4.      It has only one principal office of publication and that office in is the city, district, or public notice district for which it is seeking adjudication.”

Section 6008 specifies that all four criteria must be met. These requirements were not modified when the statute was last amended in 2016, long after the internet had become ubiquitous.

According to the verified Petition and the nearly-identical brief in support of the Petition, Petitioner’s website is a “bona fide online digital legal notice newspaper” that has published legal notices since 2018. (Petition, ¶ 1; Brief, ¶ 1.) Petitioner presents no evidence, though, that the website also disseminates local or telegraphic news of a general character, that it has a “substantial distribution to paid subscribers,” that at least 25% of its publication is devoted to local or telegraphic news and intelligence, or even that there is a single principal office of publication in Los Angeles County, where Petitioner seeks this order. All of these items are required by Government Code section 6008. As such, Petitioner provides insufficient evidence that CalPublications.com is a newspaper of general circulation as defined by the California legislature.

The Court’s personal visit to the website on July 14, 2026, confirms that it does not contain local news of a general character. Rather, the website has one page titled “LEGAL NEWS” with four posts that were “published” from May 21 to June 1, 2026. The first post, “Recent Legal Developments,” contains a list of links to articles posted by other legal newspapers and websites. The second post, “Guide to Service by Publication in California,” explains California service of process requirements. The final two posts duplicate the first two. None of these posts – even if they were considered local news – have existed for at least one year or were published by Petitioner at regular intervals. Nor is there any apparent means to subscribe. By all indicia, this is not a newspaper of general circulation as contemplated by the statute.

The Court does not dispute Petitioner’s contention that the definition of “newspaper” should expand as “modern methods of communication have changed over recent years as our society has evolved into an increasingly mobile and technologically advanced way of getting news and obtaining information.” (Petition, ¶ 9; Brief, ¶ 11.) This argument, though, needs to be made to the California legislature, which has the power to amend Government Code sections 6000 and 6008 should amendment be warranted. The Court cannot make that amendment for Petitioner.

Disposition

Petitioner Nelson Tucker dba CalPublications.com’s petition for adjudication of newspaper pursuant to California Government Code section 6000 is denied.


Case Number: 26STCV03153    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 10 - Dolores Marana v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, et al.

    Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority's Demurrer to Plaintiff's Complaint

Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (“LACMTA”) demurs to the second and third causes of action alleged by Plaintiff Dolores in her complaint. Plaintiff did not file an opposition.

As an initial matter, the Court notes that Plaintiff brought three causes of action against LACMTA for (1) motor vehicle negligence; (2) negligence; and (3) “liability for the wrongful act or omissions by public entity employees, Government Code section 815.2.” While LACTMA’s memorandum of points and authorities asks the Court to sustain the demurrer as to Plaintiff’s first cause of action on the grounds that it is duplicative of the second cause of action, the Court cannot do this. LACMTA identified only the second and third causes of action in the demurrer itself. “Each ground of demurrer must be in a separate paragraph and must state whether it applies to the entire complaint, cross-complaint, or answer, or to specified causes of action or defenses.” (California Rule of Court, rule 3.1320(a).)

Second Cause of Action: Negligence

The second cause of action for negligence fails due to sovereign immunity. “[A]ll government tort liability must be based on statute.” (County of San Bernardino v. Superior Court (2022) 77 Cal.App.5th 1100, 1107.) “ ‘[S]overeign immunity is the rule in California; governmental liability is limited to exceptions specifically set forth by statute.’ ” (Id. at p. 1108.) Because LACMTA is a public entity, Plaintiff must provide a statutory basis for liability. She did not do so here.

The demurrer as to the second cause of action is sustained without leave to amend.

Third Cause of Action: Liability for the Wrongful Act or Omissions by Public Entity Employee – Gov’t Code § 815.2

          Defendant argues that liability under Government Code section 815.2 is not a standalone cause of action, and does not provide a private right of action. The Court agrees. The statute provides: “A public entity is liable for injury proximately caused by an act or omission of an employee of the public entity within the scope of his employment if the act or omission would, apart from this section, have given rise to a cause of action against that employee or his personal representative.” (Gov. Code, § 815.2(a) [emphasis added].) This statute codifies public entities’ vicarious liability under the “doctrine of respondeat superior.” (Mary M. v. City of Los Angeles (1991) 54 Cal.3d 202, 209.) LACMTA can thus only be held liable if its individual employees are liable.

While the title of the cause of action implies a violation of Government Code section 815.2 itself, the actual allegations repeat what appear under the negligence causes of action. “It has long been established that in ruling on a demurrer, the trial court is obligated to look past the form of a pleading to its substance. Erroneous or confusing labels attached by the inept pleader are to be ignored if the complaint pleads facts which would entitle the plaintiff to relief.” (Saunders v. Cariss (1990) 224 Cal.App.3d 905, 908.) Therefore, Court will treat this as a cause of action for negligence against LACMTA based on vicarious liability.

However, even so, Plaintiff does not allege sufficient facts. For negligence, a public entity employer can only be liable if its employees breached a common law duty of care to the plaintiff. (Hoff v. Vacaville Unified School Dist. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 925, 933.) The complaint does not allege LACMTA’s employees breached any duties owed to Plaintiff.

The demurrer as to the second cause of action is sustained with leave to amend.

Disposition

Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint is sustained without leave to amend as to the second cause of action and sustained with 21 days’ leave to amend as to the third cause of action.
