I.       BACKGROUND  

On September 5, 2024, Plaintiff Deborah Nunez (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants Kia America, Inc. (“Defendant”) and Does 1 to 10 alleging one cause of action for violation of Song-Beverly Act – Breach of Express Warranty.

On June 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for judgment on the pleadings.

On July 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed her opposition.

On July 10, 2026, Defendant filed its reply.

 

II.          LEGAL STANDARDS

“A motion for judgment on the pleadings performs the same function as a general demurrer, and hence attacks only defects disclosed on the face of the pleadings or by matters that can be judicially noticed.” (Burnett v. Chimney Sweep (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 1057, 1064.) “A motion for judgment on the pleadings may be made either prior to trial or at trial, on the same grounds as could be urged by a general demurrer.” (Ponderosa Homes, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (1994) 23 Cal.App.4th 1761, 1767 [citing Stockton Newspapers, Inc. v. Redevelopment Agency (1985) 171 Cal.App.3d 95, 99].) “In deciding or reviewing a judgment on the pleadings, all properly pleaded material facts are deemed to be true, as well as all facts that may be implied or inferred from those expressly alleged.” (Fire Ins. Exchange v. Superior Court (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 446, 452.) However, the court does not accept as true “conclusions of law or fact, opinions, speculation, or allegations contrary to law or facts that are judicially noticed.” (Stevenson Real Estate Servs., Inc. v. CB Richard Ellis Real Estate Servs., Inc. (2006) 138 Cal.App.4th 1215, 1219-20.) Further, like a general demurrer, a motion for judgment on the pleadings does not lie as to a portion of a cause of action, and if any part of a cause of action is properly pleaded, the motion will be overruled. (Fire Ins. Exchange, supra, 116 Cal.App.4th at 552.)

“In the case of either a demurrer or a motion for judgment on the pleadings, leave to amend should be granted if there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action.” (Gami v. Mullikin Medical Ctr. (1993) 18 Cal.App.4th 870, 876.) If leave to amend is granted, the party against whom the motion is granted must be given 30 days to file an amended pleading. (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 438(h)(2).)

 

 

 

III.       DISCUSSION

Requests for Judicial Notice and Evidentiary Objections

            As a preliminary matter, Defendant’s June 18, 2026, requests for judicial notice of Plaintiff’s discovery response and deposition transcript are granted pursuant to Cal. Evid. Code § 452(h). Specifically, a party’s admissions or concessions and deposition transcripts are proper for judicial notice upon a motion for judgment on the pleadings. (See, Arce v. Kaiser Foundation Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 485; see Garcia v. Sterling (1985) 176 Cal.App.3d 17, 22; see also Myers v. Trendwest Resorts, Inc. (2009) 178 Cal.App.4th 735, 746.)

            Plaintiff’s July 6, 2026, evidentiary objections are overruled since, upon a motion for judgment on the pleadings, a court may consider matters which are judicially noticeable. (See Shin v. Kong (2000) 80 Cal.App.4th 498.)

 

Timeliness

            Next, the Court addresses Plaintiff’s argument that the instant motion is untimely. Plaintiff argues that, pursuant to CCP § 438(e), “[n]o motion [for judgment on the pleadings] may be made . . . if a pretrial conference order has been entered pursuant to Section 575, or within 30 days of the date the action is initially set for trial, whichever is later, unless the court otherwise permits.” (Opposition at 2:3-7.) Initially, this action was set for trial on April 13, 2026. On March 20, 2026, the Court continued trial to August 17, 2026. However, as Defendant notes, pursuant to the parties’ March 16, 2026, joint stipulation to continue trial, all non-discovery trial dates were reset based on the new trial date. (See Stipulation.) Accordingly, the motion is timely.

 

 

Analysis

            Defendant moves for judgment on the pleadings on the grounds that Plaintiff’s 2021 Kia K5 (the “Subject Vehicle”) was purchased as a used vehicle and no manufacturer’s new car warranty was issued in connection with that sale, thereby failing to qualify as a “motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer’s new car warranty” under Civ. Code § 1793.22(e)(2). (See Motion at p. 2.)

            Importantly, in Rodriguez v. FCA US, LLC (2024) 17 Cal.5th 189, the California Supreme Court held that the Song-Beverly Act does not apply to a used car unless a manufacturer’s “new car” warranty is issued at the time of sale. (Rodriguez, supra, 17 Cal.5th at 196-197.) Here, under sworn testimony, Plaintiff has stated that the Subject Vehicle was not a new motor vehicle when purchased. (See RJN Exhibit 1 [RFAs] at 3:18-4:6 and 4:24-5:15; see also RJN Exh. 2 [Nunez Depo.] at 9:25-10:2.) These sworn allegations, while contradicting the complaint, take priority over the unsworn allegations within the complaint. (See Today’s IV v. LA County Metropolitan Transport Authority (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 1137, 1175.) Additionally, at her deposition, Plaintiff stated under sworn testimony that the “warranty contract” alleged in her complaint (see Complaint at ¶ 8) was actually the Retail Installment Sale Contract. (See RJN Exh. 2 [Nunez Depo.] at 19:17-20:1.) Accordingly, Plaintiff fails to show that the Subject Vehicle is a “new vehicle” subject to the Song-Beverly Act.

            As there is no reasonable possibility that this deficiency can be cured by amendment, Defendant’s motion is granted without leave to amend.

IV.    CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleadings is GRANTED without leave to amend.

Judgment is entered in favor of Defendant and against Plaintiff.

24VECV04984 COURTNEY FLEMING vs ROXANNE MISTRY, et al.

July 17, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.

Van Nuys Courthouse East, Dept. I

Tentative Ruling - Motion to Set Aside Default (CCP § 473)

 

 

 

I.        Background

 

On October 10, 2024, Plaintiff Courtney Fleming (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants Roxanne Mistry (“Mistry”), RoxxRoxx, LLC (“RoxxRoxx”)(collectively “Defendants”), and Does 1 to 50 alleging four causes of action: (1) breach of contract; (2) fraud; (3) breach of fiduciary duty; and (4) conversion.

 

On March 16, 2026, the Court entered default judgment against Defendants.

 

On June 2, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion to set aside default judgment.

 

On July 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed her opposition.

 

On July 10, 2026, Defendants filed their reply.

 

II.     Motion to Set Aside Default

 

“‘A summons is the process by which a court acquires personal jurisdiction over a defendant in a civil action’ [citation], and a defendant has an absolute right to demand that process be issued against him in a manner prescribed by law.” (Mannesmann DeMag, Ltd. v. Superior Court¿(1985) 172 Cal.App.3d 1118, 1122.) “Constitutional due process requirements are satisfied where the form of service provided and employed is¿reasonably¿calculated¿to give a litigant actual notice of the proceedings and an opportunity to¿be heard.” (Crescendo Corp. v. Shelted, Inc.¿(1968) 267 Cal.App.2d 209, 213.) “‘[C]ompliance with the statutory procedures for service of process is essential to establish personal jurisdiction. [Citation.] Thus, a default judgment entered against a defendant who was not served with a summons in the manner prescribed by statute is void. [Citation.]’” (Ellard v. Conway¿(2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 540, 544.)  

 

The Court may set aside any judgment or order that is void on its face at any time. (Code Civ. Proc. § 473(d); Kremerman v. White (2021) 71 Cal.App.5th 358, 360-70.) “‘A judgment or order is said to be void on its face when the invalidity is apparent upon an inspection of the judgment-roll.”’ [Citation.] This inquiry, however, ‘does not hinge on evidence: A void judgment’s invalidity appears on the face of the record.’ [Citation.]” (Kremerman v. White, supra, 71 Cal.App.5th at p. 370.)  

 

When considering the facial validity of a judgment, the Court may only consider the contents of the judgment roll. (OC Interior Services, LLC v. Nationstar Mortgage, LLC (2017) 7 Cal.App.5th 1318, 1327 (holding that “[t]o prove that the judgment is void, the party challenging the judgment is limited to the judgment roll, i.e., no extrinsic evidence allowed.”).) “If the invalidity can be shown only through extrinsic evidence, such as declarations or testimony, the order/judgment is not void on its face. [Citation.]” (Kremerman v. White, supra, 71 Cal.App.5th at p. 370.) If the judgment is not void on its face, the time limitations of Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5 apply. (Trackman v. Kenney (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 175, 180-81; Schekel v. Resnik (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th Supp. 1, 3-4 (“[t]he Rogers court held that the time limitation set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5 applies by analogy to motions for relief from default judgment valid on its face but otherwise void for improper service” [citing Rogers v. Silverman (1989) 216 Cal.App.3d 1114, 1124.]).) 

 

III.  Analysis


Requests for Judicial Notice

 

As a preliminary matter, Plaintiff’s July 6, 2026, requests for judicial notice of various business records, filed with the California Secretary of State, for 2Spirit Tattoo, LLC, 3649 Kanan Road, Agoura Hills, CA 91301, and Atelier Roxx LLC are granted pursuant to Cal. Evid. Code § 452(h).

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move to set aside default judgment on the grounds that the March 16, 2026, default judgment is void because Plaintiff failed to effect valid service of the summons and complaint, and the Court therefore lacked personal jurisdiction over Defendants. (See Motion at p. ii.) Specifically, Defendants argue that, on November 13, 2024, an identified tenant at 24955 Pacific Coast Highway in Malibu (the “Malibu address”) informed the process server that Mistry had not opened her business there and was not permitted to do so. (Motion at 1:17-22 [see Mears Decl. Exh. B.) On November 26, 2024, Plaintiff filed proofs of substituted service stating that the summons and complaint were left for Mistry at the Malibu address with “John Doe – Manager,” with proof reflecting mailing on November 27, 2024. (Ibid.; see Mears Decl. Exh. C.) On January 27, 2025, the summons and complaint were left for RoxxRoxx at 3649 Kanan Road (the “Kanan address”) with “Jane Doe,” identified as an occupant and authorized to accept service, followed by mailing on January 28, 2025. (See Mears Decl. Exh. D.)

 

Defendants argue that substituted service on an entity requires leaving the summons and complaint during usual office hours at the office of the person to be served, or at that person’s usual mailing address, with the person apparently in charge, followed by mailing to the same location. (Motion at 3:19-24 [citing CCP § 415.20].) Defendants argue that leaving service with “Jane Doe” at the Kanan address does not satisfy this requirement. Defendants further argue that service was not effected on Mistry because it was not left at her dwelling house, usual place of abode, usual place of business, or usual mailing address. (Id. at 4:16-27 [citing CCP § 415.20].)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the Articles of Organization for 2Spirit Tattoo, LLC identify Mistry as an authorized agent for service of process at the Malibu address. (See Opposition at 2:16-21; see also RJN Exh. 1.) Additionally, 2Spirit Tattoo’s Statements of Information identify Mistry as the Chief Executive Officer and the Malibu address as its principal office address, mailing address, and agent-for-service address. (Id. at 2:22-3:4 [RJN Exhs. 2 and 3].) Therefore, Plaintiff argues, the Malibu address constitutes the office of Mistry. (See CCP § 415.20.)

 

Plaintiff further argues RoxxRoxx was properly served through Mistry, who was identified on the proof of service as RoxxRoxx’s Chief Executive Officer. (Opposition at p. 8.) “Deed and title records show that Mistry owned [the Kanan address] at the time of service . . . and Mistry’s own Articles of Organization for Atelier Roxx LLC used that same address as the entity’s principal office, mailing address, and agent-for-service address.” (Id. at 17-21 [citing RJN Exhs. 4-5].) Accordingly, the summons and complaint were served at Mistry’s usual mailing address, as Chief Executive Officer of RoxxRoxx, with mailing to the same as required by CCP § 415.20. Contrary to Defendants’ argument, CCP § 415.20 does not require identification of the person served so long as they had apparent authority. Additionally, pursuant to Cal. Evid. Code § 647, a registered process server’s proof of service is presumed correct.

 

In reply, Defendants argue that their declarations that these are not their usual address of residence or business rebut the presumption that proof of service was valid. (See Reply at p. 3.) However, to rebut this presumption, Defendants must provide evidence beyond a “mere denial” to support their arguments that service was invalid. Defendants fail to rebut the presumption that service was proper. (Palm Property Investments, LLC v. Yadegar (2011) Cal.App.4th at 1427.)

 

The Court finds that Plaintiff satisfied the statutory requirements of CCP § 415 et seq., and Defendants fail to provide any actual evidence in rebuttal. Defendants’ motion is denied.

 

IV.  Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion to set aside default is DENIED.

I.       BACKGROUND  

            On July 18, 2025, Plaintiffs Masoud Radparvar (“Masoud”), Sina Radparvar (“Sina”), Maryam Simani (“Simani”), and Mehran Sarrafzadeh (“Sarrafzadeh”)(collectively “Plaintiffs”) filed a complaint against Defendants Saeed Radparvar (“Defendant”) and Does 1 to 50 alleging eight causes of action: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of fiduciary duty; (3) fraudulent misrepresentation; (4) unjust enrichment; (5) declaratory relief; (6) accounting; (7) fraud; and (8) conversion.

            On October 23, 2025, Plaintiffs filed their first amended complaint (“FAC”) alleging eight causes of action: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of fiduciary duty; (3) fraudulent misrepresentation; (4) equitable lien; (5) declaratory relief; (6) accounting; (7) fraud; and (8) conversion.

            On April 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their second amended complaint (“SAC”) alleging four causes of action: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of fiduciary duty; (3) declaratory relief; and (4) accounting.

            On May 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant demurrer to and motion to strike portions of Plaintiffs’ SAC.

            On June 23, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their oppositions.

            On July 1, 2026, Defendant filed his reply.

 

II.          LEGAL STANDARDS

Demurrer

            A demurrer is a pleading that may be used to test the legal sufficiency of the factual allegations in a complaint. (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 430.10.) There are two types of demurrers: general demurrers and special demurrers. (See McKenney v. Purepac Pharmaceutical Co. (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 72, 77.)

            General demurrers can be used to attack pleadings for failure to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action or for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. (CCP § 430.10(e); McKenney, supra, 167 Cal.App.4th at 77.) Such demurrers can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable; evidence or extrinsic matters are not considered. (CCP §§ 430.30 and 430.70; Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318; Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) To test the sufficiency of the cause of action, the Court admits “all material facts properly pleaded” and “matters which may be judicially noticed,” but does not consider contentions, deductions, or conclusions of fact or law. [Citation].” (Blank, supra, 39 Cal.3d at 318.) The Court gives these facts “a reasonable interpretation, reading it as a whole and its parts in their context.” (Ibid.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) The face of the complaint includes exhibits attached to the complaint. (Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94.) "If facts appearing in the exhibits contradict those alleged, the facts in the exhibits take precedence." (Holland v. Morse Diesel Intern., Inc. (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 1443, 1447.)

            CCP § 430.41 requires that “[b]efore filing a demurrer pursuant to this chapter, the demurring party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (CCP § 430.41(a).) The parties are to meet and confer at least five days before the date the responsive pleading is due. (CCP § 430.41(a)(2).) Thereafter, the demurring party shall file and serve a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts. (CCP § 430.41(a)(3).)

            When a demurrer is sustained, the Court determines whether there is a reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment. (Blank, supra, 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) When a plaintiff “has pleaded the general set of facts upon which his cause of action is based,” the court should give the plaintiff an opportunity to amend his complaint, since plaintiff should not “be deprived of his right to maintain his action on the ground that his pleadings were defective for lack of particulars.” (Reed v. Norman (1957) 152 Cal.App.2d 892, 900.) Generally, the court will allow leave to amend on at least the first try, unless there is absolutely no possibility of overcoming the issue. (See Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1227 [“Denial of leave to amend constitutes an abuse of discretion unless the complaint shows on its face it is incapable of amendment. (Citation.) Liberality in permitting amendment is the rule, if a fair opportunity to correct any defect has not been given."].)

 

Motion to Strike

            The court may, upon a motion, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper, strike any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (Cal. Code of Civ. Proc. § 436(a).) The court may also strike all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Id. at (b).) The grounds for a motion to strike are that the pleading has irrelevant, false or improper matter, or has not been drawn or filed in conformity with laws. (Ibid.) The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice. (CCP § 437.)

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Blank, supra, 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

Before filing a motion to strike, the moving party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike. (CCP § 435.5(a).)

 

 

 

III.       DISCUSSION

Supplemental Brief

            As a preliminary matter, the Court disregards Plaintiffs’ supplemental brief filed on July 14, 2026, filed without leave of court. (See CCP § 1005.)

 

Meet and Confer

            The parties contend that they discussed the instant demurrer and motion to strike by telephone. (Farivar Decl. at ¶¶ 9 and 13.) However, the parties were unable to reach an agreement. (Ibid.) Accordingly, Defendant satisfies the meet and confer requirements of CCP §§ 430.41(a) and 435.5(a).

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

            As a preliminary matter, Defendant’s May 18, 2026, requests for judicial notice are denied as to Exhs. A through E. (See May 18, 2026, Requests for Judicial Notice.) Judicial notice of the Court’s own records is unnecessary and redundant since the Court can always review its own case file. However, Defendant’s request for judicial notice of a copy of the records of the California Secretary of State reflecting the entity status of Raha Enterprises, LLC (“Raha”) is granted pursuant to Cal. Evid. Code § 452(h).

            Next, Plaintiffs’ June 23, 2026, request for judicial notice of Raha’s application for registration with the California Secretary of State is granted pursuant to Evid. Code § 452(h). Judicial notice of Raha’s federal supplemental information is also granted. Evid. Code §452(h).

 

//

First Cause of Action – Breach of Contract

            First, Defendant demurs to Plaintiffs’ first cause of action for breach of contract on the grounds that this action is a “sham pleading,” pursuant to D’Amico v. Board of Medical Examiners (1974) 11 Cal.3d 1, because the SAC “now retrofits a written ‘Operating Agreement’ never before mentioned.” (Demurrer at ii:5-14.) . The sham pleading doctrine states that “[i]f a party files an amended complaint and attempts to avoid the defects of the original complaint by either omitting facts which made the previous complaint defective or by adding facts inconsistent with those of previous pleadings, the court may take judicial notice of prior pleadings and may disregard any inconsistent allegations.” (Colapinto v. County of Riverside (1991) 230 Cal.App.3d 147, 151.) “Courts routinely apply this rule to reject post-demurrer amendments that attempt to cure a legal deficiency by rewriting the facts.” (Demurrer at 5:15-23 [citing Hendy v. Losse (1991) 54 Cal.3d 723, 742-743].) Here, in both Plaintiffs’ original complaint and subsequent FAC, Plaintiffs allege a joint venture partnership for profit between themselves and Defendant without mention of an operating agreement. (See Complaint at ¶ 12; see also FAC at ¶¶ 12 and 14.) “After the Court’s March 27, 2026 ruling that the oral, indefinite-duration theory was statute-of-frauds-barred, Plaintiffs suddenly ‘discover’ a twenty-year-old, unsigned operating agreement . . .” (Demurrer at 5:24-6:7.)

            In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that they circumvent the sham pleading doctrine by “explain[ing] the changes.” (See Opposition at p. 7 [citing Deveny v. Entropin, Inc. (2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 408, 425-426.) Plaintiffs argue that they adequately explained the requisite changes because “they located what they thought was an otherwise lost Operating Agreement for Raha.” (Opposition at 7:14-26.) “Plaintiffs had previously provided the unsigned Operating Agreement to Defendant in discovery prior to the hearing on the Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint on March 27, 2026, and before Plaintiffs had any knowledge of what this Court’s ruling would be on that Demurrer.” (Ibid.) “Plaintiffs also referred to that Operating Agreement in their oral arguments in opposition to the Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint.” (Ibid.) However, Plaintiffs fail to adequately explain the direct contradictions to the Operating Agreement in their original and first amended complaints. Specifically, Plaintiffs previously alleged only an oral agreement that Raha’s profits would be apportioned amongst the partners indefinitely and never referenced a written agreement. This does not meet the evidentiary standard of Deveny, supra.

            Accordingly, the Court finds that Plaintiffs’ SAC violates the sham pleading doctrine by incorporating the alleged written Operating Agreement. Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s first cause of action for breach of contract is sustained on these grounds.

            Furthermore, the Operating Agreement still violates the statute of frauds. Civ. Code § 1624(a)(1) requires that an agreement that by its terms is not to be performed within a year from the making thereof must be in writing and subscribed by the party to be charged. (See Civ. Code § 1624(a)(1).) Here, the alleged Operating Agreement is “perpetual” yet unsigned. (See SAC Exh. 1.)

            In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that Defendant is estopped from raising the statute of frauds because of his alleged conduct which created an enforceable contract between the parties. (Opposition at 8:15-9:25.) However, to rely on estoppel, Plaintiffs must plead unconscionable injury or unjust enrichment. (See Monarco v. Lo Greco (1950) 35 Cal.2d 621, 623-624.) As Defendants note, the SAC pleads neither.

            Additionally, Plaintiffs have been given multiple opportunities to cure their defects for the breach of contract claim. Plaintiffs fail to do so, and Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s first cause of action is therefore sustained without leave to amend.

 

Second Cause of Action – Breach of Fiduciary Duty

            Next, to state a cause of action for breach of fiduciary duty, a plaintiff must allege ultimate facts showing (1) the existence of a fiduciary relationship; (2) breach of a fiduciary duty; and (3) damages caused by the breach. (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 820.) Here, the SAC relies on the written operating agreement to establish a breach of fiduciary duty. (See SAC at ¶¶ 60-64.) However, as discussed supra, the Operating Agreement is barred by the statute of frauds. While Plaintiffs argue that they properly plead partnership in an investment, Plaintiffs do not argue against the fact that such partnership is only alleged through the unsigned Opearting Agreement. (See Opposition at pp. 13-14.)

            Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ second cause of action for breach of fiduciary duty is sustained without leave to amend.

 

Third and Fourth Causes of Action – Declaratory Relief and Accounting

            Next, a demurrer to a cause of action for declaratory relief is properly sustained when it is superfluous or duplicative of another cause of action. (General of America Ins. Co. v. Lilly (1958) 258 Cal.App.2d 465, 470.) As Defendant notes, “[b]ecause the alleged Operating Agreement creates no enforceable legal rights, there is nothing for the Court to declare.” (Demurrer at 11:3-14.)

            In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that this action survives because it arises from their fourth cause of action for accounting. (See Opposition at pp. 14-15.) A cause of action for accounting arises from a fiduciary relationship. (See Teselle v. McLoughlin (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 156, 179-180.) Once again, Plaintiffs fail to properly allege, in their original complaint, first amended complaint, and second amended complaint, a breach of fiduciary duty. As discussed supra, Plaintiffs cannot rely on an unsigned operating agreement. Furthermore, Plaintiffs fail to plead unconscionable injury giving rise to estoppel.

            Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ third and fourth causes of action for declaratory relief and accounting are sustained without leave to amend.

Motion to Strike

            Defendant’s demurrer is sustained in its entirety. Accordingly, the motion to strike is moot.

Motion for an Undertaking

            On its own motion, the Court takes Defendant’s Motion for Order Requiring an Undertaking off calendar as moot considering its ruling on Defendant’s Demurrer.

 

IV.    CONCLUSION

Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED in its entirety without leave to amend.

Defendant’s motion to strike is MOOT.

Plaintiff’s complaint is dismissed with prejudice.
