Case Number: 23VECV02649    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Rostarmian v. Maly, Case no. 23VECV02649

Hearing date August 4, 2026

Cross-Defendant Kavoosisharifabad’s Motions for Judgment on the Pleadings

Plaintiff minor Ava M., by and through GAL Rostarmian, sues defendants Maly (her father) and Kavoosisharifabad (her mother) for breach of contract. Defendants entered into a written agreement to transfer real property to Ava M., which they allegedly failed to do.

Ava. M. was consolidated with an unlawful detainer complaint filed by Maly against Ava M. and Kavoosisharifabad arising from the same allegations. Maly sought to remove Kavoosisharifabad from the property in Ava M., alleging it was to be deeded to Ava M. upon reaching adulthood.

Maly cross-complains against Kavoosisharifabad and Moghaddem for breach of contract and declaratory relief, alleging Kavoosisharifabad engineered Ava M. to circumvent a family court judgment.

Kavoosisharifabad cross-complained against Maly for breach of contract, fraud, constructive trust and unjust enrichment, alleging Maly breached the terms of a marital settlement agreement by failing to transfer the property to Ava M. Kavoosisharifabad subsequently settled with plaintiff and assigned all her claims and interests to plaintiff.

Moghaddem cross-complains against Ava M. and Kavoosisharifabad for quiet title, declaratory relief, conspiracy, abuse of process and related claims.

Kavoosisharifabad moves for judgment on the pleadings as to Maly’s cross-complaint, as well as to Moghaddem’s FACC, on the basis that Kavoosisharifabad has assigned all her claims and interests in this litigation to plaintiff and as such is not a proper defendant. Maly and Moghaddem filed a joint opposition to separate MJOPs. The court considers the motions jointly.

Kavoosisharifabad requests judicial notice of: (1) 1/12/26 Petition for Approval of Minor’s Compromise, and (2) 3/6/26 order approving minor’s compromise. Maly and Moghaddem request judicial notice of: (1) Maly’s cross-complaint, pp. 1-11; (2) 3/6/26 order; (3) mutual settlement and release between plaintiff and Kavoosisharifabad; and (4) Kavoosisharifabad’s 3/24/26 Anti-SLAPP motion to strike. GRANTED pursuant to Evid. Code §452(d).

A motion for judgment on the pleadings should be granted when, under “the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216. Code Civ. Proc. §438 permits a party to make a motion for judgment on the pleadings per Code Civ. Proc §438(c)(1)(B) if moving party is a defendant, that either (i) the court has no jurisdiction; or (ii) the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

Kavoosisharifabad argues neither Maly’s cross-complaint nor Moghaddem’s FACC states sufficient facts to constitute causes of action against Kavoosisharifabad. Kavoosisharifabad argues the settlement between herself and plaintiff assigned “all of her breach of contract claims," “all of her claims for breach of fiduciary duty," “her claims of fraud and fraudulent conveyance” and “her claims of conversion" to plaintiff. See Maly RJN 3. Kavoosisharifabad agreed to "forego any and all rights and claims to the [subject property].” Id. Kavoosisharifabad argues she assigned all rights and claims, and plaintiff now stands in her shoes.

The joint opposition argues the January 2026 agreement between plaintiff and Kavoosisharifabad assigned Kavoosisharifabad’s affirmative claims to plaintiff and released claims between those two only. The joint opposition argues an assignment of a claimant’s rights does not immunize the assignor from liabilities owed to someone else, and two parties cannot release a nonparty’s claims without that nonparty’s consent.

The first three causes of action in Maly’s cross-complaint allegedly arise from Kavoosisharifabad’s own obligations and conduct under the marital judgment and incorporated settlement agreement, her alleged prevention or frustration of Maly’s performance and her alleged misuse of process. See Maly RJN 1. The fourth cause of action in Maly’s cross-complaint was brought against Moghaddem only for declaratory relief and dismissed without prejudice.

The settlement agreement identifies “Plaintiff Ava M.” and “Defendant Armita Kavoosi Sharifabad” as the “Parties.” See Maly RJN 3. The recitals describe a resolution of disputes “by and between Plaintiff and the Defendant” (Id.) and state Kavoosisharifabad’s claims are assigned to plaintiff, and plaintiff’s claims against Kavoosisharifabad are released. Id. The document does not state that Maly releases Kavoosisharifabad, that plaintiff assumes Kavoosisharifabad’s liabilities to Maly or that Maly’s cross-complaint is dismissed or adjudicated. The 3/6/26 order states the approval of the minor’s compromise and related settlement was as to Kavoosisharifabad only. See Maly RJN 2.

An assignment transfers the assignor’s rights; it does not, without the creditor’s consent, transfer the assignor’s burdens or erase obligations already owed to another person. Civ. Code §1457 provides: “The burden of an obligation may be transferred with the consent of the party entitled to its benefit, but not otherwise.” The mere assignment of rights does not cast the assignor’s contractual obligations on the assignee absent an assumption of those obligations. Recorded Picture Co. (Productions) Ltd. v. Nelson Entertainment, Inc. (1997) 53 Cal.App.4th 350, 362.

Maly has not consented to discharge of his claims against Kavoosisharifabad. The settlement between plaintiff and Kavoosisharifabad did not state plaintiff assumed Kavoosisharifabad’s liabilities against Maly. Kavoosisharifabad’s argument that she “lacks standing to be sued” because she assigned her own claims has no support in law. Standing concerns who may prosecute a claim; an assignor’s loss of standing to prosecute an assigned claim does not create immunity from claims owned by other creditors. Kavoosisharifabad’s reliance on Snover v. Gupta (2025) 116 Cal.App.5th 111 is misplaced; Snover, supra, was a MICRA damages-apportionment and settlement-setoff case did not address assignment or standing. The MJOP is DENIED as to Maly’s cross-complaint.

The joint opposition makes no reference to Moghaddem’s claims for declaratory relief or Moghaddem’s FACC. As the opposition is stated to be by both Maly and Moghaddem, the court treats this as an admission of the arguments in Kavoosisharifabad’s MJOP as to Moghaddem’s FACC. Further, assignment of Kavoosisharifabad’s claims in the property to plaintiff extinguish any claim for declaratory relief as to Kavoosisharifabd’s alleged rights to the property. MJOP GRANTED as to Moghaddem’s FACC only.

Case Number: 25VECV06195    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Nouri v. Jaguar Land Rover North America, Case no. 25VECV06195

Hearing date August 4, 2026

Defendant JLRNA’s Motion to Strike - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Nouri sues defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC for violations of the Song-Beverly Act and seeks civil penalties under Civ. Code §1794(c). Defendant moved to strike the claim for civil penalties, arguing noncompliance with Code Civ. Proc. §871.24. Plaintiff opposed. The court granted the motion, with 20 days leave to amend. See 2/5/26 Min. Order.

Defendant moves a second time to strike plaintiff’s prayer for civil penalties from the first amended complaint, again arguing noncompliance. The motion is unopposed.

Under subdivision (a) of §871.24, at least 30 days prior to filing a civil action seeking penalties under Civ. Code §1794(c), a consumer must: (1) Notify the manufacturer of the consumer’s name, the accurate Vehicle Identification Number (“VIN”), and a brief summary of the repair history and problems with the vehicle; (2) demand that the manufacturer repurchase or replace the vehicle; and (3) send this written notice either by (a) email to the manufacturer’s designated address, or (b) certified or registered mail, return receipt requested, to the address in the owner’s manual or warranty booklet. See Civ. Code §871.24(a)–(d).

The FAC adds a single new paragraph: “Plaintiff has satisfied his statutory prerequisites pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, § 871.24(a), therefore is entitled to seek, inter alia, civil penalties pursuant to Civil Code § 1794(c).” FAC, para. 19. This conclusory paragraph fails to state required facts such as VIN, repair history or whether notice was sent pre-litigation.

The court’s prior ruling stated failure to attach the notice to the complaint, or to plead its contents in detail, was fatal to plaintiff’s Civ. Code §1794(c) claim. See 2/5/26 Min. Order. Plaintiff failed to cure these defects via the FAC. The motion is unopposed.

GRANTED; plaintiff’s prayer for civil penalties (FAC, 9:7) is struck. No further leave to amend will be granted.
