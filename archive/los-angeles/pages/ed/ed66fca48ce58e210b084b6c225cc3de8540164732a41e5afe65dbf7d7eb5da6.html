          Defendant Transportation Services of America (“TSA”) (“Defendant”) demurs to Plaintiff Starr Indemnity & Liability Company’s (“Starr”) (“Plaintiff”) first amended complaint (“FAC”) as to the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd causes of action.  (Notice of Demurrer, pgs. 1-2; C.C.P. §§430.10(e), (f), (g).)

Real International SCM Corporation, et al., Case No. 2:23-cv-3292-DSF-PVCx, United States District Court for the Central District of California (November 17, 2023); (2) the Court’s order Granting Defendant Value Logistics, Inc.’s Motion to Dismiss Plaintiff’s in the case entitled Jeunesse, Inc. v. Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, the moving party must meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading to attempt to reach an agreement that would resolve the objections to the pleading and obviate the need for filing the demurrer.  (C.C.P. §430.41, emphasis added.)

Defendant’s counsel declares that on March 4, 2026, the parties met and conferred telephonically, and the parties were unable to reach an agreement.  (See Decl. of Berberich ¶3.)  Defendant’s counsel’s declaration is sufficient under C.C.P. §430.41.  Accordingly, the Court will consider Defendant’s demurrer.

 

          Background

          Plaintiff filed its initial Complaint on October 17, 2025, against Defendant.

          Plaintiff filed the operative FAC on November 17, 2025, against Defendant, alleging three causes of action: (1) loss of cargo; (2) breach of contract; and (3) negligence/willful misconduct.

          Defendant filed the instant demurrer on March 6, 2026.  Plaintiff filed its opposition on June 24, 2026.  Defendant filed its reply on June 25, 2026.

 

Summary of Demurrer

          Defendant demurs to the 1st cause of action on the basis that it fails to state facts sufficient to state a cause of action.  (Notice Demurrer, pg. 2; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)[1]  Defendant demurs to the 2nd cause of action on the basis that it cannot be ascertained from the pleading if the contract at issue is written, oral, or implied by contract.  (Notice Demurrer, pg. 2; C.C.P. §430.10(g).)  Defendant also demurs to the 2nd cause of action on the basis that the terms of the contract are not alleged.  (Notice Demurrer, pg. 2; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)  Defendant demurs to the 3rd cause of action on the basis that the cause of action is preempted by the Federal Aviation Administration Authorization Act, 49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)(1) (“FAAAA”) and/or Carmack Amendment, 49 U.S.C. § 14706.  (Notice Demurrer, pg. 2; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)  Defendant also demurs to the 3rd cause of action on the basis that the cause of action fails to allege a duty it owes to Plaintiff.  (Notice Demurrer, pg. 2; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)

 

Legal Standard

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.)  A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable.  (See Donabedian v. Mercury Insurance Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].)  For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law.  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital District (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

Failure to State a Claim

Loss of Cargo (1st COA)

Enacted as part of the Hepburn Act in 1906, the Carmack Amendment to the Interstate Commerce Act, title 49 United States Code section 14706, applies to motor carrier transportation of property between a location in one state to a place in another state. (49 U.S.C. § 13501; Project Hope v. M/V IBN SINA (2d Cir. 2001) 250 F.3d 67, 74-75.)  State courts have jurisdiction over Carmack Amendment claims.  (49 U.S.C. § 14706(d); Missouri, Kansas & Texas Railway Co. v. Harris (1914) 234 U.S. 412, 421, 34 S.Ct. 790, 58 L.Ed. 1377.)

The general scope of the Carmack Amendment federal preemption of state shipping statutes is as follows: “[S]tate statutes are preempted by the Carmack Amendment if they ‘in any way enlarge the responsibility of the carrier for loss or at all affect the ground of recovery, or the measure of recovery.’ [Citation.] [¶] The Carmack Amendment and the set of federal regulations that complement it cover not only the actual transport of goods, but they also govern the claims process.” (Rini v. United Van Lines, Inc. (1st Cir.1997) 104 F.3d 502, 504-505, quoting Charleston & Western Car. R.R. v. Varnville Co. (1915) 237 U.S. 597, 603.)

While the Carmack Amendment applies to motor carriers and freight forwarders, the statute does not apply to brokers.  (ASARCO LLC v. England Logistics Inc. (2014) 71 F.Supp.3d 990, 994.)  Under the Carmack Amendment, a “broker” is defined as: “A person, other than a motor carrier or an employee or agent of a motor carrier, that as a principal, or agent sells, offers for sale, negotiates for, or holds itself out by solicitation, advertisement, or otherwise as selling, providing, or arranging for, transportation by motor carrier for compensation.”  (49 U.S.C. §13102(2).) Federal regulations also specify, “[a] broker shall not, directly or indirectly, represent its operations to be that of a carrier. Any advertising shall show the broker status of the operation.  (49 C.F.R. §371.7(b).)

The Carmack Amendment defines “motor carrier” as “a person providing motor vehicle transportation for compensation.”  (49 U.S.C. § 13102(14).)  Federal regulations further clarify that:

Motor carriers, or persons who are employees or bona fide agents of carriers, are not brokers within the meaning of this section when they arrange or offer to arrange the transportation of shipments which they are authorized to transport and which they have accepted and legally bound themselves to transport.

 

(49 C.F.R. § 371.2.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant TSA was a “motor carrier,” yet pleads no facts showing that TSA owned or operated trucks, issued a bill of lading as a carrier, took possession of the cargo, or otherwise assumed carrier responsibility. (See FAC ¶¶2, 11.) Because Plaintiff’s claim alleges in conclusory fashion that TSA was a carrier without stating sufficient facts, Plaintiff’s first cause of action fails to state a claim.

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s 1st cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Breach of Contract (2nd COA)

“To prevail on a cause of action for breach of contract, the plaintiff must prove (1) the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance of the contract or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) resulting damage to the plaintiff.”  (Richman v. Hartley (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1182, 1186.)

In an action based on a written contract, a plaintiff may plead the legal effect of the contract rather than its precise language.  (Construction Protective Services, Inc. v. TIG Specialty Ins. Co. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 189, 198-199, as modified (Nov. 14, 2002).)

Plaintiff alleges that on September 27, 2024, TSA contractually agreed to assume responsibility for the safe handling, storage, forwarding, transportation and delivery of shipments destined for Costco, including the Cargo destined for Monroe Township, New Jersey, in the same good order and condition as when received from Plaintiff’s insured, via RND International in La Mirada, California. (FAC ¶12.)  Plaintiff alleges TSA further agreed to provide proactive communication which included the obligation to timely provide written notice of claim to those engaged to physically haul the freight and otherwise comply with TSA’s contractual agreements with Friendly Freight in order to protect the interests of the owner of the Cargo.  (FAC ¶12.) 

Plaintiff alleges that its insured is a third party beneficiary to the foregoing contracts which Defendants, and each of them, materially and substantially breached by failing to safely handle, store, forward, transport and deliver the Cargo, by failing to provide timely written notice of claim to those engaged to haul the Cargo and by engaging CDMAX Transport, a third party, to haul the Cargo.  (FAC ¶14.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a legal result of the material breach of, and deviation from the agreements by Defendants, Plaintiff has been damaged in the sum of $222,432.00, plus miscellaneous expenses, interest and costs, no part of which has been paid by Defendants.  (FAC ¶16.)

Plaintiff’s cause of action fails to allege whether the contract at issue was written, oral, or implied.  (C.C.P. §430.10(g).)

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s 2nd cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Negligence/Willful Misconduct (3rd COA)

Interstate commerce and the flow of goods are an issue of federal domain. (See U.S. Constitution. Art. I, §8, cl. 3 [giving Congress the power to “regulate commerce with foreign nations, among states, and with the Indian tribes”].) Congress deregulated the trucking industry in 1980.  (S.C. Johnson & Sons, Inc. v. Transportation Corp. of America, Inc. (7th Cir. 2012) 697 F.3d 544, 548 [citing Motor Carrier Act of 1980, Pub. L No. 96-296, 94 Stat. 793].) This did not, however, stop states from enacting and enforcing laws affecting prices, routes and services provided by motor carriers.  On August 23, 1994, the U.S. Congress enacted the FAAAA. The FAAAA amended the Interstate Commerce Act (1994) effective January 1, 1995. Pub. L. No. 103-105, 108 Stat. 1569, 1607.  Among other things, the FAAAA expressly preempted a wide variety of state and local regulations and state law claims affecting motor carriers. (See FAAA §601 (originally codified at 49 U.S.C. §11501(h).)

Further, on December 29, 1995, the U.S. Congress enacted the Interstate Commerce Commission Termination Act (the “ICCTA”), effective January 1, 1996. Pub. L. No. 104-88, 109 Stat. 803, 804.  Section 103 of the ICCTA recodified former 49 U.S.C. §11501(h) as 49 U.S.C. §14501(c).  In addition, the ICCTA also expanded federal preemption under the FAAAA to include preemption of claims not only against motor carriers but also against transportation brokers in particular.  (H.R. Rep. No. 311, 104th Cong., 1st Sess. 119-20 (1995), reprinted in 1995 U.S.C.C.A.N. 793, 831-832.)

The FAAAA provides, in pertinent part:

(b) Freight forwarders and brokers. –

 

(1) General Rule. – Subject to paragraph (2) of this subsection, no State or political subdivision thereof and no interstate agency or other political agency of 2 or more States shall enact or enforce any law, rule, regulation, standard, or other provision having the force and effect of law relating to intrastate rates, intrastate routes, or intrastate services of any freight forwarder or broker.

 

(49 U.S.C. §§14501(b)(1), emphasis added.)

The language of the FAAAA preemption clause expressly prohibits enforcement of state laws “related to” a “service of any . . . broker” with respect to “the transportation of property.”  (49 U.S.C. §14501(c).)  The FAAAA defines a “broker” as one who “sells offers for sale, negotiates for, or holds itself out by solicitation, advertisement, or otherwise as selling, providing, or arranging for, transportation by motor carrier for compensation.”  (49 U.S.C. §13102(2).)  The phrase “related to” in the FAAAA encapsulates state laws “having a connection with or reference to” such services, whether directly or indirectly.  (Dan’s City Used Cars, Inc. v. Pelkey (2013) 569 U.S. 251, 260.)  The FAAAA expresses a broad pre-emptive purpose. (Id.)  The FAAAA broadly preempts state common law claims and laws directly or indirectly related to the services provided by federally licensed freight brokers with respect to the transportation of property. (Id. at pg. 261.)  The scope of FAAAA preemption does not cover such laws that have only a tenuous, remote, or peripheral connection to such service.  (Rowe v. New Hampshire Motor Transportation Assn. (2008) 552 U.S. 364, 370.)

If TSA is a carrier, “the Carmack Amendment established a uniform national liability policy for interstate carriers.”  (Hughes Aircraft Co. v. N. Am. Van Lines, Inc. (9th Cir. 1992) 970 F.2d 609, 613; see also 49 U.S.C. § 14706(a).)  It is well settled that the Carmack Amendment is the exclusive cause of action for interstate-shipping contract claims alleging loss or damage to property.”  (Hall v. N. Am. Van Lines, Inc. (9th Cir. 2007) 476 F.3d 683, 688.)  As such, the statute “provides the exclusive cause of action for interstate shipping contract claims, and it completely preempts state law claims alleging delay, loss, failure to deliver and damage to property.”  (White v. Mayflower Transit, L.L.C. (9th Cir. 2008) 543 F.3d 581, 584.) The Ninth Circuit has held that common law claims are preempted by the Carmack Amendment “to the extent that it arises from the same conduct as the claims for delay, loss or damage to shipped property.”  (Id. at pg. 586.)

Here, Plaintiff’s state law claims are based entirely on the same conduct that forms the basis of its claims for property damage under the Carmack Amendment.  Accordingly, they are subject to “a complete defense” under the Carmack Amendment.  (White, 543 F.3d at pg. 584 [stating Carmack Amendment “constitutes a complete defense to common law claims against interstate carriers for negligence, fraud, and conversion”].)

Plaintiff’s argument in opposition that the cause of action is properly alleged based on the U.S. Supreme Court’s recent decision in Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC (2026) 146 S.Ct. 1199 is not well taken.  Montgomery involved claims arising from a motor vehicle accident resulting in bodily injury and allegations concerning highway safety.  The Montgomery Court’s analysis focused on the FAAAA’s safety exception and whether state-law negligence claims relating to motor vehicle safety fell within that exception.  Montgomery addressed only whether the FAAAA’s safety exception preserves state law negligence claims arising from motor vehicle accidents involving bodily injury. Plaintiff’s claim presents a materially different question.  Here, Plaintiff seeks recovery solely for economic losses arising from the alleged theft of cargo.  Because Plaintiff’s claim does not concern motor vehicle safety, it falls outside the FAAAA’s safety exception and remains subject to express preemption.  Indeed, although the Supreme Court abrogated Ye v. GlobalTranz Enters., Inc. (7th Cir. 2023) 74 F.4th 453 and Aspen American Ins. Co. v. Landstar Ranger, Inc. (11th Cir. 2023) 65 F.4th 1261, it only abrogated those cases in the context of personal injury claims tied to motor vehicle safety.

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s 3rd cause of action is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

          Conclusion

Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s 1st, 2nd, 3rd causes of action is sustained with 20 days leave.

ERIC W. PEARSON, AS AN INDIVIDUAL AND AS TRUSTEE OF THE ERIC W. PEARSON REVOCABLE LIVING, et al.,

JENNIFER J. MACLELLAN, AS AN INDIVIDUAL AND AS TRUSTEE OF THE JENNIFER J. MACLELLAN SEPARATE, et al.

 Hearing Date:  July 8, 2026

 

Defendant Jennifer J. MacLellan’s, individually and as Trustee of the Jennifer J. MacLellan Separate Property Trust, unopposed motion to compel arbitration of Plaintiffs Eric W. Pearson’s, individually and as Trustee of the Eric W. Pearson Revocable Living Trust, and Ashley Platz’s claims in this action is granted.  This case is stayed pending arbitration.

The Court notes that Non-moving Defendants are not compelled to arbitration.

The Court sets a non-appearance case review for July 8, 2027, at 8:30 a.m.  The parties are directed to submit a joint statement five calendar days in advance, apprising the Court of the status of the arbitration.

 

Defendant Jennifer J. MacLellan’s, individually and as Trustee of the Jennifer J. MacLellan Separate Property Trust (“MacLellan”) (“Moving Defendant”) moves unopposed for an order compelling arbitration of all claims asserted by Plaintiffs Eric W. Pearson, individually and as Trustee of the Eric W. Pearson Revocable Living Trust (“Pearson”), and Ashley Platz (“Platz”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) and staying the instant action during the pendency of arbitration.  (Notice of Motion, pgs. 2-3; 9 U.S.C. §§3-4; C.C.P. §§1281.2, 1281.4.) 

 

Background

On October 27, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the instant action against Moving Defendant and Non-moving Defendants Krause Realty (“Realty”) and Joshua Krause (“Krause”) (collectively, “Defendants”), asserting six causes of action: (1) breach of contract; (2) negligence; (3) fraud; (4) failure to make statutory disclosures pursuant to Civ. Code §§2079 et seq.; (5) failure to make statutory disclosures pursuant to Civ. Code §§1102 et seq; and (6) unfair business practices pursuant to Bus. & Prof. Code §§17200 et seq.  (See Complaint.)

Moving Defendant filed the instant motion on March 2, 2026.  Plaintiffs filed their opposition on April 29, 2026.  On May 5, 2026, Moving Defendant filed their reply.

On May 12, 2026, the Court posted a tentative ruling and heard argument on the instant motion.  This Court continued the hearing on the instant motion to July 8, 2026, to allow for further briefing on whether the arbitration agreement is governed by the FAA.  (See 5/12/26 Minute Order.)

On June 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of non-opposition, indicating that they withdrew their opposition and do not oppose this Court ordering the case to arbitration as previously indicated in this Court’s tentative ruling.  (See Notice of Non-Opposition, pg. 2.)

Based on Plaintiff’s consent to arbitration, the Court grants Moving Defendants’ motion and also compels for the reasons below that were stated in the Court’s earlier tentative ruling:

 

Motion to Compel Arbitration

A.  Arbitration Agreement

1.     The Arbitration Agreement is enforceable.

Federal law provides for enforcement of this Arbitration Agreement. The Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. §1, et seq. (“FAA”), establishes a strong federal policy in favor of arbitration of disputes where a written arbitration agreement exists. Section 2 of the FAA provides, in pertinent part that “[a] written provision . . . to settle by arbitration a controversy thereafter arising out of such contract . . .  shall be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable.”  (9 U.S.C. §2.)  The purpose of the FAA is to “reverse the longstanding judicial hostility to arbitration agreements.” (Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp. (1991) 500 U.S. 20, 24.)  The FAA places arbitration agreements “on an equal footing with other contracts and [requires courts] to enforce them according to their terms.”  (AT&T Mobility, LLC v. Concepcion (2011) 563 U.S. 333, 339; see also Rent-A-Center West, Inc. v. Jackson (2010) 561 U.S. 63, 67 [“The FAA reflects the fundamental principle that arbitration is a matter of contract.”].)  The FAA will preempt not only a state law that “discriminat[es] on its face against arbitration,” but also a state law that “covertly accomplishes the same objective by disfavoring contracts that (oh so coincidentally) have the defining features of arbitration agreements.”  (Kindred Nursing Centers Limited Partnership v. Clark (2017) 137 S.Ct. 1421, 1426.)

The FAA restricts a court’s inquiry related to compelling arbitration to two threshold questions: (1) whether there was an agreement to arbitrate between the parties; and (2) whether the agreement covers the dispute.  (Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. (2002) 537 U.S. 79, 84.)  Here, both criteria are satisfied. First, Plaintiffs agreed to arbitration when they entered into The California Residential Purchase Agreement and Joint Escrow Instructions (“RPA”) on May 16, 2022, which contained the relevant arbitration clause (“Arbitration Agreement”).  (Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 §31(A).)  Second, the Arbitration Agreement expressly covers “that any dispute or claim in Law or equity arising between them out of this Agreement or any resulting transaction, which is not settled through mediation, shall be decided by neutral, binding arbitration.”  (Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 at §31(A).)

That the mediation pre-condition has also been met:

MEDIATION:

A.   The Parties agree to mediate any dispute or claim arising between them out of this Agreement, or any resulting transaction, before resorting to arbitration or court action. The mediation shall be conducted through the C.A.R. Real Estate Mediation Center for Consumers (www.consumermediation.org) or through any other mediation provider or service mutually agreed to mediation prior to, or within a reasonable time after, the dispute or claim is presented to the Agent. Mediation fees, if any, shall be divided equally among the Parties involved, and shall be recoverable under the prevailing party attorney fees clause.

 

(Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 at §30(A), emphasis added.)  Moving Defendant’s counsel’s declaration states, “The parties participated in mediation on October 7, 2025. The mediation did not result in a settlement.”  (Decl. of Waggoner ¶4.)

California law also favors arbitration for dispute resolution. The California Arbitration Act (“CAA”), codified at C.C.P. §1281 et seq., provides, “A written agreement to submit to arbitration an existing controversy or a controversy thereafter arising is valid, enforceable and irrevocable, save upon such grounds as exist for the revocation of any contract.”  (C.C.P. §1281; see also Grafton Partners L.P. v. Superior Court (2005) 36 Cal.4th 944, 955 [“[U]nlike predispute jury waivers, predispute arbitration agreements are specifically authorized by statute.”].) 

“California law, like federal law, favors enforcement of valid arbitration agreements.”  (Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services, Inc. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 83, 97, 99.)  The public policy in favor of arbitration is so strong that California courts have held that an employee is “bound by the provisions of the [arbitration] agreement regardless of whether [he] read it or [was] aware of the arbitration clause when [he] signed the document.”  (Brookwood v. Bank of America (1996) 45 Cal.App.4th 1667, citing Macaulay v. Norlander (1992) 12 Cal.App.4th 1.)  The only prerequisite for a court to order arbitration is a determination that the parties have entered into an agreement to arbitrate the dispute.  (United Transportation Union v. Southern California Rapid Transit District (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 804, 808.)  Thus, arbitration must be ordered “unless the agreement clearly does not apply to the dispute in question.”  (Vianna v. Doctors’ Management Co. (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 1186, 1189.)

Plaintiffs agreed to arbitration when they entered into the RPA.  (Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 §31(A).)  The Arbitration Agreement expressly covers “that any dispute or claim in Law or equity arising between them out of this Agreement or any resulting transaction, which is not settled through mediation, shall be decided by neutral, binding arbitration.”  (Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 at §31(A).)

Plaintiff argues that other necessary and indispensable parties are part of the overall fact situation giving rise to this lawsuit and that those parties refuse to be part of any arbitration process.  (Opposition, pgs. 3-4.)  Plaintiff’s argument is unavailing.  The RPA mandates that “any motion to compel arbitration pursuant to, this agreement to arbitrate shall be governed by the procedural rules of the Federal Arbitration Act, and not the California Arbitration Act.”  (See Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1, § 31(A).)  Plaintiffs rely on C.C.P. §1281.2(c), arguing it “provides that a court shall order arbitration unless it determines, among other things, that ‘[a] party to the arbitration agreement is also a party to a pending court action or special proceeding with a third party, arising out of the same transaction or series of related transactions and there is a possibility of conflicting rulings on a common issue of law or fact.’”  (Opposition, pg. 4.)  But C.C.P. §1281.2(c) does not override the FAA where the underlying arbitration agreement involves interstate commerce.  The FAA preempts state laws that grant courts discretion to deny arbitration on grounds inconsistent with the federal mandate.  If Non-moving Defendants remain in court, the claims between Plaintiffs and Moving Defendant must be compelled to arbitration.

The FAA interprets “involving commerce” broadly, covering all of Congress’ Commerce Clause power.  (Allied-Bruce Terminix Companies, Inc. v. Dobson (1995) 513 U.S. 265, 277 [concluding “involving commerce” “signals an intent to exercise Congress’ commerce power to the full”].)  Residential real estate transactions, involving an escrow holder, title insurer, lender, and wire transfers usually involve national financial institutions and out-of-state banks, and courts routinely enforce FAA governance clauses in such circumstances.  (Hedges v. Carrigan (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 578, 586 [holding home purchasers’ use of federal loan and national organization’s transaction documents “evidenced a transaction ‘involving commerce’”].)

Further, Code of Civil Procedure § 1281.2(c) is inapposite because of the RPA’s clear displacement of the CAA in favor of the FAA.  Simply, Plaintiffs and Moving Defendant agreed that the FAA would govern disputes arising out of the RPA.  (See Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 §31(A).)  Plaintiffs admit that they initialed the RPA’s arbitration provision.  (Opposition, pg. 4.)  Plaintiffs do not contend the RPA is not a valid contract based on arguments that it is void, rescinded, unconscionable, or otherwise unenforceable under Civil Code §1550 or any other contract defense.

Based on the foregoing, Moving Defendant proved the existence of a valid Arbitration Agreement that is enforceable by Moving Defendant.

 

2.     Covered Claims

The Arbitration Agreement states that “any dispute or claim in Law or equity arising between them out of this Agreement or any resulting transaction, which is not settled through mediation, shall be decided by neutral, binding arbitration.”  (Decl. of Waggoner ¶2, Exh. 1 at §31(A) [emphasis added].)

(Decl. of Casillas ¶8, Exh. A at pg. 1 of 5, §2.)  All claims arise out of Plaintiffs’ agreement to purchase the Property and the parties’ performance of that agreement. There is no claim independent of the transaction created by the RPA.  Accordingly, the arbitration provision squarely encompasses the dispute. Based on the foregoing, Defendant met its burden of establishing the Arbitration Agreement covers the causes of action asserted in Plaintiffs’ Complaint.

 

B. Unconscionability

“[P]rocedural and substantive unconscionability must both be present in order for a court to exercise its discretion to refuse to enforce a contract or clause under the doctrine of unconscionability.”  (Armendariz, 24 Cal.4th at pg. 102.)  Courts invoke a sliding scale which disregards the regularity of the procedural process of the contract formation, that creates the terms, in proportion to the greater harshness or unreasonableness of the substantive terms themselves, i.e., the more substantively oppressive the contract term, the less evidence of procedural unconscionability is required to conclude that the term is unenforceable, and vice versa.  (Id., at pg. 114.)  Plaintiff bears the burden of proving that the provision at issue is both procedurally and substantively unconscionable.  Plaintiff does not raise an argument related to unconscionability and thereby concedes that the Arbitration Agreement is not unconscionable.   

 

C.    Stay of Current Action

The FAA mandates a stay.  Where an action involves “any issue referable to arbitration under an agreement in writing,” the court “shall on application of one of the parties stay the trial of the action until such arbitration has been had.”  (9 U.S.C. §3.)  The statutory directive is not discretionary as to claims subject to arbitration.  Plaintiffs’ claims against all Defendants arise from the same real estate transaction and are based on the same alleged representations and disclosures. Because the arbitration will address the core factual and legal issues underlying the action, a stay of the entire matter pending completion of arbitration would serve judicial efficiency and avoid inconsistent results.

Accordingly, this case is stayed pending arbitration.

 

D.   Conclusion

Moving Defendant’s motion to compel arbitration is granted. 

The Court notes that Non-moving Defendants are not compelled to arbitration.

The case is stayed pending arbitration. The Court sets a non-appearance case review for July 8, 2027, at 8:30 a.m.  The parties are directed to submit a joint statement five calendar days in advance, apprising the Court of the status of the arbitration.
