CASE:                         CBL Partners, LLC v. Jin Chul Kim, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 21STCV01584

 

 

PROPOSED JUDGMENT

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff CBL Partners, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     No opposition

 

 

  On January 13, 2021, Plaintiff, CBL Partners, LLC, filed a Complaint against defendants Jin Chul Kim (“JC Kim”), Young Hee Kim (“YH Kim”), and Does 1 through 25, for breach of lease agreement and breach of guaranty for the property at 1818 S. Western Ave., Suite 100, Los Aneles, California 90004. (the “Property”).

 

On July 20, 2021, YH Kim filed a Cross-Complaint against JC Kim for implied contractual indemnity, equitable indemnity, and contribution. 

 

On August 25, 2021, default was entered against JC Kim on the Complaint. 

 

On September 20, 2021, the default against JC Kim was set aside by stipulation.

 

On October 1, 2021, JC Kim filed a Complaint.

 

This case proceeded to trial on January 21, 2026.  JC Kim did not appear.  At the trial, the court dismissed YH Kim’s Cross-Complaint action against JC Kim and dismissed Plaintiff’s Complaint as to YH Kim.  The court proceeded with the trial as a prove-up hearing against JC Kim.  After the hearing, the court found that Plaintiff had proved up its damages against JC Kim in the sum of $714,164.16 and a contractual basis to award attorney fees.  The court directed Plaintiff to file a memorandum of costs and a declaration to support the attorney fees award.

 

On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a request for court judgment against JC Kim on Form CIV-100, a proposed judgment, and a memorandum of costs in the sum of $1,347.55.  Plaintiff requests a total judgment against JC Kim in the sum of $741,762.01, consisting of $714,164.16 (Demand of complaint), $1,347.85 in costs, and $26,250.00 in attorney fees. 

On March 27, 2026, the court rejected the proposed judgment for the following reasons:

 

1.     Despite being directed to do so, Plaintiff did not submit any evidence to support the attorney fees request.

2.     Plaintiff improperly uses Form CIV-100 for entry of judgment.

3.     Does 1 through 25 had not been dismissed from this action. 

 

On June 22, 2026, Does 1 through 25 were dismissed from this action.  On the same day, Plaintiff filed another proposed judgment and memorandum of costs.  Plaintiff requests a total judgment against JC Kim in the sum of $742,733.21, consisting of $714,164.16, $1,356.55 in costs, and $27,212.50 in attorney fees. 

 

            After review, the court finds the proposed judgment, cannot be approved for the following reasons:

 

            1.  Plaintiff requests costs in the sum of $1,356.55, but the Memorandum of Costs supports only the recovery of $1,347.85 in costs.

 

            2.  The proposed judgment indicates that Plaintiff obtained entry of default against JC Kim.  However, the entry of default was set aside pursuant to stipulation of the parties. 

 

            Based on the foregoing, the proposed judgment against Defendant Young Chul Kim is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.  Plaintiff is ordered to file a revised proposed judgment and supporting documents consistent with this order before the next hearing date.

 

The clerk of the court to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV17696    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     June 30, 2026                                     TRIAL DATE:  June 5, 2028

                                                          

CASE:                         Veritext, LLC v. Nancy Anfanger

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV17696

 

 

DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES - GENERAL, SET ONE, FROM PLAINTIFF VERITEXT, LLC

doing business as ATKINSON BAKER, A VERITEXT COMPANY

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant Nancy B. Anfanger, in pro per

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      No opposition

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

            On June 4, 2026, Defendant, Nancy B. Anfanger, filed this motion to compel responses from Plaintiff, Veritext, LLC, to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories – General, Set One.  Defendant requests sanctions against Plaintiff and its counsel of record in the sum of $1,500.  Defendant is a self-represented litigant.

 

At the time of this writing, no opposition has been filed.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

If a party to whom interrogatories were directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order to compel responses without objections.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(b).)¿Failure to timely serve responses waives objections to the requests. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a).) A party moving to compel discovery responses under these statutory provisions is not required to meet and confer prior to filing the motion.¿ (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(b); see also Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411 [citing Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 906 for the proposition that “meet and confer” requirement “did not apply when propounding party sought order compelling responses to interrogatories and sanctions for responding party's failure to respond ‘within the statutorily permitted time’”].)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

Monetary Sanctions¿ 

¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿ 

If the court finds that a party has unsuccessfully made or opposed a motion to compel, the court “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(c).)¿¿¿ 

 

Sanctions may also be imposed against counsel if a party seeking sanctions against an attorney offers sufficient evidence of a misuse of the discovery process, and the attorney fails to demonstrate that he or she did not recommend that conduct. (Kwan Software Engineering, Inc. v. Hennings (2020) 58 Cal.App.5th 57, 81 (Kwan Software).)¿ 

 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

Defendant moves the court for an order compelling Plaintiff to provide responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories-General, Set One.

¿¿ 

It is undisputed that Defendant properly served Plaintiff with the at-issue discovery requests on April 16, 2026.¿ To date, Plaintiff has not served responses.¿ Accordingly, all objections to the discovery requests are waived.  Defendant is entitled to an order compelling responses to the interrogatories.¿¿ 

¿¿ 

¿Monetary Sanctions¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Defendant request sanctions against Plaintiff and its counsel.¿ Given the court’s ruling, sanctions are warranted.¿ Pursuant to Kwan Software, imposition of monetary sanctions against counsel is also proper unless counsel shows that he or she did not counsel the discovery abuse.¿ (Kwan Software, supra, 58 Cal.App.5th at p. 81.)¿ Plaintiff’s counsel does not meet their burden.¿ The court will impose sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel.  Because Defendant is self-represented and did not include any statement regarding the costs she incurred in bringing this motion (see Kravitz v. Superior Court (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 1015, 1016-1017, 1020 [holding that pro se litigants, including pro se lawyers, who have prevailed on motions to compel responses to requests for production of documents, cannot recover attorney fees as a discovery sanction, but may recover reasonable expenses that were incurred, such as photocopying and computer-assisted legal research]), the court imposes sanctions in the amount of $100 against Plaintiff and its counsel of record.¿¿ 

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

The unopposed motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to serve verified, objection-free responses to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories-General, Set One, within 30 days of this order. 

 

Defendant’s request for sanctions is GRANTED.  Plaintiff and its counsel of record is ordered to pay sanctions in the amount of $100 to Defendant within 30 days of this order.¿ 

 

Defendant to give notice, unless waived. 

 

 

 

Dated:   June 30, 2026                                            

 

   

 

  Brock T. Hammond  

  Judge of the Superior Court

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

CASE:                         Brandi Blue v. Michael Girourad, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV25184

 

 

DEMURRER WITHOUT MOTION TO STRIKE

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendants City of Los Angeles and Michael Girourad

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Plaintiff Brandi Blue

 

           

            Plaintiff Brandi Blue (“Blue” or “Plaintiff”) was employed by defendant City of Los Angeles (“City”) as a Heavy-Duty Equipment Operator in the City’s Bureau of Sanitation’s Hyperion Water Reclamation Plant.  Blue alleges that, between 2019-2025, Blue’s supervisors subjected her to discrimination, harassment, and retaliation.

 

I.         BACKGROUND

 

            On August 27, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against Defendants, the City and Michael Girourad (“Girourad”), alleging causes of action for (1) Sex Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (2) Race Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (3) Disability Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (4) Perception of Disability Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (5) Failure to Engage in Good Faith Interactive Process in Violation of FEHA; (6) Failure to Provide Reasonable Accommodation; (7) Violation of Exercising Medical Leave Rights Under CFRA and FMLA; (8) Associational Disability Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (9) Combined Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (10) Sex Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (11) Race Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (12) Disability Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (13) Associational Disability Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (14) Combined Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (15) Retaliation for Disclosing, Reporting, Opposing, Complaining of FEHA Violations and Exercising CFRA Rights; (16) Retaliation for Engagement of Statutory Protected Activities; (17) Failure Engage, Failure to Prevent Discrimination, Harassment, and Retaliation in Violation of FEHA; (18) Whistleblower Retaliation; (19) Whistleblower Retaliation; and (20) Defamation.  The tenth, eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth, and twentieth causes of action are alleged against Girourad. 

 

            On November 18, 2025, Defendants filed a demurrer to the Complaint.

On April 27, 2026, the court issued a ruling to sustain the demurrers to the first, second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, fifteenth, sixteenth, eighteenth, and nineteenth causes of action with leave to amend, and to overrule the demurrers to the tenth, eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth, fourteenth, seventeenth, and twentieth causes of actions.

            On the same day, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), alleging causes of action for (1) Sex Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (2) Race Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (3) Disability Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (4) Associational Disability Discrimination in Violation of FEHA; (5) Combined Discrimination in Violation of FEHA, (6) Sex Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (7) Race Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (8) Disability Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (9) Associational Disability Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (10) Combined Harassment-Hostile Work Environment in Violation of FEHA; (11) Retaliation for Disclosing, Reporting, Opposing, Complaining of FEHA Violations and Exercising CFRA Rights; (12) Failure to Prevent Discrimination, Harassment, and Retaliation in Violation of FEHA; (13) Whistleblower Retaliation (Labor Code § 1102.5); (14) Whistleblower Retaliation (Labor Code §§ 6310, 6400-6404); and (15) defamation.

 

            On May 26, 2026, Defendants filed a demurrer to the first, second, third, fourth, fifth, eighth, ninth, eleventh, thirteenth, and fourteenth causes of action in the FAC.

 

            On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

            On June 23, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

            A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿ (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)¿ When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.¿ (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)¿ “Because a demurrer challenges defects on the face of the complaint, it can only refer to matters outside the pleading that are subject to judicial notice.”¿ (Arce ex rel. Arce v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 556.)¿ 

III.      DISCUSSION

Defendants demur to the first, second, third, fourth, fifth, eighth, ninth, eleventh, thirteenth, and fourteenth causes of action.  As a threshold issue, Plaintiff argues the demurrers to the eighth and ninth causes of action should be overruled because the court rejected Defendants’ previous demurrers to these causes of action.  The eighth and ninth causes of action were asserted as the twelfth and thirteenth causes of action in the Complaint.  However, the court did not address whether the twelfth and thirteenth causes of action were deficient because of a lack of causal connection to protected status.  Accordingly, the court considers all demurrers raised herein.[1]

 

Defendants advance one general argument: the FAC fails to allege facts to show Defendants’ alleged adverse actions were motivated because of a retaliatory or discriminatory animus.  Causes of action one through five are FEHA discrimination claims.  Causes of action eleven, thirteen, and fourteen are retaliation claims.  Previously, the court agreed with Defendants challenge to these claims (alleged as cause of action one, two, three, four, seven, eight, fifteen, sixteen, and eighteen, and nineteen in the Complaint), noting there were only five lines concerning retaliatory and discriminatory animus.  (See Minute Order, 04/27/26.)  Defendants renew this challenge to the FAC. 

 

After reviewing the parties’ briefing, the Complaint, and the FAC, the court finds the FAC adequately alleges retaliatory and discriminatory animus as to all causes of action except the ninth cause of action.  The FAC is replete with allegations establishing Plaintiff’s protected status, protected activities, and the actions undertaken by Defendants because of Plaintiff’s status and activities.  (See FAC, ¶¶ 1-24.)[2]  For the purposes of demurrer, these allegations are more than sufficient to notify Defendants of the nature of her claims.  The second, third, fourth, fifth, eighth, eleventh, thirteenth, and fourteenth causes of action do not fail on these grounds.

 

The court takes a different view as to the ninth cause of action for associational disability harassment.  The claim is predicated on alleged harassment based on Plaintiff’s association with her son who is autistic.  However, there are simply no allegations connection harassment to Plaintiff’s association with her son.  The allegations are connected to her status as the sole Black woman in a male-dominated workplace, requests for accommodations because of injury, and Defendants’ alleged efforts to prevent Plaintiff from accruing the necessary hours to advance in her career, among other things.  The ninth cause of action is deficient.

 

Defendants lodge one additional argument as to the fourteenth cause of action for violation of Labor Code section 6310.  Defendants argue the fourteenth cause of action fails because Plaintiff did not exhaust her administrative remedies before filing suit. For this proposition, Defendant relies on Sacramento County Deputy Sheriffs' Assn. v. County of Sacramento (1990) 220 Cal.App.3d 280 (Sacramento). The argument fails.  Sacramento did not concern a violation of Labor Code section 6310.  Moreover, prevailing authority holds otherwise.  In Sheridan v. Touchstone Television Productions, LLC (2015) 241 Cal.App.4th 508, 818-819, the court of appeal held that a plaintiff bringing a Labor Code section 6310 claim is not required to exhaust administrative procedures by filing a claim with the Labor Commissioner prior to bringing the claim. 

 

IV.        CONCLUSION

The demurrers to the second, third, fourth, fifth, eighth, eleventh, thirteenth, and fourteenth causes of action are Overruled.

The demurrers to the ninth cause of action are Sustained.  Leave to amend is Denied Without Prejudice.

Defendants are ordered to serve and file their Answer to the First Amended Complaint within 5 court days of this order.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice, unless waived.

 

 

Dated:   June 30, 2026

  Judge of the Superior Court

[2] These allegations also appear in the Complaint.  The court overlooked these allegations in ruling on Defendants’ prior demurrer. 


Case Number: 25STCV36167    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 407

CASE:                         Keith Saunders v. William Blackwell, as Conservator of the Estate of Ted H. Blackwell, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV36167

 

 

DEMURRER WITHOUT MOTION TO STRIKE

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant William Blackwell, as Conservator of the Estate of Ted H. Blackwell aka Theodore Blackwell and Theodore H. Blackwell

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Plaintiff Keith Saunders

 

           

            Plaintiff Keith Saunders (“Saunders” or “Plaintiff”) was employed as a 24-hour, in-home caregiver by Ted H. Blackwell (“T. Blackwell”).  T. Blackwell has been under a conservatorship at all relevant times.  In 2023, Saunder’s employment was terminated.  In 2025, Saunders filed a Creditor’s Claim against T. Blackwell’s Conservatorship Estate for wages owed from the period of February 2021 to September 2023..  William Blackwell (“W. Blackwell”), T. Blackwell’s brother and conservator over T. Blackwell and his estate, rejected the claim.  Saunders now brings this action pursuant to Probate Code section 9353 to enforce his claim for unpaid wages against T. Blackwell.

 

I.         BACKGROUND

 

            On December 11, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against defendant William Blackwell, as Conservator of the Estate of Ted H. Blackwell aka Theodore Blackwell and Theodore H. Blackwell (“Defendant”), alleging causes of action for (1) Wage Theft in Violation of the Domestic Worker Bill of Rights (Lab. Code §§ 1194, 1450 et seq.); (2) Failure to Pay Minimum Wages (Labor Code §§ 1194, 1197); (3) Violation of Labor Code §1194.2; (4) Violation of Labor Code §§ 201, 203; and (5) Violation of Business & Professions Code § 17200.

 

            On April 23, 2026, Defendant filed a demurrer to the Complaint.

 

            On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

            On June 23, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

II.        JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

            Defendant’s request for judicial notice is GRANTED.

 

III.      LEGAL STANDARD

            A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿ (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)¿ When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.¿ (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)¿ “Because a demurrer challenges defects on the face of the complaint, it can only refer to matters outside the pleading that are subject to judicial notice.”¿ (Arce ex rel. Arce v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 556.)¿ 

IV.       DISCUSSION

 

Defendant argues the Complaint fails for two reasons: (1) the Complaint fails to state

any cause of action against Ted individually because, as a person suffering from dementia who has been under a conservatorship since 2007, Ted was not and could not have been Saunders’ “employer”; (2) as to William in his capacity as Conservator, Plaintiff’s claims involve a dispute over personal property of the conservatorship estate and must be adjudicated in probate court pursuant to Probate Code sections 850 and 855.

 

            Defendant’s first argument lacks merit.  The Domestic Worker Bill of Rights states, in pertinent part:

 

A person, including corporate officers or executives, who directly or indirectly, or through an agent or any other person, including through the services of a third-party employer, temporary service, or staffing agency or similar entity, employs or exercises control over the wages, hours, or working conditions of a domestic work employee.

 

(Lab. Code, § 1451(c)(1); see also Guerrero v. Superior Court (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 912, 946 fn. 26 [in a household occupation case, observing that Wage order No. 15-2001 defined employer as any person who directly or indirectly, or through an agent or any other person, employs or exercises control over the wages, hours, or working conditions of any person].)  Under this broad language, an individual, regardless of mental capacity, could qualify as an employer, including a conservatee with dementia acting indirectly through his conservator.  Defendant does not provide any authority supporting a contrary conclusion. 

 

            Defendant’s second argument fares no better.  Probate Code section 9353 states: 

 

(a) Regardless of whether the statute of limitations otherwise applicable to a claim will expire before or after the following times, a claim rejected in whole or in part is barred as to the part rejected unless, within the following times, the creditor commences an action on the claim or the matter is referred to a referee or to arbitration:

(1) If the claim is due at the time the notice of rejection is given, 90 days after the notice is given.

(2) If the claim is not due at the time the notice of rejection is given, 90 days after the claim becomes due.

(b) The time during which there is a vacancy in the office of the personal representative shall be excluded from the period determined under subdivision (a).

 

In other words, Plaintiff was required to bring this civil action to avoid the expiration of his claims.  Case law supports this procedure.  (See, e.g., McDonald v. Structured Asset Sales, LLC (2007) 154 Cal.App.4th 1068, 1074 [in a case concerning a rejected creditor’s claim as to a decedent’s estate, noting “appropriate course of action when a creditor's claim is rejected is for the creditor to commence a separate action on the rejected claim challenging rejection of the creditor's claim”].)[1]  The demurrers fail.

IV.        CONCLUSION

           

The demurrers are OVERRULED.

 

Defendant is ordered to serve and file its Answer to the Complaint within 5 court days of this order.

 

Moving party to give notice, unless waived.

 

 

Dated:   June 30, 2026                                            

 

   

 

  Brock T. Hammond  

  Judge of the Superior Court 

 

           


[1] Probate Code section 9353 applies to creditor’s claims against a decedent’s estate.  There are no allegations nor judicially noticed facts establishing that T. Blackwell is deceased.  Neither party discusses whether Section 9353 is properly invoked in this action.  In any event, the court notes the underlying principle of Section 9353 also applies to rejected creditor’s claims against a decedent’s estate.  (See, e.g., Stevenson v. Superior Court (1970) 9 Cal.App.3d 904, 906, 907 [law holding “that a creditor is authorized to petition the probate court for an order directing the conservator to pay a rejected claim based upon a debt, expense or charge, only if the claim has been Lawfully established to be due and payable” because “rejected claims in a conservatorship must be established by an action at law”].)


Case Number: 26STCV00488    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

CASE:                         USAmplitude v. Tiprah Commerce LLC, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 26STCV00488

 

 

DEFENDANT CALIFORNIA NUTRITIONAL PRODUCTS, INC.’S MOTION FOR

JUDGMENT ON THE PLEADINGS

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant California Nutritional Products, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      No opposition

 

Plaintiff USAmplitude (“Plaintiff”) operates as a licensed motor carrier.  Defendant Tiprah Commerce LLC (“Tiprah”), and others, including but not limited to defendant California Nutritional Products, Inc. (“CNP”), solicited Plaintiff to provide shipment services.  Tiprah executed and delivered a “Rate and Load Confirmation” (“Rate Confirmations”) to Plaintiff which, according to Plaintiff, constitute contracts for carriage.  Plaintiff provided the shipment services to Tiprah.  However, Tiprah defaulted under the Rate Confirmations and failed to make payment.  This action followed.

 

I.          BACKGROUND

 

            On January 7, 2026, Plaintiff commenced this action against Tiprah, CNP, and other defendants, alleging causes of action for (1) breach of written contract, (2) goods and services rendered, (3) reasonable value, (4) account stated, (5) open book account, and (6) unjust enrichment.

 

            On February 17, 2026, CNP filed an Answer to the Complaint.

 

            On May 27, 2026, CNP filed this motion for judgment on the pleadings. 

                                                    

            The motion is unopposed.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

“A motion for judgment on the pleadings performs the same function as a general demurrer, and hence attacks only defects disclosed on the face of the pleadings or by matters that can be judicially noticed.¿ [Citations.]”¿ (Burnett v. Chimney Sweep (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 1057 1064.)¿ The court must assume the truth of all properly pleaded material facts and allegations, but not contentions or conclusions of fact or law.¿ (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318; Wise v. Pacific Gas and Elec. Co. (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 725, 738.)¿ “A judgment on the pleadings in favor of the defendant is appropriate when the complaint fails to allege facts sufficient to state a cause of action.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 438(c)(3)(B)(ii).)”¿ (Kapsimallis v. Allstate Ins. Co. (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 667, 672.)¿ “Presentation of extrinsic evidence is therefore not proper on a motion for judgment on the pleadings.¿[Citation.]”¿ (Cloud v. Northrop Grumman Corp. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 995, 999.)¿ “The common law ground for a motion for judgment on the pleadings is identical to the statutory ground[.]”  (Korchemny v. Piterman (2021) 68 Cal.App.5th 1032, 1055.)  Allegations are to be liberally construed.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)¿¿¿ 

 

If the motion for judgment on the pleadings is granted, it may be granted with or without leave to amend.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 438(h)(1).)¿ “Where a demurrer is sustained or a motion for judgment on the pleadings is granted as to the original complaint, denial of leave to amend constitutes an abuse of discretion if the pleading does not show on its face that it is incapable of amendment.”¿ (Virginia G. v. ABC Unified School Dist.¿(1993) 15 Cal.App.4th 1848, 1852.)¿

 

III.      DISCUSSION

CNP argues Plaintiff’s claims against CNP are uncertain.  The court agrees.  Plaintiff asserts six causes of action against CNP.  Yet, the Complaint’s only allegations specific to CNP are pleaded in the alternative and on information and belief.   Specifically, the Complaint alleges:

“Plaintiff is informed and believes and thereon alleges that Defendant CALIFORNIA NUTRITIONAL PRODUCTS INC, a California Corporation, conducted business in Los Angeles County, California and at all relevant times acted as the shipper and/or receiver of goods for which Plaintiff provided transportation services.”

 

(Complaint, ¶ 7; see also Complaint, ¶ 14 [conceding “[a]ll allegations in the complaint are based on information belief and/or are likely to have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery.  Whenever allegations in this complaint are contrary or inconsistent, such allegations shall be deemed alternative.”].)

 

These allegations are insufficient to apprise CNP of the nature of the allegations it is called to answer.  There are no allegations or exhibits attached to the Complaint establishing that CNP entered into a contractual relationship with Plaintiff.  Likewise, there are no facts alleged to establish Plaintiff rendered any serves to CNP.  The Complaint is uncertain.

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

            The motion for judgment on the pleadings is GRANTED. Leave to amend is GRANTED.

 

            Plaintiff is ordered to serve and file the First Amended Complaint within 15 days of this order.

 

            Moving party to give notice, unless waived.

 

 

Dated:   June 30, 2026                                            

 

   

 

  Brock T. Hammond  

  Judge of the Superior Court
