Case #24CHCV02287

 

MOTION TO COMPEL CELL PHONE RECORDS

 

Motion filed on 2/23/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Mario Wong

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant Alex Anthony Quintana

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order compelling Defendant Alex Anthony Quintana to produce an executed authorization required to obtain his subpoenaed cell phone records from Verizon.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests sanctions against Quintana and his counsel of record in the amount of $4,092.00.

 

RULING: The motion is denied without prejudice. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of a motor vehicle collision that occurred on 6/24/22 when Plaintiff Mario Wong (Plaintiff) was rear-ended by a vehicle driven by Defendant Alex Anthony Quintana (Quintana) who was driving a vehicle leased by his employer, Defendant Johnson Controls, Inc. (JCI).

 

On 6/24/24, Plaintiff filed this action asserting causes of action for motor vehicle and general negligence.  On 10/8/24, JCI, Johnson Controls International, Inc. and Quintana answered the complaint.  Trial is scheduled for 8/31/26. 

 

After Quintana gave different versions of the circumstances leading up to the collision, in December 2025, Plaintiff’s counsel requested that Quintana sign an authorization so that Plaintiff  could obtain Quintana’s cell phone records for 30 minutes before and after the collision.  (Booker Decl., Ex.C).  Defense counsel agreed to have Quintana sign the authorization.  Id.  As of 1/12/26, the signed authorization had not been received by Plaintiff’s counsel.  Id.

 

On 1/12/26, Plaintiff’s counsel served a subpoena on Verizon seeking:

 

“1. Any and all documentation including notes, records, logs, data, reports, and electronically stored information, created, maintained, stored, or possessed by Cellco Partnership d/b/a Verizon Wireless, relating to any and all cellular telephone activity by Alex Anthony Quintana on June 24, 2022, for the time period of 9:55 p.m. through 10:55 p.m.

2. Any and all documentation including incoming and outgoing call records, reflecting the date, time, duration, and associated telephone numbers, relating to cellular telephone activity during the above-referenced time period in connection with the following telephone numbers:714-713-6383 (work cellular telephone through Johnson Controls) and 626-476-8326 (personal cellular telephone).

3. Any and all documentation including text message transmission logs, reflecting the date, time, and associated telephone numbers, relating to any text messages sent or received during the above-referenced time period.

4. Any and all documentation including internet usage, email activity, music or media application usage, and other data or application-related activity records, reflecting timestamps of activity or usage during the above-referenced time period.

5. The documents requested herein relate to a motor vehicle collision that occurred on June 24, 2022, at approximately 10:25 p.m., at or near Interstate 210 westbound, approximately 600 feet west of Van Nuys Boulevard, Los Angeles, California.6. The documents requested herein are limited to the 30 minutes immediately prior to and 30 minutes immediately following the subject incident.

 

(Id., Ex.D).

 

Plaintiff contends that no records have been received from Verizon because Quintana has not provided a signed authorization.  (Booker Decl. ¶15).   

 

On 2/23/26, Plaintiff filed and served the instant motion seeking an order compelling Quintana to produce an executed authorization required to obtain his subpoenaed cell phone records from Verizon.  Additionally, Plaintiff requests sanctions against Quintana and his counsel of record in the amount of $4,092.00.  Quintana has not responded to the motion. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

The motion fails for both procedural and substantive reasons.

 

Procedurally, there are issues with the service of the motion.  Although the meet and confer emails were sent to Defendants’ local counsel and Defendants’ counsel admitted pro hac vice, the proof of service for the motion only shows service on Defendants’ pro hac vice counsel.  There is no proof that the motion was served on Defendants’ local counsel.  As noted above, Quintana has not responded to the motion to cure any defect in notice.    

 

Even if the motion is considered to have been properly served, it fails.  First, Plaintiff does not provide any evidence (e.g. a response to the subpoena from Verizon) showing that Verizon refused  to comply with the subpoena and/or why Verizon refused.  Instead, Plaintiff’s counsel merely concludes that “[b]ecause Defendant refuses to provide the requisite signed authorization, records have not been received from Verizon.”  (Booker Decl. ¶15).  Additionally, the motion does not set forth authority for an order compelling Quintana to sign the authorization.  Rather, Plaintiff cites authority regarding a deponent’s obligation to produce documents and a party’s obligation to respond to document requests.  Here, Quintana is not the deponent in the deposition subpoena for business records and there is no evidence that Plaintiff has served a document request on Quintana for the cell phone records.  (See Booker Decl., Ex.D).  Next, Plaintiff argues that since Quintana did not object to the subpoena or file a motion to quash, he is precluded from challenging the subpoena.  While this may be true, Plaintiff has not filed a motion to compel the deponent’s (Verizon) compliance with the deposition subpoena and Quintana has not opposed such a motion.    

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is denied without prejudice.

Case #26CHCV01514

 

PETITION TO COMPEL JOINDER OF MOBILITAS INSURANCE COMPANY OF ARIZONA AND REFUSE ENFORCEMENT OF UM ARBITRATION AGREEMENT

 

Petition filed on 6/1/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Petitioners Arman Hovhannisyan and Karen Avagyan

RESPONDING PARTY: Respondent Mobilitas Insurance Company of Arizona (specially appearing)

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order:

 

(1) Refusing to enforce the UM arbitration agreement between Petitioners and Mobilitas Insurance Company of Arizona (Mobilitas) and ordering the joinder of Mobilitas as a party defendant in this action, pursuant to CCP 1281.2(c) on the ground that a party to the UM arbitration (Petitioner Karen Avagyan) is also a party to this pending court action with Mobilitas — a third party to that action — arising out of the same transaction, with a possibility of conflicting rulings on common issues of law and fact; and

 

(2) Granting such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

 

RULING:

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises from a motor vehicle accident that occurred on 7/2/24.  On that date, Plaintiffs/ Petitioners Arman Hovhannisyan and Karen Avagyan (collectively, Plaintiffs) were rear seat passengers in a Lyft-dispatched vehicle (Lyft Vehicle), driven by Defendant Strahinja Veljkovic, when an unidentified hit-and-run motorist struck the vehicle on or near the Balboa Blvd. off-ramp from the 118 Freeway in Granada Hills.  (Nazaryan Decl. ¶¶2-3).  Aftermarket seat covers installed by the driver, Veljkovic, blocked the seatbelt buckle receivers leaving both Plaintiffs unrestrained.  (Id. ¶3).  As a result of the accident, both Plaintiffs suffered personal injuries.  (Id. ¶¶4-5).  Hovhannisyan had booked the Lyft ride through the Lyft mobile application.  (Id. ¶2). 

 

The Lyft Vehicle was covered under a commercial auto policy issued by Respondent Mobilitas Insurance Company of Arizona (Mobilitas) which provided uninsured motorist/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) bodily coverage with a limit of $1,000,000.  (Nazaryan Decl. ¶6).  On 4/7/25, Plaintiffs served a policy limits demand on Mobilitas.  (Id. ¶7).  On 4/23/25, Mobilitas offered $43,200 in settlement, accepting only $22,151.08 in special damages — with the offer addressed to and limited to Avagyan only.  Mobilitas made no offer, and has made no offer to date, on Hovhannisyan’s claim.  (Id. ¶8). 

On 10/20/25, Plaintiffs served formal UM arbitration demands on Mobilitas pursuant to Insurance Code 11580.2(f) and 11580.2(i).  (Nazaryan Decl. ¶9).  The parties agreed upon Alyse Golden Berkley as the neutral arbitrator.  Id.  On 11/21/25, Mobilitas served written discovery and on 12/26/25, Plaintiffs served responses.  Id.  The arbitration is actively proceeding.  Id.

 

On 4/14/26, Plaintiffs filed this action against Defendants Lyft, Inc. and Veljikovic for negligence, negligence per se, common carrier liability, vicarious liability, and negligent hiring, supervision, and retention arising from the obstructed seatbelts.  Mobilitas is not currently named as a defendant in this action. 

 

On 6/1/26, Plaintiff filed the instant petition seeking an order:

 

(1) Refusing to enforce the UM arbitration agreement between Petitioners and Mobilitas Insurance Company of Arizona (Mobilitas) and ordering the joinder of Mobilitas as a party defendant in this action, pursuant to CCP 1281.2(c) on the ground that a party to the UM arbitration (Petitioner Karen Avagyan) is also a party to this pending court action with Mobilitas — a third party to that action — arising out of the same transaction, with a possibility of conflicting rulings on common issues of law and fact; and

 

(2) Granting such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

 

Mobilitas has opposed the petition and Plaintiffs have filed a reply to the opposition.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Insurance Code 11580.2(f) requires every automobile liability policy to provide for arbitration of UM coverage disputes.  This statutory mandate creates a contractual arbitration subject to the California Arbitration Act (CAA).  It has been held that “[t]his law is implicated because the uninsured motorist coverage law requires an automobile liability insurance policy, which is a contract, to provide for arbitration.”  Mercury Insurance Group (1998) 19 C4th 332, 342; See also Pilimai (2006) 39 C4th 133; Cronus Investments, Inc.  (2005) 35 C4th 376, 393.   

 

CCP 1281.2 provides, in relevant part:

 

“On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party to the agreement refuses to arbitrate that controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists, unless it determines that:

 

. . .

 

(c) A party to the arbitration agreement is also a party to a pending court action or special proceeding with a third party, arising out of the same transaction or series of related transactions and there is a possibility of conflicting rulings on a common issue of law or fact. For purposes of this section, a pending court action or special proceeding includes an action or proceeding initiated by the party refusing to arbitrate after the petition to compel arbitration has been filed, but on or before the date of the hearing on the petition. This subdivision shall not be applicable to an agreement to arbitrate disputes as to the professional negligence of a health care provider made pursuant to Section 1295.

 

. . .

 

If the court determines that a party to the arbitration is also a party to litigation in a pending court action or special proceeding with a third party as set forth under subdivision (c), the court (1) may refuse to enforce the arbitration agreement and may order intervention or joinder of all parties in a single action or special proceeding; (2) may order intervention or joinder as to all or only certain issues; (3) may order arbitration among the parties who have agreed to arbitration and stay the pending court action or special proceeding pending the outcome of the arbitration proceeding; or (4) may stay arbitration pending the outcome of the court action or special proceeding.

 

Here, the requirements set forth in CCP 1281.2(c) are met.  First, Plaintiffs are parties to the instant action against Lyft and Veljkovic as well as parties to the arbitration proceeding with Mobilitas, a third party to this action within the meaning of CCP 1281.2(c).  See Prudential Property & Casualty Insurance Co. (1995) 36 CA4th 275, 279.  Second, this action and the UM arbitration arise from the same 7/2/24 motor vehicle collision.  Third, separate proceedings present a substantial possibility of conflicting rulings on issues of comparative fault, seatbelt-enhanced injury, causation and total damages.  A possibility of conflicting rulings is all that is necessary.  See Acquire II, Ltd. (2013) 213 CA4th 959, 972; Abaya (2010) 189 CA4th 1490, 1498-1499; L.A. Unified School District (2017) 13 CA5th 471, 484; Birl (2009) 172 CA4th 1313, 1319-1321.

 

Plaintiff Avagyan was a guest passenger in the Lyft Vehicle and did not have a Lyft account.  Therefore, Avagyan cannot be compelled to arbitrate her civil claims alleged in this action.  (Nazaryan Decl. ¶4).  Lyft, who answered the complaint in this action on 5/26/26, has not responded to the petition arguing otherwise.      

 

In opposition to the petition, Mobilitas fails to address the application of CCP 1281.2(c) and solely argues that the Examinations Under Oath of Plaintiffs, which have not occurred, are a condition precedent to civil liability.  Therefore, joinder of Mobilitas in this action is premature.  If Plaintiff’s ultimately fail to submit to Examinations Under Oath (EUO), they may be precluded from obtaining UM benefits under the policy.  However, Mobilitas has failed to establish that ordering its joinder in this action pursuant to CCP 1281.2(c) is precluded merely because Plaintiffs have yet to submit to an EUO.  Nor has Mobilitas established that UM proceedings must be stayed until coverage issues are resolved. 

 

However, Plaintiffs may not simply file a First Amended Complaint upon the granting of this motion as indicated in their intent in the motion.  (See Motion Memo of Ps&As, p.9:27-28).  Since Lyft filed an answer to the complaint on 5/26/26, Plaintiffs must file a properly noticed motion for leave to amend.  See CCP 472(a).

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.  The joinder of Mobilitas Insurance Company of Arizona as a defendant in this action is granted.
