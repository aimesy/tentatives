NIAL COLEMAN v. MAURO CRUZ GOMEZ, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV01102 

HEARING: 07/17/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#15

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Counsel Anthony Werbin, Esq.’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff Nial Coleman is GRANTED. 

This Order will take effect upon the filing of proof of service of the signed order upon the client and on all parties that have appeared in the case.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Counsel Anthony Werbin, Esq. (Counsel) moves to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff Nial Coleman (Plaintiff).

 

Background

 

On April 11, 2023, Plaintiff filed this personal injury action against Defendants Mauro Cruz Gomez; Anel Gomez Hernandez; Tyra Roberta Toler; and Does 1 to 50 arising out of injuries Plaintiff sustained in a motor vehicle collision on April 27, 2021, near Alameda Str. & Florence Ave., Los Angeles, CA 90255. The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The attorney in an action or special proceeding may be changed at any time before or after judgment or final determination, as follows: ¶ 1. Upon the consent of both client and attorney, filed with the clerk, or entered upon the minutes; ¶ 2. Upon the order of the court, upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 284.)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires that the following be submitted in support of an attorney’s motion to be relieved as counsel pursuant Code of Civil

Procedure section 284, subd. (2): (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (Form MC-051); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subd. (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subd. (1) (Form MC-052); (3) a proof of service evidencing service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) a proposed order relieving counsel (Form MC-053). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (d), (e).)

 

Discussion

 

Counsel moves to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff.

 

Here, in compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, Counsel submits a notice of motion and motion on Form MC-051 directed to Plaintiff. Counsel also submits a declaration on Form MC-052 demonstrating good cause to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff. Specifically, Counsel states that despite numerous efforts, Counsel has been unable to establish regular communication with Plaintiff resulting in a breakdown of the attorney-client relationship. Counsel provides a proposed order on Form MC-053 and proof of service of each document to the parties in this action. Therefore, the Court finds that Counsel has fully complied with the requirements under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.

 

Accordingly, Counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiff is GRANTED. This Order will take effect upon the filing of proof of service of the signed order upon the client and on all parties that have appeared in the case.

Case Number: 23NWCV02877    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: P

GERALDINE F. SANDOVAL v. PAUL J. VALANIS, et al. 

CASE NO.:  23NWCV02877 

HEARING:  07/17/2026 @ 10:00 AM

 

#18

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Defendant Paul Valanis’s motion is GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN PART.

 

II.            Defendant’s motion is GRANTED as to confirming the value of the Subject Property at $860,000.

 

III.          Defendant’s motion is DENIED as to all other requests.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendant Paul Valanis (Defendant) moves to (1) confirming the fair market value of the subject property, (2) confirm the offsets owed by Plaintiff Geraldine Sandoval (Plaintiff) to Defendant, and (3) set the buyout price. Alternatively, Defendant requests appointment of a referee to determine the offsets.

 

Background

 

This is an action for partition. On September 11, 2023, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendants Paul J. Valanis, MERS, Inc., and Does 1 to 10. The complaint alleges that Plaintiff and Defendant are former romantic partners. On May 16, 2005, the parties acquired their interests in the real property located at 9658 Belcher Street, Downey, California, 90242, zoned DOR105, a single-family residential zoning designation, with APN 6282-005-041 (Subject Property) as joint tenants through a Grant Deed recorded with Los Angeles County. (Complaint, ¶ 11.) Plaintiff and Defendant each hold a 50% interest in the Subject Property. (Complaint, ¶¶ 3-4.) The complaint alleges that the parties’ relationship has since deteriorated, and the parties have been unable to reach an agreement to divide the Subject Property. (Complaint, ¶ 19.) The complaint alleges one cause of action for partition by sale.

 

On September 20, 2023, a Notice of Lis Pendens was recorded on the Subject Property.

 

On July 23, 2024, Defendant recorded a quitclaim deed severing the joint tenancy.

 

On April 21, 2025, the Court denied Plaintiff’s motion for interlocutory judgment and appointment of partition referee. The Court found that an interlocutory judgment was premature and better suited to be decided by trial or a dispositive motion. (Order, 4/21/25.)

 

On September 4, 2025, the Court granted in part Plaintiff’s motion to determine value and send buyout notice. The Court ordered an appraisal of the Subject Property and denied Plaintiff’s request to order the property to be sold. (Order, 9/4/25.)

 

On November 4, 2025, the Court denied Defendant’s motion to confirm the offsets or, alternatively, to appoint a referee to determine the offsets without prejudice.

 

On April 3, 2026, the parties filed a Joint Stipulation to Appoint Appraiser. The Court granted the Joint Stipulation and appointed Michael Stark. On April 23, 2026, Defendant submitted a declaration of Mr. Stark verifying the appraisal of the Subject Property.

 

On May 18, 2026, the instant hearing was scheduled for today’s date.

 

Legal Standard

 

Under the Partition of Real Property Act (PRPA), Code of Civil Procedure section 874.316 provides in pertinent part:

 

“(a) Except as otherwise provided in subdivisions (b) and (c), the court shall determine the fair market value of the property by ordering an appraisal pursuant to subdivision (d).

[…]

(c) If the court determines that the evidentiary value of an appraisal is outweighed by the cost of the appraisal, the court, after an evidentiary hearing, shall determine the fair market value of the property and send notice to the parties of the value.

(d) If the court orders an appraisal, the court shall appoint a disinterested real estate appraiser licensed in the State of California to determine the fair market value of the property assuming sole ownership of the fee simple estate. On completion of the appraisal, the appraiser shall file a sworn or verified appraisal with the court.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 874.316, subds. (a), (c), (d).)

 

“If an appraisal is conducted pursuant to subdivision (d), not later than 10 days after the appraisal is filed, the court shall send notice to each party with a known address, stating all of the following: (1) The appraised fair market value of the property. (2) That the appraisal is available at the court clerk’s office. (3) That a party may file with the court an objection to the appraisal not later than 30 days after the notice is sent, stating the grounds for the objection.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.316, subd. (e).) “If an appraisal is filed with the court pursuant to subdivision (d), the court shall conduct a hearing to determine the fair market value of the property not sooner than 30 days after a copy of the notice of the appraisal is sent to each party under subdivision (e), whether or not an objection to the appraisal is filed under paragraph (3) of subdivision (e).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.316, subd. (f).) “After a hearing under subdivision (f), but before considering the merits of the partition action, the court shall determine the fair market value of the property and send notice to the parties of the value.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.316, subd (g).)

 

Discussion

 

Defendant moves to (1) confirm the fair market value of the Subject Property, (2) confirm the offsets owed by Plaintiff to Defendant, and (3) set the buyout price. Defendant “requests that the Court confirm the Property’s fair market value of $860,000, confirm offsets totaling $440,118.85, and set the buyout price for Plaintiff’s 50% interest at 50% of the Property’s equity (fair market value less mortgage) less offsets. Because Plaintiff’s 50% share of equity is approximately $247,859 and offsets are $440,118.85, the buyout price floors at $0, with Defendant waiving any excess offsets above Plaintiff’s 50% share of equity in exchange for the conveyance of Plaintiff’s 50% interest.” (Mot., 2:15-20.) Alternatively, Defendant requests appointment of a referee to determine the offsets.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff opposes Defendant’s arguments that the value is determined by taking equity into consideration. Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s arguments depart from the PRPA. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 874.317, subdivision (c), “[t]he purchase price for each of the interests of a cotenant that requested partition by sale is the value of the entire parcel determined under Section 874.316 multiplied by the cotenant’s fractional ownership of the entire parcel.”

 

Here, the Court agrees with Plaintiff that Defendant’s requests to confirm the offsets and confirm a value based on equity are both premature and not prescribed under the statutory regime. As explained by Plaintiff, the PRPA established a four-step procedure for a buyout. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 874.317.) These steps dictate that the allocations that Defendant seeks occur after an interlocutory judgment and after buyout notices are sent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.317, subd. (e)(1).)

 

Based on the Notice of Filing of Verified Appraisal filed on April 23, 2026, the Court conducts this hearing. (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.316, subd. (e)-(f).) Therefore, Defendant’s requests for accountings and offsets are premature at this time.

 

Here, the Notice of Filing of Verified Appraisal shows that the value of the Subject Property is $860,000. Neither party disputes this value.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s motion to confirm the value of the Subject Property is GRANTED IN PART. The Court sets the value of 9658 Belcher Street, Downey, California, 90242 at $860,000.

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 874.316, subdivision (g), the Court is required to give notice of the value to all parties. The Court will and hereby does give notice of the value of the Subject Property to all parties.

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 874.317, subdivision (b), “[n]ot later than 45 days after the notice is sent under subdivision (a), any cotenant except a cotenant that requested partition by sale may give notice to the court that it elects to buy all the interests of the cotenants that requested partition by sale.” The purchase price of each interest is $430,000. (Code Civ. Proc., § 874.317, subd. (c).)

 

As discussed above, Defendant’s motion is DENIED as to the confirmation of offsets and setting the buyout price at $0.

 

The Court acknowledges Defendant’s alternative request for a referee regarding the offsets and accounting. The Court finds Plaintiff’s opposing arguments, regarding moving under the improper statute, well taken. The Court notes that the parties are not precluded from moving for the appointment of a referee to conduct the sale or buyout after entry of an interlocutory judgment.

 

The Court will confer with the parties at the hearing to schedule a date for a court trial to determine the parties’ respective interests and appoint a referee to conduct the sale, buyout, and accounting.

 

 


Case Number: 24NWCV00312    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: P

CHRISTINA BERMUDEZ v. ROSE MEAT SERVICES, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.: 24NWCV00312 

HEARING: 07/17/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#16

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Christina Bermudez’s motion for leave to amend is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Christina Bermudez (Plaintiff) moves for leave to file a first amended complaint (FAC).

 

Background

 

On January 30, 2024, Plaintiff Christina Bermuedez filed this sexual harassment action against Defendants Rose Meat Services, Inc. (Rose Meat), Employee Force Provider, Inc. (EFP), and Does 1 through 10 (collectively, Defendants). The complaint alleges the following: Defendants employed Plaintiff from September of 2021 through May of 2023 and throughout the course of the employment, “Defendants’ managing agents and/or supervisors, including Fernando and Fidel, sexually harassed Plaintiff by initiating unwanted sexual advances and visual and verbal conduct of a sexual nature.” (Complaint, ¶¶ 16-17.) Part of the workplace culture included Fernando and Fidel leering at female employee’s buttocks and making comments. (Complaint, ¶ 18.) Fernando and Fedel sexually harassed Plaintiff through “(a) remarks such as ‘I would like to have your legs over my shoulders,’ (b) inappropriate comments about the size of Ms. Bermudez’s buttocks, (c) unwanted persistent sexual advances from Fernando, and (d) requests for sex from Fernando, such as ‘say you want to go home early, let’s get a room.’” (Complaint, ¶ 19.) The complaint alleges that when Plaintiff reported Fernando’s conduct as to her and other female employees to Defendants’ management in late December of 2022, Defendants fired Plaintiff and other female employees. (Complaint, ¶ 21.)

 

The complaint asserts eight causes of action: (1) Sex and/or Gender Discrimination (Gov. Code, §§ 12940, et seq.); (2) Sexual Harassment (Gov. Code, §§ 12940, et seq.); (3) Retaliation for Opposing Sex and/or Gender Discrimination and Harassment (Gov. Code, §§ 12940, et seq.); (4) Failure to Prevent Discrimination, Harassment, and Retaliation (Gov. Code, §§ 12940, et seq.); (5) Gross Negligence; (6) Negligence Per Se; (7) Negligent Hiring, Supervision, and Retention; and (8) Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public Policy.

 

On May 5, 2026, the Court denied Rose Meat’s motion for summary judgment. The Court granted in part and denied in part Rose Meat’s motion for summary adjudication. The Court granted Rose Meat’s motion for summary adjudication as to the first and sixth causes of action and claim for punitive damages.

 

On May 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for leave to amend her complaint.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The court may…in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473 subd. (a).)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a) provides: “A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:

 

(1) Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;

(2) State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and

(3) State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.”

 

California Rules of Court rule 3.1324(b) requires a separate declaration accompany the motion. The separate declaration must include: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

 

“If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend and where the refusal also results in a party being deprived of the right to assert a meritorious cause of action or a meritorious defense, it is not only error but an abuse of discretion.” (Morgan v. Superior Court of California (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for leave to file a FAC. Plaintiff moves on the grounds that Plaintiff’s prior counsel failed to include a standalone cause of action for pregnancy discrimination despite exploring Plaintiff’s pregnancy during discovery.

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court rule 3.1324(a), Plaintiff submits a redlined copy of the proposed FAC. (Ranen Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.) Plaintiff argues that the proposed FAC seeks to add allegations regarding Plaintiff’s pregnancy, Defendants’ knowledge of the pregnancy, pregnancy-related accommodations requests, and Defendants’ conduct after learning Plaintiff was pregnant. Plaintiff also shows that the proposed FAC seeks to add a cause of action for pregnancy discrimination under FEHA. (Ranen Decl., ¶ 4.)

 

As to California Rules of Court rule 3.1324(b), Plaintiff’s counsel states that “the omission of a separate cause of action for pregnancy discrimination was not the result of any strategic decision, but instead was an inadvertent pleading omission by prior counsel;” however, Plaintiff’s counsel explains that “[u]pon recognizing that the Complaint did not expressly include a standalone pregnancy discrimination claim, Plaintiff promptly took steps to seek leave to amend.  (Ranen Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.)

 

Plaintiff argues that Defendants cannot establish prejudice because they already conducted discovery regarding Plaintiff’s pregnancy. Plaintiff argues that despite the omission of the cause of action, Defendants conducted depositions and engaged in written discovery in which they inquired about Plaintiff’s pregnancy and pregnancy disclosure. Plaintiff points to certain discovery, including special interrogatories, nos. 47, 49, 50, and 52, in “which Plaintiff disclosed detailed allegations concerning her pregnancy disclosure, pregnancy-related adverse employment actions, negative comments regarding her pregnancy, and Defendants’ alleged failure to address pregnancy-related work-duty concerns.” (Ranen Decl., ¶ 9, Exh. E.)

 

In opposition, Rose Meat argues that Plaintiff unreasonably delayed in bringing this motion, Rose Meat would be severely prejudiced if Plaintiff is allowed to amend the complaint, and that amendment would be futile.

 

Rose Meat emphasizes that Plaintiff is seeking this amendment three weeks before trial after being aware of this claim and expressly disclaiming a pregnancy discrimination claim throughout this litigation. Rose Meat argues that Plaintiff is improperly bringing this motion after the Court’s ruling on Rose Meat’s motion for summary adjudication.

 

“Although leave to amend should be liberally granted, the trial court has discretion to deny it when a party unreasonably delays making the request.” (Champlin/GEI Wind Holdings, LLC v. Avery (2023) 92 Cal.App.5th 218, 224.) “Unreasonable delay may be found where a plaintiff…seeks leave to amend only after the defendant…moves for summary judgment on grounds addressed by the proposed amendment and the proposed amendment is based on facts previously known to the plaintiff. [Citation.] ‘It would be patently unfair to allow plaintiffs to defeat [the] summary judgment motion by allowing them to present a “moving target” unbounded by the pleadings.’” (Ibid., citations omitted.)

 

Rose Meat cites to a January 16, 2024 pre-litigation demand letter showing that Plaintiff was aware of these claims prior to commencing this action. (Vásquez Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.) Rose Meat additionally points out that Plaintiff substituted in her current counsel on January 16, 2026, months before her opposition to Rose Meat’s motion for summary adjudication was due. Rose Meat refers to Fisher v. Larsen (1982) 138 Cal.App.3d 627, 649, in which a Court of Appeal found that a trial court was within its discretion in denying leave to amend after a plaintiff knew about a pending summary judgment motion but did not seek leave for to amend for five months. Rose Meat argues, and the Court agrees, that Plaintiff’s delay is worse than that in Fisher as Plaintiff waited until after the Court ruled on the motion for summary judgment to move for leave.

 

In reply, Plaintiff argues that the delay is not unreasonable because it was based on an inadvertent error by prior counsel and any delay should only be measured based on current counsel. As shown by Defendant, Plaintiff’s current counsel was substituted in two months before Plaintiff’s opposition was due and three months before the hearing on the motion for summary judgment. Plaintiff’s counsel states she discovered prior counsel’s omission of the claim “while preparing Plaintiff’s opposition.” (Reply, 5:26.) Plaintiff does not explain why Plaintiff’s counsel did not seek a continuance or leave to amend prior to the hearing on the motion for summary judgment so as to get a ruling based on all Plaintiff’s purported claims.

 

Rose Meat further argues that it will be “extremely prejudiced” if amendment is permitted because trial is three weeks away, the Court has ruled on the motion for summary adjudication, experts have been designated, discovery has closed, and the parties have filed their motions in limine. (Opp., 7:11-14.) Rose Meat argues that because Plaintiff never pled pregnancy discrimination, Rose Meat was deprived of the opportunity to move for summary adjudication as to the claim.

 

Moreover, Rose Meat provides evidence that it relied on Plaintiff’s representations that she is not alleging a pregnancy cause of action. Rose Meat refutes that it has obtained pregnancy-related discovery and argues that any discovery was related to the timing of the pregnancy. Rose Meat points to its requests for production and interrogatories inquiring into Plaintiff’s pregnancy and Plaintiff’s objections in response. (Vásquez Decl., Exhs. B, C, D.) Rose Meat additionally points to Plaintiff’s meet and confer correspondence on July 16, 2024, regarding Plaintiff’s objections, explaining “[t]hese interrogatories expressly apply to disability discrimination claims only. Plaintiff has not alleged a disability discrimination claim here. To be clear, Plaintiff’s pregnancy discrimination claim derives from her sex/gender discrimination claim.” (Vásquez Decl., Exh. E.)

 

Third, Rose Meat argues that the amendment would be futile as pregnancy was addressed in the Court’s ruling on motion for summary adjudication finding that Plaintiff’s “termination was based on legitimate, nondiscriminatory factors.” (Order, 5/5/26.)

 

Here, the Court finds that Plaintiff unreasonably delayed in proposing this amendment without justification and that the delay is unduly prejudicial to Rose Meat. First, while Plaintiff claims that her prior counsel inadvertently failed to allege a pregnancy claim, Rose Meat’s showing of the pre-litigation demand letter and meet and confer correspondence show that Plaintiff, under prior counsel, expressly disclaimed a stand-alone pregnancy claim. Even if the Court measures delay based on the substitution of counsel, Plaintiff’s current counsel substituted into this action over three months before the hearing on the motion for summary adjudication and discovered this omission while writing an opposition. Seeking leave to amend at that time could have permitted Rose Meat to re-file a motion for summary judgment based on all the claims before the current trial date. However, allowing the summary judgment hearing to proceed and moving to amend afterwards turns Plaintiff’s claims into a “moving target.” (Champlin/GEI Wind Holdings, LLC, supra, 92 Cal.App.5th at p. 224.)

 

While Plaintiff’s arguments regarding pregnancy being addressed in discovery could mitigate a finding of prejudice, Rose Meat’s evidence refutes that it was able to get meaningful discovery from Plaintiff on the issue. Rose Meat shows that Plaintiff either objected to pregnancy-related discovery, included the same statement regarding pregnancy that was in the pre-litigation demand letter, or expressly disclaimed that Plaintiff had a pregnancy-related cause of action. The evidence here shows that Plaintiff’s pregnancy claim was raised in a pre-litigation demand letter, disclaimed throughout the actual litigation, and only raised for the first time in the weeks before the scheduled trial date. The Court finds that Rose Meat was entitled to rely on Plaintiff’s comments disclaiming a pregnancy claim during the litigation of this action, including in bringing a dispositive motion. Permitting the amendment now would thus unfairly prejudice Rose Meat by requiring additional discovery and a trial continuance after an unreasonable delay in which it was led to believe that Plaintiff would not bring the claim.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to amend is DENIED.

KEVIN DEON WOODS v. RANISHA D. GRAY, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 25NWCV00241

#17

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Plaintiff Kevin Deon Woods’s motion for leave to file a first amended complaint is GRANTED.

 

II.            Plaintiff is ORDERED to file and serve the proposed first amended complaint attached to the moving papers within 5 days of this Order.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Kevin Deon Woods (Plaintiff) moves for leave to file a first amended complaint (FAC).

 

Background

 

On January 21, 2025, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendants and Cross-Complainants Ranisha D. Gray (Ranisha), Shirley A. Allen (Allen), Demetrius Gray (Demetrius),[1] and Does 1 to 50 (collectively, Defendants). The complaint alleges the following: In 2017 or 2018, Plaintiff and Artricia Woods (Artricia or Decedent) created a business operating as a dba known as “Affinity Real Estate and Mortgage Services” (Affinity) and on December 6, 2018, they created Affinity Real Estate & Mortgage Services, LLC (Affinity LLC). (Complaint, ¶¶ 12-13.) On February 29, 2019, Affinity Real Estate and Mortgage Services, Inc. (Affinity Inc.) was created. (Complaint, ¶ 19.) Plaintiff and Artricia were the sole members of Affinity LLC and equal owners of Affinity Inc. from the time of their creation until the time of Artricia’s death and each held their ownership in both as community property during Artricia’s lifetime. (Complaint, ¶¶ 15, 19.) Upon Atricia’s death on October 23, 2020, Plaintiff became the sole owner of Affinity LLC and Affinity Inc. (Complaint, ¶¶ 15, 21.) On March 3, 2022, the Los Angeles Superior Court issued an order confirming that Plaintiff owns 100% of a PayPal Account which was opened by Atricia and in which income and receipts generated by the Affinity LLC were collected through, deposited, and thereafter withdrawn to accounts for the Affinity LLC and/or Affinity, Inc. (Complaint, ¶¶ 24-27.)

 

The complaint alleges that on August 6, 2024, when attempting to access the PayPal Account, the password was changed and Plaintiff believes that Affinity LLC employee Ranisha, with assistance by Defendants Allen and Demetrius, transferred most of the balance of the PayPal Account into an account owned and/or controlled by themselves. (Complaint, ¶¶ 30-34.) Further, the complaint alleges that Defendants transferred the Affinity LLC payroll to a service that Plaintiff cannot access. (Complaint, ¶ 34.) The complaint asserts six causes of action: (1) Conversion, (2) Violation of the Uniform Trade Secrets Act, (3) Violation of Penal Code § 496, (4) Tortious Interference with Economic Advantage, (5) Violation of Business & Professions Code § 17200, and (6) Declaratory Relief.

 

On April 9, 2025, Cross-Complainants filed a cross-complaint (XC). On the same date, Cross-Complainants filed a Notice of Lis Pendens as to the real property known as 9835 Crestbrook Avenue, Bellflower, California 90706 with the Tax Identification Number APN: 6281-020-029 (Bellflower Property).

 

On June 11, 2025, Cross-Complainants filed a first amended cross-complaint (FAXC). The FAXC alleges the following: Artricia was the sole owner and proprietor of Affinity and Cross-Complainants allege that Affinity constitutes Artricia's separate property and was never transmuted into community property. (FAXC, ¶ 11.) Plaintiff and Artricia acquired the Subject Property after their marriage but Artricia removed Plaintiff from the grant deed and re-financed the property herself due to Plaintiff’s credit history. (FAXC, ¶ 12.) Between 2018 and 2020, Artricia explicitly expressed her testamentary intent to multiple people for Affinity and the Subject Property to pass solely to Ranisha and that this intent is contained in a holographic will in the bottom drawer of her desk. (FAXC, ¶¶ 14-16.) The FAXC alleges Plaintiff falsely claimed that Artricia died intestate in his Spousal Property Petition in Los Angeles Superior Court (Case No. 20STPB10011). (FAXC, ¶ 23.) On September 20, 2021, Woods filed a second fraudulent Spousal Property Petition (Case No. 21STPB09167), asserting intestacy and claiming Affinity as community property. (FAXC, ¶ 26.) Cross-Complainants allege that Plaintiff misappropriated Artricia’s funds. (FAXC, ¶¶ 29-31.)

 

The FAXC asserts eleven causes of action: (1) Fraud and Deceit (Intentional Misrepresentation and Concealment), (2) Breach of Fiduciary Duty, (3) Tortious Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage, (4) Conversion, (5) Quiet Title (Real Property and Business), (6) Quasi-Contract / Restitution Based on Unjust Enrichment (pled in the alternative), (7) Constructive Trust, (8) Accounting, (9) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress, (10) Civil Conspiracy, and (11) Action to Set Aside Fraudulent Judgments/Orders.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The court may…in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473 subd. (a).)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a) provides: “A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:

 

(1) Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;

(2) State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and

(3) State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.”

 

California Rules of Court rule 3.1324(b) requires a separate declaration accompany the motion. The separate declaration must include: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

 

“If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend and where the refusal also results in a party being deprived of the right to assert a meritorious cause of action or a meritorious defense, it is not only error but an abuse of discretion.” (Morgan v. Superior Court of California (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for leave to file a FAC against Defendants. This motion is unopposed.

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a), Plaintiff submits the declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel. Plaintiff also submits a clean and red-lined copy of the proposed FAC. (McManus Decl., ¶¶ 3-4, Exhs. A, B.)

 

Plaintiff argues the proposed FAC adds allegations regarding defendants FAXC and Defendants’ filing of Notices of Lis Pendens as to two of Plaintiff’s properties. The proposed FAC additionally adds a sixth cause of action for slander of title and a seventh cause of action for quiet title.

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b), Plaintiff’s counsel states that these amendments became necessary after Defendants filed a FAXC alleging claims regarding the Bellflower Property while also filing a Notice of Lis Pendens regarding a property located at 40116 Highland Avenue, Big Bear Lake, California 92315 (Big Bear Property). (McManus Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) Plaintiff’s counsel states that after it became apparent that Defendants asserted claims concerning the Bellflower Property but not the Big Bear Property, yet filed a Notice of Lis Pendens as to the Big Bear Property, Plaintiff sought to add the slander of title claim regarding the Big Bear Property. (McManus Decl., ¶¶ 8, 10-12.) Plaintiff’s counsel states the quiet title claim is necessary because Defendants’ pleadings challenge Plaintiff’s ownership rights regarding the Bellflower Property. (McManus Decl., ¶ 13.)

 

Plaintiff’s counsel further states “[t]he proposed amendment is necessary and proper to protect Plaintiff’s ownership interests in those properties and to permit adjudication of the claims that became ripe after Defendants filed the lis pendens.” (McManus Decl., ¶ 16.)

 

Here, the Court finds Plaintiff complies with the procedural requirements for an order granting leave. Plaintiff further argues that any prejudice to Defendants is minimal as these claims arise out of the same facts, witnesses, documents, and properties that are already at issue. Moreover, Plaintiff argues that no depositions have taken place yet and this amendment will not impact the trial date. The Court agrees that any prejudice to Defendants is minimal and additionally notes that Defendants do not oppose this motion to raise any arguments regarding prejudice.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file a first amended complaint is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ORDERED to file and serve the proposed first amended complaint attached to the moving papers within 5 days of this Order.

TENTATIVE ORDER

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiffs Sinthia G. Perez Garcia, Erlita R. Melchor Carrillo and Milagro Minaya Vasquez (Plaintiffs) move for leave to file a second amended complaint (SAC).

 

Background

 

On September 25, 2025, Plaintiffs filed this personal injury action against Defendant Jeffrey Cervantes and Does 1 through 20. On October 7, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC) against Defendant Jeffrey Cervantes, City of Huntington Park (City), and Does 1 through 20. This action arises out of a motor vehicle collision on March 16, 2024, when a vehicle driven by Cervantes, while in the scope of his employment with City, collided into Plaintiffs’ vehicle in the intersection of Gage Avenue and Regent Street. (FAC, ¶¶ 6-7, 26.) The FAC asserts three causes of action: (1) motor vehicle negligence, (2) negligence per se, and (3) Government Code sections 811.2 and 815.2.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The court may…in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473 subd. (a).)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a) provides: “A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:

 

(1) Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;

(2) State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and

(3) State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.”

 

California Rules of Court rule 3.1324(b) requires a separate declaration accompany the motion. The separate declaration must include: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

 

“If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend and where the refusal also results in a party being deprived of the right to assert a meritorious cause of action or a meritorious defense, it is not only error but an abuse of discretion.” (Morgan v. Superior Court of California (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiffs move for leave to file a SAC. This motion is unopposed.

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a), Plaintiffs submit Plaintiffs’ counsel’s declaration. Plaintiff submits a copy of the proposed SAC showing that Plaintiffs seek to remove the first cause of action for motor vehicle negligence and amend the third cause of action to allege liability under Government Code section 815.2 only. (Echavarria Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.)

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b), Plaintiffs’ counsel states Plaintiffs seek to amend the FAC to clarify allegations and incorporate facts discovered during discovery. (Echavarria Decl., ¶ 6.) Plaintiffs’ counsel explains that they make these amendments after meeting and conferring with defense counsel. (Echavarria Decl., ¶¶ 3-5.) Plaintiffs’ counsel puts forth meet and confer correspondence suggesting Defendant intended to stipulate to permitting Plaintiffs to file the SAC. (Echavarria Decl., ¶¶ 3-5.) Together with Defendants’ Notice of Non-Opposition, the Court finds that Defendants are agreeable to granting leave to file the proposed SAC and will not be prejudiced by its filing. The Court therefore finds that leave to file the proposed SAC is warranted.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiffs’ motion for leave to file a second amended complaint is GRANTED. Plaintiffs are ORDERED to file and serve the proposed second amended complaint within 5 days of this Order.
