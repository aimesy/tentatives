DEPARTMENT L LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23NWCV01111    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: L

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC’s Demurrer is taken OFF-CALENDAR.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an unlawful detainer case (“UD Action”) regarding real property located at 10700-10712 Sessler Street, South Gate, CA 90280 (“Subject Property”). On April 12, 2023, Plaintiff Medline Management Corporation (“Medline”) filed this action against Defendants Dan Kloss and Ioana Kloss, doing business as CNC Aero Machining.

 

On April 9, 2024, this case was consolidated with a related case titled Dan Kloss vs Jamal Dawood, et al., LASC Case No. 23NWCV02306 (“Title Action”). (4/9/24 Minute Order.). The Title Action was filed on April 12, 2023. A First Amended Complaint (FAC) was filed in that action on April 18, 2024. The FAC alleges that, in November 2021, Defendants Jamal Dawood, Christine Armani-Dawood, Quantum Properties LLC, and Does 1 to 100 fraudulently induced Kloss to grant his one-half interest in the Subject Property via a Grant Deed to Quantum Properties LLC (“Grant Deed”) in order to “safeguard” his interest. (FAC, ¶ 16.) In return, Defendants issued an LLC membership certification transferring a one-half interest in Quantum Properties LLC to Precision Forging Dies, Inc. However, the membership interest was intended to be transferred to Kloss, not Precision Forging Dies, Inc. Through the action, Kloss seeks “to cancel and rescind the fraudulently obtained Grant Deed or alternative, to involuntarily dissolve the LLC and have Plaintiff’s one-half interest in the Property transferred back to Plaintiff.” (FAC, ¶ 28.)

 

The Title Action FAC alleges the following causes of action:

 

1.    Cancellation Of Written Instrument (Civ. Code § 3412);

2.    Fraud;

3.    Negligent Misrepresentation;

4.    Breach of Oral Contract;

5.    Financial Elder Abuse;

6.    Slander of Title;

7.    Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress;

8.    Unfair Business Practices;

9.    Declaratory Relief; and

10.        Involuntary Dissolution.

 

Defendants Commercial Funding, LLC, Colony Capital Management LLC, Colony Capital LLC, and Shore Pine LLC were later named as Does 1 to 4, respectively.

 

On May 23, 2024, Defendant/Cross-Complainant Quantum Properties LLC (“Quantum”) filed a Cross-Complaint in the Title Action against Cross-Defendants Dan Kloss and Ioana Kloss, doing business as CNC Aero Machining, Anca Tiplea, and Roes 1 to 20.

 

On February 25, 2025, Quantum filed the First Amended Cross-Complaint (FACC), adding C&C Aerol Machining and Aerospace Machining Company, Precision Forging Dies, Inc. as Cross-Defendants. On May 29, 2025, Quantum filed the Second Amended Cross-Complaint.

 

On August 1, 2025, Defendant Commercial Funding, LLC filed a Cross-Complaint in the Title Action.

 

Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC (hereinafter, “CCM”) now demurs to the Title Action FAC. Defendants Colony Capital LLC (hereinafter, “CCL”) and Shore Pine LLC (hereinafter, “Shore Pine”) have filed a “Notice of Joinder in the Demurrer.”

 

The Court notes that, on August 3, 2026, CCM and CCL were dismissed from the FAC without prejudice.

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

The Court rules on CCM’s Requests for Judicial Notice as follows:

 

-      Omnibus Declaration of Dan Kloss in Support of Debtor’ Emergency Motion filed in the United States Bankruptcy Court on April 7, 2023 in the matter of In re Precision Forging Dies, Inc. (Case No. 2:23-bk-12015) (Exhibit 1) is denied.

-      Notice of Default recorded on 7/29/21 in the Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office, and Notice of Trustee Sale recorded on 11/3/21 at the Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office (Exhibit 2) is granted.

-      Grant Deed executed by Joanna Kloss and recorded on 11/29/21 in Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office (Exhibit 3) is granted.

-      Grant Deed executed by Dan Kloss and recoded on 12/29/21 in Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office (Exhibit 4) is granted.

-      Articles of Organization for Colony Capital Management, LLC (Exhibit 5) is denied.

 

The Court only takes notice of the existence of the documents and their filing/recording dates for purposes of notice, not for the truth of the matters asserted therein.  

 

Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that the parties meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or by video conference.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

On May 7, 2026, Counsel for CCM, CCL, and Shore Pine (and all Defendants) met and conferred via telephone with Kloss’ counsel but the parties were unable to come to a resolution. (Bryner Decl., ¶ 2.)

 

The Court finds that the meet and confer requirement has been met.

 

Discussion

 

CCM’s Demurrer primarily argues that CCM was not involved in the transactions between Dan Kloss, Quantum, and the Dawoods. Specifically, CCM was organized as a Wyoming LLC on July 13, 2023, approximately 1.5 years after the November 2021 subject transaction in which Kloss signed the Grant Deed. (See RJN, Exh. 5.) CCM argues that the FAC’s theories of liability based on agency, membership, alter ego, aiding/abetting allegations are inapplicable where CCM was formed after the subject transaction. Because all causes of action depend on upon the subject transaction, the entire FAC fails to state a claim against CCM.

 

CCM further argues that the ninth cause of action for declaratory relief, which requests a judicial determination regarding the membership certificates of Quantum Properties, and the parties’ rights and duties therein. Precision Forging Dies, LLC is one alleged member of Quantum. While the FAC seeks a determination of Quantum’s members, the FAC fails add Precision Forging Dies, LLC as a Defendant to this action. Precision Forging Dies, LLC should be added as in indispensable party to the FAC.

 

As previously noted, CCL and Shore Pine filed a Notice of Joinder to CCM’s Demurrer. On August 3, 2026, CCM and CCL were dismissed from the FAC without prejudice.

 

The issue is thus whether the Demurrer survives based on Shore Pine’s Notice of Joinder.

 

The Court acknowledges that, while there is no specific Code of Civil Procedure provision governing joinder for motions in general, a party can generally join another party’s motion by filing a Notice of Joinder. However, to be effective, the joinder must 1) be timely filed and served, 2) establish the necessary factual foundation to support the motion, and 3) request affirmative relief on behalf of the joining party. (See, e.g., Barak v. The Quisenberry Law Firm (2006) 135 Cal.App.4th 654, 661 [finding trial court did not abuse its discretion to entertain a joinder to an anti-SLAPP motion to strike, where the joinder requested affirmative relief and stated how the underlying motion would also be applicable to the joining party].)

 

Here, Shore Pines’ Notice of Joinder only states the following: “PLEASE TAKE NOTICE that Defendants Shore Pine, LLC and Colony Capital, LLC hereby join in Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC’s Demurrer to the Complaint and all of the causes of action therein, to wit: [all causes of action].” (See Notice of Joinder.) Thus, the Notice of Joinder fails to request any affirmative relief as to Shore Pines specifically. Further, it is not apparent from either the Demurrer or the Notice of Joinder how the Demurrer applies to Shores Pines. As noted by Plaintiff, the Demurrer’s “central premise was unique to [CCM]: it allegedly did not exist when the 2021 conveyance occurred and supposedly had no interest or stake in [Quantum]. Neither premise disposes of the claims against Shore Pine.” (Opp., 2:4-6.) Further, the bare-bones Notice of Joinder “supplies no Shore Pine-specific facts, formation record, operating agreement, membership record, or argument.” (Opp., 4:5-6.) The Court agrees.

 

Based on this procedural issue, the Court does not reach the merits of the Demurrer to the extent that the opposition and reply anticipate arguments that would have been made by Shore Pines in a proper joint Demurrer.

 

Accordingly, Defendant Colony Capital Management, LLC’s Demurrer is taken off-calendar.

Case Number: 24NWCV00192    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: L

#9 

TENTATIVE ORDER 

 

Plaintiff Gregory Gorman’s Motion to Compel Deposition of First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable and Request for Production of Documents is GRANTED. 

 

Plaintiff to give notice. 

 

Background 

 

This is a negligence action. On January 18, 2024, Plaintiff Gregory Gorman (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants First Transit, Inc. (“First Transit”), Jane Doe 1, and Does 1 to 10. 

 

On April 28, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC), naming Loreen Joseph as Jane Doe 1 and naming Transdev Group, S.A., Transdev North America, Inc., and Transdev Services, Inc. as Does 2, 3, and 4. 

 

On January 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Second Amended Complaint. 

 

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Third Amended Complaint (TAC). The TAC alleges that Plaintiff boarded a bus owned and operated by Defendant First Transit. The bus driver, Loreen Joseph, allegedly accelerated the bus before Plaintiff could sit down, causing Plaintiff to fall to the floor. The TAC alleges the following causes of action: 1) Negligence, 2) Negligence Training and Supervision, and 3) Common Carrier Liability. 

 

In November 2025, Plaintiff served its Notice of Deposition of Defendant First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable (“PMK”) and Request for Production of Documents, setting the deposition for January 27, 2026. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 27, Exh. 19; Smith Decl., ¶ 1, Exh. A.) On January 13, 2026, Defendants First Transit, Inc., Loreen Joseph, Transdev Services, Inc., and Transdev North America, Inc. served their objections. (Smith Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. B.) To date, First Transit has failed to produce its PMK for deposition. (Ibid.) 

 

Plaintiff now moves to compel the deposition of First Transit’s PMK with request for production of documents. Defendants First Transit, Inc., Loreen Joseph, Transdev Services, Inc., and Transdev North America, Inc. (hereinafter, “Defendants”) oppose.  

 

Evidentiary Objections 

 

Plaintiff’s evidentiary objections are overruled to the Declaration of Paul Smith in support of the Opposition are overruled as to Nos. 1-3. 

 

Defendant’s evidentiary objections to the Supplemental Declaration of Herbert N. Wiggins in support of the Reply to the extent it offers new evidence not offered in the original Motion.  

 
Legal Standard 

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a), a party may move to compel a deponent’s attendance and production for inspection of documents if after serving the deposition notice the responding party fails to serve valid objections and fails to appear for the deposition or produce any document described in the deposition notice. A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a) must (1) provide specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document and (2) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., §¿2025.450, subd. (b).) 

 

Meet and Confer 

 

“[W]hen the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, [the 

motion shall be accompanied] by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450 subd. (b)(2).) 

 

On January 13, 2026, Defendants served a meet and confer letter regarding their objections to Plaintiff’s Notice of Deposition. (Smith Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. C.) On March 3, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel attempted to meet and confer with Defense counsel, in which Plaintiff offered to pare down the categories or examination and requests for documents. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 28.) Between March 10, 2026 and March 18, 2026, the parties met and conferred further—however, it became apparent that Defendant First Transit would not produce their PMK for deposition. (Ibid.) On May 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion.  

 

The Court finds that the meet and confer requirement has been met.  

 

Discussion  

 

Plaintiff argues that there is good cause to compel the deposition of Defendant First Transit’s PMK, as such testimony and documents is relevant to the issue of punitive damages. Specifically, the deposition may shed light on the following issues: “1) Knowledge of the previous, substantially similar accidents involving passengers thrown to the floor; 2) Knowledge of the assessments in the hundreds of thousands and millions of dollars by OCTA against First Transit; and 3) Knowledge of poor training and supervision.” (Mot., 11:1-6.) Such information would tend to show that Defendants had knowledge of a dangerous condition/practice and failure to address such condition, which would support an award of punitive damages. Plaintiff has obtained information from other depositions of OCTA employees (First Transit’s employer) suggesting that Defendants had corporate knowledge of the repeated pattern of elderly, disabled passengers being thrown to the floor.  

 

In opposition, Defendants assert that the scope of the Notice of Deposition is inappropriate for the issues in this case. Defendants concede liability for the incident by “by acknowledging that Ms. Joseph was acting within the course and scope of her employment as a bus operator” when she caused Plaintiff’s fall. (Opp., 2:10-13.) Defendants assert that the only remaining issues in this case are whether Plaintiff suffered injury, the nature and extent of those injuries, and damages. The Notice of Deposition seeks information outside of the scope of the remaining issues in this case—specifically, it seeks “the maintenance history, service history, and operation manuals of the bus model despite no claims of equipment malfunction; incidents involving other passengers with no connection to Ms. Joseph, Plaintiff, or his claims; and evaluations, performance measurement reports, assessments and fines by the Orange County Transit Authority.” (Opp., 4:6-10.) Such categories and requests for production are not limited to a specific time period or jurisdiction. Defendants thus object to the Notice of Deposition’s categories for examination and requests for production of documents on grounds that they are not reasonably particularized and are overly broad in scope and time. 

 

Defendants notes that the Court has previously struck punitive damages from the FAC and the SAC. Defendants have filed a motion to strike punitive damages from the TAC, which is currently set for hearing on December 3, 2026.  

 

In reply, Plaintiff reasserts that the information sought is discoverable. Plaintiff asserts that the TAC contains new allegations regarding Defendants’ corporate knowledge to support a punitive damages claim and that Plaintiff should be able to conduct discovery to explore the theory. Plaintiff notes that the Court has not ruled on the viability of these new punitive damages allegations in the TAC.  

 

The Court notes that, under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.230, Plaintiff need only provide matters for examination to guide Defendant First Transit in selecting a PMK for deposition. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.230 [“[I]f the deponent named is not a natural person, the deposition notice shall describe with reasonable particularity the matters on which examination is requested. In that event, the deponent shall designate and produce at the deposition those of its officers, directors, managing agents, employees, or agents who are most qualified to testify on its behalf as to those matters to the extent of any information known or reasonably available to the deponent.”].) 

 

Here, the Court finds that the categories of examination are reasonably particularized such that Defendants can determine a PMK. The categories are further limited to the time period of 2014 to present and generally seek information regarding OCTA buses. To the extent the categories involve other jurisdictions (i.e., the entirety of California, all jurisdictions), the Court finds such categories are not reasonably particularized. Defendants need only determine a PMK who can testify as to OCTA buses. 

 

The Court further notes that the request for documents in conjunction with a deposition notice is not the equivalent of a request for production of documents under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 et seq. A request for production of documents in a deposition notice need only specify with reasonable particularity the materials or category of materials to be produced by the deponent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.220, subd. (a)(4).) A motion to compel production of documents related to a deposition only requires a showing of “good cause justifying production.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(1).) “Objections to the competency of the deponent, or to the relevancy, materiality, or admissibility at trial of the testimony or of the materials produced are unnecessary and are not waived by failure to make them before or during the deposition.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.460 subd., (c).) 

 

Here, the Court finds that the requests for production are reasonably particularized. The requests for production seek communications between the parties, safety protocols and training materials for bus operation, information regarding the subject bus, writings relating to the incident, writings from OCTA regarding incidents of passengers falling, etc. The Court finds good cause justifying the production—the requests would tend to prove Defendants’ corporate knowledge and ratification of a recurring dangerous practice, which could support a punitive damages award. The Court agrees with Plaintiff that his punitive damages theory is still viable at this point and that he is entitled to discover information that may lead to further supporting allegations. Further, the requests for production are limited to the time period of 2014 to present and seek information involving the subject bus or OCTA buses. The Court finds that such information is reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence. 

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Deposition of First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable and Request for Production of Documents is granted, as modified. Defendant First Transit’s PMK is ordered to appear for a deposition and produce the requested documents. The parties shall meet and confer to determine the date, time, and location of the deposition. The deposition shall take place no later than 30 days from the date of the Court’s issuance of this order. The date may be extended by agreement of the parties.

Plaintiff has been damaged in the total amount of $55,011.30 plus prejudgment interest regarding the first Agreement and $63,635.20 plus prejudgment interest regarding the second Agreement.

 

The Complaint alleges the following causes of action:

 

1.    Breach of Equipment Finance Agreement (against HK Trans and Does 1-5);

2.    Breach of Guaranty (against Roque and Does 6-10);

3.    Breach of Equipment Finance Agreement (against HK Trans and Does 11-15);

4.    Breach of Guaranty (against Roque and Does 16-20);

5.    Account Stated (against all Defendants); and

6.    Claim and Delivery (against all Defendants).

 

The following matters are on calendar:

 

-      Plaintiff’s Application for Writ of Attachment as to HK Trans

-      Plaintiff’s Application for Writ of Attachment as to Roque

-      Plaintiff’s Application for Writ of Possession as to HK Trans

 

The Court will address the Applications for Writ of Attachment concurrently, and then address the Application for Writ of Possession.

 

The Court initially notes that Defendants were served the Summons and Complaint, the Notice of Applications and Hearing, and Applications for Writ of Attachment and Possession via substituted service on May 5, 2026. (See 5/12/26 POS.)

 

No oppositions have been filed as of August 12, 2026.

 

     I.        Applications for Writ of Attachment

 

Legal Standard

 

“Upon the filing of the complaint or at any time thereafter, the plaintiff may apply pursuant to this article for a right to attach order and a writ of attachment by filing an application for the order and writ with the court in which the action is brought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.010.)¿ 

 

The application shall be executed under oath and must include:¿ 

¿ 

1.    a statement showing that the attachment is sought to secure the recovery on a claim upon which an attachment may be issued;¿ 

2.    a statement of the amount to be secured by the attachment;¿ 

3.    a statement that the attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on the claim upon which the attachment is based;¿ 

4.    a statement that the applicant has no information or belief that the claim is discharged or that the prosecution of the action is stayed in a proceeding under the Bankruptcy Act (11 U.S.C. section 101 et seq.); and

5.    a description of the property to be attached under the writ of attachment and a statement that the plaintiff is informed and believes that such property is subject to attachment.¿ 

¿ 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 484.020.)¿ 

¿ 

“The application [for a writ of attachment] shall be supported by an affidavit showing that the plaintiff on the facts presented would be entitled to a judgment on the claim upon which the attachment is based.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.030.) Statutory attachment procedures are purely creations of the legislature and as such “are subject to strict construction.” (Hobbs v. Weiss (1999) 73 Cal.App.4th 76, 79 (internal quotations omitted).) A judge does not have authority to order any attachment that is not provided for by the attachment statutes.¿(Jordan-Lyon Productions, Ltd. v. Cineplex Odeon Corp. (1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 1459, 1466.) “In contested applications, the court must consider the relative merits of the positions of the respective parties and make a determination of¿the probable outcome of the litigation.” (Id. at p. 80 (ellipses and internal quotations omitted).)¿ 

 

Before the hearing on the Writ of Attachment, the defendant must be served with (1) a copy of the summons and complaint; (2) a Notice of Application and Hearing; and (3) a copy of the application and any affidavit in support thereof.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 484.040.)

¿ 

The Court shall issue a right to attach order if the Court finds all of the following:¿ 

 

1.    The claim upon which the attachment is based is one upon which an attachment may be issued.¿ 

2.    The plaintiff has established the probable validity of the claim upon which the attachment is based.¿ 

3.    The attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on the claim upon which the attachment is based.¿ 

4.    The amount to be secured by the attachment is greater than zero.¿ 

¿ 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 484.090.)

 

Discussion

 

Basis of Attachment

 

The Court shall issue a right to attach order if the claim upon which the attachment is based is one upon which an attachment may be issued. (Code Civ. Proc., § 484.090.) “[A]n attachment may be issued only in an action on a claim or claims for money, each of which is based upon a contract, express or implied, where the total amount of the claim or claims is a fixed or readily ascertainable amount not less than five hundred dollars ($500) exclusive of costs, interest, and attorney’s fees.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 483.010, subd. (a).)

 

“If the action is against a defendant who is a natural person, an attachment may be issued only on a claim which arises out of the conduct by the defendant of a trade, business, or profession. An attachment may not be issued on a claim against a defendant who is a natural person if the claim is based on the sale or lease of property, a license to use property, the furnishing of services, or the loan of money where the property sold or leased, or licensed for use, the services furnished, or the money loaned was used by the defendant primarily for personal, family, or household purposes.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 483.010, subd. (c).)

 

Here, Plaintiffs’ attachment claim is based on HK Trans’ breach of the Agreements and the Guaranties with Roque. Plaintiff claims that, pursuant to the Agreements, Defendants currently owe an outstanding principal balance of $55,011.30, plus prejudgment interest regarding the first Agreement and $63,635.20 plus prejudgment interest regarding the second Agreement. (See Jueschke Decl., ¶¶ 4-14.) Plaintiff asserts that the total fair market value of the equipment is $25,000.00 and the total amount owed to Plaintiff is $118,646.50, and thus, Plaintiff is unsecured in the amount of $93,646.50. (Jueschke Decl., ¶ 27.) Plaintiff requests a total writ of attachment in the amount of $125,161.87, consisting of the unsecured amount of $93,646.50, prejudgment interest of $28,077.44 (rate of 18 percent pursuant to the Agreements calculated from April 13, 2024 to December 11, 2025), attorney’s fees of $2,762.93, and costs of $675.00. (Jueschke Decl., ¶ 29.)

 

Plaintiff asserts that attachment is proper against Roque as a personal grantor because Roque personally benefitted from the Agreements and Guaranties. In other words, Plaintiff’s claim against Roque arises out of his conduct of a trade, business, or profession.

The Court agrees. “[I]f the sum total of the circumstances justifies the conclusion that the guarantor occupied himself to a substantial degree and on a continuing basis in promoting his own profit through provision of credit or management to the primary obligor, a guarantee executed in the course of such activity may properly be considered an obligation arising out of the conduct of the guarantor's business.” (Advance Transformer Co. v. Superior Court (1974) 44 Cal.App.3d 127, 144.) Here, Roque signed the Agreements and signed the related Guaranties as a member of HK Trans. Plaintiff’s claim against him arises out of his conduct of business.

The Court finds that Plaintiff demonstrates a valid basis for attachment under written agreements.

 

          Property to be Attached

 

All property within California held by a corporation is subject to attachment if there is a statutory method of levy for the property. (Code Civ. Proc., § 487.010, subd. (a).) Where the defendant is a natural person, real property, accounts receivable, chattel paper, and general intangibles arising out of the conduct by the defendant of business, equipment, inventory, money on the premises where a business is conducted by the defendant, money in deposit accounts off the premises (except for the first one thousand dollars ($1,000)), and other property are appropriate property to be attachment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 487.010, subd. (c).)

 

As to HK Trans, a limited liability company, Plaintiff seeks to attach all property. (HK Trans Attach. App., ¶ 8.)

 

As to Roque, a natural person, Plaintiff seeks to attach the following property:

 

“1. Property including equipment and inventory of a going business pursuant to CCP Sections 488.375 and 488.395; 2. All vehicles of a going business pursuant to CCP section 488.385; 3. Deposit accounts thereof pursuant to CCP section 488.455; 4. Property in a safe-deposit box pursuant to CCP 488.460; 5. Any accounts receivable or general intangibles pursuant to CCP section 488.470; 6. Any and all real property.”

 

(Roque Attach. App., ¶ 8.)

 

The Court finds that the property sought to be attached it appropriate. The Application is unopposed, and Roque has not filed a claim of exemption as to the property to be attached.

 

Probable Validity of Plaintiff’s Claims

 

“A claim has ‘probable validity’ where it is more likely than not that the plaintiff will obtain a judgment against the defendant on that claim.” (Code of Civ. Proc., §¿481.190.) “If the defendant opposes the application, ‘the court must then consider the relative merits of the positions of the respective parties and make a determination of the probable outcome of the litigation.’ [Citations.]” (Pech v. Morgan (2021) 61 Cal.App.5th 841, 855.) 

 

To state a cause of action for breach of contract, Plaintiff must be able to establish “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) the resulting damages to the plaintiff.” (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.)

 

Plaintiff has established the elements for breach of contract and breach of guaranty. On July 15, 2026, Plaintiff and HK Trans entered into the Agreements, whereby HK Trans leased certain equipment. (Jueschke Decl., ¶¶ 4, 11, Exhs. 1, 3.) To induce Plaintiff to enter into the Agreements, Plaintiff and Roque entered into the related Guaranties, whereby Roque agreed to guaranty HK Trans' obligations under the Agreements. (Jueschke Decl., ¶¶ 5, 11, Exhs. 2, 4.) HK Trans defaulted on the first Agreement on April 13, 2022 and on the second Agreement on February 28, 2025. (Jueschke Decl., ¶¶ 6, 13.) Pursuant to the Agreements, the entire balance of the payments under the Agreements became immediately due and payable to Plaintiff. (Jueschke Decl., ¶¶ 7, 14.) Plaintiff has performed all of its obligations under the terms of the Agreements, except as excused or prevented by the conduct of HK Trans. (Jueschke Decl., ¶¶ 10, 17.) Plaintiff has been damaged in the amount of $55,011.30 regarding the first Agreement and $63,635.20 regarding the second Agreement, plus 18 percent pre-judgment interest pursuant to the terms of the Agreements. (Ibid.) The Agreements and Guaranties also provide for reasonable attorney’s fees and costs incurred for their enforcement. (Jueschke Decl., ¶ 19.)

 

No oppositions have been filed.

 

The Court finds that Plaintiff has established a probable validity of its claims.

 

Purpose and Bankruptcy Status

 

In compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 484.020, subdivisions (c) and (d), Plaintiff states that 1) attachment is not sought for a purpose other than the recovery on a claim upon which the attachment is based, 2) Plaintiff is not

seeking attachment of Defendants’ property for any purpose other than recovery for my claim, and 3) Plaintiff has no information or believe that my claim is discharged or has been stayed by any bankruptcy proceeding. (HK Trans Attach. App., ¶¶ 4-5; Roque Attach. App., ¶¶ 4-5 ; Jueschke Decl., ¶ 20.)

 

Undertaking

 

“Before issuance of a writ of attachment…or an order under subdivision (b) of Section 491.415, the plaintiff shall file an undertaking to pay the defendant any amount the defendant may recover for any wrongful attachment by the plaintiff in the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 489.210.) “[T]he amount of an undertaking filed pursuant to this article shall be ten thousand dollars ($10,000).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 489.220.)

 

Plaintiff has not filed an undertaking for both Applications. (HK Trans Attach. App., ¶ 15; Roque Attach. App., ¶ 15.) Thus, Plaintiffs are ordered to file a $10,000.00 undertaking for each Application.

 

    II.        Application for Writ of Possession


Legal Standard

 

“Upon the filing of the complaint or at any time thereafter, the plaintiff may apply pursuant to this chapter for a writ of possession by filing a written application for the writ with the court in which the action is brought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 512.010, subd. (a).)¿¿ 

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 512.010, subdivision (b), the application must be submitted under oath and include:¿¿ 

 

1.    A showing of the basis of the plaintiff's claim and that the plaintiff is entitled to possession of the property claimed; 

2.    A showing that the property is wrongfully detained by the defendant, of the manner in which the defendant came into possession; 

3.    A particular description of the property and a statement of its value; 

4.    The property’s location based on plaintiff’s knowledge, information, and belief; and

5.    A statement that the property has not been seized by statute or execution against it.¿ 

 

Before the hearing on the Writ of Possession, the defendant must be served with (1) a copy of the summons and complaint; (2) a Notice of Application and Hearing; and (3) a copy of the application and any affidavit in support thereof.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 512.030.)¿¿ 

 

 “If a writ of possession is issued, the court may also issue an order directing the defendant to transfer possession of the property to the plaintiff. Such order shall contain a notice to the defendant that failure to turn over possession of such property to plaintiff may subject the defendant to being held in contempt of court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 512.070.) 

 

¿“The writ will be issued if the court finds that the plaintiff's claim is probably valid and the other requirements for issuing the writ are established.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc. § 512.040, subd. (b).)¿ “A claim has ‘probable validity’ where it is more likely than not that the plaintiff will obtain a judgment against the defendant on that claim.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 511.090.)¿ 

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff applies for Writ of Possession of certain property (hereinafter, “Equipment”) wrongfully retained by HK Trans. Plaintiff’s claim and right to possession is detailed in the Complaint and the Declaration of David Jueschke. (Poss. App., ¶¶ 3, 5.) Specifically, pursuant to HK Trans’ default on the Agreements, Plaintiff is entitled to immediate possession of the Equipment. (Jueschke Decl., ¶ 24.) The Equipment is described as follows:

 

-      2018 Freightliner Cascadia VIN 3AKJHHDRSJSJJ1435; Fair Market Value $12,500.00

-      2018 Freightliner Cascadia Serial #3AKJHHDROJSJJ0807; Fair Market Value $12,500.00

 

(Poss. App., ¶ 4; Jueschke Decl., ¶ 26.)

 

To Plaintiff’s knowledge, the Equipment is located at 8689 Bowers Avenue, South Gate, CA 90280. (Poss. App., ¶ 5.)

 

No opposition has been filed.

 

As discussed previously, the Court finds that Plaintiff has established a probable validity of its claims.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 515.010, subdivision (a) states that the court shall not issue a writ of possession until the plaintiff has filed an undertaking with the court. However, “[i]f the court finds that the defendant has no interest in the property, the court shall waive the requirement of the plaintiff's undertaking and shall include in the order for issuance of the writ the amount of the defendant's undertaking sufficient to satisfy the requirements of subdivision (b) of Section 515.020.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 515.010, subd. (b).)

 

Based on the evidence, the Court finds that HK Trans has no interest in the Equipment. Thus, the undertaking requirement is waived.

Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that the parties meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or by video conference.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

Counsel met and conferred via telephone and written communication regarding the issues in this Demurrer but were unable to come to a resolution. (Lewis Decl., ¶¶ 4-5; Gold Decl., ¶¶ 6-7, Exh. 7.) The Court finds that the meet and confer requirement has been met.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant provides the following context regarding this action: On April 21, 2005, the parties purchased the Subject Property during their marriage as joint tenants. (RJN, Exh. E.) On October 19, 2022, Plaintiff filed a petition for dissolution of marriage in Los Angeles County Superior Court under Case No. 22WHFL01798 (hereinafter, “Dissolution Matter”). (RJN, Exh. B.) On February 1, 2023, the family court entered the Judgment of Dissolution (RJN, Exh. D.) On December 1, 2025, Plaintiff filed a request for temporary emergency orders in the Dissolution Matter seeking an order shortening time to divide the Subject Property. The Court denied the request, ruling that Plaintiff has not “articulated reason why the request could not be made through noticed motion.” (RJN, Exh. F.) On December 4, 2025, Plaintiff filed the same request, and it was denied again. (RJN, Exh. G.) Plaintiff filed this partition action on January 5, 2026.

 

Defendant makes three primary arguments in this Demurrer: 1) this action is barred by Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b), 2) the family court that adjudicated the Dissolution Matter retains exclusive jurisdiction to determine the division of omitted community property pursuant to Family Code section 2556, and 3) this action is an improper motion for reconsideration.

 

Plaintiff has filed an opposition. No reply has been filed as of date.

 

The Court will address each argument in turn.

 

The Court notes that the hearing on Defendant’s Demurrer was continued to today’s date based on a potential changing status of the Dissolution Matter. (See 7/14/26 Minute Order.) No further briefing has been submitted to the Court.

 

i.             Whether this Action is Barred by CCP § 872.210

 

Defendant argues that this action is barred by Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b), which states the following: “[A]n action between spouses or putative spouses for partition of their community or quasi-community property or their quasi-marital interest in property may not be commenced or maintained under this title.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.210, subd. (b).)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that this action is not an attempt to adjudicate the parties’ marital interests in the Subject Property outside of family court. Rather, Plaintiff seeks to adjudicate the parties’ interest in the property as joint tenants after the conclusion of their divorce. Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b) does not apply here because the parties are no longer spouses.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff. Because the parties are no longer spouses, this action is not an “action between spouses” for purposes of Code of Civil Procedure section 872.210, subdivision (b). Thus, this action is not barred by that section.

 

ii.            Jurisdiction

 

Defendant also asserts that the family court that adjudicated the Dissolution Matter retains exclusive jurisdiction to determine the division of unadjudicated community property, citing Family Code section 2556. In other words, this civil court has no jurisdiction to adjudicate division of the Subject Property.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that, while Family Code section 2556 states that a family court retains “continuing jurisdiction” to adjudicate division of community property after judgment, such jurisdiction is not exclusive. Regardless of whether the family court retained continuing jurisdiction here, under the exclusive jurisdiction rule, when two courts have concurrent jurisdiction over the over the subject matter and parties, the first court to obtain jurisdiction has exclusive and continuing jurisdiction. Here, Plaintiff filed his partition action on January 5, 2026, giving this Court jurisdiction over the Subject Property, and served Defendant on January 10, 2026, giving this Court jurisdiction over Defendant. (Gold Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.) As of the date of this opposition, Defendant has not filed and served a postjudgment motion in the family court action pursuant to Family Code section 2556. (Gold Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, Plaintiff argues that this Court has exclusive jurisdiction over this matter.

 

The Court provides context for Family Code section 2556. Prior to the enactment of section 2556, community estate assets not before the court in a dissolution or otherwise left unadjudicated by the judgment, or “omitted assets”, were subject to later litigation between the parties. (See Henn v. Henn (“Henn”) (1980) 26 Cal.3d 323, 330 [“[U]nder settled principles of California community property law, ‘property which is not mentioned in the pleadings as community property is left unadjudicated by decree of divorce, and is subject to future litigation, the parties being tenants in common meanwhile.’”].) Such independent civil actions—known as “Henn proceedings”—were the only avenue for postjudgment property claims. Acting on criticism that Henn proceedings were expensive and burdensome on judicial resources, the Legislature enacted Family Code section 2556 in 1990 to authorize postjudgment adjudication of “Henn-type” claims in the underlying family law action. (See In re Marriage of Hixson (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 1116, 1121.)

 

Family Code section 2556 states the following:

 

“In a proceeding for dissolution of marriage, for nullity of marriage, or for legal separation of the parties, the court has continuing jurisdiction to award community estate assets or community estate liabilities to the parties that have not been previously adjudicated by a judgment in the proceeding. A party may file a postjudgment motion or order to show cause in the proceeding in order to obtain adjudication of any community estate asset or liability omitted or not adjudicated by the judgment. In these cases, the court shall equally divide the omitted or unadjudicated community estate asset or liability, unless the court finds upon good cause shown that the interests of justice require an unequal division of the asset or liability.”

 

(Fam. Code, § 2556, emphasis added.)

 

Considering the history and language of Family Code section 2556, the Court agrees with Plaintiff’s interpretation. While a family court has continuing jurisdiction to adjudicate division of community property after judgment upon a party’s filing of a postjudgment motion, such jurisdiction is not exclusive. Family Code section 2556 states that a party “may” file such postjudgment motion, not that a party must do so. Family Code section 2556 only authorizes “an alternative procedure” to accomplish the object of an action authorized under Henn. (11 Witkin, Summary 11th Com. Prop § 258 (2026). Thus, the Court finds that the family court’s continuing jurisdiction under Family Code section 2556 does not necessarily bar this Henn-type action.

 

Moreover, under the rule of exclusive concurrent jurisdiction, “when two [California] superior courts have concurrent jurisdiction over the subject matter and all parties involved in litigation, the first to assume jurisdiction has exclusive and continuing jurisdiction over the subject matter and all parties involved until such time as all necessarily related matters have been resolved.” (People ex rel. Garamendi v. American Autoplan, Inc. (1993) 20 Cal.App.4th 760, 769-70.) Here, Plaintiff filed this action and served Defendant, through which this Court obtained jurisdiction over the Subject Property and Defendant. (Gold Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.) To the Court’s knowledge, there is no pending postjudgment motion in the family court filed prior to this action. (Gold Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, this Court has exclusive jurisdiction over this matter.

 

Thus, the Demurrer cannot be sustained on these grounds.

 

iii.           Improper Motion for Reconsideration

 

Defendant argues that this action is an improper motion for reconsideration pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008. Specifically, Defendant asserts that Plaintiff’s filing this partition action effectively seeks improper reconsideration of the divorce court’s denial of Plaintiff’s December 1, 2025, and December 4, 2025, emergency requests for orders shortening time for a hearing to divide the subject property. Defendant requests sanctions in the amount of $8,000.00 under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (d).

 

In opposition, Plaintiff asserts that this action is not a motion for reconsideration. Plaintiff’s previous ex partes in the family court sought an order shortening time to hold a hearing to divide the Subject Property, and the family court denied the ex partes on procedural grounds. There was no adjudication on the merits of any postjudgment motion. In other words, there is no order for this Court to reconsider. Further, motions for reconsideration must be heard in the same court in front of the same judge.

 

Relatedly, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s request for sanctions is procedurally and substantively deficient. The request is procedurally deficient because Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 sanctions, which are authorized by Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (d), must be filed in a separate motion. The request is substantively deficient because Plaintiff has committed no violation of Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, as explained previously.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff. Plaintiff has filed no improper motion for reconsideration that warrants sanctions. As discussed in the prior section, Plaintiff’s partition action is valid.

 

Thus, the Court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (d).

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the Demurrer is OVERRULED.

 

    II.        Plaintiff’s Motions to Compel Further Responses to Discovery Requests

 

Legal Standard

 

A party may move to compel a further response to a discovery demand if the demanding party deems that the statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete; the representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive; or an objection in the response is without merit or too general.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300, subd. (a) [interrogatories], 2031.310, subd. (a) [documents and inspections], 2033.290, subd. (a) [requests for admission].)¿

 

Meet and Confer

 

A motion to compel further responses to requests for production and to interrogatories must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040. (Code of Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300 [interrogatories], subd. (b)(1)., 2031.310, subd. (b)(2) [documents and inspections], 2033.290, subd. (b)(1) [requests for admission].)

 

Plaintiff’s meet and confer efforts are detailed in the next section.

 

Discussion

 

On January 20, 2026, Plaintiff served the Discovery Requests on Defendant. (Gold Decls., ¶ 5, Exh. 2.) The responses were originally due on February 19, 2026 but Plaintiff granted an extension to Defendant such that responses were due March 6, 2026. (Gold Decls., ¶ 6.) On March 6, 2026, Defendant served responses to the Discovery Requests. (Gold Decls., ¶¶ 7, Exh. 3.)

 

On March 13, 2026, Defense counsel sent Plaintiff’s counsel a meet and confer letter requesting further responses. (Gold Decls., ¶ 8, Exh. 4.) On March 24, 2026, Defense counsel attempted to meet and confer via telephone, and left a voicemail requesting a call back. (Gold Decls., ¶ 9.) Defense counsel thereafter received no response. (Gold Decls., ¶¶ 9-10.)

 

Plaintiff explains that Defendant objected to each Discovery Request on grounds that the Court lacks subject matter jurisdiction to partition the Subject Property and that the request is burdensome and harassing. Plaintiff now moves to compel further responses to the Discovery Requests on grounds that 1) lack of subject matter jurisdiction is not a proper objection to discovery, and 2) the requests seek relevant information. Defendant’s objections are an “improper repetition of the merits-based argument in Defendant’s demurrer relating to subject-matter jurisdiction.” (See Sep. Statements.)

 

The Court notes that the hearing on Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Discovery Responses to Form Interrogatories was originally set for hearing on May 14, 2026. Defendant argues that the Court does not have jurisdiction to maintain the action pursuant to Family Code section 2556, reiterating similar arguments in her Demurrer.

Further, Plaintiff makes the following representations in the reply: “Defendant waited until June 2, 2026, to file a Request for Order in the family law case, and filed a continuance on July 15, 2026, because she failed to personally serve Plaintiff with the request as required by Family Code § 215. Plaintiff has still not been personally served with the request, and consequently, the Family Court still has not acquired personal jurisdiction over the Plaintiff.” (Reply., 5:15-19.)

 

Defendant does not address the other objections in her singular opposition.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Discovery Responses to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, Requests for Admission, and Requests for Production of Documents are GRANTED. Defendant is ORDERED to provide further responses to the discovery requests, without objections, within 30 days of this Order. This deadline may be extended by agreement between the parties.

 

Sanctions

 

The court shall impose a monetary sanction against the party who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further responses to a demand for production of documents, interrogatories, or requests for admission unless the party subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or the sanction would otherwise be unjust. (Code of Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300, subd. (d) [interrogatories], 2031.310, subd. (h) [documents and inspections], 2033.290, subd. (d) [requests for admission].)¿

 

Plaintiff request $4,560.00 for each Motion.

 

The Court reduces sanctions, considering the Motions are substantially similar and no oppositions were filed to three of the four motions.

 

Accordingly, the Court awards $750.00 in sanctions for all Motions.
