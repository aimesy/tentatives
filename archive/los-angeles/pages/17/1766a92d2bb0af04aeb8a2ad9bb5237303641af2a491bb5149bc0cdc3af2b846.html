Case Number: 24STCV31906    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 735

Villasana v. General Motors, LLC

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production of Documents from Defendant

 

BACKGROUND 

This is a lemon law action arising out of Plaintiffs’ purchase of a 2017 Chevrolet Malibu, manufactured and distributed by Defendant General Motors, LLC.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs move to compel further responses to RPDs, set one, nos. 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 15, 16, 23, 24, 29, 30, 39, 40, 51, 52, 61, 62, 67, 68, 71, 72, 73, 74, 83, 84, 89, 91, 93, 100, 114, 115, 116, 117, 118, 119, 131, 132, 135, and 136 from Defendant. Plaintiffs served the subject discovery on March 20, 2025. Plaintiffs assert Defendant served unverified responses on April 24, 2025 and verifications on June 11, 2025. The requests seek documents relating to Defendant's internal investigation and analysis of the Engine Defects and Transmission Defects plaguing Plaintiffs' vehicle and establishing that Defendant previously knew of such Defects but nevertheless refused to repurchase the vehicle.

Plaintiffs assert that further responses are necessary because Defendant has refused to produce any responsive documents and because Defendant’s objections lack merit. In opposition, Defendant represents that it produced over 13,000 documents on April 16, 2025.

The Court previously continued the hearing on this motion to allow the parties to further meet and confer over the sufficiency of Defendant’s supplemental responses. The Court ordered Plaintiffs to file a declaration no later than July 2, 2026, stating which requests remain at issue for Court determination following meet and confer. The Courts record reflects no such declaration has been filed. Accordingly, the Court assumes that the parties have resolved all disputes relating to this motion and takes the motion off calendar.

CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs’ motion to compel further responses to discovery is off calendar.

Plaintiffs to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV13621    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 735

Sagrero v. General Motors, LLC

Defendant GM’s Demurrer to First Amended Complaint and Motion to Strike

 

BACKGROUND 

This is a lemon law action arising out of the purchase of a 2020 Chevrolet Equinox, manufactured and distributed by Defendant. Plaintiffs bring this action for violations of the Song-Beverly Act and fraudulent concealment.

DISCUSSION

Demurrer

Fraudulent Concealment

Defendants demur to the fifth cause of action for fraudulent concealment on the ground that it fails to allege sufficient facts. The Court of Appeal in Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828 addressed issues almost identical to those in this demurrer. The Court found that the following allegations were sufficient to state a cause of action for fraud: “plaintiffs alleged the CVT transmissions installed in numerous Nissan vehicles (including the one plaintiffs purchased) were defective; Nissan knew of the defects and the hazards they posed; Nissan had exclusive knowledge of the defects but intentionally concealed and failed to disclose that information; Nissan intended to deceive plaintiffs by concealing known transmission problems; plaintiffs would not have purchased the car if they had known of the defects; and plaintiffs suffered damages in the form of money paid to purchase the car.” (Id. at 844.) Plaintiffs have alleged analogous facts here.

Defendant asserts that the Supreme Court ruling in Rattagan v. Uber Technologies (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1 requires a different result. Specifically, Defendant contends that Rattagan requires the Plaintiff allege a direct dealing or relationship between the parties in order to establish a duty to disclose for a fraudulent concealment claim. Defendant asserts that Plaintiffs have not done so here. Plaintiffs allege that Plaintiffs entered into a warranty agreement with Defendant. This allegation is sufficient to create a direct transactional relationship. The demurrer to the cause of action for fraud is overruled.

Violation of the CLRA

Defendant asserts the claim for violation of the CLRA fails because Plaintiffs have not sufficiently alleged fraud. As stated, Plaintiffs have sufficiently pleaded the cause of action for fraud. The demurrer to the cause of action for violation of the CLRA is overruled.

Motion to Strike

Defendant moves to strike Plaintiffs’ claims for punitive damages. As Plaintiffs have sufficiently alleged a cause of action for fraud, Plaintiffs have alleged entitlement to punitive damages. The motion to strike is denied.

CONCLUSION 

The Court overrules Defendant’s demurrer.

The Court denies Defendant’s motion to strike.

Defendant shall file and serve its answer within 10 days.

Defendant shall give notice.


Case Number: 26STCV08326    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 735

Atwater v. PLC Law Group, APC, et al.

Specially Appearing Defendants’ Motion to Extend Time to File Responsive Pleading

 

BACKGROUND 

This is a legal malpractice action. On March 16, 2026, Plaintiff Lorie Atwater filed a complaint against Defendants PLC Law Group, APC, Peter Laurence Carr, Na’Shaun Neal and Lauren McRae, asserting causes of action for (1) professional negligence; (2) breach of fiduciary duty; (3) negligent misrepresentation; and (4) breach of contract.

LEGAL STANDARD

“A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: (1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.”  (CCP § 418.10(a)(1).) 

DISCUSSION

Specially Appearing Defendants move for an extension of time to file their motion to quash service of summons. Defendants represent that they attempted to file the motion within the statutory deadline on May 18, 2026, but Defendants’ counsel mistakenly believed that a filing fee was unnecessary due to Defendants’ special appearance status. The clerk’s office rejected the filing for lack of filing fee. Upon learning of the rejection, Defendants’ counsel paid the filing fee and filed the motion on June 3, 2026. The Court will allow the late filing. The delay in filing was due to counsel’s excusable error and was diligently corrected. The Court grants Defendants’ motion.

CONCLUSION 

The Court grants Specially Appearing Defendants' motion to extend time to file a responsive pleading.

The clerk will give notice.
