SUPERIOR COURT OF CALIFORNIA, COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES

Tentative Ruling

24STCV14962

JANE DOE 1, et al. vs MCGRATH KAVINOKY, LLP, et al.

July 8, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: DEFENDANT MCGRATH KAVINOKY LLP’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO ITS SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES TO DOE 2 (SET ONE) IN COMPLIANCE WITH THE COURT’S MARCH 2, 2026 ORDER.

 

RULING:

The Motion is granted, except as to request number 44 and the sanctions request.

On or before September 8, 2026, Plaintiff JANE DOE 2 shall serve further responses in full compliance with the California Discovery Act (Code Civ. Proc. § 2016.010 et seq.), as to Defendant MCGRATH KAVINOKY LLP’S Special Interrogatories, (Set One), numbers 3, 62, 70, 83, 92, 95, 97, and 100.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

The Court incorporates by reference the Background stated in the Minutes entered on November 17, 2025.

On May 19, 2026, MCGRATH KAVINOKY LLP (Defendant) filed the Motion to Compel Further Responses to certain Special Interrogatories, as the supplemental responses are not full and complete, as ordered on March 2, 2026. Defendant also requests sanctions against Doe 2 in the amount of $5,000.00.

JANE DOE 2 (Plaintiff) opposes, arguing that supplemental responses have been served in compliance with the Order, and much of the information Defendants seek cannot be provided since Defendants have not produced the complete client file. Plaintiff also advocates appointing the Discovery Referee for this and any future discovery matters.

 

I.                    LEGAL STANDARD

“While the party propounding interrogatories may have the burden of filing a motion to compel if it finds the answers it receives unsatisfactory, the burden of justifying any objection and failure to respond remains at all times with the party resisting an interrogatory.” (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 541.)

Monetary discovery sanctions are mandatory as to parties losing discovery motions, unless courts find substantial justification or other injustice. (Deck v. Devs. Inv. Co. (2023) 89 Cal.App.5th 808, 830.) “‘[T]he phrase “substantial justification” has been understood to mean that a justification is clearly reasonable because it is well grounded in both law and fact.’” (Kwan Software Engineering, Inc. v. Hennings (2020) 58 Cal.App.5th 57, 75.) “If the party seeking a monetary sanction meets its burden of proof, the burden shifts to the opposing party attempting to avoid a monetary sanction to show that it acted with ‘substantial justification.’” (Doe v. U.S. Swimming, Inc. (2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1424, 1435.)

 

II.                 ANALYSIS

Below the Court analyzes the Separate Statement filed on June 24, 2026.

 

1.      “SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 3: Describe the circumstances under which YOU learned that Plaintiff [Doe 1] intended to file this ACTION.”

Granted.

Plaintiff failed to address circumstances not privileged and separable from attorney-client communications.

“[A] responding party may object to an interrogatory that seeks privileged information by clearly stating the objection and the particular privilege invoked. (Hernandez v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 285, 293 [“But the existence of a document containing privileged information is not privileged.”].)

 

2.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 44: State all facts that SUPPORT YOUR contention in paragraph 67 of the COMPLAINT that ‘Other plaintiffs experienced exactly the same change in how Defendants treated them.’ ”

Denied.

Contention interrogatories involve no minimum requirement as to the content being supportive. Instead, if there is any willful obfuscation of responsive information other remedies may apply, including drastic sanctions, or summary judgment.

Contention interrogatories involve the subjective reflection of attorneys in formulating a response in a sophisticated process of legal reasoning, sorting through evidence and organizing it in terms of contentions a party is asserting. (Rifkind v. Superior Court (1994) 22 Cal.App.4th 1255, 1263.) “‘There is no doubt that a defendant is entitled to discover by appropriate interrogatories the facts, if any, presently known to the plaintiff upon which he bases the allegations of his complaint and upon which he presently relies to prove his case.’” (Tehachapi-Cummings Cnty. Water Dist. v. Superior Court of Kern Cnty. (1968) 267 Cal.App.2d 42, 46.) In responding to contention requests, respondents’ willful obfuscation, game playing, stonewalling by referencing continuing discovery, providing immaterial information, or concealing support or a lack thereof, may support orders imposing drastic discovery sanctions. (See Liberty Mutual Fire Ins. Co. v. LcL Administrators, Inc. (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 1093, 1102-1104.) Any factually devoid or unsupportive responses in response to contention interrogatories may shift the burden of proof for a motion for summary judgment. (Union Bank v. Superior Court (1995) 31 Cal.App.4th 573, 580.)

 

3.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 62: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged professional negligence.”

Granted.

A final and binding amount of damages calculated with expert assistance, need not be provided in discovery responses. Instead, an interrogatory response must be as complete and straightforward as reasonably available information permits. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.220.) Here, Plaintiff has not estimated some damages amounts in addressing several types of alleged damages, such as for emotional distress.

A response must “represent the interrogated party's present best and complete answer.” (Fuss v. Superior Court (1969) 273 Cal.App.2d 807, 816.) If a respondent does not have personal knowledge for a full response, the party shall state that and make a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain the information by inquiries. (Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 406; Regency Health Services, Inc. v. Superior Court (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 1496, 1504.) Where interrogatory responses reveal all information currently available to a party, but the respondent cannot furnish all requested information, then the party should set forth the efforts made to obtain that information. (Deyo v. Kilbourne (1978) 84 Cal.App.3d 771, 782, superseded by statute on another ground as stated in Guzman v. General Motors Corp. (1984) 154 Cal.App.3d 438, 444.)

And many rulings from various other jurisdictions have required parties to calculate damages based on the best information available at the time of the discovery responses and to provide any later available information in response to any proper supplemental discovery requests. (See generally Damages, 7 Annotated Patent Digest § 41:41.50 [available on Westlaw].)

In this case, causation of damages is determined by case-within-a-case approach, involving a decision as to what should have been the result in the underlying matter, and questions about what would have happened had lawyers acted otherwise, are issues of fact, unless reasonable minds could not differ. (Ambriz v. Kelegian (2007) 146 Cal.App.4th 1519, 1531, 1532.) Further, no fixed or absolute standard exists for computing the dollar value of emotional distress damages, and juries have vast discretion in determining such damages amounts. (Hope v. California Youth Auth. (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 577, 595.)

 

4.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 70: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged breach of fiduciary duty.”

Granted, as already addressed above regarding Special Interrogatory number 62.

 

5.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 83: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged fraudulent misrepresentation.”

Granted, as already addressed above regarding Special Interrogatory number 62.

 

6.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 92: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged fraudulent concealment.”

Granted, as already addressed above regarding Special Interrogatory number 62.

 

7.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 95: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged breach of contract.”

Granted, as already addressed above regarding Special Interrogatory number 62.

 

8.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 97: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.”

Granted, as already addressed above regarding Special Interrogatory number 62.

 

9.      “MK’S SPECIAL INTERROGATORY NO. 100: State the amount of damages YOU allege YOU suffered due to DEFENDANTS’ alleged conversion.”

Granted, as already addressed above regarding Special Interrogatory number 62.

 

III.              CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the Court grants the Motion in part, as specified herein.

Finally, the Court denies Defendant’s sanctions request, the Court finding substantial justification based on (1) substantially improved supplemental responses, (2) the code-compliant response as to Special Interrogatory number 44, and (3) involvement of a first impression issue in California regarding requirements of discovery responses involving calculating damages. Courts may determine substantial justification based on the “whole picture” of the discovery dispute, including where parties partially prevailed as to some discovery items in dispute. (In re Marriage of Moore (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 1275, 1297.)

24STCV25722

REGINA HALL vs JUAN F. PEREZ, et al.

July 8, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT.

 

RULING:

The Court continues the Motion to August 7, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., for a corrected Proof of Service to be served and filed within five days.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On October 3, 2024, REGINA HALL (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against JUAN F. PEREZ and JP CONTRACTOR SERVICES, INC. (Defendants).

The proposed First Amended Complaint (FAC) lists Causes of Action for:

(1) BREACH OF CONTRACT;

(2) FRAUD;

(3) NEGLIGENCE; and

(4) BREACH OF FIDUCIARY DUTY.

(Charles G. Gomez Declaration, filed 6/1/26, Exhibit B.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants breached a written contract to do remodeling at 4805 Hasekian Rd., Tarzana, by not completing work and negligently supervising a subcontractor.

On June 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion for Leave to File FAC, to add more details about damages.

There is no Opposition filed, which may be attributable to the Proof of Service stating counsel’s former address and email addresses, instead of those reflected in Defendants April 7, 2026 Notice of Change of Address. (See, e.g., Lee v. Placer Title Co. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 503, 509-510 [where notice was not mailed to the “‘office address as last given by that person on any document filed in the cause,’” per Code Civ. Proc., § 1013, subd. (a), notice was ineffective, and order entered was void].)

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a)(1) allows courts “in furtherance of justice” to “allow a party to amend any pleading ... in any ... respect”, which is reviewed for abuse of discretion and requires courts to consider factors including the conduct of moving parties and belated presentations of amendments without a showing of excuse. (Emerald Bay Cmty. Assn. v. Golden Eagle Ins. Corp. (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 1078, 1097.)

“When a trial court denies leave to amend, the decision has been upheld on grounds such as the fact that the amendment contained objectionable subject matter or because of the conduct of the moving party or belated presentation of the amendment.” (Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2003) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 759.)

“ ‘If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend….’ ” (Jaimez v. DAIOHS USA, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 1286, 1308.) “[D]iscretion should be exercised liberally in favor of amendments, for judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters in the same lawsuit.” (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047.)

III.              ANALYSIS

The reasons Plaintiff provides for amending are to allege damages more particularly based on the same general set of facts, and to “fully address the harm suffered … as alleged in the original Complaint and to deter Defendants and other contractors from violating California’s contractor licensing laws, as intended by the California legislature.” (Motion, 5:20-24.)

The Court has received no Opposition raising any issues to address.

“If the original pleading has not framed the issues in an articulate and precise manner, a plaintiff should not be precluded from having a trial on the merits.” (Honig v. Fin. Corp. of Am. (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 960, 965.)

Here, it appears that Plaintiff is merely adding more details to the same claims and general set of facts.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court continues the Motion, for a corrected Proof of Service.

24STCV29543

GOHAR KHACHATRYAN, et al. vs BMW OF NORTH AMERICA, LLC, et al.

July 28, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION OF PERSON(S) MOST KNOWLEDGEABLE AT DEFENDANT BMW OF NORTH AMERICA, LLC, AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS.

 

RULING:

The Court grants the Motion as to the request to compel and denies it as to sanctions.

Commencing at 10:00 a.m., on September 1, 2026, Person(s) Most Knowledgeable of Defendant BMW OF NORTH AMERICA, LLC shall attend a deposition by remote electronic means. (E.g., Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1010; Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.310). The deposition shall proceed in accordance with the deposition notice evidenced with the Motion, except as modified hereby.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On November 12, 2024, GOHAR KHACHATRYAN and KRISTINE OHANYAN (Plaintiffs) filed a Lemon Law Complaint against BMW OF NORTH AMERICA, LLC and FAA BEVERLY HILLS, INC. (Defendants), alleging Causes of Action for:

1.      FRAUD AND DECEIT

2.      BREACH OF WRITTEN WARRANTY PURSUANT TO THE MAGNUSON-MOSS WARRANTY ACT

3.      BREACH OF IMPLIED WARRANTY PURSUANT TO THE MAGNUSON-MOSS WARRANTY ACT

4.      BREACH OF WRITTEN WARRANTY PURSUANT TO THE SONG-BEVERLY CONSUMER WARRANTY ACT

5.      BREACH OF IMPLIED WARRANTY

6.      PURSUANT TO THE SONG-BEVERLY CONSUMER WARRANTY ACT

7.      VIOLATION OF BUSINESS & PROFESSIONS CODE § 17200, ET SEQ.

8.      VIOLATION OF BUSINESS & PROFESSIONS CODE § 17500, ET SEQ.

9.      NEGLIGENCE

10.  STRICT LIABILITY.

Plaintiff alleges purchasing, with warranty provisions, a 2022 BMW X6 that Defendant manufactured, which had defects, including engine, braking, and others listed at Complaint paragraph 6. Plaintiff further alleges that Defendant failed to repair the vehicle within a reasonable number of attempts and did not promptly replace it or make restitution.

On May 26, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the Motion to Compel Deposition of Person(s) Most Knowledgeable at Defendant BMW OF NORTH AMERICA, LLC, and for Monetary Sanctions, on grounds including that Defendant was uncooperative in setting a deposition date, missed a noticed deposition, and still has not set a mutually agreeable date.

Defendant opposes, based upon counterarguments such as that Plaintiffs did not meet and confer in good faith and caused confusion as to the noticed deposition date, eventually resulting in the PMK having a busy scheduling conflict.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

A motion lies to compel deposition attendance or document production, after service of a deposition notice, where a deponent fails to appear at, or proceed with, a deposition, without having served a valid objection under section 2025.410. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

“Code of Civil Procedure section 2025, subdivision (d)(6), provides that if a deposition notice describes matters on which examination is requested, ‘the deponent shall designate and produce at the deposition those of its officers, directors, managing agents, employees, or agents who are most qualified to testify on its behalf as to those matters to the extent of any information known or reasonably available to the deponent.’” (Maldonado v. Superior Court (2002) 94 Cal.App.4th 1390, 1395-1398.)

Monetary discovery sanctions are mandatory as to parties losing discovery motions, unless courts find substantial justification or other injustice. (Deck v. Devs. Inv. Co. (2023) 89 Cal.App.5th 808, 830.) “‘[T]he phrase “substantial justification” has been understood to mean that a justification is clearly reasonable because it is well grounded in both law and fact.’” (Kwan Software Engineering, Inc. v. Hennings (2020) 58 Cal.App.5th 57, 75.) “If the party seeking a monetary sanction meets its burden of proof, the burden shifts to the opposing party attempting to avoid a monetary sanction to show that it acted with ‘substantial justification.’” (Doe v. U.S. Swimming, Inc. (2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1424, 1435.) Courts may determine substantial justification based on the “whole picture” of the discovery dispute, including where parties partially prevailed as to some discovery items in dispute, or where discovery concessions occurred only after improperly asserted objections. (In re Marriage of Moore (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 1275, 1297.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

According to Plaintiffs, Defendant delayed the PMK deposition over a year, failed to appear at the mutually agreed-upon deposition date, proposed a date for after the discovery cutoff, and generally obstructed Plaintiffs’ taking the deposition.

Defendant disagrees and contends it participated in good faith in scheduling efforts for the exceedingly busy PMK, Plaintiff unilaterally noticed one deposition, and the mutually agreeable deposition date of May 14, 2026 failed because Plaintiff did not provide timely notice and sent confusing emails.

No meet and confer is required to compel initial deposition attendance, but instead there must be a declaration showing that moving party inquired about the nonappearance. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).) "Implicit in the requirement that counsel contact the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance is a requirement that counsel listen to the reasons offered and make a good faith attempt to resolve the issue," including by rescheduling. (Leko v. Cornerstone Bldg. Inspection Serv. (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 1109, 1124. See also Super. Ct. LA County, Local Rules, rule 3.26 [“guidelines adopted by the Los Angeles County Bar Association are adopted as civility in litigation recommendations….”].)

Because the parties have been unable to agree to a deposition date, the Court will now set a date certain.

As for Plaintiff’s request for sanctions, the Court finds that the Opposition shows substantial justification, such as the PMK’s heavy schedule that is credible considering the increasing numbers of Lemon Law cases filed in recent times. Further, it appears that both sides had substantially interacted regarding the deposition date, and the evidence is in direct conflict and could be resolved either way.

 

IV.              CONCLUSION

The Court grants the Motion in part, and denies it in part, as specified above.

25STCV02493

BLANCA AREVALO vs COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, et al.

July 8, 2026

8:30 AM

 

 

 

NATURE OF PROCEEDINGS: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR PITCHESS DISCOVERY OF PEACE OFFICER PERSONNEL RECORDS.

 

RULING:

 

The hearing on the matter is continued to July 31, 2026, at 10:30 a.m., in Department 311, for an in camera inspection.

 

At the hearing, Defendant COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES shall bring all potentially relevant documents to the in camera inspection records requested in the subject Motion as to which Pitchess procedure is applicable, excluding only those subject to properly supported discovery objections including privilege logs regarding attorney-client privilege, work product, and privacy. (Cf., City of Vallejo v. Superior Court (2025) 112 Cal.App.5th 565, 579 [“pursuant to a stipulation by the parties as to how to move forward procedurally—the superior court heard and ruled on a Pitchess motion … with the amended privilege log the police department had provided….”].)

Additionally, Defendant shall at that time bring a completed log listing all the documents to be reviewed at the in camera inspection, for inclusion in the Court record.  (See People v. Mooc (2001) 26 Cal.4th 1216, 1228.)

 

Only the party authorized to claim the privilege is allowed into the in camera inspection. (See Evid. Code § 915, subd. (b); Haggerty v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 1079, 1086.)

 

The Court finds that the Pitchess motion sufficiently describes the record or information types sought, and includes a declaration showing good cause for each type, including the materiality and a reasonable belief that the governmental agency has the information or records.

 

I.                    BACKGROUND

On January 29, 2025, BLANCA AREVALO (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, LOS ANGELES COUNTY SHERIFF’S DEPARTMENT, ROBERT LUNA, HOLLY FRANCISCO, JACK EWELL, and THOMAS GIANDOMENICO (Defendants).

On May 22, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (FAC) for:

(1) RACIAL DISCRIMINATION IN VIOLATION OF FEHA (CAL. GOV. CODE § 12940(A));

(2) SEX DISCRIMINATION IN VIOLATION OF FEHA (CAL. GOV. CODE § 12940(A));

(3) HOSTILE WORK ENVIRONMENT AND WORKPLACE HARASSMENT IN VIOLATION OF FEHA (CAL. GOV. CODE § 12940(J)); and

(4) FAILURE TO TAKE CORRECTIVE ACTION IN VIOLATION OF FEHA (CAL. GOV. CODE § 12940(K)).

Plaintiff alleges that, “Plaintiff has been harassed, discriminated against, and forced to endure a racist and sexist hostile work environment because of Defendant LASD’s apparent want to maintain the white-male status quo.” (FAC, ¶ 1.)  

On April 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Motion for Pitchess Discovery of Peace Officer Personnel Records.

Defendants’ Opposition raises several issues, including that some requested documents are outside the scope of the Pitchess procedure, some requests are in overbroad categories, and good cause for production is not shown.

 

II.                 LEGAL STANDARD

Under Evidence Code sections 1043 and 1046, complainants seeking discovery of peace officers’ personnel records, or records regarding a complaint, must file a motion satisfying the statutory requirements, and disclosure is permitted only upon a showing of good cause. (Santa Ana Police Officers Assn. v. City of Santa Ana (2025) 109 Cal.App.5th 296, 309.

The law mandates an in camera inspection for Pitchess matters, and requires ordering excluded (1) any information contained in the personnel files of little or no practical benefit, (2) complaints over five years before the incidents, and (3) and conclusions of investigating officers in the context of any criminal proceedings. (See Haggerty v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 1079, 1087 (Haggerty).) The five-year limitation of Evidence Code section 1045, providing for disclosure of complaints, is not an absolute bar, where the information is pertinent. (Abatti v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 39, 60.)

Penal Code sections 832.7 and 832.8 do not automatically apply to all information contained in an employer’s file, but instead make confidential and protect from disclosure only information specifically enumerated in section 832.8. (Ibarra v. Superior Court (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 695, 704.) Where a Pitchess motion does not apply to the information sought, the proper procedure is to demand an inspection of the information pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.030. (Ibid., at p. 706].)

Section 832.7 creates three categories of confidential documents: 1) Personnel records of peace officers and custodial officers, including discipline and complaints; 2) state or local records held pursuant to Penal Code section 832.5 regarding investigating complaints from the public against  personnel; and 3) information obtained from those records. (City of Vallejo v. Superior Court (2025) 112 Cal.App.5th 565, 585-586.)

After an in camera inspection, courts must order the disclosure of those peace officer personnel records that are relevant to the subject matter of the litigation. (People v. Mooc (2001) 26 Cal.4th 1216, 1226.) “The court in each case must make an initial finding that the requested information is material, and after reviewing the information at an in camera hearing, that the documents are relevant within the meaning of section 1045, subdivision (a) and that the disclosure would not fall within any of the exceptions set forth in the statute.” (Haggerty, supra 117 Cal.App.4th at p. 1092.)

As to Pitchess motions, in civil proceedings, an investigating officer’s conclusions can be discoverable, depending on relevance or benefit. (Id., pp. 1088-1089 [remanding for redacting portions of an Internal Affairs report reflecting solely the investigating officer’s “conclusions,” defined as “‘thought processes of, and factual inferences and deductions drawn by, an officer investigating a complaint, concerning such matters as the credibility of witnesses or the significance, strength, or lack of evidence.’” ].)

 

Orders to exclude material may include redacted portions of documents. (Id., p. 1089 [“trial court to redact those portions of the Internal Affairs report reflecting solely the investigating officer’s ‘conclusions….’”].)

 

Additionally, courts generally do not order production of verbatim police reports re citizen complaints and witnesses, if plaintiffs can obtain the information from the witnesses, after being given just the names, addresses and telephone numbers of witnesses, and incident dates, that the Court observes in the personnel files. (City of Hemet v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1411, 1423; Chambers v. Appellate Div. of Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 674, 679.) However, disclosure is not necessarily limited to witness contact information, if there is direct relevance of investigation (e.g., Internal Affairs reports), and the requested discovery otherwise meets statutory criteria. (Haggerty, supra, 117 Cal.App.4th at p. 1090.) As to Pitchess matters, “the practice of disclosing only the name of the complainant and contact information must yield to the requirement of providing sufficient information to prepare for a fair trial.” (Alvarez v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 1107, 1112 [“petitioner's ability to investigate ... has been stymied by ... refusal to cooperate.”].)

 

As to Pitchess matters, disclosure of officer personnel records under Penal Code section 832.7 is not limited by the official information privilege. (Berkeley Police Assn. v. City of Berkeley (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 385, 400.)

 

“[I]f documents responsive to a document request are withheld on privilege grounds, a privilege log or some equivalent specification of any asserted privilege objection ‘shall’ be supplied.” (Roche v. Hyde (2020) 51 Cal.App.5th 757, 813.)

A privacy objection is not ripe for adjudication before a court where there is no factual basis for ruling, such as respondents’ identifying the documents withheld in a privilege log or elsewhere. (Connecticut Indem. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 23 Cal.4th 807, 818.)

Trial judges have broad discretion in ruling upon Pitchess matters. (Haggerty, supra, 117 Cal.App.4th at p. 1086.)

 

III.              ANALYSIS

Plaintiff seeks an in-camera review and disclosures of specified peace officer personnel records and related materials relevant to Plaintiff’s FAC, including the following discovery requests:

SROG 28 IDENTIFY all COUNTY and/or LASD investigations into Defendants Ewell and/or Giandomenico for workplace harassment, including racial and sex discrimination.

SROG 29 IDENTIFY all actions taken by the COUNTY and/or LASD in response to Plaintiff’s allegations in the Amended Complaint for Damages in this matter.

SROG 30 IDENTIFY all training programs Defendants Ewell and/or Giandomenico attended to address workplace harassment, including racial and sex discrimination, and include the dates of attendance for each.

RFP 46 Please provide all complaints against Jack Ewell, including all entries in his personnel file, for maintaining a hostile work environment, including racial and sex discrimination.

RFP 47 Please provide all complaints against Thomas Giandomenico, including all entries in his personnel file, for maintaining a hostile work environment, including racial and sex discrimination.

RFP 48 Please provide all documents Jack Ewell prepared relating to Plaintiff, including all responses to the allegations in the Amended Complaint for Damages in this matter, from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 49 Please provide all documents prepared by Thomas Giandomenico relating to Plaintiff, including all responses to the allegations in the Amended Complaint for Damages in this matter, from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 50 Please provide all communications between Jack Ewell, and/or Thomas Giandomenico, and/or any County or LASD personnel related to Plaintiff from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 51 Please provide all disciplinary records (including counseling memos, warnings, suspensions, or investigations) involving Jack Ewell and/or Thomas Giandomenico, regardless of whether they resulted in disciplinary action, from 2018 to the present.

RFP 52 Please provide all documents relating to, and/or that constitute the County’s and/or LASD’s Investigation into the allegations in the Amended Complaint for Damages in this matter, including all documents maintained by the LASD Intake Unit, Internal Affairs, County Equity Oversight Panel, Equity Investigation Unit, and all other investigating offices.

RFP 53 Please provide all communications between LASD management and the County Equity Oversight Panel, County Counsel, or any outside counsel relating to Plaintiff or allegations in the Amended Complaint for Damages in this matter.

RFP 54 Please provide all LASD (Including Internal Affairs and EEO) documents showing complaints of workplace harassment, including racial and/or sex discrimination from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 58 Please provide all documents, including sign-in sheets, attendance records, certificates of completion, for LASD training programs to address workplace harassment, including racial and sex discrimination or retaliation. 

RFP 61 Please provide all communications to and from the Sheriff, Undersheriff or Assistant Sheriff relating to Plaintiff, and/or Aero Bureau staffing issues from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 63 Please provide all documents and/or video recordings relating to any meeting, debriefing, or gathering where Plaintiff was discussed by name or title from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 64 Please provide all LASD equity complaints and tracking summaries or databases showing case numbers, findings, and outcomes from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 65 Please provide all documents relating to any investigation into retaliation against Plaintiff following her complaints of workplace harassment or EEO filings from May 1, 2022, to the present.

RFP 71 Please provide all e-HR reports and/or other LASD documents that specify what positions are to be staffed at Aero Bureau from May 1, 2022, to the present.

(Motion, pp. 12-13.)

Defendants summarize their arguments, at Opposition page 2, as follows: “(1) many of Plaintiffs requests do not seek "personnel records" and are not properly brought as Pitchess requests, (2) the categories of documents are overbroad, and (3) the declaration submitted in support of discovery is insufficient in that it fails to establish good cause and materiality for the production of the requested documents.” Also, Defendants concede that request numbers 48, 49, 53, and 71 are not within the Pitchess motion procedure. (Opposition, p. 5.) Additionally, they indicate that objections apply, including privilege and privacy. (Opposition, p. 11 (Jake Hertzberg Declaration), ¶ 5 (“objections on the grounds of overbreadth, relevance, undue burden, the privacy rights of non-party peace officers, the attorney-client privilege, and the attorney work-product doctrine.”).)

 

Procedurally, a Pitchess motion must describe the record or information types sought with sufficient specificity, and include affidavits showing good cause, including the materiality and a reasonable belief that the governmental agency has the information or records. (Garcia v. Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 63, 70.) An attorney declaration based upon information and belief and containing hearsay may be used to evidence good cause in support of a Pitchess motion. (Haggerty, supra 117 Cal.App.4th at p. 1086.)

 

As to a Pitchess motion, the two-part showing of good cause for discovery is a relatively low threshold. (Zanone v. City of Whittier (2008) 162 Cal.App.4th 174, 187 [in a case re FEHA discrimination, peace officer’s memorandum was not a “personnel record” failing within the scope of a Pitchess motion]. Cf. Riske v. Superior Court (2016) 6 Cal.App.5th 647, 664 [regarding alleged employer retaliation for whistleblowing, employee “established good cause to obtain an in camera review of the personnel records—the TEAMS reports and last two performance evaluations—he requested. Whether and to what extent any information in any of the identified officers' personnel files is thereafter discoverable is for the superior court to determine….”].)

 

“[T]o obtain in-chambers review a defendant need only demonstrate that the scenario of alleged officer misconduct could or might have occurred.” (Warrick v. Superior Court (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1011, 1016.) Only documentation of past misconduct that is similar to misconduct allegedly committed by defendant in the pending litigation is relevant and subject to discovery. (California Highway Patrol v. Superior Court (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 1010, 1021.) “A showing of good cause is measured by ‘relatively relaxed standards' that serve to 'insure the production' for trial court review of 'all potentially relevant documents.’” (Becerrada v. Superior Court (2005) 131 Cal.App.4th 409, 413.) The “‘relatively low threshold for discovery embodied in section 1043 is offset … by section 1045's protective provisions….’” (Chambers v. Appellate Div. Of Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 673, 680.) However, “Warrick permits courts to apply common sense in determining what is plausible, and to make determinations based on a reasonable and realistic assessment of the facts and allegations.” (People v. Thompson (2006) 141 Cal.App.4th 1312, 1318-1319.)

 

A trial court is to conclude whether a Pitchess motion shows good cause for discovery of relevant evidence in an officer's personnel files, and a custodian is to bring all potentially relevant documents to the in camera inspection, and may omit only those that are clearly irrelevant. (Fletcher v. Superior Court (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 386, 405.)

The Court finds that the Motion sufficiently describes the record or information types sought and includes an affidavit showing good cause for each type, including the materiality and a reasonable belief that Los Angeles County has the information or records. (Motion, pp. 25-27 (Arash Aramesh Declaration).)

By the Motion, filed May 27, 2026, counsel moves to be relieved as attorney of record for Defendant FALLAS BORROWERS IV, LLC, by declaring, in the form Declaration, at paragraph 2: “There has been a breakdown in the relationship.”

The Court has ruled upon similar Motions, as to other clients, on June 25 and 29, 2026.

Here, the form notice, declaration, proposed order, and proof of service sufficiently comply with the requirements for a motion to be relieved as counsel. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)

Further, moving counsel’s declaration shows a cognizable ground for withdrawal:

·         A breakdown in the attorney-client relationship. (Estate of Falco v. Decker (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004, 1014.)

Finally, no opposing document is filed in order to show any prejudice caused by attorney withdrawal. (See Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(d); Vann v. Shilleh (1975) 54 Cal.App.3d 192, 197.)

Therefore, the Court grants the Motion and will sign the proposed Order counsel submitted.
