DEPARTMENT 514 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV20849    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 514

Mendoza v. Bautista
25STCV20849
Defendants’ Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff 

Tentative Ruling

The motion is denied without prejudice.

Background

On July 15, 2025, Adriana Gonzalez Mendoza (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against Rodrigo Bautista, Enterprise Truck Rental Inc., Crystal ICE Company aka Crystal ICE Los Angeles, and Does 1 through 20, asserting causes of action for motor vehicle negligence and general negligence arising out of a vehicle accident at or near the intersection of East Gage Avenue and South Central Avenue in Los Angeles on September 8, 2024.

On August 5, 2025, Plaintiff amended the complaint to name Ean Holdings LLC dba Enterprise Rent a Truck Enterprise Rent a Car Alamo Rent a Car National Car Rental (“Ean”) as Doe 1.

On August 7, 2025, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed Defendant Enterprise Truck Rental Inc.

On August 20, 2025, Rodrigo Bautista and U.S. Crystal Ice Company (collectively “Defendants”) filed an answer.

On September 22, 2025, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed Ean.

On June 8, 2026, Defendants filed a motion to compel Plaintiff’s deposition. On June 10, 2026, Defendants filed a similar (or perhaps the same) motion with a new reservation number.  The Court treats the motion filed on June 10 as superseding the motion filed on June 8.  In the motion, Defendants also seek sanctions.

On June 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition, along with her own request for sanctions.

No reply has been filed.

Trial is set for December 20, 2027.

Legal Standard

“Any party may obtain discovery … by taking in California the oral deposition of any person, including any party to the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.) Code of Civil Procedure sections 2025.210 through 2025.280 provide the requirements for (among other things) what must be included in a deposition notice, when and where depositions may be taken, and how and when the notice must be served.

“The service of a deposition notice … is effective to require any deponent who is a party to the action … to attend and to testify, as well as to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing for inspection and copying.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.280, subd. (a).)

Section 2025.410 requires any party to serve a written objection at least three days before the deposition if the party contends that a deposition notice does not comply with the provisions of sections 2025.210 through 2025.280. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.410, subd. (a).)

Code of Civil Procedure § 2025.450(a) provides:

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.230, without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

“The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040, or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).)

When a motion to compel is granted, “the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).)

 In Chapter 7 of the Civil Discovery Act, section 2023.010, subdivision (d), defines “[m]isuses of the discovery process” to include “[f]ailing to respond to or to submit to an authorized method of discovery.” Where a party or attorney has engaged in misuse of the discovery process, the court may impose a monetary sanction in the amount of “the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (a).)

Discussion

Defendants made several unsuccessful efforts to schedule the deposition of Plaintiff, beginning in December 2025.  (Huang Decl., ¶¶ 2-7, 12.)  On March 12, 2026, Defendants noticed the deposition for May 14.  (Huang Decl., ¶¶ 8-11 & Exh. A.)  Plaintiff apparently did not serve any objection to the deposition notice, and she did not appear for her deposition on May 14.  (Huang Decl., ¶ 9.)

Defendants then filed this motion to compel.  Defendants, however, skip a mandatory step required by statute: Defendants do not submit with their motion a declaration showing that Defendants met and conferred with Plaintiff or contacted Plaintiff to inquire about the nonappearance.  This is required.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).) 

Under the Civil Discovery Act, Defendants plainly have the right to take Plaintiff’s deposition.  But to obtain a court order compelling the deposition, Defendants must comply with all applicable procedural requirements.  They have not done so.  Accordingly, the Court must deny the motion, without prejudice, on procedural grounds.

As the motion is denied, so too is Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions.

The Court also denies the request of Plaintiff for monetary sanctions.  The Court finds that under all of the circumstances of this matter, including Defendants’ multiple attempts to schedule the deposition and Plaintiff’s failure to object to the deposition notice, it would be unjust to impose sanctions on Defendants or their counsel.

Conclusion

The Court DENIES without prejudice Defendants’ motion to compel the deposition of Plaintiff Adriana Gonzalez Mendoza.

The Court DENIES each side’s request for sanctions.

The Court ORDERS the parties to meet and confer (in person, by telephone, or by video conference) by no later than July 24, 2026, regarding the scheduling of Plaintiff’s deposition.

Moving Party is ORDERED to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV22677    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 514

Vargas v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority
25STCV22677
Plaintiff’s Motion to Set Aside Dismissal 

Tentative Ruling

The motion is granted.

Background

On August 1, 2025, Anthony Vargas (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, City of Los Angeles, County of Los Angeles, State of California, California Department of Transportation, John Doe 1, John Doe 2, and Does 1 through 50, asserting causes of action for motor vehicle negligence and general negligence arising out of an alleged bus accident at or near the intersection of Western Avenue and Washington Boulevard in Los Angeles on August 7, 2024.

On February 24, 2025, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed City of Los Angeles.

On March 5, 2026, the Court, at the request of Plaintiff, dismissed State of California and California Department of Transportation.

On March 5, 2026, County of Los Angeles filed an answer to the complaint.

On April 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed a request to dismiss the entire action without prejudice.  Based on Plaintiff’s request, the Court dismissed the entire action that same day.

On May 19, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to set aside the dismissal entered by the Court on April 30.  The hearing on the motion was set for June 16, 2026.  When Plaintiff did not appear at the hearing, the Court placed the motion off calendar.

On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff against filed a motion to set aside the dismissal entered by the Court on April 30.  No opposition has been filed.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) (as amended effective January 1, 2026) provides for both discretionary and mandatory relief from (among other things) a default, a dismissal, or entry of a judgment. 

As to discretionary relief, the statute states: “The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or the party’s legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against the party through the party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)  An application for discretionary relief must “be accompanied by a copy of the answer or pleading proposed to be filed …; otherwise the application shall not be granted.”  (Ibid.) 

An application for discretionary relief must be made “within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.”  (Ibid.)

Section 473, subdivision (b) also contains a separate provision for mandatory relief from a dismissal, default, or default judgment.  The statute provides for mandatory relief:

“whenever an application for relief … is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect … unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” 

(Ibid.)  An application for mandatory relief must be made “no more than six months after entry of judgment.”  (Ibid.)  “[W]henever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault,” the court must “direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties.”  (Ibid.)

“When relief under section 473 is available, there is a strong public policy in favor of granting relief and allowing the requesting party his or her day in court.” (Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal.4th 975, 981-982.)

Subdivision (c)(1) of section 473 provides:

“Whenever the court grants relief from a default, default judgment, or dismissal based on any of the provisions of this section, the court may do any of the following:

(A) Impose a penalty of no greater than one thousand dollars ($1,000) upon an offending attorney or party.

(B) Direct that an offending attorney pay an amount no greater than one thousand dollars ($1,000) to the State Bar Client Security Fund.

(C) Grant other relief as is appropriate.”

Discussion

Plaintiff moves for mandatory relief, under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), from the dismissal of his entire complaint entered on April 30, 2026.

All substantive and procedural requirements are satisfied.  Plaintiff’s counsel, Jacob Chobanian, submits a declaration attesting to his mistake in filling out the Request for Dismissal form (CIV-110): counsel explains that he intended to dismiss only County of Los Angeles, not the entire action.  (Chobanian Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.)

The motion is granted.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS the motion of Plaintiff Anthony Vargas to set aside the dismissal of the entire action entered on April 30, 2026.

The Court VACATES the dismissal entered on April 30, 2026.

If Plaintiff seeks to dismiss Defendant County of Los Angeles, Plaintiff must file a new request for dismissal.

The Court SETS a Case Management Conference on October 7, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Department 514 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

The Court SETS an Order to Show Cause re Why Plaintiff’s Counsel Should not be Subject to Monetary Sanctions in an Amount of up to $500 for Failing to Serve all Defendants and File Proof of Service Within the Time Periods Set Forth in California Rules of Court, Rule 3.110, for October 7, 2026, at 8:30 a.m., in Department 514 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

Any response to the OSC must be in writing and must be filed no later than September 30, 2026.

The Judicial Assistant is directed to give notice to Plaintiff. 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice to all defendants who have appeared or who appears prior to the Case Management Conference.


Case Number: 25STCV33981    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 514

Hwang v. Ralphs Grocery Company
25STCV33981
Plaintiff’s Motion for Trial Preference

Tentative Ruling

The motion is granted.

Background

On November 19, 2025, Yong Hwang (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Ralphs Grocery Company (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 50 for arising out of an alleged slip and fall incident at a grocery store operated by Defendant on West Ninth Street in Los Angeles on July 12, 2024.

On December 23, 2025, Defendant filed an answer.

On April 13, 2026, Defendant filed a cross-complaint against HRA 9th & Flower, LP and Roes 1 through 20.  On July 8, 2026, Defendant filed a request to dismiss the cross-complaint without prejudice.

Currently before the Court and set for hearing on July 10 is a motion for a trial preference.

On April 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed her first motion for trial preference.  On April 30, 2026, the Court denied that motion on procedural grounds, without prejudice.

On June 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed the pending motion for trial preference.  No opposition has been filed.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (a) provides:

“A party to a civil action who is over 70 years of age may petition the court for a preference, which the court shall grant if it makes both of the following findings:

(1)  The party has a substantial interest in the action as a whole.

(2)  The health of the party is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudicing the party’s interest in the litigation.” 

A motion for a preference must be “supported by a declaration of the moving party [stating] that all essential parties have been served with process or have appeared.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 36, subd. (c)(1).  The motion may be supported by a declaration by the attorney for the party seeking the preference “based upon information and belief as to the medical diagnosis and prognosis of any party.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 36.5.)

“Upon the granting of such a motion for preference, the court shall set the matter for trial not more than 120 days from that date and there shall be no continuance beyond 120 days from the granting of the motion for preference except for physical disability of a party or a party’s attorney, or upon a showing of good cause stated in the record.” 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 36, subd. (f).)  “Any continuance shall be for no more than 15 days and no more than one continuance for physical disability may be granted to any party.  (Ibid.)

Discussion

Plaintiff seeks an order for trial preference under Code of Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (a) based on Plaintiff’s age and health. In order to grant the motion, the Court must find (1) that Plaintiff is over the age of 70; (2) that Plaintiff has a substantial interest in the action; and (3) that because of Plaintiff’s health, a preference is necessary to prevent prejudice to Plaintiff’s interest in the litigation. 

Based on the evidence submitted by Plaintiff, the Court makes each of these findings on this record.

The procedural issue previously identified by the Court has been fully cured.

The motion is granted.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS the motion of Plaintiff Yong Hwang for a trial preference.

The Court ADVANCES the date of trial to October 26, 2026, at 9:30 a.m., in Department 514 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

The Court ADVANCES the date of the final status conference to October 16, 2026, at 9:30 a.m., in Department 514 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

Moving Party is ordered to give notice.
