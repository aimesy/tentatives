DEMURRER & MOTION TO STRIKE TO THE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Demurrer & Motion filed on 4/15/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Bryan Veilman

NOTICE: ok

 

Demurrer is to the 2nd cause of action:

1.      Personal  Injury (Negligence)

2.      Gross Negligence (Government Code 820(a), 815.2)

 

RELIEF REQUESTED IN MOTION TO STRIKE: An order striking Plaintiff’s claim for punitive and exemplary damages and specifically: ¶6 of the Prayer; ¶21, ¶38, ¶39 and ¶4.

 

RULING: The demurrer is sustained without leave to amend.  The motion to strike is moot, in part; denied, in part; and granted without leave to amend, in part. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises from an auto accident that occurred on 12/4/23 between Plaintiff Bryan Veilman’s (Plaintiff) vehicle and a bus owned by Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (Defendant).

 

On 4/25/25, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant alleging causes of action for: (1) Personal Injury (Negligence), (2) Negligence Per Se and (3) Negligent Hiring, Supervision and Retention.  In response to Defendant’s demurrer and motion to strike the original complaint, on 2/27/26, Plaintiff filed and served the operative First Amended Complaint which alleges causes of action for: (1) Personal Injury (Negligence) and (2) Gross Negligence (Government Code 820(a) and 815.2).  After meet and confer efforts failed to resolve the issues Defendant has with Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint, on 4/15/26, Defendant filed and served the instant demurrer to the 2nd cause of action and motion to strike portions of the First Amended Complaint.  Plaintiff has not opposed or otherwise responded to the demurrer and/or motion to strike.

 

ANALYSIS

 

A public entity is not liable for an injury except as provided by statute.  Government Code  815(a).  Government Code 815.2(a) provides that “a public entity is liable for injury proximately caused by an act or omission of an employee of the public entity within the scope of his employment if the act or omission would, apart from this section, have given rise to a cause of action against that employee or his personal representative.”  However, common law claims such as negligence may not be maintained against public entities.  See Hilts (1968) 265 CA2d 161, 169-171; Miklosy (2008) 44 C4th 876, 899.

 

A plaintiff must plead every fact essential to the existence of statutory liability against a public entity.  Lopez (1985) 40 C3d 780, 795.  A demurrer is properly sustained where the plaintiff fails to allege a statutory basis for liability against a public entity.  Tilton (2006) 142 CA4th 848, 863-864; See also Searcy (1986) 177 CA3d 792, 798.  Additionally, a plaintiff must plead with particularity, showing that the complaint against a public entity falls outside any statutory immunity.  People ex rel Dept. of Transportation v. Sup. Court (1992) 5 CA4th 1480.

 

“California does not recognize a separate cause of action for ‘gross negligence’ independent of a statutory basis.”  Eriksson (2011) 191 CA4th 829, 856.  Plaintiff fails to allege a proper statutory basis for his 2nd cause of action for gross negligence.  Plaintiff has properly stated a claim against Defendant for its employee’s alleged negligence based on Government Code 815.2(a) in the 1st cause of action. 

 

The only portion of the motion to strike which is not rendered moot by the elimination of the 2nd cause of action is the request to strike ¶21 which is within the 1st cause of action and ¶6 of the Prayer regarding punitive damages. 

 

In ¶21, Plaintiff alleges:

 

“Plaintiff is informed and believes and thereupon alleges that, at all times mentioned herein, Defendant MTA and his respective supervisors and/or managers, and each of them, were the agents, servants, and employees of the defendants, and were acting within the course and scope of their employment, thereby rendering the defendants, and each of them, liable for the injuries proximately caused by the acts or omissions of Aaron Botel and his respective supervisors and/ or managers, pursuant to California Government Code section 815.2.”

 

Defendant has failed to establish that ¶21 contains improper allegations regarding negligent hiring, training and retention allegations. 

 

Plaintiff has also failed to allege sufficient facts to support a prayer for punitive damages.      

 

CONCLUSION

 

The demurrer to the 2nd cause of action for gross negligence is sustained without leave to amend. 

 

The request to strike ¶¶38, 39 and 40 is moot due to the ruling on the demurrer.  The request to strike ¶21 is denied.  The request to strike ¶6 of the Prayer regarding punitive damages is granted without leave to amend. 

 

Plaintiff has now had two opportunities to plead his claims against Defendant and has failed to adequately plead a cause of action for gross negligence and/or a claim for punitive damages.  Additionally, Plaintiff has not opposed the demurrer or motion to strike or given any indication that he can cure the defects in the First Amended Complaint.

MOTION FOR INJUNCTION/PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

 

Motion for Injunction filed on 3/23/26.  Motion for Injunction filed on 9/9/26.  Amended Motion for Preliminary Injunction filed on 9/14/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Michael Collins

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendant MU Holding Enterprises Inc.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED IN ORIGINAL MOTION FILED ON 3/23/26 & MOTION FILED ON 9/9/26:

 

An injunction ordering Defendant MU Holding Enterprises, Inc. to:

 

1.      Open and assume the LADWP utility account for 15023 Devonshire Street;

2.      Remove the $8,o45 LADWP debt unlawfully transferred into the name of an elderly tenant;

3.      Restore and maintain essential utilities(water and electricity) in the owner's name;

4.      Replace the nonfunctional water heater;

5.      Complete roof repairs and eliminate water intrusion;

6.      Repair foundation drainage failures;

7.      Remediate mold and water-damaged areas;

8.      Replace the pool filter;

9.      Repair plumbing issues;

10.  Replace repair bathtub;

11.  Comply with the injunctive relief pled in the First Amended Complaint (FAC).

 

RELIEF REQUESTED IN AMENDED MOTION FILED ON 9/14/26:

 

A preliminary injunction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure §§526 and 527, Civil Code §§789.3 and 1942.5, Business and Professions Code §§17200 and 17203, and Los Angeles Municipal Code §§165.03, 165.05, 165.06, and 165.07.  Specifically, Plaintiff seeks the following orders:

 

• Enjoin MU Holding from prosecuting, enforcing, or obtaining possession through MU Holding Enterprises, Inc. v. Michael Collins et al., Case No. 26CHCV01621. Alternatively, enjoin MU Holding from effectuating Plaintiff’s displacement through that proceeding until statutory termination requirements are established.

• Enjoin MU Holding from relying upon or enforcing the March 30, 2026 Three-Day Notice against Plaintiff unless it establishes lawful service and compliance with California law and LAMC §165.05.

• Enjoin MU Holding from treating Plaintiff’s tenancy as terminated or Plaintiff as unauthorized without compliance with LAMC §§165.03 and 165.05 and proof of any asserted at-fault violation.

• Require compliance with LAMC §165.05, including the requirement that an at-fault notice state specific facts sufficient to determine the date, place, witnesses, and circumstances of the asserted violation.

• Require MU Holding, as current owner and successor landlord, to establish and maintain electrical and water service under an owner-controlled account or other reliable owner controlled arrangement.

• Enjoin intentional interruption, termination, withholding, or causing termination of essential utility service for the purpose of forcing or inducing Plaintiff to vacate.

• If MU Holding relies upon a termination ground triggering LAMC §165.06, require compliance with all applicable relocation-assistance requirements before requiring Plaintiff to surrender possession.

• Enjoin retaliation, harassment, threats, coercion, or other interference because Plaintiff exercised rights protected by California or Los Angeles landlord-tenant law.

• Grant further equitable relief necessary to preserve Plaintiff’s rights and prevent threatened harm.

 

RULING: The motion is placed off calendar. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

On 11/26/25, Plaintiff Michael Collins (Plaintiff) filed this action against Defendant MU Holding Enterprises Inc. (Defendant) alleging various causes of action relating to Plaintiff’s purported tenancy at residential real property located at 15023 Devonshire St., Mission Hills, CA 91345.  On 12/9/25, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint against Defendant. 

 

On 1/20/26, default was entered against Defendant.  (See 1/20/26 Request for Entry of Default and 1/27/26 Minute Order).  On 7/21/26, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to set aside default.  (See 7/21/26 Minute Order).  On 8/5/26, Defendant answered the complaint. 

 

On 3/23/26, Plaintiff filed a Motion for Injunction which was purportedly served by mail on Defendant’s former counsel on 4/1/26.  (See Proof of Service by Mail Re: Motion for Preliminary Injunction filed 9/21/26).  However, the address where the motion papers were mailed (700 N. Central Ave., Suite 305, Glendale, CA 91203) does not match the address of record for  Defendant’s former counsel (3100 W. Burbank Blvd., Burbank, CA 91505).  On 5/1/26, the Court continued the hearing on the motion from 7/27/26 to 9/28/26.  (See Notice of Continuance and Order filed 5/1/26).

 

On 9/9/26, Plaintiff re-filed the Motion for Injunction listing the current 9/28/26 hearing date.  No proof of service for this filing appears in the court file. 

 

On 9/14/26, Plaintiff filed an “Amended Notice of Motion and Motion for Preliminary Injunction” which also lists the 9/28/26 hearing date.  The amended motion was purportedly electronically served on Defendant’s current counsel on 9/21/26.  (See Proof of Electronic Service Re: Amended Motion for Preliminary Injunction filed 9/21/26).

 

Oddly, before the amended motion was purportedly served, on 9/18/26, Defendant filed and served an opposition to the amended motion.

 

ANALYSIS

 

There is no evidence that the original motion filed on 3/23/26 and/or the motion filed on 9/9/26, which seek the same relief, were properly served on Defendant’s former or current counsel.  As noted above, the proof of the purported service by mail on 4/1/26 sets forth an incorrect address for Defendant’s counsel at the time.  (See Proof of Service Re: Motion for Preliminary Injunction filed 9/21/26).  Therefore, Defendant had no obligation to respond to that motion.  

 

The motion filed on 3/23/26 and the motion filed on 9/9/26 seek an injunction ordering Defendant MU Holding Enterprises, Inc. to:

 

1.      Open and assume the LADWP utility account for 15023 Devonshire Street;

2.      Remove the $8,o45 LADWP debt unlawfully transferred into the name of an elderly tenant;

3.      Restore and maintain essential utilities(water and electricity) in the owner's name;

4.      Replace the nonfunctional water heater;

5.      Complete roof repairs and eliminate water intrusion;

6.      Repair foundation drainage failures;

7.      Remediate mold and water-damaged areas;

8.      Replace the pool filter;

9.      Repair plumbing issues;

10.  Replace repair bathtub;

11.  Comply with the injunctive relief pled in the First Amended Complaint (FAC).

 

The foregoing is different from the relief sought in the amended motion.  The amended motion for a preliminary injunction filed on 9/14/26 and purportedly served on 9/21/26 seeks a preliminary injunction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure §§526 and 527, Civil Code §§789.3 and 1942.5, Business and Professions Code §§17200 and 17203, and Los Angeles Municipal Code §§165.03, 165.05, 165.06, and 165.07.  Specifically, Plaintiff seeks the following orders:

 

• Enjoin MU Holding from prosecuting, enforcing, or obtaining possession through MU Holding Enterprises, Inc. v. Michael Collins et al., Case No. 26CHCV01621. Alternatively, enjoin MU Holding from effectuating Plaintiff’s displacement through that proceeding until statutory termination requirements are established.

• Enjoin MU Holding from relying upon or enforcing the March 30, 2026 Three-Day Notice against Plaintiff unless it establishes lawful service and compliance with California law and LAMC §165.05.

• Enjoin MU Holding from treating Plaintiff’s tenancy as terminated or Plaintiff as unauthorized without compliance with LAMC §§165.03 and 165.05 and proof of any asserted at-fault violation.

• Require compliance with LAMC §165.05, including the requirement that an at-fault notice state specific facts sufficient to determine the date, place, witnesses, and circumstances of the asserted violation.

• Require MU Holding, as current owner and successor landlord, to establish and maintain electrical and water service under an owner-controlled account or other reliable owner controlled arrangement.

• Enjoin intentional interruption, termination, withholding, or causing termination of essential utility service for the purpose of forcing or inducing Plaintiff to vacate.

• If MU Holding relies upon a termination ground triggering LAMC §165.06, require compliance with all applicable relocation-assistance requirements before requiring Plaintiff to surrender possession.

• Enjoin retaliation, harassment, threats, coercion, or other interference because Plaintiff exercised rights protected by California or Los Angeles landlord-tenant law.

• Grant further equitable relief necessary to preserve Plaintiff’s rights and prevent threatened harm.

 

As noted in the opposition, the amended motion is not timely for the 9/28/26 hearing date.  Unless specially set by the court, a motion must be filed and served at least 16 court days before the hearing date.  See CCP 1005(b).  It is not clear when the amended motion was served on Defendant’s counsel because Defendant filed and served an opposition before the proof of service for the motion claims it was served.  Even if the amended motion was served on the day it was seemingly prepared, 9/12/26, it could not have been timely served because 9/12/26, a Saturday, was only 10 court days before the hearing.  The motion was filed the following Monday, 9/14/26, which is also only 10 court days before the 9/28/26 hearing date. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is placed off calendar.
