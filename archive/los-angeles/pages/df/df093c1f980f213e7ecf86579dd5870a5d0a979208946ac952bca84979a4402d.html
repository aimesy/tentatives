DEPARTMENT L LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22NWCV00692    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: L

Plaintiff Truworks Construction’s Motion for Protective Order is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for breach of construction contract. On August 10, 2022, Plaintiff Truworks Construction (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Monique Zaky (“Defendant”) and Does 1 to 30. The Complaint alleges the following causes of action: 1) Breach Of Contract, 2) Account Stated, and 3) Open Book Account.

 

On January 19, 2026, Defendant served Form Interrogatories, Specially Prepared Interrogatories, Demand to Produce Documents and Requests for Admissions on Plaintiff (hereinafter, “Discovery”). (Sperling Decl., ¶ 3.)

 

Plaintiff now moves for a protective order extending the time for Plaintiff to respond to the Discovery for 60 days. Plaintiff also requests sanctions in the amount of $2,600.00.

 

On May 26, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. No reply has been filed as of July 15, 2026.


Legal Standard

 

In regard to interrogatories, Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.090 states the following: “(b) The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party or other natural person or organization from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense. This protective order may include, but is not limited to, one or more of the following directions: (3) That the time specified in Section 2030.260 to respond to the set of interrogatories, or to particular interrogatories in the set, be extended.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.090, subd. (b)(3).)

 

In regard to inspection demands, Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.090 states the following: “(b) The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party or other person from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense. This protective order may include, but is not limited to, one or more of the following directions: (2) That the time specified in Section 2031.260 to respond to the set of demands, or to a particular item or category in the set, be extended.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.060, subd. (b)(2).)

 

In regard to requests for admission, Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.080 states the following: “(b) The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden and expense. This protective order may include, but is not limited to, one or more of the following directions: (3) That the time specified in Section 2033.250 to respond to the set of admission requests, or to particular requests in the set, be extended.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.080, subd. (b)(3).)

 

Discussion

 

The Court initially notes that this Motion was filed by Plaintiff’s former counsel, Mr. Dean Sperling.

 

On February 16, 2026, Mr. Sperling met and conferred with Defense counsel via telephone. (Sperling Decl., ¶ 4.) Mr. Sperling explained that he planned to file a motion to be relieved as counsel due to his health issues and lack of communication by Plaintiff. (Ibid.) Mr. Sperling requested that Defendant grant Plaintiff an extension to respond to the Discovery so that Plaintiff could obtain new counsel. (Ibid.) Defense counsel denied Mr. Sperling’s request, stating that 1) there was no hearing date on the motion to be relieved as counsel, and 2) the discovery responses were necessary in in light of the Court’s July 13, 2026 mediation deadline. (Ibid.) On February 19, 2026, Mr. Sperling filed this Motion.

 

The Court notes that the Notice of Motion states that the requested extension date is “60 days after the hearing on this motion.” (Notice, 2:2.) However, the Motion later states that an “extension of up to 60 days after the hearing on the motion withdraw as counsel for Plaintiff is proper.” (Mot., 11:11-12.)

 

The Court notes that, on March 19, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s former counsel’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel. (3/19/26 Minute Order.) Thus, more than 60 days have passed since the hearing on Mr. Sperling’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel. The Motion is thus moot as to that request.

 

 

On May 26, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition to this Motion. Defendant asserts that a 60-day extension past the hearing on the instant Motion would set the response deadline to approximately September 2026, a date after the scheduled July 13, 2026 mediation and the August 12, 2026 trial date. Further, Plaintiff fails to demonstrate good cause in support of the Motion—Plaintiff does not demonstrate how the discovery is oppressive, harassing, privileged, impossible to answer, or unduly burdensome.

 

No reply has been filed.

 

The Court notes that, in May 2026, Defendant filed motions to compel responses to the Discovery and a motion to deem the Requests for Admissions, admitted.

 

On June 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Substitution of Attorney, in which Mainak D’Attaray was substituted in as counsel.

 

On July 2, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, and denied as moot Defendant’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions, Set One, Admitted. The Court also continued trial to April 19, 2027. (7/22/26 Minute Order.)

 

The Court finds that the instant Motion is thus moot as to those discovery requests.

 

Considering the moving papers and the changed circumstances, the Court finds that a protective order is not warranted as to the two remaining discovery requests. Plaintiff has not demonstrated how the discovery is oppressive, harassing, unduly burdensome, or any other circumstance warranting an extension 60 days past this hearing.

 

The Court further notes that, as to the two remaining discovery requests, Defendant has two motions to compel responses on calendar for August 25, 2026 and October 13, 2026. The parties are encouraged to meet and confer regarding the remaining discovery requests in light of these upcoming discovery motions.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion for Protective Order is denied.

 

Sanctions

 

Plaintiff requests sanctions in the amount of $2,600.00.


In opposition, Defendant requests sanctions in the amount of $1,350.00 based on Plaintiff’s former counsel’s misuse of the discovery process.

 

The Court denies both parties’ requests for sanctions in light of the circumstances. 


Case Number: 24NWCV00192    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: L

I.             Plaintiff’s Motion for Protective Order Regarding Defendants’ Notice of Taking Deposition of Plaintiff and Request for Production of Documents is DENIED.

II.            Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash Defendant’s Notice of Deposition of Jacob Spencer, D.O, is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a negligence action. On January 18, 2024, Plaintiff Gregory Gorman (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants First Transit, Inc. (“First Transit”), Jane Doe 1, and Does 1 to 10.

 

On April 28, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC), naming Loreen Joseph as Jane Doe 1 and naming Transdev Group, S.A., Transdev North America, Inc., and Transdev Services, Inc. as Does 2, 3, and 4.

 

On January 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Second Amended Complaint.

 

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Third Amended Complaint (TAC). The TAC alleges that Plaintiff boarded a bus owned and operated by Defendant First Transit. The bus driver, Loreen Joseph, allegedly accelerated the bus before Plaintiff could sit down, causing Plaintiff to fall to the floor. The TAC alleges the following causes of action: 1) Negligence, 2) Negligence Training and Supervision, and 3) Common Carrier Liability.

 

There are two matters on calendar:

 

-         Plaintiff’s Motion for Protective Order Regarding Defendants’ Notice of Taking Deposition of Plaintiff and Request for Production of Documents (“Motion for Protective Order”)

-         Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash Defendants’ Notice of Deposition of Jacob Spencer, D.O (“Motion to Quash”)

 

On May 12, 2026, Defendants Transdev Services, Inc., Transdev North America, Inc., First Transit, and Loreen Joseph (initially Jane Doe 1) (hereinafter, “Defendants”) filed an opposition to Plaintiff’s Motion for Protective Order.

 

The Court will address each Motion in turn.

 

In regard to the Motion to Quash, the Court notes that Plaintiff does not object to the Notice of Deposition on grounds that the form of the Notice is improper or that the Notice seeks privileged, burdensome, or irrelevant information. Rather, Plaintiff seeks a protective order to quash the deposition, citing Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.420 and provisions describing misuses of the discovery process. Both Motions are thus analyzed under the same legal standard.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Before, during, or after a deposition, any party, any deponent, or any other affected natural person or organization may promptly move for a protective order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.420, subd. (a).)

 

“The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party, deponent, or other natural person or organization from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense. This protective order may include, but is not limited to, one or more of the following directions: ... (2) That the deposition be taken at a different time [and/or] (5) That the deposition be taken only on certain specified terms and conditions.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.420, subd. (b).)

 

A party unsuccessfully moving for a protective order is subject to monetary sanctions unless it demonstrates substantial justification for the motion or other circumstances that would make imposition of a sanction unjust. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.420(h), 2017.020(b).)

 

Meet and Confer

 

A motion for protective order shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.420, subd. (a).)

 

Declarations from the parties’ counsel demonstrate they met and conferred sufficiently regarding both Motions. (Wiggins Decls., ¶ 15; Wells Decls., ¶ 8.)

 

I.             Motion Protective Order

 

 

Evidentiary Objections

 

Plaintiff’s Evidentiary Objections to the Declaration of Aubrey Wells are overruled as to Nos. 1 to 9.

 

Defendants’ evidentiary objections to the Declaration of Herbert Wiggins in support of the reply and Exhibits 1 and 2 attached to the Declaration are sustained, in part, as to paragraphs 7 to 9 of the Declaration, and Exhibits 1 and 2. The Court notes that Plaintiff referred to the deposition testimony of Mr. Isaac Ibarra and Mr. Johnny Dunning, Jr. taken on April 8, 2026, in the Declaration of Herbert Wiggins in support of the Motion. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 8.) However, the Court may not consider excerpts from the deposition transcripts offered for the first time on reply (Exhibits 1 and 2).

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff now moves for a protective order preventing Plaintiff’s deposition. Plaintiff provides the following context to this Motion: On November 26, 2025, Plaintiff noticed a deposition for Defendant First Transit’s Person Most Knowledgeable (PMK). (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 9, Exh. 2.) As of April 16, 2026, Defendants have refused to produce the PMK witness. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 10.) On March 25, 2026, Defendants noticed Plaintiff’s deposition for April 17, 2026. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 12, Exh. 8.)

 

Plaintiff argues that Defendants have obstructed discovery by (1) resisting a full deposition of Defendant Loreen Joseph until Plaintiff obtained a court order compelling her to appear, and (2) refusing to produce Defendant First Transit’s PMK for deposition before Plaintiff sits for his own. Plaintiff moves for an order to “halt [Plaintiff’s] deposition until Defendant allows [its] PMK’s [sic] depositions; provides full answers to overdue discovery responses; and ceases and desists from its abusive tactics, such as taking oral depositions of treating physicians, when their treatment records will suffice before expert discovery commences.” (Mot., 6:20-25.)

 

Plaintiff cites to Rosemont v. Superior Court of Los Angeles County (“Rosemont”) (1964) 60 Cal.2d 709 and Poeschl v. Superior Court In and For Ventura County (“Poeschl”) (1964) 229 Cal.App.2d 383.

 

In opposition, Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s suggested sequencing of discovery is unsupported by the law. Further, Plaintiff offers no explanation as to why Defendant’s responses to Plaintiff’s discovery is related to Plaintiff’s ability to testify. Specifically, Plaintiff’s Notice of Deposition to Defendant First Transit, Inc.’s PMK seeks “information regarding First Transit’s history of operating municipal passenger buses, safety records, operation manuals, safety protocols, maintenance history, and training history.” (See Wiggins Decl., ¶ 9, Exh. 2.) The written discovery seeks information regarding individuals who worked for the Transdev Defendants. (See Wiggins Decl., ¶ 11, Exhs. 4-5.) Thus, Plaintiff’s cited cases are distinguishable from this case. In those cases, the propounding party demonstrated that responses to the discovery were necessary for the party’s ability to testify.

 

Further, Defendants argue that “one party’s ongoing discovery does not freeze or prevent another party’s discovery,” citing Code of Civil Procedure, section 2019.020, subdivision (a) [“[T]he methods of discovery may be used in any sequence, and the fact that a party is conducting discovery, whether by deposition or another method, shall not operate to delay the discovery of any other party.”].) Plaintiff also has not requested any sequencing of discovery under Code of Civil Procedure section 2019.020, subdivision (b).

 

Lastly, Plaintiff has failed to demonstrate good cause warranting a protective order. In other words, Plaintiff does not argue that the Notice of Deposition and Request for Production of Documents is unduly burdensome, oppressive, or in violation of Plaintiff’s privacy rights. Plaintiff’s deposition testimony and production of documents are “reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence regarding causation and damages, the only disputed issues in this case.” (Opp., 8:24-26.)

 

In reply, Plaintiff emphasizes that Defendant First Transit and its management has had knowledge of “safety problems” as early as 2022. (Reply, 2:4.) Plaintiff suggests that Defendant First Transit’s PMK testimony is relevant to support his corporate liability and punitive damages claims. Plaintiff reiterates that Defendant First Transit refuses to produce its PMK while simultaneously demanding the deposition of Plaintiff, his family, and his doctors. Plaintiff argues that courts may intervene in the order of discovery “if it appears that one side is seeking to alter the order, or otherwise to take unfair advantage.” (Reply, 5:11-12.)

 

The Court acknowledges Plaintiff’s arguments that the deposition of Defendant’s PMK is relevant to the issue of corporate liability and punitive damages. However, the issue here is not whether Plaintiff is entitled to take Defendant First Transit’s PMK’s deposition—rather, the issue is whether the deposition of Defendant’s PMK is necessary to be taken before Plaintiff’s deposition to avoid prejudice.

 

“Since discovery proceedings can seldom if ever be conducted simultaneously, it is inherent in such proceedings that the party who secures discovery first may derive advantages by securing information from his adversary before he is required to reciprocate by divulging information to him. Parties should be encouraged to expedite discovery and should not needlessly be deprived of the advantages that normally flow from prompt action. Accordingly, the existence of such advantages alone will ordinarily not constitute good cause for changing the normal timing of discovery or justify a conclusion that such timing will result in ‘annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression.’ ”

(Rosemont, supra, 60 Cal.2d at p. 714.)

 

In both Rosemont and Poeschl, cited by Plaintiff in his Motion, the Court of Appeal found it proper for the trial court to sequence discovery in a manner that did not disadvantage the plaintiff. However, Rosemont and Poeschl are distinguishable to this case on their facts. In both cases, the opposing party sought to withhold records of prior statements by the deponent until after the deponent sat for deposition. The trial courts found good cause to delay the depositions until after the deponents could review the recordings of their prior statements that were already in their opponents’ possession, finding an unfair advantage would accrue if the opposing party did not disclose recordings before the depositions were taken.

 

Here, Plaintiff does not identify a specific prejudice that will result from taking his deposition before the deposition of Defendant First Transit’s PMK. Plaintiff only complains of unfairness. Specifically, Plaintiff contends that 1) Defendants have withheld other discovery, including a prior-noticed PMK deposition, and 2) Defendants should not be permitted to depose Plaintiff while resisting Plaintiff’s discovery. But unfairness is not good cause, by itself, to delay the discovery process. Unlike in Rosemont and Poeschl, Plaintiff does not contend that Defendants are withholding information he requires to refresh his recollection of events or that might be used to impeach him at trial or during his deposition.

 

Further, the Court notes that Defendants have stipulated to duty and breach—the only remaining issues are causation and damages. These issues will depend primarily on those with percipient knowledge of the incident. Disputes about other discovery do not warrant delaying or preventing Plaintiff’s deposition now that the relevant issues have been narrowed by stipulation.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion for Protective Order Regarding Defendants’ Notice of Taking Deposition of Plaintiff and Request for Production of Documents is DENIED.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to appear for a deposition and produce the requested documents. The parties shall meet and confer to determine the date, time, and location of the deposition, which shall take place no later than 30 days from the date of the Court’s issuance of this order. The date may be extended by agreement of the parties.

 

Sanctions

 

In the opposition, Defendants request sanctions in the amount of $2,600.00. Plaintiff objects to the form of the sanctions request.

 

Defendants contend Plaintiff’s motion is not substantially justified because it has no legal basis. Although Plaintiff’s Motion is unsuccessful, the Court disagrees that it has no legal basis. Rosemont and Poeschl are distinguishable, but Plaintiff’s interpretation of the holdings are reasonable.

 

Thus, Defendants’ request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

II.            Motion to Quash

 

Evidentiary Objections

 

Plaintiff’s Evidentiary Objections to the Declaration of Aubrey Wells are overruled as to Nos. 1 to 9.

 

Discussion

 

On April 1, 2026, Defendants noticed the deposition of Jacob Spencer, D.O. (hereinafter, “Dr. Spencer”) for April 23, 2026. (Wiggins Decl., ¶ 12, Exh. 7.)

 

Plaintiff now moves to quash the Notice of Deposition. Plaintiff requests that the Court “block any deposition by Defendant until Plaintiff can depose Defendant’s PMK.” (Mot., 11:10-11.) In support of this Motion, Plaintiff makes similar arguments as he did in support of the Motion for Protective Order. Specifically, Plaintiff argues that Defendants should not be able to take the deposition of Plaintiff’s medical provider, where Defendants have been stonewalling discovery.

 

Plaintiff further argues that standard for health care provider discovery is to subpoena records of treaters first, and then take oral depositions of designated experts when expert discovery open. Plaintiff suggests that it is against custom for Defendants to take oral depositions of every treating physician before the opening of expert discovery. Plaintiff argues that such practice is “excessive, and apparently meant to intimidate Plaintiff, cause delay, and drive up costs […].” (Mot., 10:24.)

 

In opposition, Defendants reiterate that “[d]iscovery may proceed in any sequence, and one party’s pending discovery does not operate to delay another party’s properly noticed deposition.” (Opp., 2:18-19.) In support, Defendants make similar arguments as they did in its opposition to the Motion for Protective Order. Further, Defendants argue that Plaintiff makes no showing of defects in the notice, privilege, or undue burden. Defendants are entitled to depose Plaintiff’s treating witnesses as relevant fact witnesses for the issues of causation and damages.

 

Additionally, Defendants assert that Plaintiff’s stated “standard for health care provider discovery” is unsupported by any authority. Defendants are able to depose Plaintiff’s treating physicians to establish the factual record. Such factual record would then be analyzed by expert witnesses. Thus, Plaintiff’s contention that physician depositions should be delayed until expert discovery would “invert that process and impede Defendants’ ability to develop the factual record needed for expert work.” (Opp., 10:5-6.)

 

In reply, Plaintiff reiterates that, in attempting to depose Plaintiff’s treating doctors out of the proper sequence, “Defendants seek to drive up costs, and intimidate Plaintiff into an unfair, unreasonable, and unconscionable settlement.” (Reply, 5:3-4.) The Court should quash the deposition to prevent abuse of the discovery process.

 

The Court acknowledges Plaintiff’s concerns. However, for the reasons stated in the Motion on Protective Order, the Court find that sequencing discovery in the manner suggested by Plaintiff is not appropriate under the circumstances.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash is DENIED.

 

The parties are encouraged to meet and confer regarding the remaining Motions to Quash on calendar.

 

Sanctions

 

In the opposition, Defendants request sanctions in the amount of $1,402.50.

 

For the same reasons stated the Motion for Protective Order, the Court denies Defendants’ request for sanctions.

Case Number: 24NWCV03358    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: L

Defendants Maria S. Rodriguez and Galo P. Rodriguez’s Demurrer to the fourth cause of action in the FAC is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

 

Defendants’ Motion to Strike is GRANTED without leave to amend, in part, and DENIED, in part.

 

Defendants to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a personal injury action regarding an alleged battery that occurred on September 26, 2022 . On September 24, 2024, Plaintiff Brian Botelho (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Chris Hammond, Maria S. Rodriguez and Galo P. Rodriguez, as individuals and as Trustees of the Rodriguez Family Trust, and Does 1 to 10.

 

On February 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC). The FAC alleges that Defendant Chris Hammond and Plaintiffs were neighbors. The parties were engaged in a conversation regarding building a wall between their properties when Defendant Hammond punched Plaintiff in the face (“Incident”).

 

The FAC alleges that, four months after the alleged Incident, Defendant Hammond transferred real property located at 13042 Premier Avenue Downey, CA 90242 (“Subject Property”) to Defendants Maria S. Rodriguez and Galo P. Rodriguez, as individuals and as Trustees of the Rodriguez Family Trust (hereinafter, “Rodriguez Defendants”). (FAC, ¶ 19, Exh. A.) The Subject Property consisted of the only asset Defendant Hammond owned that could have been liened to satisfy any monetary damages related to the Incident. Thus, the FAC alleges that Defendants “fraudulently and unlawfully agreed and conspired together to conceal the true ownership of the real property with intent to defraud creditors of [Defendant Hammond], and especially plaintiff […].” (FAC, ¶ 24.)

 

The FAC further alleges that, in the case it is determined that the Subject Property is owned by Rodriguez Defendants, they were the landlord of Defendant Hammond. The FAC alleges that Rodriguez Defendants should have known of Defendant Hammon’s “violent temperament and disposition” and “they should never have allowed him to rent and/or occupy” the Subject Property. (FAC, ¶ 31.) In doing so, they were negligent.

 

The FAC alleges the following causes of action:

 

1.    Battery (against Defendant Hammond)

2.    Negligence (against Defendant Hammond)

3.    Fraudulent Transfer (against all Defendants)

4.    Negligent (against Rodriguez Defendants)

 

Rodriguez Defendants now demur to the fourth cause of action. Defendant also move to strike portions of third cause of action.

 

Evidentiary Objections

 

Plaintiff’s Objection to the Declaration of Galo P. Rodriguez is sustained as improper extrinsic evidence that cannot be considered on Demurrer.

 

Requests for Judicial Notice

 

Rodriguez Defendants’ Request for Judicial Notice of the following documents are granted, as officially recorded documents:

 

-      Exhibit A – Trust Transfer Deed, signed and dated on December 10, 2020, recorded on January 15, 2021, granting title of the Subject Property to Defendant Hammond.

-      Exhibit B – Trust Transfer Deed, signed and dated on December 9, 2022, recorded on December 28, 2022, granting title of the Subject Property to Rodriguez Defendants.


Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, subdivision (a) and section 435.5 require that the parties meet and confer “in person,¿by telephone, or by video conference.” 

The parties were unable to meet and confer within the required time period, so Defense counsel filed a declaration for automatic extension on April 6, 2026. (Murdock Decl., ¶ 4.) On April 7, 2026, the parties met and conferred regarding the issues in the instant Demurrer and Motion to Strike. (Murdock Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

Although Defense counsel does not specific the means by which the parties met and conferred, the Court will nevertheless address the merits of the Demurrer and Motion to Strike in the interests of judicial efficiency.

 

Discussion

 

Rodriguez Defendants now demur to the fourth cause of action for negligence.

 

In order to state a claim for negligence, Plaintiff must allege the elements of (1) “the existence of a legal duty of care,” (2) “breach of that duty,” and (3) “proximate cause resulting in an injury.” (McIntyre v. Colonies-Pacific, LLC (2014) 228 Cal.App.4th 664, 671.) 

 

Firstly, the FAC alleges that Rodriguez Defendants owed a duty to Plaintiff based on their alleged ownership of the Subject Property where the Incident occurred. However, Defendants did not own the Subject Property at the time of the Incident. Defendant Hammond owned the Subject Property at the time of the Incident, on September 26, 2022, after being granted the Property on December 10, 2020. (See RJN, Exh. A.) Defendants obtained the Subject Property on December 9, 2022. (See RJN, Exh. B.) The third cause of action and Exhibit A in the FAC admit that Defendant Hammond owned the Subject Property at the time of the Incident.

 

Further, the FAC alleges no specific facts that Rodriguez Defendants had actual or constructive knowledge of any violent propensity on the part of Defendant Hammond prior to the Incident—i.e., foreseeability.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that FAC adequately alleges all elements of negligence. Plaintiff asserts that the FAC may plead alternative theories. The FAC alleges fraudulent transfer under the Uniform Fraudulent Transactions Act (UVTA) in the third cause of action. The Court may find, in later proceedings, that the transfer was dated December 10, 2020, and recorded on January 15, 2021, purporting to transfer the Subject Property from Rodriguez Defendants to Defendant Hammond and his wife, (RJN, Exh. A) is invalid as a fraudulent transfer. If that transfer deed is declared invalid, then it would be shown that Defendants owned the Subject Property on the date of the Incident.

 

Thus, the FAC sufficiently alleges Rodriguez Defendants owned a duty to Plaintiff based on their ownership of the Subject Property. (See FAC, ¶ 31.) The FAC further alleges that Defendants breached their duty by allowing Defendant Hammond to rent the Subject Property where they knew or should have known of his violent temperament. (Ibid.) The FAC adequately alleges causation, alleging that Defendants’ decision to rent the Subject Property to Defendant Hammond brought him into contact with Plaintiff. (Ibid.) Plaintiff was damaged as a result. (Ibid.)

 

In reply, Rodriguez Defendants argue that the opposition relies on speculation regarding possible ruling of the Court, not specific allegations regarding Defendants’ ownership. Defendants argue that the fraudulent transfer cause of action focuses on the later December 9, 2022 transfer from the Hammonds to Defendants. (See FAC, ¶¶ 20, 22-24.) The fraudulent transfer cause of action alleges that this transfer occurred after Plaintiff’s claim arose and was made to hinder Plaintiff’s ability to collect against Defendant Hammond. (See FAC, ¶¶ 21-26.) Thus, the FAC does not plead any facts that the January 15, 2021 transfer deed, transferring the Subject Property from Defendants to the Hammonds, was void or ineffective.

 

The negligence cause of action is based on the following allegation: “Assuming that the court were to determine that the residence located at 13042 Premier Avenue Downey, CA 90242, were owned by the RODRIGUEZ defendants, was not owned by defendants CHRIS and MONICA on date of injury, then they were the land lord [sic] of defendant CHRIS.” (FAC, ¶ 31.)

 

The Court agrees with Rodiguez Defendants. The third cause of action is based on the alleged fraudulent transfer from Defendant Hammond to Defendants, dated December 9, 2022. (See FAC, ¶¶ 20-26.) The December 9, 2022 transfer is the only exhibit attached to the FAC. (See Exh. A.) There is no mention of earlier transfer from Defendants to Defendant Hammond. Therefore, there is no basis on which the Court could hypothetically rule the transfer from Defendants to Defendant Hammond is void, and that Defendants owned the Subject Property on the date of the Incident. The Court finds that the FAC fails to allege any duty owned by Defendants to Plaintiff.

 

Further, the Court finds that the FAC’s allegations showing breach and causation are conclusory. Even if it is accepted that Defendants rented to Defendant Hammond, the fact that they “brought [Defendant Hammond] into contact with Plaintiff” (FAC, ¶ 31) is insufficient to establish proximate causation. There are no specific allegations showing that Rodriguez Defendants knew or should have known of Defendant Hammond’s propensity for violence.

 

The Court is not persuaded that Plaintiff can amend the FAC to correct such deficiency.

 

Accordingly, the Demurrer to the fourth cause of action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

 

Motion to Strike

 

Code of Civil Procedure, section 436 permits trial courts to strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in a pleading:

 

“The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:

(a)¿Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.

(b)¿Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 436.)

 

Rodriguez Defendants seek to strike the following portions of the third cause of action:

 

-         All references to Civil Code section 3439.4 (a)(2);

-         All references to Civil Code section 3439.07, subd. (a)(1);

-         All references to Civil Code section 1227; and

-         The phrase “and for Constructive Trust.”

 

          Constructive Trust

 

“A constructive trust is an involuntary equitable trust created by operation of law as a remedy to compel the transfer of property from the person wrongfully holding it to the rightful owner. [Citations.] The essence of the theory of constructive trust is to prevent unjust enrichment and to prevent a person from taking advantage of his or her own wrongdoing. [Citations.] [¶] ... [A] constructive trust may only be imposed where the following three conditions are satisfied: (1) the existence of a¿res¿(property or some interest in property); (2) the¿right¿of a complaining party to that res; and (3) some¿wrongful¿acquisition or detention of the res by another party who is not entitled to it. [Citations.]” (Communist Party v. 522 Valencia, Inc.¿(1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 980, 990-991.)

 

Rodriguez Defendants argue that the statement “and for Constructive Trust” is vague and is based on no statutory authority. The FAC only alleges the common law elements of a constructive trust. (FAC, ¶ 28.) However, the FAC fails to allege “what Plaintiff is asking for, when a Constructive Trust would be appropriate, or what facts Plaintiff intends to use to get a Constructive Trust.” (Mot., 6:24-25.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the only statute authorizing constructive trust as a remedy for fraudulent transfer is Civil Code section 2224 [“One who gains a thing by fraud, accident, mistake, undue influence, the violation of a trust, or other wrongful act, is, unless he or she has some other and better right thereto, an involuntary trustee of the thing gained, for the benefit of the person who would otherwise have had it.]. Plaintiff argues that there is no legal authority requiring a plaintiff to the specific statute or caselaw in the complaint.

 

The FAC alleges the following in conjunction with the third cause of action:

 

“Based on the wrongful acts complained above, [Plaintiff] seeks for a remedy of a constructive trust. [Plaintiff] has a right to the res, in that he has a potential judgment lien claim against the property to satisfy any damages related to the assault by [Defendant Hammond]. The RODRIGUEZ defendants wrongfully obtained title and ownership to the real property as herein alleged. The RODRIGUEZ defendants would be unjustly enriched at Plaintiff's expense. Equity compels the property to be restored to [Defendant Hammond] because the RODRIGUEZ defendants or any Successor Trustee of the Rodriguez Family Trust, dated January 10, 2024, are not justly entitled to own the property.”

 

(FAC, ¶ 28.)

 

The Court finds that the third cause of action’s inclusion of a request for constructive trust as an equitable remedy is not so unclear that Rodriguez Defendants cannot ascertain whether what is being alleged against them.

 

Accordingly, the Motion to Strike as to this portion is DENIED.

 

          Civil Code section 1227

 

Civil Code section 1227 states the following: “Every instrument, other than a will, affecting an estate in real property, including every charge upon real property, or upon its rents or profits, made with intent to defraud prior or subsequent purchasers thereof, or incumbrancers thereon, is void as against every purchaser or incumbrancer, for value, of the same property, or the rents or profits thereof.” (Civ. Code, § 1227.)

 

Rodriguez Defendants argue Civil Code section 1227 only applies to “prior or subsequent purchasers.” This time, Plaintiff is neither a prior nor subsequent purchaser for value. Thus, he cannot invoke such statute.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the Civil Code section 1227 claim is offered as an alternative theory of fraudulent transfer. There is no conflict with the UVTA. Plaintiff argues that the statute “applies to any deed holder who is indebted to another, whether there is a judgment lien or not.” (Opp., 3:20-21.)

 

“A judgment plaintiff has a mere general lien upon the real property of the judgment defendant. Such lien is an encumbrance, but the original judgment plaintiff is not ordinarily an encumbrancer for a valuable consideration. . .The phrase ‘encumbrancers in good faith and for a valuable consideration,’ means persons who have taken or purchased a lien, or perhaps merely the means of obtaining one, and who have parted with something of value in consideration thereof. The payment of the money, or the parting with something of value, is essential.” (Fulkerson v. Stiles (1909) 156 Cal. 703, 705–706.)

 

The Court finds that there are no allegations that Plaintiff is a purchaser or encumbrancer for value. At most, Plaintiff has a potential judgment lien claim against Defendant Hammond. Thus, the Court finds that Civil Code section 1227 does not apply.

 

Accordingly, Motion to Strike is GRANTED without leave to amend as to this portion.

 

          Civil Code section 3439.4

 

Civil Code section 3439.4 states the following:

 

“(a) A transfer made or obligation incurred by a debtor is voidable as to a creditor, whether the creditor's claim arose before or after the transfer was made or the obligation was incurred, if the debtor made the transfer or incurred the obligation as follows:

(1) With actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud any creditor of the debtor.

(2) Without receiving a reasonably equivalent value in exchange for the transfer or obligation, and the debtor either:

(A) Was engaged or was about to engage in a business or a transaction for which the remaining assets of the debtor were unreasonably small in relation to the business or transaction.

(B) Intended to incur, or believed or reasonably should have believed that the debtor would incur, debts beyond the debtor’s ability to pay as they became due.”

 

(Civ. Code, § 3439.04, subd. (a), emphasis added.)

 

Rodriguez Defendant argue that the FAC fails to allege facts sufficient to establish that Defendant Hammond “[i]ntended to incur, or believed or reasonably should have believed that the debtor would incur, debts beyond the debtor’s ability to pay as they became due.” (Civ. Code, § 3439.04, subd. (a)(2)(B).) Specifically, the FAC fails to allege Defendant Hammond’s financial status was such that he reasonably should have believed his debts would exceed his ability to pay debts arising from this action—i.e., that this suit would result in his insolvency.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the FAC sufficiently alleges Defendant Hammond’s financial condition. The FAC alleges that Plaintiff believes “there would be equity in the real property to satisfy monetary claims concerning the assault cause of action,” and the Subject Property “would have been the only asset [Defendant Hammond] would have owned that could have been liened to satisfy any monetary damages related to the assault count alleged in the First Cause of Action of this complaint.” (FAC, ¶ 20.) It can be assumed that Defendant Hammond is insolvent where he transferred his only asset to Rodriguez Defendants. Further, there is no requirement that Plaintiff must state specific allegations of Defendant Hammond’s insolvency in the FAC.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff. It can be inferred from the FAC that Defendant Hammond, in concert with Rodriguez Defendants, transferred the Subject Property without receiving consideration and with the intention to evade debts he believed he would incur as a result of this action.

 

Accordingly, the Motion to Strike is DENIED as to this portion.

 

          Civil Code section 3439.07

 

Civil Code section 3439.07 lists the available remedies for UVTA claims. Civil Code section 3439.07, subdivision (a)(1), cited in the FAC, states that a creditor may obtain “Avoidance of the transfer or obligation to the extent necessary to satisfy the creditor's claim.” (Civ. Code, § 3439.07, subd. (a)(1).)

 

Rodriguez Defendants argue that, since Plaintiff’s Civil Code section 3439.04 and Civil Code section 1227 claims fail, the reference to Civil Code section 3439.07 should be stricken.

 

Because the Court has found that the Plaintiff’s Civil Code section 3439.04 adequately states a claim, the reference to Civil Code section 3439.07 regarding remedies is also viable.

 

Accordingly, the Motion to Strike is DENIED as to this portion.


Case Number: 24NWCV03832    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: L

Defendant Estate of Roman Rodriguez’s Motion to Continue Trial is DENIED.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a motor vehicle negligence action regarding an accident that occurred on December 26, 2022. On October 21, 2024, Plaintiff Maria Prieto, individually and as successor-in-interest to William Joseph Prieto, (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Estate of Roman Rodriguez, by and through the administrator and personal representative of the Estate of Roman Rodriguez, and Does 1 to 100.

 

On December 12, 2024, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC), adding Defendant Josefina Romo De Sharp as a defendant. The FAC alleges two causes of action for 1) Wrongful Death and 2) Survival Claims.

 

At the June 11, 2025 Case Management Conference, jury trial was set for June 1, 2026. (6/11/26 Minute Order.)

 

On March 4, 2026, Defendant Estate of Roman Rodriguez (hereinafter, “Defendant”) filed a Substitution of Attorney, substituting in current counsel.

 

On April 17, 2026, the Court denied, in part, and granted, in part, Defendant’s Ex Parte Application to Stay all Proceedings in this Action of in the Alternative to Continue the Trial Final Status Conference and Related Discovery/Motion Dates and Deadlines. Jury trial was continued to the current date of August 17, 2026. (4/17/26 Minute Order.)

 

Defendant now moves to continue trial and all related dates for at least 6 months.


Legal Standard

 

“Although continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332(c). The Court, in making its determination to continue the trial should consider the following factors: “(1) The proximity of the trial date; (2) Whether there was any previous continuance, extension of time, or delay of trial due to any party (3) The length of the continuance requested; (4) The unavailability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion or application for a continuance; (5) The prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; (6) If the case is entitled to a preferential trial setting, the reasons for that status and whether the need for a continuance outweighs the need to avoid delay; (7) The court’s calendar and the impact of granting a continuance on other pending trials; (8) Whether trial counsel is engaged in another trial; (9) Whether all parties have stipulated to a continuance; (10) Whether the interests of justice are best served by a continuance, by the trial of the matter, or by imposing conditions on the continuance; and (11) Any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332(d).)

 

Discussion

 

Defendant asserts that good cause exists to continue trial because Defendant’s insurance carrier, Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company, (“LMFIC”) filed a Complaint against Plaintiff on April 8, 2026, entitled Liberty Mutual Fire Insurance Company v. Maria Prieto, et al., LASC Case No. 26STCV11213 (hereinafter, “Liberty Action”). (Marchesini Decl., ¶ 4, Exh. A.) On May 19, 2026, Defendant filed a Notice of Related Case in this matter. (Marchesini Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. B.) On June 11, 2026, LMFIC filed a Notice of Related Case in the Liberty Action.

 

In the Liberty Action, LMFIC alleges that a settlement agreement was reached to settle all of Plaintiff’s claims in the instant action, and Plaintiff refuses to abide by the settlement agreement. (Ibid.) Defendant argues that, in the event that LMFIC prevails in that action and all claims are settled, trial in this action will be rendered moot. (Marchesini Decl., ¶ 5.) Defendant is informed that LMFIC intends to file a dispositive motion. (Marchesini Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, Defendant argues that trial should be continued in this matter to allow the Liberty Action to be resolved. Defendant will be prejudiced if it is forced to proceed with trial in this matter before the Liberty Action can be adjudicated.

 

Defendant also notes that Plaintiff has not yet appeared in the Liberty Action. (Marchesini Decl., ¶ 9.) However, Defendant is informed that Plaintiff will be represented in the Liberty Action by the same attorneys who are representing her in the instant action. (Ibid.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that no good cause exists to continue trial based on the Liberty Action. Firstly, Defendant should have been aware of LMFIC’s claims regarding settlement since the settlement was allegedly reached in May 2023. On August 1, 2025, Plaintiff sent a written correspondence to Defendant stating that Plaintiff disputed the existence of any settlement, and that if Defendant intended to rely on a prior settlement as a defense, it was required to plead that defense in its Answer. (Hovhannisyan Decl., ¶¶ 5-6, Exh. B.) Defendant’s Answer contains no defense based on prior settlement agreement. (Hovhannisyan Decl., ¶ 3.) Further, Defendant asks this Court to continue trial based only on a “bare possibility that Liberty Mutual might one day file a dispositive motion and might one day prevail on it.” (Opp., 4:8-9.)

 

Further, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s motion rests on identical arguments offered in support of its April 17, 2026 Ex Parte Application. In ruling on that Ex Parte Application, the Court denied Defendant’s request for a stay and alternative request for a six-month trial continuance. Rather, the Court continued trial for eleven weeks to allow new counsel to get up to speed on the case and to conduct discovery. Since then, Plaintiff Maria Prieto and Officer Roger Cancio (the responding officer) have been deposed. (Hovhannisyan Decl., ¶ 8.) Expert designations have been exchanged. (Hovhannisyan Decl., ¶ 9.) The only witnesses involved in the collision are deceased. (Hovhannisyan Decl., ¶ 10.) Plaintiff therefore argues that this case is trial-ready.

 

In reply, Defendant reiterates that trial should be continued so that the Liberty Action may be resolved first. Further, this action is not “trial-ready” as Plaintiff suggests, especially in light of the fact that Plaintiff has recently filed two discovery motions that are set for hearing after trial.

 

The Court acknowledges that, on May 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed an objection to Defendant’s Notice of Related Case. The Court denied the Notice of Related Case on June 18, 2026.

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff that no good cause exists to continue trial based on the Liberty Action at this time. The Court has previously denied Defendant’s request to continue trial based on the same grounds as this Motion in its April 17, 2026 Ex Parte Application. (4/17/26 Minute Order.) As admitted by Defendant, trial was only briefly continued to allow recently-substituted in counsel to catch up in the case and to conduct discovery. (See Marchesini Decl., ¶ 6.) Defendant’s Motion is merely based on speculation regarding the outcome of the Liberty Action. As noted by Defendant, LMFIC has not yet filed a dispositive motion. Overall, the Court is not persuaded that trial should be continued at this point.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Continue Trial is denied without prejudice.


Case Number: 26NWCV000171    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: L

Attorney Raymond Ghermezian, Esq.’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is GRANTED.

 

Counsel to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for breach of contract. Attorney Raymond Ghermezian, Esq., counsel for Plaintiff Jaime Campos, now moves to be relieved as counsel.

 

The Motion is unopposed as of July 15, 2026.

 

Legal Standard

 

Good cause exists to grant a motion to be relieved as counsel based on any of the grounds under California Rules of Professional Conduct Rule 1.16(b). Rule 1.16(b) provides that an attorney may withdraw based on any of the following: (1) the client insists upon presenting a claim or defense that is not warranted under existing law and cannot be supported by good faith argument for an extension, modification, or reversal of existing law, (2) the client seeks to pursue a criminal or fraudulent course of conduct, (3) the client insists that the lawyer pursue a course of conduct that is criminal or fraudulent, (4) the client by other conduct renders it unreasonably difficult for the member to carry out the representation effectively, (5) the client breaches a material term of an agreement with, or obligation, to the lawyer relating to the representation, and the lawyer has given the client a reasonable warning after the breach that the lawyer will withdraw unless the client fulfills the agreement or performs the obligation, (6) the client knowingly and freely assents to termination of the representation, (7) the inability to work with co-counsel indicates that the best interests of the client likely will be served by withdrawal, (8) the lawyer’s mental or physical condition renders it difficult for the lawyer to carry out the representation effectively, (9) a continuation of the representation is likely to result in a violation of these rules or the State Bar Act, or (10) the lawyer believes in good faith, in a proceeding pending before a tribunal, that the tribunal will find the existence of other good cause for withdrawal.

 

California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1362 requires that the following be submitted in support of an attorney’s motion to be relieved as counsel pursuant Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2): (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on Notice of Motion and Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil (Judicial Council Form, MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (1) (made on Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil (Judicial Council Form, MC-052)); (3) a proof of service evidencing service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) a proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil (Judicial Council Form, MC-053)). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subds. (a), (c), (d), (e).)

 

The court has discretion to allow an attorney to withdraw, provided that there is no prejudice to the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)

 

Discussion

 

Attorney Raymond Ghermezian, Esq. (“Counsel”) filed a Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel. (See Form MC-051.) Counsel also filed a proposed order. (See Form MC-053.) Counsel’s declaration demonstrates good cause for withdrawal based on an irreconcilable breakdown of the attorney-client relationship. (Ghermezian Decl., ¶ 2.) Specifically, “certain significant issues surfaced related to the prosecution of this case, which to date remain unresolved,” and such issues are “fundamental and crucial to the outcome of this case.” (Ibid.)

 

Counsel states that the client was served copies of the Motion papers by mail at the client’s last known address, which was confirmed via telephone, in the past thirty (30) days. (Ghermezian Decl., ¶ 3.)

 

Counsel has attached Proofs of Service indicating that the client and all other appearing parties have been served the Motion papers via mail and/or electronic service.

 

Accordingly, the Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is granted.
