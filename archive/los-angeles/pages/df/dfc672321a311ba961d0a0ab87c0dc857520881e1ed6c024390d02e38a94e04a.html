MOVING PARTY:                Defendant, City of Los Angeles

RESPONDING PARTY:       Plaintiff, Felicia Gale

 

(1)   Defendant City of Los Angeles’ Motion for Relief from Late Designation of Expert Witness is DENIED pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2034.710 and 2034.720.

The Court considers the moving papers filed on April 7, 2026, the opposition brief filed on July 6, 2026, and the reply brief filed on July 10, 2026.

BACKGROUND

Factual Background

On August 11, 2023, plaintiff Felicia Gale (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint against defendants City of Los Angeles (“the City”), Fannie King, Fannie King Trust, Luis Hernandez, Alejandro Lopez, and DOES 1 through 50.  The Complaint includes the following two causes of action: (1) General Negligence and (2) Premises Liability.

On November 22, 2023, the City filed the Cross-Complaint against cross-defendants Fannie King, Fannie King Trust, Luis Hernandez, Alejandro Lopez, and ROES 1 through 10.  The Cross-Complaint includes the following causes of action: (1) Indemnification; (2) Apportionment of Fault; and (3) Declaratory Relief.

On an unspecified date, Robin Jung, counsel for the City, reviewed Plaintiff’s emergency room records and observed that Plaintiff’s alcohol level was recorded on the night of the incident.  (Declaration of Robin Jung (“Jung Decl.”), ¶ 1.)  For reasons that remain unclear, Jung erroneously believed that the alcohol level was measured using Blood Alcohol Concentration (“BAC”), not realizing this assumption was incorrect until a later unspecified date.  (Jung Decl., ¶¶ 2, 4.)

Upon identifying the incorrect assumption, Jung asserts that they “immediately began the process to retain a toxicology expert,” as a toxicology expert would be necessary to interpret and convert the results to the BAC scale.  (Jung Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.)  Jung further states, “It is also often extremely difficult to find experts to retain, as the City does not pay for high expert rates, usually requires invoicing instead of fronting retention fees, and often takes months to pay expert invoices.”  (Jung Decl., ¶ 5.)

On February 25, 2026, Plaintiff served her expert designation on the City.  (Jung Decl., ¶ 7.)  Jung claims that they overlooked this email due to their pretrial preparations for another case, compounded by the absence of their administrative assistant from the office on that day.  (Jung Decl., ¶ 7.)

The deadline for serving supplemental expert designations was set for March 17, 2026; however, the City did not serve its amended expert witness list until March 26, 2026, which included the toxicology expert.  (Jung Decl., ¶¶ 6-8, Exh. A.)  Jung indicates that the deadline of March 17, 2026, was not entered into their calendar and that they did not become aware of their oversight until the amended expert list was served.  (Jung Decl., ¶ 8.)  In this list, the City informed Plaintiff that it “will be filing a Motion for Relief from late Designation of Expert Witnesses, pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 2034.710, shortly.”  (Jung Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. A.)

On April 6, 2026, the City served supplemental expert designations.  (Jung Decl., ¶ 10, Exh. B.)

On an unspecified date, Jung proposed a stipulation to continue the trial to facilitate the completion of expert depositions; nevertheless, Plaintiff’s counsel declined to agree to the stipulation or to accept the late designation of experts.  (Jung Decl., ¶ 11.)

Procedural Background

On April 6, 2026, the City filed the motion.

On April 7, 2026, the City filed the Ex Parte Application for an Order Continuing the Trial Date and Specially Setting Hearing on Motion for Relief from Late Designation of Expert Witness.  The Court granted the ex parte application on April 8, 2026.  (April 8, 2026, Minute Order, p. 1.)  Consequently, the Court continued the FSC to September 17, 2027, the non-jury trial to September 29, 2027, and adjusted all discovery deadlines to follow these new trial dates.  (April 8, 2026, Minute Order, p. 1.)

On July 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition brief.  On July 10, 2026, the City filed a reply brief.

LEGAL STANDARD

Any party may demand a mutual and simultaneous exchange by all parties of a list containing the name and address of any person whose oral or deposition testimony in the form of an expert opinion any party expects to offer in evidence at the trial.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.210, subd. (a).)

“On motion of any party who has failed to submit expert witness information on the date specified in a demand for that exchange, the court may grant leave to submit that information on a later date.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.710, subd. (a).)  The motion “shall be made a sufficient time in advance of the time limit for the completion of discovery … to permit the deposition of any expert to whom the motion relates to be taken within that time limit.  Under exceptional circumstances, the court may permit the motion to be made at a later time.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.710, subd. (b).)  “The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.710, subd. (c).)

“The court shall grant leave to submit tardy expert witness information only if all of the following conditions are satisfied:

(a) The court has taken into account the extent to which the opposing party has relied on the absence of a list of expert witnesses.

(b) The court has determined that any party opposing the motion will not be prejudiced in maintaining that party’s action or defense on the merits.

(c) The court has determined that the moving party did all of the following:  ¶  (1) Failed to submit the information as the result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  ¶  (2) Sought leave to submit the information promptly after learning of the mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  ¶  (3) Promptly thereafter served a copy of the proposed expert witness information described in Section 2034.260 on all other parties who have appeared in the action.

(d) The order is conditioned on the moving party making the expert available immediately for a deposition under Article 3 (commencing with Section 2034.410), and on any other terms as may be just, including, but not limited to, leave to any party opposing the motion to designate additional expert witnesses or to elicit additional opinions from those previously designated, a continuance of the trial for a reasonable period of time, and the awarding of costs and litigation expenses to any party opposing the motion.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.720.)

MEET AND CONFER REQUIREMENT

The City must meet and confer with Plaintiff before bringing this motion.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.710, subd. (c).)  Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040 states that a meet and confer declaration “in support of a motion shall state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040.)  “‘The parties must present to each other the merits of their respective positions with the same candor, specificity and support during informal negotiations as during the briefing of discovery motions.  Only after all the cards have been laid on the table, and a party has meaningfully assessed the relative strengths and weaknesses of its position in light of all available information, can there be a ‘sincere’ effort to resolve the matter.’”  (In re Marriage of Moore (2024) 102 Cal.App.5th 1275, 1293-1294, quoting Townsend v. Superior Court (1998) 61 Cal.App.4th 1431, 1435.)

On March 26, 2026, the City served its amended expert witness list.  (Jung Decl., ¶¶ 6-8, Exh. A.)  In this list, the City informed Plaintiff that it “will be filing a Motion for Relief from late Designation of Expert Witnesses, pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 2034.710, shortly.”  (Jung Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. A.)

On an unspecified date, Jung proposed a stipulation to continue the trial to facilitate the completion of expert depositions; nevertheless, Plaintiff’s counsel declined to agree to the stipulation or to accept the late designation of experts.  (Jung Decl., ¶ 11.)

Consequently, the Court finds that the City made sufficient attempts to meet and confer with Plaintiff prior to filing this motion.

DISCUSSION

Untimely Opposition Brief

The City requests that the Court not consider the untimely opposition brief filed by Plaintiff is GRANTED. (Reply, p. 1:24-27.) 

“All papers opposing a motion so noticed shall be filed with the court and a copy served on each party at least nine court days, and all reply papers at least five court days before the hearing.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).)

Given that the motion is scheduled to be heard on July 14, 2026, Plaintiff was required to file and serve their opposition brief by June 30, 2026.  However, Plaintiff submitted their opposition brief on July 6, 2026, which is three court days past the deadline.

The Court typically exercises its discretion to consider an opposition brief that is filed a couple of days late, particularly when the moving party submits a reply brief.  (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d).)  Here, however, Plaintiff submitted their opposition brief three court days after the established deadline, which forced the City to file a late reply brief only two court days before the hearing.  As such, the Court will not exercise its discretion to consider the opposition brief, as doing so would be prejudicial.

 

Mistake, Inadvertence, Surprise, or Excusable Neglect

The City requests leave to submit expert witness information concerning a toxicologist beyond the established deadline of March 17, 2026, for the exchange of such information.  (Motion, p. 1:24-26.)

For the Court to grant this motion, it must determine “that the moving party did all of the following:  ¶  (1) Failed to submit the information as the result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  ¶  (2) Sought leave to submit the information promptly after learning of the mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  ¶  (3) Promptly thereafter served a copy of the proposed expert witness information described in Section 2034.260 on all other parties who have appeared in the action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.720, subd. (c).)¿

Here, the City has not provided a legal analysis that articulates how its failure to include the toxicologist constitutes a mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  Rather, the City claims that “[t]he late designation was simply a result of mistake, inadvertence, and excusable neglect[,]” effectively placing the burden on the Court to justify its position.  (Motion, p. 6:23-24.) 

Moreover, the Court notes the ambiguity regarding whether the City sought permission promptly after recognizing the alleged mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect, or whether it served a copy of the proposed expert witness information in a timely manner.  Jung asserts that they “immediately began the process to retain a toxicology expert,” yet fails to specify the date when the alleged mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect was recognized or when the retention process commenced.  (Jung Decl., ¶¶ 4-5.)  In the absence of this information, the Court is unable to ascertain the promptness of the City’s actions.

Accordingly, the Court DENIES the City’s motion.

ORDERS

1)      Defendant City of Los Angeles’ Motion for Relief from Late Designation of Expert Witness is DENIED.

2)      The City is ordered to give notice of this Court’s ruling.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

DATED:  July 14, 2026

The Court considers the moving papers filed on May 1, 2026, the opposition brief filed on July 2, 2026, and the reply briefs filed on July 7, 2026.

BACKGROUND

Factual Background

On November 6, 2024, plaintiffs Donald James Vargo and Alejandra Vargo (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed the Complaint against defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (“Volkswagen”), PVW South Bay, LLC, dba Pacific Volkswagen, and DOES 1 through 10.  The Complaint includes the following causes of action: (1) Violation of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act – Breach of Express Warranties; (2) Breach of Implied Warranty (Song Beverly); (3) Violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200; and (4) Negligent Repair.

On February 24, 2026, Volkswagen served Plaintiffs with two sets of discovery requests: (1) Inspection Demand, Set One, and (2) Request for Admissions, Set One.  (Declaration of Chiamaka S. Enemuoh re: RFP (“Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP”), ¶ 3, Exh. A; Declaration of Chiamaka S. Enemuoh re: RFA (“Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA”), ¶ 3, Exh. A.)  Plaintiffs were required to respond by March 30, 2026, without any extensions.  (Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP, ¶ 4; Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA, ¶ 4.)

On April 21, 2026, Volkswagen sent a letter to Plaintiffs’ counsel requesting a status update on the responses.  (Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP, ¶ 5, Exh. B; Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA, ¶ 5, Exh. B.)  Plaintiffs failed to respond to the meet-and-confer correspondence or to provide responses to the discovery requests by the filing date of May 1, 2026.  (Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP, ¶ 6; Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA, ¶ 6.)

Procedural Background

On May 1, 2026, Volkswagen filed these omnibus motions.  On July 2, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition to the motion to deem admissions as admitted, but they did not submit an opposition brief concerning the motion to compel.  On July 7, 2026, Volkswagen filed reply briefs.

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for production and admission within thirty (30) days after service.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.260, subd. (a), 2033.250, subd. (a).)

When there has been no timely response to a Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 demand, the demanding party may seek an order compelling a response.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).)  Failure to respond in a timely manner waives all objections, including those based on privilege or work-product protection.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).)  Thus, unless the party to whom the demand was directed obtains relief from a waiver, they cannot raise objections to the documents demanded.  There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions, fifteen (15) days before trial.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2024.020, subd. (a), 2031.300.)  Likewise, for failure to respond, the moving party need not attempt to resolve the matter outside court before filing the motion.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300.)  Where the motion seeks only a response to the inspection demand, no showing of “good cause” is required.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300.)

“Except as provided in subdivision (d), the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.  If a party then fails to obey the order compelling a response, the court may make those orders that are just, including the imposition of an issue sanction, an evidence sanction, or a terminating sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010).  In lieu of or in addition to this sanction, the court may impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010).”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280, “[i]f a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response,” the court shall make the order deeming the truth of the matters admitted unless the responding party serves before the hearing a proposed response to the requests for admission that is in substantial compliance with Code Civil Procedure section 2033.220.  Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.220 requires each answer to either admit, deny, or specify that the responding party lacks sufficient information or knowledge.  As stated in Demyer v. Costa Mesa Mobile Home Estates, the moving party is not required to meet and confer before bringing this action.  (Demyer v. Costa Mesa Mobile Home Estates, 36 Cal.App.4th 393, 395.)

“The court shall make this order, unless it finds that the party to whom the requests for admission have been directed has served, before the hearing on the motion, a proposed response to the requests for admission that is in substantial compliance with Section 2033.220.  It is mandatory that the court impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) on the party or attorney, or both, whose failure to serve a timely response to requests for admission necessitated this motion.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (a), “The court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.  The court may also impose this sanction on one unsuccessfully asserting that another has engaged in the misuse of the discovery process, or on any attorney who advised that assertion, or on both.  If a monetary sanction is authorized by any provision of this title, the court shall impose that sanction unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (a).)  “Misuses of the discovery process include … (d) Failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subd. (d).)

“The court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed, or opposition to the motion was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1348(a).)

DISCUSSION

Omnibus Motion

Volkswagen is seeking court orders to compel the initial responses and documents pertaining to Inspection Demand, Set One, as well as to have the Requests for Admission, Set One, deemed admitted for the two plaintiffs, Donald James Vargo and Alejandra Vargo.  (MTC re: RFP, p. 3:3-22; MTC re: RFA, p. 3:3-18.)  Volkswagen has erroneously combined four distinct motions into two separate filings.

Multiple motions should not be combined into a single filing.  (See¿Gov. Code,¿§ 70617, subd. (a)(4) [setting forth the required filing fee for each motion, application, or any other paper or request requiring a hearing].)  Combining multiple motions under the guise of one motion with one hearing reservation manipulates the Court Reservation System and unfairly jumps ahead of other litigants.  Moreover, combining motions to avoid payment of separate filing fees deprives the Court of the filing fees it is otherwise entitled to collect.  Filing fees are jurisdictional, and it is mandatory for court clerks to demand and receive them.  (See Duran v. St. Luke’s Hospital (2003) 114 Cal.App.4th 457, 460.)

The Court reminds Volkswagen that all future motions must be filed individually, pursuant to Government Code section 70617.  On June 24, 2026, the Court noted that Volkswagen had submitted an omnibus motion and stated, “Any omnibus filings submitted in the future shall be denied without prejudice.”  (June 24, 2026, Minute Order, p. 6.)  As Volkswagen filed these omnibus motions on May 1, 2026, the Court will not deny them without prejudice.  However, any omnibus motions filed after the June 24, 2026, hearing will be denied without prejudice.  Furthermore, the following orders are contingent upon Volkswagen paying two additional filing fees within ten (10) days of the date of this order.

Untimely Opposition Brief

Volkswagen requests that the Court not consider any untimely opposition briefs filed by Plaintiffs.  (Reply re: RFA, p. 2:20-27.)  The Court DENIES Volkswagen’s request.

“All papers opposing a motion so noticed shall be filed with the court and a copy served on each party at least nine court days, and all reply papers at least five court days before the hearing.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).)

Given that the motions are scheduled to be heard on July 14, 2026, Plaintiffs were required to file and serve their opposition briefs by June 30, 2026.  However, Plaintiffs submitted their opposition brief on July 2, 2026, which is two court days past the deadline.

The Court typically exercises its discretion to consider an opposition brief that is filed a couple of days late, particularly when the moving party submits a reply brief.  (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d).)  In this case, Plaintiffs submitted their opposition brief two court days after the established deadline, while Volkswagen submitted its reply brief within the required time frame.  Consequently, the Court chooses to exercise its discretion to consider the untimely opposition brief.

Motions to Compel

In their opposition brief regarding Requests for Admission, Set One, Plaintiffs have attached their unverified responses.  (Opp. re: RFA, pp. 6-38.)  Moreover, although Plaintiffs appear to have served an opposition brief on Volkswagen regarding Inspection Demand, Set One, they did not file an opposition brief with this Court.  Consequently, it remains undisputed that Plaintiffs did not provide verified responses to Volkswagen’s discovery requests.  Given that unverified responses are tantamount to no responses at all, the Court GRANTS Volkswagen’s omnibus motion to compel initial responses to Inspection Demand, Set One, and deems the matters admitted.  (See Appleton v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 632, 635-636.)

Requests For Monetary Sanctions

Volkswagen requests a total of $3,280.00 in sanctions to cover attorney’s fees and costs incurred from the filing of each omnibus motion, with each motion accounting for $1,640.00.  (MTC re: RFP, pp. 6:10-7:3; MTC re: RFA, pp. 6:10-7:2.)  These requests are made pursuant to the Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010, 2023.030, 2031.300, and 2033.280.  (MTC re: RFP, p. 6:12-20; MTC re: RFA, p. 6:11-19.)

The requests are supported by declarations from Volkswagen’s counsel, Chiamaka S. Enemuoh.  In these declarations, Enemuoh states that their hourly rate is $395.00 and that they have dedicated 2.0 hours to drafting each motion.  (Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP, ¶ 7; Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA, ¶ 7.)  Furthermore, Enemuoh anticipates spending an additional 2.0 hours to review each opposition and prepare a reply brief.  (Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP, ¶ 7; Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA, ¶ 7.)  Additionally, Volkswagen incurred $120.00 in filing fees.  (Enemuoh Decl. re: RFP, ¶ 7; Enemuoh Decl. re: RFA, ¶ 7.)

Considering that the Court is granting these motions and does not find the imposition of sanctions to be unjust, the Court GRANTS Volkswagen’s requests for sanctions against Plaintiffs.  The Court finds the requested amounts to be appropriate.

ORDERS

1)      Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Initial Responses to Inspection Demand, Set One, from Plaintiff Donald James Vargo is GRANTED.

2)      Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Initial Responses to Inspection Demand, Set One, from Plaintiff Alejandra Vargo is GRANTED.

3)      Plaintiffs Donald James Vargo and Alejandra Vargo are ordered to provide verified, code-compliant responses and documents to Inspection Demand, Set One, on or before July 28, 2026, without objections.

4)      Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted as to Plaintiff Donald James Vargo is GRANTED.

5)      Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted as to Plaintiff Alejandra Vargo is GRANTED.

6)      Defendant Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.’s Requests for Monetary Sanctions are GRANTED.

7)      Plaintiffs Donald James Vargo and Alejandra Vargo shall pay Volkswagen’s counsel $3,280.00 on or before July 28, 2026.

8)      These orders are contingent upon Volkswagen paying two additional filing fees within ten (10) days of the date of this order.

9)      Volkswagen is ordered to give notice of this Court’s ruling.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
