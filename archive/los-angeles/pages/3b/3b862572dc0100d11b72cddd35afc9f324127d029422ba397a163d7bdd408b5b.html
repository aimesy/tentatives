I.            INTRODUCTION

Plaintiffs Luxe Pasadena, LLC and Boston Dynasty Trust (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) move for leave to file a Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”).

By way of background, Plaintiffs’ original Complaint was filed on July 5, 2023, and named Amcal General Contractors (“Amcal”) and Mutual Roofing Company, Inc. as defendants. On October 17, 2024, Plaintiffs filed an amendment naming Titan Sheet Metal, Inc. (“Titan”), Art Deck, Inc. (“Art Deck”), and Signature Glass & Windows, Inc. (“Signature”), among others, as Doe defendants. Titan, Art Deck, and Signature were previously substituted into Amcal’s cross-complaint as Roes on October 23, 2023.

Plaintiffs attempted to file a First Amended Complaint on May 19, 2025, but the filing was rejected on May 23, 2025, because Plaintiff failed to seek leave of court. A status conference was held on July 22, 2025, and Plaintiffs were given leave to file the FAC. Plaintiffs then submitted for filing a document titled “Amendment to Complaint (Fictitious/Incorrect Name)” on the docket. The filing was rejected because the document name and document type were incorrect. A second notice of rejection erroneously added that leave to amend was required. Plaintiffs did not attempt to re-submit the FAC, but it appears that the parties understood that the operative complaint was the FAC because the Court’s minute order on July 22, 2025, stated that the answers initially filed by Amcal and Mutual Roofing Company, Inc. on October 5 and December 8, 2023, would be deemed answers to the anticipated FAC.

It does not appear that Art Deck, Signature, and Titan have been personally served with either the Complaint or the FAC.

Plaintiffs filed this motion on August 18, 2026.

Art Deck and Signature filed an opposition brief on September 2, 2026. Titan filed a joinder to this opposition brief. Art Deck, Signature, and Titan argue that they should be dismissed from this action due to Plaintiffs’ failure to personally serve them with any of the pleadings.

Amcal filed a separate opposition brief on September 2, 2026. 

Plaintiffs filed a reply brief on September 10, 2026.

 

 

II.          DISCUSSION

The court may, in its discretion and after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).) A motion to amend a pleading must include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments and must state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted or added, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the allegations are located.  (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a).) The motion shall also be accompanied by a declaration attesting to the effect of the amendment, why the amendment is necessary and proper, when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered, and why the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 1.324(b).) “Although courts are bound to apply a policy of great liberality in permitting amendments to the complaint at any stage of the proceedings, up to and including trial . . . this policy should be applied only ‘where no prejudice is shown to the adverse party.’ [Citation]. A different result is indicated ‘where inexcusable delay and probable prejudice to the opposing party’ is shown. [Citation.]” (Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 47 Cal.App.4th 471, 487.)

The proposed SAC adds causes of action for fraudulent concealment and nondisclosure in addition to unfair business practices. Plaintiffs state that the basis for these proposed amendments is their “discovery from consultation with … consultants that the nature and extent of many Plaintiffs’ alleged defects could not exist in the subject Project absent Amcal’s knowledge and concealment of said conditions.” (Motion, p. 4; Jackson Decl., ¶¶ 8-10.)

The original complaint was filed over three years ago, and nowhere in Plaintiffs’ moving or reply papers do they specify how or when they discovered the facts underlying their proposed claims. Plaintiffs’ experts were retained in June 2023, even before the complaint was filed. In fact, it appears that Plaintiffs seek to broaden their remedies since mediation was unsuccessful. “There is a platoon of authority to the effect that a long unexcused delay is sufficient to uphold a trial judge’s decision to deny the opportunity to amend pleadings, particularly where the new amendment would interject a new issue which requires further discovery.” (Green v. Rancho Santa Margarita Mortgage Co. (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 696, 692 [citing Rainer v. Community Memorial Hosp. (1971) 18 Cal.App.3d 240, 258].) Here, there is no excuse for Plaintiffs’ delay and adding a claim for fraud, which has different elements than the currently-pleaded claims for strict products liability and negligence, would undoubtedly require further discovery. Therefore, the motion for leave to file the proposed SAC is DENIED.

The Court does not consider the arguments made by Art Deck, Signature, and Titan regarding mandatory dismissal due to the delay in service; a noticed motion requesting dismissal pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 583.250 may be made if the parties desire.

III.        CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs’ motion for leave to file the proposed SAC is DENIED.

On April 13, 2026, plaintiff Jie Liu (“Plaintiff”) filed five motions for orders compelling defendant Zhang Yanyan (“Defendant”) to provide further responses to Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, Requests for Admission, Set One, Form Interrogatories – General, Set One, and Form Interrogatories – Employment Law, Set One. The motions were originally scheduled to be heard on August 6, August 26, August 27, August 28, and September 16, 2026.

On August 6, 2026, Plaintiff’s motion regarding responses to her special interrogatories came on for hearing. The Court granted the motion and ordered Defendant to provide further responses but denied Plaintiff’s request for sanctions because the notice of motion failed to identify the party, person, or attorney against whom the monetary sanctions were sought. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

On August 25, 2026, Plaintiff filed amended notices of motion for the four pending motions to specify that monetary sanctions were sought against Defendant.

On August 26, 2026, the Court continued the hearings on the remaining four motions to September 16, 2026, and ordered Plaintiff’s counsel to have a telephonic or personal oral meet and confer with Defendant and to file a declaration no later than 5 days before the next hearing. The Court also ordered Plaintiff’s counsel to give notice of the new hearing date.

On September 11, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel filed a declaration stating that she has made multiple good-faith efforts to meet and confer with Defendant and described written correspondence exchanged between March 30 and April 13, 2026. In Plaintiff’s initial meet and confer letter, dated March 30, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel proposed a telephonic meet and confer to discuss the identified issues with Defendant’s initial and supplemental responses. Another request to meet and confer over the phone was made on April 8, 2026, in response to an email sent by David Yao, Esq. who stated he would be “actively assisting” Defendant with her defense due to their friendship, but would not be counsel of record.

It does not appear that Plaintiff’s counsel abided by the Court’s order because Plaintiff’s counsel’s declaration does not include any description of recent attempts to contact Defendant and schedule a meet and confer in person or by telephone. Also, no notice of the continued hearing date was filed. Accordingly, the hearings on ____ motions are CONTINUED to ______.

The Court observes that when Defendant first interacted with Plaintiff’s counsel around November 2025, Defendant stated that she “prefer[red] all communication be conducted in writing, either by email or text message” and that she was “not able to participate in a phone call.” (Ahn Decl., Ex. 9.) Therefore, Plaintiff’s counsel and Defendant are ORDERED to meet and confer in person or over the telephone and file a joint statement identifying the date it took place, how long it lasted, and whether the discussion resolved any of the issues raised in Plaintiff’s pending motions. The declaration shall be filed no later than 5 days before the hearing. Failure to obey this order without good cause may result in the imposition of sanctions.

Plaintiff to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: APPLICATION FOR SALE OF DWELLING

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

)

 

 

Judgment creditors David Wei Lin and Vicki Weinchih Huang (collectively, “Judgment Creditors”) apply for an order to sell judgment debtor Deyun Shi’s right, title, and interest in two properties.

          The sale of a dwelling to satisfy a judgment requires a court order and is governed by Code of Civil Procedure (“CCP”) §§ 704.10-704.850. A hearing must be held to confirm whether the dwelling is an exempt homestead and to determine the amount of the exemption and the fair market value of the property. (CCP § 704.780(b).) At the hearing, the judge must order the sale of the dwelling, subject to the homestead exemption, unless the judge determines that the sale proceeds would not be likely to satisfy any part of the amount due on the judgment under CCP § 704.800. (Ibid.)

          A judgment creditor’s application for order for sale of the dwelling must be filed within 20 days after receiving notice from the levying officer that the dwelling has been levied. (CCP 704.750(a).)

          There is no notice of levy in the Court’s records or attached to Judgment Creditors’ application and the Court cannot determine, based on the information before it, whether the application is timely.

Moreover, and perhaps more significant, the proposed order setting the order to show cause why an order for sale of the dwelling should not be made does not provide for notice of the application to be given to the co-owner of the properties. If the co-owner is, indeed, the defendant-debtor’s former spouse, the application should address precisely how the former spouse’s interest in the properties is expected to be dealt with upon consummation of the sale(s).

Accordingly, the application is DENIED without prejudice.
