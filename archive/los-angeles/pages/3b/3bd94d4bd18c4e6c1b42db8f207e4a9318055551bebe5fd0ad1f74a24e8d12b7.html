DEPARTMENT 534 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 26SMCV02651    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: 534

 

Background

 

On May 12, 2026, Teresa Vazquez filed this Song-Beverly and negligent repair action against Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and DCH Calabasas-A, LLC.

 

On July 9, 2026, Edward B. Moreton, Jr. issued a minute order providing, in relevant part, “A transfer cannot be granted by way of stipulation. The Court takes no action on the stipulation submitted. Parties are directed to reserve/schedule a hearing for Motion to Transfer in Department 534 at Stanley Mosk Courthouse.”

 

Defendants filed the instant motion on July 24, 2026 to transfer the action from the Beverly Hills Courthouse in the West District to the Van Nuys Courthouse East in the Northwest District.

 

Judicial Notice

 

Defendants’ request for judicial notice of a DMV record is GRANTED.

 

Motion to Transfer Between Districts

 

Standard

 

The Local Rules of the Superior Court of Los Angeles County govern the assignment of cases between its districts and departments. (Code Civ. Proc. § 402.) SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2) authorizes the Supervising Judge of the Civil Division to transfer civil cases from one judicial district to another via a noticed motion on three enumerated grounds: (1) when the case was filed in an improper district; (2) for the convenience of witnesses; or (3) to promote the ends of justice. (SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2).)

 

The Case Was Filed in an Incorrect District

 

Pursuant to SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(a)(1)(A) and the Civil Case Cover Sheet Addendum and Statement of Location, this was subject to mandatory filing in the district where the cause of action arose.

 

The complaint alleges the repair attempts and negligent repair occurred at Audi Calabasas, which is located at 24650 Calabasas Road, Calabasas, California 91302. Plaintiff used this address on the civil case cover sheet addendum in Step 4: Statement of Reason and Address and identified the West District as the proper filing location.

 

“The filing locator on the Superior Court of Los Angeles County website . . . should be used to determine the appropriate filing location.” Inputting Calabasas as the city and 91302 as the zip code splits the proper district using Mulholland Highway as the demarcation line. Causes of action arising from a location North of Mulholland Highway are assigned to the Van Nuys Courthouse East in the Northwest District. The address 24650 Calabasas Road is North of Mulholland Highway.

 

As stipulated by the parties, the case was filed in an incorrect district.

 

Defendants’ Sanction Request is Improper

 

Defendants seek sanctions “in the amount of $5,866.50 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 575.2, subdivision (a).” (Mot. at 8:11-10:25.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 575.2(a) provides, “[l]ocal rules promulgated pursuant to Section 575.1 may provide that if any counsel, a party represented by counsel, or a party if in pro se, fails to comply with any of the requirements thereof, the court on motion of a party or on its own motion may strike out all or any part of any pleading of that party, or, dismiss the action or proceeding or any part thereof, or enter a judgment by default against that party, or impose other penalties of a lesser nature as otherwise provided by law, and may order that party or his or her counsel to pay to the moving party the reasonable expenses in making the motion, including reasonable attorney fees. No penalty may be imposed under this section without prior notice to, and an opportunity to be heard by, the party against whom the penalty is sought to be imposed.”

 

Defendants do not cite to any provision in the Local Rules regarding the proper filing location that “provide that if any counsel, a party represented by counsel, or a party if in pro se, fails to comply with any of the requirements thereof, the court on motion of a party . . . may order that party or his or her counsel to pay to the moving party the reasonable expenses in making the motion, including reasonable attorney fees.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 575.2(a).) By its express terms, sanctions are permissible under Section 575.2 only if a Local Rule provides for them. (Compare SCLAC Local Rule 3.10 (permitting discretionary sanctions for failure to comply with Chapter 3 of the Local Rules). See also Sino Century Development Limited v. Farley (2012) 211 Cal.App.4th 688, 693 n.5 (“Section 575.2 authorizes local court rules to include sanctions for failure to comply with local rule requirements.”).)

 

The request for sanctions is DENIED as procedurally improper. Moreover, the Court would not impose sanctions for the filing error, even if such sanctions were authorized.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and DCH Calabasas-A, LLC’s Motion for Intracounty District Transfer and Monetary Sanctions under Code Civ. Proc. § 575.2 and Los Angeles Superior Court Local Rules 2.2 and 2.3 is GRANTED, in part.

 

This action is ordered reassigned to the Van Nuys Courthouse East in the Northwest District. The request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

All hearings currently set in 26SMCV02651 are hereby advanced and vacated.

 

Notice of case assignment shall be issued shortly.

 

Counsel for Defendants to give notice.

                       


Case Number: 26STUD00941    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: 534

Background of 26STUD00941

 

On January 28, 2026, Pacific Loanworks filed this unlawful detainer action against Walter Berry and Marion Chatmon related to real property located at 4703 ½ Crenshaw (Back Entrance) Blvd., Los Angeles, California, 90043.

 

On July 8, 2026, the court granted a motion to substitute Plaintiffs Richard D. Allsup, Shelly Ann Allsup, Charles Lee Lanzrath, Brian D. McKay, and Robert Cooke for Pacific Loanworks.

 

Background of 26STCV19547

 

On June 22, 2026, Ashlei Antionette Chatmon-James and Marion L. Chatmon sued Pacific Loanworks, Inc., Total Lender Solutions, Inc., Michael W. Gross, Matthew W. Gross, Brian D. Mckay, Richard D. Allsup, Shelly Ann Allsup, Charles Lee Lanzrath, and Robert Cooke asserting causes of action for: (1) wrongful foreclosure; (2) cancellation of instruments (Civ. Code § 3412); (3) declaratory relief (Code Civ. Proc. § 1060); (4) quiet title (Code Civ. Proc. § 760.020); (5) request for injunctive relief; and (6) violation of Business and Professions Code § 17200.

 

The complaint alleges real property located at 4703 Crenshaw Boulevard, Los Angeles, California 90043 was unlawfully sold at a foreclosure sale and seeks to quiet title in favor of Plaintiffs.

 

On July 24, 2026, Judge Tony L. Richardson issued an order summarily finding “26STCV19547 and 26STUD00941, are not related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a).”

 

On August 14, 2026, Marion Chatmon filed the instant motion seeking to relate 26STCV19547 and 26STUD00941.

 

Judicial Notice

 

The requests for judicial notice of the complaint in 26STCV19547 and the minute order declining to relate the cases are GRANTED. (Evid. Code § 452(d).) The requests are DENIED as to the other documents as immaterial.  

 

Motion to Relate Cases

 

Standard

 

Pursuant to Superior Court of Los Angeles County Local Rules, rule 3.3(f)(3), “[i]n the event that the judge designated under California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(h)(1)(A)(B)(C) to make the decision, does not order related any of the cases set forth in the Notice of Related Cases, any party may file a motion to have the cases related. Department 1 [renumbered as of May 4, 2026, Department 534]  shall hear the motion, if the cases are all pending in the Central District or are pending in two or more different districts.” The Court does not address the merits when ruling on a Notice of Related Case.

 

The Court Relates the Cases at Issue

 

On July 24, 2026, Judge Tony L. Richardson issued an order finding 26STCV19547 and 26STUD00941 were not related. The motion is therefore properly before this Court. (SCLAC Local Rules, rule 3.3(f)(3); Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(h)(1)(D).)

 

Cases are related when they (1) involve the same parties and are based on the same or similar claims, (2) arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact, (3) involve claims against, title to, possession of, or damages to the same property, or (4) are likely for other reasons to require substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a).) Chatmon contends the cases are related based upon the first, second, and third grounds enumerated in Rule 3.300(a). The oppositions argue the cases should not be related.

 

The cases do not involve the same parties. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(1).) The cases also are not based on the same or similar claims, (ibid.), and do not arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(2).) Unlawful detainer proceedings only concern straightforward issues regarding the right to possession of disputed premises and the award of damages for any unlawful detention; therefore, issues regarding the property’s title cannot be adjudicated therein because unlawful detainer proceedings are designed to be summary proceedings. (Martin-Bragg v. Moore (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 367, 385.)

 

However, the cases involve title to, and possession of, the same property, (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(3)), and will require a substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(4).) The property at issue in the unlawful detainer action is subject to competing quiet title claims in the civil case and the unlawful detainer defendant has challenged plaintiff’s ownership. (Martin-Bragg v. Moore (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 367, 385 (“a successful claim of title . . . would defeat the [plaintiff’s] right to possession.”).) Additionally, the cases involve similar issues regarding the property’s foreclosure. The Court finds the cases are related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300.

 

Where, as here, “an unlawful detainer proceeding and an . . . action concerning title to the property are simultaneously pending, the trial court in which the . . . action is pending may stay the unlawful detainer action until the issue of title is resolved in the unlimited action, or it may consolidate the actions.” (Ibid.) In the Superior Court of Los Angeles County, a party cannot request consolidation unless the cases are pending in the same department, (SCLAC Local Rules, rule 3.3(g)(1), which is achieved via an order relating the cases. A single judicial officer should determine which of these remedies, if any, are appropriate under the circumstances and resolve all disputes between the parties related to the property. (Harris v. Rojas (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 817, 820 (“The notice allows the trial court to promote efficiency, to avoid duplicative effort, to combat judge-shopping, and to minimize the prospect of conflicting results.”).)

 

The motion is GRANTED.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Marion Chatmon’s Motion Relating Cases is GRANTED.

 

The Court relates 26STCV19547 and 26STUD00941 and orders 26STUD00941 reassigned to Department 415 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse for all purposes.

 

All hearings currently set in 26STUD00941 are hereby advanced and vacated.

 

Counsel for Chatmon to give notice.
