CITY OF MONTEBELLO.

Plaintiff,

vs.

KAZUHIKO YAMAGUCHI, et al.

Defendants.

Case No.:

22STCV24991

Hearing Date:

September 14, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER CONTINUINING MOTION FOR ORDER RECIEVER'S FINAL REPORT AND ACCOUNTING AND DISCHARGING RECIEVER

 

I.           BACKGROUND

Plaintiff City of Montebello (“City”) filed this nuisance abatement and receivership complaint against Defendants regarding the real property at 836 North Vail Avenue, Montebello, California 90640 (“subject property”), on the basis that the subject property had fire hazards, overgrown and dead vegetation, and hoarder-type accumulation of debris, among other issues. Causes of action included receivership, public nuisance, and nuisance per se.

As of November 22, 2022, all named Defendants are in default.

On December 12, 2022, the Court appointed non-party Eric P. Beatty (“Beatty”) as the receiver in this action.

On April 22, 2026, Beatty filed the instant motion for order approving receiver's final report and final accounting and discharging receiver.

 

II.         LEGAL STANDARD

California Rules of Court rule 3.1184, subdivision (a),[1] provides that a receiver must present, by noticed motion or stipulation of all parties: (1) a final account and report, (2) a request for the discharge, and (3) a request for exoneration of the receiver’s surety. Rule 3.1184, subdivision (c), further provides that “[n]otice of the motion or of the stipulation must be given to every person or entity known to the receiver to have a substantial, unsatisfied claim that will be affected by the order or stipulation, whether or not the person or entity is a party to the action or has appeared in it.” 

 

 

 

III.       DISCUSSION

Rule 3.1184, subdivision (c), requires that notice of motion be served on all persons or entities known to the receiver to have a substantial, unsatisfied claim, that will be affected by the order, whether or not the person or entity is a party to the action or has appeared in it.  Here, non-party Rumi Yamaguchi (“Rumi”), as intestate heir to Defendants Kazuhiko Yamaguchi and Mitsuru Yamaguchi, has a claim to the remaining assets that will result in payment to Rumi upon approval of the order.  Beatty has provided a proof of service of the motion and proposed order only on the City.  Beatty has also provided a proof of service on both the city and Rumi of his declaration in lieu of reply and her declaration indicating review of the Sale Approval Order and waiving an appeal of the Sale Approval Order.  Rumi appears to have a substantial, currently unsatisfied claim, that will be affected by the order and so Rumi should have been served with notice of this motion prior to the court issuing a ruling on the merits.  Although there is no standard proof of service of the motion, Rumi’s and Beatty’s declarations indicate her knowledge of the issues.  Under these circumstances, if Rumi appears at the hearing (telephonically is sufficient), confirms her knowledge of the motion and her declaration, waives formal service of motion, and has no other objections, the Court will deem Rule 3.1184 satisfied and grant the motion.

Case Number: 24NWLC05362    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 730

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

Department 730

 

SOCAL LIEN SOLUTIONS LLC

Plaintiff,

vs.

COUNTRYWIDE DEVELOPMENT & CONTRACTING, INC., et al.,

Defendants.

Case No.:

24NWLC05362

Hearing Date:

September 14, 2026

ORDER DENYING MOTION TO EXPUNGE MECHANIC’S LIEN AND GRANTING MOTION TO EXPUNGE LIS PENDENS

 

I.          BACKGROUND

This is real property action arising out of alleged failure to pay for drywall work done to the 1298 W 37th St, Los Angeles, CA 90007, property ("subject property”) by Plaintiff SoCal Lien Solutions, LLC’s (“SoCal”) assignee. SoCal sued Defendants for five causes of action, including breach of contract.

Defendants Campus Rentals LLC (“Campus”) and Countrywide Development & Contracting Inc. (“CDC”) filed a Cross-Complaint against SoCal and New Concept Drywall, Inc. (“NC”) for four causes of action, including breach of contract.

Named defendants were dismissed on April 15, 2026. Doe Defendants have not been dismissed.

On November 15, 2023, NC filed a mechanic’s lien against the subject property.

On March 8, 2024, SoCal filed a lis pendens for the subject property.

On July 2, 2026, Campus filed the instant motion to expunge NC’s mechanic’s lien.

On August 19, 2026, Campus filed the instant motion to expunge lis pendens.

II.         LEGAL STANDARD

California Civil Code section 8480 provides that the owner of property may petition the court for an order to release the property from the claim of lien if the claimant has not commenced an action to enforce the lien within the time provided in section 8460. (Civ. Code § 8480(a).) Section 8460 provides that “[t]he claimant shall commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days after recordation of the claim of lien.” (Id., § 8460(a).) Section 8460 further provides that “[i]f the claimant does not commence an action to enforce the lien within that time, the claim of lien expires and is unenforceable.” It also provides that the 90-day time limit to commence an action to enforce a lien does not apply if there was an agreement to extend credit and a notice of that fact was recorded within 90 days after recordation of the claim of lien or more than 90 days after recordation of the claim of lien but before a purchaser or encumbrancer for value and in good faith acquires rights in the property.

A property owner may not petition for a release order until he or she gives the claimant notice demanding that the claimant execute and record a release of lien claim at least ten days before filing the petition. (Civ. Code § 8482.) A petition for a release order must be verified and include all of the following: (1) the date of recordation of the claim of lien, with a certified copy of the lien attached; (2) the county in which the claim of lien is recorded; (3) the book and page or series number of the place in the official records where the claim of lien is recorded; (4) the legal description of the property subject to the claim; (5) whether an extension of credit has been granted and if so to what date and that the time for commencement of an action to enforce the lien has expired; (6) that the owner has given the claimant notice under section 8482 demanding that the claimant execute and record a release of the lien and that the claimant is unable or unwilling to do so or cannot with reasonable diligence be found; (7) whether an action to enforce the lien is pending; and (8) whether the owner of the property or interest in the property has filed for relief in bankruptcy or there is another restraint that prevents the claimant from commencing an action to enforce the lien. (Civ. Code § 8484.)

A lis pendens is an instrument recorded in the office of the county recorder that gives constructive notice of a pending lawsuit affecting title to the real property described in the lis pendens.  (Gale v. Superior Court (2004) 122 Cal.App.4th 1388, 1395.)  The pleadings in the lawsuit to which the lis pendens refers must describe a specific parcel of real property and must state a “real property claim.”  (Ibid.; Code of Civil Procedure § 405.1[1].)  For purposes of expunging a lis pendens, a real property claim is a cause of action that, if successful, would affect title to or the right of possession of specific real property or the use of an easement identified in a pleading.  (§§ 405.2, 405.4.) 

Where a motion to expunge a lis pendens has been filed, the proponent of the lis pendens carries the burden of showing evidence that the lis pendens is supported by a real property claim which is probably valid.  (§§ 405.30, 405.32; Hunting World, Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 22 Cal.App.4th 67, 70-71 (Hunting World).)  Where a party does not establish the probable validity of their claim underpinning a lis pendens by a preponderance of the evidence, the lis pendens shall be expunged.  (§ 405.32.)   

III.       DISCUSSION

A.         Service

At the previous hearing on the motions, the Court continued the hearing due to a lack of proof of service of the Cross-Complaint on Cross-Defendant Balam Arturo Cardenas Zotelo dba New Concepts Drywall (“NC”). As of August 28, 2026, Campus filed proof of service on NC for the Cross-Complaint, resolving service issues.  

B.          Motion to Expunge Mechanic’s Lien

Campus requests the Court expunge NC’s mechanic’s lien filed on November 15, 2023, as Instrument No. 20230789692 in the Official Records of Los Angeles County against the subject property. Campus also requests an award of attorney’s fees and in the amount of $2,992.80 for bringing this motion.

However, Campus only requests the Court expunge the mechanic’s lien under sections 405.31 and 405.32, both of which only grant the Court the right to expunge a lis pendens. These sections do not apply to mechanic’s liens. Campus has provided no legal basis for the Court to expunge the subject lien. Therefore, the Court denies the motion and attorney fees as to the mechanic’s lien.

C.          Motion to Expunge Lis Pendens

Campus requests the Court expunge SoCal’s Notice of Pendency of Action [lis pendens] filed on March 8, 2024, in this case, against the subject property. Campus also requests an award of attorney’s fees and in the amount of $2,992.80 for bringing this motion. “A court shall order a notice of lis pendens expunged if it determines (1) that the pleading on which the notice is based does not contain a real property claim (§ 405.31); (2) that the claimant has not established, by a preponderance of the evidence, the probable validity of a real property claim (§ 405.32); or (3) that adequate relief can be secured by an undertaking. (§ 405.33.).” (Shoker v. Superior Court of Alameda County (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 271, 277.)

Campus asserts SoCal cannot maintain a lis pendens because SoCal dismissed their complaint, which was the only pleading that supported a real property claim in regard to the subject property. The pleadings in the lawsuit to which the lis pendens refer must describe a specific parcel of real property and must state a “real property claim.” (§ 405.1.) For purposes of expunging a lis pendens, a real property claim is a cause of action that, if successful, would affect title to or the right of possession of specific real property or the use of an easement identified in a pleading.  (§§ 405.2, 405.4.) To determine whether the complaint involves a real property claim, the court considers the allegations of the complaint. (Urez Corp. v. Superior Court (1987) 190 Cal.App.3d 1141, 1149.) [“The issue is simply whether the action as pleaded is one that affects title or possession of the subject property.”] 

Here, it is undisputed only the underlying complaint concerned a real property claim. Campus’s Cross-Complaint only has causes of action for breach of contract, specifically alleging NC failed to complete work under subcontract as outlined by contract. Although the relevant contract relates to work performed on the subject property, it does not affect title or possession of the subject property. Therefore, only SoCal’s complaint is potentially relevant to the lis pendens claim. SoCal’s complaint has been dismissed. Therefore, there are no currently pending pleadings that state a “real property claim” against the subject property; notice is proper for expungement pursuant to section 405.31. It is also proper for expungement under section 405.32, as SoCal has not established, by preponderance of the evidence, the probable validity of the real property claim as SoCal has dismissed its claims. Therefore, the Court grants the motion.

D.         Attorney Fees

The Court “shall direct that the party prevailing on any motion under this chapter be awarded the reasonable attorney’s fees and costs of making or opposing the motion unless the court finds that the other party acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of attorney’s fees and costs unjust.” (§ 405.38.)

Given the fact no party opposes this motion, the Court does not find substantial justification in SoCal maintaining the lis pendens following the request for dismissal, as, at that point, SoCal was aware they had no viable property claims. Therefore, the Court awards attorney fees.

Campus requests attorney’s fees totaling $2,992.80, based upon $2,730.00 in attorney fees and $262.80 in filing costs. The Court first notes, outside of identifying 1 hour will be spent at the hearing on the motion, Campus did not provide information as to time spent on the motion; it is unclear if the allotted time is based upon attorney time or paralegal time. Absent this information, the Court finds the total requested attorney’s fees unwarranted. (In re Marriage of Huntington (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1513, 1524 [“When the trial court is informed of the extent and nature of the legal services rendered, it may rely on its own experience and knowledge in determining their reasonable value.”].) The Court also finds the motion filing fees, nearly $200.00 over the usual request for filing fees, to be unsupported by the evidence. Instead, the Court imposes sanctions of $960.00, based upon 3 hours of attorney’s work at a rate of $300.00 per hour and 1 $60.00 filing fee.

 

IV.        CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, Campus’s motion to expunge NC’s mechanic’s lien is DENIED.

Campus’s motion to expunge lis pendens is GRANTED.

SoCal may not file another lis pendens regarding the subject property without first seeking leave of court.

SoCal and counsel of record are ordered to pay attorney’s fees in the amount of $960.00 within 30 calendar days of this order.

Moving Party is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV27912    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 730

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

Department 730

 

ELENA M. ROSCHDI, et al.,

Plaintiff,

vs.

AGAM PROPERTIES, INC., et al.,

Defendant.

Case No.:

24STCV27912

Hearing Date:

September 14, 2026

ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR ORDER PERMITTING DISCOVERY OF DEFENDANTS’ FINANCIAL CONDITION

 

I.           BACKGROUND

During Plaintiffs Elena M. Roschdi (“Elena”), Nasser Roschdi (“Nasser”), Sara Raquel Roschdi (“Sara”), and Sanam Rebecca Roschdi’s (“Sanam”) tenancy with landlord Defendants Agam Properties, Inc. (“Agam”) and Pacifica Mortgage Fund, LLC (“PMF”), Defendants allegedly failed to make requested repairs, violating Plaintiffs’ implied warranty of habitability. Plaintiffs sued Defendants for seven causes of action, including tortious breach of warranty of habitability.

On May 21, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion for order permitting discovery of Defendants’ financial condition. Defendants filed an opposition. Plaintiffs filed a reply.

II.         Objections

A.         Defenants’ Objections

All of Defendants’ objections are OVERRULED, unless otherwise indicated below:

1.          The objection to Plaintiff’s Exhibit 6 is SUSTAINED as irrelevant.

2.          The objection to Plaintiff’s Exhibit 7 is SUSTAINED as irrelevant.

3.          The objection to Plaintiff’s Exhibit 8 is SUSTAINED as hearsay, and lack of authentication.

B.          Plaintiffs’ Objections

All of Plaintiffs’ objections are SUSTAINED on the basis of lack of personal knowledge and foundation.

 

 

III.       LEGAL STANDARD

A plaintiff may not conduct pretrial discovery into a defendant’s financial condition absent a court order permitting such discovery. (Civ. Code § 3295, subd. (c.)[1].) In making the order, the court must find, on the basis of the supporting and opposing affidavits presented, that the plaintiff has established a “substantial probability” of prevailing on a claim for punitive damages. (Ibid.) A court must weigh the evidence submitted in favor of and in opposition to the motion and find that it is “very likely” that the plaintiff will prevail on his or her claim for punitive damages. (Jabro v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 754, 758.) It is not sufficient for a plaintiff to simply demonstrate a prima facie showing that they have a reasonable probability of prevailing sufficient to defeat summary judgment.  (Ibid.) 

 

IV.        DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs request the Court issue an order, pursuant to section 3295, allowing Plaintiffs to conduct discovery into Defendant’s financial condition. The Court may only enter such an order upon a showing that Plaintiffs have a substantial probability of prevailing on their section 3294 punitive damages claims via noticed motion and affidavits. (§ 3295, subd. (c).)

Plaintiffs’ complaint alleges Plaintiffs are entitled to punitive damages as Defendants oppressively and/or fraudulently refused to repair, maintain, and exterminate Plaintiffs’ leased home, despite multiple requests by Plaintiffs, on the basis that the rental agreement contained an illegal ‘as-is’ clause making Plaintiffs responsible for all repairs. (Complaint ¶¶ 9, 38, 32, Ex. B.) Plaintiffs specifically alleged that, during their tenancy, the subject property experienced a cockroach infestation, rodent infestation, widespread water leaks and chronic mold, a lack of functional heaters, and physical defects, all of which Defendants failed to fix. (Complaint ¶ 10.)

All residential leases contain an implied warranty of habitability requiring that the landlord maintain and fix the property to comply with statutory and regulatory requirements, regardless of other provisions of the lease agreement. (Green v. Superior Court (1974, 10 Cal.3d 616, 629.) Case law holds punitive damages are authorized for tortious breach of the warranty of habitability, one of Plaintiffs’ articulated causes of action, so long as the plaintiff meets the threshold for establishing punitive damages. (See Smith v. David (1981) 120 Cal.App.3d 101, fn 3.) Punitive damages may only be imposed upon a showing a defendant is guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice. (§ 3294, subd. (a).)

In support of their claims, Plaintiffs provided affidavits and evidence demonstrating that Defendants knowingly relied upon the lease contract’s illegal ‘as-is’ clause to avoid making requested repairs pursuant to their obligations under the warranty of habitability. According to Elena, issues arose as early as 2013 and ranged from inoperable appliances to sewage overflows to vermin/mold infestations. (Mot., Elena Decl. ¶ 4.) Elena provided a 2016 e-mail between Elena and Defendants’ agent, Scott Radin (“Radin”), in which Elena notified Defendants that Plaintiffs lacked hot water, violating section 1941.1, subdivision (3); Defendants stated Plaintiffs were responsible for all repairs and maintenance. (Id. at ¶ 5, Ex. 2.) Sara contacted Radin via e-mail in August 2024, identifying plumbing issues, mold growth, and rainwater seepage (violating Health and Safety Code § 17920.3, subd. (a)); although Radin initially expressed interest in reviewing the issues, Radin later responded that Defendants would not make any repairs to the home unless Plaintiffs agreed to a new rental rate (approximately $1,000.00 more per month), relying upon the ‘as-is’ clause of the initial lease. (Mot., Sara Decl. ¶¶ 3-5, Ex. 4-5.) At bare minimum, Plaintiffs demonstrated that as early as 2016, Defendants refused to perform requested and necessary repairs by relying upon an illegal contract provision contrary to public policy.

Defendants rely upon the existence of the ‘as-is’ provision and argues that, at best, this reflects a dispute as to the terms of the contract. Defendants also argue that Plaintiffs testified at their deposition that the initial lease was only intended to apply for a short term while Plaintiffs looked for alternate, more affordable housing. However, neither of these arguments address the fact a residential lease agreement that waives or modifies the implied warranty of habitability is void as a matter of public policy, unless the provision agrees that the tenant takes on the obligation to repair in consideration for a reduced rental rate. (§ 1942.1.) Nothing within the agreement specifies Plaintiffs were paying a reduced rate in return for undertaking repair responsibilities. (Mot., Elena Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. 1.) Defendants have provided no evidence that otherwise demonstrates this obligation came with a reduced rental rate. Therefore, the evidence shows any reliance upon the ‘as-is’ provision was without merit; failure to repair when provided with notice while relying upon void portions of a contract is substantially likely to establish Defendants were acting oppressively and/or fraudulently, providing a basis for punitive damages.

The Court finds Plaintiffs have sufficiently established a substantial probability of prevailing on a claim for punitive damages as to tortious breach of warranty of habitability. Defendants request that any order permitting discovery into financial condition be narrowly tailored to information reasonably necessary for purposes of punitive damages. Section 3295 already outlines the limits of financial discovery: profits Defendants gained by virtue of wrongful course of conduct and the financial condition of the defendant. (§ 3295, subd. (a)(1-2).) The Court adopts the statutory limits of discovery.

 

IV.        CONCLUSION

Plaintiffs’ motion for order permitting financial discovery is GRANTED. Plaintiffs are entitled to serve discovery concerning section 3295, subdivision (a)(1-2).

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.
