ARSALAN HAMIDI

 

         vs.

 

BRETT GAGNON, et al.

 

 Case No.: 22SMCV02125

Related Case No.: 22STCV30718

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 16, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to enforce settlement and enter judgment is GRANTED.

 

On 10/25/2022, Plaintiff Arsalan Hamidi (Plaintiff) filed suit against Brett Gagnon, George B. Gagnon, Geminon Properties, LLC, and Ashley R. Gagnon (collectively, Defendants), alleging: (1) violation of Georgia Code section 14-11-313; (2) fraud; (3) negligent misrepresentation; (4) breach of operating agreement; (5) breach of operating agreement; (6) breach of fiduciary duties; (7) constructive fraud; (8) unjust enrichment; (9) conversion; (10) cessation/withdrawal of memberships and management; and (11) cancellation of written instruments. 

 

On 6/15/2026, Plaintiff moved to enforce settlement and enter judgment.

 

The motion is unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff moves to enforce settlement and enter a judgment in the amount of $165,871.46.

 

            In support, Plaintiff submitted a copy of the Settlement Agreement and Stipulation for Entry of Judgment entered into by the parties. Plaintiff submitted evidence that in addition to repeatedly making untimely payments, Defendant has not made any monthly payments since 4/24/2026. Defendant is thus in material breach.

 

            The Court takes Defendant’s non-opposition to be a concession to the motion on the merits.

 

Based on the forgoing, Plaintiff’s motion to enforce settlement and enter judgment is granted.

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 24STCV31938    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

STEPHON WATFORD

 

         vs.

 

PROMISE ASSISTED LIVING, LLC   

 

 Case No.: 24STCV31938 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 16, 2026

 

Defendants’ motion to compel Plaintiff’s mental examination is GRANTED.

 

On 12/4/2024, Plaintiff Stephon Watford (Plaintiff) initiated this action. On 11/3/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint against Promise Assisted Living, LLC, Promise Hospice, Inc., Zulficar Gregory Restum, D’arolyn Azevedo, and Roxana Aparicio alleging: (1) whistleblower retaliation; (2) retaliation and discrimination; (3) wrongful termination; and (4) defamation.

 

            On 8/11/2026, Defendants moved to compel Plaintiff’s mental examination.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants argue good cause exists to compel the mental examination of Plaintiff as follows:

 

[Plaintiff] has testified under oath that he suffered severe emotional distress and has produced medical records attributing chronic hypertension that he now suffers to the actions of all five Defendants. This includes unrelenting emotional injuries and associated physical symptoms caused by Promise Assisted Living LLC’s mandatory reporting of Plaintiff’s suspected elder abuse, Plaintiff’s claimed wrongful termination, and various other alleged defamatory statements he asserts were made about him by each of the five Defendants. The injuries that Plaintiff asserts he continues to experience are claimed to be permanent in nature and not fully understood by him. A mental exam by a licensed professional with the requisite experience in the diagnosis of emotional and mental disorders is needed to ascertain what is admittedly unknown by Plaintiff in order to enable the parties to prepare fully for trial.

 

            (Motion, 2: 11-22.)

 

            After review, the Court agrees.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion to compel Plaintiff’s mental examination is granted.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 26STCV04280    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

CRESSIDAC LLC

 

         vs.

 

TRAVELERS CASUALTY INSURANCE COMPANY OF AMERICA, et al.

 

 Case No.: 26STCV04280   

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 16, 2026

 

Defendant’s demurrer to the first and second causes of action is OVERRULED.

 

            On 2/10/2026, Plaintiff Cressidac LLC (Plaintiff) filed suit against Travelers Casualty Insurance Company of America and Pacific Way Insurance Services, Inc. On 4/15/2026, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC), alleging: (1) breach of insurance contract; (2) tortious breach of the duty of good faith and fair dealing; and (3) professional negligence.

 

            On 5/14/2026, Defendant Travelers Casualty Insurance Company of America (Defendant or Travelers) demurred to the first and second causes of action.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s first and second causes of action are insufficiently pled. 

 

            As for the first cause of action for breach of contract, Defendant argues that claim is barred by the policy’s two-year suit limitation provision, and pursuant to California Insurance Code Section 2071. Defendant further argues that claim is defective as it alleges damages to “units and addresses” that are not listed locations or premises under the policy, which is unambiguous with respect to the listed location, premises, and building.

 

            Indeed, the Travelers’ policy provides, in relevant part:

 

F. COMMERCIAL PROPERTY CONDITIONS . . .

…

4. Legal Action Against Us. No one may bring a legal action against us under this Coverage Form unless:

 

b. The action is brought within 2 years after the date on which the direct physical loss or damage occurred.

 

 (RJN, ¶4, Ex. D, Special Provisions-California, Form MP T1 02 02 05, at Bates Number: TRAVELERS DOC MGMT Page 70 of 152.)

 

Here, Plaintiff alleges that:

 

-         Defendant Pacific Way procured a commercial property insurance policy issued by Travelers, which provided coverage for Plaintiff’s commercial property located at or about 11035 Sherman Way, Los Angeles, California 91352 (Property).

 

-         That, on or about 5/6/2023, a fire occurred at the Property, causing significant physical damage to the building and resulting in loss of use and rental income (the “Loss”). (FAC, ¶ 17)

 

-         It timely reported the Loss to Travelers, and that Plaintiff fully cooperated with Travelers’ investigation, including providing access to the Property, documentation, estimates, and other information requested for the claim. (FAC, ¶18.)

 

-         On 1/26/2024, Travelers confirmed that after review of the leases for the Property, it would issue payment for loss of use (FAC, ¶26) and that on or about February 13, 2024, Travelers issued a second payment to Plaintiff (FAC, ¶27).

 

Given that the alleged loss took place on 5/6/2023, Defendant contends that the two-year statute of limitations expired on 5/6/2025. However, Plaintiff alleges that on 2/13/2024, Travelers issued a second payment (FAC, ¶27), but thereafter, denied Plaintiff’s requests for additional benefits (FAC ¶28). Accordingly, the facts supporting the alleged breach of contract did not arise until after 2/13/2024. This action was filed on 2/10/2026, and thus Plaintiff has alleged facts which could show that the claim is not barred. (Prudential-LMI Commercial Ins. v. Superior Court (1990) 51 Cal.3d 674, 684.) Moreover, the Court finds Plaintiff’s allegations sufficient at the pleadings stage to support equitable tolling.

 

The Court finds Plaintiff’s allegations otherwise to be sufficient at the pleadings stage, and does not find them to be either so ambiguous or uncertain that Defendant cannot reasonably discern the nature of the claims against it.

 

            As for the second cause of action, Defendant argues that this claim is barred by the two-year statute of limitations for tortious bad faith, and is insufficiently pled.

 

            For the same reasons set forth above, the Court does not find the claim time-barred. Moreover, the Court finds Plaintiff’s allegations to be sufficient at the pleadings stage.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer is overruled.

 

 

It is so ordered.
