Case Number: 23STLC02679    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  

           

Judgment Creditor Richard Heng Taing dba USA Donuts’ Motion to Compel Responses to Request to Inspect Writings to Enforce Order Dated March 14, 2024, and Request for Sanctions is denied without prejudice.

 

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

On April 24, 2023, Judgment Creditor Richard Heng Taing DBA USA Donuts (“Judgment Creditor”) filed the instant action against Judgment Debtor Worldwide ATM, Inc. (“Judgment Debtor”). Judgment Debtor filed an Answer and Cross-Complaint on June 28, 2024 and July 5, 2023, respectively.

 

On March 5, 2024, the Court granted defense counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel. (Minute Order, 03/05/24.) On March 14, 2024, the Court granted Judgment Creditor’s (1) Motion to Compel Attendance at Deposition and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted and Request for Sanctions. (Minute Order, 03/14/24.) Pursuant to that ruling, the Court ordered Judgment Debtor to pay sanctions of $1,112.82 to Judgment Creditor’s counsel within 20 days’ notice. (Ibid.)

 

On May 7, 2024, at the Order to Show Cause Re: Legal Representation, Judgment Debtor failed to appear and the Court struck its Answer. (Minute Order, 05/07/24.) The Court also set an Order to Show Cause Re: Default/Default Judgment for November 5, 2024. (Ibid.) Judgment Creditor’s request for entry of default was entered on November 5, 2024. Default judgment was entered on June 23, 2025, in the amount of $8,027.00 principal and $1,089.05 costs. (Default Judgment, 06/23/25, ¶6.)

 

Judgment Creditor filed and served the instant Motion to Compel Responses to Request to Inspect Writings to Enforce Order Dated March 14 2024, and Request for Sanctions, on August 12, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 680.230, 680.270, and 708.030 on the grounds that any court order requiring the payment of money is treated as a judgment.

 

Monetary sanction orders are enforceable through the execution of judgment laws. (Weil & Brown, Cal. Practice Guide: Civil Procedure Before Trial 3 (1994) ¶¶ 9:344.21, 9:344.22, p. 9(1)–92.) These orders have the force and effect of a money judgment, and are immediately enforceable through execution, except to the extent the trial court may order a stay of the sanction. (See §§ 680.230, 680.270, 699.510, subd. (a); Jones v. Otero, supra, 156 Cal.App.3d at p. 759, 203 Cal.Rptr. 90.)

 

(Newland v. Superior Court (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 608, 615.) Enforcement of judgment laws provide that the judgment creditor may demand that the judgment debtor produce and permit the inspection of a document if the demand requests information to aid in the enforcement of the money judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.030, subd. (a).) The judgment debtor must respond and comply with the demand within 30 days of its service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.030, subd. (a); Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260.)

 

Judgment Creditor’s counsel contends they served Judgment Debtor with the Request to Inspect Writings to Enforce Order dated 3-14-24 on March 11, 2026, by mail. (Motion, Gentino Decl., ¶2.) However, the only discovery attached to the Motion is the Interrogatories to Enforce Order Dated 3-14-24, which was served on Judgment Debtor on April 5, 2024. (Id. at Exh. 1, p. 4.) Therefore, the Motion does not demonstrate service of the discovery request that is the basis of the Motion to Compel.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Judgment Creditor Richard Heng Taing dba USA Donuts’ Motion to Compel Responses to Request to Inspect Writings to Enforce Order Dated March 14, 2024, and Request for Sanctions, is denied without prejudice.

Case Number: 24STLC08071    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING: 

 

Plaintiff Angelus Block, Inc.’s Motion for Entry of Judgment as to Defendants DLR Associates and Jose Barajas Pursuant to CCP § 579 is granted.

 

The proposed judgment filed on 08/07/2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On November 5, 2024, Plaintiff Angelus Block, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for (1) breach of contract; (2) common count: goods and services rendered; (3) open book account; (4) breach of personal guaranty; (5) foreclosure of mechanic’s lien; and (6) recovery on license bond against Defendants DLR Associates (“Defendant DLR”), Jose Barajas (“Defendant Barajas”), Postmaster Group, LLC (“Defendant PMG”), and The North River Insurance Company, which was subsequently dismissed.

 

Although defaults were entered against Defendants on March 28, 2025, and April 8, 2025, the parties later stipulated to vacate the defaults against Defendants DLR and Barajas. (Stip and Order, 07/23/25.) Defendants DLR and Barajas filed their Answer to the Complaint on August 1, 2025.  No stipulation to vacate was filed as to Defendant PMG’s default at the time.

 

On August 4, 2025, Defendant DLR filed a Cross-Complaint against Cross-Defendant PMG and Roes 1-10 for indemnification, apportionment of fault, and declaratory relief. Defendant PMG filed Answers to the Complaint and Cross-Complaint on September 3, 2025. On the same day, Defendant PMG also filed a Cross-Complaint against Cross-Defendants DLR, Barajas, MID Construction Group, David Hirsch, and Steve Ghermezian.

 

On December 23, 2025, Plaintiff Angelus Block filed a Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication against Defendants. At the hearing on March 16, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff summary adjudication as to the first, second, and third causes of action against Defendant DLR and as to the fourth cause of action against Defendant Barajas, and prejudgment interest against both. (Minute Order, 03/16/26, p. 8.) The Court also pointed out that Defendant PMG was in default as to the Complaint because its default had never been set aside.  The Court struck Defendant PMG’s Answer and Cross-Complaint and indicated that the Court would set a prove-up hearing on the default judgment against Defendant PMG, which was named solely in count five. (Id. at pp. 1-2.)

 

On March 25, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s ex parte application for leave to file an amended Complaint. (Minute Order, 03/25/26.) The Court noted that the proposed Amended Complaint removed Defendant DLR from the only cause of action left unresolved after summary judgment, and therefore left no claims unresolved against Defendant DLR.  The Court ordered Plaintiff to file and serve the Amended Complaint within ten days upon Defendants PMG and Merchants Bonding Company, the bond surety. (Ibid.) Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint on March 27, 2026. Defendants DLR and Barajas filed their Answer to the First Amended Complaint on April 2, 2026. Defendant PMG filed its Answer to the First Amended Complaint on April 20, 2026; Defendant Merchants Bonding filed its Answer on May 8, 2026.

 

On June 24, 2026, the Court dismissed Cross-Defendant PMG from the Cross-Complaint pursuant to an oral request by Cross-Complainant DLR. Cross-Complainant DLR then added Merchants Bonding as a Cross-Defendant via doe amendment. (Amendment to Cross-Complaint, 07/27/26.)

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Entry of Judgment as to Defendants DLR Associates and Jose Barajas Pursuant to CCP § 579 on August 5, 2026. Defendants DLR and Barajas filed an opposition on August 28, 2026, and Angelus Block filed a Reply on September 8, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for entry of judgment against Defendants DLR and Barajas on the Complaint, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 579, on the grounds that all claims against these Defendants were determined against them in the Court’s March 16, 2026 ruling on Plaintiff’s Motion for Summary Judgment. Code of Civil Procedure section 579 states in relevant part: “In an action against several defendants, the Court may, in its discretion, render judgment against one or more of them, leaving the action to proceed against the others, whenever a several judgment is proper.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 579.) A several judgment is proper where the liability of the moving defendant does not depend upon the determination of the liability of the parties who remain. (See Trans-Pacific Trading Co. v. Patsy Frock & Romper Co. (1922) 189 Cal. 509, 513.) Plaintiff contends that because all the claims against Defendants DLR and Barajas have been resolved, a several judgment is proper.

 

The Court agrees that a several judgment is appropriate.  On March 16, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff Angelus Block summary adjudication as to the first, second, and third causes of action against Defendant DLR, and the fourth cause of action against Defendant Barajas.  That left pending the fifth cause of action against Defendant DLR and Defendant PMG, and the sixth cause of action against Defendant DLR and the North River Insurance Co.  Plaintiff dismissed the sixth cause of action and, in filing the Amended Complaint, removed Defendant DLR from the fifth cause of action. The Amended Complaint did not amend the causes of action as to which summary adjudication had been entered.  (See Minute Order, 03/25/26.)  Accordingly, Plaintiff Angelus Block’s causes of action against Defendants DLR and Barajas are resolved in their entirety.  Following entry of a judgment as to those two Defendants, the Complaint would remain pending as to Defendants Merchants Bonding Company and Defendant PMG, who are named only in the fifth cause of action, for Claim on Mechanics Lien Release Bond. (Am. Compl., ¶¶34-45.) DLR’s Cross-Complaint is against Merchants, and does not name Angelus Block or Barajas.  (Although the Opposition states otherwise, the Cross-Complaint is not filed on behalf of Defendant Barajas.)

 

The Opposition cites no authority for its positions.  Defendants DLR and Barajas argue that a several judgment against them is not proper, because “[a]ll parties are responsible for the same debt for materials from Angelus Block.  DLR ordered the materials. Jose Barajas guaranteed the account.  [PMG] is responsible on the mechanic’s lien since it received the materials and failed to fund the project, leaving DLR unable to pay its vendors including Angelus Blocl. Now Merchants is alleged to responsible for the same debt under the mechanics lien release bond.”  (Opp., p. 5:1-10.)  These facts do not describe a scenario that warrants separate judgments.  Plaintiff’s claims against Defendants DLR and Barajas have been fully adjudicated, with nothing left to decide.  Judgment may be entered as to those claims, and Defendants DLR and Barajas may seek recovery from other defendants whom they believe should be held accountable.  

 

The opposition also asserts broadly that, “Plaintiff wants a separate judgment against DLR and Barajas then to get a judgment against Postmaster and Merchants for the same debt. That result is not in the interest of justice. There should be only one judgment in this case.”  (Opp., p. 3:15-19.)   That argument falls in the category of issues that can be litigated among the remaining parties.  Defendants PMG and Merchants can raise the issue of double-recovery, if they believe it applies. It does not make entry of judgment on the adjudicated claims less appropriate.

 

The Opposition is incorrect on several points of procedure that the Court will take a moment to clarify. As noted, the Cross-Complaint is not filed on Defendant Barajas’ behalf.  (See Opp., 3:12-13.)  Also, Defendant Merchants is not delinquent in responding to the Cross-Complaint, because no proof of service has yet been filed.  (See Opp., p. 3:20-22.)  Third, the Opposition incorrectly states that DLR and Barajas have affirmative defenses available.  (See Opp., p. 3:22-26.)  They do not because all claims against them have been adjudicated.  Relatedly, the Opposition states that the Amended Complaint negated the prior summary adjudication ruling, leaving all the adjudicated claims again at issue.  (Opp., p.4:9-17.)  The Opposition cites no authority for this contention.  Although “the filing of an amended complaint moots a motion directed to a prior complaint,” (see State Comp. Ins. Fund v. Superior Ct. (2010) 184 Cal. App. 4th 1124, 1131 (emphasis added), the Opposition cites no authority for the proposition that it moots an already adjudicated motion where the amendment did not change the adjudicated causes of action.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Angelus Block, Inc.’s Motion for Entry of Judgment as to Defendants DLR Associates and Jose Barajas Pursuant to CCP § 579 is granted.

 

The proposed judgment filed on 08/07/2026 is signed and entered this date.

Case Number: 25STLC04281    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Nadia Zay’s Motion to Vacate Entry of Default is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Armaz “LLC” (“Defendant Armaz”) and Nadia Zay (“Defendant Zay”) on May 30, 2025. On December 1, 2025, the Court entered default against Defendant Zay. On December 22, 2025, Defendant Zay filed a General Denial, despite being in default. Default was entered against Defendant Armaz on January 2, 2026.

 

On April 6, 2026, the Court heard Plaintiff’s Motion to Strike Answer, which it denied. (Minute Order, 04/06/26.) However, on its own motion, the Court struck the Answer for being filed while Defendant Zay was in default. (Ibid.) Defendant Zay filed a Motion to Set Aside Default on April 13, 2026, which was initially set for hearing on May 6, 2026. However, two days prior to the hearing, Defendant asked that the hearing be continued to June 4, 2026. On the same day, Defendant then filed a Notice of Reschedule Hearing and Motion to Set Aside Default (incorrectly identified as an “Answer” on the docket) and set the new, second motion for hearing on June 4, 2026.

 

On June 4, 2026, the Court denied the second Motion to Set Aside Default without prejudice, noting that it was not accompanied by a memorandum of points and authorities, or by a supporting declaration. (Minute Order, 06/04/26.) Defendant Zay then filed another Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default on August 13, 2026, but set it for hearing only four days later. The Court denied that Motion to Set Aside on August 17, 2026. (Minute Order, 08/17/26.)

 

Defendant has now filed a fourth Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default on August 21, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on September 1, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The instant Motion again seeks to vacate the default entered against Defendant Zay on account of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. (Motion, p. 1:27-29; Motion filed 08/13/26, p. 1:27-29.) When a party seeks the same relief that was previously denied, it must bring a renewed motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (b). (California Correctional Peace Officers Ass’n v. Virga (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 30, 43, fn. 11; see also Tate v. Wilburn (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 150, 156-157.) When a motion has been denied in whole or in part, the moving party may apply again for the same relief at a later time only upon “new or different facts, circumstances or law.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (b); see Graham v. Hansen (1982) 128 Cal.App.3d 965, 969-970.) Also, the statute requires the moving party “to show a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence earlier, which can only be described as a strict requirement of diligence,” the purpose of which is to incentivize parties “to efficiently marshal their evidence.” (Baldwin v. Home Sav. of America (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 1192, 1199 [citing Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 689-690].)

 

The instant Motion to Vacate is neither brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, nor fulfills its statutory requirements. Defendant Zay’s supporting declaration fails to address the Motion to Vacate filed on August 13, 2026 or the Court’s ruling dated August 17, 2026. Nor does the Motion demonstrate new or different facts, circumstances or law that warranted a renewed request for relief from the default. Therefore, the Court lacks the jurisdiction to grant the renewed Motion to Enforce Settlement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (e); see Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1106.)

 

Even if the Court were to consider the renewed Motion on the merits, it is untimely.  The request for relief from the entry of default is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), which is no longer available to Defendant. Under that section, an application for relief must be made no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s or counsel’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) The six-month deadline is jurisdictional and not subject to tolling. (Manson, Iver & York v. Black (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 36, 42.) Default was entered December 1, 2025.  The instant Motion was filed more than eight months later and, therefore, cannot be granted under the moving statute.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Nadia Zay’s Motion to Vacate Entry of Default is denied.

Case Number: 25STLC05445    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services’ Motion for Order that Matters be Deemed Admitted; Request for Monetary Sanctions is granted. Defendant Shawn Fortune aka Shawn Michael Fortune is ordered to pay sanctions of $775.77 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services (“Plaintiff”) propounded Requests for Admission, Set One, on Defendant Shawn Fortune aka Shawn Michael Fortune (“Defendant Fortune”) on June 3, 2026. (Motion, Brown Decl., ¶3 and Exh. 1.) Despite Defendant’s effort to meet and confer, Plaintiff had not served responses as of the filing of the instant Motion to Deem Requests for Admission, Set One, Admitted and Request for Monetary Sanctions on July 23, 2026. (Id. at ¶¶4-5 and Exh. 2.)

 

Plaintiff now moves for an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280. There is no requirement for a prior meet-and-confer effort before a motion to deem requests for admission can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to serve responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Due to Defendant Fortune’s failure to timely serve verified responses and the resulting waiver of objections, Plaintiff is entitled to an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.)

 

The Court is required to award sanctions under the moving statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) However, the request for sanctions is excessive given the lack of any opposition and Plaintiff’s counsel’s ability to appear remotely for the hearing. Plaintiff is awarded sanctions of $775.77 based on one hour of attorney time billed at $700.00 per hour, plus $75.77 in filing fees. (Motion, Brown Decl., ¶6.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services’ Motion for Order that Matters be Deemed Admitted; Request for Monetary Sanctions is granted. Defendant Shawn Fortune aka Shawn Michael Fortune is ordered to pay sanctions of $775.77 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

Case Number: 26STLC02162    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:  

           

Plaintiff Kewan Vines’ Motion to Compel Defendant’s Answers to Interrogatories is granted. Defendant Milad Investments, LLC is to serve verified responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One, without objections, within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

Defendant and counsel of record are jointly and severally ordered to pay sanctions of $500.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 11, 2026, Plaintiff Kewan Vines (“Plaintiff”) served Special Interrogatories, Set One, on Defendant Milad Investments, LLC (“Defendant”). (Motion, Mehrban Decl., Exh. A.) Despite a meet-and-confer effort extending the deadline to serve verified responses without objections, Defendant has not served responses. (Id. at ¶¶4-5 and Exh. B.) Plaintiff filed and served the instant Motion to Compel Defendant’s Answers to Interrogatories, on July 31, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Further, the motion can be brought any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Based on Defendant’s failure to respond to the propounded discovery, Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling it to serve verified responses to the discovery requests without objections.

 

Plaintiff also moves for an award of sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, subdivision (c), which provides for sanctions if a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses without reasonable justification. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c) [“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”].) However, Defendant did not file an opposition to the instant Motion.

 

Plaintiff alternatively cites California Rules of Court, rule 3.1348, which does provide for an award of sanctions “in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even

though no opposition to the motion was filed.” (Cal. Rules of Court, 3.1348(a).) In light of this rule, Plaintiff is entitled to an award of sanctions. Sanctions are awarded against Defendant and counsel of record in the amount of $500.00 based on one hour of attorney time billed at $500.00 per hour. (Motions, Mehrban Decl., ¶6.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Kewan Vines’ Motion to Compel Defendant’s Answers to Interrogatories is granted. Defendant Milad Investments, LLC is to serve verified responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One, without objections, within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

Defendant and counsel of record are jointly and severally ordered to pay sanctions of $500.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.
