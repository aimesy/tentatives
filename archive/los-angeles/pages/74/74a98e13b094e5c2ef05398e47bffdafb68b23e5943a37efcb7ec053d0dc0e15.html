Case Number: 25CHLC07758    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: 25

CASE NAME:           Wicked Good Films LLC v. The Fabulous Group, Inc., et al.

COMP. FILED:         02-24-25

CASE NUMBER:      25CHLC07758

MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT JUDGMENT

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 473(d))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court DENIES Kevin T. Van Belois’s motion to vacate the default entered against him and to quash or for a protective order.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Wicked Good Films LLC sued The Fabulous Group, Inc. and Kevin T. Van Belois.  Fabulous and Belois failed to respond, so Wicked obtained a default against each of them in June 2025.  Belois now moves to have the default vacated under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) because the default is void for invalid service and lack of personal jurisdiction.[1]

 

            In April 2025, a registered California process server personally served Belois with copies of the summons, complaint, and various other case-opening materials at “3513 Glenhurst Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90039.”  The server declared that Belois identified himself as the named defendant, with “identity confirmed by subject saying yes when named.”  The person who self-identified as Belois appeared to be a 45–55-year-old White man with brown hair, standing between five-feet-ten-inches and six-feet tall and weighing 160–180 pounds.  That person accepted the documents from the server, whose declaration is prima facie evidence that Wicked effected service on Belois.  (Evid. Code, § 647; see Lee v. Yan (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 975, 978.)  That places the burden on Belois to offer evidence to the contrary.  (Palm Property Investments, LLC v. Yadegar (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 1419, 1428.)

 

            Belois concedes that he resided at the Glenhurst address.  He argues, though, that he was not personally served, and that the proof of service describes a person weighing between 160 and 180 pounds.  Belois says he weighs “over 240 pounds.” 

 

Belois provides, e.g., no evidence where he was at the time of alleged service if not his residence.  Nor does he dispute the rest of the server’s description of him.  The Court is not persuaded that the server’s declaration is false by an averred discrepancy in a defendant’s mutable weight, declared over a year after the relevant events.  And, unless process servers put people on scales—something likely to subject them to more opprobrium than they already face in their line of work—a person’s weight is probably going to be a best-guess.  Belois presents no other evidence adequately to rebut the presumption of valid personal service.

 

Belois also asks the Court to quash Wicked’s request for documents or to issue a protective order.  Belois cites to no authority and makes little argument on this point.  Regardless, a motion to do so would need to be brought separately from the motion to vacate.

 

            Accordingly, the Court DENIES Belois’s motion.

 

            Wicked is to give notice.


[1] The Court notes that Belois references a judgment, but no judgment has been entered against him yet.  Belois does mention the date June 30, 2025, which is when default was entered, and thus the Court rules on that default.


Case Number: 25STLC03481    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Tues., August 4, 2026                                     JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NUMBER:      25STLC03481                                   

NOTICE:                   OK                                                                 

                                               

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION JUDGMENT ON TIME-BARRED CLAIMS

 

MOVING PARTY:   Defendant Renee McKeller

RESP. PARTY:         Plaintiff Jack Maya

 

MOTION FOR ORDER PRECLUDING ENTRY OF JUDGMENT

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court DENIES AS MOOT Renee McKeller’s motion.

 

Maya is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          Filed July 21, 2026                                         [   ] Late                      [  ] None

REPLY:                     Filed July 22, 2026                                         [   ] Late                      [  ] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Jack Maya sued Renee McKeller for back rent owed on property that he had leased to her. McKeller tried unsuccessfully to quash service of Maya’s summons and complaint.  She then failed timely to answer the complaint, and Maya took her default.  McKeller subsequently moved unsuccessfully to have the default set aside.  After that, she filed this motion to preclude the entry of judgment, arguing that Maya’s causes of action are time-barred.

 

The Court entered default judgment against McKeller on July 27, 2026.  McKeller’s remedies at this point are from the judgment.  The Court therefore DENIES AS MOOT McKeller’s motion. 

 

Maya is to give notice.
