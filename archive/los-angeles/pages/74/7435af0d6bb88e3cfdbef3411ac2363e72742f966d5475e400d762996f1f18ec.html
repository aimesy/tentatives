Case Number: 24STCV04845    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV04845 Enrique Aguilar, et al. v. Arturo Carmona

 

Thursday, August 6, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION FOR SANCTIONS PURSUANT TO CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE SECTION 128.5

 

 

I.       Background

      Plaintiffs leased residential property from Defendant. Plaintiffs allege seven causes of action arising from the uninhabitable conditions of the premises.

II.     Arguments

      Plaintiffs move for monetary sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5 against Defendant Arturo Carmona, and his counsel for engaging in bad faith actions by filing a frivolous demurrer based on uncertainty. Defendant allegedly took words out of context and ignored all allegations in the complaint. The demurrer is set for hearing on October 8, 2026.

      Plaintiffs also argue that Defendant filed a first motion to quash arguing lack of proper service of the summons and complaint, which the Court granted. (M.O. 6.20.25.) Plaintiffs served Defendant at an address given by Defendant, however, Defendant filed a second motion to quash service of the summons and complaint without any factual support. The Court denied the motion. (M.O. 2/26/26.) Defendant then demurred to the complaint, rather than file a responsive pleading.  

      Defendant responds that the motion to vacate default and motion to quash service of summons and complaint were meritorious and were granted. Defendant believes the demurrer and motion to strike are meritorious and legally viable and were not filed for harassment or delay. Defendant contends that Plaintiffs did not comply with the safe harbor rule requiring that the motion be served but not filed until 21 days after receipt of the motion to allow Defendant to withdraw the offending paper.

      In reply, Plaintiffs contend Defendant f ails to excuse the core issue, which is Defendant’s systematic use of meritless, repetitive motion practice designed solely to delay these  proceedings, inflate costs, and harass Plaintiffs. Defendant has engaged in an ongoing strategy of gamesmanship. Plaintiffs adhered to the statute’s safe harbor provision.

III.    Discussion

      The court has discretion to "order a party, the party's attorney, or both, to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, incurred by another party as a result of actions or tactics, made in bad faith, that are frivolous or solely intended to cause unnecessary delay.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5, subd. (a).) “Frivolous” is defined as “"totally and completely without merit or for the sole purpose of harassing an opposing party.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5, subd. (b)(2).) The section does not apply to disclosures and discovery requests, responses, objections, and motions.” (Id.). subd (e).)

      In effect, filing a motion for sanctions is a two-step procedure. First, the moving party must serve a notice of motion for sanctions on the offending party at least 21 days before filing the motion with the court.  The motion must specifically describe the sanctionable conduct.  If the offending party does not withdraw the pleading or otherwise correct the sanctionable conduct, then the motion may be filed, and the court may determine an appropriate sanction. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5 (f)(1)(B).)

      Plaintiffs complied with the safe harbor rule by first electronically transmitting the motion and intent to seek sanctions to defense counsel on April 16, 2026, as Defendant acknowledges. (Opp. Decl. of Richard Jacobs, Ex. D.) Plaintiffs did not file this motion until May 29, 2026, 43 days later.   

      Plaintiffs argue that Defendant engages in a “pattern” of repetitive motion practice, which Plaintiffs believe to be frivolous. However, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to set aside default and motion to quash service of summons. (M.O. 6/20/25.) As they were meritorious, they cannot be regarded as frivolous or in bad faith.

      The Court subsequently denied Defendant’s second motion to quash service of summons and complaint because Defendant did not overcome the presumption of proper service. (M.O. 2/26/26.) The Court does not find that the second motion to quash was frivolous or “solely” intended to cause delay.

      Defendant has the statutory right to challenge the complaint by demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10.) Defendant filed a demurrer to the complaint and motion to strike on March 20, 2026. Exercising a statutory right to challenge the pleading and successfully moving the Court for relief does not demonstrate a pattern of bad faith or frivolous motion practice. Plaintiffs’ belief that the demurrer lacks merit does not establish that Defendant acted in bad faith.

      Sanctions under Civil Procedure section 128.5 should not be imposed except in the clearest of cases. (Luke v. Baldwin-United Corp. (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 664, 669.) It should deter only the most egregious misconduct. (Id. at 668.) Luke cautioned that "any definition must be read so as to avoid a serious chilling effect on the assertion of litigants' rights ... .” (Id. at 668.) Plaintiffs have not met the required standing for imposing sanctions under this section.

         A party prevailing on the motion may be awarded the reasonable expenses and attorney’s fees incurred in opposing the motion “if warranted.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5, subd. (f)(1)(C).) The Court has reviewed the parties’ email correspondence showing defense counsel attempted to meet and confer with Plaintiffs’ counsel six times with respect to both the demurrer and this motion. (Jacobs decl., ¶ 6.) Mr. Jacobs was unsuccessful in speaking with an attorney, as none was in the office. Mr. Jacobs was offered a case manager to confer with, which he refused. (Id.) Defense counsel was then able to speak with an attorney who was a “contract” attorney that did not work in the office, but who informed defense counsel to file the demurrer without any discussion of sanctions or bad faith. (Id.)

      The 21-day safe harbor rule is of no effect if the moving party does not confer with opposing counsel about the merits of the motion and the paper or alleged misconduct supporting the motion. Accordingly, Defendant’s request for imposition of sanctions against Plaintiffs’ counsel is warranted.

      The Court finds that defense counsel’s hourly rate of $550 per hour is reasonable, and the time incurred or to be incurred in opposing the motion and appearing at the hearing (six hours) is also reasonable for a total of $3,300 in monetary sanctions.

IV.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ motion for imposition of sanctions pursuant to Civil Procedure section 128.5 is DENIED. Defendant’s request for imposition of sanctions against Plaintiffs’ counsel and counsel’s law firm is GRANTED. Plaintiffs’ counsel is ordered to pay $3,300 to defense counsel within 10 days. The statute provides that “absent exceptional circumstances, a law firm shall be held jointly responsible for violations committed by its partners, associates, and employees.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5 (f)(1)(C.)

24STCV08426 Myers v. Christian Louboutin, LLC., et al.

Thursday, August 6, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF'S AMENDED MOTION FOR APPROVAL OF PAGA SETTLEMENT (3077)

  

       

      The complaint alleges wage and hour claims in violation of the Labor Code, race discrimination under Fair Employment and Housing Act, a tort claim for wrongful termination in violation of public policy, and to recover penalties under the Private Attorney General Act (PAGA). Plaintiff seeks approval of settlement under PAGA. The Court has considered the motion, declarations, and evidence in support of the motion. No opposition has been filed.  

      The court must review and approve PAGA settlements to ensure that any negotiated resolution is fair to those affected. (Lab. Code, § 2699(l)(2); Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 549.) The court conducts its assessment based on information “about the amount in controversy and the realistic range of outcomes." (Moniz v. Adecco USA, Inc. (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 56, 76-77 ["We therefore hold that a trial court should evaluate a PAGA settlement to determine whether it is fair, reasonable, and adequate in view of PAGA's purposes to remediate present labor law violations, deter future ones, and to maximize enforcement of state labor laws.”].) The trial court has broad discretion in approving the settlement. (Id. at 78.)

       A presumption of fairness exists where: “(1) the settlement is reached through arm’s-length bargaining; (2) investigation and discovery are sufficient to allow counsel and the court to act intelligently; (3) counsel is experienced in similar litigation; and (4) the percentage of objectors is small.” (Dunk v. Ford Motor Co. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 1794, 1802.) The court may also consider other factors as this list is not exhaustive. (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 244–245.)       

      Plaintiff complied with the requirement to submit a copy of the proposed settlement agreement to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency (LWDA). (Lab. Code, § 2699 subd. (s)(2); (Lacour supp. decl. ¶ 2, Ex. E.) Documents submitted to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency LWDA "shall be transmitted online through the same system established for the filing of notices and requests.” (Lab. Code, § 2699 subd. (s)(4).)  

      The parties settled the action after participating in a mediation and after conducting informal discovery and investigation. (Lacour Am. decl., 11-12.) Counsel’s firm’s litigation practice is made up of a substantial percentage of wage and hour violations in both class action and complex litigation matters. (Lacour supp. Decl., ¶ 26-28.) Counsel investigated and researched the facts and engaged in formal and informal discovery to assess the validity of Plaintiff’s claims. (Id. ¶¶ 9-11.)

      The parties agreed to a gross settlement of $150,000 from which fees and costs and enhancement payments will be made. The payment to LWDA and the aggrieved employees are taken from the net settlement of $73,250.

PAGA counsel fees (1/3 of gross settlement)

$50,000

 

PAGA counsel costs and expenses (not to exceed 25,000)

20,000

 

Settlement Administration Costs

1,000

 

TOTAL deductions

$76,750.00

 

Net settlement

$54,937.50

 

For distribution to 95 aggrieved employees (25 percent)

18,312.50

 

 

      Accordingly, the court approves the settlement. 

Case Number: 24STCV18027    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV18027 Rustem Orynbassar, et al. v. Abylaikhan Abualiyev

Thursday, August 6, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT, U-HAUL CO. OF CALIFORNIA’S MOTION FOR TERMINATING SANCTIONS (5274)

 

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      This action arises from an automobile collision between Plaintiffs and Defendants. On November 6, 2025, the court relieved counsel for Rustem Orynbassar, Plaintiff, from further representation. Plaintiff Orynbassar is the only remaining Plaintiff as co-Plaintiff, Ulan Mussayev, was dismissed. (Dism. 11/21/24.)

      Defendant, U-Haul of California, requests terminating sanctions against Plaintiff for his repeated failure to serve responses to Defendant’s discovery requests and his failure to comply with multiple orders of this Court. Specifically, Defendant requests that the Court strike Plaintiff's Complaint and dismiss this action based on Plaintiff’s repeated misuse of the discovery process.

      Defendant served all parties with the motion, and no opposition has been filed.

II.     DISCUSSION

       The court has discretion to impose monetary, evidentiary, issue, or terminating sanctions against a party where that party fails to obey an order to provide discovery responses which is a misuse of the discovery process. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030.)

      Defendant successfully obtained a Court order compelling Plaintiff to serve verified responses to form interrogatories and a request for production of documents. (M.O. 2/6/26.) The motions were unopposed, and Plaintiff did not appear at the hearing.

      Plaintiff did not serve verified responses as ordered. The Court granted Defendant’s subsequent motion for terminating sanctions in part by imposing monetary sanctions as a first measure. (M.O. 4/21/26.) Defendant contends Plaintiff ignored this order as well.

 

III.    CONCLUSION

      As monetary sanctions do not have an effect on Plaintiff’s willingness to comply with discovery obligations, Defendant’s motion is GRANTED. The Court strikes Plaintiff’s complaint as alleged against Defendant, U-Haul of California.


Case Number: 25NNCV01902    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 414

25NNCV01902 Lawrence H. Title v. Tomika Smalls, et al.

Thursday, August 6, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING DEFENDANT, TOMIKA SMALLS’, MOTION TO STRIKE FRAUDULENT DISMISSAL AND FILINGS (9441)

 

I.         BACKGROUND

      Plaintiff leased an apartment to Defendant, Tomika Smalls, pursuant to a written agreement. The lease expired on December 31,2023, at which point the tenancy became month-to-month. Plaintiff alleges Defendant breached the lease agreement by engaging in a course of conduct with intent to disturb her upstairs tenant, causing the tenant to demand to be released from his lease obligations. The tenant has since vacated the property. Plaintiff alleged claims for breach of contract, interference with contract relations, negligent interference with prospective economic advantage and nuisance.

      The clerk entered dismissal of the complaint pursuant to Plaintiff’s request for dismissal. (Dism. 6/9/26.)

      Defendant moves to strike the dismissal on grounds of fraud and extrinsic fraud. The dismissal was rejected by the Court Clerk on June 8, 2026, for lack of required signatures, proof of service, and authorization. Defendant contends that the parties’ settlement called for a dismissal with prejudice, however, the dismissal is made without prejudice.

      Defendant also finds fault with this Court’s denial of Defendant’s motion to disqualify and the Court’s decision to relate this case formerly pending in Burbank (the Burbank case) to a case she filed at the Stanley Mosk Courthouse bearing Case No. to 24STCV09235 Tomika Smalls v. Lawrence Title (the Employment Action.)

      Defendant contends Plaintiff used an incorrect email addresses for Defendant, which prevented Defendant’s notice of hearings.

      In opposition, Plaintiff contends this motion is an attack on the Court’s authority to rule on the Notice of Related Cases that Ms. Smalls filed. Ms. Smalls cannot dictate Title’s filing of a request for dismissal of his own claims.

      In reply, Defendant files a supplemental brief contending that the opposition was filed late and is a distraction. Defendant contends the dismissal is a manipulated paper trail to illegally extinguish Defendant’s active-cross complaint while sweeping undeniable extrinsic fraud under the rug.   

II.        DISCUSSION

A.     The opposition

      The opposition is not untimely as Defendant Smalls contends. It was due nine court days before this hearing, which is July 24, 2026. The opposition was filed on July 24, 2026. Even if it was untimely, the court has discretion to consider late papers in favor of the strong policy favoring disposition of the case on the merits. (Kapitanski v. Von’s (1983) 146 Cal.App.3d 29, 32). [“Judges are well aware of the unnecessary burdens placed on courts and counsel when strict compliance with local procedural rules results in the expenditure of unnecessary time and money for the preparation of later section 473 motions.”].)

      The Court has considered Defendant Smalls’ Motion to Strike, the supplemental declaration filed four days later, and the second supplemental brief and declaration in reply to the opposition filed July 29, 2026. All of Defendant Smalls’ briefs have been considered, and therefore, Defendant Smalls has not suffered any prejudice, even if the opposition was filed late.

B.     Analysis

      Defendant Smalls has not shown that Plaintiff Title’s dismissal of his own action is fraudulent.  An action may be dismissed with or without prejudice upon Plaintiff’s written request to the clerk at any time before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 581(b)(1).)

      Defendant has not shown how her due process rights were violated. Defendant does not identify which particular hearings, on what date, and in which case she was denied notice and an opportunity to be heard. The argument does not support striking anything as “fraudulent.” Defendant does not reference a particular order that was made without notice to her or what particular order should be stricken for lack of notice.

      Defendant’s reference to the “Related Case Consolidation” as a “procedural hijacking” is unsupported. Smalls filed a notice of related cases 24STCV09235 to relate it to the Burbank case. (NORC 4.21.26.) The Court ordered the matters related, and the Burbank matter was transferred to this Court. (M.O. 4.29.26.) The Court has authority to relate cases as requested by parties. (CA ST CIVIL RULES Rule 3.300 (h)(2)(A).)

      Defendant’s accusation that the Court is colluding with Plaintiff’s counsel and that it has abandoned its role as a neutral arbiter is inappropriate and untrue. Defendant does not identify what filings requiring her consent were filed improperly without her consent.

      The “Smoking Gun” evidence appears to relate to Plaintiff Title’s request for dismissal in the Burbank action. Again, Plaintiff Title is entitled to dismiss his own action upon written request to the clerk. Defendant’s consent is not required.

      It is true that Plaintiff Title first attempted to dismiss the Burbank action on June 8, 2026, but the clerk rejected the request because a request for dismissal without prejudice and with the court retaining jurisdiction pursuant to Cal. Code Civ. Procedure § requires both parties’ signature. (RFD 6/8/26.) The subsequent request for dismissal did not check box 1(a)(3) which required both signatures. Instead, Plaintiff Title, submitted a request for dismissal of the complaint without prejudice, which did not need Defendant’s consent.

      Defendant’s complaint that Title lacked professionalism has no bearing on purported fraud.

      Defendant refers to the June 8, 2026, Case Management Conference and Order to Show Cause why the case should not be dismissed, why sanctions should not be imposed, and why this case should not be dismissed pursuant to the settlement agreement and the Court’s order in Case No. 24STCV09235. The Court set that CMC by order dated May 13, 2026. The clerk gave notice to Defendant Smalls at [email protected]. Defendant filed a consent to be served electronically at that address. (Consent 5/1/26.) There is no merit to Defendant’s contention that the June 8, 2026, order was “intentionally buried.” Defendant was given notice according to her written consent.

      Defendant’s examination of the procedural history of the case does not reveal anything fraudulent or any particular order that should be stricken. The “narrative style entries” in the Court’s Register of Actions are irrelevant. Defendant contends that Plaintiff’s filing of a motion to enforce settlement “conflicts with the actual status of the case.” Defendant does not elaborate or explain why a mistake in the status of a case warrants striking any particular order.

III.       CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion to strike is DENIED.


Case Number: 25STCV17447    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV17447 Koutsolioutsos v. Tarr, et al.

Thursday, August 6, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFF AND CROSS-DEFENDANT, DINO KOUTSOLIOUTSOS’, MOTION FOR JUDGMENT ON THE PLEADINGS OF THE CROSS-COMPLAINT (1707)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The complaint alleges that the parties own neighboring real property. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants have been using Plaintiff’s backyard to install a palm tree, an inflatable jacuzzi and building a patio that encroached upon Plaintiff’s yard. Plaintiff alleges claims for trespass, private nuisance quiet title and for declaratory relief.

      The Tarr Defendants filed a Cross-Complaint asserting claims for declaratory relief and to quiet title to a prescriptive easement consisting of a strip of land on which a concrete slab sits, along with a jacuzzi, patio decking, landscaping and perimeter fencing (Disputed Land.)

II.     ARGUMENTS

      Cross-Defendant/Plaintiff moves for judgment on the pleading of the Cross-Complaint on grounds that a prescriptive easement over a portion of the true owner’s land is not permissible under California law. Cross-Defendant argues that the declaratory relief claim is duplicative of the quiet title claim.

      Cross-Complainants (or “the Tarrs”) argue in opposition that the motion assumes the Disputed Land is necessarily part of Plaintiff’s land. Rather the verified Cross-Complaint alleges that the area “may lie” within Cross-complainants’ property. The claim to establish a prescriptive easement based on adverse possession is an alternative theory. Cross-Complainants argue that the motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 438 cannot determine the true boundary, resolve competing survey and historical-title evidence, or convert Plaintiff’s disputed characterization of the land into an adjudicated fact.

      The Tarrs argue the “proposed amendment” (submitted with their opposition and marked “received”) therefore states a direct quiet-title claim based on record boundary and title, not merely an easement over land assumed to belong to Plaintiff. They argue the permit, terrain, fence history, and unresolved title chain establish far more than a speculative possibility that amendment will cure the defects asserted by Plaintiff.

      Cross-Defendant replies that if the Disputed Land is located on the Tarrs’ property, their prescriptive easement claim necessarily fails as a matter of law because, the Tarrs cannot obtain a prescriptive easement to maintain their encroachment on another’s property. If the Disputed Area is on their property, the Tarr’s claim still fails because they cannot obtain a prescriptive easement over their own property.

III.    LEGAL STANDARDS

      A motion for judgment on the pleadings may be granted if the court lacks jurisdiction of the subject of the cause of action or the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant (in this case, Cross-Defendant). (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (c)(B).) A motion for judgment on the pleadings performs the same function as a general demurrer and attacks only defects disclosed on the face of the pleadings or by matters subject to judicial notice. (Burnett v. Chimney Sweep (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 1057, 1065 [“a motion for judgment on the pleadings, like a demurrer, raises only questions of law ... .”)

      For the purposes of this motion, all properly alleged material facts are deemed true as well as all facts that may be implied or inferred from those expressly alleged. (Fire Ins. Exchange v. Superior Court (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 446, 452).

IV.   DISCUSSION

      The Cross-Complaint alleges that the Tarrs own the real property at 2490 Cheremoya. (Cross-Complaint ¶ 8.) Cross-Defendant allegedly owns 2492 Cheremoya. (Cross-Complaint ¶ 9.)  The Tarr property is adjacent to and immediately north of Cross-Defendant’s property. ) Complaint ¶ 10.)

      The pleading alleges that the Tarrs believe that the Disputed Area is on their property and believe this will be confirmed by a survey. (Cross-Complaint ¶ 11.) If the Disputed Land is on Cross-Defendant’s property, then the Tarrs “bring the causes of action set forth herein.” (Id.)

      A verified complaint for quiet title requires all of the following:

“(a) A description of the property that is the subject of the action. In the case of tangible personal property, the description shall include its usual location. In the case of real property, the description shall include both its legal description and its street address or common designation, if any.

 

(b) The title of the plaintiff as to which a determination under this chapter is sought and the basis of the title. If the title is based upon adverse possession, the complaint shall allege the specific facts constituting the adverse possession.

(c) The adverse claims to the title of the plaintiff against which a determination is sought.

 

(d) The date as of which the determination is sought. If the determination is sought as of a date other than the date the complaint is filed, the complaint shall include a statement of the reasons why a determination as of that date is sought.

 

(e) A prayer for the determination of the title of the plaintiff against the adverse claims." (Code Civ. Proc., § 761.020.)

 

      A quiet title action declares the parties” right to real property as declared by the trial court.  

(Western Aggregates, Inc. v. County of Yuba (2002) 101 Cal.App.4th 278, 305 [“… a trial court should not dismiss a regular declaratory relief action when the plaintiff loses, but instead should issue a judgment setting forth the declaration of rights and thus ending the controversy.”]
      The Cross-Complaint requests declaratory relief and to quiet title to a prescriptive easement consisting of the Disputed Area. (Cross-Complaint ¶ 1.) The Tarrs’ ownership of the Disputed Area is based on their continued use and maintenance of the existing concrete slab and deck, landscaping, jacuzzi and perimeter fencing and their continuous and uninterrupted possession and hostile use of the area for more than five years.  (Cross-Complaint ¶ 13-14.) The Tarrs claim title to the Disputed Area on an alternative theory, the Tarrs claim ownership by way of adverse possession through their “actual, open, hostile, continuous and exclusive possession of the Disputed Land, and Cross-Complainants are informed and believe that they have paid all taxes and assessments that have been levied or assessed against the Disputed Land for at least five (5) years prior to the filing of their Cross-Complaint.”

      The Tarrs cannot prevail on their prescriptive easement theory, as Cross-Defendant has established, and which the Tarrs do not meaningfully dispute. The Court cannot grant a  prescriptive easement based on a party’s encroachment. (Harrison v. Welch (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 1084, 1093.) Plaintiff alleges that the Disputed Area encroaches on his property and that the Tarrs are trespassing. (Complaint, ¶¶ 7, 14.) Harrison concluded that an exclusive prescriptive easement, ‘which as a practical matter completely prohibits the true owner from using his land’ (Silacci v. Abramson, supra, 45 Cal.App.4th at p. 564, 53 Cal.Rptr.2d 37), will not be granted in a case (like this) involving a garden-variety residential boundary encroachment." (Id.)

      However, the Cross-Complaint also seeks a declaration of title based on their belief that the Disputed Area is really on their property. (Cross-Complaint ¶ 11.) The Tarrs are still entitled to such a declaration if proven in order to declare the parties’ rights. (Western Aggregates at 305.)

      The claim of title is alternatively sought on grounds of “adverse possession” which is distinct from prescriptive easement. Title can be established either by prescriptive easement (prohibited under these circumstances as it involves encroachment on a neighboring party) or by adverse possession. (Harrison at 1095-1096.) Establishing title by adverse possession requires payment of taxes to acquire title while "one seeking to establish the right to an Easement by prescription need not prove payment of taxes for since an easement ordinarily is not assessed for property tax purposes, the burden of proving that it was, and that taxes were levied, is on the party contesting the prescriptive easement.” (Welsher v. Glickman (1969) 272 Cal.App.2d 134, 137.)

      To establish adverse possession "the claimant must prove: (1) possession under claim of right or color of title; (2) actual, open, and notorious occupation of the premises constituting reasonable notice to the true owner; (3) possession which is adverse and hostile to the true owner; (4) continuous possession for at least five years; and (5) payment of all taxes assessed against the property during the five-year period. " (Mehdizadeh v. Mincer (1996) 46 Cal.App.4th 1296, 1305.)

      Cross-Defendant does not address this alternative theory, and therefore, is not entitled to judgment on the pleading. Cross-complainants can plead alternative and inconsistent theories of recovery. (Rader Co. v. Stone (1986) 178 Cal.App.3d 10, 29 [“Where the exact nature of the facts is in doubt, or where the exact legal nature of plaintiff's right and defendant's liability depend on facts not well known to the plaintiff, the pleading may properly set forth alternative theories in varied and inconsistent counts.”].)

V.     CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Cross-Defendant’s motion for judgment on the pleading is DENIED. To the extent Cross-complainants wish to amend to allege other theories establishing their title, Cross-complainants should move to amend the Cross-Complaint.

 


Case Number: 25STCV25966    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV25966 Ferguson v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, et al.

Thursday, August 6, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER SUSTAINING DEMURRER OF DEFENDANT, JPMORGAN CHASE BANK, N.A., TO PLAINTIFF’S FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT (2076)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The first amended complaint alleges that Defendant, JPMorgan Chase Bank (“Chase”), approved a wire transfer from Plaintiff’s account to a Capital One, N.A. account owned by an individual who presented multiple high fraud risks that were readily discoverable by both institutions. (FAC ¶ 16.) Plaintiff alleges claims for negligence and violation of Commercial Code UCC Article 4A against both Defendants, and breach of contract and breach of fiduciary duty against Chase only.

      Chase demurs to the claims for (1) negligence, (3) breach of contract, and (4) breach of fiduciary duty.

      Defendant timely served the demurrer on Plaintiff who did not file an opposition.

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      A demurrer tests the sufficiency of a complaint as a matter of law and raises only questions of law. (Schmidt v. Foundation Health (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 1702, 1706.) In testing the complaint’s sufficiency, the court must assume the truth of the properly pleaded factual allegations as well as facts that can be reasonably inferred from those expressly pleaded facts. The court may also consider matters properly subject to judicial notice. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

III.    DISCUSSION

      Defendant argues the common law claims for negligence, breach of contract, and breach of fiduciary duty are displaced by Division 11 of the California Commercial Code which provides exclusive remedies under the alleged circumstances. This is supported by Zengen, Inc. v. Comerica Bank (2007) 41 Cal.4th 239, 253 which held that “division 11 provides that common law causes of action based on allegedly unauthorized funds transfers are preempted in two specific areas: (1) where the common law claims would create rights, duties, or liabilities inconsistent with division 11; and (2) where the circumstances giving rise to the common law claims are specifically covered by the provisions of division 11." (Id.) The intent of the drafters was to provide a "comprehensive body of law to govern the rights and obligations resulting from wire transfers. [Citations.]" (Zengen, at 253 [italics in original.].)

      In Zengen, the plaintiff’s claims were based on the allegation that the bank should not have accepted and executed the fraudulent payment order. (Id. at 254-255.) The statute sets forth the respective rights and liabilities of the parties upon the acceptance of a payment order. (Id. at 255.) Here, Plaintiff’s claims arise from conduct surrounding the alleged wire transfer.

 

IV.   CONCLUSION

      Because Plaintiff's common law claims arise entirely from the alleged fraudulent wire transfer, they are preempted. Plaintiff did not file an opposition and thus does not address the viability of those claims or the issue of preemption. Accordingly, the demurrer to the first, third, and fourth causes of action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend. Leave to amend is unwarranted because the defect is one of substantive law rather than pleading. Where the material facts are not in dispute, the nature of the claims is clear, and no liability exists as a matter of substantive law, leave to amend should be denied. (Lawrence v. Bank of America (1985) 163 Cal.App.3d 431, 436.)
