DEPARTMENT P LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25NNCV03820    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANTS’ HUNTINGTON HEALTH AND AMAL KAMIL OBAID-SCHMID, MD MOTION TO COMPEL THE DEPOSITION OF PLAINTIFF

I.         INTRODUCTION

            Plaintiff Elvis Tadevosyan ("Plaintiff") brings this medical malpractice action against Defendants Huntington Health, Amal Kamil Obaid-Schmid, M.D., Swapnil Bharat Shah, M.D., and Risser Orthopaedic Group, A Medical Corporation (collectively, "Defendants"), alleging Defendants negligently treated and discharged Plaintiff following orthopedic surgery, resulting in postoperative infection and permanent injuries.

On June 17, 2026, Defendants Huntington Health and Amal Kamil Obaid-Schmid, M.D. (“Defendants”) filed the instant motion to compel Plaintiff's deposition. No opposition has been filed.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

            C.C.P. §2025.480 provides, in pertinent part, as follows: 

(a) If a deponent fails to answer any question or to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing under the deponent’s control that is specified in the deposition notice or a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer or production. 

(b) This motion shall be made no later than 60 days after the completion of the record of the deposition and shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040. 

(c) Notice of this motion shall be given to all parties and to the deponent either orally at the examination, or by subsequent service in writing. If the notice of the motion is given orally, the deposition officer shall direct the deponent to attend a session of the court at the time specified in the notice. 

*** 

(j) The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel an answer or production, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust. commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of that party and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.

III.      ANALYSIS

            Defendants move to compel Plaintiff's deposition pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450. The motion is unopposed.

Defendants first noticed Plaintiff's deposition in July 2025. Plaintiff objected to several earlier deposition notices on the grounds that the dates were unilaterally selected, and that counsel was unavailable. Defendants thereafter renoticed the deposition several times and ultimately, after Plaintiff's counsel provided available dates, noticed Plaintiff's deposition for May 21, 2026. (Mosely Decl., ¶¶ 4-13.) Two days before the deposition, defense counsel contacted Plaintiff's counsel to confirm Plaintiff's appearance and was advised that counsel had been unable to locate Plaintiff and intended to file a motion to withdraw. (Id., ¶ 14.) On May 21, 2026, neither Plaintiff nor his counsel appeared for the deposition, and a Certificate of Non-Appearance was issued. (Id., ¶ 15.)

The Court finds Defendants properly noticed Plaintiff's deposition and made repeated good faith efforts to secure his appearance. Plaintiff failed to appear for the May 21, 2026, deposition, which was scheduled after Plaintiff's counsel provided available dates, and has shown no substantial justification for his nonappearance. The motion is therefore GRANTED pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450.

IV.       CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            The Court GRANTS Defendants motion to compel.

The parties are urged to meet and confer in person or otherwise and agree on a date for the deposition. Defendants are ordered to provide notice of this order.

Case Number: 25NNCV04643    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR ATTORNEY’S FEES IN THE AMOUNT OF $24,065.26

I.         INTRODUCTION

On July 1, 2025, Plaintiff William Gilmore ("Plaintiff") filed this action against Defendant Subaru of America, Inc. ("Defendant") asserting claims under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act arising from alleged defects in a 2023 Subaru Ascent. The parties ultimately resolved the matter through Defendant's Code of Civil Procedure section 998 offer, which Plaintiff accepted on January 23, 2026.

On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for attorney's fees. Defendant filed an opposition on July 1, 2026, and Plaintiff filed a reply on July 8, 2026.

Evidentiary objections

            The Court OVERRULES Plaintiff’s evidentiary objections. 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Attorneys’ fees are allowed as costs when authorized by contract, statute, or law. (Code Civ. Proc, § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10)(B).)   

In a lemon law action, costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees, may be recovered by a prevailing buyer under the Song-Beverly Act. (See Civ. Code, § 1794(d).) Section 1794 provides: 

“If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.” (Civ. Code, § 1794 [emphasis added].) Thus, the statute includes a “reasonable attorney’s fees” standard.   

The attorney bears the burden of proof as to “reasonableness” of any fee claim. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5(c)(5).) This burden requires competent evidence as to the nature and value of the services rendered. (Martino v. Denevi (1986) 182 Cal.App.3d 553, 559.) “Testimony of an attorney as to the number of hours worked on a particular case is sufficient evidence to support an award of attorney fees, even in the absence of detailed time records.” (Id.) 

A plaintiff’s verified billing invoices are prima facie evidence that the costs, expenses, and services listed were necessarily incurred. (See Hadley v. Krepel (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 677, 682.) “In challenging attorney fees as excessive because too many hours of work are claimed, it is the burden of the challenging party to point to the specific items challenged, with a sufficient argument and citations to the evidence. General arguments that fees claimed are excessive, duplicative, or unrelated to not suffice.” (Lunada Biomedical v. Nunez (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 459, 488, quoting Premier Med. Mgmt. Sys., Inc. v. California Ins. Guarantee Ass’n (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 564.) The Court has discretion to reduce fees that result from inefficient or duplicative use of time.  (Horsford, supra, 132 Cal.App.4th at 395.)   

In determining a reasonable attorney fee, the trial court begins with the lodestar, i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate.  (Warren v. Kia Motors America, Inc. (2018) 30 Cal.App.5th 24, 36.)  The lodestar may then be adjusted based on factors specific to the case in order to fix the fee at the fair market value of the legal services provided.  (Ibid.)  These facts include (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award.  (Ibid.)

III.      ANALYSIS

Entitlement to Attorney’s Fees

Plaintiff seeks $25,864.80 in attorney's fees, reflecting a lodestar of $21,554.00 enhanced by a 1.2 multiplier, and $2,511.26 in costs pursuant to Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d). The parties resolved this matter through a Code of Civil Procedure section 998 offer, which Plaintiff accepted on January 23, 2026.

Thus, Plaintiff is the prevailing party and entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees. 

Reasonableness of Fees

Reasonable Hourly Rate:

To calculate a lodestar amount, the Court must first determine whether the hourly rates sought are reasonable. A reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work performed by attorneys of comparable skill and experience. (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1133.)

            Here, Plaintiff seeks compensation for attorney Joseph A. Kaufman at hourly rates of $595 for work performed in 2025 and $615 for work performed in 2026, and for attorney Christopher R. Hunt at hourly rates of $375 for work performed in 2025 and $395 for work performed in 2026. Kaufman declares that he has practiced law since 2003, has devoted more than twenty-three years to litigating lemon law matters, previously and has handled thousands of lemon law matters. (Kaufman Decl., ¶¶ 3-10.) Kaufman further declares that numerous California courts have approved his requested hourly rates. (Id., ¶¶ 6-8.) Hunt declares that he has exclusively practiced consumer lemon law since joining Joseph Kaufman & Associates in 2023, has successfully handled more than 100 lemon law matters, and that numerous California cases approved his requested hourly rates. (Hunt Decl., ¶¶ 6-9.)

            Defendant argues the requested hourly rates are excessive because this was a routine Song-Beverly action and do not reflect the prevailing market rates for similar work. Defendant further contends the requested rates are unreasonable in light of the complexity of the case and that Plaintiff's proposed lodestar does not represent noncontingent market rates for the services provided. Defendant argues reasonable hourly rates would instead be $450 for Kaufman, $350 for Agyeman, and $250 for Hunt. (Opposition, pp. 12-15.)

            The Court is not persuaded. Defendant's proposed rates appear to be based primarily on its characterization of this case as routine rather than evidence of the prevailing market rate for attorneys of comparable skill and experience. Plaintiff, on the other hand, submitted competent evidence supporting the requested rates through counsel's declarations and numerous recent fee awards approving comparable rates. Thus, the Court finds the requested hourly rates reasonable.

Reasonable Hours Incurred:

The party seeking fees and costs bears the burden to show “the fees incurred were allowable, were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation, and were reasonable in amount.”  (Nightingale v. Hyundai Motor America (1994) 31 Cal.App.4th 99, 104.) 

Here, Plaintiff seeks compensation for 52.2 hours of attorney time incurred in prosecuting this action and supports the request with detailed billing records. (Kaufman Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. A.)

Defendant, in opposition, argues the requested hours are unreasonable because this was a routine Song-Beverly action that Plaintiff unnecessarily over-litigated. Defendant contends certain billing entries are duplicative, arguing that Kaufman billed for reviewing discovery, deposition notices, and communications with the client regarding depositions that overlapped with Hunt's work. Defendant also asserts Hunt improperly billed 0.9 hours reviewing the file and a memorandum that had already been reviewed by Kaufman and Agyeman. Finally, Defendant argues Plaintiff is not entitled to recover attorney's fees incurred after Defendant served its initial Code of Civil Procedure section 998 offer. (Opposition, pp. 10-12.)

The Court is not persuaded. Although Defendant identifies categories of work it contends are duplicative, it does not identify with specificity the billing entries that should be excluded or demonstrate that the challenged work was unnecessary or unreasonable. General assertions that the requested fees are excessive or duplicative do not suffice. (Lunada Biomedical v. Nunez (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 459, 488 quoting Premier Med. Mgmt. Sys., Inc. v. California Ins. Guarantee Ass’n (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 564.) On this record, the Court finds the requested hours reasonable.

      The Court finds the hours reasonable.

1.2 Lodestar multiplier

Plaintiff seeks a 1.2 multiplier ($4,310.80) based on favorable outcome obtained, the contingent risk assumed by counsel, and delay in payment. (Motion, pp. 9-11.) Defendant argues a multiplier is unwarranted because this case did not present novel or complex issues requiring exceptional skill. (Opposition, pp. 15-16.)

The Court declines to apply a multiplier. While Plaintiff obtained a favorable result and counsel litigated this matter on a contingency basis, those circumstances are common to many Song-Beverly actions. Further, this case did not involve unusually complex or factual issues and resolved through settlement before trial.

Thus, the Court DENIES the request for 1.2 lodestar multiplier.

Costs

A prevailing buyer under the Song-Beverly Act is entitled to recover reasonably incurred costs and expenses. (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d) Here, Plaintiff seeks $2,511.26 in costs. (Hunt Decl., ¶ 2, Exh. 2.) Defendant does not oppose costs.

The Court finds the requested costs were reasonably incurred and awards Plaintiff $2,511.26 in costs.

IV.       CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            The Court GRANTS Plaintiff's motion for attorney's fees and costs. Plaintiff is awarded $21,554.00 in attorney's fees and $2,511.26 in costs, for a total award of $24,065.26. The Court declines to apply the requested 1.2 lodestar multiplier.

Plaintiff is to give notice.
