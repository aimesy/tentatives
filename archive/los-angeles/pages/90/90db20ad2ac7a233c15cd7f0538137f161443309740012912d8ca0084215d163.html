DEPARTMENT H LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25PSCV03240    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: H

Hsu v. Dinalux Associates, LP, Case No. 25PSCV03240


ORDER ON DEFAULT JUDGMENT APPLICATION

Plaintiff Danny Hsu’s Application for Default Judgment is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Background

Plaintiff Danny Hsu (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows:

Plaintiff is a tenant of the commercial premises located at 750 Terrado Plaza Suite 48 in Covina (“subject premises”), pursuant to a written rental agreement with Dinalux Associates, LP (“Defendant”). In January 2022, Plaintiff first provided notice to Defendant that the building roofs had a hole and that ongoing water damage was compromising the subject premises. Defendant failed to perform any repairs at that time or thereafter, despite Plaintiff’s notice to Defendant in or around October 2023 that toxic mold was growing on the interior walls and ceilings and numerous times in or around February 2025 that conditions were worsening and adversely affecting his health. While Defendant provided Plaintiff with a temporary office in Suite 52 in February 2025, this space did not have an adequate network/telephone infrastructure and workspace. On April 2, 2025, Plaintiff underwent a Low Dose CT (LDCT) thorax examination, which revealed multiple lung nodules opined by Plaintiff’s treating physician to be caused by long-term inhalation of particulate matter and bacteria from the subject premises. 

On September 8, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against Defendant and Does 1-20 for: (1) Breach of Contract, (2) Breach of Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment, (3) Conversion, (4) Trespass to Chattels, (5) Constructive Eviction, (6) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress, (7) Negligence, and (8) Declaratory and Injunctive Relief 

On December 2, 2025, Defendant’s default was entered. 

An Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment is set for August 11, 2026.

 

Discussion 

Plaintiff’s Application for Default Judgment is denied without prejudice. The following defects are noted:

1.       Plaintiff identifies the [d]emand of complaint” in Paragraph 2 of the Request for Court Judgment (Judicial Council Form CIV-100) as $146,862.82 (i.e., including $100,862.82 in special damages and $45,000.00 in general damages). Plaintiff, in this instant default prove-up application, identifies and calculates his damages as follows: 

               “1. Inventory Loss: $9,361.00

               2. Current Medical Expenses: $4,147.75

               3. Estimated 10 Year Ongoing Medical Expenses: $40,000

               4. Mover’s Cost (Emergency Relocation): $360.00

               5. Mold Inspection Fees: $600.00

               6. Business Interruption: $46,394.27

               7. Emotional Distress: $45,000.00

               8. Total Damages: $145,863.02”

(Statement of the Case[, etc.], 4:6-18). 

“The relief granted to the plaintiff, if there is no answer, cannot exceed that demanded in the complaint, in the statement required by Section 425.11, or in the statement provided for by Section 425.115 . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 580, subd. (a); Airs Aromatics, LLC v. CBL Data Recovery Technologies, Inc. (2018) 23 Cal.App.5th 1013, 1018 [“A default judgment greater than the amount specifically demanded in the complaint is void as beyond the court’s jurisdiction”]; Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 824 [“[I]n all default judgments the demand sets a ceiling on recovery”].) 

Plaintiff’s complaint makes reference to only the following monetary amounts (aside from references to the $35,000.00 minimum jurisdictional threshold): $360.00 (i.e., for debris removal; Complaint, ¶ 18), $4,374.00 (i.e., for contaminated and destroyed inventory; Id.), $1,263.69 (i.e., for Plaintiff’s April 2, 2025 thorax examination; Id., ¶ 21), $600.00 (i.e., for professional mold inspection fees; Id., ¶ 48), $4,000.00 (i.e., for medical treatment; Id., ¶ 77) and $4,700.44 (i.e., for restitution of wrongfully collected rent; Prayer, ¶ 2). These amounts total $15,298.13. Plaintiff makes reference to business interruption losses, but identifies this amount as “TBD.” (Complaint, Prayer, ¶ 1). Further, although Plaintiff’s complaint seeks damages for personal injuries in addition to punitive damages, there is no indication that Plaintiff has served Defendant with a statement required by Section 425.11, or a statement provided for by Section 425.115. 

          2.       Paragraphs 6 and 7 of the Prayer in Plaintiff’s complaint seek declaratory and injunctive relief, respectively. The proposed judgment makes reference to monetary damages only. It is unclear to the Court whether or not Plaintiff has abandoned his request for such relief. The Court requests clarification in this regard.

3.       Plaintiff has failed to dismiss Does 1-20.

4.       None of the exhibits have been authenticated.

5.       Plaintiff has not provided the Court with any of the underlying lease documents; while these documents are purportedly attached to the complaint, the complaint is not verified.

6.       Plaintiff’s declaration is scant, consisting of ten paragraphs each comprised of one sentence. Plaintiff’s declaration is lacking in factual detail.

Accordingly, the application is denied without prejudice, and Plaintiff is directed to submit a revised and complete default judgment application that addresses the aforementioned issues. Any subsequent default prove-up application must be full and complete in and of itself. The Court will not entertain piecemeal submissions.
