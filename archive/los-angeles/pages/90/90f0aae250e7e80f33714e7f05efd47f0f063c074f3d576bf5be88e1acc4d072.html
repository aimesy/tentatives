Case Number: 24SMCV01661    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: P

Tentative Ruling

Blanca E. Vasquez v. American Honda Motor Co., Inc., Case No. 24SMCV01661

Motions:

(1)   Honda’s Motion to Compel Vehicle Inspection

(2)   Honda’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Deposition

Hearing Date: July 21, 2026

Background

Plaintiff Blanca E. Vasquez sues Defendant American Honda Motor Co. for three violations of the Song-Beverly Act pertaining to Honda’s alleged failure to repair or repurchase Plaintiff’s 2024 Honda CR-V when it experienced steering, electrical, engine, and suspension system defects. (Complaint, ¶ 10.)

On April 9, 2024, Plaintiff filed the complaint.

On November 11, 2024, the Court held an informal discovery conference regarding Honda’s failure to produce documents responsive to Plaintiff’s requests for production.

On February 13, 2026, Honda filed these motions to compel vehicle inspection and motion to compel deposition. On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed opposition to the Motion to compel vehicle inspection, but not the motion to compel deposition.

Motion to Compel Vehicle Inspection

Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010(d) allows a party to inspect land or other property in the possession, custody, or control of any other party to the action.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010, subd. (d).)¿ 

The party to whom a demand for inspection has been directed must respond with (1) a statement that the party will comply with the particular demand, (2) a representation that the party lacks the ability to comply with the demand or (3) an objection to the particular demand.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).)¿¿¿ 

Under CCP section 2031.310, on receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply:¿ 

(1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete.¿ 

(2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive.¿ 

(3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.¿ 

A motion to compel further production must set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the inspection demand. (See Cal. Code Civ. Proc., §¿2031.310(b)(1).) In¿Digital Music News LLC v Superior Court¿(2014) 226 Cal.App.4th 216 at 224, the Court defined “good cause” as a showing that there¿is¿“a disputed fact that is of consequence in the action and the discovery sought will tend in reason to prove or disprove that fact or lead to other evidence that will tend to prove or disprove the fact.”¿¿¿¿¿ 

If the moving party has shown good cause for the requests for production, the burden is on the objecting party to justify the objections. (Kirkland v.¿Sup.Ct¿(2002) 95 Cal. App.4th 92, 98.)¿¿¿¿ 

Trial courts are vested with “wide discretion” to allow or prohibit discovery. (Williams v. Superior Court¿(2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 540.)¿¿¿¿ 

Notice of the motion to compel further must be given “within 45 days of service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or any specific later date to which the requesting party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).)¿ 

It is undisputed that Honda has good cause to inspect the vehicle at issue because it is the center point of this lemon law case. (See Motion to Compel, p. 4; Opposition, p. 3) Honda represents that in the six months since this motion was filed, the parties have been unable to set a date for the inspection and therefore require a court order.

Honda’s Motion to Compel Vehicle Inspection is GRANTED.

Motion to Compel Deposition

If a party to an action is served with a deposition notice and fails to appear or proceed, the noticing party may move for an order compelling the deponent’s testimony and the production of any documents described in the deposition notice. (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450(a).)

The Discovery Act confers upon litigants the right to take depositions without prior court order or approval and, accordingly, with an exception not here applicable, does not require any showing of good cause for the taking of depositions.” (Kramer v. Superior Ct. Of Los Angeles Cnty. (1965) 237 Cal.App.2d 753, 755.) A deponent may be examined “regarding any matter, not privileged, which is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action, whether it relates to the claim or defense of the examining party or to the claim or defense of any other party.” (Id. at 756.)

“A Motion under subdivision (a) shall comply with both of the following:

(1)   The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.

(2)   The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450(b).)

“If a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 . . . in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450(g).)

Honda represents that it noticed Plaintiff’s deposition for January 19, 2026, that Plaintiff failed to appear for the deposition, and that it has been unable to take Plaintiff’s deposition in the intervening six months. (Knighton Decl., ¶¶ 5, 8.) There is undisputedly good cause to take a Plaintiff’s deposition in a civil case.

This motion is unopposed. Plaintiffs offer no justification for her failure to appear at the deposition. Honda does not request sanctions.

Conclusion

Honda’s Motions to Compel Vehicle Inspection and Plaintiff’s Deposition are GRANTED.


Case Number: 25SMCV03803    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: P

Tentative Ruling

Anthony Jones v. Velocity Commercial Capital, LLC, et al., Case No. 25SMCV03803

Plaintiff’s Motion Re: Declare Foreclosure Sale Void

Hearing Date: July 21, 2026

Anthony Jones, in pro per, sues Velocity Commercial Capital, LLC; U.S. Bank Trust company, N.A.; McCalla Raymer Leibert Pierce, LLC; Nabeel Zuberi; Vivimei, LLC; and Gaymond Lee for damages arising out of the alleged wrongful foreclosure of a property at 2704 Strongs Drive, Venice, CA 90210.

On March 9, 2026, Jones filed this motion to: (1) Declare the foreclosure sale void; (2) cancel the Trustee’s deed upon sale recorded against the property; and (3) preserve the status quo pending adjudication of Jones’ wrongful foreclosure claims.

On July 7, 2026, Defendant Viviemei, LLC filed opposition arguing that Jones’ motion is procedurally improper.

Jones’ motion is procedurally improper. He seeks resolution of the bulk of the claims in his complaint, but does not move for summary judgment or under any other code section that would allow the Court to consider his motion on the merits. (See First Amended Complaint, p. 5.)

Jones’ Motion is DENIED.


Case Number: 25SMCV04767    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: P

Tentative Ruling

Vistancia MP LLC, et al. v. Benchmark 2022-B35 Mortgage Trust, Case No. 25SMCV04767

Defendant’s Motion to Dismiss for Forum Non Conveniens

Hearing Date: July 21, 2026

Background

On September 12, 2025, Plaintiffs Vistancia MP, LLC and Martin Landis sued Defendant Benchmark 2022-B35 Mortgage Trust for declaratory relief. Plaintiffs are a group of investors who purchased real property with a loan through a Deutsche Bank subsidiary. (First Amended Compliant (“FAC”), ¶ 3.) Plaintiffs allege that the Deutsche Bank subsidiary sold the loan Defendant. (Ibid.) Plaintiffs allege that Defendant re-interpreted the Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) to add Defendant’s expenses, lowering the DSCR below the 1.20 threshold required by the Loan Agreement, and effectively eliminating Plaintiffs profit on the investment. (First Amended Complaint, ¶ 6.) Plaintiffs seek to reverse this change.

On September 12, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the initial complaint.

On September 16, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the FAC.

On September 30, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a motion for trial preference.

On October 14, 2025, Defendants filed a notice of removal, staying this case.

On April 29, 2026, the Federal Court denied jurisdiction and remanded the case.

On May 6, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Motion for Trial Preference under Code Civ. Proc. § 1062.3(a), citing the Federal Court’s remand. On June 11, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiffs’ motion for trial preference.

On June 1, 2026, Defendant filed this motion to dismiss for forum non conveniens. On June 30, 2026, Plaintiffs filed opposition. On July 6, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a notice of errata and an amended opposition. On July 7, 2026, Defendants replied.

Arguments

Defendant argues that the loan agreement at issue contains a forum-selection clause providing that any lawsuit arising out of the agreement is to be heard in state or federal court in New York County, New York. (Motion to Dismiss, p. 3.) Defendant argues that Plaintiffs’ claim for declaratory relief falls within the forum selection clause because it is an action “arising out of or relating to this agreement.” (Id. at p. 5.) Defendant argues that once the Lender exercises their option to select a New York forum, the forum selection clause is mandatory because the agreement waives plaintiffs’ venue and forum non conveniens objections. (Id. at p. 6.) Defendant argues that Plaintiffs’ allegations of Plaintiffs’ loan servicer’s residence in California and Defendant’s contact with California do not defeat the forum collection clause and that Plaintiffs cannot show that enforcement of the forum selection clause is unreasonable. (Id. at pp. 6-8.) Finally, Defendant argues that even if the forum-selection clause is construed as permissive, the traditional forum non conveniens factors require dismissal. (Id. at p. 10-13.)

In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that the forum selection clause is only effective if Defendant first exercises the option to sue. (Opposition, p. 2.) Plaintiffs also argue that the forum-selection clause is permissive because it uses the word “may,” and does not contain any exclusivity language. (Id. at p. 3.) Plaintiffs argue that much of Defendant’s motion relies on an unsworn recital in the loan agreement that is contradicted by Plaintiffs’ sworn declaration. (Id. at pp. 4-5.) Defendant argues that California is the proper forum for this action because both Plaintiffs and Defendants are California citizens. (Id. at pp. 5-6.) Plaintiffs argue that KeyBank, the loan servicer, runs Defendant’s operation, and is based in California. (Id. at p. 8-10.) Plaintiffs argue that it would not be fair to dismiss this case and require that the matter be heard in New York because New York does not have a right to reciprocal attorneys’ fees in a contract action. (Id. at p. 9.)

In reply, Defendant argues that Plaintiffs attempt to re-write the loan agreement to require that Defendant sue first in order to exercise the New York Forum option. Defendant argues that the provision allows the Defendant to exercise its forum selection right even when it is sued. (Reply, p. 1.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff focuses on the word “may,” while ignoring the rest of the sentence, which Defendants assert renders the provision mandatory. (Id. at p. 4-5.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s California-residency argument is irrelevant because it does not show unconscionability and because sophisticated business practices often involve selecting forums different from where the parties reside. (Id. at p. 6.) Defendant argues that Plaintiffs’ public policy argument is unavailing because the attorneys’ fees issue has not yet been raised and because there is no support for a finding that the public policy exception applies in this situation. (Id. at p. 8-9.) Finally, Defendant argues that traditional forum non conveniens factors favor New York. (Id. at p. 10.)

Request for Judicial Notice

Defendant requests notice of the complete loan agreement, Exhibit 1 to the Vanderfin Declaration, arguing that portions of the agreement are attached to the operative complaint, so the full agreement is subject to notice per Scott v. J.P. Morgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 743, 753.

Notice is GRANTED.

Governing Law

California Code of Civil Procedure § 410.30 provides: “When a court upon motion of a party or its own motion finds that in the interest of substantial justice an action should be heard in a forum outside this state, the court shall stay or dismiss the action in whole or in part on any conditions that may be just.” (Cal. Civ. Proc. § 410.30, subd. (a).) A mandatory forum selection clause is a clause containing “express language of exclusivity of jurisdiction.” (Olinick v. BMG Entertainment (2006) 138 Cal.App.4th 1286, 1294.) A forum non conveniens motion generally requires the balancing of public and private factors. (Id.) However, where there is a mandatory forum selection clause, the test is simply whether application of the clause is unfair or unreasonable. (Id.) Thus, Courts typically enforce mandatory forum selection clauses unless they are found to be unfair or unreasonable. (Id.; see also Lu v. Dryclean-U.S.A. of California, Inc. (1992) 11 Cal.App.4th 1490, 1493.)  

If a forum selection clause is not mandatory, courts consider the private interests of the litigants and the interests of the public in litigating the matter in one forum or another. (Stangvik v. Shiley Inc. (1991) 54 Cal.3d 744, 751.) The moving party bears the burden of establishing both components in the analysis. (Ibid.) The trial court has considerable discretion to decide motions based on forum non conveniens. (Ibid.) 

Discussion

There is no requirement that Defendant sue first

The loan agreement’s forum selection clause states that “any legal suit, action or proceeding against lender or borrower arising out of or relating to this agreement may at Lender’s option be instituted in any federal or state court in the City of New York, County of New York, pursuant to Section 5-1402 of the New York General Obligations Law . . .” (Venderfin Decl., Exhibit 1, p. 111, § 10.4(b).) It does not require that Defendant sue first to exercise its forum-selection option. If the clause required the Lender to file suit first, it would not say that the Lender has a forum-selection option in suits against the Lender, which are necessarily brought by another party. The forum selection clause is not inapplicable because Plaintiffs sued first.

The Forum Selection Clause is Mandatory

A mandatory forum selection clause contains “express language of exclusivity of jurisdiction, specifying a mandatory location for litigation.” (Olinick v. BMG Entertainment, supra, 138 Cal.App.4th at 1294.)

The loan agreement’s forum-selection clause reads:

ANY LEGAL SUIT, ACTION OR PROCEEDING AGAINST LENDEROR BORROWER ARISING OUT OF OR RELATING TO THIS AGREEMENT MAY ATLENDER'S OPTION BE INSTITUTED IN ANY FEDERAL OR STATE COURT IN THE CITYOF NEW YORK, COUNTY OF NEW YORK, PURSUANT TO SECTION 5-1402 OF THENEW YORK GENERAL OBLIGATIONS LAW, AND BORROWER WAIVES ANYOBJECTIONS WHICH IT MAY NOW OR HEREAFTER HAVE BASED ON VENUEAND/OR FORUM NON CONVENIENS OF ANY SUCH SUIT, ACTION OR PROCEEDING, AND BORROWER HEREBY IRREVOCABLY SUBMITS TO THE EXCLUSIVE JURISDICTION OF ANY SUCH COURT IN ANY SUIT, ACTION OR PROCEEDING. BORROWER DOES HEREBY DESIGNATE AND APPOINT: [Agent for Service of Process]” (Venderfin Decl., Exhibit 1, p. 111, § 10.4(b).)

Defendant’s opposition focuses on agreement’s use of the word “may,” which is generally a permissive word. However, in the context of the loan agreement’s language waiving the borrower’s objections to venue and submitting the borrower to the “exclusive jurisdiction” of a New York court, the word “may” is properly read as part of the lender’s option to exercise the forum selection clause, and not as applicable to the entire section of the loan agreement. The lender’s forum-selection option does not become effective if and until the lender chooses to exercise it. If the option did not contain permissive language like “may,” the lender’s failure to exercise the option could be construed as waiver of its contractual right to litigate the case in New York. Here, the forum-selection agreement becomes mandatory once the lender exercises their option because it states that the borrower waives their forum non conveniens objections and “irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction” of the New York court.

Plaintiffs also seemingly argues that the forum selection clause is invalid because it is an “unsworn recital,” but did not object to Defendant’s request for judicial notice. (Opposition, p. 4.) The loan agreement is properly noticed as the complete version of a contract described by the complaint, so there is no requirement that it be “sworn.” Additionally, any extrinsic evidence Plaintiffs submit is irrelevant to the Court’s interpretation of the forum-selection clause because Plaintiffs make no argument that the clause is ambiguous, nor does the Court find it to be open to multiple interpretations.

The mandatory forum selection clause applies to “any legal suit or action proceeding against lender or borrower arising out of or relating to this agreement.” This complaint falls within that provision. Since the Court finds the agreement to be mandatory, the forum-selection clause is enforceable unless the Court determines it to be unfair or unreasonable.

a.      The forum-selection clause is not unfair or unreasonable.

Plaintiffs make no argument that the forum-selection clause is unfair or unreasonable, except for its argument that enforcing the forum-selection clause would be against California public policy. Instead, Plaintiffs argue that the parties are domiciled in California, entered into the agreement in California, and that venue is proper in California. Defendant does not dispute that, absent the forum selection clause, this Court is a proper venue for this action, but Plaintiffs cite no authority holding that the Court has the power to disregard a mandatory forum-selection clause simply because it also has jurisdiction over the matter. The Court finds that the forum-selection clause is not unfair or unreasonable.

b.      Public Policy concerns do not justify denying Defendant’s motion

Plaintiffs argue that California’s law allowing for recovery of attorneys’ fees by the prevailing party if a contract only provides for one-sided fee recovery is a public policy reason to keep this case in California. However, they cite no statute or case law supporting a finding that California’s fee reciprocity is a sufficient public policy concern to justify circumnavigating a mandatory forum-selection clause. A motion for forum non conveniens would never succeed if it could be defeated by differences in state law alone.[1]

Conclusion

Defendant’s Motion to Dismiss for Forum Non Conveniens is GRANTED.

This case is DISMISSED.


[1] Neither party discusses the fact that the loan agreement also contains a choice of law clause stating that the agreement is governed by New York law. California’s attorneys’ fees reciprocity would therefore likely be irrelevant. (See Venderfin Decl., Exhibit 1, p. 110-111, § 10.4(a).)
