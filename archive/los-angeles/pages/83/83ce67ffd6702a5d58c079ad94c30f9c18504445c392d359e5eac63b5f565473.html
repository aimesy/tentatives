Case Number: 23STLC08086    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendants Janet Nazarian and AB Inspections’ Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On December 20, 2023, Plaintiff Gilberto Soto (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action for breach of contract against Defendants Janet Nazarian and AB Inspections (“Defendants”). Trial was set for June 18, 2025. Defendants filed a Demurrer to the Complaint on February 29, 2024. On April 23, 2024, the Court sustained the Demurrer to the Complaint with 30 days’ leave to amend. (Minute Order, 04/23/24.)

 

Plaintiff substituted in new counsel on June 12, 2024 and filed the First Amended Complaint on June 18, 2024. Plaintiff’s name in the First Amended Complaint changed from “Gilberto Soto” to “Gilberto Soto, doing business as Soto Ready Mix, Inc., a California Corporation” and it added a cause of action for fraud. (FAC, filed 06/18/24, p. 1.) Summons with respect to the First Amended Complaint was not filed until April 22, 2025; Proofs of Personal Service with respect to the Summons and Complaint were filed for both Defendants on May 23, 2025.

 

Following the lack of any response to the First Amended Complaint by Defendants, the Court entered their default on May 30, 2025. Default judgment was then entered on July 21, 2025 in favor of “Gilberto Soto”. In September 2025, Plaintiff substituted out counsel to become self-represented and then assigned the judgment to Brenda E. Vargas c/o Rapid Lien Recovery. (Assignment of Judgment, 09/24/25, pp. 1-3.) The Assignment of Judgment was served directly to Defendants, instead of to their counsel, Eugene W. Matthews, Esq. (Id. at p. 4.)

 

On May 19, 2026, Defendants filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment, which they served upon Gilbert Soto by first class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

As a threshold matter, the Court notes that Defendants did not serve the Motion and Notice of Hearing upon the correct party, Judgment Assignee, Brenda E. Vargas.  On September 17, 2025, the right to collect and enforce the judgment in this case was assigned to Brenda E. Vargas.  (Acknowledgment of Assignment of Judgment, 09/24/25.)  The Proof of Service attached to the Motion shows service only on the Assignee, Gilberto Soto.  Failure to give notice of a motion is not only a violation of the statutory requirements but of due process. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.) The Motion cannot be granted in light of the lack of proper notice.

 

Furthermore, however, the Motion cites as the only basis for relief Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).  (See Mot. at 1, 5.)  Motions under that provision are subject to an outer six-month deadline which is jurisdictional and not subject to tolling. (Manson, Iver & York v. Black (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 36, 42.) Because section 473(b) is the only basis raised for relief and it is necessarily time-barred, the Court denies the Motion without any continuance for service.  Relief from the entry of default and default judgment is not available to Defendants under section 473, subdivision (b).

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants Janet Nazarian and AB Inspections’ Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment is denied.

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff, in propria persona, Kimberly Yvette Hampton (“Plaintiff”), brought this action for violations of the Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (RFDCPA), California’s Unfair Competition Law (UCL), and regulations set forth by the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) against Defendants Jose G. Franco (erroneously sued as Jose G. Franco dba Franco’s Towing Service) (“Defendant Franco”) and Five Star Registration (“Defendant Five Star”) on Janaury 21, 2025. Plaintiff’s ex parte applications for injunctive relief were denied on January 21 and 23, 2025. (Minute Orders, 01/23/25 and 01/25/25.)

 

Plaintiff’s Motion for Summary Judgment was denied on February 19, 2025. (Minute Order, 02/19/25.) At that hearing, the Court also set an Order to Show Cause Re: Why Plaintiff Should not be Sanctioned $100.00 for Failure to Comply with CCP in the Litigation of This Case. (Ibid.) On March 3, 2025, Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Discovery was denied and the Order to Show Cause was discharged. (Minute Order, 03/03/25.)

 

Defendant Franco filed an Answer to the Complaint on May 5, 2025. On June 2, 2025, Plaintiff sought to file a First Amended Complaint without leave to amend. The First Amended Complaint has not been accepted for filing as there is no Court order permitting it in accordance with Code of Civil Procedure sections 472 and 473, subdivision (a)(1). On the same day, Plaintiff filed a Renewed Motion for Immediate Possession of Trust Owned Property. The Renewed Motion was denied on August 20, 2025. (Minute Order, 08/20/25.)

 

The matter came for trial on January 29, 2026, at which time service of the Summons and Complaint upon Defendant Five Star was discussed. (Minute Order, 01/29/26.) Plaintiff was ordered to file the proof of service and an Order to Show Cause regarding said service was set for April 8, 2026. (Ibid.) At the Order to Show Cause, the Court granted Plaintiff’s request to dismiss Defendant Five Star from the action. (Minute Order, 04/08/26.)

 

On April 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Immediate Release of Vehicle. Defendant filed an opposition on May 12, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff filed this action, requesting that the Court immediately restrain Defendant from selling Plaintiff’s alleged motor vehicle (“the subject vehicle”) in a pending sale, or placing a lien on the property pending the resolution of this case. (Compl., p. 6:8-11.) The Court previously denied Plaintiff’s Renewed Motion for Immediate Possession of Trust Owned Property, which was brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 526. (Minute Order, 08/20/25.) In the instant Motion, Plaintiff again seeks possession of the subject vehicle, despite the Court’s prior order. In this respect, and others, the instant Motion is defective.

 

First, as Defendant points out, the Motion is not supported by any Notice of Motion as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 1010. Without the Notice of Motion, it is not clear upon which grounds and papers Plaintiff seeks an order giving her possession of the subject vehicle. Even if the Court were to find that the Motion itself provides the grounds upon which Plaintiff seeks relief—it cites the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments and various provisions of the California Vehicle Code—none of these permits her to seek injunctive relief. Although seeking the same relief sought in Plaintiff’s previously filed Renewed Motion for Immediate Possession of Trust Owned Property, the instant Motion does not cite the injunctive relief statute, Code of Civil Procedure section 526. Section 526 explains under what circumstances a preliminary injunction can be granted before judgment. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 526, 527.)

 

Next, to the extent Plaintiff again seeks possession of the subject vehicle, the request is not brought pursuant to the correct legal authority. When a party seeks the same relief that was previously denied, it must bring a renewed motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (b). (California Correctional Peace Officers Ass’n v. Virga (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 30, 43, fn. 11; see also Tate v. Wilburn (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 150, 156-157.) When a motion has been denied in whole or in part, the moving party may apply again for the same relief at a later time only upon “new or different facts, circumstances or law.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (b); see Graham v. Hansen (1982) 128 Cal.App.3d 965, 969-970.) Also, the statute requires the moving party “to show a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence earlier, which can only be described as a strict requirement of diligence,” the purpose of which is to incentivize parties “to efficiently marshal their evidence.” (Baldwin v. Home Sav. of America (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 1192, 1199 [citing Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 689-690].)

 

The instant Motion is neither brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, nor fulfills its statutory requirements. Plaintiff’s supporting declaration does not mention her earlier request for injunctive relief, and fails to demonstrate new or different facts, circumstances or law, nor provide a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence earlier. Therefore, the Court lacks the jurisdiction to grant the renewed request to give Plaintiff possession of the subject vehicle. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (e); see Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1106.)

 

The Court also notes that trial in this matter is set for July 17, 2026, which is three days after the hearing on this motion.  Plaintiff has not shown any basis to grant “immediate release” of the vehicle to her possession three days before adjudication of the entire dispute on the merits.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds Plaintiff’s request for an injunction granting her possession of the subject vehicle cannot be granted.

 

The Court continues trial to 1:30 PM on July 17, 2026 in this Department.  All parties and witnesses must appear in person.  The Court has reviewed the Declaration of Stephen Devlin, filed July 9, 2026. Defendant is ordered to prepare and bring to trial five copies of the exhibit binder containing both parties’ exhibits.

 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Kimberly Yvette Hampton’s Motion for Immediate Release of Vehicle is denied.

 

Trial is continued to a later time on the same date:  July 17, 2026, at 1:30 PM in Dept. 26 of the Spring Street Courthouse.  All parties and witnesses must appear in person.

 

Defendant is ordered to prepare and bring to trial five copies of the exhibit binder containing both parties’ exhibits.

Case Number: 25STLC04466    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Bridger Claims Services, LLC’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions is granted. Defendant Evelyn Briggs is ordered to pay sanctions of $360.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Bridger Claims Services, LLC (“Plaintiff”) served Requests for Admissions, Set One, on Defendant Evelyn Briggs on April 3, 2026. (Motion, Gaba, Jr. Decl., Exh. A.) Despite a meet and confer effort, as of the filing of the instant Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions on June 3, 2026, Defendant has not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶¶6-7.) Plaintiff now moves for an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280. Plaintiff served Defendant with the Motion on the same date it was filed, but no opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to deem requests for admission can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to serve responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Due to Defendant’s failure to timely serve verified responses and resultant waiver of objections, Plaintiff is entitled to an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.)

 

The Court is required to award sanctions under the moving statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) However, the amount sought is excessive for an unopposed motion to deem admissions admitted; Plaintiff’s counsel does not need to spend 1.5 hours traveling to Court and attending the hearing. (Motion, Gaba, Jr. Decl., ¶8.) Sanctions are granted against Defendant in the amount of $360.00 based on two hours of attorney time billed at $150.00 per hour, plus the $60.00 filing fee. (Ibid.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Bridger Claims Services, LLC’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions is granted. Defendant Evelyn Briggs is ordered to pay sanctions of $360.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

Plaintiff Joseph Eugene Scialfa’s Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement and for Entry of Judgment is continued to 08/20/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/06/2026, Plaintiff is to file proof of service of the Motion papers and Notice of New Hearing Date. The first appearance fee for Defendant Roseark, Inc. must be paid by 08/17/2026. Failure to comply with the Court’s order may result in the Motion being denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On July 31, 2025, Plaintiff Joseph Eugene Scialfa (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Roseark, Inc. (“Defendant”). Proof of Substitute Service of the Summons and Complaint was filed on August 27, 2025. On October 1, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case. At the Order to Show Cause Re: Dismissal (Settlement) on December 1, 2025, the Court dismissed the action and retained jurisdiction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. (Minute Order, 12/01/25.)

 

On February 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement and for Entry of Judgment.

 

Discussion

 

The following issues with the Motion as filed make it improper for the Court to enforce the parties’ settlement agreement and enter judgment at this time. First, the Motion is not accompanied by a proof of service showing that Plaintiff gave Defendant notice of the Motion and hearing date. Failure to give notice of a motion is not only a violation of the statutory requirements but of due process. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.)   No provision in the settlement agreement waives notice.

 

Second, no first appearance fee has been paid with respect to Defendant. A filing fee is required for the filing of a first paper in any civil action or proceeding. (Code Civ. Proc. § 405.5.) Accordingly, the Court cannot enter a stipulated judgment against Defendant until Defendant’s first appearance fee has been paid.

 

The Court continues the hearing on the Motion to allow these issues to be remedied.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Joseph Eugene Scialfa’s Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement and for Entry of Judgment is continued to 08/20/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/06/2026, Plaintiff is to file proof of service of the Motion papers and Notice of New Hearing Date. The first appearance fee for Defendant Roseark, Inc. must be paid by 08/17/2026. Case Number: 25STLC08001    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 26


TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Appellant Fidencio Barcenas’ Motion to Set Aside Order for Lack of Notice and Request New Hearing Date is denied.

 

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

On October 21, 2025, Appellant Fidencio Barcenas (“Appellant”), in propria persona, filed the instant appeal of an administrative hearing against Respondent City of Norwalk (“Respondent”). The Court set the appeal for hearing on January 23, 2026. (Notice of Appeal, 10/22/25.) On January 23, 2026, Appellant failed to appear and the Court held the hearing with only Respondent appearing. (Minute Order, 01/23/26.) Thereafter, the Court affirmed the citation and ordered Appellant to recover nothing. (Ibid.)

 

Appellant filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Order for Lack of Notice and Request New Hearing Date on February 5, 2026. Appellant personally served Respondent with the Motion on February 26, 2026. (Proof of Service, filed 02/27/26.) At the initial hearing on April 28, 2026, the Court ordered Appellant “to file and serve an affidavit in support of the Motion, in compliance with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 2015.5.” (Minute Order, 04/28/26.) Appellant filed an affidavit the same day, however, Respondent filed an opposition on May 19, 2026 contending that it was not properly served with the supplemental papers.

 

At the next hearing on June 2, 2026, the Court continued the matter again to ensure that Respondent had sufficient time to respond to the affidavit. (Minute Order, 06/02/26.) Respondent filed its substantive opposition on June 24, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is made pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). An application for discretionary relief pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) must be made within a reasonable time, no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought, and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the defendant’s excusable mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) The statute also requires that the motion be accompanied by a copy of the moving defendant’s proposed pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Appellant timely filed the instant Motion less than two weeks after the hearing date on the appeal and supports it with an affidavit of fault. Specifically, Appellant contends that Appellant did not receive notice of the hearing on the appeal by any manner (mail, in-person, or other) and would have appeared if provided proper notice. (Motion, Barcenas Decl., p. 2.) Respondent argues in opposition that the Notice of Appeal filed by the Deputy Clerk on October 22, 2025 lists Plaintiff’s address as 15602 Cameo Ave., Norwalk, CA 90650. (Opp., Olague Decl., ¶4 and Exh. A.) This is the same address on Appellant’s papers. Respondent’s counsel also spoke to Deputy Clerk M. Guzman, who explained that the file-stamp and electronic signature on the Notice of Appeal indicate that they mailed it to Appellant’s address as listed. (Id. at ¶¶6-8.)  Consistent with this, the Notice of Appeal advised Appellant that the Court would return a copy of the Notice with the notice with “the date, place, and time of the hearing filled in” for Appellant to serve on Respondent.  Respondent asks the Court to discount Appellant’s declaration that they did not receive notice as conclusory and insufficient.


 The Court agrees that Appellant has not shown that the Clerk’s office did not return the Notice of Appeal to Appellant at Appellant’s address listed thereon, or any other basis for discretionary relief.  A letter correctly addressed and properly mailed is presumed to have been received in the ordinary course of mail.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 641.)  When a party by declaration denies receipt, the presumption is removed.  (Bear Creek Master Assn. v. Edwards (2005) 130 Cal. App. 4th 1470, 1486.) “[T]he disappearance of the presumption does not mean there is insufficient evidence to support the [finding that notice was received.]  The trier of fact must then weigh the denial of receipt against the inference of receipt arising from proof of mailing and decide whether or not the letter was received.” (Id. (internal citations omitted).) 

 

Here, Appellant’s declaration states only that Appellant did not receive notice, yet Appellant appears to have received all the other mailings in this case, including the January 23, 2026 order, which was sent to the same address.  Appellant did not file a reply to address the evidence in support of the opposition regarding service of the Notice of Appeal by the clerk’s office.  Accordingly, on this record, the Court concludes that the Notice of Appeal was mailed properly.  Appellant does not claim any other basis for discretionary relief, such as having received but misplaced the Notice, thus the Court has no representation to that effect to evaluate.  Nonetheless, receiving but misplacing the Notice of Appeal, combined with failing to follow up to confirm the hearing date in any other manner, would likely not constitute excusable neglect, i.e. neglect “that might have been the act of a reasonably prudent person under the same circumstances.”  (Huh v. Wang (2007) 158 Cal. App. 4th 1406, 1419; McClain v. Kissler (2019) 39 Cal. App. 5th 399, 415.)  This was Appellant’s appeal; a reasonably prudent person pursuing an appeal would review the docket for the hearing date at some point during the three months after the Notice of Appeal was initially filed. (See Cnty. of San Bernardino v. Mancini (2022) 83 Cal. App. 5th 1095, 1103 (failure to attend hearing inexcusable where counsel “could have easily confirmed whether the hearing would proceed . . .  by calling the court or checking the docket.”)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds relief is not warranted under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).

 

Conclusion

 

Appellant Fidencio Barcenas’ Motion to Set Aside Order for Lack of Notice and Request New Hearing Date is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC02958    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ Demurrer to the Complaint is sustained without leave to amend.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Ronnie Polk (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for civil rights violations against Defendant City of Los Angeles (“Defendant”) on April 30, 2026. On June 3, 2026, Defendant filed and served the instant Demurrer to the Complaint. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Complaint alleges causes of action for civil rights violations arising from a parking citation issued to Plaintiff. (Compl., ¶¶1-20.) Specifically, Plaintiff alleges that the Los Angeles Police Department violated its fundamental duties to consider and resolve a citizen complaint in accordance with the law. (Id. at ¶1.)  Plaintiff pursued a small claims action related to the incident but did not recover any damages. (Id. at ¶6.)  Plaintiff formally complained in a certified letter dated April 29, 2024.  (Id. at ¶7.)  After LAPD responded, Plaintiff sent a second letter on May 20, 2024.  (Id. at ¶19.)  When LAPD did not respond, Plaintiff contacted the Office of the Inspector General (“OIG”) by letter on July 29, 2024.  (Id. at ¶22.)  LAPD sent a response dated November 20, 2024 that failed to address Plaintiff’s concerns. (Id. at ¶¶24-32.)  Plaintiff again contacted OIG, and ultimately OIG responded on May 23, 2025, stating that it concluded the allegations were not proven by a preponderance of the evidence.  (Id. at ¶35-39.)  Plaintiff filed a claim for damages with the City Clerk on October 8, 2025, which was denied by written letter on November 6, 2025.  (Id. at 41.)  Plaintiff thereafter sued the City on April 28, 2025.  (Id. at ¶43.)  The Complaint asserts causes of action for what it characterizes as the following violations of California Law: (1) LAPD Failed to “Listen”; (2) LAPD Failed to “Determine Strategy”; (3) LAPD Failed to “Research”; (4) LAPD Failed to “Summarize”; and (5) LAPD Never Contacted the Plaintiff.

 

The Demurrer is accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. (Demurrer, Ford Decl., ¶3.) Defendant demurs to the Complaint for failure to allege sufficient facts, citing Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e). In support of the Demurrer, Defendant asks the Court to take judicial notice of (1) Plaintiff’s Tort Claim concerning this incident submitted to the City of Los Angeles on September 30, 2020; (2) Defendant’s Government Claims Act denial letter to Plaintiff, dated October 26, 2020; (3) Plaintiff’s Tort Claim concerning this incident submitted to the City of Los Angeles on October 8, 2025; and (4) Defendant City of Los Angeles’s Government Claims Act denial letter to Plaintiff, dated November 6, 2025. The Court takes judicial notice of these documents pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (c).

 

Defendant first demurs on the basis that the Complaint is barred by the statute of limitations under the Government Claims Act. When brought against a public entity, a claim for money damages must be submitted to said entity prior to bringing a lawsuit. (Gov. Code, § 905; Rason v. Santa Barbara City Housing (1988) 201 Cal.App.3d 817, 822.) A claim for death or injury to person or property must be submitted no later than six months after the cause of action accrued. (Gov. Code, § 911.2, subd. (a).)  Claims relating to any other cause of action must be presented within a year.  (Id.)  Leave to file a late claim can be submitted within one year. (Gov. Code, § 911.4, subd. (a).)  The Complaint must allege the plaintiff’s compliance with the claims submission. (State of Calif. v. Sup. Ct. (Bodde) (2004) 32 Cal.4th 1234, 1243-1244.)

 

Plaintiff alleges that the cause of action accrued on November 20, 2024—the date of OIG’s response to his written formal complaint—and that Plaintiff filed a claim with respect to that response on October 8, 2025. (Compl., ¶¶7,8.)  The Court is not prepared to conclude that the six-month deadline for personal injury or property claims applies to the claims raised in the instant Complaint, which concern “refusing to consider [Plaintiff’s] complaint” (RJN, Exh. 3); thus it appears that Plaintiff has pled compliance with the Government Claims act.

 

Defendant also demurs on the grounds that the causes of action are not cognizable claims under the moving statute, Penal Code section 832.5, which is cited in each of the first four causes of action, and that the fifth cause of action also does not state a claim.  The Court agrees. 

 

Section 832.5(a) states that “Each department or agency in this state that employs peace officers shall establish a procedure to investigate complaints by members of the public against the personnel of these departments or agencies, and shall make a written description of the procedure available to the public.” (Pen. Code, § 832.5, subd. (a)(1).) Plaintiff does not allege that Defendant failed to establish a procedure. Rather, Plaintiff alleges that the LAPD established a procedure and made it public, (Compl., ¶¶52-53), but that Defendant did not act in accordance with its procedures.  These allegations do not state a claim.  Government liability exists by statute. (Gov. Code, § 815, subd. (a).) The procedure created by the LAPD does not create a mandatory duty under any statute, including section 832.5. Mandatory duties are imposed by enactments, which are defined as a “constitutional provision, statute, charter provision, ordinance or regulation.” (Gov. Code, §§ 810.6, 815.6.)  Plaintiff’s allegations that Defendant did not comply with its own processes does not allege a violation of law, and therefore does not state a cause of action.  Count Five is no different; it cites only an alleged violation of LAPD’s policy manual.  

 

The Court sustains the demurrer on that basis. Plaintiff bears the burden to demonstrate that leave to amend should be granted, which must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) This burden is unmet in light of Plaintiff’s non-opposition to the Demurrer.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ Demurrer to the Complaint is sustained without leave to amend.
