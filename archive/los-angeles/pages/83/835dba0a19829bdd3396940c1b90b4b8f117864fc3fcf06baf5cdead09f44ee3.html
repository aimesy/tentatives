DEPARTMENT 224 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV11833    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 224

Love v. A-L Financial Corporation Autonation DBA South Bay Nissan et al.,

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff's Motion for Reconsideration is DENIED.

Introduction

On July 31, 2012, Plaintiff Tiffany Love filed the Complaint in this action.

On July 31, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Default Judgment.

On December 5, 2025, the Court continued the hearing on that motion. It ordered Plaintiff to give notice of the ruling requiring the responding party to file a formal opposition.

On April 17, 2026, the Court denied the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Default Judgment.

On April 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Reconsideration.

No opposition has been filed.

Legal Standard

Under Code of Civil Procedure Section 1008, subdivision (a), “When an application for an order has been made to a judge, or to a court, and refused in whole or in part, or granted, or granted conditionally, or on terms, any party affected by the order may, within 10 days after service upon the party of written notice of entry of the order and based upon new or different facts, circumstances, or law, make application to the same judge or court that made the order, to reconsider the matter and modify, amend, or revoke the prior order. The party making the application shall state by affidavit what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order to decisions were made, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown.”

Section 1008 is “the clear legislative intent to restrict motions to reconsider to circumstances where a party offers the court some fact or authority that was not previously considered by it” and could not have been considered by it. (Gilberd v. AC Transit (1995) 32 Cal.App.4th 1494, 1500.) Section 1008 is the exclusive means for modifying, amending or revoking an order. That limitation is expressly jurisdictional.” (Id. at p. 1499.)

There is a strict requirement for diligence on the moving party; the moving party must present a satisfactory explanation for failing to provide the evidence or different facts earlier. (Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 690.)

The Court lacks the jurisdiction to reconsider a prior ruling, on motion of a party, where the motion does not comply with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1008. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (e); Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1106.) The purpose of this jurisdictional bar is to protect the Court from repetitive motions. (Ibid.)

Discussion

Plaintiff asks the Court to reconsider its denial of her motion to set aside/vacate default judgment. She states that she served notice of the Court's December 5, 2025 ruling on December 29, 2025, but did not file proof of that service before the hearing.

Reconsideration or renewal under Code of Civil Procedure section 1008 requires new or different facts, circumstances, or law. It also requires a satisfactory explanation for not presenting them earlier. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subds. (a), (b); Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 690.)

Plaintiff's only "new fact" is that she mailed the notice in December 2025. That fact was within her own knowledge, and the mailing receipt was in her possession, before the prior hearing. It is not new. Her explanation for not filing it is that the task "simply escaped" her during a difficult period. Inadvertence in presenting evidence she already had is not the showing section 1008 requires.

Further, an application must be supported by an affidavit stating what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order was made, and what new or different facts are shown. Plaintiff's declaration does not identify the date of the prior ruling or the judicial officer who made it.

The motion is DENIED.

Case Number: 25STCV27327    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 224

Philly Auto Inc. v. PARS Auto Wholesales Inc. et al.,

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff's Motion to Compel Further Responses and Compliance re Requests for Production is GRANTED. Within 20 days, Defendant Pars Auto Whole Sales Inc. shall serve verified, code-compliant further responses to RFP Nos. 1-7 and 9-18, including a privilege log for any responses withheld on the basis of privilege.

Sanctions of $1,671.76 are imposed against Defendant and counsel Yasha Rahimzadeh, jointly and severally, payable within 30 days.

Legal Standard

“A party may demand that any other party produce . . . a document that is in the possession, custody, or control of the party on whom the demand is made.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010(b).) The demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that (1) a statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete, (2) a representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive, or (3) an objection in the response is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(a).) “The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand,” and “[t]he motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310(b).)

Unlike with requests to compel further interrogatories, a moving party seeking to compel further responses for requests for production must show good cause along with satisfaction of the meet and confer requirement. (CCP§ 2031.310(b).)

To prevail, a party moving for an order compelling further responses to a document production demand must first offer specific facts demonstrating “good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).) This burden “is met simply by a fact-specific showing of relevance.” (TBG Ins. Servs. Corp. v. Superior Court (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 443, 448.) If “good cause” is shown by the moving party, the burden shifts to the responding party to justify any objections made to document disclosure. (Kirkland v. Superior Court (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 92, 98.)

“The party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, or testing, or sampling has been directed shall respond separately to each item or category of item by any of the following: (1) A statement that the party will comply with the particular demand... A statement that the party ... will comply with the particular demand shall state that the production will be allowed either in whole or in part, and that all documents or things in the demanded category that are in possession, custody, or control of that party and to which no objection is being made will be included in the production. (2) A representation that the party lacks the ability to comply with the demand... A representation of inability to comply with the particular demand ... shall affirm that a diligent search and a reasonable inquiry has been made in an effort to comply with that demand. This statement shall also specify whether the inability to comply is because the particular item or category has never existed, has been destroyed, has been lost, misplaced, or stolen, or has never been, or is no longer, in possession, custody, or control of the responding party. The statement shall set for the name and address of any natural person or organization known or believed by that party to have possession, custody, or control of that item or category of items. (3) An objection to the particular demand... If only part of an item of category of item in a demand... is objectionable, the response shall contain a statement of compliance or a representation of inability to comply with respect to the remainder of that item or category. If the responding party objects to the demand... the response shall do both of the following: [a] Identify with particularity any document or tangible thing... falling within any category of item in the demand to which an objection is being made, [b] Set forth clearly the extent of, and the specific ground for the objection.... If an objection is based on a claim of privilege or a claim that the information sought is protected work product, the response shall provide sufficient factual information for other parties to evaluate the merits of that claim, including, if necessary, a privilege log.” (California Code of Civil Procedure §§ 2031.210, 2031.220, 2031.230, and 2031.240)

Good Cause

Plaintiff seeks the core files, “wash-out sheets”, and repair records pertaining to the vehicle at the center of this litigation. Good cause has been established.

Discussion

Plaintiff served Requests for Production (Set One) on February 9, 2026. On March 11, 2026, Defendant served unverified responses and a 34-page production. The responses asserted general objections, vagueness objections to Nos. 1, 2, and 5, and relevance and vagueness objections to No. 14. For Nos. 12 and 13, Defendant stated only that "[d]iscovery is continuing." Every response closed with: "Responding Party will produce all documents in its possession or control." General Objection No. 2 stated that documents would not be organized by request. (Noy Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. B.)

Plaintiff sent a meet-and-confer letter on March 16, 2026. On March 23, 2026, defense counsel replied that the letter was "without merit." Counsel declined to identify documents by request number and stated that repair documents "do not exist." Plaintiff responded the same day and followed up on April 1 and April 5, 2026. Defendant did not reply. Defendant served a verification on April 1, 2026. (Noy Decl. ¶¶ 4–7, Exs. C–D.) No opposition has been filed.

On May 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel.

Defendants have not opposed the motion.

The responses ("will produce all documents in its possession or control") are not complete statements of compliance under section 2031.220. Defense counsel represented that repair documents do not exist. Where documents do not exist or are not in Defendant's possession, custody, or control, Defendant must make a verified statement under section 2031.230, including the name of anyone believed to hold them.

Defendant has not justified its objections. (Coy v. Superior Court (1962) 58 Cal.2d 210, 220-221.) The vagueness objections fail because the terms are defined in the requests. "Discovery is continuing" is not a valid objection. The general objections are too general.

Defendant must identify produced documents by the request number to which they respond. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.280, subd. (a), 2031.320, subd. (a).)

Further response is ordered.

Sanctions

Sanctions are warranted against a party or attorney who unsuccessfully opposes, or fails without substantial justification to comply with, discovery obligations. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, 2031.310, subd. (h), 2031.320, subd. (b).) Sanctions may be awarded even where no opposition is filed. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1348(a).)

No substantial justification is shown.

Counsel claims 3.0 hours spent, 1.0 hour for a reply, and 1.0 hour for the hearing, all at $400 per hour. Because no opposition was filed, no reply time is allowed. The Court awards 4.0 hours at $400 ($1,600) plus $71.76 in filing costs, for a total of $1,671.76.

The request for sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $1,671.76.

Conclusion

Plaintiff's Motion to Compel Further Responses and Compliance re Requests for Production is GRANTED. Within 20 days, Defendant Pars Auto Whole Sales Inc.

shall serve verified, code-compliant further responses to RFP Nos. 1-7 and 9-18, including a privilege log for any responses withheld on the basis of privilege.

Sanctions of $1,671.76 are imposed against Defendant and counsel Yasha Rahimzadeh, jointly and severally, payable within 30 days.
