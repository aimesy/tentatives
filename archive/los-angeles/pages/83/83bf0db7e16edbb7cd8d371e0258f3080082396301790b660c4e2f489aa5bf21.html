I.                BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff Siala Siliga (“Plaintiff”) is a world champion kickboxer and mixed martial artist. (Complaint ¶ 6.) Plaintiff was employed by Defendant Team Combat Leave, LLC working as a professional fighter. (Complaint ¶ 7.) Defendant Ahmed Sheikh owns Team Combat Leave, LLC and is Team Combat Leave, LLC’s chief executive officer. (Complaint ¶ 11.) Defendant Ricky Funnes was Plaintiff’s coach and supervisor. (Complaint ¶ 12.) Throughout his employment, Plaintiff was subjected to discrimination and harassment because of his national origin, color, race, ancestry, and age. (Complaint ¶ 23.)

 

On April 30, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Team Combat Leave, LLC, Sheikh, Funnes, and Does 1 through 10, alleging causes of action for: (1) discrimination in violation of the FEHA of the FEHA Government Code section 12940, subdivision (I) based on race, color, national origin, and/or ancestry and age; (2) harassment in violation of the FEHA Government Code section 12940, subdivision (J), based on race, color, national origin, and/or ancestry and age; (3) failure to prevent harassment and discrimination in violation of FEHA, Government Code section 12940, subdivision (K); (4) failure to pay wages [Labor Code section 210]; and (5) intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

On April 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for leave to amend complaint (the “Motion”). Defendant Ricky Funnes opposed the motion on substantive and procedural grounds. The Court denied the motion on May 22, 2026 for failure to satisfy procedural requirements.

 

Plaintiff renewed his motion on July 22, 2026. He applied ex parte on August 2, 2026 to advance the hearing on his motion to this date. The Court granted Plaintiff’s application.

 

Defendants filed no opposition to Plaintiff’s motion, nor Plaintiff any reply.

 

Jury trial is set to begin on November 2, 2026.

 

II.             DISCUSSION

 

“A motion to amend a pleading before trial must: (1) Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a).)

The motion must be accompanied by a separate declaration, which must specify: “(1) The effect of the amendment; (2) Why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) The reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

Substantively, the Court is not satisfied with Plaintiff’s explanation why his request for amendment was not made earlier. This case was filed more than two years ago. Although Plaintiff attests he did not discover all the facts in his proposed amendments until he conducted discovery, he does not explain when the facts were discovered. Also, it is unclear why these amendments are necessary or proper this close to trial. (See Siliga Decl., ¶¶ 8-12.) Plaintiff proposes a substantial expansion of his complaint more than two years into the litigation, less than two months before trial. A better showing is necessary.

Perhaps more importantly: Plaintiff has not proven service of his motion on any defendant. There is no proof of service attached to Plaintiff’s moving papers. The only demonstrated notice to Defendants in connection with this motion is Plaintiff’s notice of his ex parte application to advance the hearing date. However, no defendants appeared at the hearing on that application. Plaintiff was ordered to give notice of hearing on this date, but he has not filed proof he did so. The Court notes that Defendants opposed Plaintiff’s most recent motion for leave to amend his complaint; their failure to do so here is conspicuous in the absence of a proof of service.

III.      CONCLUSION

The Court reserves ruling on the substance of Plaintiff’s motion. The Court instead CONTINUES the hearing to October 7, 2026, for Plaintiff to effect and prove service of his papers on all interested parties.

COURT’S [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: ¿ 

Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment,

Quash Service of Summons, and for Leave to

File Answer

 

[THE FOLLOWING IS A TENTATIVE RULING IN THE ABOVE CASE]:

 

Grant in part.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

This is a collections action. Plaintiff filed its complaint on March 9, 2026. On June 22, 2026, the clerk entered Defendant’s default, and on June 29, 2026, the Court entered default judgment.

 

On August 31, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to vacate the default and default judgment, quash service of summons, stay enforcement of the judgment, and for leave to file an answer to Plaintiff’s complaint.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD¿

 

“The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(d).)

 

III.           DISCUSSION

Defendant’s exhibits in support of her motion demonstrate she is not the person described as the recipient of service in Plaintiff’s proof of personal service. (See D. Exh. B [driver’s license contradicting description], Exh. C-D [travel records confirming absence from alleged service address].)

Based on the uncontradicted evidence, the default and default judgment are void for lack of service. The Court grants Defendant’s motion to vacate the default and default judgment.

 

Defendant’s motion to quash service is granted on the same evidentiary basis.

 

“[N]o motion [to quash service], or under Section 473 or 473.5 when joined with a motion [to quash], ... shall be deemed a general appearance by the defendant.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10(d).) Defendant has not made a general appearance and has therefore not conceded to the Court’s jurisdiction. As a result, Defendant’s motion for leave to file an answer is denied as unnecessary. Service has not been effected.

 

Defendant may file an answer at her discretion; the time for her to do so does not begin to run until she is properly served with the summons and complaint.

 

IV.           CONCLUSION 

¿ 

            Defendant’s motion is GRANTED IN PART. The Court vacates the default and default judgment. Service of the summons is quashed. Defendant’s request for leave to file an an answer is unnecessary, as service has not been accomplished.

COURT’S [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

Demurrer without Motion to Strike¿ 

 

 

[THE FOLLOWING IS A TENTATIVE RULING IN THE ABOVE CASE]:

 

Sustain with leave to amend.

I.                BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff filed this slip-and-fall action against defendants the City of Los Angeles, the State of California, the Superior Court of California, the County of Los Angeles, and Does 1-50 on April 21, 2026. He has since dismissed his claims against the City and State. On June 29, 2026, he identified Doe 1 as the Judicial Council of California by fictitious name amendment.

 

The Judicial Council of California (“Defendant”) now demurs to Plaintiff’s complaint based on Plaintiff’s failure to comply with government claims requirements. Plaintiff opposes the demurrer.

 

II.             MEET AND CONFER

¿ 

California Code of Civil Procedure section¿430.41, subdivision (a) requires parties to “meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference” before the filing of a demurrer to attempt to resolve the objections raised in the demurrer.¿The demurring party is required to file and serve with the demurrer a declaration either confirming that parties were unable to resolve the issues raised by the demurrer despite having met and conferred or that the opposing party failed to meet and confer with the demurring party in good faith. (Code Civ. Proc., §¿430.41, subd. (a)(3).)

Defendant’s counsel’s declaration does not establish she met and conferred with Plaintiffs’ counsel in person, by telephone, or via videoconference. She did not comply with section 430.41. The Court proceeds to the substance of the demurrer because Counsel exchanged substantial email correspondence; the spirit of section 430.41 is satisfied. However, the Court suggests counsel review their meeting-and-conference obligations more carefully in the future to avoid unnecessary delay.

III.           LEGAL STANDARD

 

“A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or the facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Id.)  The court also considers “judicially noticed matters.” (Id.) A complaint must contain “[a]¿statement¿of the¿facts¿constituting the cause of action, in ordinary and concise language.” (Code Civ. Proc., §¿425.10, subd. (a)(1).) A “complaint ordinarily is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts.” (Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550.)¿

¿ 

IV.           REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Defendant requests judicial notice of (1) Plaintiff’s complaint, (2) the amendment identifying Defendant as Doe 1, (3) Plaintiff’s Government Claim form signed December 12, 2025, and (4) a witness declaration in support of Defendant’s demurrer.

Requests 1 and 2 are denied as unnecessary. The records are within the Court’s file.

Request 3 is granted.

Requested 4 is denied. The declaration is not a proper subject of judicial notice.

V.              DISCUSSION

Defendant demurs on the ground that Plaintiff failed to timely submit a government claim prior to filing suit.

Defendant relies on Government Code section 915(c)(4):

“A claim, any amendment thereto, or an application for leave to file a late claim shall be presented to a judicial branch entity in accordance with the following means:

... (4) Delivering or mailing it to the Administrative Director of the Judicial Council, if against the Judicial Council or the Administrative Office of the Courts.”

At Defendant’s request, the Court has taken judicial notice of Plaintiff’s government claim. The claim states that it was submitted to the Office of Court Counsel for the Superior Court of California, County of Los Angeles – not to the Administrative Director of the Judicial Council, as required by section 915(c)(4).

Plaintiff argues Defendant should be equitably estopped from relying on the claims requirement because the form used to submit his claim bears the heading “Government Claim – Judicial Branch”. According to Plaintiff, Defendant may not author a form suggesting he can submit his claim to an entity other than the Administrative Director, then rely on Plaintiff’s submission of that form to defeat Plaintiff’s claim.

However, Plaintiff’s facts that purport to establish equitable estoppel do not appear on the face of Plaintiff’s complaint. His allegations and the judicially-noticed materials establish he did not comply with section 915(c)(4), and he has failed to allege an excuse from compliance.

The demurrer is sustained.

VI.           LEAVE TO AMEND

“Where the defect raised by a motion to strike or by demurrer is reasonably capable of cure, ‘leave to amend is routinely and liberally granted to give the plaintiff a chance to cure the defect in question.’” (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1146.)¿

 

Plaintiff contends he may plead facts that excuse him from the claims requirement. The Court permits him leave to do so.

 

IV.           CONCLUSION¿ 

 

For the foregoing reasons, the court sustains the demurrer. Plaintiff is granted leave to amend within thirty (30) days of this order.
