DEPARTMENT 408 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV08765    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 408

Defendants 1616 Holdings, Inc. and Five Below, Inc.’s Motion for Order Authorizing Substituted Service on Secretary of State as to Subpoenas for Urban Imports International, Inc. is GRANTED.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

On April 8, 2024, Plaintiff Consumer Protection Group, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants 1616 Holdings, Inc. and Five Below, Inc. (“Defendants”) alleging a single cause of action for Violation of Proposition 65.

 

On August 28, 2024, the court overruled Defendants’ Demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint.

 

On September 5, 2024, Defendants field an answer to Plaintiff’s complaint.

 

On February 4, 2026, the court denied Defendants’ Motion for Summary Judgment and granted Defendants’ Motion to Seal.

 

On June 2, 2026, Defendants filed this Motion for Order Authorizing Substituted Service on Secretary of State as to subpoenas issued to Urban Imports International, Inc. (“Urban”). On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. As of July 14, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

 

“If an agent for service of process . . . cannot with reasonable diligence be found at the address designated for personal delivery of the process, and it is shown by affidavit to the satisfaction of the court that process . . . cannot be served with reasonable diligence upon the designated agent by hand . . . the court may make an order that the service shall be made upon a domestic limited liability company [or domestic corporation] . . . by delivering by hand to the Secretary of State, or to any person employed in the Secretary of State’s office in the capacity of assistant or deputy . . . .” (Corp. Code, § 17701.16, subd. (c); Corp. Code, § 1702, subd. (a).) Process in this manner contemplates inability to serve by hand pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 415.10(a) [personal delivery], 415.20 [substitute service], or 415.30(a) [service by mail with a notice and acknowledgment of receipt]. (Ibid.)

 

III.          DISCUSSION

 

Defendants move the court for an order authorizing substituted service of subpoenas issued to nonparty Urban Imports International, Inc. (“Urban”) pursuant to Corporations Code section 1702.

 

A.   Request for Judicial Notice

 

Defendants’ Request for Judicial Notice is granted. Judicial notice may be taken of “[f]acts and propositions that are not reasonably subject to dispute and are capable of immediate and accurate determination by resort to sources of reasonably indisputable accuracy.” (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (c), (h).) 

         

B.    Merits

 

Defendants provide that Urban has a principal place of business in Los Angeles, California with Ashkan Naami (“Naami”) as its officer and agent for service of process. (Motion, at p. 7; Ellis Decl., ¶ 14, Exh. I.) The Statement of Information for Urban,  as filed with the California Secretary of State, lists a principal address, an address for its agent for service of process, and a P.O. Box for its corporate officer. (Ibid.) On January 20, 2026, Defendants issued a deposition subpoena for Urban’s PMK using the address listed for its registered agent. (Motion, at p. 7; Ellis Decl., ¶ 15, Exh. J.) The deposition subpoena was provided to a third party process server, First Legal, for service at 2833 Leonis Boulevard #104, Huntington Park, CA 90255. (Motion, at p. 7; Ellis Decl., ¶ 16.) On January 21, 2026, First Legal was unable to serve Urban at such location as there was no street number matching this address. (Motion, at pp. 7-8; Ellis Decl., ¶ 16, Exh. K.)

 

Subsequently, Defendants re-issued the subpoena updating the address to 4601 S. Soto Street, Vernon, CA 90255 as listed for the principal office of Urban. (Motion, at p. 8; Ellis Decl., ¶ 17, Exh. L.) On January 22, 2026, First Legal was unable to serve Urban reporting that the address was for a suite located in a building owned by Lincoln International Inc. where Urban rents a space. (Motion, at p. 8; Ellis Decl., ¶ 18, Exh. M.) First Legal further stated that the process server spoke to a woman at the front desk of the office building who stated that the Urban office is hardly open; that the employees do not have a set time they come in; and that employees come in approximately once a month. (Ibid.)

 

On January 23, 2026, Defendants again re-issued the subpoena updating the address to 3977 Marcasel Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90066 for Naami. (Motion, at p. 8; Ellis Decl., ¶ 20, Exh. P.) However, on January 26, 2026, First Legal was unable to serve Urban or Naami as there was no answer and no activity seen at the location. (Motion, at pp. 8-9; Ellis Decl., ¶ 21, Exh. Q.) On January 27, 2026, First Legal made a second unsuccessful attempt to serve at such address reporting that an unknown person spoke from behind the door and failed to respond or open the door when the process server identified herself. (Ibid.)

 

On January 27, 2026, Defendants mailed the subpoena via USPS Certified Mail to Urban’s P.O. Box at 2305, Huntington Park, CA 90255, with no response. (Motion, at p. 9; Ellis Decl., ¶ 22, Exh. R.) On February 19, 2026, Defendants served an amended subpoena on Plaintiff, requesting Urban’s PMK’s appearance on April 2, 2026 which was also unsuccessful. (Motion, at p. 9; Ellis Decl., ¶ 23, Exh. S.)

 

          In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Defendants failed to demonstrate that Section 1702 authorizes substituted service of a deposition subpoena on a nonparty corporation. (Opp., at pp. 4-6.) Plaintiff further argues that, even if Section 1702 was applicable, Defendants failed to show reasonable diligence to warrant substituted service on Urban. (Id., at pp. 6-9.)

 

          The court finds that substituted service as prescribed under Section 1702 applies to service of “process” which is defined as a writ or summons issued in the course of a judicial proceeding, including a subpoena. (Code Civ. Proc., § 17, subd. (7); Code Civ. Proc., § 1985, subd. (a).) Moreover, the court finds that Defendants sufficiently demonstrated that the subpoena cannot with reasonable diligence be served based on the information provided.

 

Accordingly, the motion is granted.

 

IV.          CONCLUSION

 

Defendants 1616 Holdings, Inc. and Five Below, Inc.’s Motion for Order Authorizing Substituted Service on Secretary of State as to Subpoenas for Urban Imports International, Inc. is GRANTED.


Case Number: 25STCV19540    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 408

Plaintiff Curtis Addison’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Sanctions as to Defendant FCA US, LLC is DENIED.

 

I.                BACKGROUND

 

On June 30, 2025, Plaintiff Curtis Addison (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendants FCA US, LLC and San Leandro Chrysler Dodger Jeep Ram (“Defendants”) alleging causes of action for:

 

1.     Violation of Subdivision (d) of Civil Code Section 1793.2;

2.     Violation of Subdivision (b) of Civil Code Section 1793.2;

3.     Violation of Subdivision (a)(3) of Civil Code Section 1793.2;

4.     Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability;

5.     Negligent Repair; and

6.     Fraudulent Inducement Concealment.

 

On January 8, 2026, the court sustained in part Defendants’ Demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint without leave to amend as to the fifth cause of action, overruled in part as to the sixth cause of action, and denied Defendants’ Motion to Strike portions of Plaintiff’s complaint.

 

On January 28, 2026, Defendant FCA US, LLC (“FCA”) filed an answer to Plaintiff’s complaint.

 

On May 21, 2026, the court dismissed Defendant San Leandro Chrysler Dodger Jeep Ram with prejudice.

 

On May 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures as to FCA. On July 8, 2026, FCA filed an opposition. As of July 15, 2026, no reply has been filed.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

In civil actions seeking restitution or the replacement of a motor vehicle pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.20, within 60 days of the filing of an answer or other responsive pleading, the “parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (b).)

 

Defendants must provide the documents outlined in subdivision (h), as follows: “The defendant or manufacturer shall provide the following documents to all other parties pursuant to the timelines prescribed in subdivision (b):

 

 

“The defendant or manufacturer shall provide the following documents to all other parties pursuant to the timelines prescribed in subdivision (b): 

 

(1) Copy of or access to a version of the owner's manual for a motor vehicle of the same make, model, and year. 

(2) Any warranties issued in conjunction with the sale of the motor vehicle.

(3) Sample brochures published for the motor vehicle. 

(4) The motor vehicle's original invoice, if any, to the selling dealer.

(5) Sales or lease agreement, if the manufacturer is in possession. 

(6) Motor vehicle information reports, including build documentation, component information, and delivery details. 

(7) Entire warranty transaction history for the motor vehicle. 

(8) Listing of required field actions applicable to the motor vehicle.

(9) Published technical service bulletins for the same make, model, and year reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle. 

(10) Published information service bulletins for the same make, model, and year reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle. 

(11) Records relating to communications between the manufacturer or dealership and the owner or lessee of the motor vehicle, including those related to repair orders or claims involving the motor vehicle. 

(12) Warranty policies and procedure manuals. 

(13) Service manuals reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle. 

(14) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, all call recordings of pre-suit communications with the consumer available at the time of service of the complaint. 

(15) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, the manufacturer's written statement of policies and procedures used to evaluate customer requests for restitution or replacement pursuant to “Lemon Law” claims.

(16) If a pre-suit restitution or replacement request is made, any nonprivileged, prelitigation evaluation. 

(17) Any warranty extensions or modifications issued by the manufacturer on the motor vehicle.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (h).) 

 

Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: “A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff's attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(j)(1).) 

 

III.          DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff seeks to compel initial disclosures from FCA pursuant Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(h), arguing that FCA filed its Demurrer on November 5, 2025, and the 60-day deadline for initial disclosures expired on January 4, 2026. (Motion, at pp. 5-6.) On May 19, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel sent a meet-and-confer letter to FCA’s counsel identifying deficiencies in the information provided. (Id., at pp. 5-6.) Plaintiff argues that as of May 29, 2026, FCA failed to cure the deficiencies or provide verified responses, and therefore FCA has failed to comply with its statutory obligations under Section 871.26. (Id., at p. 6.) Specifically, Plaintiff argues that FCA did not provide all documents required under Section 871.26, subdivisions (h)(6), (h)(7), (h)(8), (h)(9), (h)(10), (h)(12), and (h)(13). (Id., at p. 2.)

 

In opposition, FCA argues verified written responses are not required under Section 871.26. (Opp., at pp. 2-3.) FCA further argues that Section 871.26 requires limited initial disclosures, and Plaintiff’s requests go beyond the scope of the statute. (Id., at p. 3.) Accordingly, FCA contends that it has produced the required documents, rendering Plaintiff’s motion moot. (Id., at p. 4.)

 

The court finds that there is no indication that FCA did not comply with its statutory obligation to propound initial disclosures. The information Plaintiff seeks goes beyond the scope of Section 871.26(h) and may be requested through formal discovery procedures, rather than through a motion to compel initial disclosures. There is also no requirement under Section 871.26 that a manufacturer serve verified written responses in conjunction with its initial disclosures. Accordingly, the motion and the request for sanctions are denied.

 

IV.          CONCLUSION

 

Plaintiff Curtis Addison’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Sanctions as to Defendant FCA US, LLC is DENIED.
