MOTION TO VACATE DEFAULT AND DEFAULT JUDGMENT

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(d))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court GRANTS Salvador Morales’s motion. 

 

The May 6, 2026 default and June 15, 2026 default judgment are hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED. 

 

The purported service on Morales is quashed.  The writ of execution naming Morales as an additional judgment debtor is recalled and quashed as to him only.

 

            Morales is to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Karapet Dermendjian Inc. sued Carmen Isabel Estrada for breach of contract and fraud.  Karapet then amended the complaint to name Salvador Morales as Doe 2.[1]  Karapet obtained a default judgment against Estrada and Morales on June 15, 2026.  Morales then filed a motion and amended motion to vacate the default and default judgment, but failed to serve them on Karapet.  The Court continued this hearing to allow for proper service and proof of that service to be filed, which Morales did.  Morales moves to have the judgment and underlying default vacated under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), arguing the default and default judgment are void for invalid service of process.

 

The default and default judgment are void for noncompliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 474, which applies to fictitiously named defendants.  That section bars entry of a default or default judgment against a fictitiously named defendant “unless it appears that the copy of the summons or other process . .  . bore on the face thereof a notice stating in substance” that the person is “served . . . as (or on behalf of) the person sued under the fictitious name of (designating it).”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 474.)  The proof of service must also “state the fictitious name under which such defendant was served and the fact that notice of identity was given by endorsement upon the document served.”  (Ibid.)  Compliance with both the summons and proof-of-service requirements is mandatory before default or default judgment may be entered.  (Pelayo v. J. J. Lee Management Co., Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 484, 496–497 (Pelayo).)

 

Whether or not it complied with the summons requirement, Karapet did not comply with the proof-of-service requirement.  Both the original and amended proofs of service leave blank the “Notice to the Person Served” entry for service “as the person sued under the fictitious name of (specify),” and instead check “as an individual defendant.”  Furthermore, neither states the fictitious name under which Morales was served nor that the notice of identity was given by endorsement.  These recitals are mandatory prerequisites that must appear in the proof of service; their omission from the proof of service makes the default and default judgment void.  (Pelayo, supra, 174 Cal.App.4th at pp. 496–497.)

 

The Court notes that service of the amendment to complaint on Morales does not cure the defect.  The amendment document is not “process” within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure section 474.  (Carol Gilbert, Inc. v. Haller (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 852, 859.) 

 

Accordingly, Morales is entitled to relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) for invalid service of process.  The Court therefore GRANTS Morales’s motion.  The May 6, 2026 default and June 15, 2026 default judgment are hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.  The writ of execution naming Morales as an additional judgment debtor is therefore recalled and quashed as to him only.  The Court ORDERS Karapet to SHOW CAUSE on November 4, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse, regarding the service of process on Morales.

 

            Morales is to give notice.

RESP. PARTY:         None  

 

DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.10, et seq.)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

            A demurrer must be filed and served on all relevant parties at least 16 court days—plus five additional calendar days if service is done by mail—before the hearing in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 1005.

 

            Andy Miri served Juan Pinedo, a self-represented litigant, with his demurrer by mail on August 31, 2026, only 15 court days before this hearing.  To give Pinedo the statutory notice period and time to file an opposition if he wishes to do so, the Court CONTINUES this hearing to November 5, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

            Miri is to give notice.
