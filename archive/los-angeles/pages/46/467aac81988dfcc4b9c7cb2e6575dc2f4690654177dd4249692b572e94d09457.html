Case Number: 22STCV19406    Hearing Date: September 8, 2026    Dept: H

Hernandez v. Dean Enterprises, et al., Case No. 22STCV19406


ORDER ON MOTIONS IN LIMINE

 

Plaintiff’s Motions in Limine 

For the reasons stated herein, Plaintiff’s motions in limine are summarily denied as untimely. 

On June 23, 2026, the Court held a hearing on Plaintiff’s ex parte application for an order continuing trial and trial-related dates. As reflected in the minute order, the Court granted the ex parte application and continued the Final Status Conference (“FSC”) to September 8, 2026, at 10 a.m. and the Jury Trial to September 22, 2026, at 10 a.m. That order further directed the parties to “carefully review this order and file pretrial documents in accordance with this order.” (Minute Order dated 6/23/2026.) 

As to motions in limine, the order was clear that “[m]otions in limine will be heard at the time of the FSC,” that motions in limine “shall be filed and served fourteen (14) calendar days before the FSC,” and that “[a]ny opposition to the motions in limine shall be filed and served seven (7) calendar days before the FSC.” (Id.) 

Based on the Court’s order, motions in limine were required to be filed and served by August 25, 2026. Plaintiff’s motions in limine were filed on September 4, 2026, just one court day prior to the FSC. Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motions in limine are summarily denied as untimely.

That said, as noted below, the Court obviously will follow controlling law and not permit improper argument, such as argument appealing to jurors’ self-interest, jurors’ personal circumstances, or the broader interests of the community; counsel shall ensure that all arguments are confined to the evidence presented and the specific parties, claims, and issues in this case. 

 

Defendants’ Motions in Limine

Defendants filed ten timely motions in limine on August 25, 2026. Oppositions were due September 1, 2026. As of this date, no opposition briefs have been filed. At the time of the FSC, the Court will hear from Plaintiff’s counsel as to why the Court should not summarily grant Defendants’ motions in limine based on Plaintiff’s failure to file any timely opposition. The Court will also hear from Plaintiff’s counsel as to which specific motions in limine (if any) Plaintiff opposes. 

For the parties’ guidance, the Court notes that it does not appear that any of the following issues are relevant to the claims in this trial: (a) unrelated habitability, construction issues, or complaints, (b) evidence of other lawsuits and claims against Defendants, (c) evidence of Defendants’ wealth or other properties, and (d) evidence of Defendants’ insurance. 

The Court also advises the parties that its standard practice is to prohibit references to specific dollar amounts during voir dire, although counsel may ask qualitative questions regarding jurors’ views on damages, such as whether any juror would have difficulty awarding “a substantial damages award” if warranted by the evidence. As to references to monetary amounts during opening statement, the Court generally does not permit reference to specific noneconomic damages figures, as such reference would be argumentative; however, counsel may refer to specific economic damages figures in opening statement to the extent those figures are expected to be supported by admissible evidence. 

Finally, the Court obviously intends to follow controlling law, including Kennemur v. State of California (1982) 133 Cal. App. 3d 907, and will not permit improper argument, including argument appealing to jurors’ self-interest, jurors’ personal circumstances, or broader societal interests. Counsel shall ensure that all arguments are confined to the evidence presented and the specific parties, claims, and issues in this case.

Case Number: 23PSCV01923    Hearing Date: September 8, 2026    Dept: H

Grassy Sprain Group, Inc. v. Medgomics Inc., et al., Case No. 23PSCV01923

 

ORDER ON DEFAULT JUDGMENT APPLICATION

 

Plaintiff Grassy Sprain Group, Inc.’s Application for Default Judgment is DENIED without prejudice.

 

Background 

Plaintiff Grassy Sprain Group, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) is a debt buyer. Plaintiff was assigned its collection rights from LoanMe, Inc. 

On June 28, 2023, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against Medgomics Inc. (“Medgomics”), Steve Sommer (“Sommer”) and Does 1-10 for: (1) Open Book Account, (2) Account Stated, (3) Reasonable Value of Goods/Services Received/Funds Provided, (4) Agreement, (5) Promissory Note, and (6) Quantum Meruit. 

On June 10, 2024, an “Order for Publication” as to Sommer was entered; on October 23, 2024, Plaintiff filed proof of publication. 

On December 6, 2024, Sommer’s default was entered. 

On February 27, 2025, an order directing that service of the summons as to Medgomics be made on the California Secretary of State was entered. 

On July 31, 2025, Medgomics’ default was entered. 

An Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment is set for September 8, 2026. 

 

Discussion 

Plaintiff’s Application for Default Judgment is denied without prejudice. The following defects are noted:

1.       The Court has denied Plaintiff’s application for default judgment on two prior occasions. On October 16, 2025, the Court issued an order denying the application without prejudice and noting that “Exhibit B does not contain any signatures, electronic or otherwise. Plaintiff is directed to clarify for the Court whether or not the entire document has been provided.” Then, on May 8, 2026, the Court again denied the application for default judgment, noting that the same defect persisted and referencing the prior October 16, 2025 order. Specifically, the order stated:

“The Court denied Plaintiff’s previous default prove-up application on October 16, 2025 without prejudice; in doing so, the Court observed, in part, that: 

‘Exhibit B does not contain any signatures, electronic or otherwise. Plaintiff is requested to clarify for the Court whether or not the entire document has been provided.’

This defect persists. The Court notes that the Note makes reference to, inter alia, ‘the undersigned,’ to ‘the personal guarantee signed below,’ and to ‘no party who signs this Note,’ indicating that there is a signature page. There is also a paragraph entitled ’Electronic Signatures.’ The Court requests that these signatures be furnished or that Plaintiff explain why said signatures cannot be furnished.” 

Plaintiff still has failed to provide Exhibit B containing signatures or provide any explanation as to why signatures cannot be furnished. In the declaration in support of Plaintiff’s request for default judgment, Elizabeth Schaefer does not provide any explanation as to why Exhibit B does not contain signatures. The Court asks the moving party to brief this issue to explain why this unsigned document is sufficient to constitute an enforceable contract.

 

2.       Again, Plaintiff has utilized Judicial Council Form CIV – 105 Request for Entry of Default, which is “[f]or use only in actions under the Fair Debt Buying Practices Act.” The Note is entitled “LoanMe, Inc. Small Business Loan” and provides that “the proceeds of this Loan will be used solely for business and commercial purposes and not for any agricultural, personal, family or household purpose.” The Court directs Plaintiff use Judicial Council Form CIV-100.

 

Accordingly, the Application is denied without prejudice, and Plaintiff is directed to submit a revised and complete default judgment application that addresses the aforementioned issues. The Court continues the Order to Show Cause Re Default Judgment to December 15, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. The revised and complete default judgment application is due by November 15, 2026. The Court also sets an Order to Show Cause Re Dismissal for Failure to Obtain Default Judgment for December 15, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. given that Plaintiff has failed to address the specific deficiencies identified by the Court in two prior orders.

 

Case Number: 25PSCV03112    Hearing Date: September 8, 2026    Dept: H

Noriega v. Los Angeles County Fire Department, et al., Case No. 25PSCV03112

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO CONTINUE TRIAL

Defendant County of Los Angeles’ Motion to Continue Trial, the Mediation Completion Deadline, and All Trial-Related Dates and Deadlines is GRANTED.

 

Background

Plaintiff Christopher John Noriega (“Plaintiff”) alleges that on August 4, 2024, Plaintiff sustained injuries from a side-swipe motorcycle versus automobile collision.

On August 27, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against Defendants Los Angeles County Fire Department, County of Los Angeles (“County”), City of Los Angeles (“City”), Giobani Rigoberto Lopez Meza, and Does 1-50 for: (1) Negligence, (2) Negligence Entrustment, and (3) Negligent Hiring, Training, Retention, and Supervision.

On December 15, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed City as a defendant.

The Final Status Conference is set for October 9, 2026. Trial is set for October 21, 2026.

Pending before the Court is County’s motion to continue trial, the mediation completion date, and all trial-related dates and deadlines.

 

Legal Standard 

“Every court shall have the power to . . . amend and control its process and orders so as to make them conform to law and justice. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 128, subd. (a).) 

“A party seeking a continuance of the date set for trial, whether contested or uncontested or stipulated to by the parties, must make the request for a continuance by a noticed motion or an ex parte application. . . , with supporting declarations. The party must make the motion or application as soon as reasonably practical once the necessity for the continuance is discovered.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(b).)

“Although continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance. Circumstances that may indicate good cause include: . . . (6) A party’s excused inability to obtain essential testimony, documents, or other material evidence despite diligent efforts. . .” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(c).)

“In ruling on a motion or application for continuance, the court must consider all the facts and circumstances that are relevant to the determination. These may include:

(1) The proximity of the trial date;

(2) Whether there was any previous continuance, extension of time, or delay of trial due to any party;

(3) The length of the continuance requested;

(4) The availability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion or application for a continuance;

(5) The prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; . . .

(7) The court’s calendar and the impact of granting a continuance on other pending trials; . . .

(10) Whether the interests of justice are best served by a continuance, by the trial of the matter, or by imposing conditions on the continuance; and

(11) Any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1332(d).)

 

Discussion

County moves the Court for an order, per Code of Civil Procedure sections 128 and 2024.050 and California Rules of Court, rule 3.1332, to continue trial four months from the current date of October 21, 2026, and all pre-trial related deadlines, including discovery cutoff. 

County argues that, despite exercising diligence, essential discovery remains outstanding. Although County deposed three third-party eyewitnesses in May 2026, it contends those depositions could not be completed because Plaintiff’s counsel instructed the witnesses not to answer certain questions and terminated one of the depositions. County further represents that four to five additional eyewitnesses have not been deposed and that the incomplete factual record will interfere with the parties’ expert discovery. 

Plaintiff opposes the continuance. Plaintiff argues that the Court has already addressed the parties’ inability to meet the former mediation deadline by ordering them to participate in a Mandatory Settlement Conference through Resolve Law. Plaintiff further argues that County caused the mediation delay by failing to confirm a July 15, 2026, mediation date or select from fourteen subsequently proposed dates. Plaintiff also notes that his physical examination was confirmed for August 18, 2026, more than one month before the fact-discovery cutoff. According to Plaintiff, the remaining discovery can be completed under the existing schedule, and a continuance would prejudice him because he continues to experience physical pain, has unpaid medical expenses, has missed work for treatment, and has already endured nearly two years of litigation. Plaintiff additionally argues that County did not provide the meet-and-confer declaration required to reopen discovery under Code of Civil Procedure section 2024.050. 

The Court has reviewed the accompanying declarations and determines that a continuance of the trial date is warranted. While Plaintiff has agreed to proceed with the physical examination, that development does not resolve the outstanding percipient-witness discovery. County began taking the eyewitness depositions approximately five months before trial and filed motions to compel further deposition testimony when the depositions could not be completed. In addition to the resumed depositions, County represents that four to five additional eyewitnesses remain to be deposed. This is the first request to continue the first trial date, the requested continuance is limited to approximately 120 days, and the motion was filed more than two months before trial. Although Plaintiff identifies the hardship associated with additional delay, he does not identify any loss of evidence, witness unavailability, or comparable trial-related prejudice that would outweigh the need to try this disputed-liability action on an incomplete record. The Court therefore finds good cause to continue the trial under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1332. 

As to the request to continue other trial-related deadlines, including the discovery cutoff, the Court tends to agree with Plaintiff that County has not provided a sufficient meet-and-confer declaration regarding the discovery and discovery-motion deadlines under Code of Civil Procedure section 2024.050. While the Court admonishes County’s counsel in this regard, the Court nonetheless finds that based on the status of discovery, there is good cause to continue all trial-related deadlines, including the discovery cutoff date. As such, the motion to continue trial and all trial-related dates is GRANTED. 

The Court will discuss continued dates with the parties at the time of the hearing. The Court’s order requiring the parties to participate in a Mandatory Settlement Conference through Resolve Law no more than 60 days prior to trial remains in effect.
