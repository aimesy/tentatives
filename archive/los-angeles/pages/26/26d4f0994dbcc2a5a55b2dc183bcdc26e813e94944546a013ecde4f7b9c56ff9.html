DEPARTMENT N LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22SMCV01309    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant Jason Varughese’s Motion for a New Trial is DENIED.

Defendant Jason Varughese’s Motion to Set Aside and Vacate Judgment and to Enter a Different Judgment is DENIED.

Defendant Jason Varughese to give notice.

REASONING

Request for Judicial Notice
Defendant Jason Varughese (“Defendant”) requests judicial notice of seven volumes of reporter’s transcripts from the trial in this action, exhibits admitted at trial, the special verdict, the judgment, and minute orders. Defendant’s request is GRANTED pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d). Defendant also requests judicial notice of exhibits not admitted at trial. There is no basis to take judicial notice of such evidence; thus, Defendant’s request is DENIED in that regard.

Plaintiff Grace Kang (“Plaintiff”) requests judicial notice of the verdict in four actions in California trial courts. Plaintiff’s request is GRANTED pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d); however, the Court notes that such verdicts are not binding on this Court in any way and simply show the conclusion reached by different juries.

Analysis
This action went to trial on May 4, 2026 until May 12, 2026. On May 11, 2026, the jury returned a Special Verdict finding in Plaintiff’s favor as to her first cause of action for violation of Penal Code section 11172, subdivision (a), and second cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress, with $1,500,000 in damages as to the first cause of action and $2,000,000 as to the second cause of action. On May 12, 2026, the jury returned a Punitive Damages Verdict finding in Plaintiff’s favor as to her second cause of action only, awarding punitive damages of $1,000,000, for a total award of $4,500,000. On June 16, 2026, Defendant filed a notice of intention to move for new trial and notice of intention to move to set aside and vacate judgment and to enter a different judgment. The supporting memoranda of points and authorities were timely filed 10 days thereafter.

Code of Civil Procedure section 657 sets forth the grounds upon which a verdict may be vacated, in whole or in part, and a new or further trial granted on all or part of the issues. “The determination of a motion for a new trial rests so completely within the court’s discretion that its action will not be disturbed unless a manifest and unmistakable abuse of discretion clearly appears.” (Jiminez v. Sears, Roebuck & Co. (1971) 4 Cal.3d 379, 387.) Similarly, Code of Civil Procedure section 663 states that an aggrieved party may move to set aside and vacate a judgment based upon a decision by the Court or the special verdict of a jury when the judgment materially affects “the substantial rights of the party” and thereby entitles “the party to a different judgment” because (1) there was an “[i]ncorrect or erroneous legal basis for the decision, not consistent with or not supported by the facts” or (2) the “judgment or decree [is] not consistent with or not supported by the special verdict.”

In his motion for a new trial, Defendant argues that the Court improperly excluded evidence of his good faith state of mind while admitting prejudicial evidence over objection, the evidence was insufficient as to the culpable mental state required by Penal Code section 11172 and the element of severe emotional distress, the damages are excessive as a matter of law because the compensatory damages award duplicates emotional distress damages across both causes of action while the punitive damages award rests on no evidence of ability to pay, the Court improperly ordered Defendant to retain counsel to depose Plaintiff and appear at a settlement conference, the Court improperly excluded Defendant’s treating clinical psychologist, and certain documentary evidence was improperly excluded. In his motion to set aside and vacate the judgment, Defendant argues again that the judgment is awards duplicative emotional distress damages, the conduct does not satisfy Penal Code section 11172, the punitive damages award was not based on Defendant’s ability to pay, and the Court improperly included and excluded certain evidence.

Put simply, Plaintiff is seeking a second bite at the proverbial apple in these motions, making the same arguments already considered and rejected by the Court in prior rulings and making new arguments he failed to assert at trial. These arguments do not properly or accurately reflect the court record. As to the Court’s evidentiary rulings, the Court excluded the therapist because Defendant did not identify him as an expert, and his proposed testimony was impermissible expert testimony and hearsay; the Court excluded the family law attorney’s testimony because she was also not identified as a potential witness during discovery; Defendant did not produce certain evidence during discovery; Defendant failed to object to certain testimony; the Court limited the audio recording evidence to avoid prejudice to Defendant; and Defendant failed to properly challenge the admission of certain reports. As to the Penal Code section 11172 claim, the record contained substantial evidence of Defendant’s culpable state of mind, which the jury credited, and as to the severe emotional distress finding, the record contained substantial evidence of Plaintiff’s emotional distress, such as describing the investigation as “very traumatic” and that she lives in constant fear. As to the damages, the jury was properly instructed, Defendant provides no evidence that the jury did not properly follow the instructions, the jury awarded different amounts under different categories, the harm differed as to each claim, the punitive damages award tends to indicate that the jury did not conflate the two causes of action, and Defendant did not object to the special verdict form. As to punitive damages, the evidence supported a jury finding that Defendant acted with malice, oppression, or fraud, and Defendant failed to comply with a notice to provide financial information, thereby warranting an adverse inference instruction as to his financial state. Finally, as to pretrial proceedings, Defendant provides no cognizable basis to find that any proceedings was improper; specifically, as to the order to obtain counsel, this was based on Defendant’s prior conduct in misusing discovery. Moreover, Defendant cannot demonstrate that any of the purported errors would have made any difference in the jury’s verdict. Accordingly, Defendant Jason Varughese’s Motion for a New Trial is DENIED, and Defendant Jason Varughese’s Motion to Set Aside and Vacate Judgment and to Enter a Different Judgment is DENIED.

Case Number: 25SMCV03455    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Jeff A. Mann of Law Offices of Jeff A. Mann’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant Yousef Maghen, M.D. is CONTINUED to TBD for counsel to provide sufficient proof of service of all documents associated with this motion as previously ordered.

Counsel has asserted a valid reason for withdrawal, i.e., the clients has breached material obligations under the parties’ retention agreement, and the attorney-client relationship has broken down. (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4), (b)(5).) Counsel has provided the required forms under rule 3.1362 of the California Rules of Court, but he failed to provide proof of service of the proposed order as required by rule 3.1362, subdivisions (d) and (e), of the California Rules of Court. While the proof of service filed on July 16, 2026, shows service of an unidentified order, the Court lacks a basis to conclude that the order served was the one related to the present motion. Moreover, the Court previously stated concerns about service of the motion, and the Court ordered counsel to re-serve the motion, but counsel has provided proof of service only as to notices of continued hearing on the motion. Thus, Jeff A. Mann of Law Offices of Jeff A. Mann’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant Yousef Maghen, M.D. is CONTINUED to TBD for counsel to provide sufficient proof of service of all documents associated with this motion as previously ordered.

Case Number: 25SMCV04737    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant Jennifer Kashdan’s Motion for Order Compelling Plaintiff, Sarkis Mesrobian, to Answer Form Interrogatories, Set No. One, is DENIED as MOOT.

Defendant Jennifer Kashdan’s Motion for Order Compelling Plaintiff, Sarkis Mesrobian, to Answer Special Interrogatories, Set No. One, is DENIED as MOOT.

Defendant Jennifer Kashdan’s Motion for Order Compelling Plaintiff, Sarkis Mesrobian, to Respond to Demand for Production, Set No. One, is DENIED as MOOT.

Defendant Jennifer Kashdan’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,580, payable by Plaintiff Sarkis Mesrobian and Plaintiff’s counsel to Defendant Jennifer Kashdan and defense counsel within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.

Defendant Jennifer Kashdan to give notice. 

REASONING

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses and for a monetary sanction. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subds. (b), (c).) Failure to timely respond waives all objections, including privilege and work product. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).) Thus, unless the party to whom the demand was directed obtains relief from waiver, he or she cannot raise objections to the documents demanded. (Ibid.) There is no deadline for a motion to compel responses. Likewise, for failure to respond, the moving party need not attempt to resolve the matter outside court before filing the motion. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Where the motion seeks only a response to the interrogatories, no showing of “good cause” is required. (Contra Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1) [good cause requirement for motion to compel further responses].) All that need be shown in the moving papers is that a set of interrogatories was properly served on the opposing party, that the time to respond has expired, and that no response of any kind has been served. (Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)

Similarly, where there has been no timely response to a Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 demand, the demanding party must seek an order compelling a response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) Failure to timely respond waives all objections, including privilege and work product.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) Thus, unless the party to whom the demand was directed obtains relief from waiver, he or she cannot raise objections to the documents demanded. (Ibid.) There is no deadline for a motion to compel responses. Likewise, for failure to respond, the moving party need not attempt to resolve the matter outside court before filing the motion. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300.) Where the motion seeks only a response to the inspection demand, no showing of “good cause” is required. (Contra Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1) [good cause requirement for motion to compel further responses].)

Defendant Jennifer Kashdan (“Defendant”) served Plaintiff Sarkis Mesrobian (“Plaintiff”) with her Form Interrogatories, Set No. One, Special Interrogatories, Set No. One, and Demand for Production, Set No. One, on December 11, 2025. (Mots., Davtyan Decls. ¶ 2.) Defense counsel communicated with Plaintiff’s counsel to obtain the responses, but responses were not received as of the date of filing each motion. (Mots., Davtyan Decls. ¶¶ 3-4.) However, on August 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Service of Verified Discovery Responses as to each of the identified set of requests. 

Accordingly, Defendant Jennifer Kashdan’s Motion for Order Compelling Plaintiff, Sarkis Mesrobian, to Answer Form Interrogatories, Set No. One, Motion for Order Compelling Plaintiff, Sarkis Mesrobian, to Answer Special Interrogatories, Set No. One, and Motion for Order Compelling Plaintiff, Sarkis Mesrobian, to Respond to Demand for Production, Set No. One, are DENIED as MOOT.

If a motion to compel responses to interrogatories or requests for production is filed, the Court shall impose a monetary sanction against the losing party “unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (c); 2031.300, subd. (c).) Further, “[t]he court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed, or opposition to the motion was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1348(a).)

Defendant requests $3,609.85 in monetary sanctions for the three motions. The Court finds that monetary sanctions are proper, given that responses were served the day before the hearing on these motions, but the Court reduces the amount to $1,580, representing two hours spent preparing each motion and one spent appearing at the hearing on the motions, totaling seven hours at the rate of $200 per hour, as well as three filing fees. Thus, Defendant Jennifer Kashdan’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $1,580, payable by Plaintiff Sarkis Mesrobian and Plaintiff’s counsel to Defendant Jennifer Kashdan and defense counsel within thirty (30) days of entry of this order.
