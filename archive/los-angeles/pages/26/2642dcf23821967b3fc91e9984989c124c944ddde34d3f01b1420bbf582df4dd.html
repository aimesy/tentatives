DEPARTMENT 316 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 21STCV31601    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 316

BACKGROUND

This case is an employment dispute. Plaintiff Candy Martina Melgoza alleges that suffered from unlawful discrimination, harassment, retaliation, and wage practices while employed by Defendant Whelan Security of California, Inc.

On August 26, 2021, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Whelan Security of California, Inc., Roscoe Krease, Steve Heaney, Darryl Whitt, Veronica Valenzuela, and Sarah Becker. The complaint alleged fifteen causes of action stemming from the acts alleged above.

On March 22, 2022, the Court issued a Minute Order granting Defendants’ Motion to Compel Arbitration.

On March 17, 2026, Defendant Whelan Security of California, Inc. filed a Motion to Confirm Arbitration Award.

On April 14, 2026, the Court issued a Minute Order denying Plaintiff’s separate Motion to Vacate or Alter Arbitration Award.

On July 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to Defendant’s Motion.

On July 27, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant’s Motion to Confirm Arbitration Award is GRANTED. 

LEGAL STANDARD

Once arbitration is concluded, “any arbitrator’s award is enforceable only when confirmed as a judgment of the superior court.” (O’Hare v. Municipal Resource Consultants (2003) 107¿Cal.App.4th 267, 278.) Any of the parties may file a petition with the court, which must then “confirm the award, correct and confirm it, vacate it, or dismiss the petition.” (Code¿of¿Civ. Proc., §§¿1285, 1286; EHM Productions, Inc. v. Starline Tours of Hollywood,¿Inc.¿(2018) 21 Cal.App.5th 1058, 1063 (EHM Productions).) “It is well settled that the scope of judicial review of arbitration awards is extremely narrow.” (California Faculty Assn. v. Superior Court¿(1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 935, 943.) “Neither the trial court, nor the appellate court, may ‘review the merits of the dispute, the sufficiency of the evidence, or the arbitrator’s reasoning, nor may we correct or review an award because of an arbitrator’s legal or factual error, even if it appears on the award’s face.’” (EHM Productions, supra, at 1063-1064.) Courts should vacate arbitration awards only when:  

(1)¿The award was procured by corruption, fraud or other undue means.¿

(2)¿There was corruption in any of the arbitrators.¿

(3)¿The rights of the party were substantially prejudiced by misconduct of a neutral arbitrator.¿

(4)¿The arbitrators exceeded their powers and the award cannot be corrected without affecting the merits of the decision upon the controversy submitted.¿

(5)¿The rights of the party were substantially prejudiced by the refusal of the arbitrators to postpone the hearing upon sufficient cause being shown therefor or by the refusal of the arbitrators to hear evidence material to the controversy or by other conduct of the arbitrators contrary to the provisions of this title.¿

(6)¿An arbitrator making the award either: (A) failed to disclose within the time required for disclosure a ground for disqualification of which the arbitrator was then aware; or (B) was subject to disqualification upon grounds specified in Section 1281.91 but failed upon receipt of timely demand to disqualify himself or herself as required by that provision. However, this subdivision does not apply to arbitration proceedings conducted under a collective bargaining agreement between employers and employees or between their respective representatives.¿

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.2, subd. (a).)¿

Code of Civil Procedure section 1285.4 states: “A petition under this chapter shall:¿(a) Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.¿(b) Set forth the names of the arbitrators.¿(c) Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.”¿Furthermore:¿¿

¿(a) A copy of the petition and a written notice of the time and place of the hearing thereof and any other papers upon which the petition is based shall be served in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice.¿¿¿

¿(b) If the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made and the person upon whom service is to be made has not previously appeared in the proceeding and has not previously been served in accordance with this subdivision: (1) Service within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4.)¿

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 provides that: “The neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.”¿Additionally, a party may seek a court judgment confirming an arbitration award by filing and serving a petition no more than four years, but not less than 10 days, after the award is served.¿(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288, 1288.4.)¿¿

DISCUSSION

Here, Defendant seeks an order confirming the arbitration award (“Award”) entered on November 10, 2025, by JAMS arbitrator, the Honorable Elizabeth A. White (Ret.). After consideration, the Court holds that the Award shall be confirmed.

I.               Procedural Requirements

The Court finds that Defendant has satisfied all procedural requirements in bringing its motion.

Defendant has provided a copy of the arbitration agreement and has set forth the name of the arbitrator. (See Lueng Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. A; see also Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4, subds. (a)-(b).) A copy of the Award is also provided. (See Lueng Decl., Exh. D; see also Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4, subd. (c).) Lastly, this motion was not filed earlier than 10 days and not later than four years after the date of service of the Award. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1288, 1288.4.)

Defendant has satisfied all statutory requirements to confirm the Award.

II.             Confirming Arbitration Award

The Court finds that Plaintiff has not established any statutory ground for the Court to refuse to confirm the Award.

Plaintiff put forth many reasons as to why the Court should not confirm the Award. (See generally Opp’n.) However, these reasons were presented to and ultimately rejected by the Court in its prior order denying Plaintiff’s Motion to Vacate Arbitration Award. For instance, the Court already rejected Plaintiff’s arguments that the Award should not be confirmed because:

Ø  The arbitrator did not allow Plaintiff to amend her complaint. (See Opp’n, pp. 4:14-7:19; Apr. 14 Minute Order, pp. 8-9.)

Ø  Plaintiff was not allowed to conduct full and essential discovery. (See Opp’n, pp. 7:22-8:23; Apr. 14 Minute Order, pp. 11-13.)

Ø  The arbitrator unjustly restricted the use of “Me Too” evidence. (See Opp’n, pp. 9:26-10:26; Apr. 14 Minute Order, pp. 12-13.)

Ø  The arbitrator improperly placed the burden on Plaintiff. (See Opp’n, pp. 11:1-13; Apr. 14 Minute Order, pp. 14-15.)

Ultimately, the Court’s conclusion in its prior Minute Order was that “Plaintiff has not established any of the statutory grounds under which the Court may vacate the Arbitrator’s award.” (Apr. 14 Minute Order, p. 15.) Given that Plaintiff presented the same arguments in opposition to this motion, the same conclusion applies. The Court does not find any statutory grounds under which the Court would not confirm the Arbitrator’s award. Therefore, the Award shall be confirmed.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Defendant’s Motion to Confirm Arbitration Award is GRANTED. Judgment will be entered in conformity with the arbitration award of November 10, 2025

Case Number: 25VECV04188    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 316

On November 12, 2025, Plaintiffs Joseph Rangel and Emily Fabian filed their first amended complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants SoFi Stadium, Apex Security Group, Inc., and DOES 1-100. The FAC alleges seven causes of action: (1) Negligence; (2) Bane Act; (3) Assault; (4) Battery; (5) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress; (6) False Arrest; and (7) Ralph Act.  

On March 25, 2026, Defendants filed a Motion to Compel the Deposition of Plaintiff Emili Fabian.

On July 21, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition.

On July 27, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants’ Motion to Compel the Deposition of Plaintiff Emili Fabian is GRANTED. Plaintiff Fabian is to appear for deposition on a mutually agreed date within thirty days of the Court’s ruling unless Defendants agree to a later date. No later than five days before the deposition date, Defendants are to provide written confirmation that all video footage of the incident in their possession, custody or control has been produced to Plaintiffs.

Plaintiffs are ordered to pay Defendants monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,160 within thirty days of the Court’s ruling.

LEGAL STANDARD

Any party may obtain discovery, subject to restrictions, by taking the oral deposition of any person, including any party to the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.) A party¿desiring¿to take an oral deposition shall give a notice in writing which¿states¿the specification of¿reasonably particularly¿of any materials to be produced by the deponent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.220, subd. (a)(4).) A properly served deposition notice is effective to require a party to attend and to testify, as well as to produce documents for inspection and copying. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.280, subd. (a).) The party served with a deposition notice waives any error or irregularity, unless that party promptly serves a written objection at least three calendar days prior to the date for which the deposition is scheduled. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.410, subd. (a).)¿ 

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action … without having served a valid objection … fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document … described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent's attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document … described in the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

If the Court grants a motion to compel a deposition, it “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).)

DISCUSSION

I.               Meet and Confer

When filing a motion to compel a deposition, said motion must be accompanied with a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).)

Here, when Plaintiffs objected to Defendants’ deposition notices, defense counsel sent an email to find a mutually agreeable date to meet and confer. (See Brouses Decl., ¶¶ 5, 7, 9-10, 12, 14, 17.) These attempts were futile, however. (See id. at ¶¶ 4, 8-10, 12, 14, 17.) Therefore, although the meet and confer requirement has not been satisfied, the Court will still consider the merits of Defendants’ motion because defense counsel attempted in good faith to meet and confer to resolve this matter.

II.             Deposition of Fabian

The Court will compel the deposition of Plaintiff Emili Fabian and order Plaintiffs to pay monetary sanctions.

Here, Defendants seek to compel the deposition of Plaintiff Emili Fabian. In August of 2025, Defendants served its initial notice of Fabian’s deposition for October 10, 2025, nearly ten months ago. (Brouses Decl., ¶ 3.) Plaintiffs objected to this notice of deposition. (Id. at ¶ 4.) Defendants attempted to meet and confer with Plaintiffs to find a mutually agreeable date but got no response. (Id. at ¶¶ 5, 7.) Accordingly, on December 2, 2025, Defendants issued an amended notice of deposition. (Id. at ¶ 7.) Plaintiffs then objected to the amended notice of deposition. (Id. at ¶ 8.) Defendants again attempted to meet and confer with Plaintiffs to find a mutually agreeable date, but Plaintiffs did not provide a proposed date for deposition at the time of the filing of this motion. (Id. at ¶¶ 9-10, 12, 14, 17.)

Defendants have been attempting to depose Fabian for almost a year now. And although Plaintiffs objected to Defendants’ noticed depositions, they did not timely provide any alternative dates as of the filing of this motion. (See Brouses Decl., Exhs. 2-3, 6-7, 9-13.) The Court does not find that Plaintiffs’ objections were valid. For this reason, the Court shall compel the deposition of Plaintiff Fabian. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

In opposition, Plaintiffs contend that it agreed to produce Fabian for deposition on May 15, 2026. (See Gage Decl., Exh. 14.) However, Plaintiffs agreed to this deposition date only after Defendants filed this motion. (See Brousas Decl., Exh. 12 and Gage Decl., Exh. 14 [showing that Plaintiffs’ counsel took the deposition off calendar on March 19 and did not offer new dates, and then offered a deposition date on March 27, 2026, after the motion was filed].) Notably, Plaintiffs later postponed this agreed-upon deposition because Defendants allegedly failed to fully comply with Plaintiffs’ prior production requests, and specifically surveillance footage of the subject incident. (See id. at Exhs. 19-20.) However, Defendants’ alleged failure to fully comply with Plaintiffs’ prior production requests does not relieve Plaintiffs of the responsibility to appear for deposition, particularly where no motion regarding the production has been filed.

With regard to the surveillance footage, it appears that Defendants ultimately provided surveillance footage of the subject incident. (See id. at Exh. 21 [Dropbox link of surveillance footage found in June 12 email exchange]; see also Doumar Decl., Exh. 15.) However, Defendants appear not to have confirmed that the produced footage is all of the footage of the incident in their possession, custody or control. While this discovery dispute does not prevent the Court from compelling Plaintiff Fabian’s deposition, the Court believes that Defendants should adhere to their agreement to produce all video footage they have of the incident prior to the deposition taking place.

For these reasons, the Court will compel the deposition of Plaintiff Emili Fabian. Plaintiff Fabian is to appear for deposition on a mutually agreed date within thirty days of the Court’s ruling unless Defendants agree to a later date. No later than five days before the deposition date, Defendants are to provide written confirmation that all video footage of the incident in their possession, custody or control has been produced to Plaintiffs.

III.          Sanctions

Defendants request monetary sanctions in the amount of $4,960. The Court finds that sanctions are mandatory as Plaintiffs did not have substantial justification for their failure to provide deposition dates prior to the filing of this motion. The Court finds the requested rate of $350 per hour to be reasonable. The Court finds the requested 14 hours to be excessive. While the parties felt it necessary to attach dozens of exhibits to the motion, opposition and reply briefs as well as extensive argument, the motion is ultimately about getting one deposition scheduled.  The Court finds that an appropriate sanction is six hours of attorney time at the requested rate for a total of $2,100, plus the filing fee of $60.

CONCLUSION¿

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants’ Motion to Compel Deposition of Plaintiff Emili Fabian is GRANTED. Plaintiff Fabian is to appear for deposition on a mutually agreed date within thirty days of the Court’s ruling unless Defendants agree to a later date. No later than five days before the deposition date, Defendants are to provide written confirmation that all video footage of the incident in their possession, custody or control has been produced to Plaintiffs.

Plaintiffs are also ordered to pay Defendants monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,160 within thirty days of the Court’s ruling.

Case Number: 26STCV08770    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 316

BACKGROUND

This case is a habitability dispute. Plaintiffs Sandra Cha and Giulio Gallese allege that Defendant The Summit failed to properly maintain and manage the rental property in which Plaintiffs resided. As a result of Defendants’ poor management, Plaintiffs allege they were constructively evicted from the property.

On March 18, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a complaint against Defendant. The complaint alleges eight causes of action stemming from the facts alleged above.

On April 1, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Related Case with Case No. 25STUD09673, an unlawful detainer action filed by Defendant against Plaintiffs. Possession is longer at issue in that case given that Plaintiffs have vacated the property.

On July 10, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Motion to Consolidate Cases.

On July 15, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiffs’ Notice of Related Case and ruled that this specific action (Case No. 26STCV08770) was the lead case.

No opposition or reply has been filed.

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiffs’ Motion to Consolidate Case is GRANTED.

LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 1048, subdivision (a), provides that “[w]hen actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the court,¿courts¿may order a joint¿[. . . .]¿trial of any or all of the matters in issue in the actions; it may order all the actions consolidated, and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (a).)¿

A trial court has broad authority to grant or deny a motion to¿consolidate.¿(See Fellner v. Steinbaum¿(1955) 132 Cal.App.2d 509, 511.)¿A court generally considers the following: (1) whether granting consolidation would delay the trial of any of the cases involved; (2) whether joining the actions involved would make the trial too confusing or complex for a jury; and (3) whether consolidation would adversely affect the rights of any party. (State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. v. Superior Court¿(1956) 47 Cal.2d 428, 430-431.) Further, courts evaluate whether consolidation would promote judicial economy¿and convenience, avoid unnecessary duplication, and reduce the risk of inconsistent adjudications.¿(See Todd-Stenberg v.¿Dalkon¿Shield Claimants Trust¿(1996) 48 Cal.App.4th¿976, 978-979.)¿Courts have discretion to enact complete consolidation where there is a single set of findings and judgment and where no doubt exists as to the applicability of the various findings to each of the consolidated cases. (Page v. Bakersfield Uniform & Towel Supply Co. (1966) 239 Cal.App.2d 762, 772.) Similarly, consolidation is appropriate where the causes could have been joined together originally. (Jaffe v. Albertson Co. (1966) 243 Cal. App. 2d 592, 606-607.) 

Under the local rules, “[c]ases¿may not be¿consolidated¿unless they are in the same department. A motion to¿consolidate¿two or more cases may be noticed and heard after the cases, initially filed in different departments, have been related into a single department, or if the cases were already assigned to that department.” (Super. Ct. L.A. County, Local Rules, rule 3.3, subd. (g)(1).) Under the California Rules of Court, a motion to consolidate must “[l]ist¿all named parties in each case, the names of those who have appeared, and the names of their respective attorneys of record”; “[c]ontain¿the captions of all the cases sought to be consolidated, with the lowest numbered case shown first”; and “[b]e filed in each case sought to be consolidated.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1).)

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs seek to consolidate this action (Case No. 26STCV08770) with Plaintiffs’ unlawful detainer action (Case No. 25STUD09673).

On July 15, 2026, the Court related the two cases, and they are both pending in this department. (See July 15 Minute Order.) Furthermore, Plaintiffs have listed all named parties in each case, the name of the parties who have appeared, the names of their respective attorneys of record, the captions of the two cases (with the lowest numbered case shown first), and filed this motion in each case sought to be consolidated. (See Motion, ¶¶ 1-2.) Therefore, Plaintiffs have satisfied all procedural requirements. (See Super. Ct. L.A. County, Local Rules, rule 3.3, subd. (g)(1); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1).)

Plaintiffs contend that the two cases involve common questions of law and facts, and that consolidation will avoid unnecessary costs and delay. (See Motion, ¶ 3.) The Court agrees. The two cases stem from the same circumstances and share overlapping issues of habitability, retaliation, and rent. Therefore, to best promote judicial economy¿and convenience, avoid unnecessary duplication, and reduce the risk of inconsistent adjudications, the Court will grant the motion.¿(See Todd-Stenberg v.¿Dalkon¿Shield Claimants Trust¿(1996) 48 Cal.App.4th¿976, 978-979; see also Fellner v. Steinbaum¿(1955) 132 Cal.App.2d 509, 511.)

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiffs’ Motion to Consolidate Case is GRANTED.
