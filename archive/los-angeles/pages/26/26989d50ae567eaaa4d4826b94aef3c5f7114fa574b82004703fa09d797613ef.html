Case Number: 25LBCV00100    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Defendant The Vons Companies, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff Bobbie Ann Shett to Appear for Spine Physical Examination  

Moving Party:  Defendant The Vons Companies, Inc. (“Defendant”)  

Responding Party: Plaintiff Bobbie Ann Shett (“Plaintiff”)  

 

Tentative Ruling  

 

If a defendant seeks to obtain an additional mental examination of a plaintiff, the defendant must obtain leave of court.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.310, subd. (a).)  A motion to compel an additional mental examination must “specify the time, place, manner, conditions, scope and nature of the examination, as well as the identity and specialty, if any, of the person or persons who will perform the examination,” and must include a meet and confer declaration.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.310, subd. (b).)  Additionally, the defendant must make a showing of “good cause” to obtain the second mental examination.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.320, subd. (a).)  Under the Discovery Act, there is no limit on the number of mental or physical examinations, provided that a showing of good cause is made to justify more than one mental or physical examination of a party.  (Shapira v. Superior Court (1990) 224 Cal.App.3d 1249, 1255.)  The Shapira court also noted that “multiple examinations should not be ordered routinely; the good cause requirement will check the potential harassment of plaintiffs by repetitive examinations,” and “multiple examinations by medical specialists in different fields” may be necessary based upon a plaintiff’s claimed injuries.  (Ibid.) 

 

The parties have sufficiently met and conferred.  (Herzog Decl., ¶ 14, Ex. J.)   

 

Defendant moves the Court for leave to conduct an independent medical examination of Plaintiff by Dr. Yoshida, an orthopedic spine surgeon, on September 29, 2026 at 10:00 AM or at such time and place as the Court may direct.  Defendant contends that good cause exists to obtain an additional mental examination of Plaintiff because Plaintiff claims spinal injuries alongside other injuries.  (Shapira v. Superior Court (1990) 224 Cal.App.3d 1249, 1254.)  Defendant states that the July 31, 2026 examination by Dr. Kim addresses Plaintiff’s claimed knee, foot and shoulder injuries.   

 

NO OPPOSITION HAS BEEN RECEIVED.   

 

The Court grants the instant motion as spinal injuries have been claimed and the first medical examination with Dr. Kim did not evaluate Plaintiff’s spinal injuries.  (Shapira v. Superior Court (1990) 224 Cal.App.3d 1249, 1255; Herzog Decl., ¶¶ 5-8, Exs. A-C.)  Good cause has been shown by Defendant to justify an independent medical examination.   

 

The Court further orders the parties to find a mutually agreeable date for the independent medical examination to occur within 30 days of this hearing.   

 

The instant motion is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to submit to an orthopedic spine physical examination by Gregory Yoshida, M.D., and the parties must find a mutually agreeable date, time and place for the independent medical examination to occur within 30 days of this hearing. 

Case Number: 25LBCV01900    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Defendant American Honda Motor Company’s Motion for Summary Judgment or, in the Alternative, for Summary Adjudication  

Moving Party:  Defendant American Honda Motor Company (“Defendant”)   

Responding Party: Plaintiff Brendon J Forrestal (“Plaintiff”) 

 

Tentative Ruling  

 

The Court grants Defendant’s request for judicial notice of the complaint.  Taking judicial notice of a document is not the same as accepting the truth of its contents or accepting a particular interpretation of its meaning.  (Herrera v. Deutsche Bank National Trust Co. (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1366, 1375.)   

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(b) states: “If summary adjudication is sought, whether separately or as an alternative to the motion for summary judgment, the specific cause of action, affirmative defense, claims for damages, or issues of duty must be stated specifically in the notice of motion and be repeated, verbatim, in the separate statement of undisputed material facts.”   

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1010 also states that: “Notices must be in writing, and the notice of a motion, other than for a new trial, must state when, and the grounds upon which it will be made, and the papers, if any, upon which it is to be based.”   

 

While the Court acknowledges that the notice and separate statement fails to adhere to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(b) and the notice fails to comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 1010, the Court will still consider the instant motion.   

 

The Court agrees that a separate statement is required for the opposition.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 437b, subd. (c)(3) [“[t]he opposition papers shall include a separate statement that responds to each of the material facts contended by the moving party to be undisputed, indicating if the opposing party agrees or disagrees that those facts are undisputed . . . [f]ailure to comply with this requirement of a separate statement may constitute a sufficient ground, in the court's discretion, for granting the motion”].)  

 

While the Court acknowledges that the opposition fails to adhere to Code of Civil Procedure section 437b, subdivision (c)(3), the Court will still consider the opposition.   

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s warranty claims under the Song-Beverly Act are time-barred and that the fraud claim is also time-barred.  (Defendant’s Separate Statement of Undisputed Material Facts (“DSSUMF”) Nos. 1-2) In opposition, Plaintiff asserts that Defendant has not met its initial burden of proving every element of statute of limitations defense.  Plaintiff also notes that the complaint concerns a transmission defect which is covered by a five year of 60,000 miles warranty, pointing out that the applicable period ran to approximately July 6, 2022 and the four-year window closed in July 2026.  Plaintiff asserts that this action, filed on June 26, 2025, is timely.   

 

First to Third Causes of Action – Song-Beverly Act Violations  

The Court notes that the complaint was unverified.  Moreover, the Court judicially noticing the complaint does not establish the truth of the matters asserted within it.  Even if the Court accepts the allegations in the complaint at face value, the complaint allegations do not establish the delivery date of the Subject Vehicle; instead, the complaint only states that the warranty contract was entered into on or around July 6, 2017.  (Defendant’s Separate Statement of Undisputed Material Facts (“DSSUMF”) Nos. 1-3; Compl., ¶ 6.)  Next, Defendant has not met its initial burden of establishing the statute of limitations has passed.  Even if the Court assumes July 6, 2017 was the delivery date of the Subject Vehicle, an argument can be made as to which warranty, the New Vehicle Limited Warranty or the Powertrain Limited Warranty, applies.  The instant motion is DENIED as to these causes of action.   

 

Fourth Cause of Action – Breach of Implied Warranty of Merchantability  

“(2) A cause of action accrues when the breach occurs, regardless of the aggrieved party's lack of knowledge of the breach. A breach of warranty occurs when tender of delivery is made, except that where a warranty explicitly extends to future performance of the goods and discovery of the breach must await the time of such performance the cause of action accrues when the breach is or should have been discovered.”  (Cal. U. Com. Code, § 2725, subds. (1)-(2).)  The statute of limitations on a claim for breach of an implied warranty of merchantability is four years.  (Montoya v. Ford Motor Co. (2020) 46 Cal.App.5th 493, 495.)   

As stated in the above section, the unverified complaint does not allege the delivery date of the Subject Vehicle.  Defendant has not met its initial burden of establishing the statute of limitations has passed.  The instant motion is DENIED as to this cause of action.   

 

Fifth Cause of Action – Fraudulent Inducement- Concealment 

Code of Civil Procedure section 338, subdivision (d) provides for a three-year statute of limitations for fraud.  The unverified complaint does not allege the delivery date of the Subject Vehicle.  Defendant has not met its initial burden of establishing the statute of limitations has passed.  The instant motion is DENIED as to this cause of action.   

 

Defendant American Honda Motor Company’s Motion for Summary Judgment or, in the Alternative, for Summary Adjudication is DENIED.   

 

 

Case Number: 26LBCV01252    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Defendant Long Beach Rescue Mission’s Demurrer to Plaintiff Larry William Robinson’s Complaint  

Moving Party:  Defendant Long Beach Rescue Mission (“Defendant”)  

Responding Party: Plaintiff Larry William Robinson (“Plaintiff”)  

 

Tentative Ruling  

 

DEMURRER  

 

Defendant demurs to the complaint on the ground that it is uncertain and never develops the asserted claims of professional negligence and intentional infliction of emotional distress.  Defendant also asserts that the complaint fails to state facts sufficient to constitute any cause of action as the essential elements of professional negligence, intentional infliction of emotional distress or any other cognizable basis for civil liability are not alleged.   

 

NO OPPOSITION HAS BEEN RECEIVED.  

 

Here, the Court finds that the complaint is uncertain as it does not clearly state the causes of action asserted.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subds. (f) and (g).)   

 

The Court SUSTAINS the instant demurrer with 20 days leave to amend.   

 

 

MOTION TO STRIKE  

 

Defendant moves the Court strike the following portions of the complaint:  

  1. The entirety of the narrative attachment to the Complaint, including the section styled as a “Declaration” and all purported “Proof” sections contained therein. (Compl., Attachment, pp. 1-10.)  

  1. Under “IV. Prayer for Relief,” paragraph 2, requesting: “Injunctive Relief (Facility Modifications): An order requiring the Long Beach Rescue Mission to construct physical dividers within all resident bathroom and shower areas to guarantee basic bodily privacy.” (Compl., p. 4.)  

  1. Under “IV. Prayer for Relief,” paragraph 3, requesting: “Injunctive Relief (Equal Access to Services & Amenities): An order mandating that the Defendant balance and equalize services between the 90-Day Program residents and the New Life Program (NLP) residents. This includes removing discriminatory regulations, thereby ensuring all residents have equal rights to sit in any common area and watch television.” (Compl., p. 4.)  

  1. Under “IV. Prayer for Relief,” paragraph 4, requesting: “Production of Documents: Immediate disclosure of Incident Report No. 1, Incident Report No. 2, the full administrative file regarding Larry William Robinson’s termination, and the identity of the instigating resident.” (Compl., p. 4.) 

 

Defendant asserts that the challenged portions of the complaint are irrelevant, immaterial and improper matter subject to being stricken.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 431.10, subd. (b) and 436, subds. (a), (b).)  Defendant also asserts the challenged portions of the complaint are not drawn in conformity with the laws of this state.   

 

NO OPPOSITION HAS BEEN RECEIVED.   

 

The instant motion to strike is MOOT.
