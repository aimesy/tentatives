DEPARTMENT 509 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV17738    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: 509

Kristin Jacobson v. Disney Human Resources Services Co., LLC, et al.
 
MOTION TO TRANSFER VENUE
 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Disney Human Resources Services Co., LLC; The Walt Disney Company; Disney Worldwide Services, Inc.; and Walt Disney Parks and Resorts U.S., Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Kristin Jacobson

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

This is an employment case. Plaintiff Kristin Jacobson (Plaintiff) alleges her employers, defendants Disney Human Resources Services Co., LLC; The Walt Disney Company; Disney Worldwide Services, Inc.; and Walt Disney Parks and Resorts U.S., Inc. (collectively, Defendants), wrongfully terminated her employment based on her disability. Plaintiff asserts nine causes of action, six of those under FEHA.

On February 16, 2024, this Court denied Defendants original motion to transfer venue under Code of Civil Procedure, section 396(b). (See 2/16/24 Final Ruling.)

Defendants now move to transfer this action to Orange County pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, section 397(c). Plaintiff opposed.

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendants’ Motion to Transfer Venue to Orange County is DENIED.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice, unless waived.

DISCUSSION:

Motion to Transfer Venue

I. Legal Standard

“Venue is determined based on the complaint on file at the time the motion to change venue is made.” (Brown v. Superior Court of Alameda County (1984) 37 Cal.3d 477, 482.) If an action has been filed in a court other than the court designated as the proper court by statute, a defendant may, within the time allowed to respond to the Complaint, move to transfer the action to the proper court. (Code Civ. Proc. § 396b subd. (a).) Transfer is mandatory if the action was not filed in the proper court. (Ibid.) If venue is proper in more than one court, the plaintiff may choose where to file the action. (Battaglia Enterprises, Inc. v. Superior Court (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 309, 313.) A plaintiff’s choice of venue is presumptively correct, and the defendant bears the burden to demonstrate that the choice of venue is not proper on any grounds. (Fontaine v. Superior Court (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 830, 836.)

II. Objections to Evidence 

Each party has submitted objections to evidence. This Court is unaware of any legal authority which requires a court to rule on evidentiary objections on a motion, except as to a motion for summary motion/adjudication (CCP § 437c (q)] or a special motion to strike (CCP § 425.16 (b)(2)); see also Sweetwater Union High School Dist. v. Gilbane Building Co. (2019) 6 Cal.5th 931, 947-949.)

As such, this Court respectfully declines to rule on these objections. This Court is well aware of the rules of evidence, and to how much weight, if any, should be given to any of the proposed evidence.

III. Analysis

Defendants move for discretionary relief to transfer the case to Orange County under Code of Civil Procedure, section 397(c) which allows for the Court to order a transfer “[w]hen the convenience of witnesses and the ends of justice would be promoted by the change.”

“It is well settled that a motion for change of venue grounded upon the convenience of witnesses rests largely in the discretion of the trial court.” (Wrin v. Ohlandt (1931) 213 Cal.158, 159.) “There is no time fixed by statute when an action may be transferred for trial because of convenience of witnesses or to promote justice. The courts have said that it must be made within a reasonable time after the case is at issue on the facts, the determination of which rests largely in the discretion of the trial court.” (Cooney v. Cooney (1944) 25 Cal.2d 202, 208.)

However, when a party moves to transfer venue for the convenience of witnesses, the party must provide affidavits setting forth “the name of the witnesses, the nature of the testimony expected from each, and the reasons why the attendance of each would be inconvenient.” (Peiser v. Mettler (1958) 50 Cal.2d 594, 607.)  “Convenience of witnesses” may be shown by the fact that the residence of all witnesses is in the county to which the transfer is requested.  (Richfield Hotel Management, Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 22 Cal.App.4th 222, 227.) 

The burden is on the moving party to establish facts justifying the transfer. (Mission Imports, Inc. v. Superior Court¿(1982) 31 Cal.3d 921, 928-929.) However, “the rule is well settled that the convenience of employees of a party to an action need not be consulted in passing upon a motion for change of venue.” (Dillman v. Superior Court of Los Angeles County (1962) 205 Cal.App.2d 769, 773-774.) Where a party moves to transfer venue based on the convenience of witnesses, it must submit affidavits showing the names of each witness expected to testify for both parties, the substance of their expected testimony, whether the witness has been deposed or given a statement, the reasons why it would be inconvenient for the witnesses to appear, and the reasons why the ends of justice would be promoted by transfer to a different county. (Juneau v. Juneau (1941) 45 Cal.App.2d 14, 16; Edwards v. Pierson (1957) 156 Cal.App.2d 72, 75 [“The burden rests upon one who seeks a change of venue . . . to prove that both the convenience of witnesses and the ends of justice will be promoted thereby . . . This he must do through affidavits which contain something more than generalities and conclusions”]; Mission Imports, supra, 31 Cal.3d at pp. 928-929 [moving party may submit declarations containing admissible evidence in support of the motion to transfer venue].)

Here, Defendants argue five employees reside in Orange County as well as an unnamed representative of Plaintiff’s union, United Food & Commercial Workers, Local 324, and Plaintiff’s therapist, Dr. Linda Schnaible. As discussed above, “the court may not consider the convenience of the parties or of their employees in passing upon the motion.” (Lieberman v. Superior Court (1987) 194 Cal.App.3d 396, 401.) Instead, Defendants must provide sufficient evidence that it would be inconvenient for the non-employee witnesses to appear in Los Angeles County. Further, a motion must identify the nonparty witness by name. (See Peiser v. Mettler, supra, 50 Cal.2d at p. 607.) The unnamed representative of Local 324 does not evidence a basis for relief.

 As a result, this leaves the Court to solely consider the inconvenience this trial would have on Dr. Linda Schnaible (Dr. Schnaible). However, Defendants do not explain the reasons why the attendance of Dr. Schnaible would be inconvenient. Instead, Defendants merely state “Plaintiff’s therapist, Dr. Linda Schnaible, is located in Anaheim, California.” (Mot. at p. 14:27-28.) To be precise, Defendants believe Dr. Schnaible’s business is in Anaheim, California. There is no information as to the location of Dr. Schnaible’s current residence. Even assuming Dr. Schnaible’s current residence is in Anaheim, Defendants provide no evidence that Dr. Schnaible is in any way inconvenienced with the venue of this trial. Therefore, Defendants did not meet their burden to establish facts justifying the transfer to Orange County. 

The Court is aware of the action’s substantial ties to Orange County and Plaintiff’s lack of witnesses in Los Angeles County. However, “the burden of making a sufficient showing is on the moving party, and he ‘must stand upon the strength of his showing rather than upon the weakness, if any, of the opposition.’” (Union Trust Life Ins. Co. v. Superior Court for Ventura County (1968) 259 Cal.App.2d 23, 28, internal citations omitted.) Defendants have not met their burden.

Accordingly, Defendants’ Motion to Transfer Venue to Orange County is DENIED.

IV. Attorney’s Fees

Code of Civil Procedure section 396(b) provides the Court with discretion to award attorneys’ fees to the prevailing party on a motion to transfer. It states, in relevant part: 

“In its discretion,¿the court may order the payment to the prevailing party of reasonable expenses and attorney’s fees incurred in making or resisting the motion to transfer whether or not that party is otherwise entitled to recover his or her costs of action. In determining whether that order for expenses and fees shall be made, the court shall take into consideration (1) whether an offer to stipulate to change of venue was reasonably made and rejected, and (2) whether the motion or selection of venue was made in good faith given the facts and law the party making the motion or selecting the venue knew or should have known.¿As between the party and his or her attorney, those expenses and fees shall be the personal liability of the attorney not chargeable to the party. Sanctions shall not be imposed pursuant to this subdivision except¿on notice contained in a party’s papers, or on the court’s own noticed motion, and after opportunity to be heard.” 

(Code Civ. Proc. § 396b, subd. (b), emphasis added.)

Accordingly, the Court declines to award any expenses or attorney’s fees since neither party has formally or properly requested sanctions. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV03197    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: 509

Chen Chen v. Bestco Financial Services, Inc., et al.

MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH SUBPOENA DUCES TECUM 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant/Cross-Complainant Bestco Financial Services, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Unopposed

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

Plaintiff Chen Chen (Plaintiff) brings this employment action against defendant Bestco Financial Services, Inc and Xun Wang. Plaintiff alleges nine causes of action including Labor Code violations and wrongful termination.

Bestco Financial Services, Inc. (Bestco) filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff for: (1) breach of fiduciary duty; (2) breach of duty of loyalty; (3) intentional interference with prospective economic advantage; (4) negligent intentional interference with prospective economic advantage; (5) breach of contract; (6) unfair business practices; (7) violation of the California Uniform Trade Secrets Act; and (8) violation of the Defend Trade Secrets Act.

Bestco now moves to compel non-party, Allstate Joanna Lu Agency (Responding Party) to comply with Bestco’s subpoena of business records. There is no opposition.  Bestco filed a notice of non-opposition.

TENTATIVE RULING:
 
Bestco’s Motion to Compel is GRANTED.  
 
Responding Party is ordered to turn over all responsive documents within 30-days of this Ruling.  
  
Bestco’s request for sanctions against Allstate Joanna Lu Agency is GRANTED, in an amount to be determined at the hearing. 

Bestco is ordered to give notice by personal service upon Allstate Joanna Lu Agency.


DISCUSSION:

Compel Compliance

A. Legal Standard

“If a deponent fails to answer any question or to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing under the deponent’s control that is specified in the deposition notice or a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer or production.” (Code Civ. Pro. § 2025.480.) “When a subpoenaed nonparty fails to appear for a deposition or produce documents that were properly requested, the party who subpoenaed the witness may move to compel compliance with the subpoena.” (Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Nat’l Union Fire Ins. Co. of Pittsburgh (2005) 131 Cal.App.4th 1342, 1351.) 

B. Analysis

Responding Party “was served with the Subpoena for Documents, which requested Bestco’s former employee and Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant, Chen Chen’s, employment records, payroll records, insurance policies reflecting clients Ms. Chen has brought to Lu Agency during her employment (Category 5), a list of all clients Ms. Chen has brought to Lu Agency during her employment (Category 6), among other documents.” (Hernandez Decl. ¶2.) “Lu Agency provided Ms. Chen’s payroll records on March 12, 2026. Lu Agency provided supplemental documents on March 26, 2026 and April 17, 2026. However, none of the documents provided are insurance policies reflecting clients Ms. Chen has brought to Lu Agency during her employment or a list of all clients Ms. Chen has brought to Lu Agency during her employment. Bestco has good reason to believe that Ms. Chen did, in fact, bring clients to Lu Agency during her employment and that Lu Agency possesses documents relating to those clients. Bestco is still without this information.” (Id. at ¶4.) “On May 1, 2026, First Legal received this email: ‘On behalf of Joanna Lu, she has already provided your office with all the documents she has. Joanna Lu is requesting that you and your office stop contacting her regarding this matter.’” (Id. at ¶6.) “As of today, Lu Agency has not produce any information regarding the clients Ms. Chen brought to it during her employment and refused to meet and confer with Bestco.” (Id. at ¶7.)

Bestco now seeks a motion to compel compliance with the subpoena. 

Bestco argues that there is good cause to compel production on the requests because, “Bestco has good reason to believe that Ms. Chen brought clients to Lu Agency and that Lu Agency possesses documents relating to those clients. Lu Agency’s blanket assertion that it has produced everything, without addressing Categories 5 and 6 specifically, fails to demonstrate compliance.” (Mot. at p. 5:11-14.)
  
Here, Bestco has demonstrated that Responding Party failed to produce all responsive documents required by the subpoena. This discovery is relevant to the claims and defenses in this action, and thus, further discovery is warranted. 
 
Accordingly, Bestco’s Motion to Compel is GRANTED.  
 
Responding Party is ordered to turn over all further responsive documents within 30-days of this Ruling.  

C. Sanctions

In making an order pursuant to California Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, “the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2, subd. (a).)

In the notice of the motion, “Bestco further moves this Court for an order requiring Lu Agency to pay monetary sanctions to Bestco for causing tremendous delay in the discovery process and failing to produce responsive documents, necessitating this Motion. After the Court order on this motion, Bestco reserves the right to request that monetary sanctions be imposed against Lu Agency for its costs and fees for having to file this motion.” (Not. of Mot. at p. 2:8-12.)

Here, Bestco is entitled to their reasonable attorney’s fees. However, Bestco did not indicate the amount of fees incurred in bringing this motion. Bestco must demonstrate their reasonable fees at the hearing.

Bestco’s request for sanctions against Allstate Joanna Lu Agency is GRANTED, in an amount to be determined at the hearing. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
