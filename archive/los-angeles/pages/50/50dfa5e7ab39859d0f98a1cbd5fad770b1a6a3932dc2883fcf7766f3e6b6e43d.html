DEPARTMENT 534 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV06264    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 534

Background

 

On March 5, 2025, Vanessa Ortiz filed this employment action against Fuentes Family Insurance Agency. The First Amended Complaint, filed February 13, 2026, asserting seventeen causes of action.

 

Motion

 

On August 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion seeking to transfer this action to the Pomona Courthouse South in the East District.

 

The motion is unopposed. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1005.)

 

Motion to Transfer Between Districts

 

Standard

 

The Local Rules of the Superior Court of Los Angeles County govern the assignment of cases between its districts and departments. (Code Civ. Proc. § 402.) SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2) authorizes the Supervising Judge of the Civil Division to transfer civil cases from one judicial district to another via a noticed motion on three enumerated grounds: (1) when the case was filed in an improper district; (2) for the convenience of witnesses; or (3) to promote the ends of justice. (SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2).)

 

Considering that the same language appears in both SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2) and Code of Civil Procedure § 397(c), the Court finds that a party acting under the authority of Los Angeles Superior Court Local Rule 2.3(b)(2) should bear the burden of proof if it seeks a district transfer out of a presumptively correct forum, just as in motions for change of proper venue pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 397(c). (See Lieberman v. Superior Court (1987) 194 Cal.App.3d 396, 401.) That burden of proof should, moreover, call for affidavits that contain more than generalities and conclusions. (See Hamilton v Superior Court (1974) 37 Cal. App.3d 418, 424.) Such affidavits or declarations, like those for change of venue under Code Civ. Proc. section 397(c), should show the name of each witness, the expected testimony of each witness, and facts showing why the attendance of said witnesses at trial would be inconvenient or why the ends of justice would be served by a transfer. (See Stute v. Burinda (1981) 123 Cal. App. 3d Supp. 11, 17.) Convenience to non-party witnesses, not the convenience of parties or of the parties’ own employees, is generally the relevant consideration.  (Ibid. See also J. C. Millett Co. v. Latchford-Marble Glass Co. (1959) 167 Cal.App.2d 218, 227 (“While generally the convenience of the employees of either party will not be considered [Citations], when such employees are being called by an adverse party, the court may properly consider their convenience.”).)

 

The Case was Filed in a Permissible District

 

Defendant seeks to transfer this action from the Stanley Mosk Courthouse to the Pomona Courthouse South in the East Judicial District of the Superior Court of Los Angeles County.

 

Defendant contends the case was not filed in the correct district based upon the applicable Local Rules.

 

In Column B of the civil case cover sheet addendum, Plaintiff identified the case as an employment case for wrongful termination. In “Step 4” of the civil case cover sheet addendum where the filing party is asked to “[c]heck the appropriate boxes for the numbers shown under Column C for the type of action that you have selected. Enter the address, which is the basis for the filing location including zip code,” Plaintiff selected Reason 2, which corresponds to optional filing in the Central District.

 

Pursuant to the SCLAC Local Rules, rule 2.3(a)(1)(B), an employment case may be filed in three permissible locations: (1) in the Central District, (2) where the contract of employment was performed or (3) where the employer resides or does business. Defendant acknowledges “[a]s with any other case not specified in subd. (A) (torts and bodily injury claims), the optional filing rule allows them all to be filed in the Central District.” (Mot. at 5:26-28.)

 

Thus, this case was properly filed in the Central District under the applicable Local Rules. A transfer cannot be granted based upon an asserted incorrect filing location.

 

The Court Shall Transfer the Action

 

On July 27, 2026, the parties submitted a stipulation and order to transfer the action and were directed to file a noticed motion. In the stipulation, the parties agreed “All witnesses identified to date reside and/or work within the East District of the Los Angeles County Superior Court (East District) . . . The East District, Pomona Courthouse, would be far more convenient for trial and hearings for all witnesses . . . employment agreement at issue in this action was entered into in the East District . . . All work to be performed and all incidents thereof under the employment agreement were to occur in the East District . . . The events giving rise to Plaintiff’s claims for Wrongful Termination, Retaliation, and Wage Violations occurred in the East District . . . [and] There is no material connection between this action and the Central District.” (Dieringer Decl. Ex. D.) Defendant’s counsel further notes “the Pomona Courthouse would be preferred and more convenient for the percipient witness to attend trial, then a long burdensome rush-hour drive into Downtown Los Angeles.” (Dieringer Decl. ¶ 7.)

 

The Court is satisfied that the convenience of witnesses and ends of justice are supported by the requested, stipulated transfer.

 

The motion is GRANTED.

 

Conclusion

 

Motion to Transfer Case to Superior Court for Los Angeles County, East District Pomona Courthouse is GRANTED.

 

All hearings currently set in 25STCV06264 are hereby advanced and vacated.

 

Notice of case assignment shall be issued shortly.

 

Counsel for Defendant to give notice.


Case Number: 25VECV06512    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 534

Background

 

On November 13, 2025, Emeka Peter filed this action against Stone Dean, LLP and Suzanne R. Feffer arising out of allegations Defendant included Plaintiff’s social security number in a public court filing. The First Amended Complaint, filed November 24, 2025, asserted causes of action for: (1) negligence; (2) negligent infliction of emotional distress; (3) invasion of privacy; (4) violation of Civil Code § 1798.81.5; (5) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (6) negligence per se; (7) breach of confidentiality; (8) intrusion upon seclusion; (9) violation of California right to privacy; (10) unfair business practices; (11) willful misconduct; (12) breach of fiduciary duty; (13) legal malpractice; (14) declaratory & injunctive relief.

 

On May 19, 2026, the court granted Defendants’ motion to deem their requests for admission admitted. On June 16, 2026, Defendants filed a motion for summary judgment, set for hearing on September 29, 2026.

 

On June 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed a verified statement of disqualification of Judge Michael R. Amerian. The Court struck the statement on June 30, 2026.

 

On September 8, 2026, the court denied Plaintiff’s request to continue the summary judgment hearing.

 

Motion

 

On August 31, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion seeking to transfer this action to the Central District (Stanley Mosk Courthouse) or the North Central District (Pasadena Courthouse.

 

Defendants opposed the motion on the merits and therefore waived any objection to Plaintiff’s late filing. (Carlton v. Quint (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 690, 697.)

 

Motion to Transfer Between Districts

 

Standard

 

The Local Rules of the Superior Court of Los Angeles County govern the assignment of cases between its districts and departments. (Code Civ. Proc. § 402.) Plaintiff Failed to Demonstrate a Basis for a Transfer

 

Plaintiff’s action herein is based upon Defendants’ alleged filing of Plaintiff’s unredacted social security number in 23VECV05342.

 

In the motion, Plaintiff contends the ends of justice warrant a transfer “[b]ecause the

records administration, personnel, or operational safety of the Van Nuys Courthouse are the central subject of active litigation, maintaining this separate civil matter in the same physical location creates a profound structural conflict.” (Mot. at 3:9-13.) Plaintiff further argues “courthouse administration cannot seamlessly adjudicate an independent claim for an individual who is concurrently engaged in active data liability litigation against or involving that exact facility’s clerk and record systems. Continuing proceedings in the Northwest District creates an unavoidable perception of systemic bias or friction.” (Mot. at 3:26-4:3.)

 

The Court does not find a transfer warranted based upon the facts asserted. Plaintiff has not demonstrated an inability to have a fair trial or an appearance of impropriety that must be avoided via a district transfer.

 

The case shall remain as assigned and the motion is DENIED.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Transfer Case Between Districts is DENIED.

 

Counsel for Defendant to give notice.


Case Number: 26CMCV00640    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 534

Background

 

On April 7, 2026, Dorinda Brown and Henderson Martin filed this action against the City of Gardena and Shawn Washington arising out of a motor vehicle collision “at or near Vermont Ave & W. 135th St., Gardena, CA 90247.” (Compl. ¶ 7.)

 

Motion

 

On August 21, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion seeking to transfer the action to the Torrance Courthouse in the Southwest District.

 

The motion is unopposed. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1005.)

 

Motion to Transfer Between Districts

 

Standard

 

The Local Rules of the Superior Court of Los Angeles County govern the assignment of cases between its districts and departments. (Code Civ. Proc. § 402.) SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2) authorizes the Supervising Judge of the Civil Division to transfer civil cases from one judicial district to another via a noticed motion on three enumerated grounds: (1) when the case was filed in an improper district; (2) for the convenience of witnesses; or (3) to promote the ends of justice. (SCLAC Local Rule 2.3(b)(2).)

 

The Case Was Filed in an Incorrect District

 

Defendants seek to transfer this action from the Compton Courthouse in the South Central District to the Torrance Courthouse in the Southwest District. Defendants contend the case was not filed in the correct location based upon the applicable Local Rules.

 

The original complaint alleged the collision at issue took place “at or near Vermont Ave & W. 135th St., Gardena, CA 90247.” (Compl. ¶ 7.) Plaintiff, using an outdated version of the Civil Case Cover Sheet Addendum from 2018, listed this address as the basis for the filing location and certified the proper district was the South Central District.

 

“Every unlimited civil tort action for bodily injury, wrongful death, or damage to personal property (hereinafter referred to as ‘Personal Injury Action’) must be filed in the judicial district where the incident arose. The filing court locator on the Los Angeles Superior Court website (www.lacourt.org) should be used to determine the appropriate filing location.” (SCLAC Local Rules, rule 2.3(A)(1)(A).) Plaintiffs’ case is a Personal Injury Action and subject to the mandatory filing rules. The filing locator indicates either the Inglewood Courthouse or the Torrance Courthouse in the Southwest District are the appropriate filing locations.

 

Accordingly, the case was filed in an incorrect district and the motion is GRANTED, in part.

 

While Defendants’ Notice of Motion indicates a preference for the Torrance Courthouse over the Inglewood Courthouse in the Southwest District, the proper filing location is based upon the relevant district, not the individual courthouses within the district.

 

On August 26, 2026, Defendants filed a “Supplemental Declaration Re Companion Case” in which they identify a potentially related case pending in Department B of the Torrance Courthouse. The declaration contends “since there is another matter pending in Department B of the LASC Torrance in this matter, Defendants are requesting transfer be made to that department for all further proceedings.” (Supp. Dumont Decl.¶ 4.)

 

Department 534 shall not designate a courthouse at this time and the case shall be reassigned as though it were properly filed in the first instance.

 

To the extent Defendants assert there is a related case, they must file a Notice of Related Case pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.300.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants City of Gardena and Shawn Washington’s Motion to Transfer is GRANTED, in part.

 

All hearings currently set in 26CMCV00640 are hereby advanced and vacated.

 

Notice of case assignment shall be issued shortly.

 

Counsel for Defendants to give notice.

On August 20, 2026, Defendants Envision EDH MB Auto, LLC, Envision Motors Holdings, LLC, and Michael Marquez filed the instant motion seeking to relate 26STCV08973 and 26STCV08987.

 

The request for judicial notice of various filings in both actions is GRANTED. (Evid. Code § 452(d).)  

 

Motion to Relate Cases

 

Standard

 

Pursuant to Superior Court of Los Angeles County Local Rules, rule 3.3(f)(3), “[i]n the event that the judge designated under California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(h)(1)(A)(B)(C) to make the decision, does not order related any of the cases set forth in the Notice of Related Cases, any party may file a motion to have the cases related. Department 1 [renumbered as of May 4, 2026, Department 534]  shall hear the motion, if the cases are all pending in the Central District or are pending in two or more different districts.” The Court does not address the merits when ruling on a Notice of Related Case.

 

The Court Shall Relate the Cases at Issue

 

On August 6, 2026, Judge Karine Mkrtchyan issued a minute order finding 26STCV08973 and 26STCV08987 were not related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, Rule 3.300(a). The motion is therefore properly before Department 534. (SCLAC Local Rules, rule 3.3(f)(3); Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(h)(1)(D).)

 

Cases are related when they (1) involve the same parties and are based on the same or similar claims, (2) arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact, (3) involve claims against, title to, possession of, or damages to the same property, or (4) are likely for other reasons to require substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a).) Chatmon contends the cases are related based upon the first, second, and third grounds enumerated in Rule 3.300(a). The oppositions argue the cases should not be related.

 

As seen above, the cases do not involve the same parties. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(1).) However, the cases do arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact, (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(2)), and are likely to require a substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges. (Cal. R. Ct., rule 3.300(a)(4).)

 

The two pleadings allege shared interactions that serve as the basis for Plaintiffs’ claims including, an alleged battery on both Plaintiffs, (Nicolay FAC ¶ 13; Shaker FAC ¶ 262), a meeting wherein both Plaintiffs shared their grievances to management regarding Defendant Marquez, (Nicolay FAC ¶ 26), an alleged fraudulent vehicle deal blamed on both Plaintiffs, (Nicolay FAC ¶ 37; Shaker FAC ¶ 50), and a meeting wherein Defendant Marquez allegedly threatened Plaintiffs. (Nicolay FAC ¶¶ 40-41; Shaker FAC ¶ 56.) Additionally, both cases assert causes of action for failure to prevent harassment and negligent hiring based on Defendant Marquez’s conduct. Having a single judicial officer preside over the entirety of the disputes regarding Marquez’s alleged conduct promotes judicial efficiency and decreases the potential for conflicting rulings. (Harris v. Rojas (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 817, 820 (“The notice allows the trial court to promote efficiency, to avoid duplicative effort, to combat judge-shopping, and to minimize the prospect of conflicting results.”).)

 

The Court finds the cases are related within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300.

 

The motion is GRANTED.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants’ Motion to Relate Cases is GRANTED.

 

The Court relates 26STCV08973 and 26STCV08987 and orders 26STCV08987 reassigned to Department 512 of the Stanley Mosk Courthouse for all purposes.

 

All hearings currently set in 26STCV08987 are hereby advanced and vacated.

 

Counsel for Chatmon to give notice.
