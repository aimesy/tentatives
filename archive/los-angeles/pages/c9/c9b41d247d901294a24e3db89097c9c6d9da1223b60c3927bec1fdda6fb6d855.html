DEPARTMENT 407 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 21STCV42196    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 407

Yu Fang Chen v. Zhi He, et al. 

LASC Case No. 21STCV42196 

 

Court’s Ruling on Plaintiff’s Objections to Tentative Statement of Decision  

This Court has reviewed and considered the Plaintiff’s Objection to Statement of Decision, filed on 6/22/26. 1  This Court now issues the following rulings and orders: 

The Plaintiff’s objection is OVERRULED, as being without merit, nor a proper objection to the proposed tentative ruling.  The Plaintiff’s objection does not challenge any portion of the court’s tentative statement of decision.   

The Court also notes there is no requirement to issue a written findings of fact and conclusions of law.  CCP § 632 expressly states, in relevant part, that “[i]n superior courts, upon a trial of a question of fact by the court, written findings of fact and conclusions of law shall not be required.  (Emphasis added.)   Moreover, it is well settled that: 

“A trial court rendering a statement of decision under Code of Civil Procedure section 632 is required only to state ultimate rather than evidentiary facts. A trial court is not required to make findings with regard to detailed evidentiary facts or to make minute findings as to individual items of evidence. Only where a trial court fails to make findings as to a material issue which would fairly disclose the determination by the trial court would reversible error result. Even though a court fails to make a finding on a particular matter, if the judgment is otherwise supported, the omission is harmless error unless the evidence is sufficient to sustain a finding in favor of the complaining party which would have the effect of countervailing or destroying other findings. A failure to find on an immaterial issue is not error. [Citation.] In issuing a statement of decision, the trial court need not address each question listed in a party’ s request. All that is required is an explanation of the factual and legal basis for the court’s decision regarding such principal controverted issues at trial as are listed in the request.”  

 

(Ribakoff v. City of Long Beach (2018) 27 Cal.App.5th 50, 162-163.)  

 

The Plaintiff also requests findings on material controverted issues.  The request is DENIED.  The Plaintiff had the burden in proving her case and failed to do so. 

This Court’s Tentative Statement of Decision adequately satisfies its obligation under CCP § 632.  As such, no other modifications are required nor needed. This Court is not required, per se, to “modify” or otherwise change its SOD, unless there is a formal need to do so.  See, CRC, rule 3.1590 (b) [“If the court subsequently modifies or changes its announced decision . . .”] (Emphasis added.) 

Moreover, generally speaking, the trier of fact does not need to explain or justify, in any considerable detail, each and every piece of evidence it relied upon in reaching its verdict.   

Pursuant to the provisions of CRC, rule 3.1590, this Court issues the following additional orders, as follows: 

  1. In view of the foregoing, and pursuant to CRC, rule 3.1590 (f), this Court will adopt its prior tentative statement of decision, as the final statement of decision, without modification. 

  1. Defendant is directed to file a proposed judgment conforming with the Court’s decision forthwith.  

  1. The parties may reserve any and all rights they may have per CRC, rule 3.1590 (j). 

  1.  

The Clerk is hereby to give notice of this ruling forthwith to all counsel of record. 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 24STCV07324    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     July 31, 2026                          TRIAL DATE:  March 1, 2027

                                                          

CASE:                         HFC Acceptance, LLC v. Zeeba Company, Inc., et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV07324

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, AND REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION FROM DEFENDANTS KAMBIZ MARASHI, KAYVON MARASHI, AND SAEID MARASHI

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

On March 13, 2026, Plaintiff, HFC Acceptance, LLC, filed nine motions to compel further responses to Plaintiff’s first set of Form Interrogatories, Requests for Production of Documents, and Requests for Admission, from Defendants, Kambiz Marashi, Kayvon Marashi, and Saeid Marashi.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendants.

 

            On July 20, 2026, Defendants filed three omnibus oppositions.

 

            On July 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed an omnibus reply.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.300, 2031.310, and 2033.290, parties may move for a further response to interrogatories, requests for production of documents, and requests for admissions where an answer to the discovery requests are evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general.¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Notice of the motions must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(c); 2031.310(c); 2033.290(c).)¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Finally, California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(a)(3) requires that all motions or responses involving further discovery contain a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses.¿ 

 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

Sanctions shall be awarded against any party, person or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further responses, unless the Court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(d); 2031.310(h); 2033.290(d).)¿¿ 

 

III.      DISCUSSION  

 

Plaintiff seeks further responses to its first set of Form Interrogatories, Requests for Production of Documents, and Requests for Admission, from Defendants.  The motions are meritorious.  Plaintiff properly served the discovery requests.  Defendants served meritless, objection-only responses. Further, contrary to Defendants’ position, the service of supplemental responses during the pendency of a motion to compel further does not moot the motion.  (See County of San Benito v. Superior Court (2023) 96 Cal.App.5th 243, 256 [“Moreover, to adopt the County's interpretation of the discovery act—that a party opposing a motion to compel further responses may unilaterally divest the court of authority to rule on the motion by serving a further response in the interim—would permit a party faced with a motion to compel to delay its adjudication indefinitely by successive supplemental but inadequate responses, each prompting a further motion to compel, each of which in turn would be defeated by piecemeal supplementation.”].)  The court will grant the motions.                     

 

Monetary Sanctions

 

Given the court’s ruling, and Defendants’ assertion of meritless objections to relevant discovery requests, the court finds sanctions are warranted.  Accordingly, the court will grant Plaintiff’s request for sanctions in full.

 

IV.       CONCLUSION 

 

            The motions are GRANTED.  Defendants are ordered to serve further verified responses without objection to the at-issue discovery requests within 30 days of this order.

 

            Plaintiff’s requests for sanctions are GRANTED.  Each defendant is ordered to pay sanctions in the sum of $1,500, for a total award of $4,500 within 30 days of this order.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 24STCV17998    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: 407

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

CASE:                              Madhusudan Thapa Magar v. Dhurba Dhakal, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV17998

 

DEFENDANTS’ MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF MADHUSUDAN THAPA MAGAR TO PROVIDE RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES (SET ONE),

SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES (SET ONE), AND REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS (SET ONE) AND; REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendants Dhurba Shakal and Rajendra Singh Thakuri

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      Plaintiff Madhusudan Thapa Magar

 

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

            On March 11, 2026, Defendants, Dhurba Shakal and Rajendra Singh Thakuri, filed this motion to compel Plaintiff, Madhusudan Thapa Magar, to provide initial responses to Defendants’ first set of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Request for Production of Documents.  Defendants request sanctions against Plaintiff.

 

            On July 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed oppositions.[1]

 

            On July 24, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.[2]

 

            As indicated in Plaintiff’s oppositions, this motion is largely moot.  Plaintiff served verified responses without objections to Defendants’ discovery requests during the pendency of the motion.  (See Ikonte Decl., Ex. A.)  The court finds these discovery responses substantially comply with the Code of Civil Procedure.  The court proceeds to consider Defendants’ request for sanctions only. 

 

II.        DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff argues sanctions are not warranted because the failure to provide timely discovery responses was the result of personal tragedies. 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿ 

If the court finds that a party has unsuccessfully made or opposed a motion to compel responses, the court “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(c), 2031.300(c).)

 

Here, Plaintiff’s counsel states that he lost his uncle and father within 15 days.  For this reason, Plaintiff’s counsel did not notice that Defendants served discovery requests until Defendants filed this motion.  (Ikonte Decl., ¶¶ 2-7.)  Given this background and Plaintiff’s service of substantially compliant discovery responses, the court finds the imposition of sanctions would be unjust.  The court declines to impose sanctions at this time. 

 

III.      CONCLUSION

           

The motions are Moot.   

¿ 

¿           Defendants’ request for sanctions is DENIED.¿ 

 

            Plaintiff is ordered to give notice, unless waived.

  Judge of the Superior Court
