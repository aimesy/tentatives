DEPARTMENT 734 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22STCV34970    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 734

Defendant Nicolas P. Bru brings two ex parte applications.  The first seeks a continuance of the trial and trial-related deadlines; the second seeks a shortening of time on the hearing for Defendant’s motion for summary judgment, which is currently set for hearing on October 6, 2026.  The Court has considered the applications and finds the matters suitable for decision in chambers without argument.

The application for an order shortening time on the motion for summary judgment is DENIED.  Absent a stipulation of all parties, a court cannot “shorten time” on a motion for summary judgment, which has an inflexible mandatory notice period.  (McMahon v. Superior Court (2003) 106 Cal.App.4th 112, 115.)  Where a party serves notice of a hearing on a motion for summary judgment motion that does not comply with the timing requirements of section 437c, that notice is void.  (See Robinson v. Woods (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1258, 1267-68 (“Defendants noticed their motion for hearing within 30 days of the trial date without first obtaining a determination of good cause from the trial court. (See § 437c, subd. (a).) Unless and until the trial court found good cause, the notice of the hearing was invalid.”).)   Defendant filed and served a motion for summary judgment on July 13, 2026 with a hearing date of August 27, 2026—less than 81 days notice.  The notice was therefore invalid when served.  Robinson teaches that a court cannot save defective (i.e., too short) notice of a summary judgment hearing by continuing the hearing date, unless the notice period begins anew.  (Robinson, 168 Cal.App.4th at 1268.)  Therefore, granting the ex parte application to advance the hearing would be futile.

The application for a trial continuance is GRANTED IN PART.  The Court vacates the October 19, 2026 final status conference and November 2, 2026 trial dates, and sets a trial setting conference on January 4, 2027 at 8:30 a.m.  The discovery and motion cutoffs shall be extended and calculated by reference to the new trial date once set.  Defendant may reschedule the motion for summary judgment via CRS to a date that provides sufficient time for the minimum notice period of section 437c.

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 24STCV12256    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 734

Defendant Hyder & Company applies ex parte for an order specially setting its eight motions for summary judgment during the period of December 14, 2026 through December 18, 2026, or in the alternative, continuing the trial date.  The Court has considered the application and finds the matter suitable for decision in chambers without argument.

As Defendant correctly states, a party that timely files a motion for summary judgment has a right for the motion to be heard in advance of trial, such that a trial continuance must be granted if the Court’s calendar does not permit an earlier hearing date.  (Sentry Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 207 Cal.App.3d 526, 629; but see Robinson v. Woods (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1258, 1267-68 (“Defendants noticed their motion for hearing within 30 days of the trial date without first obtaining a determination of good cause from the trial court. (See § 437c, subd. (a).) Unless and until the trial court found good cause, the notice of the hearing was invalid. The party opposing a summary judgment motion should not be under an obligation to respond on the merits—and risk wasting its resources—given that the trial court may ultimately decide that good cause does not exist.”).)  The eight motions at issue have been filed sufficiently in advance of the January 19, 2027 trial date to be heard on 81 days statutory notice at least 30 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc. § 437c(a)(2), (3).)  The Court cannot advance the hearings on Defendant’s motions that were reserved for January and February 2027.  Thus, under Sentry Insurance, the Court must continue the trial.  The Court therefore vacates the January 4, 2027 final status conference and January 19, 2027 trial dates, and sets a trial setting conference for February 23, 2027 at 8:30 a.m.  The discovery and motion cutoffs are not extended, and shall remain calculated by reference to the January 19, 2027 trial date unless the parties obtain an order extending discovery. 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV14642    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: 734

Plaintiff Anthony Ayala applies ex parte for an order shortening time on Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production.  The Court has considered the application and finds the matter suitable for decision in chambers without argument.

First, the Court should point out a misnomer in the caption of the application.  An order “shortening time” is an order prescribing shorter times for the performance of an act, such as an order reducing the time for filing and service of papers under Code of Civil Procedure section 1005.  (See Cal.R.Ct. 3.1300(b).)  An order that a hearing will be held on standard notice, but on an earlier date than initially set, is an order advancing a hearing.  The difference is not mere semantics, because the two forms of relief require different showings.  When the Court shortens time on a motion and allows an abbreviated briefing schedule, the Court must account for its own ability to hear the motion on less than statutory notice, as well as the parties’ ability to meet earlier briefing deadlines.  On the other hand, advancing a hearing date (while preserving the briefing times prescribed by Code of Civil Procedure section 1005) is simply a matter of the Court’s own ability to accommodate the hearing.

The basis for the application is the fact that the earliest available hearing date that Plaintiff was able to obtain through CRS was July 16, 2027, which is after the trial date in this case.  When parties seek to advance and specially set hearing dates on discovery motions that have been reserved after the discovery cutoff (or after the trial) the Court has historically denied such requests, because the requests are often the product of procrastination.  In the instant case, there is no procrastination:  Plaintiff filed the subject motion on September 15, 2026—ten months before the trial date and nine months before the statutory discovery cutoff.  Unfortunately, the Court’s motion calendar has become increasingly congested due to the tremendous increase in civil filings in Los Angeles Superior Court. 

The application is GRANTED.  The July 16, 2027 hearing on Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production is advanced to January 11, 2027 at 8:30 a.m.  The deadlines under Code of Civil Procedure section 1005 for filing opposition and reply papers will be set by reference to the new hearing date.

Moving party to give notice.

            Plaintiffs allege that Defendant’s structure which is being rebuilt following the Pacific Palisades wildfires will obstruct Plaintiffs’ views.

Defendant Patricia W. Cheng, Trustee of the Cheng Trust, for the benefit of Patricia W. Cheng demurs to the First Amended Complaint.

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant Patricia W. Cheng, Trustee of the Cheng Trust, for the benefit of Patricia W. Cheng’s demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED as second cause of action.

            Defendant is to answer the First Amended Complaint within 10 days.

ANALYSIS

Demurrer

Meet and Confer

            The Declaration of Jonathan J. Boustani reflects that Defendant’s counsel satisfied the meet and confer requirement set forth in Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41.

Discussion

The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

Defendant Patricia W. Cheng, Trustee of the Cheng Trust, for the benefit of Patricia W. Cheng demurs to the First Amended Complaint as follows:

1.         Second Cause of Action (Private Nuisance).

Defendant argues that Plaintiffs’ private nuisance claim fails because it is still improperly based on alleged view obstructions caused by Defendant’s rebuilt home, and there is no nuisance simply because a building or structure obstructs the view from neighboring property.

            Plaintiff argues that the 1AC now identifies the source and nature of the legally protected property interest, the reciprocal servitudes creating that interest, Defendant’s actual and constructive notice of those restrictions, and the manner in which Defendant’s post-fire reconstruction interferes with those rights.

“[I]t is well settled in this state that an owner of land may not do even nonnegligent acts on his property with impunity where they create a nuisance as to his neighbor.” (Citation omitted].) “To prevail on an action for private nuisance, a plaintiff must first prove an interference with the plaintiff’s use and enjoyment of his or her property. (Citation omitted .) Second, ‘the invasion of the plaintiff’s interest in the use and enjoyment of the land [must be] substantial, i.e., that it cause[s] the plaintiff to suffer “substantial actual damage.”‘ (Ibid.) Third, ‘“[t]he interference with the protected interest must not only be substantial, but it must also be unreasonable” [citation], i.e., it must be “of such a nature, duration or amount as to constitute unreasonable interference with the use and enjoyment of the land.”‘ (Citations omitted.) 

(Lynch v. Peter & Associates (2024) 104 Cal.App.5th 1181, 1197.)

“ ‘[A]s a general rule, a landowner has no natural right to air, light or an unobstructed view and the law is reluctant to imply such a right.’ “ (Citations omitted.) [and] “. . . the courts have held that a building or structure cannot be complained of as a nuisance merely because it obstructs the view from neighboring property” (citation omitted). . . .

(Oliver v. AT&T Wireless Services (1999) 76 Cal. App. 4th 521, 535-36.)

The Court previously ruled that the CC&Rs would permit the erecting of a single story dwelling that does not “unreasonably obstruct” the view from any other lot. Whether Defendant’s structure “unreasonably obstructs” the view from any other lot is a question of fact outside the scope of this demurrer. As such, the first cause of action pleads facts sufficient to state a cause of action.

            Plaintiffs have amended to allege:

55. Plaintiffs do not contend that California common law recognizes an unrestricted right to an unobstructed scenic view. Rather, Defendant’s conduct constitutes an unreasonable interference with legally protected property interests arising from the reciprocal equitable servitudes created by the recorded CC&Rs. Those servitudes burden Defendant’s property, benefit Plaintiffs’ properties, run with the land, and define the permissible use of Defendant’s property.

 

56. Before commencing reconstruction of the residence located at 16787 Charmel Lane, Defendant had actual and constructive notice of the recorded CC&Rs and actual notice that Plaintiffs contended the proposed reconstruction exceeded the historic roofline and would materially interfere with the property rights protected by those servitudes.

 

57. Despite that notice, Defendant knowingly proceeded with construction that materially increased the height, elevation, massing, and roof configuration of the improvements on Defendant’s property in violation of the recorded restrictions benefiting neighboring dominant estates.

 

58. Defendant’s conduct constitutes an unreasonable use of Defendant’s property because it knowingly interferes with the reciprocal property rights established by the recorded CC&Rs and substantially interferes with Plaintiffs’ use and enjoyment of their respective properties.

 

59. The interference alleged herein is substantial and continuing. It is not merely aesthetic or limited to the loss of a scenic vista. Defendant’s construction materially impairs Plaintiffs’ beneficial use and enjoyment of their homes, diminishes the utility and value of their properties, interferes with the intended use of principal living areas and outdoor spaces designed to take advantage of the protected view corridors, and substantially interferes with Plaintiffs’ quiet enjoyment of their properties.

 

60. Defendant’s conduct is unreasonable because Defendant elected to construct improvements materially exceeding the historic roof profile despite actual notice of Plaintiffs’ objections, despite the existence of recorded reciprocal land-use restrictions, and despite the availability of reasonable alternative designs that would have substantially reduced or eliminated the interference. 

(1AC, ¶¶ 55 – 60.)

            In this regard, a violation of CC&Rs may constitute a nuisance if it interferes with the free use and enjoyment of the Plaintiff’s property:

It is settled that where conduct which violates a duty owed to another also interferes with that party’s free use and enjoyment of his property, nuisance liability arises. (Citations omitted.) Under this definition of nuisance, the Association’s violation of its duty to Cutujian under the CC&R’s gave rise to liability for the nuisance caused by the slump, which plainly interfered with Cutujian’s use of the land. Moreover, the CC&R’s governing BHE provide that any violation of a duty created by the CC&R’s will give rise to an action for nuisance. It also appears that the nuisance was abatable, inasmuch as Cutujian was able to abate it. Consequently, it can reasonably be deemed a continuing nuisance for which a new cause of action arises each day on which the nuisance continues. (Citations omitted.)

(Cutujian v. Benedict Hills Estates Assn. (1996) 41 Cal. App. 4th 1379, 1384-1390.)

            Plaintiffs have sufficient amended to plead a cause of action for nuisance.

            The demurrer to the second cause of action is OVERRULED.

            Defendant is to answer the First Amended Complaint within 10 days.
