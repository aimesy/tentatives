I.                BACKGROUND

On January 8, 2026, 9000 Arlington Ave LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Richard Irwin Kane, Trustee of the Richard Irwin Kane 2006 Trust (“Kane”); Bronzetree Terraces, LLC (“Bronzetree”); RTI Properties, Inc.; Rediger Investment Mortgage Fund; ISAOA; Peak Foreclosure Services, Inc.; and Lida Kohansameh (collectively “Defendants”). The complaint contained the following causes of action: (1) wrongful foreclosure; (2) cancellation of instruments; (3) promissory estoppel; and (4) quiet title.

Plaintiff is a limited liability company in California and Cole Moscatel (“Moscatel”) is the sole member. Rediger holds a first priority deed of trust which was recorded against the 23476 Palm Drive Calabasas, California 91302 (“Subject Property”). On April 7, 2025, Plaintiff and Kane and Bronzetree entered into a Second Lien for $500,000.

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

 

a.     Demurrer

            A demurrer is a procedure to object to a pleading set forth under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, et seq.¿ Because a demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters, a demurrer analysis considers only those defects which appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed.¿ (SKF Farms v. Superior Court (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 902, 905.) When considering demurrers, courts assume the truth of the facts alleged in the pleading and read the allegations liberally and in context.¿ (Schifando v. City of Los Angeles (2003) 31 Cal.4th 1074, 1081.)

b.     Motion to Strike

            The Court has broad authority to strike any portion of a pleading drawn not within conformity of the law and its orders.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) The trial court shall strike an allegation of punitive damages on motion unless the plaintiff has pleaded ultimate supporting facts.¿ (Clauson v. Superior Court (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1253, 1255.)

III.           DISCUSSION

a.     Judicial Notice

Kane asks the Court to take judicial notice of Items 1 to 10. Items 1-3 are real property instruments record with the Los Angeles Official Records Recorder’s Office, and Item 10 is the Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale recorded with the Los Angeles Official Records Recorder’s Office.

            The Court grants judicial notice as to the Items 1-3 and 10 according to Evidence Code, section 452, subdivision (c).

            Plaintiff objects to the Court taking judicial notice of Items 4 to 9. Item 4 is “the fact that the delivery of good funds by a wire transfer from one bank to another bank, even domestically, cannot with certainty be confirmed within 25 minutes.” Items 5 to 9 are bank websites which purport to establish the delivery time of wire transfers. The Court finds that the information and web pages contained in Items 4 to 9 are not within the proper scope of subjects for judicial notice. Kane requests the Court to take notice of the content of the websites, specifically statements regarding the time it takes for wire transfers to be completed. (Ragland v. U.S. Bank National Association, et al. (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 182, 194) (“The contents of the Web sites and blogs are “plainly subject to interpretation and for that reason not subject to judicial notice.”)

            Therefore, the Court denies the request for judicial notice as to Items 4-9.  

b.     Meet and Confer

Kane’s counsel declares on February 2, 2026, he sent a meet and confer letter to Plaintiff’s counsel. The parties then had discussions regarding the demurrer and motion to strike. It is unclear whether the parties discussed the issues over the phone, via video conference or in-person. However, the Court will exercise its discretion and decide the motion on its merits.

c.      Demurrer

            Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s first through third causes of action alleging they do not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

                                     i.          First Cause of Action: Wrongful Foreclosure Cancellation

            “The elements of a wrongful foreclosure cause of action are: (1) the trustee or mortgagee caused an illegal, fraudulent, or willfully oppressive sale of real property pursuant to a power of sale in a mortgage or deed of trust; (2) the party attacking the sale (usually but not always the trustor or mortgagor) was prejudiced or harmed; and (3) in cases where the trustor or mortgagor challenges the sale, the trustor or mortgagor tendered the amount of the secured indebtedness or was excused from tendering.” (Citrus El Dorado, LLC v. Chicago Title Co. (2019) 32 Cal.App.5th 943, 948, quotation marks and brackets omitted.) 

            Here, Plaintiff has not sufficiently stated a cause of action for wrongful foreclosure cancellation. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants proceeded to a foreclosure sale “in contravention of the ongoing agreement between Plaintiff and RIT Properties, acting as a loan servicer for Kane and Bronzetree, that the Second Lien Lenders would forbear from foreclosing in exchange for receiving a $50,000 payment on December 12, 2025 before the foreclosure sale was to occur.” (Compl., ¶ 42.)

The alleged agreement arises out of a series of text messages and emails between Moscatel and Robert Abbasi, the president of RTI Properties. The exchange between Moscatel and Abbasi began on December 1, 2025 when Abbasi stated “If you send $5,000 to the account I sent then we can delay the sale til this coming Friday.” (Compl., ¶ 27.) Moscatel then responded on December 2, 2025, stating: “Hi Robert, As per our agreement memorialized in your recent text message wherein you have agreed to accept a wire of $5,000 in exchange for postponing the auction date to this Friday (December 5), I have initiated a wire to you. Please confirm receipt of the funds once received and when the postpone of the auction has occurred. Thank you!” (Compl., ¶ 28.) The foreclosure sale was continued. (Compl., ¶ 29.) On December 9, 2025, Abbasi stated: “I’ll extend the sale till this coming Friday, but we need a conclusion to this by then, at least on one large payment. There are no other promises or guarantees here, we reserve all of our rights under the loan documents. Thanks.” (Compl., ¶ 32.) On the same day, Abbasi texted stating: “I extended till Friday, but we need results by then.” (Compl., ¶ 33.) The final text from Moscatel on December 12, 2025, the day of the foreclosure sale, reads: “Robert, I was able to pull together $50,000. Can you confirm that if I wire you this amount now, you will cancel the sale per our discussions? Also, please confirm the attached wire instructions are correct.” (Compl., ¶ 35.) Plaintiff alleges that Defendants then proceeded with the foreclosure sale. (Compl., ¶¶ 36-39.) Kane argues the communications between Plaintiff and Defendants did not constitute an agreement. In opposition, Plaintiff argues the December 12, 2025, email is a direct reference to a promise and agreement previously reached between Plaintiff and Second Lien Lenders. (Opp. p. 6:5-8.)

            Plaintiff had defaulted on the Second Lien on the Property. (Compl., ¶ 21.) The Court finds Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged that there was an agreement in place that Defendants had to accept the $50,000 offered on December 12, 2025. Moscatel’s December 12, 2025, text message was an offer, which Abbasi was free to accept or reject. Abbasi never responded to the text message and thus did not accept Moscatel’s offer. (Donovan v. Rrl Corp. (2001) 26 Cal.4th 261, 270-271 (“An essential element of any contract is the consent of the parties, or mutual assent … Mutual assent usually is manifested by an offer communicated to the offeree and an acceptance communicated to the offeror.”) Abbasi, at most, agreed to extend the deadline until Friday, December 12, 2025. He did not agree to accept a specific amount, and he did not accept Moscatel’s December 12th offer of $50,000 to cancel the sale. Plaintiff had defaulted on the Second Lien and thus Defendants were entitled to proceed with foreclosure. Therefore, Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged that Defendants acted illegally, fraudulently, or willfully oppressive in proceeding with the foreclosure sale as scheduled.

            Therefore, the demurrer to the first cause of action is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.  

                                   ii.          Second Cause of Action: Cancellation of Instruments

            The necessary elements for cancellation of instrument are: (1) a written instrument; (2) a reasonable apprehension that it may cause serious injury to someone; (3) as to whom it is void or voidable.¿ (Civ. Code., §3412; see also Kroeker v. Hurlbert (1940) 38 Cal.App.2d 261, 266.) 

            Because Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged a cause of action for wrongful foreclosure, the cause of action for cancellation of instruments is insufficient because Plaintiff has not alleged the instrument was void or voidable.

            Therefore, the demurrer to the second cause of action is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

                                 iii.          Third Cause of Action: Promissory Estoppel

            “The elements of a promissory estoppel claim are (1) a promise clear and unambiguous in its terms; (2) reliance by the party to whom the promise is made; (3) the reliance must be both reasonable and foreseeable; and (4) the party asserting the estoppel must be injured by his reliance.” (Flintco Pacific, Inc. v. TEC Management Consultants, Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.App.5th 727, 734, quotation marks and brackets omitted.) 

            Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged a promise clear and unambiguous in its terms. Mr. Abassi’s only promise to Plaintiff was the foreclosure sale would be extended until December 12, 2025 but they needed “at least one large payment.” (Compl., ¶ 32.) It is ambiguous as to the amount of what constitutes one large payment. Plaintiff argues it was $50,000, however, nowhere in Mr. Abassi’s email does he state one large payment would be $50,000. The term “one large payment” is vague and thus any alleged agreement was not unambiguous. Therefore, Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged a cause of action for promissory estoppel.

Accordingly, the demurrer to the third cause of action is SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

d.     Motion to Strike

Kane moves to strike the following lines from Plaintiff’s complaint:

(1)   The actions of Kane, Bronzetree and RTI Properties were malicious, oppressive and

            fraudulent, and undertaken with a conscious disregard of Plaintiff’s rights, and Plaintiff is       entitled to an award of punitive damages as a result.” (Compl., p. 8, ¶ 45, lns. 20-22.)

(2)   For punitive damages against Kane, Bronzetree and RTI;” (Compl., p. 10, Prayer ¶ 2, ln.        25.)

Punitive damages may be recovered upon a proper showing of malice, fraud, or oppression. (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).) “Malice” is defined as conduct intended to cause injury to a person or despicable conduct carried on with a willful and conscious disregard for the rights or safety of others. (Turman v. Turning Point of Cent. Cal., Inc. (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 53, 63.) “Oppression” means despicable conduct subjecting a person to cruel and unjust hardship, in conscious disregard of the person’s rights. (Ibid.) “Fraud” is an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known by defendant, with intent to deprive a person of property, rights or otherwise cause injury. (Ibid.) Conclusory allegations, devoid of any factual assertions, are insufficient to support a conclusion that parties acted with oppression, fraud or malice. (Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1042.)

Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged Defendants acted with malice, fraud, or oppression. As Plaintiff’s allegation for punitive damages was based on the alleged conduct that gave rise to the first cause of action for wrongful foreclosure, and the first cause of action has been sustained on demurrer, Plaintiff has not adequately alleged punitive damages.

Therefore, the motion to strike is GRANTED.

IV.           CONCLUSION

            Defendant Kane’s demurrer to the first through third causes of action is SUSTAINED with twenty (20) days’ leave to amend.

            The motion to strike is GRANTED.

            Plaintiff is to give notice unless  all parties waive notice at the hearing.
