[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

 

DEFENDANT SAN MARTIN DE PORRES MEDICAL CLINIC OF SOUTH GATE, INC.’S MOTION FOR MANDATORY DISMISSAL

 

Background

On June 19, 2020, Plaintiff Ricardo Armando Nunez (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendant San Martin De Porres Medical Clinic of South Gate, Inc. (“San Martin”), alleging eight causes of action for (1) age discrimination, (2) FEHA retaliation, (3) violation of Labor Code §§510, 1194 & 1198 (unpaid overtime), (4) violation of Labor Code §2802 (reimbursements), (5) violation of Labor Code §§226.7 & 512 (meal and rest breaks), (6) violation of Labor Code §226(a) (inaccurate wage statements), (7) violation of Labor Code §§200-204 (waiting time penalties), and (8) violation of Labor Code §§1182.12-1194 (failure to pay minimum wage). On December 22, 2020, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint. On May 17, 2021, Plaintiff filed a second amended complaint.

On September 27, 2021, San Martin filed a motion for summary adjudication, which was granted in part on March 4, 2022 as to the first cause of action for age discrimination.  

On April 17, 2023, Plaintiff filed the operative third amended complaint (“TAC”) against San Martin, alleging seven causes of action, omitting the previous first cause of action for age discrimination.

San Martin now moves to “dismiss[] this action with prejudice pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 583.310 and Code Civ. Proc. § 583.360.” (Notice of Mot., 2:5-6.) Plaintiff opposes. San Martin replied.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 583.310 provides, “[a]n action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant.” Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 583.360, “(a) An action shall be dismissed by the court on its own motion or on motion of the defendant, after notice to the parties, if the action is not brought to trial within the time prescribed in this article. (b) The requirements of this article are mandatory and are not subject to extension, excuse, or exception except as expressly provided by statute.”

“In computing the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article, there shall be excluded the time during which any of the following conditions existed: (a) The jurisdiction of the court to try the action was suspended. (b) Prosecution or trial of the action was stayed or enjoined. (c) Bringing the action to trial, for any other reason, was impossible, impracticable, or futile.” ((Id., § 583.340.) “[T]he Legislature has specifically stated that for purposes of that section impossibility, impracticability or futility must be due to causes beyond a party’s control.” (Westinghouse Electric Corp. v. Superior Court (1983) 143 Cal.App.3d 95, 105 (Westinghouse).) Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 583.330, “[t]he parties may extend the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article by…written stipulation” and “[b]y oral agreement made in open court, if entered in the minutes of the court or a transcript is made.”

Discussion

As noted above, this action was filed on June 19, 2020; thus, without any tolling, the five-year deadline would have expired on June 19, 2025.

San Martin contends that dismissal is proper because “[t]he five-year deadline expired solely because of Plaintiff’s failure to diligent[ly] prosecute the case. Plaintiff was not prevented from proceeding to trial by a stay, suspension of jurisdiction, or any circumstance outside his control. For 4.5 years, the case languished in litigation without much effort from Plaintiff (or his counsel). Then, on October 16, 2024, the parties entered into a settlement agreement.” (Mot., 5:7-15.)

It is undisputed that a settlement agreement tolls the five-year deadline because it makes it futile to bring an action to trial. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1385(c)(4) [“the filing of a notice of conditional settlement removes the case from the computation of time used to determine case disposition time.”].) However, the dispute here is how long the tolling period lasted. “[T]he ‘salient factor’ for applying the tolling exceptions in section 583.340(c) ‘is whether during the period covered by the agreement, [a plaintiff] would have been acting in bad faith and in violation of the spirit of the agreement by proceeding with the prosecution of the action.’” ((Seto v. Szeto (2022) 86 Cal.App.5th 76, 91 (Seto).)

The parties entered into a settlement agreement on October 16, 2024. The terms of which state that: “A. Defendant is to pay gross sum of $200,000.00 in two installments by way of checks made payable to West Coas [sic] Trial Lawyers, Client Trust Account: (1) By 10/26/24, $100,000.00; and (2) By 12/20/24, $100,000.00. B. If the settlement funds are not paid in full by the court hearing set in response to the Notice of Settlement filed, then the agreement is null, and Plaintiff will seek a trial date.” (Stip. Settlement 10/16/24, 1:16-25; Lopez Decl., ¶1, Ex. 1.) The Court’s Minute Order from October 18, 2024, states that “[p]ursuant to the Notice of Settlement filed on 10/16/2024, the Court sets the following: Order to Show Cause Re: Dismissal is scheduled for 12/05/2024 at 10:00 AM in Department 50 at Stanley Mosk Courthouse.” (Lopez Decl., ¶2, Ex. 2.) After failing to receive the first payment pursuant to the settlement, on November 26, 2024, Plaintiff filed a petition to enforce settlement.

San Martin asserts that “the settlement agreement was in effect for, at most, 50 calendar days. [O]n October 16, 2024, the parties executed a settlement agreement. It is undisputed that by November 26, 2024, the agreement was breached. On that day, Plaintiff filed a motion to enforce the settlement agreement predicated on the breach of the agreement, due to non-payment. He deliberately and strategically filed a motion to enforce the settlement, which was unsupported by the settlement agreement, rather than restore the case to the litigation track and seek a trial date, as supported by the settlement. This was his ‘tactical choice’…” (Mot., 11:25-12:3.)

San Martin further asserts that “[b]y filing a motion predicated on the breach of an agreement, Plaintiff admitted that the agreement was no longer ‘in effect’ as of November 26, 2024. The settlement agreement was thus in effect, by Plaintiff’s own admission, for 41 calendar days, extending the five-year deadline to July 30, 2025.” (Mot., 12:13-16.)

Plaintiff refutes this point by asserting that “motion to enforce a settlement agreement does not constitute an admission that the agreement has been breached, nor does it render the agreement null and void so as to require the plaintiff to seek trial on the underlying claims. A motion to enforce is an action for specific performance of a contract.” (Opp., 8:2-5.)

Plaintiff’s opposition also contends that the settlement agreement should, at a minimum, be tolled through December 20, 2024, when the final payment was due, because the agreement was still operative. Plaintiff asserts that “California courts consistently hold that a settlement agreement with installment payments remains ‘operative’ for tolling purposes under CCP § 583.340(c) until the final payment becomes due, regardless of earlier breaches or missed payments… The settlement agreement’s payment schedule created obligations extending through December 20, 2024, regardless of the defendants’ earlier breach.” (Opp., 8:20-28.)

Plaintiff further contends that, ultimately, the timeline should be tolled by 14 months because “the Agreement’s terms required Plaintiff to ‘seek a trial date’ if Defendant did not pay the settlement sum. (See Exh. ‘A.’) Plaintiff did so by way of Motion to Restore the Case in August 2025 after his Petition to Enforce the Settlement was denied in June 2025.” (Opp., 9:28-10:2.)

A settlement tolls the five-year period during the time when the settlement is operational (See Brown & Bryant, Inc. v. Hartford Accident & Indemnity Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 247, 257 [“the five-year period is nevertheless tolled for the period the agreement was in effect.].) When San Martin did not pay its first installment, Plaintiff chose to try to enforce the settlement agreement rather than comply with the express term of the settlement agreement which stated that “the agreement is null, and Plaintiff will seek a trial date.” (Lopez Decl., ¶1, Ex. 1.)  The court in Seto, supra, 86 Cal.App.5th at p. 87 stated when discussing conditional settlements, a “line of cases holds that ‘the time during which a settlement agreement is in effect tolls the five-year period, for the reason that attempting to bring an action to trial when all issues have been resolved through settlement would be futile’ within the meaning of section 583.340(c).” However, because of the settlement agreement’s nullity provision, Plaintiff “‘would [not] have been acting in bad faith and in violation of the spirit of the agreement by proceeding with the prosecution of the action.’” (Seto, supra, 86 Cal.App.5th at p. 91.) Moreover, “[b]ecause the purpose of the dismissal statute ‘is to prevent avoidable delay, ... [section 583.340, subdivision (c) ] makes allowance for circumstances beyond the plaintiff’s control, in which moving the case to trial is impracticable for all practical purposes.’” (Tanguilig v. Neiman Marcus Group., Inc. (2018) 22 Cal.App.5th 313, 323.) However, Plaintiff’s decision to enforce the settlement agreement, rather than “seek a trial date” as the settlement agreement explicitly prescribed, demonstrates that the delay was within Plaintiff’s control.

Plaintiff also asserts that equitable estoppel supports overcoming dismissal. Plaintiff cites a few cases in support, one of which is Borglund v. Bombardier, Ltd. (1981) 121 Cal.App.3d 276, 281, where the Court of Appeal reasoned that “[i]f a trial court encounters statements or conduct by a defendant which lulls the plaintiff into a false sense of security resulting in inaction, and there is reasonable reliance, estoppel must be available to prevent defendant from profiting from his deception. By following the rule of Tresway, we move away from the rigid application of a few limited exceptions to section 583, subdivision (b), and rather adopt a rule that permits substantial justice to triumph over form.” Plaintiff asserts that “the facts fit squarely within these authorities and are considerably stronger than the typical estoppel scenario. Unlike cases involving ambiguous scheduling statements or informal settlement discussions, here the defendant entered into a written, court-filed settlement agreement… Having settled the matter, Plaintiff had no reason to continue preparing for trial because the parties had negotiated a resolution that contemplated dismissal upon payment. Defendant, rather than comply with the Settlement Agreement, through its nonpayment and lack of response left Plaintiff with the choice either to abandon the negotiated resolution or pursue enforcement.” (Opp., 12:27-13:6.) Further, Plaintiff asserts that “[i]nstead of immediately restoring the case to the trial calendar and incurring further litigation costs, Plaintiff sought to effectuate the settlement the parties had already negotiated by filing a Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement in a good-faith effort to secure compliance and avoid unnecessary litigation.” (Opp., 13:7-10.) Lastly, Plaintiff contends that “[h]ad Defendant complied with the Settlement Agreement, Plaintiff would not have been forced into settlement enforcement proceedings or delayed in restoring the case to the trial calendar.” (Opp., 13:13-15.)

San Martin points out in its response, that “Plaintiff’s opposition identifies no specific representation or affirmative conduct by Defendant that induced any delay… Nonpayment of a settlement and the absence of further communication are not affirmative conduct capable of lulling a represented plaintiff into a false sense of security; they are, at most, a breach -- and a breach is what triggered Plaintiff’s own motion to enforce in the first place. As the dissenting opinion in Tresway recognized, a substantial body of California authority holds that conduct which merely fails to prevent a plaintiff’s inaction, without some affirmative act inducing that inaction, does not satisfy the estoppel doctrine.” (Reply, 7:23-8:3.) Additionally, San Martin notes that “Plaintiff has never explained why, once the enforcement motion was denied on June 13, 2025, restoration could not have been sought immediately rather than roughly two months later.” (Reply, 8:12-13.)

Based on the foregoing assertions, the Court finds that equitable estoppel does not apply. Although Plaintiff sought to enforce the terms of the settlement agreement, from the plain language of the agreement, it was “null” and Plaintiff’s option was to “seek a trial date.” Further, the Court previously discussed the parties’ settlement obligations when it ruled on Plaintiff’s petition to enforce settlement on June 13, 2025, reasoning that “the parties agreed in paragraph 1B of the Settlement Agreement that failure to make the payments in full by the designated date gave Plaintiff the option to declare the Settlement Agreement null and obtain a trial date. Obviously, Plaintiff was not obligated to declare the Settlement Agreement null if the deadline was missed; Plaintiff could wait longer for the payment if he wished to do so. But the Settlement Agreement does not state that Plaintiff may request entry of judgment in the amount of $200,000 plus attorney fees if Defendant does not make the payments.” (Order 6/13/25, 4:20-26.) Following San Martin’s breach, Plaintiff had the choice to reinstate the trial date, wait for payment, or wait for the hearing on the motion to enforce the settlement. Plaintiff did the latter to enforce a conditional settlement agreement where the condition had not been satisfied. Plaintiff’s failure to bring the case to trial or timely resolve the five-year deadline issue is due to Plaintiff’s own choices after breach of the agreement.

Under the strict prescription of Code of Civil Procedure section 583.310, this case must be dismissed because the five-year deadline has passed. Plaintiff fails to demonstrate that the settlement made bringing the action to trial “impossible, impracticable, or futile.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.340, subd. (c).) Accordingly, the Court finds it necessary to grant the motion to dismiss under the five-year statute.

Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, San Martin’s motion to dismiss is GRANTED. San Martin is ordered to file a proposed judgment within ten days of this Order.

San Martin is ordered to give notice of this Order.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

 

DEFENDANTS SATHIYA AND RAJU RASIAH’S MOTION TO DISMISS FOR FAILURE TO PROSECUTE

 

Background

On February 18, 2021, Plaintiffs Armen Temurian and Luis Amaya, individually, and dba Vista Network Technologies USA and as Vista Network (jointly, “Plaintiffs”) filed this action against Defendants Sathiya Araj Rasiah, aka Raj Milton, aka Milton Rasih, Ask Milton Group, George Akopian, and Raju Rasiah, alleging five causes of action for (1) breach of contract, (2) fraudulent inducement, (3) money had and received, (4) breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and (5) unfair business practices. 

Defendants Milton Rasiah and Raju Rasiah (jointly, “Defendants”) now move to dismiss this case for failure to prosecute. Plaintiffs oppose. Defendants replied.

Request for Judicial Notice

The Court grants Defendants’ request for judicial notice of Exhibits 1, 2, 3, and 4.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 583.310 provides, “[a]n action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant.” Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 583.360, “(a) An action shall be dismissed by the court on its own motion or on motion of the defendant, after notice to the parties, if the action is not brought to trial within the time prescribed in this article. (b) The requirements of this article are mandatory and are not subject to extension, excuse, or exception except as expressly provided by statute.”

“In computing the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article, there shall be excluded the time during which any of the following conditions existed: (a) The jurisdiction of the court to try the action was suspended. (b) Prosecution or trial of the action was stayed or enjoined. (c) Bringing the action to trial, for any other reason, was impossible, impracticable, or futile.” ((Id., § 583.340.) “[T]he Legislature has specifically stated that for purposes of that section impossibility, impracticability or futility must be due to causes beyond a party’s control.” (Westinghouse Electric Corp. v. Superior Court (1983) 143 Cal.App.3d 95, 105 (Westinghouse).) Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 583.330, “[t]he parties may extend the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article by…written stipulation” and “[b]y oral agreement made in open court, if entered in the minutes of the court or a transcript is made.”

Discussion

As set forth above, the instant action was filed on February 18, 2021. Defendants contend that “[t]rial is currently scheduled for July 15, 2026, representing an elapsed time of five years, four months, and twenty-seven days from filing of the Complaint.” (Mot., 5:2-3.)

A.    Bankruptcy Proceedings

Plaintiffs contend, and Defendants concede, that a bankruptcy stay was ordered by the Court on April 30, 2021, and this stay lasted for approximately three months until July 30, 2021. Under 11 U.S.C. § 362(a), filing for bankruptcy creates an automatic stay of proceedings. Accordingly, this tolls the five-year deadline from February 18, 2026 to May 18, 2026.

B.     Appeal of the Motion to Disqualify Counsel

Plaintiffs also contend that the action must be stayed for an additional thirteen to fourteen months because of an appeal. The Court granted Plaintiffs’ motion to disqualify counsel on July 30, 2024. Thereafter, on September 20, 2024, Defendants Milton Rasih, George Akopian, and Raju Rasiah filed a notice of appeal. A remittitur was issued on November 6, 2025, where the Court of Appeal affirmed this Court’s decision. Plaintiffs assert that this period of time made bringing the case impossible, impracticable, or futile because “[w]hen Defendants’ disqualification motion was granted, Plaintiffs’ counsel was removed from the case. The appeal of that ruling created a period during which retaining and working with replacement counsel was in procedural limbo, and during which the case could not realistically be advanced to trial. Plaintiffs did not abandon the case during that period -- they appeared in propria persona, maintained the action, and eventually retained new counsel after the Remittitur issued on November 6, 2025. That thirteen-to-fourteen-month period (September 26, 2024, to November 6, 2025) is excludable under section 583.340(c).” (Opp., 6:24-7:4.) The Court notes that Plaintiffs misstate who brought the motion to disqualify; according to the record and the Court’s Order, Plaintiffs brought the motion to disqualify Defendants’ counsel, which the Court granted, and Defendants appealed.

In response, Defendants refute Plaintiffs’ assertion that the appeal stayed proceedings or made it impractical to bring the case to trial. Defendants cite URS Corp. v. Atkinson/Walsh Joint Venture (2017) 15 Cal.App.5th 872, 887, where the Court of Appeal held “that an order disqualifying an attorney is automatically stayed by an appeal.” The court also clarified that “ongoing litigation directed toward the resolution of the parties’ respective pleadings is not automatically stayed by this appeal.” ((Id. at p. 888.) Defendants assert that the legal effect of that case on the instant action is that Feldman, Defendants’ disqualified counsel, “remained defense counsel throughout the appeal under the automatic stay, preserving the status quo. The merits proceedings were not stayed. Plaintiffs could have conducted discovery, prepared trial documents, moved to specially set the case, and alerted the Court to the five-year deadline. Nothing in the law stopped them.” (Reply, 3:15-18.) Moreover, “an appeal from an order regarding disqualification of counsel is ‘unquestionably collateral to the merits of the case,’” so an “appeal does not automatically stay the trial proceedings relating to the merits.” ((Reed v. Superior Court (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 448, 453.)

The Court finds the case law instructive. Although an appeal of a motion to disqualify counsel stays proceedings, it only stays proceedings on that specific order. This has the effect of preserving the existing state of affairs, so that the potentially disqualified counsel and appealing party may continue to litigate the case without needing to retain replacement counsel. Reed and URS Corp. directly refute Plaintiffs’ assertion that the action underwent a stay of thirteen to fourteen months pending the outcome of the appeal. Additionally, Plaintiffs contend that the pending appeal made bringing the case to trial impossible, impracticable, or futile, but Plaintiffs fail to demonstrate such an argument to the Court. Accordingly, the Court finds that the time spent waiting for the appeal of the motion to disqualify counsel does not extend the five-year deadline.

C.     Discretionary Dismissal

Plaintiffs’ opposing papers assert that “[t]he adjusted five-year deadline does not expire until 2027,” so “[t]he mandatory ground fails.” (Opp., 7:18-19.) Plaintiffs thereafter request that the Court deny discretionary dismissal because all relevant circumstances favor trial and present arguments about their diligence in prosecuting the case. However, Plaintiffs base their assertions on only two scenarios that could have stayed the proceedings in this case, which, as stated above, the Court and case law refute that proceedings were stayed for thirteen to fourteen months during the pending appeal. Accordingly, the Court finds that any arguments supporting or refuting discretionary dismissal are inapposite for deciding whether the instant action should be dismissed.

Under the strict prescription of Code of Civil Procedure section 583.310, this case must be dismissed because the five-year deadline has passed. Plaintiffs fail to make a showing that events transpired that made bringing the action to trial “impossible, impracticable, or futile.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 583.340, subd. (c).) Although bankruptcy proceedings tolled the trial deadline to May 18, 2026, this date has passed, and trial is set for July 15, 2026. Accordingly, this action is dismissed.

 

Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion to dismiss is GRANTED. Defendants are ordered to file a proposed judgment within ten days of this Order.

Defendants are ordered to give notice of this Order.

IN RE:

2514 EXPOSITION PLACE, LOS  ANGELES, CA 90018

Case No.:

26STCP01702

Hearing Date:

July 14, 2026

Hearing Time:

10:00 a.m.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: 

 

PETITIONER TOTAL LENDER SOLUTIONS, INC.’S MOTION FOR ORDER DEPOSITING SURPLUS PROCEEDS FROM THE SALE AND DISCHARGING PETITIONER; SETTING HEARING ON DISTRIBUTION OF SURPLUS PROCEEDS; DIRECTING THE CLERK TO DISCHARGE THE PEITITONER UPON DEPOSIT OF THE SURPLUS PROCEEDS

           

            Background

On May 1, 2026, Petitioner Total Lender Solutions, Inc. (“Petitioner”) filed the instant petition and declaration regarding unresolved claims and deposit of undistributed surplus proceeds of trustee’s sale with respect to real property located at 2514 Exposition Place, Los Angeles, California 90018. (Pet., ¶ 2.)

On May 11, 2026, Petitioner filed the instant motion requesting an order depositing surplus proceeds from the sale and discharging Petitioner, setting a hearing on distribution of surplus proceeds, and directing the clerk to discharge Petitioner upon deposit of the surplus proceeds. Petitioner makes this motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2924j.

On May 21, 2026, Non-Party St. John of God Retirement and Care Center’s (“SJOG”) filed a claim to surplus proceeds of trustee’s sale. SJOG seeks that the Court “[o]rder the release of all of or a portion of the available surplus funds resulting from the foreclosure sale of the Property to SJOG pursuant to California Civil Code §2924k(a); and such further relief as this Court deems just and proper.” (Not. of Claim, i:23-25.)

Request for Judicial Notice

            The Court grants Petitioner’s request for judicial notice of Exhibit 1.

Legal Standard

“[W]ithin thirty days of the of the execution of the trustee’s deed resulting from a sale in which there are proceeds remaining after payment of the amounts required by paragraphs (1) and (2) of subdivision (a) of Section 2924k,” the trustee must send written notice to individuals who had a recorded interest in the real property prior to the trustee’s sale. (Civ. Code, § 2924j, subd. (a).) This allows those individuals to “contact the trustee at the address provided in the notice to pursue any potential claim” to the surplus funds. (Civ. Code, § 2924j, subd. (a)(3).) Claimants have 30 days to make a claim. (Civ. Code, §¿2924j, subd. (a)(4).) Once claims are received, the trustee must “exercise due diligence to determine the priority of the written claims.” (Civ. Code, § 2924j, subd. (b).) If “there is no dispute as to the priority of the written claims submitted to the trustee, proceeds shall be paid within 30 days after the conclusion of the notice period. If the trustee has failed to determine the priority of written claims within 90 days following the 30-day notice period, then within 10 days thereafter the trustee shall deposit the funds with the clerk of the court pursuant to subdivision (c).” (Ibid.)

Civil Code section 2924j, subdivision (c) provides that “[i]f, after due diligence, the trustee is unable to determine the priority of the written claims received by the trustee to the trustee’s sale surplus of multiple persons or if the trustee determines there is a conflict between potential claimants, the trustee may file a declaration of the unresolved claims and deposit with the clerk of the superior court of the county in which the sale occurred, that portion of the sales proceeds that cannot be distributed, less any fees charged by the clerk pursuant to this subdivision. The declaration shall specify the date of the trustee’s sale, a description of the property, the names and addresses of all persons sent notice pursuant to subdivision (a), a statement that the trustee exercised due diligence pursuant to subdivision (b), that the trustee provided written notice as required by subdivisions (a) and (d), and the amount of the sales proceeds deposited by the trustee with the court. Further, the trustee shall submit a copy of the trustee’s sales guarantee and any information relevant to the identity, location, and priority of the potential claimants with the court and shall file proof of service of the notice required by subdivision (d) on all persons described in subdivision (a).”

Thereafter, “[t]he clerk shall deposit the amount with the county treasurer or, if a bank account has been established for moneys held in trust under paragraph (2) of subdivision (a) of Section 77009 of the Government Code, in that account, subject to order of the court upon the application of any interested party. The clerk may charge a reasonable fee for the performance of activities pursuant to this subdivision equal to the fee for filing an interpleader action pursuant to Chapter 5.8 (commencing with Section 70600) of Title 8 of the Government Code. Upon deposit of that portion of the sale proceeds that cannot be distributed by due diligence, the trustee shall be discharged of further responsibility for the disbursement of sale proceeds. A deposit with the clerk of the court pursuant to this subdivision may be either for the total proceeds of the trustee's sale, less any fees charged by the clerk, if a conflict or conflicts exist with respect to the total proceeds, or that portion that cannot be distributed after due diligence, less any fees charged by the clerk.” (Civ. Code, § 2924j, subd. (c).) “Within 90 days after deposit with the clerk, the court shall consider all claims filed at least 15 days before the date on which the hearing is scheduled by the court, the clerk shall serve written notice of the hearing by first-class mail on all claimants identified in the trustee's declaration at the addresses specified therein.” (Civ. Code, § 2924j, subd. (d).)

            Discussion

Petitioner makes an application to deposit surplus funds, pursuant to Civil Code section 2924j, subdivision (c), because the sale of the property “resulted in the receipt of funds that exceeded the amount of the funds due and owing under the Deed of Trust as foreclosed upon.” (Notice of Mot., 2:1-3.) Petitioner explains that it “is the Trustee under a Deed of Trust executed by Wesley L. Todd and Doris J. Todd, Husband and Wife, as joint tenants,” but “Petitioner is informed and believes that the Former Trustors are deceased.” (Wilkinson Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.)

Petitioner explains that “[o]n November 4, 2025, pursuant to Civil Code 2924j(a), Petitioner sent written notice to all persons with a recorded interest in the Property as of the date immediately prior to the trustee’s sale who would be entitled to notice.” (Mot., 2:27-3:4.) Petitioner received one claim from Gary A. Todd who “is the named Successor Trustee of the Wesley Todd and Doris J. Todd family Trust per the certification of Trust executed on August 23, 2016.” (Pet., ¶ 12, Attach. 12.) Petitioner states that “[p]ursuant to a title search there are liens superior to Claimant. However, the Claimant is disputing all liens senior to his claim.” (Ibid.) Petitioner explains that “[t]he potential claimant in order of entitlement is A1 Solar Power, Inc. (‘A1 Solar’), pursuant to a Deed of Trust recorded on January 27, 2015, Instrument No. 20150094853 of official records.” (Mot., 3:17-19.) “The second potential claimant in order of entitlement is [SJOG]…, pursuant to an Abstract of Judgment recorded on February 3, 2023, Instrument No. 20230072799 and on November 12, 2024 Instrument No. 20240778810 of official records.” (Mot., 3:24-27.)

The Court finds that Petitioner complied with the relevant procedural requirements under Civil Code section 2924j, subdivision (c). Petitioner’s declaration states that the sale occurred on October 7, 2025, and Petitioner attaches a description of the property to the petition. (Pet., ¶¶ 2, 4, Attach. 2.) Petitioner also submitted a copy of the Trustee’s Sale Guarantee (“TSG”) with information relevant to identifying the property. (Pet., ¶ 5, Attach. 5.) Petitioner provided notice of the surplus proceeds to all possible interested persons pursuant to subdivision (a), included their names and addresses, and provided proof of service. (Pet., ¶¶ 8, 13, Attach. 8, 13.)

Further, Petitioner provided the amount of the sales proceeds to be deposited with the Court. The petition shows the property sold for $601,000. (Pet., ¶ 16a.) The available surplus proceeds, after paying 331,564.89 in debt to the foreclosing creditor, as required by Civil Code section 2924k (a)(1)-(2), is $269,435.11. (Pet., ¶¶ 7, 16b-c.) Petitioner seeks $7,600.50 in attorney fees and $495 in costs due to the filing fee. (Pet., ¶¶ 16e, g.) Accordingly, Petitioner seeks to deposit $261,339.61. (Pet., ¶ 16h.) The Court finds the information provided sufficient to grant Petitioner’s motion and sets a hearing on the disbursement of funds, as noted below.

SJOG asserts that it “is entitled to all or a portion of the surplus proceeds resulting from the trustee’s sale of the Property since it has priority over other judgment creditors, as supported by the TSG attached to the Petition.” (Claim, 2:17-19.) The Court notes that SJOG’s claim to the surplus funds will be heard at the hearing on distribution of the surplus funds.

Civil Code section 2924k, subdivisions (a)(1) and (b) provide that Petitioner is entitled to priority of the proceeds from the sale for attorney fees, costs, and expenses incurred in exercising the power of sale and of sale, as well as “expenses incurred for such items as mailing and a reasonable fee for services rendered in connection with the distribution of the proceeds from a trustee’s sale.” Petitioner’s counsel files a declaration seeking attorney fees and costs in the amount of $7,600.50, plus the filing fee of $495, for a total of $8,098.50. (Wilkinson Decl. ¶¶ 11-12; Ex. 3.) The Court finds attorney fees and costs are reasonable. However, the Court notes that Exhibit 3 lists the total amount due in attorney fees and expenses as $7,418.00, yet Petitioner’s counsel does not explain this discrepancy. Accordingly, the Court grants Petitioner costs in the amount of $7,913 ($7,418 + $495), unless Petitioner explains to the Court at the hearing on the instant motion that Petitioner actually incurred expenses amounting to $7,600.50.

Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, Petitioner’s motion is GRANTED. The Court directs that the surplus proceeds from the sale be deposited with the Court. Pursuant to Civil Code section 2924j, subdivision (c), the Court orders that the clerk deposit the amount with the county treasurer or, if a bank account has been established for moneys held in trust under paragraph (2) of subdivision (a) of Section 77009 of the Government Code, in that account.

The Court sets a hearing on the distribution of the surplus funds for within 90 days from the clerk’s deposit, on ______________, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. in Dept. 508. Pursuant to Civil Code section 2924j, subdivision (d), the clerk will serve written notice of the hearing by first-class mail on all claimants identified, and the Court will consider all claims filed at least 15 days before the hearing date.

Given that Petitioner demonstrated due diligence and complied with the relevant procedural requirements, the Court discharges Petitioner “of further responsibility for the disbursement of sale proceeds.” (Civ. Code, § 2924j, subd. (c).) The Court awards Petitioner attorney fees and costs in the amount of $7,913.00.
