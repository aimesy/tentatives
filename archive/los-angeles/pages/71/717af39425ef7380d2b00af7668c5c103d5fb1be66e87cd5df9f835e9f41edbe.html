MOTION TO ENFORCE SETTLEMENT

(CCP § 664.6)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court GRANTS Lawrence Of La Brea’s motion to enforce settlement and enters judgment against Michael Gallion pursuant to the stipulation in the reduced amount of $19,789.70, reflecting $16,120.28 in principal, $910.00 in costs, $1,885.85 in interest, and $873.57 in attorney fees.

ANALYSIS:

 

Lawrence of La Brea sued Michael Gallion for a common counts cause of action in July 2024.  In March 2025, the parties stipulated that Gallion would pay $17,000.00 in monthly payments of $1,700.00.  Gallion made only one payment.  Lawrence now seeks to enter judgment against Gallion pursuant to the stipulation. 

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, subdivision (a), allows a court to enter judgment pursuant to the terms of a written, signed settlement.  To enforce a written settlement agreement under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, the following three elements must be met:  (1) the parties must have come to a meeting of the minds on all material points; (2) there must be a writing that contains the material terms of the agreement; and (3) the writing must be signed by the parties.¿ (Weddington Productions, Inc. v. Flick (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 793, 797–798.)¿¿¿ 

 

            The stipulation between Lawrence and Gallion is valid and enforceable under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6.  In the event of breach, the stipulation provides that Lawrence may then obtain a judgment for the entire balance owed by Gallion for the principal balance of $17,820.28, plus court costs, interest, and attorney fees, minus any payments made by Gallion.

 

            Lawrence requests a judgment of $20,484.70 against Gallion, comprising $16,120.28 in principal, which reflects the original $17,820.28 principal less $1,700.00 for payments received from Gallion, $1,605.00 in costs, $1,885.85 in interest, and $873.57 in attorney fees.

 

            The Court finds the principal, attorney fees, and interest requests supported.  As for costs, Lawrence requests $695.00 in publication costs, but the Court rejected Lawrence’s application for publication.  Thus, the Court awards Lawrence costs in the reduced amount of $910.00.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Lawrence’s motion to enforce settlement and enters judgment against Gallion pursuant to the stipulation in the reduced amount of $19,789.70, reflecting $16,120.28 in principal, $910.00 in costs, $1,885.85 in interest, and $873.57 in attorney fees.

 

            Lawrence is to give notice.

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION TO VACATE ORDER OF DISMISSAL

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Richard D. Richardson

MOTION TO VACATE DISMISSAL

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(b))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court GRANTS Richardson’s motion to vacate the court-ordered dismissal for failure to appear at trial.

 

The June 26, 2026 dismissal is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.

 

            Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (c)(1)(A), the Court imposes a penalty of $500 on Richardson’s counsel, payable to the Court within 30 days of this order.

 

            Richardson is to give notice.

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          None filed as of July 22, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

REPLY:                     None filed as of July 22, 2026                        [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Richard D. Richardson sued Everick Frances Lewis and Charles Lewis.  Richardson served Charles, but failed to serve Everick for around a year and a half.  Consistent with this lack of diligence, Richardson’s counsel failed to appear for the June 26, 2026 non-jury trial.  The Court ordered Richardson’s complaint dismissed without prejudice pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 581, subdivision (b)(3).  Only then did Richardson file proof of service on Everick—along with a motion seeking relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b)’s mandatory attorney-fault provision. 

 

            A motion pursuant to the mandatory relief provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), must be (1) timely, (2) in proper form, and (3) accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)  A court must grant relief if the party satisfies those requirements, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not actually caused by the attorney’s fault.  (Ibid.)

 

Richardson’s motion, filed on June 29, 2026, satisfies each of those requirements.  His counsel declares that the failure to appear was not Richardson’s fault but the result of counsel’s “sole mistake and clerical neglect.”  Richardson’s counsel further attests that his “firm’s recent and comprehensive transition to a digitized, cloud-based data management system caused . . . the deadlines for service on [Everick] and the hearing date for the trial [to be] inadvertently omitted from the digital tracking system.”  The Court observes that this is not the first time that this law firm has advanced this excuse for its failure to act in a timely fashion. 

 

And while Richardson’s motion is not accompanied by papers curing the lapse that led to the dismissal, Richardson represents to the Court that he “is prepared to proceed with the diligent prosecution of this case, including the immediate service of the Complaint” on Everick and Charles—which has already been done.  The Court accepts that representation.  (See Carmel, Ltd. V. Tavoussi (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 393, 401–402; Austin v. Los Angeles Unified School Dist. (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 918, 933.)

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Richardson’s motion.  The June 26, 2026 dismissal is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.

 

            Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (c)(1)(A), the Court imposes a penalty of $500 on Richardson’s counsel, payable to the Court within 30 days of this order.

 

            A trial setting conference in this matter is set for Thursday, October 1, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse.  The Court also ORDERS Richardson to SHOW CAUSE, on Thursday, October 1, 2026 at 9:30 a.m. in Department 25 of the Spring Street Courthouse why no default has been entered against either Charles or Everick Lewis, and further ORDERS Richardson to SHOW CAUSE, at the same time and place, why Richardson’s counsel should not be sanctioned up to $250.00 for failure to timely enter those defaults.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rules 3.110(g), 3.110(i).)  If either defendant files a responsive pleading, it will be sufficient to discharge the two orders to show cause as to that defendant only.

 

            Richardson is to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Robert R. Elliot lives in Twin Oaks Mobile Home Park.  After repeated violations of Twin Oaks’s park rules, Twin Oaks filed this petition, pursuant to Civil Code section 798.88, to enjoin Elliot permanently from continuing the violations.  Twin Oaks requests an order enjoining Elliot from violating its rules 2, 6(A), 6(B), 6(D), and 9, and requiring Elliot to remove all unapproved occupants from the premises who have stayed beyond the guest grace period; to stop allowing individuals to reside on the premises for a period exceeding 20 consecutive days or 30 days in a calendar year; to stop allowing unapproved guests to reside on the premises; to stop allowing individuals to reside on the premises who are not age-approved; to remove all improperly stored items from the premises and maintain the premises in a clean, safe, and orderly condition at all times; to immediately remove all animal feces from the premises and maintain the premises in a sanitary condition free from offensive odors and health hazards; and to abide by all park rules and regulations and the terms of the rental agreement.

 

The Mobilehome Residency Law gives park management an injunctive remedy for violations of “reasonable” park rules and regulations.  (Civ. Code, § 798.88.)  At the time of the hearing, the court shall issue an injunction not to exceed three years if it finds by clear and convincing evidence the existence of a continuing or recurring violation of a “reasonable rule or regulation.”  (Civ. Code, § 798.88, subd. (d).)

 

            Twin Oaks’s petition is supported by a copy of the rental agreement from August 2018; the park rules in effect; four written notices and proofs of service from May and August of 2025; and photographs taken in February 2026 depicting the continued violations of the rules.  Twin Oaks contends the violations are ongoing and continue to exist.  Based on the foregoing, the Court finds the petition is supported by clear and convincing evidence of Elliot’s violations of Twin Oaks’s reasonable park rules and regulations.  (Civ. Code, § 798.88, subd. (d).)  Elliot has not opposed the petition showing why an injunction should not be issued against him.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Twin Oaks’s petition.

 

Twin Oaks is to give notice.
