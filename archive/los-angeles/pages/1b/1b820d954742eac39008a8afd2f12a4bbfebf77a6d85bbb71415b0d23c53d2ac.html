DEPARTMENT 836 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STLC08924    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: 836

APPLICATION FOR WRIT OF POSSESSION

 

Date:               06/23/26 (1:30 PM) 

Case:               Evergreen Bank Group v.  Felix Robles (25STLC08924) 

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The UNOPPOSED Application for Writ of Possession by plaintiff Evergreen Bank Group as to defendant Felix Robles is GRANTED. The Court finds that plaintiff has established the probable validity of its claim to possession of the subject property, namely, a 2024 CAN-AM ORB Maverick RX RS (VIN 3JB8 XAT2 3RE0 0234 8).

 

Plaintiff Evergreen Bank Group, doing business as Freedom Road Financial, is the assignee of a promissory note, disclosure and security agreement that defendant had entered into for the financed purchase of the subject property, and plaintiff has perfected a lien on such property. (Urso Decl. ¶¶ 1, 5-6; Ex. 1 at 1 & Ex. 2.) Under the agreement, upon a default of any provision, plaintiff has the right to take the subject property from defendant. (Urso Decl. ¶ 8 & Ex. 1 at ¶ 12.) Defendant has been in default since January 23, 2025. (Urso Decl. ¶ 7 & Ex. 3.) Plaintiff has made demand for a return of the subject property, but defendant has not complied. (Urso Decl. ¶ 9.)

 

Under CCP § 512.060, a writ of possession cannot issue unless the Court finds “[t]he plaintiff has established the probable validity of the plaintiff’s claim to possession of the property.”  “A claim has ‘probable validity’ where it is more likely than not that the plaintiff will obtain a judgment against the defendant on that claim.” (CCP § 511.090.)

 

Based on the evidence presented, the Court finds that it is more likely than not that plaintiff would obtain a judgment in its favor for possession of the subject property. Plaintiff provides sufficient documentary evidence that it has a security interest in the vehicle at issue and that defendant is in default of the agreement. (Urso Decl. ¶¶ 6-9 & Exs. 2-3.) Plaintiff has demanded possession of the vehicle, but defendant has improperly retained it. (Urso Decl. ¶ 9.)

 

The Court also finds probable cause to believe that the vehicle may be found at defendant’s residence: 14218 Belgate Street, Baldwin Park, CA 91706. (Urso Decl. ¶ 14.)

 

The Court finds that a turnover order under CCP § 512.070 is warranted here. The Court finds that an undertaking is required because the amount owed ($23,333.93) is less than the market value of the vehicle ($24,025). (CCP § 515.010; Urso Decl. ¶¶ 7, 11 & Exs. 3-4.) While the attached declaration claims that the outstanding balance is $26,585.83 (Urso Decl. ¶ 12), this is not corroborated by the attached loan history. (See Ex. 3.) Therefore, per CCP § 515.010, an undertaking of $1,382.14 is warranted.

 

For the foregoing reasons, the Application for Writ of Possession as to defendant Felix Robles is GRANTED, subject to the plaintiff’s posting of an undertaking in the amount of $1,382.14.The Court will sign the Proposed Order electronically received 2/20/26 with changes consistent with the ruling herein.


Case Number: 26STCV02017    Hearing Date: June 23, 2026    Dept: 836

APPLICATION FOR RIGHT TO ATTACH ORDER

                                   

Date:                           6/23/26 (1:30 PM)                                         

Case:                           Galco International Inc. v. Soheil Darvish, et al. (26STCV02017)

                                                           

 TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Galco International Inc.’s UNOPPPOSED application for right to attach order with respect to defendant Soheil Darvish is GRANTED.

 

Pursuant to CCP §484.090, the Court finds:

 

1.      the claim is one upon which attachment may be issued;

2.      plaintiff has established the probable validity of the claim;

3.      attachment is not sought for any purpose other than recovery on the claim; and

4.      the amount to be attached is greater than zero.

 

On March 28, 2023, Plaintiff Galco International Inc. made a commercial loan of $2,011,072 to 202 Main LLC; the agreement was memorialized in a Promissory Note, dated March 28, 2023. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 3 & Ex. A.) In connection with the loan, defendant Soheil Darvish executed a Personal Guarantee, which was secured by a pledge of his 50% membership interest in Fig Court LLC, as well as all distributions and proceeds resulting from that membership interest. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 3 & Exs. B-C.)  On March 2, 2024, plaintiff perfected a lien against defendant’s 50% membership interest in Fig Court LLC filing a UCC-1. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 3 & Ex. D.)  The maturity date of the Promissory Note was May 22, 2023, but no payment was made. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 3.)

 

Separately, defendant and his wife, Niloofar Darvish, were in the midst of divorce proceedings; defendant’s wife claimed a one-half interest in defendant’s 50% membership interest in Fig Court LLC. (Kerekes Decl. ¶4.) Niloofar thus recorded a lis pendens against Fig Court LLC’s real property 4201 N. Figueroa Street, Los Angeles (“Fig Court Property”). (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 4 & Ex. E.) When the Fig Court Property was going to be sold, defendant, Niloofar, and plaintiff entered into a written agreement pursuant to which Niloofar would release the lis pendens, and, in exchange, the proceeds of defendant’s share would be held in a trust account controlled by Niloofar’s divorce attorney. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 5 & Ex. F.) On October 8, 2025, the Fig Court Property was sold; defendant’s share of the proceeds was $254,961.85, in accordance with his Fig Court LLC membership interest. (Kerekes Decl. ¶¶ 5-6.) Although plaintiff attempted to assert a third-party creditor claim in the divorce proceedings, the family law court issued a tentative statement of decision that split equally the proceeds of the Fig Court Property sale between defendant and his wife. (Kerekes Decl. ¶¶ 9-12 & Exs. J-M.) Thus, pursuant to the family law court decision concerning the equal split between defendant and Niloofar of defendant’s share of the of the Fig Court Property proceeds, defendant’s remaining share of the proceeds is now $127,480.92. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 12.)

 

Plaintiff has learned that defendant’s cousins, through the Raffi Family Trust, brought an action to collect $1,200,000 on a promissory note, which resulted in a stipulated judgment in the Raffi Family Trust’s favor, thereby leading to the Raffi Family Trust filing a Notice of Judgment Lien in the divorce proceedings. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 13 & Ex. O [Settlement Agreement], Q [Raffi Family Trust complaint], R [Stipulated Judgment].) On April 21, 2026, plaintiff learned that the Raffi Family Trust was seeking to release plaintiff’s share of the Fig Court Property proceeds held in the trust account, which plaintiff opposed. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 16 & Exs. T-U.) On April 29, 2026, plaintiff filed Doe Amendments to include the trustees of the Raffi Family Trust in the instant action. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 14 & Ex. Y.)

 

Plaintiff asserts that it has a priority lien on defendant’s one-half of the net proceeds because its lien has existed since March 2, 2024, whereas the Raffi Family Trust did not file a judgment lien until April 11, 2026. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 17 & Exs. D, S.)

 

Plaintiff has shown that defendant has been in breach of his Personal Guarantee since May 22, 2023, when the Promissory Note matured and no payment was or has ever been made. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 3 & Exs. A-D.) The Personal Guarantee was secured by the Pledge Agreement concerning defendant’s 50% membership interest in Fig Court, LLC, and the lien was perfected on March 2, 2024. (Kerekes Decl. ¶ 3 & C-D.) Plaintiff’s lien was perfected before the Raffi Family Trust filed its Notice of Judgment Lien against defendant on April 11, 2026.  (See Kerekes Decl., Exs. D, S.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the application is GRANTED.  The Court will sign the Proposed Order, electronically received 5/14/26, entitling plaintiff to attach $127,480.92 held in the client trust account of Jonathon Pakravan.  Before any writ will issue, plaintiff shall post an undertaking in the amount of $10,000.
