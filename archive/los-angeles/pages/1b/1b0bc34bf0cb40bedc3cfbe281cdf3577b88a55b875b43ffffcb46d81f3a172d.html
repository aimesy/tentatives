Case Number: 23CHCV03461    Hearing Date: July 20, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 7/20/26                                                             TRIAL DATE: 6/28/27

Case #23CHCV03461

 

DEMURRER & MOTION TO STRIKE TO THE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Demurrer & Motion to Strike filed on 2/20/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants Restore Tactics, Inc. and James Duran

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Ruben Nunez

NOTICE: ok

 

Demurrer is to the 3rd as to RTI and the entire First Amended Complaint as to Duran:

            1.  Breach of Contract

            2.  Negligence

            3.  Fraud

            4.  Disgorgement Pursuant to Business & Professions Code 7031

            5.  Complaint Against Contractor’s Bond

 

RELIEF REQUESTED IN MOTION TO STRIKE: An order striking portions of the First Amended Complaint relating to exemplary damages, punitive damages, attorney fees and expert fees.

 

RULING: The demurrer is moot, in part, and overruled, in part.  The motion to strike is moot, in part, and otherwise granted without leave to amend. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of contracts for work to be performed on Plaintiff Ruben Nunez’s (Plaintiff) home by Defendant Restore Tactics, Inc. (RTI).  Plaintiff contends that the work was not properly performed.  Therefore, on 11/13/23, Plaintiff filed this action against RTI, James Sotelo Duran (Duran), Okalani Manu Taufahema and Henry Renteria for: (1) Breach of Contract, (2) Negligence, (3) Fraud and (4) Disgorgement Pursuant to Business & Professions Code 7031.  The complaint also contained a 5th cause of action for Complaint Against Contractor’s Bond  against American Contractors Indemnity Company.

 

On 7/1/24, after the Court’s 5/31/24 ruling on RTI’s demurrer and motion to strike as to the original complaint, Plaintiff filed the subject First Amended Complaint which alleges the same five causes of action as the original complaint.  (See 5/31/24 Minute Order). 

 

On 2/11/25, Plaintiff filed a request for dismissal of the 3rd cause of action for fraud which was entered the same day.  (See 2/11/25 Request for Dismissal).  

 

On 4/3/25, RTI filed an answer to Plaintiff’s Complaint.

 

After meet and confer efforts failed to resolve the issues RTI and Duran (collectively, Defendants) had with the First Amended Complaint, Defendants filed and served the instant demurrer and motion to strike as to the First Amended Complaint.  RTI demurs to the 3rd cause of action for fraud and Duran demurs to the entire complaint as to him.  Plaintiff has filed an opposition to the demurrer.  Defendants have filed replies.   

 

ANALYSIS

 

3rd cause of action - Fraud

 

The demurrer by both Defendants to the 3rd cause of action for fraud is moot. 

 

After the filing of the First Amended Complaint, the parties stipulated that Plaintiff would dismiss, without prejudice, his 3rd cause of action for fraud “in its entirety as to Ruben Nunez v. Restore Tactics, Inc., et al. filed in the Superior Court of California, County of Los Angeles, assigned as case no. 23CHCV03461.” (emphasis) (See Demurrer - Coulston Decl., Ex.A p.2:21-24).  Thereafter, on 2/11/25, Plaintiff dismissed the 3rd cause of action without prejudice.  (See 2/11/25 Request for Dismissal).  Then, on 4/3/25, RTI filed an answer to Plaintiff’s complaint. 

 

RTI’s claim that it only filed an answer to Plaintiff’s original complaint is confusing.  Once Plaintiff filed his First Amended Complaint on 7/1/24, it superseded Plaintiff’s original complaint.  See Foreman & Clark Corp. (1971) 3 C3d 875, 884; Lee (1994) 27 CA4th 197, 215.  Therefore, it is not clear why RTI would file an answer on 4/3/25 to a pleading that was superseded approximately 9 months earlier.  Moreover, Defendants’ 1/29/26 meet and confer letter seems to acknowledge that the only remaining issue after the dismissal of the 3rd cause of action would be in relation to the alter ego allegations as against Duran.  (See Coulston Decl., Ex.B) 

 

Based on the foregoing sequence of events, the only logical conclusion is that the issues RTI had with the First Amended Complaint as to the 3rd cause of action were resolved.  Further, the dismissal of the 3rd cause of action in its entirety would also resolve Duran’s demurrer as to that cause of action.   

 

Alter Ego Liability Against Duran

 

Contrary to Plaintiff’s assertion in the opposition to the demurrer, the Court did not “already reject[] substantially the same argument in ruling on Defendants’ prior demurrer.”  (See Opposition to Demurrer, p.7:5-6).  Rather, the Court previously determined:

 

The individual defendants are not moving parties on this demurrer.  (See Demurrer, p.1:11-12, p.1:1-2).  Therefore, the argument that the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th causes of action fail to allege sufficient facts to support alter ego liability against the individual defendants is not properly before the Court on this demurrer which is brought solely by RTI.

 

(See 5/31/24 Minute Order, p.2)

As such, the Court did not rule on the merits of arguments made with regard to the alter ego allegations in the prior demurrer.  Now that Duran is a moving party on the instant demurrer, the Court addresses the merits of the argument and finds that the First Amended Complaint includes sufficient allegations to put Duran on notice of the basis for the claims being alleged against him.    

 

To plead alter ego liability, a plaintiff must allege facts showing (1) a unity of interest and ownership between the corporation and the individual, and (2) that treating the corporation as a separate entity would sanction a fraud or promote injustice.  Mesler (1985) 39 C3d 290, 300.   Alter ego liability is an equitable doctrine, and whether it applies depends on the particular facts of each case.  Associated Vendors, Inc. (1962) 210 CA2d 825, 837.  Therefore, alter ego determinations are generally factual in nature and rarely appropriate for resolution at the pleading stage.  Las Palmas Associates (1991) 235 CA3d 1220, 1249; First Western Bank & Trust Co. (1968) 267 CA2d 910, 915.

 

Here, Plaintiff has alleged that RTI was inadequately capitalized, lacked sufficient assets and insurance to satisfy foreseeable liabilities, operated as a mere shell corporation, and was dominated and controlled by Duran and the other individual defendants.  (FAC ¶10). Plaintiff further alleges that Duran and the other individual defendants diverted corporate assets, manipulated corporate liabilities, used Restore Tactics as a conduit for his personal business activities, and sought to insulate himself from liability through misuse of the corporate form.  Id.  Finally, Plaintiff alleges that recognizing the corporation as a separate entity would promote an inequitable result by allowing Duran and the other individual defendants to escape liability for the wrongful conduct alleged in the First Amended Complaint.  Id.   Plaintiff does not need to prove his allegations at the pleading stage. 

 

MOTION TO STRIKE

 

The motion to strike seeks to strike allegations regarding and the prayers for exemplary and punitive damages, attorney fees and expert fees.  Plaintiff has not filed an opposition to the motion to strike.    

 

The allegations regarding and the prayer for punitive and exemplary damages concern the 3rd cause of action for fraud.  To the extent that the allegations are not contained within 3rd cause of action which has since been dismissed, the motion is granted. 

 

With regard to the prayer for attorney fees and expert fees, the motion is also granted as Plaintiff has failed to set forth any bases for these prayers and has not indicated that he can do so. 

 

DISMISSAL OF UNSERVED DEFENDANTS

 

In the declarations of attorney Coulston filed with the replies, attorney Coulston states:

 

“The remaining unserved Defendants Okalani Manu Taufahema and Henry Renteria should be dismissed because they have not been served for nearly two and a half years since the original Complaint was filed.”

 

(Coulston Reply Decls. ¶8).

 

The argument is improperly raised for the first time in the replies on behalf of Defendants who are not moving parties.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The demurrer as to the 3rd cause of action for fraud by both RTI and Duran is moot due to the dismissal of the 3rd cause of action on 2/11/25. 

 

The demurrer of Duran to the entire First Amended Complaint as alleged against him is overruled.

 

To the extent the motion to strike was not rendered moot by the dismissal of the 3rd cause of action, it is granted without leave to amend. 

 

To the extent that RTI did not intend its answer filed on 4/3/25 to be to the First Amended Complaint, RTI may file and serve an answer which specifically references the First Amended Complaint within 30 days. 

 

Duran is ordered to file and serve an answer to the First Amended Complaint within 30 days.

 

The request to dismiss the First Amended Complaint as to unserved defendants improperly made in the reply declarations is denied.   

 

The Court notes that in violation of CRC 3.1110(f)(4), Defendants’ have failed to electronically bookmark the exhibits attached to the replies.  Counsel for the parties are warned that failure to comply with this rule in the future may result in matters being continued so that papers can be refiled in compliance with the rule, papers not being considered and/or the imposition of sanctions.   

 


Case Number: 24CHCV04347    Hearing Date: July 20, 2026    Dept: F47

Dept. F47

Date: 7/20/26                                                        TRIAL DATE: 3/22/27

Case #24CHCV04347

 

MOTION TO EXTEND THE TIME LIMIT FOR PLAINTIFF’S DEPOSITION

 

Motion filed on 3/17/26. 

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant MCP Sayre, LLC

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff Silvia Solorzano

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order extending the time limit for the deposition of Plaintiff Silvia Solorzano past the 7 hour limit, to add an additional 7 hours.

 

RULING: The motion is granted, in part.    

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of Plaintiff Silvia Solorzano’s (Plaintiff) tenancy in a residential building located at 13910 Sayre Street, Unit 14, in Sylmar, California (the Property) which is alleged to be owned, managed and/or operated by Defendant MCP Sayre, LLC (Defendant).  Plaintiff has lived at the Property from 6/1/19 to the present.  Plaintiff alleges that the Property suffers from defective water pipes, plumbing issues, rats, roaches, bedbugs, rodents, spiders, inoperable windows and doors caused by deterioration, holes “all over” the unit, faulty electrical fittings that sparked every time something is connected to it, deteriorating walls, mold in the living room, bedroom, hallway, closet, restroom walls, deteriorated bathroom walls and floors.  Plaintiff alleges that she became sick with rashes and breathing issues due to the alleged conditions in the Property.  Plaintiff also alleges damaged toilet and bathtub, holes around the AC Unit, damaged/clogged sinks, damaged and deteriorated carpets, a non-functional heater, damaged electrical appliances, deteriorated cabinets, broken tiles, general sanitary conditions of the unit, broken bedroom windows that did not close all the way, hot water shut offs with no notice which allegedly remained for over a week, broken garbage disposal, and closet doors that get stuck and do not slide.

 

As a result, on 11/25/24, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendant alleging causes of action for: (1) Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability; (2) Negligence; (3) Nuisance; (4) Breach of Quiet Enjoyment; (5) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress; (6) Unruh  Civil Rights Act (Civil Code 51) and (7) Anti-Harassment Statute (Civil Code 1940.2).  On 4/7/25, after Defendant’s demurrer was overruled and motion to strike was denied, Defendant answered the complaint. 

 

On 11/12/25, Defendant took the first session of Plaintiff’s deposition which lasted approximately 5 hours and 30 minutes.  On 3/9/26, the second session of Plaintiff’s deposition was taken for 1 hour and 30 minutes.  Plaintiff refused to extend the time for her deposition beyond the 7 hour statutory time limit.  Therefore, on 3/17/26, Defendant filed and served the instant motion seeking an order extending the time limit for the deposition of Plaintiff past the 7 hour limit, to add an additional 7 hours.  Plaintiff has opposed the motion and Defendant has filed a reply to the opposition. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

CCP 2025.290(a) provides:

 

“Except as provided in subdivision (b), or by any court order, including a case management order, a deposition examination of the witness by all counsel, other than the witness' counsel of record, shall be limited to seven hours of total testimony. The court shall allow additional time, beyond any limits imposed by this section, if needed to fairly examine the deponent or if the deponent, another person, or any other circumstance impedes or delays the examination.”

 

None of the exceptions set forth in subsection (b) of CCP 2025.290 apply to Plaintiff’s deposition.

 

Defendant has failed to establish that issues in this case and the fact that an interpreter was/is necessary warrant doubling the 7 hour statutory time limit for Plaintiff’s deposition.  However, the Court finds that the foregoing does warrant an additional 3.5 hours of time to fairly complete the examination of Plaintiff.     

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.  The time limit for the deposition of Plaintiff Silvia Solorzano is extended 3.5 hours past the 7 hour limit set forth in CCP 2025.290(a), not an additional 7 hours as requested in the motion.
