SANAZ SAHA v. KOUROSH NETTY, et al. [Case No. 24STCV23615]

1.    MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT OF THE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT, OR ALTERNATIVELY, SUMMARY ADJUDICATION OF THE 15TH AND 17TH CAUSES OF ACTION OF DEFENDANTS BRITTNEY GHADOUSHI, MASOUD NETTY, NILOOFAR NETTY, BIJOU NETTY, AND DOREEN NETTY

 

2.    MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO DISCOVERY INSTRUMENTS SERVED ON DEFENDANTS BRITTNEY GHADOUSHI, MASOUD NETTY, NILOOFAR NETTY, BIJOU NETTY, AND DOREEN NETTY

 

The Court addresses plaintiff’s motions to compel against the four defendants who are also the moving parties on the motion for summary judgment, namely defendants-Doreen Netty, Britteny Ghadoushi, Niloofar Netty, and Masoud Netty. These four defendants (collectively referred to as “defendants”) are represented by the Bremer, Whyte Law Firm. Plaintiff also filed a motion to compel against defendant Kourosh Netty, Kourosh is self-represented and, as far as the Court knows, did not file an opposition to plaintiff’s motion to compel against him.  

Plaintiff filed an opposition to defendants’ separate motions for summary judgment that is based on subdivision (h) of CCP 437c, to wit. claiming under subdivision (h) that “it appears from the affidavits submitted in opposition … that facts essential to justify opposition may exist but cannot, for the reason stated, be presented.” Plaintiff did not file any affidavits as required by subdivision (h), but she argues that her motions to compel the moving defendants to provide further discovery are sufficient to comply with the requirements of subdivision (h). See, Plt. Supp. Opp., filed 6/17/26. Plaintiff, in other words, has linked her ability to oppose the summary judgment motion to a claim under subdivision (h) that essential facts may exist and, therefore, in prevailing on the current discovery motions.

The Appendix to these tentative ruling provide a timeline to document the main pleadings that have been filed on defendants’ motion for summary judgment and on plaintiff’s motions to compel further discovery responses. The events on the summary judgment timeline are bolded to distinguish them from the discovery timeline.

The Court will hear argument on plaintiff’s motions to compel. The Court’s tentative view, however, is that the discovery responses (which are now completed) fail to provide evidence that could defeat the motions for summary judgment. In other words, the record does not support further delay because plaintiff does not show “that facts essential to justify opposition may exist.”

DEFENDANTS HAVING FILED SUPPLEMENTAL RESPONSES, PLAINTIFF’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES IS DENIED

The moving defendants (on the summary judgment motions) have provided supplemental responses to plaintiff’s discovery motions, e.g. to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, requests for admission and requests for documents. The moving defendants filed verified supplemental responses to satisfy plaintiff’s discovery motions on June 19, 2026. If the supplemental responses were inadequate, the Court would expect that plaintiff would have  filed a separate statement to itemize any deficiency. Plaintiff has not filed any objection to the sufficiency of the supplemental responses.

Trial is set for July 20. New discovery is closed. CCP 2024.020. The moving defendants seek summary adjudication as against the two causes of action pled against them, namely the 15th and 17th causes of action, for intentional infliction of emotion distress (IIED) and conspiracy.

The supplemental responses for the moving defendants (on the summary judgment motion), namely Doreen Netty, Britteny Ghadoushi, Niloofar Netty, and Masoud Netty, are provided in Exhibit D to the Lauren De Leon declaration filed on June 25, 2026.

The Court has examined the supplemental responses provided by Doreen Netty, one of the four moving defendants, on an individual basis. The Court’s assumption is that Doreen Netty’s supplemental responses are representative of the responses providing by the other parties moving for summary judgment. The Court, in the following paragraphs, paraphrases the requests as well as the responses for Doreen Netty. This exercise is intended to evaluate whether plaintiff’s discovery plan is well-conceived to discover admissible evidence on the two causes of action pled against the moving defendants and to determine whether the supplemental responses adequately address plaintiff’s discovery motions.

SUPPLEMENTAL RESPONSES ADEQUATELY RESPOND TO PLAINTIFF’S  DISCOVERY MOTIONS

The supplemental responses adequately provide information to the discovery requests that the Court previously ordered to be supplemented. The Court sustained defendants’ objections to requests for financial information including tax returns from the moving defendants.

The Court finds that moving defendants Doreen Netty, Britteny Ghadoushi, Niloofar Netty, and Masoud Netty have complied with their discovery obligations. The Court therefore DENIES plaintiff’s motions to compel further responses from defendants Doreen Netty, Britteny Ghadoushi, Niloofar Netty, and Masoud Netty.

No further discovery is authorized given that the scheduled trial is less than 30 days away.

SUMMARY JUDGMENT IS GRANTED BECAUSE PLAINTIFF HAS NOT RAISED A TRIABLE ISSUE OF FACT

The Court rejects plaintiff’s argument that CCP 437c, subdivision (h) precludes the Court from deciding defendants’ motion for summary judgment. The information revealed in defendants’ supplemental discovery responses supports a denial of plaintiff’s motions to compel. The Court further notes that plaintiff has not sought to take the deposition of any of the moving defendanrts, not even of Britteny Ghadoushi although she filed a declaration on February 13, 2026 supporting her motion for summary judgment.

Moving defendants’ motion carries their burden to shift to plaintiff the burden of presenting evidence to raise a triable issue to support her 15th and 17th causes of action. Plaintiff provides no evidence to support her claims for IIED and conspiracy. Thus, summary judgment should be GRANTED in favor the moving defendants.
