Case Number: 23STCV05839    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Blackburn v. 4600 Sunset Boulevard, Case no. 23STCV05839

Hearing date September 16, 2026

Defendant Black Shadow III’s Motion for Summary Judgment on the Complaint - UNOPPOSED

Cross-Defendants’ Motion for Summary Judgment on the Cross-Complaint - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Blackburn sues defendants 4600 Sunset Boulevard, Inc., Fuentes, Black Shadow III (“BS3”), Derian and Rhino’s Executive. Plaintiff alleges she was entering a club where BS3 and Wilson were contracted to provide security, was pepper-sprayed by Fuentes, causing her to enter the street, where she was struck by a vehicle. Derian and 4600 Sunset cross-complaint against BS3 and its principal Wilson for indemnity. BS3 moves for summary judgment on the complaint. BS3 and Wilson move for summary judgment on the cross-complaint. Both are unopposed.

A motion for summary judgement must be granted if no triable issue of material fact exists as to each cause of action. Code Civ. Proc. §437c(a), (c); Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843. To obtain summary judgment, moving party must prove each element of the cause of action. Harris v. Rudin, Richman & Appel (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 1332, as modified Mar. 5, 2002. A defendant can do so by showing plaintiff cannot establish one or more elements of the claim or there is a complete defense to that claim. Aguilar, supra, at 853. Once moving party meets this burden, the burden shifts to responding party to show a triable issue of material fact as to that cause of action or a defense thereto. Harris, supra, at 1337.

Defendant Black Shadow III’s Motion for Summary Judgment on the Complaint

Plaintiff brings a cause of action against BS3 for negligence. BS3 argues plaintiff cannot establish the essential element of duty, an essential element of any negligence-based claim. See Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.

BS3 did not employ, supervise, control or direct Fuentes. SSMF 1, 8-10. BS3 did not contract to provide security services at the premises. SSMF 1-6, 11-18. BS3 did not provide security services at the premises. SSMF 19-24. BS3 had no involvement in the subject incident. SSMF 31-40.

Plaintiff admitted in discovery that BS3 had no control over Fuentes. SSMF 10. 4600 Sunset Boulevard and Deria admitted in discovery that BS3 did not provide security services and had no employees or agents present during the incident. SSMF 19-38. This is sufficient to shift the burden of proof to plaintiff to show a triable issue of fact as to duty. As the motion is unopposed, plaintiff cannot meet her burden of proving a disputed issue of material fact. GRANTED.

Cross-Defendants’ Motion for Summary Judgment on the Cross-Complaint

Derian and 4600 Sunset Boulevard bring three causes of action against BS3 and Wilson for equitable indemnity, apportionment and declaratory relief. Moving cross-defendants argue cross-complainants cannot establish the necessary elements of duty or liability. A fundamental prerequisite to a claim for equitable indemnity, comparative indemnity, contribution, or declaratory relief is existence of an underlying legal duty and potential liability owed by the proposed indemnitor to the injured plaintiff. Western Steamship Lines, Inc. v. San Pedro Peninsula Hospital (1994) 8 Cal.4th 100, 114.

The court’s tentative ruling is to grant summary judgment in BS3’s favor on the sole cause of action alleged by plaintiff against BS3. Thus, there can be no finding of liability against BS3 as to plaintiff, precluding derivative indemnity or declaratory relief claims. Western Steamship Lines, Inc., supra. As Wilson’s alleged liability for indemnity arises from their role as BS3 manager or member, there can be no finding of liability against Wilson. See Corp. Code §17703.04.

Separately, cross-complainants admit in verified discovery responses that cross-defendants had no written or oral contract to provide security at the premises and did not have employees or agents present at the time of the incident. SSMF 1-7. Derian and 4600 Sunset Boulevard each produced RFA responses admitting no contracts between cross-complainants and cross-defendants for security services existed, no payment for services occurred and no services were provided. SSMF 11-28.

Cross-complainants admitted they possessed no evidence showing a duty or responsibilities assumed by cross-defendants. SSMF 25-30. This is sufficient to shift the burden to cross-complainants to show a triable issue of fact as to duty or as to liability. See Code Civ. Proc. §437c(p)(2). As the motion is unopposed, cross-complainants cannot meet their burden of proving a disputed issue of material fact. GRANTED.


Case Number: 26VECV02944    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Gomez v. Horiguchi, Case no. 26VECV02944

Hearing date September 16, 2026

Defendant Horiguchi’s Demurrer to Complaint

Plaintiff Gomez sues defendant Horiguchi for injuries arising from a motor vehicle accident, alleging defendant negligently failed to yield. Defendant demurs, arguing plaintiff previously filed and dismissed with prejudice an identical complaint on the same underlying facts, LASC case no. 26VECV02773, constituting res judicata. Plaintiff opposes.

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Defendant requests judicial notice of the following documents filed in LASC case no. 26VECV02773: (1) 5/12/26 complaint; (2) 5/27/26 request for dismissal with prejudice; and (3) 7/1/26 motion to set aside/vacate dismissal. GRANTED pursuant to Evid. Code §452(d).

“Collateral estoppel applies when (1) the party against whom the plea is raised was a party or was in privity with a party to the prior adjudication, (2) there was a final judgment on the merits in the prior action and (3) the issue necessarily decided in the prior adjudication is identical to the one that is sought to be relitigated.” Roos v. Red (2005) 140 Cal.App.4th 870, 879. It is well established that a retraxit, commonly done through a plaintiff's filing of a dismissal of his or her action with prejudice, is deemed to be a judgment on the merits against that plaintiff. Torrey Pines Bank v. Superior Court (1989) 216 Cal.App.3d 813, 820.

This matter was filed on 5/19/26, asserting claims arising from an automobile collision on 7/25/25. See Compl., para. 7. Case no. 26VECV02773 was filed on 5/12/26 by plaintiff against defendant, asserting identical claims arising out of the same accident. See RJN 1. The motion to set aside the dismissal in 26VECV02773 admits the actions are duplicative. RJN 3, 3:7-9. The motion to set aside admits the prior, identical action was dismissed with prejudice. Id. at 3:9-11.

Hon. Watkins, presiding over 26VECV02773 matter, granted plaintiff’s motion to vacate the dismissal with prejudice and re-entered the dismissal without prejudice 8/21/26. See 8/24/26 Min. Order. A dismissal without prejudice does not constitute res judicata. See Torrey Pines Bank, supra. There is no basis to sustain this demurrer on the basis of res judicata. Per the reply “[t]here is no dispute that the [c]omplaint, taken on its own, states a cause of action for negligence.” Reply, 3:8-9. OVERRULED.

Defendant demurs to the second cause of action for negligence per se. The opposition admits negligence per se is not a cause of action in California but rather an evidentiary standard. See Evid. Code §669; see also Opp., 7:27-8:2. Plaintiff requests the court grant leave to amend to incorporate negligence per se allegations into the first cause of action for negligence. Id. at 8:9-12. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.
