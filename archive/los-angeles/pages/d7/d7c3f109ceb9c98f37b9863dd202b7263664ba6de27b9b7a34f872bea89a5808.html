TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 September 17, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   24STCV19425

CASE NAME:                        Ming Lu v. Xin Min International, Inc.

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Ming Lu

OPPOSING PARTY:             Defendant Xin Min International, Inc.

PROCEEDING:                      MOTION TO QUASH OR LIMIT DEFENDANT’S DEPOSITION SUBPOENA FOR PRODUCTION OF BUSINESS RECORDS

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash Defendant’s Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records served on May 14, 2026, is GRANTED.                                   

 

Background

 

Plaintiff Ming Lu filed this employment action against defendant Xin Min International, Inc. on August 2, 2024. Plaintiff alleges he was employed by Xinmin Evening News, a news outlet owned by Defendant, from 2015 to 2023. In 2018, Defendant asked that Plaintiff move to the United States to report on news occurring here. Plaintiff contends he worked considerably more than 40 hours a week for Defendant for years for a monthly salary of $1,500.00, in violation of California wage-and-hour laws. Defendant denies it was Plaintiff’s legal employer under California law.

 

On May 14, 2026, Defendant served a subpoena for production of business records on third party financial institution the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC or the Bank). (Turk Decl., Exh. A.) Defendant demanded production of the following documents on June 5, 2026 pertaining to Plaintiff’s account with the Bank, specified by number:

 

1.

 Any and all DOCUMENTS referencing the ACCOUNT within the SPECIFIED TIM¿.

2.

 Any and all COMMUNICATIONS with Ming Lu referencing or pertaining to the ACCOUNT within the SPECIFIED TIM¿.

3.

 Any and all DOCUMENTS referencing or pertaining to the ACCOUNT opening.

4.

 Any and all signature cards pertaining to the ACCOUNT during the SPECIFIED TIME.

5.

 Any and all monthly statement DOCUMENTS pertaining to the ACCOUNT during the SPECIFIED TIM¿.

6.

 Any and all front and back copies of all canceled checks pertaining to the ACCOUNT during the SPECIFIED TIME.

7.

 Any and all deposit slips, withdrawal slips, and transaction records pertaining to the ACCOUNT during the SPECIFIED TIME.

8.

Any and all checks of any kind (e.g. cashier's) pertaining to the ACCOUNT during the SPECIFIED TIME.

9.

Any and all wire transfers pertaining or referencing to the ACCOUNT during the SPECIFIED TIME.

10.

 Any and all online or internet transfer pertaining or referencing the ACCOUNT during the specified time.

11.

 Any and all power of attorney pertaining to the ACCOUNT.

 

On June 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the motion to quash or limit the subpoena on privacy grounds. Defendant opposes.

 

MOTION TO QUASH OR LIMIT SUBPOENA

 

I.         Legal Standard

 

A party is entitled to obtain business records within this state from “a person who is not a party to the action in which the discovery is sought” through use of a deposition subpoena. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.) The obtaining of business records through a deposition subpoena “is a ‘deposition’ within the plain meaning of the Civil Discovery Act.” (Naser v. Lakeridge Athletic Club (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 571, 578.)

 

“The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party, deponent, or other natural person or organization from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.420(b).)

 

III.      Discussion

 

Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 541 (Williams) and Hill v. National Collegiate Athletic Assn. (1994) 7 Cal.4th 1, 35 (Hill) describe the test a court should apply when limiting discovery based on privacy interests. The three-pronged Hill test, applied to discovery disputes via Williams, proceeds as follows:

 

“The party asserting a privacy right must establish [1] a legally protected privacy interest, [2] an objectively reasonable expectation of privacy in the given circumstances, and [3] a threatened intrusion that is serious. ([Hill,] at pp. 35–37 ... .) The party seeking information may raise in response whatever legitimate and important countervailing interests disclosure serves, while the party seeking protection may identify feasible alternatives that serve the same interests or protective measures that would diminish the loss of privacy. A court must then balance these competing considerations. (Id. at pp. 37–40 ... .)”

 

(Williams, supra, 3 Cal.5th at p. 552.)

 

Defendant has the burden to satisfy the three prongs of Hill, and then the Court proceeds to apply a balancing test and determine whether material protected by Hill should be disclosed.

 

Plaintiff has established a legally protected privacy interest, objectively reasonable expectation, and – given the breadth of Defendant’s document requests – a serious intrusion. “The right of privacy is an ‘inalienable right’ secured by article I, section 1 of the California Constitution.  ... It protects against the unwarranted, compelled disclosure of various private or sensitive information regarding one's personal life ... , including his or her financial affairs ... .” (Hooser v. Superior Court (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 997, 1003-1004, internal citations omitted, disapproved on other grounds in Williams, supra; see also Valley Bank of Nevada v. Superior Court (1975) 15 Cal.3d 652, 656-657 [no privilege, but constitutional privacy interest in bank records].)

 

Because Plaintiff satisfies the first prong of the Williams test, it falls to Defendant to identify feasible alternatives. It has not done so. In meet-and-confer correspondence and in its opposition, it insists that the subpoena must be enforced in its entirety. It offers to stipulate to a protective order, but the wholesale disclosure of Plaintiff’s financial records to Defendant is by itself intrusive. Defendant may be entitled to limited records at least reflecting Plaintiff’s income and its sources at relevant times, but it is Defendant’s burden to identify alternatives, and it has not done so.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s motion to quash the subpoena served on May 14,  2026, is GRANTED.

Case Number: 25STCV27822    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 September 17, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV27822

CASE NAME:                        Jesus A. Saldana v. General Motors, LLC

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant General Motors, LLC

OPPOSING PARTY:             Plaintiff Jesus A. Saldana

PROCEEDING:                      DEMURRER TO FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

RULING SUMMARY:          Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND on statute of limitations grounds. Plaintiff is ordered to file an amended pleading within 10 days.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Amended Complaint for, and continues the Case Management Conference to _____________, 2026, at 8:30AM.                               

Background

 

Plaintiff filed this lemon law action on September 23, 2025 and filed his first amended complaint (FAC) on April 7, 2026. His claims arise from alleged defects in a 2022 GMC Yukon he purchased on February 21, 2022. Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s fifth cause of action for fraudulent concealment, arguing it is time-barred and Plaintiff has failed to state a claim.  Plaintiff opposes.

 

demurrer

 

I.         Legal Standard

 

Where pleadings are defective, a party may raise the defect by way of a demurrer. (Coyne v. Krempels (1950) 36 Cal.2d 257, 262.) A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint alleges facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.10; Young v. Gannon (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 209, 220.) When considering a demurrer, a court reads the allegations stated in the challenged pleading liberally and in context, and “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.) Put differently: for purposes of demurrer, the court treats all facts alleged – but only the facts alleged – in the complaint as true. (Picton v. Anderson Union High School District (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 732.) “The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.” (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

 

II.        Meeting and Conference

 

Counsel met and conferred by telephone. (Perez Decl., ¶ 2.) Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 is satisfied.

 

III.      Discussion

 

The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend.  The statute of limitations for fraud is three years. (Code Civ. Proc., § 338(d).)  Plaintiff purchased his vehicle more than three years before he filed this lawsuit. He alleges the statute should be tolled because he “discovered Defendants’ wrongful conduct ... July 30, 2025 when Plaintiff requested buyback and/or restitution ... [and] GM failed to provide restitution[.]” (FAC, ¶ 31.) But Plaintiff’s standalone common-law fraud claim depends on the defects in his vehicle, Defendant’s alleged violation of the lemon law.

 

Plaintiff’s pleading is not self-defeating on its face, but it fails to state a claim that survives the limitations period because it fails to allege a date when the fraud was discovered or the circumstances under which it was discovered.  (Community Cause v. Boatwright (1981) 124 Cal.App.3d 888, 900; see Hobart v. Hobart Estate Co. (1945) 26Cal.2d 412, 437.) 

 

Defendant’s other grounds for its demurrer are not persuasive.  In light of the fact that Defendant had superior if not exclusive knowledge of alleged defects, the level of specificity in the FAC is sufficient.  A sufficient contractual relationship is alleged through the warranty contract.  And the economic loss rule does not preclude the claims because they involve alleged pre-contractual fraud; any post-contractual fraud during the performance period does not, therefore, preclude the claim. 

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND on statute of limitations grounds. Plaintiff is ordered to file an amended pleading within 10 days.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Amended Complaint for, and continues the Case Management Conference to _____________, 2026, at 8:30AM.

Case Number: 26STCV01564    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 September 17, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   26STCV01564

CASE NAME:                        Amitiss Nasiri v. Rhett P. Warner, Esq.

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant Rhett P. Warner

OPPOSING PARTY:             Plaintiff Amitiss Nasiri

PROCEEDING:                      DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

RULING SUMMARY:            The demurrer is SUSTAINED, with leave to amend.  Plaintiff must file an amended complaint within 10 days of this Ruling.  The Court continues the Case Management Conference to, and sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Amended Complaint for, ________________, 2026, at 8:30AM. 

                                                                       

Background

 

Plaintiff Amitiss Nasiri filed this legal malpractice action against defendant Rhett P. Warner, Esq. on September 17, 2026, asserting claims for (1) legal malpractice and (2) breach of fiduciary duty; he also asserts “Punitive Damages – Civil Code § 3294” as a third, independent cause of action. Plaintiff alleges Defendant substituted in for Plaintiff’s prior counsel to represent him in Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. YC072653 (“Underlying Action”) beginning on February 19, 2021. As alleged, after undertaking representation, Defendant missed filing deadlines that resulted in evidentiary sanctions imposed by the trial court, an adverse judgment, and a failed appeal.

 

Defendant filed a Demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint on May 15, 2026.  Plaintiff filed his Opposition on September 1, 2026, and on September 10, 2026 Defendant filed a Reply.

 

DEMURRER

 

I.         Legal Standard

 

Where pleadings are defective, a party may raise the defect by way of a demurrer. (Coyne v. Krempels (1950) 36 Cal.2d 257, 262.) A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint alleges facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 430.10; Young v. Gannon (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 209, 220.) When considering a demurrer, a court reads the allegations stated in the challenged pleading liberally and in context, and “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.) Put differently: for purposes of demurrer, the court treats all facts alleged – but only the facts alleged – in the complaint as true. (Picton v. Anderson Union High School District (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 732.) “The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.” (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

 

II.        Meet and Confer

 

Defendant’s counsel states that he attempted to meet and confer by telephone, but the number for Plaintiff’s counsel as stated on his complaint does not function. (Rubin Decl., ¶ 4.) Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 is satisfied.

 

III.      Request for Judicial Notice

 

Defendant requests judicial notice of five entries in the case file for the Underlying Action. The request was submitted along with Defendant’s reply, and Plaintiff objects he did not have sufficient time to respond.

 

The Court grants the request despite its late submission. Defendant’s demurrer relies on judicially noticeable facts that placed Plaintiff on notice of his arguments; the filings in the Underlying Action are judicial records plainly subject to notice, and Plaintiff’s complaint concedes he was aware of all the underlying facts. The Court finds that taking judicial notice causes no prejudice to Plaintiff.

 

III.      Discussion

 

Defendant argues (1) the first cause of action is time-barred and uncertain or unintelligible; (2) the second cause of action fails to state sufficient facts and duplicates the first; and (3) Plaintiff’s claim for punitive damages is not an independently cognizable cause of action.

 

            First Cause of Action for Legal Malpractice

 

The Amended Judgment in the underlying action was filed on October 25, 2022.  Plaintiff appealed by counsel other than Defendant in this case.  Plaintiff has not alleged that Defendant represented Plaintiff at any time after October 25, 2022.  This action was filed on September 17, 2026, almost four years after entry of judgment in the underlying action.  Civil Code section 340.6(a) provides that an action against an attorney for a wrongful act or omission other than fraud, arising in the performance of professional services must be brought “within one year after the plaintiff discovers, or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the facts constituting the wrongful act or omission, whichever occurs first.”  As Plaintiff was represented by other counsel after October 25, 2022 and during the course of the appeal, the Complaint does not adequately allege grounds to toll the statute of limitations under section 340.6.  Moreover, many if not all of the acts or omissions on which the first cause of action is based occurred well before October 25, 2022 (many occurred even before Defendant was substituted in as Plaintiff’s counsel), which would mean that the four-year outside limitation period would apply. 

 

For the reasons explained above, the demurrer to the first cause of action is sustained with leave to amend. 

 

            Breach of Fiduciary Duty

 

The limitations period applicable to the first cause of action also applies to the second cause of action.  Therefore, the demurrer to the second cause of action is also sustained with leave to amend. 

 

The second cause of action does not allege sufficient facts to support the claim for breach of fiduciary duty.  Therefore, the demurrer to the second cause of action is sustained on that ground as well, with leave to amend. 

 

            Punitive Damages

 

A claim for punitive damages does not constitute a cause of action.  Moreover, such damages are not recoverable on a claim for attorney malpractice, which is based upon acts or omissions that fall below the standard of care.  Plaintiff has failed to plead any claims that contain factual allegations constituting oppression, fraud or malice.  Therefore, the demurrer to the third cause of action is sustained.  Plaintiff must pray for punitive damages, and allege facts supporting them, within causes of action that would support such claims.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant’s demurrer is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND as to the first and second causes of action and SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND as to the third cause of action. Plaintiff is ordered to file an amended pleading within 10 days.  The Court continues the Case Management Conference to, and sets an Order to Show Cause Re: Amended Complaint for, _________________, 2026, at 8:30AM.
