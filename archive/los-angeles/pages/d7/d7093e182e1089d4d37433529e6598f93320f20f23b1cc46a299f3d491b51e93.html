DEPARTMENT T LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24NNCV02967    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: T

Rachel Wolf vs. Carmi V. Gallo

Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set Eight

Moving Party:                         Plaintiff Rachel Wolf

Responding Party:                  None

Tentative Ruling:                    Grant

 

RULING

 

As the Court finds that Plaintiff Rachel Wolf (“Plaintiff”) properly served Defendant Carmi V. Gallo (“Defendant”) with an eighth set of requests for production of documents on June 12, 2026, via mail.  As a result, Defendant’s deadline to respond to these requests was July 17, 2026.  However, Defendant failed to timely respond by this date and thereby waived all objections.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.300, subd. (a).)  Furthermore, when directed to a production request, Code of Civil Procedure “[s]ection 2031.250 requires that a response be verified unless it contains only objections.”  (Pollock v. Superior Court (2023) 93 Cal.App.5th 1348, 1352.)  Although Defendant belatedly served her response to these requests on July 23, 2026, the accompanying verification failed to include a statement confirming that it was made “under penalty of perjury” by Defendant.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2015.5.)  Nor was the verification substantially in the form required by Section 2015.5.  “A fatally defective verification ‘is treated as a failure to verify.’ [Citation.]”  (Krueger v. Superior Court (1979) 89 Cal.App.3d 934, 939.)  Accordingly, as the facially defective verification is treated as a failure to verify, the Court finds that Defendant’s failure to properly verify her July 23, 2026, responses rendered them ineffective, as “[u]nsworn responses are tantamount to no responses at all.”  (Appleton v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 632, 636.)  Therefore, the motion to compel verified responses, without objections, to these production requests is granted.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.300, subd. (b).) 

 

Plaintiff Rachel Wolf’s Motion to Compel Defendant’s Responses to Requests for Production, Set Eight, is GRANTED.  Defendant Carmi V. Gallo’s verified responses, without objections, are due within 20 days of this order. 

Case Number: 24NNCV05732    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: T

Counts Law Firm, PC vs. Nia Jervier, et al.

Renewed Motion for Assignment

Moving Party:                         Plaintiff Counts Law Firm, PC

Responding Party:                  None

Tentative Ruling:                    Deny

 

BACKGROUND

 

On November 12, 2024, Plaintiff Counts Law Firm, PC (“Plaintiff”) filed a verified complaint against Defendants Nia Jerver (“Jervier”), Makini Martin, and Makini Regal Designs, LLC (collectively “Defendants”), alleging the following causes of action: (1) breach of written contract; and (2) common counts.

 

On April 29, 2025, the Court entered a default judgment against Defendants for $41,575.00 and ordered Defendants jointly and severally liable for the judgment.  (Judgment, 4/29/25.) 

 

On April 2, 2026, the Court granted assignment to Plaintiff of the entirety of Jervier’s rights to the residual payments due or to become due from SAG-AFTRA to the extent necessary to satisfy the judgment.

 

After learning from SAG-AFTRA that Jervier’s residual payments were made by various other entities, Plaintiff now moves for an assignment of Jervier’s payment rights as to those entities pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 708.510.

 

No opposition was filed.

 

MOVING PARTY POSITION

 

             Plaintiff states that the $41,575.00 judgment and post-judgment interest remain wholly unpaid and that it recently discovered that Jervier earns and is entitled to recurring income paid by  the Abrams Artist Agency, The Drama Dept., SisterLee Productions, Culture Machine, Code Red Productions, Homegrown Pictures, Roadside Attractions, Lionsgate Television, and Netflix including compensation and residual payments, which are assignable rights to payment under California law.

 

            Plaintiff seeks an order assigning all of Jervier’s assignable income from these entities to Plaintiff under Section 708.510, including the residual payments, as these are contractual rights to payment that are not exempt from assignment.

 

OPPOSITION

 

No opposition was filed.

 

REPLY

            No reply was filed.

 

ANALYSIS

I.              Legal Standard

Upon the application of a judgment creditor, the Court may order the judgment debtor to assign the creditor all or part of “a right to payment due or to become due, whether or not the right is conditioned on future developments.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 708.510, subd. (a).)  Assignable payment rights include, but are not limited to, the following: (1) wages due to the federal government that are not subject to withholding under an earnings withholding order; (2) rents; (3) commissions; (4) royalties; (5) payments from patent or copyright; and (6) insurance policy loan values.  (Ibid.) 

When determining whether to order an assignment, the Court may consider all relevant factors, including (1) the reasonable requirements of a judgment debtor, (2) payments the judgment debtor is required to make or that are deduction in satisfaction of other judgments and wage assignments, (3) the amount remaining due on the money judgment, and (4) the amount being received in satisfaction of the right to payment that may be assigned.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 708.510, subd. (c).)   

II.            Discussion

Plaintiff seeks an order under Code of Civil Procedure section 708.510 assigning “[a]ll rights to payment due or to become due to Judgment Debtor Nia Jervier, including but not limited to Abrams Artist Agency, The Drama Dept., SisterLee Productions, Culture Machine, Code Red Productions, Homegrown Pictures, Roadside Attractions, Lionsgate Television, Netflix, and any payroll company, residual administrator, distributor, production company, talent agency, loan-out entity, or other entity obligated to make payments to Defendant arising from her acting services or residual rights, to the extent necessary to satisfy the outstanding judgment, accrued interest, and allowable costs.”  (Proposed Order, at p. 1.) 

Although Plaintiff seeks an assignment of all rights to payment arising from Jervier’s contractual relationship with these entities, Plaintiff fails to establish that all of these rights are assignable under California law.  Specifically, in its comment to Section 708.510, the Legislative Committee noted that “[t]his section does not make any property assignable that is not already assignable.”  This limitation on Section 708.510’s reach is also consistent with Code of Civil Procedure section 703.030, which provides that, “[e]xcept as otherwise specifically provided by statute, property that is described in this chapter or in any other statute as exempt without making a claim is not subject to any procedure for enforcement of a money judgment.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 703.030, subd. (b).)  For instance, such exemptions without making a claim include, “[b]efore payment, benefits from a disability or health insurance policy or program.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 704.130, subd. (a).) 

Here, in support of the motion, Plaintiff’s counsel attested that, “[f]ollowing entry of the Court’s April 2, 2026 Assignment Order, Plaintiff learned that SAG-AFTRA does not typically function as the entity responsible for issuing Defendant’s residual payments” and that “Plaintiff thereafter conducted additional investigation and identified the entities described herein through which Defendant has received, or may receive, compensation and residual payments.”  (Counts Decl. ¶ 5.)  The entities identified in Plaintiff’s counsel’s declaration are “Abrams Artist Agency, The Drama Dept., SisterLee Productions, Culture Machine, Code Red Productions, Homegrown Pictures, Roadside Attractions, Lionsgate Television, Netflix.”  (Id. ¶ 5.)  Counsel also submitted a copy of the judgment and IMDB profiles as supporting exhibits to Plaintiff’s motion.  (See Mot., Exhs. 1 and 2.)  However, as there is insufficient evidence of Jervier’s contractual relationship with these entities and the nature of the payment rights due to her therefrom, the Court finds that it lacks a sufficient factual or evidentiary basis to determine (1) if all of the payment rights due or to become due to Jervier from these entities are assignable and (2) whether these rights should be assigned based on all relevant factors, including the amounts being received from those assignable rights in satisfaction of the judgment. 

Therefore, the motion is denied without prejudice.

RULING

Plaintiff Counts Law Firm, PC’s Renewed Motion for Assignment is DENIED without prejudice.
