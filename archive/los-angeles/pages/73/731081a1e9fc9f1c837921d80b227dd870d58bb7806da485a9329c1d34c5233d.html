DEPARTMENT B LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24TRCV03909    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                       Defendant ABM Industry Groups, LLC

Responding Party:                  Plaintiff Claudia Fimbres

Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the alternative, Summary Adjudication

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

            The motion for summary judgment is GRANTED.

BACKGROUND

On November 19, 2024, plaintiff Claudia Fimbres filed a complaint against Forum Entertainment LLC for premises liability and general negligence as to an incident on April 8, 2023 based on a slip and fall on stairs.

On December 26, 2024, Forum Entertainment LLC filed a cross-complaint against ABM Services, Inc. for apportionment of fault, indemnification, declaratory relief, and attorneys’ fees.

On April 11, 2025, plaintiff filed an amendment to the complaint designating ABM Industry Groups, LLC.

On May 30, 2025, Forum Entertainment LLC filed a request for dismissal as to its cross-complaint.

On May 12, 2026, the Court granted defendant Forum Entertainment LLC’s motion for summary judgment.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

            The purpose of a motion for summary judgment or summary adjudication “is to provide courts with a mechanism to cut through the parties’ pleadings in order to determine whether, despite their allegations, trial is in fact necessary to resolve their dispute.”  Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal. 4th 826, 843.  “Code of Civil Procedure section 437c, subdivision (c), requires the trial judge to grant summary judgment if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”  Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7 Cal. App. 4th 1110, 1119.

“On a motion for summary judgment, the initial burden is always on the moving party to make a prima facie showing that there are no triable issues of material fact.”  Scalf v. D. B. Log Homes, Inc. (2005) 128 Cal. App. 4th 1510, 1519.  A defendant moving for summary judgment or summary adjudication “has met his or her burden of showing that a cause of action has no merit if the party has shown that one or more elements of the cause of action . . . cannot be established, or that there is a complete defense to the cause of action.”  CCP § 437c(p)(2).  “Once the defendant . . . has met that burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff . . . to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to the cause of action or a defense thereto.” CCP § 437c(p)(2).

“When deciding whether to grant summary judgment, the court must consider all of the evidence set forth in the papers (except evidence to which the court has sustained an objection), as well as all reasonable inferences that may be drawn from that evidence, in the light most favorable to the party opposing summary judgment.” Avivi v. Centro Medico Urgente Medical Center (2008) 159 Cal. App. 4th 463, 467; CCP §437c(c).

OBJECTIONS

Defendant’s objection Nos. 1-4 to plaintiff’s deposition are OVERRULED.

Defendant’s objection No. 1 to plaintiff’s responses to ABM’s special interrogatories is SUSTAINED.

Defendant’s objections to Rivera’s declaration are ruled on as follows:  OVERRULED as to No. 1 and SUSTAINED as to Nos. 2-17.

DISCUSSION

            Defendant ABM Industry Groups, LLC requests summary judgment in its favor and against plaintiff on the ground that no triable issue of material fact exists and defendant is entitled to summary judgment as a matter of law.

            The complaint alleges that on April 8, 2023, plaintiff was a guest at Kia Forum for a concert hosted at defendants’ venue, which was owned and/or controlled by defendants.  As she was walking down the stairs after the concert ended, she suddenly fell due to a transitory substance – an unsafe condition which had existed for a sufficient period of time that defendants knew or should have known of it.  In spite of such actual or constructive knowledge, defendants failed to correct such unsafe condition, failed to protect plaintiff from it, and failed to warn plaintiff.

            Plaintiff filed an amendment on April 11, 2025 designating moving defendant ABM Industry Groups, LLC as Doe 1.

“In order to state a cause of action for negligence, plaintiff must state facts showing that defendant had a duty to plaintiff, that the duty was breached by negligent conduct, and that the breach was the cause of damages to the plaintiff.”  Brooks v. Eugene Burger Management Corp. (1989) 215 Cal. App. 3d 1611, 1619.   The elements of premises liability mirror those for negligence:  a legal duty of care, breach of that duty, and proximate cause resulting in injury. Kesner v. Superior Court (2016) 1 Cal.5th 1132, 1158.  “Premises liability is grounded in the possession of the premises and the attendant right to control and manage the premises; accordingly, mere possession with its attendant right to control conditions on the premises is a sufficient basis for the imposition of an affirmative duty to act.”  Id. (citations omitted).  “Those who own, possess, or control property generally have a duty to exercise ordinary care in managing the property in order to avoid exposing others to an unreasonable risk of harm.”  Annocki v. Peterson Enterprises, LLC (2014) 232 Cal. App. 4th 32, 37.

The parties do not dispute that at no time during the incident did ABM own or lease the Kia Forum.  Defendant’s Separate Statement of Undisputed Material Facts (“DUMF”) 3.  The area of the Kia Forum where the stage is and the surrounding seating area for the audience constitute the “inner bowl” or “seating bowl.”  DUMF 5.  Plaintiff’s alleged fall occurred within the “inner bowl” of the Kia Forum.  DUMF 9.  Plaintiff’s alleged fall occurred immediately following the final concert performance as plaintiff and others were attempting to leave the Kia Forum.  DUMF 10.  Prior to plaintiff’s alleged fall, plaintiff did not notice any hazardous or dangerous condition on the stairs.  DUMF 11.  Prior to plaintiff’s alleged fall, she did not report any hazardous or dangerous condition on the stairs to any ABM employees.  DUMF 12.  Plaintiff does not know for how long the allegedly hazardous or dangerous condition was on the stairs where she allegedly fell.  DUMF 16.  Plaintiff is not aware of any third party or other concert goer, prior to the incident, reporting any alleged hazardous or dangerous condition on the stairs where she allegedly fell.  DUMF 17.

Defendant argues that it owed no duty to plaintiff regarding the condition of the premises that ABM did not own, lease, or control.  Defendant asserts that its sole involvement with the Kia Forum was the provision of janitorial services, which were defined and limited by the scope of the Janitorial Services Agreement (“JSA”), which includes a “Scope of Work” addendum.  As to the “inner bowl” where plaintiff allegedly fell, the JSA states:  “Cleaning personnel shall remain in their assigned areas at all times during the Event, unless otherwise advised by MSG.  The parties acknowledge and agree that cleaning services will not be provided in the inner bowl of the Forum except upon call or request of the [Forum] command post.”  See Def.’s Ex. E at 13.  Defendant Forum’s PMQ testified during his deposition that the provision of the JSA mandating ABM not to clean the inner bowl during events unless otherwise instructed is “clear in terms of what it is saying.”  McNeff Depo. at 42.  Defendant asserts that the “command post” was not staffed by any ABM personnel on the date of the incident.  Id. at 19.

Defendant also argues that even if it did owe a duty, ABM did not breach any duty of care.  Defendant asserts that there is no evidence ABM was called upon or requested to perform cleaning services in the area of plaintiff’s alleged fall during the event.  Defendant further argues that there is no evidence ABM had, prior to the incident, any knowledge of the alleged dangerous or hazardous condition on the stairs where plaintiff allegedly fell or that the stairs had any allegedly hazardous or dangerous condition on them when plaintiff first walked up to her seat prior to the event.  Additionally, plaintiff admitted that she has no facts supporting how long the “transitory substance” was on the floor prior to the incident.  Defendant also presents evidence that the slip resistance of the stairway, in both dry and wet conditions, is within the acceptable range of industry standards such that the stairway did not present a slipping hazard whether dry or wet. 

In opposition, plaintiff argues that ABM owed plaintiff a duty because it voluntarily undertook to diligently monitor stairwells, citing to the Scope of Work addendum and the section as to “Core Cleaning Services.”  Plaintiff also cites to the deposition of McNeff, Forum’s PMQ, who testified that ABM staff were expected to “make an effort to go up the stairs just to check on things on their own, time permitting” (McNeff Depo. at 48) and its retained expert Enrique Rivera, CXLT who states in his declaration that defendant ABM’s assigned areas encompassed the entire venue, including both the inner and outer bowl, and that ABM personnel “were expected to monitor and check the stairways when able” (Rivera Dec. ¶¶ 14-15.  Plaintiff also agues that ABM had a duty based on “functional control,” as it staffed dozens of cleaners in and around the bowl before, during, and immediately after the event and it undertook the specific function of checking the stairwells.

Plaintiff further argues that there are triable issues of material fact as to breach.  Plaintiff contends that ABM’s own discovery responses establish the absence of any documented inspection and that a reasonable jury could find that no adequate inspection system existed at all.  Plaintiff cites also to Forum’s PMQ McNeff’s deposition testimony where he states that he keeps no document that is filled out that states that any walkthrough, inspection, or cleanup verification occurred and that there were no vendor’s employees assigned to stairs in the inner bowl that night.  As for constructive notice, plaintiff argues that the scale of ABM’s post-event cleanup operation, a nearly 90-person crew mobilized within an hour of the incident to address bowl, terrace, and concourse conditions, is itself evidence that spills and similar hazards in the seating bowl are a “recurring, expected condition” of the venue’s operations.  As to causation, plaintiff argues that there are triable issues of material fact as plaintiff presents retained expert Rivera’s declaration refuting Braun’s testing measures as measuring the “wrong thing” as a contaminated surface, not the bare stairway, caused plaintiff’s fall.  Rivera also opines that the presence of a contaminant was foreseeable and consistent with recognized industry standards on slip and fall hazards and that “human-factors” research explains why plaintiff would not have seen the hazard. 

In reply, defendant reiterates its argument that summary judgment is proper because plaintiff cannot prove that ABM had a duty to inspect the stairs, or actual or constructive notice of the spill, or causation.

The Court finds that plaintiff cannot succeed on her claims because she cannot prove the element of duty.  It is undisputed that defendant neither owned nor possessed the Kia Forum, where plaintiff fell.  Defendant presented evidence that its sole involvement with the Kia Forum was the provision of janitorial services as provided by the JSA.  The JSA explicitly provides that defendant was not to provide janitorial services in the inner bowl – which is where plaintiff fell – during events unless instructed by the Forum:  “The parties acknowledge and agree that cleaning services will not be provided in the inner bowl of the Forum except upon call or request of the [Forum] command post.”  Def.’s Ex. E at 13.  It is undisputed that there was no call or request from the command post to defendant to clean the incident stairway during the event.  Defendant has thus met its burden of providing affirmative evidence that plaintiff cannot satisfy the element of duty, and the burden shifts to plaintiff to raise a triable issue of material fact.

Plaintiff has not met her burden.  Plaintiff argues that defendant owed her a duty because (1) through the JSA, defendant voluntarily undertook to diligently monitor stairwells at the Kia Forum; and (2) separate and apart from the JSA, defendant exercised actual control of the area where plaintiff fell.

First, defendant did not undertake a duty to diligently monitor stairwells during events at the Kia Forum.  The JSA covers “Event Services” related to services provided during events, as well as “Core Cleaning Services” that occurred daily, when there were no events.  Def.’s Ex. D at 1.  The contract clearly divides services required under “Core Cleaning Services” from “Event Operation” services.  Def.’s Ex. E at 12-13.  Defendant was only required to “[d]iligently monitor stairwells” as part of its “Core Cleaning Services,” which are its daily, non-event services.  Id. at 12.  For “Event Operation” services, however, the JSA omits this same language.  See id. at 12-13.  In any event, the JSA differentiates between “stairwells and stair towers” on the one hand, and “seating areas/steps/aisles” on the other.  See id. at 13-14.  Plaintiff does not allege her incident occurred in a stairwell, she alleges it occurred on the steps adjacent to the seating area.   

Plaintiff argues the testimony of Timothy McNeff, Forum Entertainment’s PMQ, supports its argument.  McNeff testified that Forum Entertainment expected defendant’s staff to “make an effort to go up the stairs just to check on things on their own, time permitting.”  Plaintiff’s Ex. 1 at 48.  This does not raise a triable issue of material fact.  “The interpretation of a contract is a question of law unless the interpretation turns upon the credibility of extrinsic evidence.  The clear language of a provision governs unless it leads to an absurdity.  The undisclosed belief or intention of a party is irrelevant in the absence of fraud or mistake.”  Meyers v. Guarantee Sav. & Loan Assn. (1978) 79 Cal.App.3d 307, 311 (citations omitted).  The JAS is not ambiguous, and Forum Entertainment’s subjective expectation of what services defendant would perform is not objectively reasonable in light of the contract.  And because contract interpretation is a question of law, plaintiff’s expert is not qualified to opine on the matter.

Second, plaintiff’s argument that defendant owed her a duty independent of the JAS is not supported.  Plaintiff argues that defendant exercised control of the inner bowl by staffing cleaners for events, assigning staff to specific areas, and undertaking to check stairwells.  All of those actions arose from the JAS.

Accordingly, plaintiff has failed to raise a triable issue of material fact on the element of duty.

ORDER

            The motion for summary judgment is GRANTED.

            Moving party shall submit a proposed judgment.     

Moving party is ordered to give notice of ruling.


Case Number: 26TRCP00162    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                       Petitioner Rosaline Mills

Responding Party:                   Respondent City of Inglewood

Motion for Relief from Government Claim Presentation Requirements

 

            The Court considered the moving and opposition papers.

RULING

            The motion and petition are DENIED.

BACKGROUND

On April 23, 2026, petitioner Rosaline Mills filed a petition against City of Inglewood for relief from government claim presentation requirements.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

Before a suit for damages may be filed against a public entity, a claimant must present a timely written claim to the public entity and the claim must have been acted upon by the board or deemed rejected.  Gov. Code §945.4; Munoz v. State of Cal. (1995) 33 Cal. App. 4th 1767, 1777.  A claim relating to a cause of action for injury to person or personal property must be presented to the public entity not later than six months after the accrual of the cause of action.  Gov. Code §911.2.  Munoz, supra, 33 Cal. App. 4th at 1777.

If a claimant fails to present a claim within the statutory period, he may apply “within a reasonable time not to exceed one year after the accrual of the cause of action” to the public entity for leave to present a late claim.  Gov. Code §911.4.  If the public entity denies leave to present a late claim, a claimant may petition the Court under Government Code §946.6 for relief from the requirements of Government Code §945.4.  Munoz, supra, 33 Cal. App. 4th at 1777.

Additionally, a petition pursuant to Government Code §946.6 must be “filed within six months after the application to the board is denied or deemed to be denied pursuant to Section 911.6.”  Gov. Code §946.6(b); see also City of Los Angeles v. Superior Court (1993) 14 Cal. App. 4th 621, 627.  This requirement is mandatory.  Lineaweaver v. S. Cal. Rapid Transit Dist. (1983) 139 Cal. App. 3d 738, 741.

If the §946.6 petition is timely filed, the court may relieve a claimant from the requirements of §945.4 if it makes certain findings, including:  (1) the claimant made a timely application to the public entity for leave to present a late claim; (2) the application was denied; and (3) the failure to timely present a claim was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.  Gov. Code §946.6(c).  In determining whether relief is warranted under Government Code §946.6, the court considers the petition, any affidavits submitted in support of or in opposition to the petition, and any other evidence presented at the hearing.  Gov. Code §946.6(e); Munoz, supra, 33 Cal. App. 4th at 1778.

“The showing required for relief under [§] 946.6 because of mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect is the same as required under [CCP] [§] 473 for relieving a party from a default judgment.”  Ebersol v. Cowan (1983) 35 Cal.3d 427, 435.

DISCUSSION

            Under Gov. Code §946.6, petitioner Rosaline Mills requests relief from the government claims statute under Gov. Code §945.4 as against City of Inglewood.

            Petitioner asserts that she sustained serious injuries on March 1, 2025 due to a hazardous and dangerous sidewalk condition at or near 311 N. La Brea Avenue, in City of Inglewood.  She contends that she presented an application for leave to present a late claim to City of Inglewood within one year of the accrual of her cause of action as required by Gov. Code §911.4.  The City denied the application on March 20, 2026.  Petitioner filed this petition on April 23, 2026, which was within six months of the denial, as required by Gov. Code §946.6(b).  She argues that her failure to timely present a claim to City of Inglewood was through mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect from “reasonable confusion as to the identity of the responsible public entity.”  She also argues that the City will suffer no prejudice from the granting of relief. 

            Petitioner’s counsel Sepideh Hersel states in their declaration that counsel was first contacted by petitioner in April 2025, approximately one month after the March 1, 2025 incident.  Upon being retained, counsel’s office “acted promptly and diligently” and investigated the facts, collected relevant documentation, interviewed petitioner, reviewed photographs of the incident location, and prepared government claims for filing.  Counsel states that based on their initial investigation, they determined that the incident location could fall within the jurisdiction of either the City of Los Angeles or the County of Los Angeles as La Brea Avenue is a major thoroughfare that traverses multiple municipal boundaries and jurisdictions, “creating reasonable uncertainty as to which public entity owned and maintained the particular stretch of sidewalk” where petitioner fell.  Counsel states that on August 26, 2025, within the six-month claim presentation deadline under Gov. Code §911.2, petitioner served government claims with both the City and County of Los Angeles.  On October 22, 2025, the County of Los Angles issued a written notice rejecting the claim and stating that the County does not own or maintain the subject property.  On October 28, the City of Los Angeles also rejected the claim stating that it does not own or maintain the subject sidewalk.  Following the rejection letters, counsel’s office conducted further investigation, including review of jurisdictional maps and consultation with city record, and determined that the City of Inglewood owned and maintained the property.  Petitioner then submitted an application for leave to present a late claim on January 27, 2026, which was rejected on March 29.  Counsel states in their declaration that the incident location is in close proximity to jurisdictional boundaries, and photographs of the site show utility infrastructure including a “Bell System” utility cover, which “further contributed to the reasonable uncertainty regarding governmental responsibility for sidewalk maintenance at that specific location.”  See Sepideh Hersel decl.

            In opposition, respondent City of Inglewood argues that petitioner has not established that her failure to present a timely claim was through “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.”  City contends that based on the claim for damages, petitioner lives in Inglewood and she was allegedly injured a little over a mile away from her home.  City notes that a photograph submitted with the claim for damages indicates in the background signage that reads “Inglewood.”  City argues that “[t]his is not a case where it was difficult to determine the proper entity because city boundaries are easily determined by any number of means such as consulting with a map, looking at the street signs in the vicinity, or by conducting an internet search.”  City also notes that the claims for damages presented to the City and County of Los Angeles state that she was “lawfully walking along the public sidewalk located at or near 311 N. La Brea Avenue, Inglewood, California 90302.”  Respondent also argues that it is unclear how the “Bell System” could have contributed to uncertainty over where the incident took place.  Respondent further notes that petitioner offers no explanation for a three-month gap between being told in writing that Inglewood was a distinct public entity and actually presenting a claim to it.  Moreover, respondent contends, it is not required to show prejudice.  See Derald Brenneman decl.

            There is no reply.

            The Court finds that although the §946.6 petition was timely filed, petitioner has not met her burden of showing that the failure to timely present a claim to City of Inglewood was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.  “Relief on grounds of mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect is available only on a showing that the claimant’s failure to timely present a claim was reasonable when tested by the objective ‘reasonably prudent person’ standard.”  Dep’t of Water & Power v. Superior Court (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 1288, 1293.  It was objectively unreasonable for petitioner to not have timely discovered that the City of Inglewood was the proper entity with which to file a government claim.  The claims presented to the City and County of Los Angeles state that plaintiff was injured in Inglewood.  As pointed out by respondent, a simple search indicates that the property address is in the City of Inglewood.  Petitioner’s counsel’s purported “jurisdictional confusion” was not reasonable, especially in light of the fact that petitioner lives in Inglewood.  Even if there was reasonable confusion, it was possible to file a claim in multiple locations, which petitioner did when she filed with the City and County of Los Angeles.  Nothing prevented her from filing a third copy of the claim with the City of Inglewood. 

ORDER

            The motion and petition are DENIED.

            Respondent City of Inglewood is ordered to give notice of ruling.
