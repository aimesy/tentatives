[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO SEAL EXHIBITS IN SUPPORT OF ITS OPPOSITION TO PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO QUASH OR MODIFY SUBPOEAS FOR PLAINTIFF’S EMPLOYMENT RECORDS (4668)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      This action arises from alleged retaliation, discrimination, and sexual harassment in violation of the Fair Employment and Housing Act and the Labor Code. Plaintiff also alleges (1) retaliation in violation of the Labor Code; violations under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (“FEHA”)  for (2) sex discrimination, (3) sexual harassment, (4) hostile work environment, (5) retaliation, (6) failure to prevent harassment, discrimination, or retaliation; and tort claims for (7) negligent supervision, hiring, and retention; (8) intentional infliction of emotional distress; and (9) wrongful termination in violation of public policy.

A.     Res. No. 1597

      Defendant requests that the court seal selected exhibits conditionally filed under seal in support of its opposition to Plaintiff’s Motion to Quash or Modify Subpoenas for Plaintiff’s Employment Records. The selected exhibits are:

1.     Exhibit 2: Excerpts from the confidential portion of the transcript from Plaintiff’s November 8, 2025, deposition;

2.     Exhibit 3: Plaintiff’s therapy notes from March 13, 2023, from MDLive;

3.     Exhibit 4: Plaintiff’s therapy notes from March 2, 2022, from MDLive; and

4.     Exhibit 9: Plaintiff’s June 21, 2021, Separate Authorization for Release of Information and Waiver of Potential Claims.

      Defendant argues the exhibits contain Plaintiff’s personal and private information including medical information. The court signed a protective order for confidential information on December 16 and 19, 2025. An order to seal the selected exhibits will uphold the court’s order to protect confidential information.

      No opposition has been filed.

II.     DISCUSSION

      Court records are presumed to be open. (Cal. Rules of Court, 2.550 subd. (c).) The burden is on the moving party to show compelling reasons for sealing records. (Mary R. v. B. & R. Corp. (1983) 149 Cal.App.3d 308, 317.) To support an order to seal records, the court must find that “(1) there is an overriding interest supporting sealing records; (2) there is a substantial probability that the interest will be prejudiced absent sealing; (3) the proposed sealing is narrowly tailored to serve the overriding interest; and (4) there is no less restrictive means of achieving the overriding interest." (McNair v. National Collegiate Athletic Assn. (2015) 234 Cal.App.4th 25, 29; Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 2.550 subd. (d).)

      Defendant has shown that the protection of private and personal information may serve as an overriding interest that outweighs the public’s right to court records. (Pettus v. Cole (1996) 49 Cal.App.4th 402, 440. ["The right to control circulation of personal information is fundamental."].) The right of privacy is protected by the California Constitution and includes informational privacy such as sensitive or confidential information, the dissemination of which is precluded. (Ruiz v. Podolsky (2010) 50 Cal.4th 838, 850.)

      The proposed sealing is narrowly tailored to serve an overriding interest. There is no less restrictive means of protecting Plaintiff’s overriding privacy interests.

III.    CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s two motions to seal selected exhibits are GRANTED.
