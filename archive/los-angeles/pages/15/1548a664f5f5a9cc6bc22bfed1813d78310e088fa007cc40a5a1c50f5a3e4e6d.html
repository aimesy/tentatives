CASE:                         Felix Chac Chuo, et al. v. Minghong Investment, Inc., et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 22STCV08770

 

 

RULING ON PLAINTIFFS’ EQUITABLE CLAIMS FOLLOWING JURY VERDICT

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

This is a consolidated action concerning the sale and transfer of a farm business in Thermal, California. 

On May 19, 2026, the jury returned a verdict in favor of plaintiffs and cross-defendants Felix Chac Chuo, Stella Chuo, and Felix Chac Chuo Farm, Inc. (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) on twelve causes of action in their Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”) against defendant and cross-complainant Minghong Investment, Inc. (“Minghong”), defendant Bin “Billy Yu (“Yu”), B&D Consulting, Corp. (“B&D”), and defendant Chris Yau (“Yau”).  The jury also returned a verdict denying Minghong’s claims.  The only remaining claims to be decided are Plaintiffs’ and Minghong’s equitable claims.  Given the court’s independent evaluation of the evidence as well as the jury’s verdict in favor of Plaintiffs, the court finds in favor of Plaintiffs and against Minghong on Minghong’s equitable claims.[1] 

The court is in receipt of the parties’ written arguments, as well as Plaintiffs’ and Minghong’s respective responses to the court’s tentative ruling on Plaintiffs’ equitable claims.  The court begins by setting forth the applicable law.  In Section III, the court addresses Plaintiffs’ equitable claims against Yau.  In Section III, the court addresses Plaintiffs’ equitable claims against Minghong and Yu.

II.        APPLICABLE LAW

 

UCL claims are equitable in nature and “were intended to be decided by the court rather than a jury.”  (Nationwide Biweekly Administration, Inc. v. Superior Court (2020) 9 Cal.5th 279, 304.)  “The order of trial, in mixed actions with equitable and legal issues, has great significance because the first factfinder may bind the second when determining factual issues common to the equitable and legal issues.”  (Hoopes v. Dolan (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 146, 156–157, citing Wegner et al., Cal. Practice Guide: Civil Trials and Evidence (The Rutter Group 1993) ¶ 2:160, p. 2–32.1.)  “[W]hen the legal claims are decided first by a jury, i.e., the jury’s findings are binding in the trial court’s subsequent disposition of the equitable claims.”  (Rincon EV Realty LLC v. CP III Rincon Towers, Inc. (2019) 43 Cal.App.5th 988, 993.) 

  

III.      DISCUSSION RE EQUITABLE CLAIMS AGAINST YAU

 

            As discussed above, the jury returned a verdict in Plaintiffs favor and against Yau based, in part, on a finding of fraud.  Yau concedes this finding.  Plaintiffs now seek restitution against Yau on their seventh cause of action for violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200 (the “UCL”) in the sum of $4,000, which represents the amount Plaintiffs paid to Yau.  

 

Plaintiffs argue Yau should pay $4,000 in restitution because the funds were paid as part of the same fraudulent scheme.  Yau counters that Plaintiffs paid that amount for accounting and consulting services work she performed through nonparty Lighthouse Consultants, Inc.  Stated another way, Yau argues the $4,000 was performed separate from the fraudulent scheme.  Yau also argues that the evidence does not identify which of the plaintiffs paid Yau for her services. 

 

The UCL prohibits business practices that are “unlawful,” “unfair,” or “fraudulent.”  (Bus. & Prof. Code §17200.)  To prevail under the UCL, a plaintiff must show: (1) economic injury caused by the challenged practice; (2) a practice that fits one of the three statutory categories; and (3) available equitable relief. Restitution is an available, equitable remedy. (Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310, 322-323.)

 

The court will award Plaintiffs restitution of $4,000 on their UCL claim against Yau.  The evidence presented established that Yau performed work for Plaintiffs through Lighthouse Consultants, Inc. as part of Minghong’s acquisition of the farm.  Further, Yau testified at trial that she received the payments from Plaintiffs.  Thus, it does not matter if Plaintiffs did not identify the specific plaintiff who made the payments.  Viewed in conjunction with the jury’s fraud finding, restitution is therefore available and awarded in equity against Yau.

 

III.      DISCUSSION RE EQUITABLE CLAIMS AGAINST MINGHONG AND YU

 

            Plaintiffs’ equitable claims against Minghong and B Yu consist of the seventh, fourteenth, sixteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth, and twentieth causes of action. 

 

            A.  UCL Claim (7th COA)

 

            1.  Fraudulent Prong

 

Pursuant to the fraudulent prong of the UCL, Plaintiffs seek restitution of $3,164,279.92 for “guaranteed expenses,” that were never actually incurred in whole by Minghong, and injunctive relief in the form of cancellation and reconveyance of the deed of trust and expungement of any related liens for APN 007-010-0811.  

 

Minghong argues restitution should not be awarded on the UCL claim because (1) Plaintiffs’ own damages expert credited Minghong with $2,088,798 in actual expenses incurred in connection to the Consulting Services Agreement (“CSA”) and identified an overpayment of $1,075,482, and (2) the jury already awarded Plaintiffs $1,000,000 for breach of the CSA.  For these reasons, Minghong contends any restitution award would be duplicative of the jury’s award.

 

In reply, Plaintiffs argue they may recover the entire $3 million figure because the CSA conferred no benefit to them.  Naturally, Minghong disputes that position.

 

Based on the evidence presented and the arguments of counsel, the court finds that Plaintiffs are entitled to restitution in the sum of $2,000,000.  The court arrives at this figure by crediting Plaintiffs’ evidence showing they received little benefit from the CSA.  (See In re Tobacco Cases II (2015) 240 Cal.App.4th 779, 795.)  The court declines to award the full requested amount of $3,164,279.92 in consideration of the jury’s verdict awarding $1,000,000 for breach of the CSA.  Further, although there is no published opinion holding that cancellation and reconveyance of a deed of trust may be ordered in equity, the court concludes that the broad language of the UCL itself warrants such relief in this case.  The UCL states, in relevant part, “Any person who engages, has engaged, or proposes to engage in unfair competition may be enjoined in any court of competent jurisdiction. The court may make such orders or judgments, including the appointment of a receiver, as may be necessary to prevent the use or employment by any person of any practice which constitutes unfair competition, as defined in this chapter, or as may be necessary to restore to any person in interest any money or property, real or personal, which may have been acquired by means of such unfair competition.”  (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17203.)  Cancellation and reconveyance of the deed of trust is warranted here because Plaintiffs executed the deed of trust as part of the sale transaction with Minghong.  Because the jury found in favor of Plaintiffs, including on their claim of fraud in the transaction, Plaintiffs are entitled to the requested injunctive relief.

 

As to Yu, Plaintiffs seek restitution of $300,000 against Yu.  The amount represents the commission Plaintiffs paid to Yu in connection to the sale transaction.  Yu argues the request should be denied because Plaintiffs elected to affirm the sale transaction rather than rescind it (i.e., retain the benefits of $6 million paid under the transaction).  The court previously rejected this argument.  Upon further consideration, the court declines to grant this relief.  Awarding Plaintiffs’ the $300,000 commission paid to Yu for brokering the transaction between Plaintiffs and Minghong would result in a windfall to Plaintiffs because Plaintiffs are retaining the entire $6 million purchase paid by Minghong.  In other words, Yu’s work benefited Plaintiffs.  As such, the court finds that Plaintiffs are not entitled to this restitution award against Yu.

            B.  Failure to Maintain Payroll Records (14th COA)

 

            Plaintiffs seek a total penalty of $1,000.  However, there is no private right of action for violation of Labor Code section 1174.5.  (Noe v. Superior Court (2015) 237 Cal.App.4th 316, 336-340.)  Further, civil penalties under Labor Code section 1174.5 may be awarded only in connection to a PAGA action.  (Id.). The court has already awarded Plaintiffs the requested sums of $500 for Felix Chuo and $1,100 for Stella Chuo in connection to their PAGA claim.  In sum, the court declines to award Plaintiffs the requested relief.

 

C.  Invalid Farm Labor Contract (16th COA)

 

This cause of action provides for the same measure of damages and attorney fees that the Chuos have already been awarded.  The court does not discuss this cause of action further.

 

            D.  Independent Contractor Misclassification (17th COA)

 

The court declines to award civil penalties under Labor Code section 226.8 for the same reasons discussed in connection to the fourteenth cause of action.

 

            E.  Civil Penalties Under PAGA (18th COA)

 

            Plaintiffs seek PAGA penalties of $3,500 for Felix Chuo and $7,700 for Stella Chuo.  Under Labor Code section 2698, PAGA penalties are established on a per pay period basis, with first violations penalized at $100 and subsequent violations penalized at $200.

 

            Based on the briefing, the evidence presented, and the jury’s findings on Plaintiffs Labor Code claims, the court finds an award of $2,400 for Felix Chuo and $4,800 for Stella Chuo is warranted.  The reduction is appropriate because, as Plaintiffs acknowledge, there is no evidence that the Labor Commissioner previously notified Defendants of their Labor Code violations.  Minghong and Yu fail to show the requested penalties should be lowered any further.

 

            F.  Failure to Compensate for All Hours Worked (19th COA)

 

            There is no dispute that this claim is enforceable through Plaintiffs’ PAGA cause of action.  The court does not address this cause of action further.

 

            G.  Conversion (20th COA)

 

            Plaintiffs request that the court order cancellation of the deeds of trust and any related instruments, direct their reconveyance, and expunge any liens arising from the purported loan structure.  Given that the court has awarded this relief in connection to Plaintiffs’ UCL claim, the request is DENIED as MOOT.

MOTION OF COLDWELL BANKER RESIDENTIAL BROKERAGE

COMPANY AND RYAN MANAVI FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT,

OR IN THE ALTERNATIVE, SUMMARY ADJUDICATION

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO VACATE VOID OR VOIDABLE ORDER

SUSTAINING DEFS COLDWELL BANKER'S AND RYAN MANAVI'S

DEMURRERS WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO VACATE VOID OR VOIDABLE ORDER SUSTAINING DEFS SBS TRUST DEED NETWORK'S DEMURRERS WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO DEEM REQUESTS FOR ADMISSIONS,

SET TWO, SERVED ON DEF. PENSCO TRUST COMPANY LLC,

CUSTODIAN FBO SYLLA MCCLELLAN IRA AS ADMITTED

 

Plaintiff Willie McMullen (“Plaintiff”) brings this quiet title action concerning the real property at 2757 S. Mansfield Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90016 (the “Property”) against defendants Pensco Trust Company LLC, Custodian FBO Sylla McClellan IRA, 080000001321 (“Pensco”), SBS Trust Deed Network (“SBS”), Coldwell Banker Residential Brokerage Company (“Coldwell Banker”) and Ryan Manavi (“Manavi”), among others. 

 

Before the court are four motions: (1) Coldwell Banker and Manavi’s Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the Alternative, Summary Adjudication; (2) Plaintiff’s Motion to Vacate Void or Voidable Order Sustaining Defendants Coldwell Banker’s and Ryan Manavi’s Demurrers Without Leave to Amend; (3) Plaintiff’s Motion to Vacate Void or Voidable Order Sustaining Defendant SBS Trust Deed Network’s Demurrers Without Leave to Amend; (4) Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions, Set Two, Served on Defendant Pensco Trust Company LLC, Custodian FBO Sylla McClellan IRA As Admitted. 

 

The court addresses these matters in turn.

 

 

I.         DISCUSSION RE MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

 

            A.  Background

 

On March 13, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Pensco, SBS, Coldwell Banker, Manavi, Mural Media LLC 401K Plan, Mural Media LLC, Lawrence E. Elkins and Norma J. Elkins as Trustees of the Elkins Family Revocable Trust of 2002, Lawrence C. Henig as trustee of the Lawrence C. Henig Revocable Trust Dated 6/21/99, Lida J. Maultsby (“Maultsby”), and others, alleging causes of action for (1) quiet title, (2) cancellation of instrument, (3) unlawful business practices, and (4) negligence. 

 

According to the FAC, in 2016, Maultsby recorded affidavit and quitclaim deeds which transferred ownership of the Property to her, as trustee, after the death of her father.  In 2020, Capital Benefit, Inc. (“Capital)” caused a Deed of Trust to be recorded which lists SBS as trustee.  In 2022, SBS recorded a notice of default and election to sell under Deed of Trust.  In May 2023, the trustee’s sale was scheduled but was ultimately postponed because the SBS auctioneer was personally served with a recorded copy of a lis pendens.  Later that month, SBS recorded a Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale and defendant Mural Media LLC 401k Plan (“Mural Media”) entered into a listing agreement with Coldwell Banker to sell the Property.  In June 2023, SBS recorded a Deed of Reconveyance which reconveyed the Property back to Maultsby.  In 2024, Maultsby transferred to Plaintiff a 10% interest in the Property.  Plaintiff’s main contention appears to be that the foreclosure sale of the Property never took place.

 

On August 27, 2025, Coldwell Banker and Manavi (hereinafter referred to collectively as “Coldwell”) filed a demurrer to the FAC. 

 

On December 11, 2025, the court sustained Coldwell’s demurrer to the third cause of action without leave to amend and overruled the demurrer to the first cause of action.  Accordingly, the first cause of action for quiet title is the only remaining claim against Coldwell.

 

On December 24, 2025, Coldwell filed a verified Answer to the FAC which, in relevant part, disclaims any interest in the Property.

 

Motion for Summary Judgment

 

            On June 12, 2026, Coldwell filed this motion for summary judgment. 

 

            On August 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

            On August 28, 2026, Coldwell filed a reply.

 

            B.  Judicial Notice

 

            Coldwell requests judicial notice of eight court filings from the following cases: 23STCV21442, 23STCV25631, 24STCV21169, and 24STCV26374.

 

            Plaintiff requests judicial notice of forty-six documents. 

 

The court declines to take judicial notice of these documents as they are not relevant to the court’s disposition of this motion. 

 

            C.  Evidentiary Objections

 

            Plaintiff asserts two objections to portions of the declaration of Ryan Manavi, eight objections to Coldwell’s requests for judicial notice, and four objections to portions of the declaration of Nicole A. Docherty and exhibits attached thereto. 

 

            The court declines to rule on these objections as they are not relevant to the court’s disposition of this motion.  (Code Civ. Proc, § 473c, subd. (q).)

 

            D.  Legal Standard

 

The function of a motion for summary judgment or summary adjudication is to allow a determination as to whether an opposing party cannot show evidentiary support for a pleading or claim and to enable an order of summary dismissal without the need for trial.¿(Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843.)¿Code of Civil Procedure section 437c(c) “requires the trial judge to grant summary judgment if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1110, 1119.)¿“The function of the pleadings in a motion for summary judgment is to delimit the scope of the issues; the function of the affidavits or declarations is to disclose whether there is any triable issue of fact within the issues delimited by the pleadings.”¿(Juge v. County of Sacramento (1993) 12 Cal.App.4th 59, 67, citing FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367, 381-382.)¿¿¿¿ 

 

“On a motion for summary judgment, the initial burden is always on the moving party to make a prima facie showing that there are no triable issues of material fact.”¿ (Scalf v. D. B. Log Homes, Inc. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1510, 1519.)¿ A defendant moving for summary judgment or summary adjudication “has met his or her burden of showing that a cause of action has no merit if the party has shown that one or more elements of the cause of action . . . cannot be established, or that there is a complete defense to the cause of action.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(2).)¿ “Once the defendant . . . has met that burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff . . . to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to the cause of action or a defense thereto.”¿ (Id.)¿ “If the plaintiff cannot do so, summary judgment should be granted.”¿ (Avivi v. Centro Medico Urgente Medical Center (2008) 159 Cal.App.4th 463, 467.)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

“When deciding whether to grant summary judgment, the court must consider all of the evidence set forth in the papers (except evidence to which the court has sustained an objection), as well as all reasonable inferences that may be drawn from that evidence, in the light most favorable to the party opposing summary judgment.”¿ (Avivi, 159 Cal.App.4th at p. 467; Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (c).)¿¿¿¿¿ 

            E.  Application

 

Coldwell moves for summary judgment on the sole ground they have disclaimed any interest in the Property.  The motion lacks merit.  “In a quiet title action, a defendant who disclaims an interest in plaintiff's land is not entitled to judgment in his favor. [Citation.] Instead, the judgment should quiet title against the disclaiming defendant.”  (Linthicum v. Butterfield (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 259, 270; see also Bradley Co. v. Ridgeway (1936) 14 Cal.App.2d 326, 336-337 [holding trial court erred in entering judgment on the pleadings as to a defendant who disclaimed interest in property and did not appear at trial; rather, judgment “should have quieted title against him since he was a defendant in the action who had disclaimed any interest in the bonds or in the real property described in the amended complaint.”].)  Rather, the effect of Coldwell’s disclaimer is two-fold: (1) it serves as an evidentiary admission (see, e.g., Maddox v. Herrington (1949) 94 Cal.App.2d 265); (2) Plaintiff cannot recover costs against Coldwell (see, e.g., Water for Citizens of Weed California v. Churchwell White LLP (2023) 88 Cal.App.5th 270, 281–282 quoting Castro v. Barry (1889) 79 Cal. 443, 447 [“The rule may be stated more broadly: A property owner may bring a quiet title action ‘to determine any adverse claim whatever. For if the defendant by his answer disclaims all interest whatever, judgment may, nevertheless, be entered against him, though in such case it must be without costs.’”].)

            F.  Conclusion

 

            The motion for summary judgment is DENIED.

 

II.        DISCUSSION RE MOTIONS TO VACATE

 

            A.  Background

 

On March 13, 2025, Plaintiff filed the FAC against Pensco, SBS, Coldwell Banker, Manavi, and others, alleging causes of action for (1) quiet title, (2) cancellation of instrument, (3) unlawful business practices, and (4) negligence.  The first, third, and fourth causes of action are alleged against SBS.  The first and third causes of action are the only claims alleged against Coldwell Banker and Manavi. 

 

SBS’s Demurrer

 

On July 16, 2025, SBS filed a demurrer to the FAC.

 

On December 11, 2025, the court sustained SBS’s demurrers without leave to amend.

 

Coldwell Banker and Manavi’s Demurrer

 

On August 27, 2025, Coldwell Banker and Manavi (hereinafter referred to collectively as “Coldwell”) filed a demurrer to the FAC. 

 

On December 11, 2025, the court sustained Coldwell’s demurrer to the third cause of action without leave to amend and overruled the demurrer to the first cause of action.

 

            On December 22, 2025, Plaintiff filed this motion for reconsideration of the court’s December 11, 2025, order.

 

            On January 26, 2026, the court denied the motion for reconsideration.

 

            Motions to Vacate

 

            On June 8 and 10, 2026, Plaintiff filed two motions to vacate the court’s December 11, 2025, order sustaining SBS’s demurrer without leave to amend and sustaining Coldwell’s demurrer to the first cause of action without leave to amend. 

           

            On August 28, 2026, SBS filed an opposition.  On the same day, Coldwell filed a separate opposition.

 

            At the time of this writing, no replies had been filed.

 

            The court considers together the motions to vacate.

 

            B.  Judicial Notice

 

            Plaintiff requests judicial notice of forty-six documents.  The request for judicial notice of Exhibits 1-21 and 46, the request is DENIED.  Plaintiff fails to establish the relevance of those exhibits to this motion.  As to Exhibits 22-45, the unopposed request for judicial notice is GRANTED.  (Evid. Code, § 452, subds. (a), (b).) 

 

C.  Analysis

 

The motions lack merit.  “The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).)  “When a trial court has fundamental jurisdiction but fails to act in the manner prescribed, it is said to have acted in excess of its jurisdiction. Because an ordinary act in excess of jurisdiction does not negate a court's fundamental jurisdiction to hear the matter altogether, such a ruling is treated as valid until set aside.  A party may be precluded from seeking to set aside such a ruling because of waiver, estoppel, or the passage of time.  Thus, error in rendering a judgment or order generally falls into two categories: A court can lack fundamental authority over the subject matter, question presented, or party, making its judgment void, or it can merely act in excess of its jurisdiction or defined power, rendering the judgment voidable.”  (Braugh v. Dow (2023) 93 Cal.App.5th 76, 87–88 (cleaned up).)  “Only void judgments and orders may be set aside under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d); voidable judgments or orders may not.”  (People v. Financial Casualty & Surety, Inc. (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 405, 415.)  A

 

            Here, Plaintiff fails to show the court lacked subject matter jurisdiction over the demurrers.  Plaintiff establishes only that the court’s December 11, 2025, order, is, at most, voidable.  This is not grounds to vacate an order pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d).[1]

 

D.  Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s motions to vacate are DENIED.

Proc., § 2033.280(c), emphasis added; see also St. Mary v. Superior Court (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 762, 778.)¿ Here, Pensco served verified responses with objections to the Requests for Admission on June 16, 2026.  (See Glassman Decl., Ex. A.)   

 

C.  Conclusion

 

The motion is MOOT.
