HEARING DATE:  June 26, 2026                 TRIAL DATE: NOT SET

                                                

CASE:                    Adir International, LLC v. Katana Racing, Inc.

 

CASE NO.:             24STCV12225

 

DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:          Defendant Katana Racing, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Adir International, LLC

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

          Katana Racing’s Demurrer to the Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

          Katana is to serve and file its Answer within thirty (30) days of this order.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

BACKGROUND

         

          This is a breach of contract action. Adir alleges that Katana abandoned a rental unit it had subleased.

 

DISCUSSION:

 

Katana Racing demurs to the Complaint in its entirety. The Court overrules the demurrer.

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. (Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228.) In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)

 

Analysis

 

Katana argues that Adir cannot maintain any of its causes of action because the Complaint admits that Adir accepted Katana’s relinquishment of the subject premises. It is true that a lease may ordinarily be terminated by the surrender of the property and the landlord retaking possession. (Rognier v. Harnett (1941) 45 Cal.App.2d 570, 573-74.) This is so even if the landlord takes actual possession after abandonment of the premises and a repudiation of the lease by the tenant. (Id.) However, the issue of whether there has been a surrender and an unqualified acceptance of possession are questions of fact to be determined from the whole transaction. (Id. at 572.)

 

Katana contends that the Complaint admits that Adir accepted the keys from Katana on January 27, 2024, (Complaint ¶ 36) and thereafter re-entered the premises and began removing a partition that had been erected. (Complaint ¶¶ 62-63.)

 

Katana ignores that Adir also alleges that the keys were accepted by Adir’s weekend staff by mistake, and that, when the mistake was discovered, Adir instructed its staff to immediately halt work. (¶¶ 36-37.) Put differently, Adir alleges that it did not exercise unqualified acceptance of the premises.

 

There is thus, accepting the allegations of the Complaint as true and construing them in Adair’s favor, a factual dispute about whether there was a surrender and an unqualified acceptance of possession.  That is enough to require the Court to overrule the demurrer.

 

Meet and Confer

 

Before filing a demurrer, the demurring party shall meet and confer in person or by telephone or video conference with the party who has filed the pleading subject to the demurrer and file a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41(a).) However, an insufficient meet and confer process is not grounds to overrule or sustain a demurrer.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41(a)(4).)

 

Counsel for the parties met and conferred telephonically on July 26, 2024, but could not resolve this dispute. (Declaration of Armen Hairapetian ISO Dem. ¶ 8.) This is sufficient.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

          Accordingly, Katana Racing’s Demurrer to the Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

          Katana is to serve and file its Answer within thirty (30) days of this order.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 26STCV05174    Hearing Date: June 26, 2026    Dept: 507

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Nicholas F. Daum, Department 507

 

 

HEARING DATE:  June 26, 2026                 TRIAL DATE: NOT SET

                                                

CASE:                    City of Inglewood v. Inglewood Center, L.P., et al.

 

CASE NO.:             26STCV05174

 

MOTION FOR PREJUDGMENT POSSESSION

 

MOVING PARTY:          Plaintiff City of Inglewood

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendants Nina Wong, EM Art, Inc. d/b/a Inglewood Beauty Salon and Inglewood Beauty Supply & Wig, and Advance America, Cash Advance Centers of California, LLC.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The City of Inglewood’s Motion for Prejudgment Possession is GRANTED.

 

Moving Party to give notice.

 

BACKGROUND

         

          This is an eminent domain action. The City of Inglewood seeks to acquire the real properties located at 236 and 240 North Market Street in Inglewood, California to construct a Mobility Hub for the Inglewood Transit Connector Project.

 

DISCUSSION:

 

          The City of Inglewood moves for prejudgment possession pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1255.410. The Court grants the motion.

 

Legal Standard

 

          A municipal entity that is a plaintiff in an eminent domain action may, at the time of filing the complaint or any time thereafter prior to entry of judgment, move for an order for possession of the property at issue. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1255.410(a).) The motion must describe the property sought to be possessed, must state the date after which the plaintiff seeks possession, and contain a written notice of the form specified in the statute. (Id.) The motion must be served on the owner and occupants, and must be set for hearing not less than 60 days after service of the notice of motion. (Id. subd.(b).) Any defendant, owner, or occupant may oppose the motion not later than 30 days after service of the motion. The plaintiff may reply to any opposition no later than 15 days before the hearing. (Id. subd.(c).)

 

          Where the motion is timely opposed, the Court may order possession if it finds that (A) the plaintiff is entitled to take the property by eminent domain; (B) the plaintiff has deposited an amount satisfying the requirements of section 1255.010 et seq of the Code of Civil Procedure; (C) there is an overriding need for the plaintiff to possess the property before final judgment and the plaintiff will suffer a substantial hardship if the application is denied or limited; and (D) the hardship that the plaintiff will suffer outweighs any hardship on the defendant or occupant that would be caused by granting the order. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1255.410(d)(2).)

 

Entitlement and Appraisal

 

          The City has demonstrated that it is entitled to exercise the power of eminent domain and has deposited compensation for the appraised value of the property.  

 

          On February 10, 2026, the City of Inglewood’s City Council adopted Resolution No. 26-44, which declared, as relevant here, that:

 

(a) [the] City is a charter city and municipal corporation.

(b) The public interest and necessity require the proposed public transportation project, namely the Inglewood Transit Connector Project, a public use, which includes the development, construction, operation, and maintenance of the mobility hub on the Subject Property (“Project”).

 

(c) The proposed Project is located in the manner that will be most compatible with the greatest public good and the least private injury.

 

(d) The Subject Property sought to be acquired […] is necessary for the proposed Project.

 

(e) The offer required by Government Code section 7267.2 has been made to the Owner(s) of the Subject Property.

 

(f) Environmental review consistent with the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) for the Project has been previously certified by the City Council. Specifically, on April 12, 2022, the City Council adopted the Final Environmental Impact Report, and on April 23, 2025, a Notice of Determination for Phase 1 of the Project, which includes implementation of mobility hubs at key station locations, was received and posted by the Los Angeles County Clerk. The acquisition and, therefore, the requisite environmental review of the Project and the acquisition of the Subject Property have been completed in accordance with CEQA

 

(g) The offer required by Government Code section 7267.2, subdivision (a), together with the accompanying statement and summary of the basis for the amount established as just compensation, was made to the Owner(s) and was in a form and contained all of the factual information required by Government Code section 7267.2, subdivision (a)

 

(h) The notice of intention to adopt the Resolution was given by first class mail to the person whose property is to be acquired by eminent domain, in accordance with Code of Civil Procedure section 1245.235;

 

(i) Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1240.610 and 1240.510, to the extent that the Property is already devoted to a public use, the use to which the Subject Property is to be acquired is a more necessary public use than the use to which the Subject Property is already devoted, or, in alternative, is a compatible public use which will not unreasonably interfere with or impair the continuance of the public use to which the Property is already devoted.

 

(See Complaint Exh. 3.) The adoption of this resolution conclusively establishes that the public interest and necessity require the project, that the project is planned or located in the manner that will be most compatible with the greatest public good and least private injury, and that the property sought to be acquired is necessary for the project. (Gov. Code §§ 1245.250(a); 1240.030 [entitlement to exercise power of eminent domain].) The City is therefore conclusively entitled to exercise the power of eminent domain over the property, and none of the opposing Defendants dispute this finding in their opposition.

 

          The City has also served and filed a Notice of Deposit stating that it has deposited compensation for the subject property in the amount of $6,352,000.00 with the State Treasurer. (Notice of Deposit filed March 10, 2026; see also Summary of Basis for Appraisal Opinion).)

 

Overriding Need and Hardship to City

 

          The City has also demonstrated that it has an overriding need to possess the subject property before final judgment and that it will suffer a substantial hardship if the application is denied.

 

          The City has presented a declaration from engineer Sanjeev Shah, CEO of Simply Strategic Inc, who states that he has been involved in the planning, development, management and implementation of the Inglewood Transit Connector Project. (Declaration of Sanjeev Shah ISO Mot. ¶¶ 1-2.) Shah is responsible for reviewing the designs and plans for the project, overseeing bids and selection process for contractors, assessing scheduling, and obtaining funding. (¶ 3.)

 

Based on his experience and personal involvement with the project, Shah states that the City is acquiring the property located at 240 N. Market Street, Inglewood, CA to be redeveloped into a “Mobility Hub” to connect bus services, passenger mobility and transit services, a pedestrian bridge to the K Line, and neighboring parking structures for the 2028 Olympic and Paralympic Games, while preserving space for the implementation of an automated people mover in a future phase of the project. (¶ 8.) Shah states that the City cannot proceed with construction until it is in possession of all of the properties, because possession of the properties is a prerequisite to the required “right-of-way” certification. (¶ 11.) Shah also declares that the City must turn over the property to its contractors no later than October 21, 2026 to remain on schedule and maintain compliance with necessary grant funding for the project. (¶¶ 12-14.) Shah states that if the City is delayed in taking possession of the property, the resulting delays will cause the City to incur additional mobilization and demobilization fees for construction workers, expose the City to construction delay claims, jeopardize the Project’s funding, and would endanger the timely completion of the project. (¶¶ 18-20.)

 

Defendants argue in opposition that the Shah declaration is deficient because it relies purely on Shah’s own statements and not any supporting documentation. The Court disagrees. Almost all of Shah’s testimony is grounded in his personal knowledge of the relevant facts due to his involvement with the Project.  No documentary evidence is required for him to speak as to his own knowledge. (Evid. Code § 800.) Further, to the extent his testimony is grounded in technical matters beyond common experience, he is entitled to offer opinion based on his special knowledge, skill, experience, training, and education, and his personal involvement. (See Evid. Code § 801.) Defendants’ assertions to the contrary are not persuasive. Nor do their cited authorities detract from this conclusion, as none of the opinions offered concern expert testimony based on a witness’s personal involvement with the underlying facts. (See Kelley v. Trunk (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 519, 523-24; Bushling v. Fremont Medical Center (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 493, 510.)

 

The City has offered competent, admissible evidence demonstrating that it has an overriding need to possess the property before final judgment and will suffer a substantial hardship if the motion is denied.

 

Balance of Hardships

 

          Advance America and EM Art argue that their claimed hardships outweigh the hardship which the City would suffer. The Court is not persuaded that the threat of disruption to their respective businesses outweighs the hardship to the City from the disruption of the Project. Wong does not oppose the motion on a balance-of-hardships basis.

 

          Advance America contends that, although it has leased a replacement site to maintain its business, it has not yet obtained a Special Use Permit for the premises. (Declaration of William Lancaster ISO Opp. ¶¶ 6-9.) Advance America estimates that another one to two months will be required to complete buildout of the new premises once the permit is obtained. (¶ 10). Advance America contends that if it is displaced before the process is completed, it will be forced to suspend operations, costing it customer goodwill and revenue and forcing it to lay off employees. (¶ 11.) EM Art offers similar contentions, stating that it has not yet been able to secure a replacement site because of the substantial plumbing and electrical infrastructure that is required to maintain its business (a hair salon). (Declaration of Sam Hyun Chung ISO Opp. ¶¶ 3-5.) EM Art states that if the motion is granted, it will have to place its equipment into storage, and that this will cause EM Art to lose customers and income. (¶¶ 7-8.)

 

          Although the Court empathizes with these Defendants, their potential hardships, while significant, do not outweigh the risk to the City and to public infrastructure if the Project is halted. The needs of an individual business—or two, in this case—do not overcome the needs of their City as a whole to develop its central transit infrastructure, particularly where major international gatherings will require significant transit improvements in the coming years.

 

Put more directly, construction delay is, by itself, harm to public infrastructure.  Each day the public cannot benefit from useful transit is a loss to the public.  Substantial delay in the construction of this transit project is substantial public harm.  While the Court sympathizes with the interests of the Defendants here, the relevant test requires the Court to balance the generalized interest of the public against the particularized property interests of private individuals.  Here, the need of both Inglewood and visitors to Inglewood for appropriate transit outweighs the harm to Defendants’ private interests.  The Court therefore grants the motion.

Reply Evidence

 

          The City attempts to bolster its reply papers by supplementing them with a declaration addressing the City’s assistance in the relocation of EM Art and Advance America. These Defendants object to the consideration of that declaration as improper reply evidence. The Court disagrees.  The reply declarations respond directly and precisely to evidentiary issues raised by Defendants in opposition. Accordingly, Defendants' objection to the reply evidence is OVERRULED.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

Accordingly, The City of Inglewood’s Motion for Prejudgment Possession is GRANTED.
