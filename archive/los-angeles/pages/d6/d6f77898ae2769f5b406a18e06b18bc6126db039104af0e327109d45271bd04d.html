Do not click on the email address, either copy and paste it or type it into your email.  Include the word "SUBMITS" in all caps and the Case Number in the Subject line.  In the body of the email, please provide the date and time of the hearing, your name, your contact information, the party you represent, and whether that party is a plaintiff, defendant, cross-complainant, cross-defendant, claimant, intervenor, or non-party, etc.


            Please be advised that if you submit on the tentative and elect not to appear at the hearing, the opposing party may still appear at the hearing and argue the matter, and the court could change its tentative based upon the argument.  Unless you receive a submission from all other parties in the matter, you should assume that others might appear at the hearing to argue.  If you submit, but still intend to appear, include the words "SUBMITS, BUT WILL APPEAR" in the Subject line.    


            If you elect to argue your matter, you are urged to do so remotely, via Court-Connect. If the moving party fails to appear and/or submit to the Court’s tentative ruling, the Court will take the  matter off calendar.
                          
            Note that once the Court has issued a tentative, the Court has the inherent authority not to allow the withdrawal of a motion and to adopt the tentative ruling as the order of the court.   
 

            If you submitted a courtesy copy of your papers containing media (such as a DVD or thumb drive), unless you request the return of the media in your papers, the court will destroy it following the hearing of your matter.

DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

WILLIAM MARSHALL, 

 

         vs.

 

MARCO FERNO, et al.

 Case No.: 23STCV05502

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  August 5, 2026

 

Defendant Farhad Yaghoubi’s unopposed renewed motion for terminating sanctions is granted.  Pro per Plaintiffs Clarence Tate’s and William Marshall’s Fourth Amended Complaint is dismissed in this matter pursuant to C.C.P. §§20330.290(c), 2031.300(c), and 2023.030(d)(3).

Defendant Farhad Yaghoubi’s request for issue and evidentiary sanctions is denied as moot.

Defendant Farhad Yaghoubi’s request for monetary sanctions against pro per Plaintiffs Clarence Tate and William Marshall is denied.

Defendant Farhad Yaghoubi’s request to find pro per Plaintiffs Clarence Tate and William Marshall in contempt of court is denied.

  Defendant Farhad Yaghoubi’s motion to deem Request for Admissions (Set One) admitted against pro per Plaintiff Clarence Tate is denied as moot.

 

Defendant Farhad Yaghoubi [erroneously sued as Marco Ferno] (“Yaghoubi”) (“Moving Defendant”) moves unopposed for a renewed motion for terminating sanctions, evidentiary sanctions, and issue sanctions against pro per Plaintiffs William Marshall (“Marshall”) and Clarence Tate (“Tate”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”).  (Notice Motion, pg. i; C.C.P. §§2030.290(c), 2031.300(c).) 

Moving Defendant moves for this Court to award additional monetary sanctions against Plaintiffs in the amount of $1,500.00.  (Notice Motion, pg. i; C.C.P. §§177.5, 178.)

Moving Defendant moves for this Court to find Plaintiffs in contempt of court for disobeying this Court’s July 22, 2025, and April 13, 2026, orders.  (Notice Motion, pg. i.)  Moving Defendant also moves for any other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper, on the grounds that Plaintiffs have failed to comply with two of the Court’s prior sanctions order dated July 22, 2025, and April 13, 2026, requiring payment of sanctions and ordering Plaintiffs to serve verified discovery responses to Defendant.  (Notice Motion, pg. i; C.C.P. §§2023.010, 2023.030, 128.5, 177.5.)

          Moving Defendant moves to deem Requests for Admissions (Set One) (“RFA 1”) directed to Plaintiff Tate as conclusively established against Plaintiff Tate.  (Notice Motion, pg. i; C.C.P. §2033.280.)[1]

 

Background

          On July 22, 2025, the Court granted Moving Defendant’s unopposed motions to compel Plaintiffs to provide verified responses without objections to Form Interrogatories (Set One) (“FROG”), Special Interrogatories (Set One) (“SROG”), and Request for Production (Set One”) (“RFP”) within 30 days of the ruling, and granted sanctions against each Plaintiff in the amount of $882.50.  (7/22/25 Minute Order, pg. 2 of 3.)

On February 25, 2025, Moving Defendant filed an initial motion for terminating sanctions and associated relief against Plaintiffs before the formerly numbered Department 54 (now Department 512), which was denied without prejudice on April 13, 2026, as to the request for terminating, evidentiary and issue sanctions; granted as to Moving Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions; and denied as to Moving Defendant’s motion to deem RFAs admitted, with the Court ordering Plaintiffs to serve verified responses, without objection within 15 days of the ruling.  (See 4/13/26 Minute Order.)  With regard to the request for terminating sanctions, the Court stated, “[t]he Court will allow Plaintiffs one more opportunity to comply with their discovery obligations. If Plaintiffs again fail to comply, Defendant may again seek such sanctions.”  (See 4/13/26 Minute Order at pg. 3 of 4.)

          On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff Tate filed a notice of bankruptcy stay as to both Plaintiffs.  (5/8/26 Notice of Stay of Proceedings.)  This Court takes judicial notice of the docket in In re: Clarence Tate, C.D. Cal. Bankruptcy Court, Case No. 2:26-bk-12993-DS, which provides that only Plaintiff Tate was properly before the C.D. Cal. Bankruptcy Court, and the stay only pertained to him.  The Court also takes judicial notice of the docket in In re: Clarence Tate, which provides that the case was terminated on July 1, 2026.  Therefore, there is no existing bankruptcy stay in this matter.

Moving Defendant filed the instant renewed motion on May 28, 2026.  As of the date of this hearing no opposition has been filed.  Moving Defendant filed a reply on July 29, 2026, noting that no opposition had been filed.

          On May 29, 2026, Plaintiffs requested, and on June 1, 2026, were granted peremptory challenges to Judge Karine Mkrtchyan in Department 512.  (See 6/1/26 Court Order Re: Peremptory Challenges.)

          On June 1, 2026, the instant case was transferred from Department 512 to Department 729.

          On June 30, 2026, this Court ruled on Moving Defendant’s requests to deem Requests for Admissions (Set Two) (“RFA 2”) admitted as to each Plaintiff and awarded sanctions for Moving Defendant against Plaintiffs in the amount of $882.50, with sanctions due by August 1, 2026.  (See 6/30/26 Minute Order.)  The Court also granted Moving Defendant’s motion to compel the deposition of Plaintiff Tate and ordered sanctions against Plaintiff Tate in the amount of $2,365.00, due by August 1, 2026.  (See 6/30/26 Minute Order.)

 

Terminating Sanctions

The Court, “after notice to any affected party . . . and after opportunity for hearing,” may impose terminating and/or monetary sanctions for misuses of the discovery process.  (C.C.P. §§2023.030(a), (d).)  Misuses of the discovery process include failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery and disobeying a court order to provide discovery.  (C.C.P. §§2023.010(d), (g).) 

“A decision to order terminating sanctions should not be made lightly. But where a violation is willful, preceded by a history of abuse, and the evidence shows that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with the discovery rules, the trial court is justified in imposing the ultimate sanction.”  (Mileikowsky v. Tenet Healthsystem (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 262, 279-280.)  When deciding whether to impose terminating sanctions, courts generally weigh three factors: (1) whether the party subject to the sanction acted willfully, (2) the detriment to the party seeking discovery, and (3) the number of formal and informal unsuccessful attempts to obtain discovery.  (Creed-21 v. City of Wildomar (2017) 18 Cal.App.5th 690, 702; Lang v. Hochman (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 1225, 1246.)  Nonmonetary sanctions require evidence of willful violations of discovery orders or a history of egregious abuses of discovery.  (R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486.)

          Here, there is a history of abuse of the discovery process that justifies this Court granting terminating sanctions.  The Court previously attempted lesser corrective measures through monetary sanctions.  Those sanctions failed to secure compliance.  Plaintiffs’ refusal to obey the Court’s July 22, 2025, and April 13, 2026, rulings demonstrate willful non-compliance.  Plaintiffs have failed to produce a single document, including the alleged written lease agreement, which is central to this lawsuit and Moving Defendant’s defense.  Even this Court’s earlier threat of imposing terminating, issue, and evidentiary sanctions was not sufficient to ensure compliance.  (See 4/13/26 Minute Order at pg. 3 of 4.)

          Moving Defendant has also made numerous formal and informal attempts to obtain Code-compliant discovery, all of which have been unsuccessful.  (See Decl. of Oh-Kubisch ¶¶4-16, Exhs. 1-2.) 

Accordingly, Moving Defendant’s motion for terminating sanctions is granted.  Pro per Plaintiffs Clarence Tate’s and William Marshall’s Fourth Amended Complaint is dismissed in this matter pursuant to C.C.P. §§20330.290(c), 2031.300(c), and 2023.030(d)(3).

 

Issue and Evidentiary Sanctions

In light of the Court’s ruling on Moving Defendant’s request for terminating sanctions, Moving Defendant’s request for issue and evidentiary sanctions is denied as moot.

 

Monetary Sanctions

Moving Defendant requests monetary sanctions against Plaintiffs pursuant to C.C.P. §177.5 in the amount of $1,500.00, payable to the Court.  In light of the fact that monetary sanctions appear to have had no effect on ensuring Plaintiffs’ compliance with this Court’s ruling, Moving Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is denied.[2] 

 

Contempt Sanctions

          Moving Defendant requests contempt sanctions against Plaintiffs pursuant to C.C.P. §178.  (See Motion, pgs. 6-7.)  “A judge has a duty to exercise the power [to punish for contempt] to protect the integrity of the court and the judicial process [citation], but he must do so ‘with great caution, lest [he] stifle the freedom of thought and speech so necessary to a fair trial under our adversary system.’” (Moore v. Superior Court (2020) 57 Cal.App.5th 441, 458, internal citation omitted.)  The Court declines to exercise its discretion to order a hearing on contempt sanctions.

          Accordingly, Moving Defendant’s request for contempt sanctions is denied.

 

          Motion to Deem Request for Admissions (Set One) Admitted

          In light of the Court’s ruling on Moving Defendant’s motion for terminating sanctions, Moving Defendant’s motion to Deem RFA 1 admitted against Plaintiff Tate is denied as moot.

 

          Conclusion

Moving Defendant’s unopposed motion for terminating sanctions is granted.

Moving Defendant’s motion in the alternative for issue and evidentiary sanctions is denied as moot.

Moving Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions is denied.

Moving Defendant’s request for contempt sanctions is denied.

Moving Defendant’s motion to deem RFA 1 admitted against Plaintiff Tate is denied as moot.

Moving Party to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV34740    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 729

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

KAREN CAMARGO, 

 

         vs.

 

FORD MOTOR COMPANY, et al.

 Case No.:  24STCV34740

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  August 5, 2026

 

Defendant Ford Motor Company’s demurrer to Plaintiff Karen Camargo’s Second Amended Complaint is sustained as to the 6th cause of action without leave to amend. 

 

Defendant Ford Motor Company (“Ford”) (“Moving Defendant”) demurs to Plaintiff Karen Camargo’s (“Camargo”) (“Plaintiff”) second amended complaint (“SAC”) as to the 6th cause of action.  (Notice of Demurrer, pg. 2.)

 

Background

Plaintiff filed her initial Complaint on December 31, 2024, against Moving Defendant and Non-moving Defendant Woodland Ford (“Woodland”) (collectively, “Defendants”).  (See Complaint.) 

On July 16, 2025, Plaintiff filed her first amended complaint (“FAC”) alleging six causes action against Defendants: (1) violation of Civil Code §1793.2(d) [against Ford]; (2) violation of Civil Code §1793.2(b) [against Ford]; (3) violation of Civil Code §1793.2(a)(3) [against Ford]; (4) breach of implied warranty of merchantability (Civil Code §§1791.1, 1794, 1795.5) [against Ford]; (5) negligent repair [against Woodland]; and (6) fraudulent inducement- concealment [against Ford].

On January 12, 2026, this Court ruled on Defendants’ demurrer to the FAC, overruling the demurrer to the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 5th causes of action and sustaining with 20 days leave to amend as to the 6th cause of action.  (See 1/12/26 Minute Order.)

On February 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative SAC, alleging the same six causes of action.[1]

Moving Defendant filed the instant demurrer on April 8, 2026.  Plaintiff filed her opposition on July 23, 2026.  Moving Defendant filed its reply on July 29, 2026.

 

Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, the moving party must meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading to attempt to reach an agreement that would resolve the objections to the pleading and obviate the need for filing the demurrer.  (C.C.P. §430.41, emphasis added.)

Moving Defendant’s counsel declares that on April 7, 2026, he met and conferred with Plaintiff’s counsel via video conference, and the parties were unable to resolve the issues presented by this demurrer.  (See Decl. of Liu ¶3.)  Moving Defendant’s counsel’s declaration is sufficient under C.C.P. §430.41.  Accordingly, the Court will consider Moving Defendant’s demurrer.

 

Summary of Demurrer

Moving Defendant demurs to the 6th cause of action on the basis it fails to state a cause of action.  (Notice Demurrer, pg. 2; C.C.P. §430.10(e).) 

 

Legal Standard

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.)  A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable.  (See Donabedian v. Mercury Insurance Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].)  For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law.  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital District (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

Fraudulent Concealment (6th COA)

The required elements for fraudulent concealment are: “(1) concealment or suppression of a material fact; (2) by a defendant with a duty to disclose the fact to the plaintiff; (3) the defendant intended to defraud the plaintiff by intentionally concealing or suppressing the fact; (4) the plaintiff was unaware of the fact and would not have acted as he or she did if he or she had known of the concealed or suppressed fact; and (5) plaintiff sustained damage as a result of the concealment or suppression of the fact.”  (See Bank of America Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 862, 870; Civ. Code §1710(3); CACI 1901.)  The rule of specificity of pleading is only intended for affirmative fraud cases not fraud by concealment.  (See Alfaro v. Community Housing Improvement Systems & Planning Association, Inc. (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 1356, 1384.)

Plaintiff alleges that she experienced defects in the Subject Vehicle including, but not limited to, check engine lights and navigation screen glitches.  (SAC ¶16.)

Plaintiff alleges that prior to purchasing the Subject Vehicle, Moving Defendant was well aware and knew that the 6-speed Transmission installed on the Vehicle was defective but failed to disclose this fact to Plaintiff prior to and at the time of sale and thereafter.  (SAC ¶63.)

Plaintiff alleges Moving Defendant knew that the 6-speed Transmission had one or more defects that can result in (1) hesitation or delayed acceleration, (2) harsh or hard shifting, (3) jerking, (4) shuddering, or juddering; (5) surging and/or inability to control the vehicle’s speed, acceleration, or deceleration, (6) symptoms requiring reprogramming of the transmission control module (“TCM”) and/or powertrain control module (“PCM”), (7) failure or replacement of the transmission (“Transmission Defect”).  (SAC ¶64.)  Plaintiff alleges that these conditions present a safety hazard and are unreasonably dangerous to consumers because they can suddenly and unexpectedly cause the driver to be unable to control the speed and acceleration/deceleration of the vehicle.  (SAC ¶64.)  Plaintiff alleges such unexpected inability to control the vehicle’s speed and acceleration/deceleration thereby, exposes Plaintiff and passengers (along with other drivers who share the road or garage with Plaintiff) to a serious risk of accident and injury.  (SAC ¶64.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Moving Defendant acquired its knowledge of the Transmission Defect prior to Plaintiff acquiring the Vehicle, through sources not available to consumers such as Plaintiff, including but not limited to pre-production and post-production testing data; early consumer complaints about the Transmission Defect made directly to Moving Defendant and its network of dealers; aggregate warranty data compiled from Moving Defendant’s network of dealers; testing conducted by Moving Defendant in response to these complaints; as well as warranty repair and part replacements data received by Moving Defendant from Moving Defendant’s network of dealers, amongst other sources of internal information.  (SAC ¶65.)

Plaintiff alleges that she is a reasonable consumer who interacted with Moving Defendant’s sales representatives, considered Moving Defendant’s advertisement, and/or other marketing materials concerning Ford Vehicles prior to purchasing Subject Vehicle.  (SAC ¶66.)  Plaintiff alleges that had Moving Defendant and its dealership(s) revealed the Transmission Defect in these disclosures, Plaintiff would have been aware of it and would not have purchased Subject Vehicle.  (SAC ¶66.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that while Moving Defendant knew about the Transmission Defect, and its safety risks since prior to Plaintiff’s purchase of the Subject Vehicle, Moving Defendant and its directors, officers, employees, affiliates, and/or agents nevertheless concealed and failed to disclose the defective nature of the Vehicle, its 6-speed Transmission to Plaintiff prior to and at the time of sale.  (SAC ¶67.)  Plaintiff alleges had Plaintiff known that the Vehicle suffered from the Transmission Defect, Plaintiff would not have purchased the Vehicle.  (SAC ¶67.)  Plaintiff alleges Moving Defendant’s concealment of this safety defect was material and Plaintiff relied on Moving Defendant’s advertising materials which did not disclose the defect.  (SAC ¶67.) 

Plaintiff alleges that prior to the sale of the Vehicle to Plaintiff, Moving Defendant knew that the Vehicle and its 6-speed Transmission suffered from an inherent defect, was defective, would fail prematurely, and was not suitable for its intended use.  (SAC ¶68.) 

Plaintiff alleges Moving Defendant was under a duty to Plaintiff to disclose the defective nature of the Vehicle, its Transmission, its safety consequences and/or the associated repair costs because: (a) Plaintiff is informed, believes and thereon alleges that Moving Defendant acquired its knowledge of the Transmission Defect and its potential consequences prior to Plaintiff acquiring the subject vehicle, through sources not available to consumers such as Plaintiff, including but not limited to pre-production testing data, early consumer complaints about the Transmission Defect made directly to Moving Defendant and its network of dealers, aggregate warranty data compiled from Ford’s network of dealers, testing conducted by Moving Defendant in response to these complaints, as well as warranty repair and part replacements data received by Moving Defendant from its network of dealers, amongst other sources of internal information; (b) Moving Defendant was in a superior position from various internal sources to know (or should have known) the true state of facts about the material defects contained in vehicles equipped with the 6-speed Transmission; and (c) Plaintiff could not reasonably have been expected to learn of or discover the Vehicle’s Transmission Defect and its potential consequences until well after Plaintiff purchased the Vehicle.  (SAC ¶69.)

Plaintiff alleges that in failing to disclose the defects in the Vehicle’s 6-speed Transmission, Moving Defendant has knowingly and intentionally concealed material facts and breached its duty not to do so.  (SAC ¶70.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a result of Moving Defendant’s misconduct, Plaintiff has suffered and will continue to suffer actual damages.  (SAC ¶73.)  Plaintiff alleges she was harmed by purchasing a vehicle that she would not have purchased had she known the true facts about the Transmission Defect.  (SAC ¶73.)  Plaintiff alleges that she unknowingly exposed herself to the risk of liability, accident and injury as a result of Moving Defendant’s fraudulent concealment of the Transmission Defect.  (SAC ¶73.)

Plaintiff fails to allege facts demonstrating that Moving Defendant had a duty to disclose to Plaintiff.  (LiMandri v. Judkins (1997) 52 Cal.App.4th 326, 336 [“There are ‘four circumstances in which nondisclosure or concealment may constitute actionable fraud: (1) when the defendant is in a fiduciary relationship with the Plaintiff; (2) when the defendant had exclusive knowledge of material facts not known to the Plaintiff; (3) when the defendant actively conceals a material fact from the Plaintiff; and (4) when the defendant makes partial representations but also suppresses some material facts.’”].)  Plaintiff fails to allege a transactional relationship with Moving Defendant that would invoke a duty on the part of Moving Defendant to disclose to Plaintiff.  (Id. at pgs. 336-337.) 

The SAC does not allege that Plaintiff purchased the Subject Vehicle directly from Moving Defendant.  Therefore, any alleged concealment by Defendant did not arise in a direct transaction between Plaintiff and Moving Defendant.  Nor has Plaintiff pled any specific facts showing that the dealer from which they purchased their vehicle is Plaintiff’s agent or establishing any other circumstance in which a duty to disclose could arise.

Accordingly, Moving Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s 6th cause of action is sustained without leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

Moving Defendant’s demurrer to the 6th cause of action is sustained without leave to amend.

Moving Party to give notice.
