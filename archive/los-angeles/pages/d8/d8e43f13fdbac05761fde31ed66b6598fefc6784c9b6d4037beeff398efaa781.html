(1)   Motion to Compel Plaintiff Michael F. Wright’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with these motions

 

.

BACKGROUND

On May 6, 2026, defendant George W. Carroll (“Defendant”) withdrew his motion to compel plaintiff Michael F. Wright’s responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One.  On May 14, 2026, Defendant withdrew his motion to compel plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright’s deposition.  However, on May 29, 2026, at the request of plaintiffs Michael F. Wright and Priscilla H. Wright, the court ordered the motions restored for the limited purpose of ruling on plaintiffs’ requests for monetary sanctions made in their oppositions.

MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF MICHAEL F. WRIGHT’S RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE

            Plaintiff Michael F. Wright (“M. Wright”) requests Defendant and his attorneys, Salisian Lee LLP, to pay sanctions of $5,162.50 to M. Wright.

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)

            The court finds that sanctions are not warranted because M. Wright’s argument that Defendant violated the law by filing this motion after M. Wright sought a protective order is without merit.

M. Wright argues that Defendant did not withdraw the motion earlier when the court previously continued a motion to deem matters admitted until after the protective order was heard.  However, this is also not a basis for sanctions.

M. Wright also argues he served responses to form interrogatories and Defendant did not withdraw the motion.  However, a moving party is not required to withdraw their motion to compel responses once responses are served.

The court denies M. Wright’s request for monetary sanctions because the court finds that the circumstances presented make the imposition of sanctions unjust.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION OF PLAINTIFF PRISCILLA H. WRIGHT

            Plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright (“P. Wright”) requests Defendant and his attorneys, Salisian Lee LLP, to pay sanctions in the amount of $5,705.50.

            A party must obtain an order to depose a person in a location that is neither (1) within 75 miles of the deponent’s residence, nor (2) within the county where the action is pending and within 150 miles of the deponent’s residence.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.260., subd. (a); Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.250., subd. (a).)  Defendant’s amended notice of deposition required P. Wright to appear for deposition in person in Los Angeles and merely states that Defendant “reserves the right... to record the testimony by audio and/or video technology,” without an order from the court to appear.  (Mateescu Decl., Exh. Q.)  The court finds that Defendant’s amended deposition notice (without an order from the court) was invalid because P. Wright’s residence is in Mississippi.  (P. Wright Decl., ¶ 2.)

The court therefore grants P. Wright’s request for monetary sanctions against Defendant pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010, subdivisions (b) and (h), and 2023.030, subdivision (a).  The court finds that $2,080.00 (3 hours x attorney Rick Augustini’s $685 hourly rate = $2,055 attorney’s fees + $25 e-filing fee) is a reasonable amount of sanctions to impose against Defendant for the attorney’s fees and costs plaintiff P. Wright has incurred in opposing this motion.

ORDER

            The court denies plaintiff Michael F. Wright’s request for monetary sanctions.

            The court grants plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright’s request for monetary sanctions.

            The court orders defendant George W. Caroll to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright in the amount of $2,080.00 within 30 days of the date of service of this order.

           

 

 

 

 

The court orders plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiffs TaxAnxiety.com Inc and Rick Cordell, Trustee for Rosenberg-Cordell Trust

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

Motion for Order Authorizing Service of Summons by Electronic Means

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs TaxAnxiety.com Inc. and Rick Cordell, Trustee for Roseberg-Cordell Trust (“Plaintiffs”) move for an order authorizing service of the summons and complaint upon defendant Ishaan Dalal (“Defendant”) by electronic means, specifically by electronic mail to ish.aand@gmail.com.

“[I]f a plaintiff, despite exercising reasonable diligence, has been unable to effect service of the summons by any of the methods authorized under this chapter, the court in which the action is pending may, upon motion, direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the party to be served, including by electronic mail or other electronic technology, and that proof of such service be made as prescribed by the court.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(1).)  “A plaintiff seeking to establish reasonable diligence under this section shall set forth facts detailing all attempts to serve the defendant by each of the methods prescribed by statute, including facts demonstrating why each method was unsuccessful at every address or location where the defendant is likely to be found.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(2).)

The court finds that Plaintiffs have established reasonable diligence because (1) Plaintiffs attempted personal service on eight separate occasions between March 27, 2026 and April 24, 2026, at Defendant’s known residence (Chuah Decl., ¶ 4, Ex. A), (2) Plaintiffs were unable to serve Defendant by substitute service because, on the eight separate occasions that personal service was attempted, no one answered the door at Defendant’s residence (Chuah Decl., ¶ 5), (3) on March 12, 2026, Plaintiffs served the summons and complaint on Defendant by mail with the notice and acknowledgment of receipt addressed to Defendant at his residence, but Defendant did not return a signed acknowledgment of receipt and thus Plaintiffs were not able to complete service by mail with acknowledgement.  (Chuah Decl., ¶ 6.)  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 415.10, 415.20, 415.30.)   Plaintiffs have also shown they conducted skip-trace and investigative efforts to locate any additional address or whereabouts of Defendant, but those efforts confirmed that Defendant is only associated with his known residence which is the same address as his address of record on his court filings.  (Chuah Decl., ¶ 7.)  The court finds that electronic service is reasonably calculated to give actual notice because that is the email address Defendant used to serve documents on Plaintiffs in this action and that is the email address Defendant has stated on his previous court filings.  (Chuah Decl., ¶ 8; 12/09/24 Motion to Quash; 07/11/25 Motion to Quash.) 

The court grants Plaintiffs’ motion for an order authorizing service of summons by electronic means.

 

 

 

ORDER

            The court grants plaintiffs TaxAnxiety.com Inc and Rick Cordell, Trustee for Rosenberg-Cordell Trust’s motion for order authorizing service of summons by electronic means.

            The court orders that plaintiffs TaxAnxiety.com Inc and Rick Cordell, Trustee for Rosenberg-Cordell Trust may serve summons and complaint, and other documents, on defendant Ishaan Dalal by electronic mail at the following email address:  ish.aand@gmail.com.

            The court orders plaintiffs TaxAnxiety.com Inc and Rick Cordell, Trustee for Rosenberg-Cordell Trust to give notice of this ruling.    

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Jiezhong Li  

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       No opposition filed

Motion to Vacate Order of Dismissal

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Jiezhong Li (“Plaintiff”) moves for an order vacating the order of dismissal entered on June 2, 2026, restoring this action to the active civil docket, and a new case management conference.  Plaintiff did not file a proof of service showing that the motion was served on defendant Los Angeles County Department of Public Social Service (DPSS) (“Defendant”) as required by California Rules of Court rule 3.1300.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300 [“Proof of service of the moving papers must be filed no later than five court days before the time appointed for the hearing”].)  Defendant did not file an opposition or response to the motion.    

The court therefore denies Plaintiff’s motion to vacate order of dismissal.

ORDER

            The court denies plaintiff Jiezhong Li’s motion to vacate order of dismissal.

            The court directs the clerk to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
