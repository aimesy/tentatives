DEPARTMENT S25 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

Imed Hsini v. Mohsen Ghariani, et al.
LASC Case No. 24LBCV00194
Hearing Date: September 11, 2024
Request for Default Judgment by Court
 
 
In preparation for the September 11, 2024 default judgment hearing, the court has identified several concerns with Plaintiff’s request: 
 

  1. Request for Entry of Default was entered on May 9, 2024 which is less than 30 days after service of the summons and complaint to Defendant Danielle Canissa and CDW Logistics, LLC. (C.C.P. §587);
 
  1. Plaintiff checked the wrong box and should have checked “Court Judgment” in the “Request For” section on the 1st page. (See 7/29/2024 Request for Entry of Default/Judgment);
 
  1. No DOEs dismissal filed;
 
  1. No attached/adequate and/or authenticated exhibits provided with Plaintiff’s Declaration. Exhibits should demonstrate, e.g. costs for title and insurance for two vehicles, power of attorney, agreement entered into with Defendants to purchase trucks and provide logistic and shipping services to clients;
 
  1. No contractual or statutory basis provided by Plaintiff as to recovery of attorney fees. Also, attorney fees requested is more than what is allowed in a contract for recovery of reasonable attorney fees under Local Rule of the Court, rule 3.214(a);  
 
  1. Prayer for complaint does not specify damages claimed;
 
  1. No notice of punitive damages as required under Code Civ. Proc., § 425.11, subd. (c).  Appears Plaintiff may be waiving punitive damages based upon proposed judgment;
 
  1. Proposed Judgment (UD-100) incorrect calculations as to attorney fees and prejudgment interest calculations.


Case Number: 25LBCV00390    Hearing Date: August 26, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Plaintiff Socorro Sisneros’ Motion to Stay Proceedings  

Moving Party:  Plaintiff Socorro Sisneros (“Plaintiff”)  

Responding Party: Defendant General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) 

 

Tentative Ruling 

 

Plaintiff moves the Court to stay the proceedings pending the Second District Court of Appeal’s resolution of Price v. General Motors (B353231, app. pending) pursuant to the Court’s “inherent power to stay proceedings in the interest of justice and to promote judicial efficiency.”  (Freiberg v. City of Mission Viejo (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1484, 1489.)  Plaintiff states that the appeal of Price v. General Motors will determine a novel legal issue: whether a manufacturer’s post-filing election into the Song-Beverly Act’s new procedural regime can retroactively time-bar a complaint that was already on file when the new statutes of repose and limitations took effect.   

 

Defendant objects to the Exhibits attached to Plaintiff’s declaration submitted in support of the instant motion as no judicial notice has been properly requested.  Defendant states that the power to stay proceedings are allowed “[o]nly in rare circumstances will a litigant in one cause be compelled to stand aside while a litigant in another settles the rule of law that will define the rights of both.”  (Landis v. North American Co. (1936) 299 U.S. 248, 254-255.)  Defendant also notes Plaintiff has not meet their burden of establishing that balance of the equities favors a stay and that no timeline for appeal has been established.   

 

No reply has been received.   

 

Defendant’s objections to Exhibits A-C of the Lister Declaration are sustained.  Plaintiff should have requested judicial notice of the Exhibits.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1306.)   

 

The Court recognizes that Price v. General Motors may affect the Court’s ruling in this action; however, to indefinitely delay this case to wait for Second District Court of Appeal to rule on Price v. General Motors is unjustified and Plaintiff has not met Plaintiff’s burden of establishing that the balance of equities favors a stay.  Plaintiff Socorro Sisneros’ Motion to Stay Proceedings is DENIED.
