DEPARTMENT 6 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25PSCV01285    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 6

Plaintiff Cathay Bank’s Request for Entry of Default Judgment 

Defendant: Davit Danielyan 

TENTATIVE RULING           

Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is GRANTED in the reduced amount of $47,725.71. The Court directs Plaintiff to submit a revised proposed judgment for the Court’s review and signature.           

BACKGROUND           

            This is a lending dispute. On April 10, 2025, plaintiff Cathay Bank (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendant Davit Danielyan aka Davit Null Danielyan and doing business as Danielyan Translation and dba and doing business as [sic] Danielyan Translation Office (Defendant) and Does 1 through 20, alleging causes of action for breach of contract (business loan agreement & promissory note), money lent, and account stated. 

            On March 11, 2026, the court clerk entered default against Defendant. 

            On May 15, 2026, Plaintiff requested entry of default judgment against Defendant. 

            On July 27, 2026, the Court continued the OSC re Entry of Default Judgment to September 17, 2026 to allow Plaintiff to file a supplemental declaration regarding interest by September 7, 2026. Plaintiff did not submit a supplemental declaration.           

LEGAL STANDARD 

Code of Civil Procedure section 585 permits entry of a default judgment after a party has failed to timely respond or appear. (Code Civ. Proc., § 585.) A party seeking judgment on the default by the court must file a Request for Court Judgment and: (1) a brief summary of the case; (2) declarations or other admissible evidence in support of the judgment requested; (3) interest computations as necessary; (4) a memorandum of costs and disbursements; (5) declaration of nonmilitary status; (6) a proposed form of judgment; (7) a dismissal of all parties against whom judgment is not sought or an application for separate judgment under Code of Civil Procedure section 579, supported by a showing of grounds for each judgment; (8) exhibits as necessary; and (9) a request for attorneys’ fees if allowed by statute or by the agreement of the parties. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1800, subd. (a).) 

ANALYSIS 

Plaintiff seeks default judgment against Defendant in the total amount of $64,353.59, including $45,000.00 in damages, $16,627.88 in interest, $1,740.00 in attorney fees, and $985.71 in costs. The Court finds Plaintiff’s judgment request largely in order, except that the evidence submitted does not support the amount of interest requested. Exhibit 3 to Jose Ramirez’s declaration does not show $16,627.88 in interest. (Ramirez Decl., Ex. 3; CIV-100, ¶ 2, subd. (c).) Jose Ramirez’s declaration indicates a lesser amount of $11,027.88 in interest, which also is not supported by the evidence submitted. (Ramirez Decl., ¶ 11, Ex. 3.) The total amount of interest shown on Exhibit 3 of Jose Ramirez’s declaration is $0.00. (Ramirez Decl., Ex. 3.) Plaintiff was given an opportunity to submit a supplement declaration in support of the interest sought. Plaintiff did not file a supplemental declaration. Accordingly, the Court will award no interest, and will instead award judgment in the amount of $47,725.71, including $45,000.00 in damages, $1,740.00 in attorney fees, and $985.71 in costs. 

CONCLUSION           

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is $47,725.71. The Court directs Plaintiff to submit a revised proposed judgment for the Court’s review and signature.

Case Number: 25PSCV04367    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  LDJ Partners v. Gary D. Delgado, et al. 

1.      Defendant Gary Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void; and

2.      Defendant Rosa Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void and/or Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment Due to Surprise and/or Excusable Neglect 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court DENIES Defendant Gary Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void. 

The Court DENIES Defendant Rosa Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void and/or Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment Due to Surprise and/or Excusable Neglect. 

However, if the parties consent to eliminate the treble damages award from the default judgment, the Court will modify the monetary damages portion of the June 26, 2026 default judgment to $0.00 as to Defendants Gary Delgado and Rosa Delgado. 

             Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of the Court’s rulings within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is a lending dispute. On December 5, 2025, plaintiff LDJ Partners (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendants Gary D. Delgado (Gary), Rosa A. Delgado (Rosa), Tierra Rose, LLC (Tierra Rose), Education Consortium, LLC (Education Consortium), Andrew Dean Delgado (Andrew) (collectively, Defendants), and Does 1 through 50, alleging causes of action for violation of the Uniform Voidable Transfer Act, fraud – concealment, violation of Penal Code 496, and breach of contract – specific performance. On January 23, 2026, Plaintiff dismissed the Second Cause of Action for fraud – concealment. On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff dismissed Andrew without prejudice. 

On June 26, 2026, the Court entered default judgment in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendants Gary, Rosa, and Tierra Rose on the First, Third, and Fourth Causes of Action for violation of the Uniform Voidable Transfer Act, violation of Penal Code 496, and breach of contract – specific performance, respectively. The Court entered default judgment in favor of Defendant Education Consortium and against Plaintiff on all causes of action in the complaint against Education Consortium. 

On August 3, 2026, Gary moved to set aside the default judgment as void and Rosa moved to set aside the default judgment as void and/or vacate the entry of default and default judgment due to surprise and/or excusable neglect. On September 3, 2026, Plaintiff opposed both motions. On September 10, 2026, Gary and Rosa each replied to Plaintiff’s oppositions. 

Defendant Gary Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void 

LEGAL STANDARD – Gary 

“The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken… Notwithstanding any other requirements of this section, the court shall, whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against his or her client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

REQUESTS FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE – Gary 

            The Court GRANTS Gary’s requests for judicial notice as to Exhibits B, C, D, and E. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d).) However, the Court takes judicial notice only as to “the existence, content and authenticity of public records and other specified documents” and the legal effect of the documents’ language when that effect is clear, it does not take judicial notice of the truth of the factual matters asserted in those documents. (Dominguez v. Bonta (2022) 87 Cal.App.5th 389, 400; Fontenot v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 256, 265.) The Court DENIES Gary’s request for judicial notice as to Exhibit A, as the Court is able to review its own docket. 

PRELIMINARY ISSUES – Gary 

            Gary’s motion misquotes the language in Mechanics Bank of Richmond v. Thole (1971) 20 Cal.App.3d 884, 886: “[a] void judgment may be set aside either under statute [C.C.P. § 473(d)] or independent of statute.” (Motion, 14:14-15.) That quotation does not accurately quote the language stated in that case. The Court admonishes Gary’s counsel to ensure case quotations are accurate going forward. 

            Additionally, the Court notes that a number of Gary’s case citations omitted the word “App.”, which created cite checking issues. The Court admonishes Gary’s counsel to ensure case citations are accurate going forward. 

DISCUSSION – Gary 

Summary of Arguments 

Gary contends the default judgment entered June 26, 2026 is void because it awards $359,858.43 in monetary damages, an amount that exceeds the relief demanded in the operative complaint. Gary argues the complaint stated no dollar amount of damages, instead seeking recovery “in an amount to be proven at trial,” and that no statement of damages under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.11 or section 425.115 was served. Gary contends that under Code of Civil Procedure section 580, a default judgment granting relief beyond the amount demanded in the complaint exceeds the court’s jurisdiction and is void as a matter of law. 

In opposition, Plaintiff first offers to resolve the motion by accepting a modification that eliminates the Penal Code section 496 treble-damages award while preserving the non-monetary relief, i.e., the voiding of the fraudulent transfer and restoration of title to Tierra Rose, LLC, which Gary does not challenge. If the Court does not adopt that modification, Plaintiff argues the judgment is not void because a demand for relief “may be made in any part of the complaint, not just in the prayer for relief,” and the body of the complaint (at paragraphs 18 and 36) alleged the unpaid principal balance of exactly $119,952.81. Plaintiffs contends that this pleaded principal, combined with the prayer’s express request for treble damages under section 496, subdivision (c), gave Gary notice that $359,858.43 (three times $119,952.81) could be awarded. Alternatively, Plaintiff argues that if the award exceeds the amount properly pled, the Court should leave the underlying default in place and reduce the judgment to the relief properly pled rather than vacate it. 

In reply, Gary agrees that Plaintiff’s proposed modification of dismissing the Third Cause of Action and/or reducing the monetary award to $0.00 would remedy the issue, but argues it does not moot the motion because that relief is precisely what the motion seeks. On the merits, Gary argues that the $119,952.81 figure in paragraph 18 is only a statement of fact as to the existence of the obligation, not a demand for monetary relief. Relying on Sass v. Cohen, Gary stresses that while demands may appear anywhere in the complaint, “allegations of fact which [happen to] include numbers” do not count as demands for relief. Because the complaint contains no demand stating the amount of damages sought, Delgado argues the monetary judgment was entered in excess of jurisdiction and at least the monetary portion must be set aside. 

Analysis           

            The Court finds Gary’s motion unpersuasive and unavailing. “[A] prayer for damages according to proof passes muster under section 580 only if a specific amount of damages is alleged in the body of the complaint. [Citation.]” (Becker v. S.P.V. Constr. Co. (1980) 27 Cal.3d 489, 494.) Paragraph 18 of the complaint alleges Plaintiff loaned $119,952.81, which it defines as the “Obligation.” (Compl., ¶ 18.) Paragraph 36 of the complaint alleges that at the time of the alleged voidable transfer, Gary and Rosa were obligated to pay a debt of more than $119,952.81, i.e., the same loan amount alleged in paragraph 18, and that a default on that debt had occurred. (Compl., ¶¶ 18, 36.) Paragraph 51 of the complaint alleges, among other things, that “Plaintiff has been damaged in the amount of the value of the unpaid principal balance…” (Compl., ¶ 51.) The Court finds these allegations not just “allegations of fact which include numbers,” but rather part of Plaintiff’s principal damages claim. (Heidary v. Yadollahi (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 857, 866.) Moreover, the prayer for relief expressly prays for treble damages under Penal Code section 496, subdivision (c). (Compl., Prayer for Relief, 14:8-9.) Accordingly, the $359,858.43 amount awarded in the default judgment is simply three times the unpaid principal loan balance of $119,952.81, of which Gary had adequate notice in the complaint. (See Default Judgment (6/26/26).) Furthermore, Plaintiff correctly notes that Gary does not challenge the title transfer portion of the judgment. Accordingly, the Court finds that the default judgment does not exceed the amount alleged in the complaint. 

            Nevertheless, if Gary accepts Plaintiff’s offer to eliminate the treble damage award, the Court will modify the judgment accordingly and reduce the damages portion of the default judgment to $0.00. (See Opp., 2:2-8; Reply, 2:1-2; Phelan v. Superior Ct. in & for City & Cnty. of San Francisco (1950) 35 Cal.2d 363, 373 [“It is well settled, of course, that such an order modifying a prior judgment is proper if made with the consent of the parties. [Citations.]”]; Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 830 [plaintiff given option to accept reduced judgment or file an amended complaint and reopen the default].) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Defendant Gary Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void. However, if the parties’ consent to eliminate the treble damages award from the default judgment, the Court hereby modifies the monetary damages portion of the June 26, 2026 default judgment to $0.00 as to Defendant Gary Delgado.

 

Defendant Rosa Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void and/or Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment Due to Surprise and/or Excusable Neglect 

REQUESTS FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE – Rosa 

            The Court GRANTS Rosa’s requests for judicial notice as to Exhibits B, C, D, E, F, G, and H. (Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d).) However, the Court takes judicial notice only as to “the existence, content and authenticity of public records and other specified documents” and the legal effect of the documents’ language when that effect is clear, it does not take judicial notice of the truth of the factual matters asserted in those documents. (Dominguez v. Bonta (2022) 87 Cal.App.5th 389, 400; Fontenot v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 256, 265.) The Court DENIES Rosa’s request for judicial notice as to Exhibit A, as the Court is able to review its own docket. 

PRELIMINARY ISSUES – Rosa 

            Rosa’s motion misquotes the language in Mechanics Bank of Richmond v. Thole (1971) 20 Cal.App.3d 884, 886: “[a] void judgment may be set aside either under statute [C.C.P. § 473(d)] or independent of statute.” (Motion, 14:14-15.) That quotation does not accurately quote the language stated in that case. The Court admonishes Rosa’s counsel to ensure case quotations are accurate going forward. 

            Additionally, the Court notes that a number of Rosa’s case citations omitted the word “App.”, which created cite checking issues. The Court admonishes Rosa’s counsel to ensure case citations are accurate going forward. 

DISCUSSION – Rosa 

Summary of Arguments 

Rosa Delgado contends the default judgment is void because it awarded $359,858.43 although the complaint demanded no dollar amount and sought damages only “in an amount to be proven at trial,” exceeding the Court’s jurisdiction under Code of Civil Procedure section 580. Rosa further contends the judgment is void for defective service, arguing she was never personally served and that no copy of the summons and complaint for her was left with her husband, so substitute service under section 415.20, subdivision (b), was not completed. In the alternative, Rosa argues the default and judgment should be vacated under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), for surprise and excusable neglect, because she was unaware that service had been legally completed given the multiple defendants. Rosa maintains the motion is timely, both because a void judgment may be attacked at any time and because the motion was filed within six months of the entry of default and of the judgment. Rosa asks the Court to set aside the default and judgment and permit her to respond to the complaint. 

In opposition, Plaintiff states that it would accept a modification eliminating the Penal Code section 496 treble-damages award, provided the Court preserves the remainder of the judgment, i.e., voiding the transfer and restoring title to Tierra Rose, which Rosa does not challenge. Plaintiff argues that if the Court declines that modification, the judgment nonetheless complies with section 580 because the body of the complaint alleged the specific unpaid principal of $119,952.81 and expressly sought treble that sum, giving notice of the calculable $359,858.43 award. On service, Plaintiff argues the registered process server’s return is presumed valid under Evidence Code section 647, that Gary Delgado admits he understood the server intended to serve both spouses and that the single envelope contained two copies of each document, and that the mailing to Rosa is presumed received under Evidence Code section 641. Finally, Plaintiff argues Rosa cannot show excusable neglect because her only excuse is circular, i.e., it depends on accepting her service defect, and because she was charged with notice through papers mailed to her in January, April, and May, she made a tactical decision not to respond. 

In reply, Rosa contends that Plaintiff’s proposed modification does not moot the motion, because it is part of the relief she seeks and would resolve the matter only if the court also rejected her service and section 473, subdivision (b) grounds. Rosa argues the $119,952.81 figure is a factual recital, i.e., “allegations of fact which [happen to] include numbers” rather than a demand for relief, so under Sass v. Cohen the monetary judgment remains void. On service, Rosa argues the Evidence Code section 647 presumption is rebuttable and was rebutted, emphasizing that all agree only one envelope was handed to Gary and that the process server never denies she had a second envelope, and she asks that all photographs and video be produced before the court rules. Rosa maintains her failure to respond was at worst excusable neglect or surprise, that no authority requires a defendant to respond before service is legally completed, and that doubts must be resolved in favor of resolution on the merits, particularly absent any showing of prejudice. Rosa reiterates that the motion is timely, having been filed within six months of the default and about a month after the judgment, and that the standard is reasonable time rather than earliest possible action. 

Analysis           

            The Court finds Rosa’s motion unpersuasive and unavailing. First, for the same reasons set forth above in connection with Gary’s motion to set aside, the Court finds the complaint’s allegations provided Rosa with sufficient notice of Plaintiff’s claimed damages and that therefore the Court did not exceed its jurisdiction in awarding the monetary damages portion of the default judgment. (Compl., ¶¶ 18, 36, 51, Prayer for Relief, 14:8-9; Becker v. S.P.V. Constr. Co. (1980) 27 Cal.3d 489, 494; Default Judgment (6/26/26).) 

            Second, the Court finds substitute service on Rosa was valid. More specifically, the Court finds Gary’s declaration regarding the process server’s service attempt not credible. (Fernandes v. Singh (2017) 16 Cal.App.5th 932, 940, fn. 6 (Fernandes), italics in original [“a declaration of non-service if credited by the trial court can rebut the presumption of proper service... the trial court herein expressly declined to credit [defendant’s] declarations, finding them ‘unpersuasive.’”].) Gary is an interested codefendant with Rosa in this matter, whereas Alma Nava is an uninterested third party and registered process server who provided a meticulously detailed account of the service attempt, including the fact that she provided two copies of the summons and complaint in the same envelope. (See Gary Delgado Decl., ¶ 4; see generally Nava Decl.) Gary’s declaration therefore failed to rebut the presumption of validity afforded to Alma Nava’s return. (See Evid. Code, § 647; Fernandes, supra, 16 Cal.App.5th at p. 940, fn. 6.) Additionally, Rosa’s contention that she did not receive the mailed copy of the summons and complaint does not defeat service. (See Am. Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390 [trial court “not required to accept self-serving evidence contradicting the process server’s declaration”].) 

            Third, while Rosa’s motion is timely since it was filed within six months of the default entry on February 5, 2026, the Court finds Rosa’s excusable neglect arguments unavailing. Given the Court’s finding that substitute service on Rosa was valid, Rosa’s subjective belief service was not valid does not constitute excusable neglect. (Hearn v. Howard (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1193, 1206 [attorney’s “unsupported, subjective belief that she had not been served… failed to demonstrate that the default judgment was entered as [sic] result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. [Citation.]”].) The Court also agrees with Plaintiff that Rosa’s argument is circular, as the purported excuse depends entirely on Rosa accepting the very service that Rosa contends is defective. Rosa offered no other independent excuse for failing to act on the papers mailed to her over the ensuing months. (See generally Rosa Delgado Decl.; see also Stafford v. Mach (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 1174, 1181, 1183 [the law’s preference of resolution on the merits does not override the absence of a valid, diligently pursued excuse].) Accordingly, the Court denies Rosa’s motion.           

            Nevertheless, if the parties agree to eliminate the treble damage award, the Court will modify the judgment accordingly and reduce the damages portion of the default judgment to $0.00. (See Opp., 2:22-26; Reply, 3:17-4:1; Phelan v. Superior Ct. in & for City & Cnty. of San Francisco (1950) 35 Cal.2d 363, 373 [“It is well settled, of course, that such an order modifying a prior judgment is proper if made with the consent of the parties. [Citations.]”]; Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 830 [plaintiff given option to accept reduced judgment or file an amended complaint and reopen the default].) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Defendant Rosa Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void and/or Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment Due to Surprise and/or Excusable Neglect. However, if the parties consent to eliminate the treble damages award from the default judgment, the Court will modify the monetary damages portion of the June 26, 2026 default judgment to $0.00 as to Defendants Gary Delgado and Rosa Delgado. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court DENIES Defendant Gary Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void. 

The Court DENIES Defendant Rosa Delgado’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment as Void and/or Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment Due to Surprise and/or Excusable Neglect. 

However, if the parties consent to eliminate the treble damages award from the default judgment, the Court will modify the monetary damages portion of the June 26, 2026 default judgment to $0.00 as to Defendants Gary Delgado and Rosa Delgado. 

             Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of the Court’s rulings within five calendar days of this order.

Case Number: 26PSCV00134    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  Jose Sanchez, et al. v. County of Los Angeles, et al.

Defendant Jacqueline Nazerau’s Motion for Judgement [sic] on the Pleadings 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court GRANTS Defendant Jacqueline Nazerau’s motion for judgment on the pleadings without leave to amend. The Court directs Nazerau to submit a proposed judgment of dismissal with prejudice as to Nazerau for the Court’s review and signature within five calendar days of the Court’s order. 

             Defendant Jacqueline Nazerau is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is an auto accident case. On January 13, 2026, plaintiffs Jose Sanchez, Darlin Lizbeth Sanchez, and Emma Romero Herrera (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed this action against defendants County of Los Angeles (County), City of Los Angeles (City), Jacqueline Nazerau (Nazerau) (collectively, Defendants), and Does 1-100, alleging causes of action for negligence – vicarious liability, and negligence – motor vehicle. On March 27, 2026, County and Nazerau answered the complaint. On June 9, 2026, Plaintiff dismissed City without prejudice. 

On August 14, 2026, Nazerau moved for judgment on the pleadings. Plaintiff did not oppose the motion. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

A defendant may move for judgment on the pleadings when the “complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subds. (b)(1), (c)(1)(B)(ii).)  

A motion for judgment on the pleadings tests the legal sufficiency of the complaint and is analyzed in all material respects as though it were a demurrer.  (Wise v. Pacific Gas & Electric Co. (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 725, 738; Kapsimallis v. Allstate Insurance Co. (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 667, 672.) 

“A motion for judgment on the pleadings performs the same function as a general demurrer, and hence attacks only defects disclosed on the face of the pleadings or by matters that can be judicially noticed.” (Cloud v. Northrop Grumman Corp. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 995, 999 [citations omitted].) 

DISCUSSION 

Meet and Confer 

Per Code of Civil Procedure section 439, subdivision (a), Nazerau was required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference before bringing this motion for judgment on the pleadings. (Code Civ. Proc., § 439, subd. (a).) Nazerau failed to meet and confer by any of these methods. (Tong Decl., ¶¶ 3-15.) Nevertheless, the Court may not deny a motion for judgment on the pleadings for failure to adequately meet and confer. (Code Civ. Proc., § 439, subd. (a)(4).) The Court admonishes Nazerau to comply with the Code of Civil Procedure going forward. 

Second Cause of Action – Negligence-Motor Vehicle 

To state a cause of action for negligence, the plaintiff must allege facts demonstrating, “(1) the defendant owed the plaintiff a legal duty, (2) the defendant breached the duty, and (3) the breach proximately or legally caused (4) the plaintiff’s damages or injuries. [Citations.]” (Thomas v. Stenberg (2012) 206 Cal.App.4th 654, 662.) “A public employee is not liable for civil damages on account of personal injury to or death of any person or damage to property resulting from the operation, in the line of duty, of an authorized emergency vehicle while responding to an emergency call or when in the immediate pursuit of an actual or suspected violator of the law, or when responding to but not upon returning from a fire alarm or other emergency call.” (Veh. Code, § 17004.) 

Defendant Nazerau moves for judgment on the pleadings and dismissal with prejudice on the ground that Vehicle Code section 17004 immunizes her from personal civil liability for injuries resulting from operation of an authorized emergency vehicle while responding to an emergency call. Nazerau argues that Plaintiffs’ own complaint establishes the immunity, because it alleges she was responding to an emergency call and operating an emergency vehicle at the time of the accident. (Compl., ¶¶ 13, 18.) Relying on Cruz v. Briseno (2000) 22 Cal.4th 568, she contends that allegations of negligent operation do not defeat the statutory immunity, which is not conditioned on the officer’s compliance with emergency-equipment or pursuit procedures. Nazerau further argues, citing Brummett v. Cnty. of Sacramento (1978) 21 Cal.3d 880, that her individual immunity is separate from and independent of any potential liability of the County of Los Angeles, so she must be dismissed even if claims against the County proceed. 

Plaintiffs did not oppose the motion. 

The Court finds the complaint alleges facts showing that Nazerau is immune from liability under Vehicle Code section 17004. The complaint expressly alleges that Nazerau was responding to an emergency call while operating an emergency vehicle in the course and scope of her employment at the time of the accident. (Compl., ¶¶ 12, 13, 18, 20-21.) This triggers section 17004’s immunity for Nazerau. (Veh. Code, § 17004; see Cruz v. Briseno (2000) 22 Cal.4th 568, 572 [officer’s negligent conduct during pursuit did not cause loss of immunity under Vehicle Code section 17004].) 

The Court further construes Plaintiffs’ lack of opposition as a concession that Nazerau’s arguments are meritorious and that no facts can be alleged to remedy the defects identified herein. (D.I. Chadbourne, Inc. v. Superior Court (1964) 60 Cal.2d 723, 728, fn. 4 [where nonmoving party fails to oppose a ground for a motion, “it is assumed that [nonmoving party] concedes” that ground]; Moore v. Centrelake Med. Grp., Inc. (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 515, 537 [plaintiff bears burden of showing how the complaint can be amended to state a cause of action].) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Nazerau’s motion for judgment on the pleadings without leave to amend. The Court directs Nazerau to submit a proposed judgment of dismissal with prejudice as to Nazerau for the Court’s review and signature within five calendar days of the Court’s order. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court GRANTS Defendant Jacqueline Nazerau’s motion for judgment on the pleadings without leave to amend. The Court directs Nazerau to submit a proposed judgment of dismissal with prejudice as to Nazerau for the Court’s review and signature within five calendar days of the Court’s order. 

             Defendant Jacqueline Nazerau is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order.
