ORDER DENYING PETITIONERS’ PETITIONS FOR MINOR’S COMPROMISE

I.               BACKGROUND 

Plaintiffs Jennifer Jordan, Roberto Bolanos, Sophia Quiche, a minor by and through their guardian ad litem, Jennifer Jordan; Jennifer Quiche, a minor by and through their guardian ad litem, Jennifer Jordan; Maria Andrade; Melanie Andrade; Wilfredo Reales; Yuridia Arriaza; Jayden Arriaza, a minor by and through their guardian ad litem, Yuridia Arriaza; Jared Arriaza, a minor by and through their guardian ad litem, Yuridia Arriaza (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed a habitability action against Defendant Steve Sarafian (“Defendant”). Plaintiff allege they were tenants of residential property located at 6670 Aura Ave, Reseda, CA 91335 (hereafter “Subject Property”). Plaintiffs allege the Subject Property suffered from defects including a severe rodent infestation, mold/mildew, and water leaks. Plaintiffs allege they were forced to leave the Subject Property after a wrongful eviction claim brought by Defendant.

Petitioner Yuridia Arriaza (“Petitioner”) filed the instant petitions for minor’s compromise on behalf of Jayden Arriaza and Jared Arriaza.

Petitioner Jennifer Jordan (“Petitioner”) filed the instant petitions for minor’s compromise on behalf of Sophia Quiche and Jennifer Quiche.

II.            PROCEDURAL HISTORY

On April 10, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a Complaint alleging (1) Breach Of Contract; (2) Statutory Breach Of The Warranty Of Habitability (Civil Code §§1941, 1941.1, And 1942.4); (3) Violation Of Civil Code §1942.4; (4) Violation Of Civil Code §1942.5; (5) Tortious Breach Of The Warranty Of Habitability; (6) Violation Of Business & Professions Code §17200, Et Seq; (7) Private Nuisance; (8) Negligence; (9) Breach Of The Covenant Of Quiet Enjoyment; (10) Intentional Infliction Of Emotional Distress; (11) Breach Of The Covenant Of Good Faith & Fair Dealing; (12) Intentional Misrepresentation; (13) Negligent Misrepresentation; (14) Violation Of The Tenant Anti- Harassment Ordinance LAMC 45.33; and (15) Wrongful Retaliatory Eviction In Violation Of Civil Code Section 1942.5.

On June 11, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

On August 19, 2026, Petitioners filed the instant unopposed petitions.

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

An enforceable settlement of a minor’s or incompetent’s claim requires court approval. (See Prob. Code, §§ 2504, 3500, 3600 et seq.; Code Civ. Proc., § 372.) [1]

          Probate Code § 3600, et seq., governs how the settlement proceeds are to be paid. Pursuant to Probate Code § 3601, the order shall approve payment of reasonable expenses from the settlement as follows:¿  

The court making the order or giving the judgment referred to in Section 3600, as a part thereof, shall make a further order authorizing and directing that such reasonable expenses (medical or otherwise and including reimbursement to a parent, guardian, or conservator), costs, and attorney’s fees, as the court shall approve and allow therein, shall be paid from the money or other property to be paid or delivered for the benefit of the minor or incompetent person.

 

(See Prob. Code, § 3601(a).)¿

          In cases pursuant to Probate Code § 3601, “unless the court has approved the fee agreement in advance, the court must use a reasonable fee standard when approving and allowing the amount of attorney’s fees payable from money or property paid or to be paid for the benefit of a minor or a person with a disability.” (See Rules of Court 7.955(a)(1).) In doing so, “[t]he court must give consideration to the terms of any representation agreement made between the attorney and the representative of the minor or person with a disability and must evaluate the agreement based on the facts and circumstances existing at the time the agreement was made, except where the attorney and the representative of the minor or person with a disability contemplated that the attorney's fee would be affected by later events.” (See id., rule 7.955(a)(2).) 

          There are several nonexclusive factors a Court may consider in determining a reasonable attorney’s fee. (See id., rule 7.955(b).) Rule 7.955(c) requires that a petition requesting Court approval and allowance of an attorney’s fee under 7.955(a) must include a declaration from the attorney that addresses the factors listed in 7.955(b) that are applicable to the matter before the Court.

“In any case in which the court orders that funds to be received by a minor or a person with a disability must be deposited in a financial institution and not disbursed without further order of the court, the order must include a provision that a certified or filed endorsed copy of the order must be delivered to a manager at the financial institution where the funds are to be deposited, and that a receipt from the financial institution must be promptly filed with the court, acknowledging receipt of both the funds deposited and the order for deposit of funds.” (See id., rule 7.953(a).)

IV.          ANALYSIS

Because the facts of each petition are substantially similar, the Court will address them together. Claimants Jayden Arriaza, Jared Arriaza, Jennifer Quiche, and Sophia Quiche (collectively, “Claimants”) have agreed to settle their claims against Defendant in exchange for $10,000 each. (See e.g., Jayden Pet. Atttach, 11b(5).)[2] Each petition provides: “The Claimant minor suffered personal injury damages and emotional distress from the alleged substandard living conditions plaintiffs experienced at the subject property.” (See id., ¶ 6.) Each petition provides that no medical treatment or health care was provided to the Claimant. (See id., ¶ 7.)

For each Claimant, ¿$2,500.00 of their settlement portion will be used for attorney fees, leaving a balance of $7,500¿ to be deposited in an insured account at Bank of America Canoga Park Financial Center at 22004 Sherman Way, Canoga Park, California 91303, subject to withdrawal only by authorization of the court. (See id., ¶ 16, 18b(2), Attach 18b(2).) All Claimants filed a proposed order on Form MC-351 and Order to Deposit Money into Blocked Account on Form MC-355.

          Claimants’ counsel, Frank Chica, attaches a copy of the retainer agreement for each Claimant. (See id., Attach. 17(a).) Chica requests recovery of $2,500 from each Claimant, comprising 25% of each Claimant’s settlement portion. (See id., Attach. 13(a).) Chica attests “The attorneys fee is reasonable under all circumstances with California Rules of Court, Rule 7.955(b).” (Id.)

The Court identifies the following defect. Chica’s declaration fails to justify the reasonableness of the fee as he fails to address the factors specified in Rule 7.955(b). (Rules of Court, rule 7.955(b)-(c).) As a result, the Court will continue the matter to October 21, 2026, for counsel to supplement the record with a supplemental declaration.

V.             CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court continues the hearing on the Petitions to October 21, 2026, at 8:e0 a.m. for counsel to supplement the record with a supplemental declaration which is due by no later than October 9, 2026.

          IT IS SO ORDERED.

ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT ATRIA MANAGEMENT COMPANY, LLC’S MOTIONS FOR RELIEF FROM WAIVER OF OBJECTIONS TO FORM INTERROGATORIES (SET ONE), SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES (SET ONE), REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION (SET ONE), AND REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION (SET ONE)

 

I.               BACKGROUND 

Plaintiff Marianne Denis (“Plaintiff”) brings this action against Defendants Tarzana Assisted Living, LLC (“Tarzana”), Atria Senior Living, Inc. (“Atria Senior Living”), Atria Management Company, LLC (“Atria Management”), Irma Arteaga (“Arteaga”), Nune Gezalian (“Gezalian”), Doe 1 (“Walter”), and WG Tarzana, LP dba Atria Tarzana (“WG Tarzana”) (collectively, “Defendants”). This action arises from the death of Plaintiff’s spouse, Joseph Alexis Denis (“Decedent”), an employee at Defendants’ senior living facility who suffered a fatal cardiac event while on an authorized meal break in a vacant, locked apartment to which he was given unauthorized access by a coworker (“Walter”), and was not discovered or aided for several hours due to alleged failures in key control, monitoring, and emergency response procedures by Defendants.

Atria Management moves for relief from waiver of objections to its Form Interrogatories (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), Requests for Admission (Set One), and Requests for Production (Set One).

II.            PROCEDURAL HISTORY

On September 5, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint alleging (1) Negligence (Wrongful Death) and (2) Negligent Hiring, Retention, and Supervision against Defendants.

On November 21, 2025, Plaintiff filed her First Amended Complaint alleging the same causes of action.

On December 8, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s request for dismissal of Tarzana Assisted Living, LLC.

On April 3, 2026, the Court sustained Defendants’ Demurrer with leave to amend.

On April 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed her SAC alleging (1) Negligence (Wrongful Death), (2) Negligent Hiring, Retention, and Supervision, and (3) Negligent Undertaking.

On July 2, 2026, the Court sustained in part Defendants’ Demurrer to the second cause of action for negligent hiring, retention, and supervision with leave to amend and overrules Defendants’ Demurrer to the first cause of action for negligence.

On August 5, 2026, Atria Management filed the instant Motions.

As of September 10, 2026, no opposition has been filed.

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

The trial court has broad discretion to grant relief from waiver of objections on a noticed motion upon determining that: (a) the moving party subsequently served discovery responses which are substantially code compliant; and (b) the moving party’s failure to timely serve responses “was the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2030.290(a), 2031.300(a), and 2033.280(a).)[1]

A court may not find that only some portions of a document containing responses are code-compliant but must instead determine whether the document as whole substantially complies. (See St. Mary v. Superior Court (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 762, 779-780.)¿¿

The statutory language “mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect” in the discovery statute should be interpreted using the same general principles developed in application of the identical language in section 473(b). (See Scottsdale Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 263, 275.) Although the party moving for relief under section 473 has the burden to show the mistake, inadvertence, or neglect was excusable, any doubts as to that showing must be resolved in favor of the moving party. (See New Albertsons, Inc. v. Superior Court (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1403, 1420.)¿¿¿

IV.          ANALYSIS

Atria Management moves for relief from waiver of objections as to its Form Interrogatories (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), Requests for Admission (Set One), and Requests for Production (Set One). Atria Management’s counsel, Wendy E. Coulston, attests her firm’s staff inadvertently failed to move the discovery propounded to Atria Management on April 13, 2026, into the proper electronic file and to calendar a deadline corresponding to Atria Management’s discovery. (See FROGs Declaration of Wendy E. Coulston filed August 5, 2026 (“Coulston Decl.”), ¶ 3.) Coulston attests she did not discover this mistake until June 15, 2026. (See id., ¶ 15.) Coulston attests she conferred with Plaintiffs’ counsel requesting an extension and confirmation that Atria Management’s objections were preserved. (See id., ¶¶ 15-18, 24.) While preparing Atria Management’s discovery responses Coulston sought extensions due to medical emergencies. (See id., ¶¶ 23.) Coulston served Atria Management’s discovery responses on July 15, 2026. (See id., ¶ 28, Ex. T.) Atria Management brings these discovery motions because Plaintiffs’ counsel did not confirm that Atria Management’s objections were preserved. (See id., ¶¶ 24, 31.)

The Motions are unopposed.

The Court will grant the Motions. The Court finds Atria Management has shown its failure to serve timely discovery responses was due to counsel’s inadvertence in failing to move the discovery propounded to Atria Management to a discovery folder, thereby failing to calendar a corresponding deadline or complete the discovery. After reviewing Atria Management’s discovery responses, the Court finds they are substantially complaint such that relief is appropriate here. “Substantial compliance” doesn’t mean free from issues. A response may be in substantial compliance and still the proper subject of a (later) motion to compel. A response is in substantial compliance if it is in the form expected for an answer to the given request and conveys some information.

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Atria Management’s Motions for Relief from Waiver of Objections to its Form Interrogatories (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), Requests for Admission (Set One), and Requests for Production (Set One).
