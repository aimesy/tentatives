TENTATIVE RULING

 

LATOYA VICKERS

 

         vs.

 

MICHELIN et al.

 

 Case No.: 24STCV18603  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: June 24, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to quash subpoenas for private medical records is DENIED.

 

On 7/25/2024, Plaintiff Latoya Vickers (Plaintiff) filed suit against Michelin, Michelin North America, Inc., and Alonso Tiscareno, alleging: (1) violation of Labor Code section 1102.5; (2) violation of Equal Pay Act; (3) failure to prevent gender and racial discrimination; (4) declaratory judgment; (5) wrongful termination; and (6) intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

            On 3/23/2026, Plaintiff moved to quash subpoenas for private medical records.

 

Discussion 

 

            Plaintiff moves for an order quashing subpoenas served on:

 

1.      Custodian of Records for: Marian D. Spivey, LCSW;

 

2.      Custodian of. Records for: Kiaundra Jackson, LMFT;

 

3.      Custodian of Records for: Raafat N. Mattar, M.O; and

 

4.      Custodian of Records for: Nhordia Torres, CNP.

 

Plaintiff argues that the subpoenas are inappropriate as they seek her private medical records, and this is not a disability discrimination case.

 

However, Plaintiff alleges “severe and profound pain and emotional distress, anxiety, depression, headaches, tension, and other physical ailments, as well as medical expenses, expenses for psychological counseling and treatment, and past and future lost wages and benefits.” (Complaint ¶ 39.)

 

As such, Plaintiff has put her physical and mental health at issue, and thus has waived her constitutional right of privacy to the extent necessary for fair adjudication of the claims.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to quash subpoenas for private medical records is denied.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 24STCV29207    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

MICHAEL A. WHITE

 

         vs.

 

THE COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES, et al.

 

 Case No.: 24STCV29207  

Related Case No: 25STCV15428

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: June 24, 2026

 

Defendants’ motion to compel further responses from Plaintiff to Special Interrogatories (Set One) Nos. 1-4 is GRANTED.  Responses due within 20 days. Plaintiff is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,050, due within 60 days of entry of this order.

 

Defendants’ motion to compel further responses from Plaintiff to Requests for Production (Set One) Nos. 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 and 21 is GRANTED. Responses due within 20 days. Plaintiff is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,050, due within 60 days of entry of this order.

 

On 11/6/2024, Plaintiff Michael A. White (Plaintiff) filed suit against the County of Los Angeles, and the Los Angeles County Sherriff’s Department (collectively, Defendants). On 2/18/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC) alleging: (1) race discrimination; (2) age discrimination; and (3) retaliation.

 

On 2/17/2026, Defendants moved to compel further responses from Plaintiff to Special Interrogatories (SROGs) (Set One). Concurrently, Defendants moved to compel further responses from Plaintiff to Requests for Production (RFPs) (Set One) Nos. 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 and 21.

 

For ease, the Court has consolidated its analysis into a single ruling.

 

Discussion

 

Defendants seek to compel further responses to SROG Nos. 1-4 on the grounds that Plaintiff’s responses are incomplete and evasive. Similarly, Defendants seek to compel further responses to RFP Nos. 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 and 21 on the same grounds.

 

            After review, the Court agrees.

 

            As for the SROGs, Plaintiff’s responses to Nos. 1-4 fail to respond completely and directly to the specific interrogatory asked.  Moreover, as noted by Defendant, Plaintiff’s opposition exceeds the page limit provided for by CRC Rule 3.1113, subd. (d) and includes case quotations that do not exist.

 

            As for the RFPS, CCP section 2031.280(a) provides that “[o]nce a responding party produces documents and designates which request numbers they respond to . . . the burden of searching through the production to identify responsive documents shifts to the propounding party.” This requires that Plaintiff identify the specific request number to which the produced documents respond to, rather than listing the entire production range. 

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion to compel further responses from Plaintiff to Special Interrogatories (Set One) Nos. 1-4 is granted. Plaintiff is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,050 ($350/hr x 3 hr), due within 60 days of entry of this order.

 

Defendants’ motion to compel further responses from Plaintiff to Requests for Production (Set One) Nos. 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 and 21 is granted. Plaintiff is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $1,050, due within 60 days of entry of this order.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 26STCV09364    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

ZHOIE PEREZ

                          

         vs.

 

SALVADOR URIZAR et al.

                                         

 Case No.: 26STCV09364 

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: June 24, 2026

 

 

Defendants’ demurrer is OVERRULED IN PART, SUSTAINED IN PART. Defendants’ demurrer is OVERRULED as to the 3rd and 5th causes of action, but SUSTAINED, WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND, as to the 4th cause of action. 

 

            On 3/24/2026, Plaintiff Zhoie Perez (Plaintiff) filed suit against Defendants Salvador Urizar and Proliant Services (collectively, Defendants), alleging: (1) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (2) negligence; (3) violations of the Ralph Civil Rights Act; (4) violations of the Unruh Act; (5) violations of the Bane Civil Rights Act; (6) assault and battery; and (7) negligent hiring.

 

            On 4/14/2026, Defendants demurrer to Plaintiff’s 3rd, 4th, and 5th causes of action.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants argue that Plaintiff has not sufficiently pled her 3rd, 4th, or 5th causes of action.

 

            As for the third cause of action for violation of the Ralph Civil Rights Act, the Ralph Civil Rights Act, California Civil Code Section 51.7, provides that “[a]ll persons within the jurisdiction of this state have the right to be free from any violence, or intimidation by threat of violence, committed against their persons or property because of” specified protected characteristics, including political affiliation, sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language, or immigration status.” (Cal. Civ. Code § 51.7(a)–(b).)

 

Defendants contend that Plaintiff has failed to allege facts sufficient to establish that any conduct by Defendants was motivated by Plaintiff’s actual or perceived protected characteristic. However, Plaintiff alleges that she is a trans woman of color and that she was violently pepper sprayed on a public sidewalk without provocation. Moreover, Plaintiff alleges that “[a]ccording to Defendants Urizar’s Facebook page he has a severe negative outlook on Transgender individuals.” (Complaint ¶¶ 51, 60.) These allegations are sufficient at the pleadings stage to show that she was targeted with violence based on a protected characteristic.

 

            As for the fourth cause of action, to state a cause of action under the Unruh Act, a plaintiff must allege: (1) the defendant denied, aided or incited a denial of, or discriminated or made a distinction that denied, full and equal accommodations, advantages, facilities, privileges, or services to the plaintiff; (2) a motivating reason for the defendant’s conduct was the plaintiff’s protected characteristic; (3) the plaintiff was harmed; and (4) the defendant’s conduct was a substantial factor in causing the harm. (Stamps v. Superior Court (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 1441, 1443.) The Unruh Act applies exclusively to “business establishments.”

 

            Here, Plaintiff does not allege that she was a patron or customer of any business, and Plaintiff alleges that she was on the public sidewalk filming the exterior of a banking facility for purposes of “conducting a news story.” (Complaint ¶¶ 11–12.) As such, Plaintiff herself alleges that the alleged acts occurred on a public sidewalk and not while she was trying to avail herself of some business establishment. Accordingly, the Court finds this claim to be insufficiently pled.

 

            Finally, as for the fifth cause of action, the Bane Civil Rights Act, California Civil Code Section 52.1, provides a cause of action where a person “interferes by threat, intimidation, or coercion, or attempts to interfere by threat, intimidation, or coercion, with the exercise or enjoyment by any individual or individuals of rights secured by the Constitution or laws of the United States, or of the rights secured by the Constitution or laws of this state.” (Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(a).) As clarified by the Court of Appeal in Cornell v. City and County of San Francisco (2017) 17 Cal.App.5th 766, a Bane Act claim can be established in one of two ways: (1) by showing a violation of the plaintiff’s rights committed with “specific intent”—that is, with a reckless disregard for the plaintiff’s constitutional or statutory rights; or (2) by showing a violation of the plaintiff’s rights committed with general intent, plus threats, intimidation, or coercion beyond the coercion inherent in the wrongful act itself. (Id. at pp. 799–803.)

 

            Here, Plaintiff’s allegations are sufficient to show that Defendant acted with a reckless disregard for Plaintiff’s first amendment rights, and interference with Plaintiff’s right to conduct a news report on a public sidewalk. While Defendant may dispute this characterization of her conduct, the Court accepts well pled allegations as true at the pleadings stage. 

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ demurrer is overruled in part, sustained in part. Defendants’ demurrer is overruled as to the 3rd and 5th causes of action, but sustained, without leave to amend, as to the 4th cause of action. 

 

It is so ordered.
