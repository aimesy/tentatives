Case Number: 24PSCV00219    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

PLAINTIFFS ANGELA TAPIAS, BY AND THROUGH HER GUARDIAN AD LITEM HORTENCIA TAPIAS AND HORTENCIA TAPIAS’ NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR RECONSIDERATION OF THE COURT’S ORDER TO GRANT DEFENDANT COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES’ MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT is DENIED; the court requests Defendant file a proposed order.

 

Background

 

This is a negligence/medical malpractice case. Plaintiff Angela Tapias (“Plaintiff” or “minor”), by and through her Guardian ad Litem Hortencia Tapias, and Hortencia Tapias (“mother”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) allege the following against Defendants CARE AMBULANCE SERVICE, INC (“CAS”) and FALCK USA (collectively, “Ambulence”);[1] LOS ANGELES COUNTY FIRE DEPARTMENT (the “Fire Department”); COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES (the “County”); EMANATE HEALTH QUEEN OF THE VALLEY HOSPITAL (“Emanate” or “Hospital”) (collectively, “Defendants”): On January 23, 2023, Plaintiff, then a 5-year-old, started to have symptoms consistent with an upper respiratory infection. On January 28, 2023, she was diagnosed with pneumonia, but later that day, Plaintiff’s mother called 911 as Plaintiff started to have fevers and chills. Neither the Fire Department nor the Ambulance took Plaintiff’s body temperature or implemented treatment modalities to lower Plaintiff’s body temperature. “As a result of [the minor’s] untreated high and sustained fever, [the minor] suffered a brain injury and currently has the intellectual capacity of a 2-year-old.”

 

On January 22, 2024, Plaintiffs filed suit against Defendants for:

 

1.     Professional Negligence

2.     Violation of Government Code section 815.2

3.     Negligence

 

On February 15, 2024, Emanate filed a demurrer as to the 3rd COA (NIED), which the court sustained on 3/28/24 with leave to amend (as the complaint did not allege an injury-producing event).

 

On May 29, 2024, Plaintiffs filed their first amended complaint (FAC) realleging the same COAs.

 

On June 12, 2024, Emanate filed its answer.

 

On June 28, 2024, the County filed its answer.

 

On August 7, 2024, Defendant FALCK USA (“Falck”) filed a demurrer to the FAC.

 

On September 4, 2024, the court sustained the demurrer with leave to amend for two reasons: (i) improper lumping of defendants Falck and Care and (ii) the mother did not have contemporaneous awareness that fever was causing anoxic brain injury; the injury is not the fever.

 

On October 18, 2024, Plaintiffs filed their Second Amended Complaint (SAC) reasserting the same COAs against the same Defendants.

 

On November 15, 2024, the County filed its answer to the SAC.

 

On November 18, 2024, the Hospital filed its answer to the SAC.

 

On December 18, 2024, Falck filed a demurrer to the SAC, which the court sustained with leave to amend on 2/13/25. 

 

On January 31, 2025, the Hospital filed a motion for summary judgment, which was unopposed and granted on 4/30/25.

 

On March 13, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a third amended complaint (3AC).

 

On June 25, 2025, the court overruled Falck’s demurrer reasoning that “Plaintiffs have amended their complaint to, at this point, allege vicarious liability and Keys, infra, is instructive in that for purposes of a demurrer, Plaintiffs have alleged contemporaneous awareness of the injury (which is not anoxic brain injury but the inability to regulate Baby Tapias’ body temperature).”

 

On July 24, 2025, Falck and Care filed their respective answers.

 

On January 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed an amendment to the complaint indicating that Falck USA, Inc. is the ‘true name’ of Falck USA.

 

On March 6, 2026, an MSJ was filed by the County, hereinafter, “Defendant.”

 

On June 9, 2026, the court held its hearing on the matter and thereafter took the motion under submission.

 

On June 24, 2026, the court issued its final ruling. As set forth in that order, “Court orders judgment entered on the Amended Complaint (3rd) filed by Hortencia Tapias, et al. on 03/13/2025 as follows: Plaintiff to take nothing by virtue of the complaint.” (emphasis added.)

 

On July 7, 2026, the instant motion was filed.

 

On July 21, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their motion to tax costs. (The hearing is set for 10/14.)

 

On July 31, 2026, Defendant filed an “OPPOSITION TO PLAINTIFFS’ JURISDICTIONALLY BARRED AND PROCEDURALLY DEFECTIVE MOTION FOR RECONSIDERATION.”

 

On August 5, 2026, the reply was filed.

 

On August 13, 2026, the hearing was held. The court did not post a tentative ruling as the court received a call that the motion would be taken off calendar. During the hearing, Plaintiffs’ counsel explained that was an inadvertent clerical error. The court continued the hearing to review the papers.

 

On August 12, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a notice that they have appealed the court’s ‘judgment after an order granting a summary judgment motion.”

 

On September 9, 2026, the Ambulance Defendant filed their MSJ; the hearing is set for 12/2.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiffs seek reconsideration of this court’s order granting the COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES’ Motion for Summary Judgment (MSJ) pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) §1008. They argue that counsel but after the order learned of the additional professional experience of Dr. Pham (experience in pediatric pre-hospitalization transport, triage, procedures) that would “likely result in the reversal of the court’s decision.” (Motion p. ii.)

 

However, as noted in opposition and tacitly conceded in reply, this court cannot reach the merits because it lacks jurisdiction. A grant of summary judgment may be challenged in the trial court before the judgment is entered by way of a motion for reconsideration under C.C.P. 1008(a). (Mink v. Superior Court (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 1338, 1342.) Here, however, the motion for reconsideration was filed after the judgment was entered. “It is well settled that entry of judgment divests the trial court of authority to rule on a motion for reconsideration.” (Opp. p. 2, citing Safeco Ins. Co. v. Architectural Facades Unlimited, Inc. (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 1477, 1482.)

 

After a summary judgment, the methods of attacking the judgment are by a motion for new trial, and appeal. (Green v. Del–Camp Investments, Inc. (1961) 193 Cal.App.2d 479, 481; 6 Witkin, Cal.Procedure (3d ed. 1985) Proceedings Without Trial, § 307, p. 601; see also Jacobs v. Retail Clerks Union, Local No. 1222 (1975) 49 C.A.3d 959, 963; Passavanti v. Williams (1990) 225 Cal.App3d 1602, see 8 Cal. Proc. (6th), Attack on Judgment in Trial Court, § 18 et seq.) And even if the motion is not labeled as such, the court is free to consider a motion regardless of the label put on it by the moving party. (Graham v. Hansen (1982) 128 Cal.App.3d 965, 970.) In reply, Plaintiffs do just that. In reply, they request “that the [c]ourt exercise its authority [] to look to the substance of this timely filing, recharacterize the motion as a Motion for New Trial (CCP § 657(7)) and/or Motion to Vacate Judgment (CCP § 663(1)).” (Reply p. 5.)

The motion may be based on any available statutory ground for a new trial, such as “newly discovered evidence” or “accident or surprise.” (C.C.P. 657.) The motion must be filed within 15 days of the date of the clerk's service of notice of entry of the judgment. (C.C.P. 659.)

 

However, though in reference to the 1008 motion, as argued by Defendant, “[t]here is absolutely no evidence that any of Dr. Pham’s foundational background could not have been timely presented to the Court sooner and/or why they were only supplied to the Court after the ruling granting Defendant’s MSJ.” (Opp. p. 3:24-26.)) After all, Plaintiffs were in possession of Defendant’s MSJ since March 11, 2026. In fact, Plaintiffs were aware of Defendant’s evidentiary objections to their experts filed on 5/29 such that Plaintiffs since then had the opportunity to revisit their experts’ credentials before the 6/9 hearing. Of note, this court does not post tentative rulings on summary judgments generally until one day before the hearing meaning Plaintiffs had a handful of days to re-evaluate their expert’s credentials. But they did not until after the ruling when Dr. Pham and Plaintiffs’ counsel were discussing the ruling. (Motion pp. 5-6.) To the extent that Plaintiffs argue “they no reason to believe the Court intended to strike Dr. Pham’s testimony in toto” because of the tentative ruling, a tentative ruling is just that—tentative, not a final. (Reply p. 3.)

 

As for Plaintiffs’ request to vacate the judgment, that can be made on the grounds of an “Incorrect or erroneous legal basis for the decision, not consistent with or not supported by the facts….” or a “judgment or decree not consistent with or not supported by the special verdict.” Plaintiffs have not explained how the court made a decision based upon incorrect or erroneous law.

 

The court recognizes that the reply puts Defendant at a disadvantage since new theories of relief are being asserted. Should Defendant ask for it, the court will continue the matter for supplemental briefing.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion is denied.


[1] According to the complaint, Falck USA is the parent company of the Ambulance.

 


Case Number: 24PSCV03501    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

Defendants, ART GUADALAJARA, and GENESIS FLOOR COVERING INC., (“Defendants”) Motion for a Protective Order is DENIED; Defendants have not established good cause.

 

Background

 

This case arises from a car accident that happened on January 16, 2023.

 

On October 17, 2024, Plaintiffs WILLIAM TORRES, an individual; LYNN MENDEZ, an individual; and MATEO

TORRES, an individual, by and through his Guardian ad Litem, WILLIAM TORRES filed suit against Defendants ART GUADALAJARA, an individual and GENESIS FLOOR COVERING INC. (“Genesis”) for Negligence.

 

On January 17, 2025, Genesis filed its answer and that same day filed a cross-complaint (CC) against Plaintiff William Torres for (1) Indemnification and (2) Apportionment of Fault.

 

On March 3, 2025, Defendant Guadalajara filed his answer. (The caption states it is the answer of Genesis.)

 

On May 5, 2025, Plaintiff Torres filed his answer to the CC.

 

On February 11, 2026, the parties attended an IDC. The court allowed Defendant to file their motions.

 

On June 8, 2026, the instant motion was filed.

 

On June 24, 2026, the court issued an order indicating that the IDC is taken off calendar and plaintiffs may file their motions.

 

On August 11, 2026, an opposition was filed, with a request for sanctions.

 

To date, as of 11 AM on 9/10, no reply has been filed (due 9/9, 5 court days before the hearing per CCP 1005(b)).

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move this court pursuant to Sections 2019.020 and 2025.420 of the California Code of Civil Procedure (“CCP”) for a protective order that the deposition of Defendant Art Guadalajara shall not be taken until after the depositions of Plaintiffs William Torres, Lynn Mendez, and Mateo Torres are completed. (They also seek sanctions of $2,460.00.) More specifically, Defendants seek to maintain the “status quo” which was that Defendants would depose the three Plaintiffs first but then after Plaintiffs failed to appear at their depositions on March 26 and 27, 2026, on March 31, 2026, Plaintiffs for the first time served a notice of deposition of Defendant Art Guadalajara, setting the deposition for April 29, 2026.

 

For reasons to be explained below, Defendants have not identified good cause for the court to establish the sequence and timing of discovery. (See e.g. Opp. p. 5:21-27 [“They do not contend that Guadalajara’s deposition would subject him to annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, undue burden, or expense. Nor do they identify any substantive prejudice that would result if Guadalajara is deposed before Mateo. Defendants do not explain how Guadalajara’s testimony would be compromised, what unfair tactical advantage Plaintiffs would obtain, or why Guadalajara cannot be adequately prepared for deposition without first examining all three Plaintiffs.”].) Defendants’ citation to their two cases all but confirm the court’s ruling.[1]

 

In Poeschl v. Superior Court In and For Ventura County (1964) 229 Cal.App.2d 383, the action was one for damages for an alleged breach of an employment contract. (Id. at p. 384.) Both the parties’ depositions were set for the same day but via priority of notice and a stipulation, the deposition of the petitioner defendant was to go first. (Ibid.) About one week before the deposition date, the defendant noticed a motion for an order directing the plaintiff to produce “all recordings taken or posessed [sic] by him of conversations between himself and petitioner or any employee or agent of petitioner relating to his employment or discharge by petitioner” because the defendant “should be entitled to refresh his recollection from any recordings possessed by the real party prior to the taking of his deposition.” (Ibid, emphasis added.) The motion was denied, as were other attempts like relieving the defendant from the stipulation or alternatively allowing the petitioner to be deposed after the plaintiff. (Id. at pp. 384-385.) On appeal, the court directed the trial court to issue its order that the plaintiff produce the recordings before taking the deposition of the defendant. (Id. at p. 387.) Aside from the defendant acting promptly for the recordings, the court reasoned, while relying upon other cases, that the plaintiff was seeking the advantages that would flow from his being able to refresh his recollection from the recordings before giving his deposition while denying to the defendant those conversations equal opportunity to refresh their recollections before giving their depositions. (Id. at p. 386.)

 

Here, no such advantage has been set forth by Defendants.

 

Equally unavailing is Defendants’ citation to Rosemont v. Superior Court, (1964) 60 Cal.2d 709 as that case too involved the propriety of a discovery order permitting defendants to inspect and transcribe certain recordings prior to the taking of their depositions.

 

All in all, as demonstrated by Poeschl, if one party is seeking an unfair advantage from the timing of the deposition, the other party may seek a protective order. What is more, the burden is on the moving party to establish “good cause” for whatever relief is requested: “Generally, a deponent seeking a protective order will be required to show that the burden, expense, or intrusiveness involved in … [the discovery procedure] clearly outweighs the likelihood that the information sought will lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.” (Emerson Elec. Co. v. Sup.Ct. (Grayson) (1997) 16 Cal.4th 1101, 1110; see Nativi v. Deutsche Bank Nat'l Trust Co. (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 261, 318 burden not met by “entirely conclusory” declaration that “lacked any factual specificity”].) Here, Defendants have not their burden in demonstrating the necessary good cause aside from it being their “preference” that Plaintiffs be deposed first. (Motion p. 8:27-28.)

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion is denied. As for Plaintiffs’ request for sanctions, that is denied.

Case Number: 25PSCV03331    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

DONN E. REDIGER AND JUDITH A. REDIGER’S Demurrer to Plaintiffs’ First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

Background

 

This case arises from an explosion that happened on 10/9/23 at a property wherein certain defendants were engaged in the cannabis extraction business enterprise. As for the parties, they are as follows: Plaintiff XIAODAN HAN, an individual (spouse of decedent Guangqi Fu); Plaintiff XIAODAN HAN as the Guardian ad Litem of KEIRA FU, a minor; Plaintiff XIU WEN, an individual (surviving spouse of decedent Qizhuo Liang); and Plaintiff XIU WEN as the Guardian ad Litem of CHANGMU LIANG, a minor; Defendant TED CHIEN (“Ted”), an individual, also known as "THEODORE S CHIEN" (operator of the business); Defendant DAWN CHIEN (“Dawn”), an individual (spouse of Chien who allegedly participated in and shared the business profits); Defendant HAN QUAN JIAO SR (“Han”), an individual (operator of the business); Defendant XIAO QIN HUANG (“Xia)”, an individual (spouse of Han who participated in and shared business profits); Defendant DONN E. REDIGER, an individual; Defendant DONNE. REDIGER as Trustee of The Rediger Family Trust dated June 4, 1992; Defendant JUDITH A. REDIGER, an individual; and Defendant JUDITH A. REDIGER as Trustee of The Rediger Family Trust dated June 4, 1992 (the Rediger Trust is alleged to be the owner of record of the premises where the catastrophic explosion occurred and killed Decedents). 

 

On September 15, 2025, Plaintiffs filed suit for wrongful death. 

 

On October 3, 2025, Dawn filed a filing with the court indicating that “someone delivered” presumably the summons and complaint and that she does “not know th[e]se plaintiffs and that [sic] is [sic] scam.” 

 

On November 4, 2025, Dawn and Xiao filed an answer/general denial, and Ted and Han filed their respective answer.

 

On November 26, 2025, DONN E. REDIGER AND JUDITH A. REDIGER (collectively the “Rediger Defendants”) filed a demurrer, which on 3/12/26, the court sustained with leave to amend.

 

On May 8, 2026, a first amended complaint (FAC) was filed.

 

On July 22, 2026, the instant demurrer was filed.

 

On September 1, 2026, an opposition was filed.

 

On September 9, 2026, a reply was filed.

 

Discussion[1]

 

The Rediger Defendants demur to the FAC on similar grounds as before: that the allegations are conclusive and fail to establish individual ownership, possession, or control on the part of the Redigers, fail to establish personal fault as required under Probate Code §§ 18001–1800, and fail to establish a legal duty owed by the Redigers as individuals. (See e.g., Demurrer[2] [“[T]hese allegations remain conclusory, speculative, and unsupported by specific facts… The FAC contains no non-conclusory facts…. the FAC offers only conclusory statements that the Redigers “knew” of dangerous conditions, “should have known” about such conditions, and “failed to act”…. These are precisely the type of conclusory allegations rejected by California courts.”].)

 

For reasons to be discussed below—namely as the FAC addresses some of the court’s concerns and ultimate facts have been pled—the court overrules the demurrer.

 

Pleading Standard(s)

 

As Rediger Defendants largely argue that the FAC offers only conclusory facts, the court finds it prudent to start with a preliminary doctrinal recitation of the pleading standard in California.

 

The primary function of a pleading is to give the other party notice so that it may prepare its case. (See e.g., Shah v. Skillz Inc., (2024) 101 Cal.App.5th 285; see also Harris v. City of Santa Monica (2013) 56 Cal.4th 203; In re Marriage of Lippel (1990) 51 Cal.3d 1160; Reichert v. General Ins. Co. Of America (1968) 68 Cal.2d 822.) A complaint is sufficient if it alleges ultimate facts; it need not allege evidentiary facts.[3] Ultimate facts are those essential facts of a plaintiff’s that acquaint [the] defendant with reasonable precision and with particularity of the nature, source and extent of the plaintiff's claim." (Annocki v. Peterson Enterprises, LLC (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 32, 36, internal citations omitted.) Now, an ultimate fact may not be a conclusion of law. In practice, and even in the abstract, the difference between ultimate facts and conclusions of law is difficult to state, and the line between statements of fact and conclusions of law is not always easy to draw. (See e.g., Perkins v. Superior Court (1981) 117 Cal.App.3d 1; Krug v. Meehan (1952) 109 Cal.App.2d 274.) The test: whether any evidence outside the pleading itself could be offered in support of the allegation; if such evidence could be offered, the allegation would be an allegation of ultimate fact and not a conclusion of law. (New v. Mutual Ben. Health & Acc. Association (1938) 24 Cal.App.2s 681.) As one court has framed it: “The basic test of whether an allegation is a statement of fact or a conclusion lies in the answer to the question: Does it usurp the province of the court to draw from it all legal inferences or conclusions which are determinative of the issue? [] As a general rule, if it does, it is a conclusion; if it does not, it is a statement of fact.” (Waco-Porter Corp. v. Superior Court In and For Tuolumne County (1963) 211 Cal.App.2d 559, 567.)

 

Previous Ruling

 

The court’s previous ruling relied upon Plaintiffs’ citation to People v. Braum (2020) 49 Cal.App.5th 342 to show how unlike that case, the complaint does not allege that the individual trustees had notice of any illegal activity or that the allegations could lend to such inference. The court also was concerned with the complaint’s allegation on how the individual Defendants should have been physically present at the property to manage when such activity by a landlord would inarguably invade the tenants’ rights to privacy and amount to the landlord’s breach of the lease by entering the premises without the consent of the tenants.

 

Comparing the complaints

 

The FAC does add more.[4]

 

The (original) complaint alleged liability based on, among other things, "the inherently suspicious nature of the tenancy, unusual and dangerous modifications made to the warehouse to accommodate the lab, excessive utility consumption, the presence of chemical odors, the delivery and storage of large quantities of flammable chemicals, and other red flags indicating the presence of a possibly illegal, dangerous, and ultrahazardous business operation that would and should have been apparent to any reasonable property owner exercising ordinary care." (¶22.) 

 

The FAC largely relies upon the same allegations but adds one major change: "reports and complaints by third parties regarding their tenants' usage of the Subject Premises” (¶30). That is the nature and source of the liability asserted against the Rediger Defendants. But they argue that this allegation fails because “they are pleaded on information and belief without factual detail, they do not identify who observed these alleged conditions or when, and they do not connect these facts to the Redigers personally.” What Defendants are requiring from Plaintiffs are evidentiary facts. That is not required. (See e.g., C.A. v William S. Hart Union H.S. Dist. (2012) 53 C4th 861, 872 [the plaintiff is not required to identify by name or position which of the defendant's employees committed the negligent acts or omissions for which the defendant is allegedly liable].) All in all, this allegation satisfies the test above because evidence gathered via discovery can be offered to support this allegation (i.e., who made the report, the number of reports, etc.).[5]

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, as the FAC does allege that the Rediger Defendants had notice of potential illegality such that they failed to act in their roles to address/remedy the danger, the demurrer is overruled.
