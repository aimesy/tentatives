DEPARTMENT 406 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV26919    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 406

 

SUSAN RIDDLE and ERIC RIDDLE,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.; et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  25STCV26919

  Hearing Date:  September 23, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

defendant’s four motions to compel further discovery responses

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On September 12, 2025, Plaintiffs SUSAN RIDDLE and ERIC RIDDLE filed this action in which they claim various violations of the Song Beverly Act.

On June 30, 2026, Defendant AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC (Honda) filed four motions to compel further discovery responses.

On September 10, 2026, Plaintiffs SUSAN RIDDLE and ERIC RIDDLE filed an opposition in which they state that the motions are moot because code compliance responses were served on September 9, 2026.  Plaintiffs also contend that meet and confer.  The court, however, finds that Honda adequately met and conferred prior to filing its motions. (See, Attorney Mooradian’s declaration.)

LEGAL STANDARD

Upon receiving responses to its discovery requests, the propounding party may move for an order compelling further responses if the responses are incomplete or evasive, or objections are without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(a), 2031.310(a), 2033.290(a).)

DISCUSSION

            Plaintiffs served verified responses on May 3, 2026, emailing them to Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel. Therefore, the motion is moot except as to sanctions.

            Honda requests sanctions in the amount of $6,000.00.  The court finds reasonable sanctions to be $2,000.00.  Because of Plaintiffs actions, Honda unnecessarily incurred expenses in having to file these motions. 

CONCLUSION

            Plaintiff’s motion to compel responses is DENIED as moot.  Court imposes sanctions in the amount of $2,000.00 against Plaintiff’s counsel.  Sanctions to be paid within 30 days of this order.


Case Number: 26STCV06684    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 406

 

TERESA QUINTANILLA SANCHEZ,

                        Plaintiff,

            v.

 

ALDEN ENTERPRISES LLC, et al.,

                        Defendants.

 

  Case No.:  26STCV06684

  Hearing Date:  September 23, 2026

 

     [TENTATIVE] order RE:

defendants alden enterprises, llc’s and longwood management, llc’s motion to compel arbitration

 

 

BACKGROUND

            On March 2, 2026, Plaintiff Teresa Quintanilla Sanchez filed this employment discrimination action against Defendants Alden Enterprises, LLC; Longwood Management, LLC; Steven P. Haas; and Marvin Doe.

            On June 10, 2026, Defendants Alden Enterprises and Longwood Management filed the instant motion to compel arbitration. Plaintiff filed an opposition on August 6, 2026. Defendants filed a reply on August 12, 2026. Plaintiff filed a sur-reply on September 2, 2026.  Defendants filed a reply to Plaintiff’s sur-reply September 14, 2026.

LEGAL STANDARD

“On petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party to the agreement refuses to arbitrate that controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists….” (Code Civ. Proc, § 1281.2.) “The party seeking arbitration bears the burden of proving the existence of an arbitration agreement, and the party opposing arbitration bears the burden of proving any defense, such as unconscionability.” (Pinnacle Museum Tower Assn. v. Pinnacle Market Development (US), LLC (2012) 55 Cal.4th 223, 236.)

DISCUSSION

A.    PLAINTIFF’S ACTION IS EXEMPT FROM ARBITRATON UNDER THE “ENDING FORCED ARBITRATION OF SEXUAL ASSAULT AND SEXUAL HARASSMENT ACT” (EFAA).

`           The EFAA, provides that, notwithstanding any other provision of the FAA, at the election of the person alleging conduct constituting a sexual harassment dispute, no pre-dispute arbitration agreement shall be valid or enforceable with respect to a case filed under federal, tribal, or state law that relates to the sexual harassment dispute.  (See, 9 U.S.C.A., Section 402.)  The EFAA defines a "sexual harassment dispute" as a dispute relating to conduct that is alleged to constitute sexual harassment under applicable federal, tribal, or state law.  (See, 9 U.S.C.A., Section 401.)

            FEHA expressly defines the scope of prohibited harassment in California Government Code, Section 12940(j)(4)(C), which states: "'harassment' because of sex includes sexual harassment, gender harassment, and harassment based on pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions."  (emphasis added.)  This provision makes clear that pregnancy-based harassment is not a separate or lesser category of harassment and that it is a form of harassment because of sex, which is the same category that encompasses sexual harassment. Also, California Government Code, Section 12926(r)(1) defines "sex" to include pregnancy and medical conditions related to pregnancy.

            In the present case, Plaintiff has alleged harassment based upon pregnancy, which, as set forth above, constitutes sexual harassment.  As such, Plaintiff’s action is exempt from arbitration under EFAA.

CONCLUSION

            Defendants Alden Enterprises, LLC’s and Longwood Management, LLC’s motion to compel arbitration is DENIED.
