DEPARTMENT 224 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV38460    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 224

Zelaya v. Yeghiyan

TENTATIVE RULING

Specially Appearing Defendant Matthew Fooladian's motion to quash service of summons is DENIED. Defendant Fooladian shall file a responsive pleading within the time provided by Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, subdivision (b).

The Case Management Conference is continued to December 13, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.

Introduction

On December 31, 2025, Plaintiff Norma Zelaya filed this motor vehicle personal injury action against Defendant Armen Yeghiyan and Does 1 through 25, arising out of a traffic collision. Specially Appearing Defendant Matthew Fooladian was thereafter named by Doe amendment.

On April 17, 2026, Specially Appearing Defendant Fooladian filed the instant Motion to Quash Service of Summons and Complaint.

On July 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

On August 5, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

On August 11, 2026, Plaintiff submitted a declaration of the retained agency for service of process.

Legal Standard

“A defendant . . . may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: (1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her . . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a).) “[Clompliance with the statutory procedures for service of process is essential to establish personal jurisdiction. [Citation.]” (Dill v. Berquist Construction Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1444.) “[The filing of a proof of service creates a rebuttable presumption that the service was proper” but only if it “complies with the statutory requirements regarding such proofs.” (Id. at pp. 1441-1442.) When a defendant moves to quash service of the summons and complaint, the plaintiff has “the burden of proving the facts that did give the court jurisdiction, that is the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Coulston v. Cooper (1966) 245 Cal.App.2d 866, 868.)

Reasonable Diligence

"Ordinarily, two or three attempts at personal service at a proper place should fully satisfy the requirement of reasonable diligence." (American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 389.) Here, registered process server Javier Reyes Rios attempted personal service on three separate days at varying times. That satisfies the diligence requirement. Fooladian does not meaningfully contest diligence. Rather, his objection is to the location of service, addressed below.

Dwelling House or Usual Place of Abode

Fooladian's argument that the Glendale address had ceased to be his dwelling house or usual place of abode by February 14, 2026 is not established on this record, and is contradicted by his own official records.

Physical presence at the moment of service is not required, and the statute is construed liberally to uphold jurisdiction where the manner of service was reasonably calculated to give actual notice. (Bein v. Brechtel-Jochim Group, Inc. (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1387, 1392.) The evidence establishes that 127 N. Everett St. #9, Glendale, is the address Fooladian holds out as his own. His certified DMV Driver Record Information lists that address. (Chopurian Decl., ¶ 4, Exh. 2.) The temporary driver's license he provided to Plaintiff at the collision scene lists that address. (Id., ¶ 5, Exh. 3.) The Glendale Police Department collision report identifies him as Party No. 1 at that address. (Id., ¶ 6, Exh. 4.)

There is no indication that he has updated the address of record with the Department of Motor Vehicles in accordance with the obligation to report a change of residence address within ten days. (Veh. Code, § 14600.) His parents continue to occupy the residence.

Against that showing, Fooladian's motion offers no declaration of his own. He submits no military orders, no assignment or housing documentation, no lease, and no change-of-address record. Critically, Fooladian identifies no alternative fixed residence established in place of the Glendale address. His parents' declarations state only where he does not live. The assertion that the Glendale address "has not been" his abode "for a substantial period of time" is conclusory and unsupported by competent evidence.

Military service does not, by itself, divest a servicemember's family home of its status as a usual place of abode, particularly where the servicemember has not shown he established a separate permanent civilian residence and has left his own official records unchanged. On this record, Defendant has not rebutted the presumption of proper service.

Due Process and the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act

Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. (1950) 339 U.S. 306 requires notice reasonably calculated under the circumstances to apprise interested parties of the pendency of the action. Section 415.20, subdivision (b) codifies a procedure meeting that standard, and service on an adult household member at the defendant's address of record, followed by mailing to the same address, falls squarely within it. Defense counsel's appearance and meet-and-confer efforts within weeks of the mailing confirm that notice was in fact received. The due process argument adds nothing independent of the statutory argument and is rejected.

Conclusion

Specially Appearing Defendant Matthew Fooladian's motion to quash service of summons is DENIED. Defendant Fooladian shall file a responsive pleading within the time provided by Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, subdivision (b).

Case Number: 26STCV08439    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: 224

Teitelbaum v. State Bar of California, et al.,

TENTATIVE RULING

The Court DENIES Plaintiffs’ Motion for Relief from Discovery Stay pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16(g).

Introduction

On March 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Complaint in this action.

On June 11, 2026, the Friedman Defendants filed a Special Motion to Strike pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16, which automatically stayed discovery. The Grant Defendants filed a Special Motion to Strike as well.

On July 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Leave to Conduct Discovery While Special Motion(s) to Strike Are Pending, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16(g).

On July 8, 2026, the Grant Defendants filed an opposition.

On July 30, 2026, the Friedman Defendants filed an opposition.

On August 5, 2026 Plaintiff filed a Reply and response to objections.

Evidentiary Objections

Defendants’ evidentiary objections 1-4 are SUSTAINED.

Legal Standard

Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(g) provides, “All discovery proceedings in the action shall be stayed upon the filing of a notice of motion made pursuant to this section. The stay of discovery shall remain in effect until notice of entry of the order ruling on the motion. The court, on noticed motion and for good cause shown, may order that specified discovery be conducted notwithstanding this subdivision.”

In order to establish “good cause” within the meaning of Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(g), the plaintiff must show that “a defendant or a witness possesses evidence needed by the plaintiff to establish its probability of prevailing on a claim.” (1-800 Contacts, Inc. v. Steinberg (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 568, 572). Whether good cause has been shown is within the discretion of the Court. (See Lafayette Morehouse, Inc. v. Chronicle Publ'g Co. (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 855, 868.)

“[T]he fact evidence necessary to establish the plaintiff's prima facie case is in the hands of the defendant or a third party goes a long way toward showing good cause for discovery. But it is not the only factor. The trial court should consider whether the information the plaintiff seeks to obtain through formal discovery proceedings is readily available from other sources or can be obtained through informal discovery. The court should also consider the plaintiff's need for discovery in the context of the issues raised in the SLAPP motion.” (The Garment Workers Ctr. v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 1156, 1162.)

Discussion

Plaintiff, in pro per, seeks leave to propound requests for admission, special interrogatories, and document requests directed to 35 subject areas identified in paragraph 7 of his declaration, while the special motions to strike of the Grant Defendants and the Friedman Defendants are pending for hearing on October 29, 2026. Both sets of defendants oppose.

Here, Plaintiff has not shown good cause for the discovery. The proposed discovery is not "specified" within the meaning of section 425.16(g), is not tied to the issues actually raised in the Anti-SLAPP Motions, and is largely directed at testing Defendants' declarations.

Section 425.16(g) authorizes an order permitting "specified discovery," and the showing must be tailored to opposing the motion. (Britts, supra, 145 Cal.App.4th at p. 1125.) Plaintiff's declaration lists 35 numbered inquiries phrased as questions to an undifferentiated "you," alternating among "Grant or Rubin" and, in item 30, a group of eight individuals and firms. Plaintiff does not identify which defendant would receive which request, which of the three discovery devices he would use for which inquiry, or the text of any proposed request. The Court cannot order "specified discovery" on this record.

Good cause requires a showing that the specified discovery "is necessary for the plaintiff to oppose the motion and is tailored to that end" (Britts, supra, 145 Cal.App.4th at p. 1125), and that the plaintiff "explain what additional facts [he] expects to uncover" and why they are needed to carry his burden (Six4Three, supra, 109 Cal.App.5th at p. 659).

Here, Plaintiff's showing on this point consists of a single assertion: that all 35 items "relate specifically to plaintiff's contentions that moving parties concocted phony and false charges and had no basis whatsoever for their complaint." That describes the subject matter of the discovery – it does not explain its function. Plaintiff does not identify any ground asserted in either special motion to strike, does not explain what fact he expects the discovery to uncover, and does not connect any request to any element of his burden in opposing the motions. The papers do not address the threshold defenses at all.

It is Plaintiff's burden to make the requisite showing, and the Court will not supply the analysis he has not offered. Having failed to engage the grounds on which the motions rest, Plaintiff has not shown that any Defendant possesses evidence "needed by the plaintiff to establish its probability of prevailing on a claim." (1-800 Contacts, supra, 107 Cal.App.4th at p. 572.)

Conclusion

The Court DENIES Plaintiffs’ Motion for Relief from Discovery Stay pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16(g).
