If you desire to submit on the Court's tentative ruling, you may do so by e-mailing Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse until the morning of the motion hearing.
 
The e-mail address is SSCDEPT26@lacourt.org
 
The heading on your e-mail should contain the case name, number, hearing date, and that you submit. The message should indicate your name, contact information, and the party you represent. Please note that if you submit on the court's tentative ruling and do not appear, another party may appear to argue and the court may change its tentative ruling after the oral argument.

The deadline for a party to withdraw a pending motion, or take it off calendar, is TWO COURT DAYS before the hearing on the motion. The court will enter its tentative ruling on motions that remain on calendar after that deadline.
 
Please note, the above e-mail address is to inform the court of your submission on the tentative ruling. All other inquiries to this e-mail will not receive a response.
 
Parties may schedule remote appearances for hearings on motions via LACourtConnect (https://mylacourt.org/laccwelcome). Remote appearances are not permitted for trial or Applications for Order for Appearance and Examination proceedings absent leave of court.

Case Number: 22STLC01662    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Pacific Specialty Insurance Company’s Motion to Vacate Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial) as to Defendant Brittany Combs is granted.

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On March 14, 2022, Plaintiff Pacific Specialty Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Oscar Nava (“Defendant Nava”). Defendant Nava filed an Answer on April 26, 2022, and a Cross-Complaint against Brittany Combs on July 24, 2023. On August 3, 2023, Plaintiff added Brittany Combs to the Complaint as a defendant (“Defendant Combs”). Plaintiff dismissed Defendant Nava from the Complaint on July 25, 2024, and obtained default judgment against Defendant Combs on August 13, 2024. Plaintiff then filed an Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial) on September 9, 2024, with respect to Defendant Combs.

 

Plaintiff filed and served the instant Motion to Vacate Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial) as to Defendant Brittany Combs on August 10, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves pursuant to case law to vacate the Acknowledgement of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial) on account of its error. “It is, of course, the law that a satisfaction of judgment which has been filed and entered may be set aside by appropriate proceedings and for proper cause.” (Remillard Brick Co. v. Dandini (1950) 98 Cal.App.2d 617, 622.) Proper cause includes fraud, undue influence, and mistake. (Remillard Brick Co. v. Dandini (1950) 98 Cal.App.2d 617, 622.)

 

The Motion is supported by the declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel, who attests that Plaintiff settled its claims against Defendant Nava for $5,000.00 but inadvertently filed the Acknowledgement of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial) as to Defendant Combs. (Motion, Tapper Decl., ¶¶4-6.) Plaintiff discovered the error during its collection efforts against Defendant Combs. (Id. at ¶7.) Based on the incorrect filing of the Acknowledgement of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial), the Court grants the request to vacate it.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Pacific Specialty Insurance Company’s Motion to Vacate Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment (Partial) as to Defendant Brittany Combs is granted.

Case Number: 25STLC06561    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions is Granted. Defendant Micah Charles Roberts is ordered to pay sanctions of $460.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) served Requests for Admissions, Set One, on Defendant Micah Charles Roberts (“Defendant”) on December 5, 2025. (Motion, Ahearn Decl., Exh. A.) As of the filing of the instant Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted on January 30, 2026, Defendant has not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶¶1-2.) Plaintiff now moves for an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280. Defendant filed an opposition to the Motion, but neither argues nor declares that he served responses to the requests. (See Opp., Roberts Decl., ¶24) Instead, Defendant appears to argue regarding the merits of the action and points to a response dated January 8, 2026 stating that he would provide responses. (Ibid.)

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to deem requests for admission can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to serve responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Due to Defendant’s failure to timely serve verified responses to the requests and resultant waiver of objections, Plaintiff is entitled to an order deeming the Requests for Admission, Set One, admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.)

 

The Court is required to award sanctions under the moving statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) Sanctions are granted against Defendant in the amount of $460.00 based on two hours of attorney time billed at $200.00 per hour, plus the $60.00 filing fee. (Motion, Ahearn Decl., ¶3.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted and Request for Sanctions is Granted. Defendant Micah Charles Roberts is ordered to pay sanctions of $460.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

Case Number: 26STLC04879    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Andy Ramiro Contreras’ Motion to Quash Service of Summons is granted.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to Serve Defendant with Summons and Complaint is scheduled for 10/19/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On July 29, 2026, Plaintiff Allstate Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Andy Ramiro Contreras (“Defendant”). Plaintiff filed Proof of Service by Substituted Service on August 14, 2026. Defendant, in propria persona, filed and served the instant Motion to Quash Service of Summons on August 21, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant brings the Motion to Quash pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, subdivision (a)(1), which states in relevant part: “A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion for one or more of the following purposes: To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1), emphasis added.) A defendant has 30 days after the service of the summons to file a responsive pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 412.20, subd. (a)(3).) The Motion was timely filed less than a week after the date of purported service, August 17, 2026.

 

Substantively, “[w]hen a defendant challenges the court’s personal jurisdiction on the ground of improper service of process ‘the burden is on the plaintiff to prove the existence of jurisdiction by proving, inter alia, the facts requisite to an effective service.’” (Summers v. McClanahan

(2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 403, 413.) A proof of service containing a declaration from a registered process server invokes a rebuttable presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence, of the facts stated in the return. (Cal. Evid. Code, § 647; see American Express Centurion Bank v. Zara (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)

 

Defendant challenges the Proof of Service by Substituted Service filed by Plaintiff on August 14, 2026, which purports to demonstrate service at 8151 Broadleaf Ave., Panorama City, California. (Proof of Service, filed 08/14/26, ¶4.) The papers were purportedly delivered to “Stephanie Contreras - Co-occupant,” on August 7, 2026, and then mailed to that address on the same day. (Id. at ¶5.) The proof of service is signed by a registered process server. (Id. at ¶7.)

Defendant presents rebuttal evidence through his declaration, which attests that he moved from 8151 Broadleaf Ave. in 2020, and at the time of service lived at 1846 Fitzgerald Rd., Simi Valley, California. (Motion, Contreras Decl., ¶¶3, 6-8 and Exhs. B-C.) This evidence sufficiently disputes the contention in the proof of service that Defendant was served at his residence. The burden now shifts to Plaintiff to demonstrate that the facts in the proof of service—specifically that 8151 Broadleaf Ave. was Defendant’s residence in August 2026—are true. Plaintiff, however, has filed no opposition to the instant Motion to demonstrate that Defendant was served at his residence.

 

Therefore, the Court finds Plaintiff has failed to demonstrate service of the Summons and Complaint upon Defendant in accordance with the statutory requirements. The Court quashes service of the Summons upon Defendant and sets an Order to Show Cause regarding Plaintiff’s failure to serve Defendant with the Summons and Complaint.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Andy Ramiro Contreras’ Motion to Quash Service of Summons is granted.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to Serve Defendants with Summons and Complaint is scheduled for 10/19/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.
