Case Number: 24NWLC56591    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: Y

24NWLC56591: AMERICAN EXPRESS NATIONAL BANK vs DANE DEVOGELAERE

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed the Motion to Vacate Default Judgment and Motion to Stay filed by Defendant Dane DeVogelaere (“Defendant”) on May 29, 2026.  The Court has also reviewed the Opposition filed by Plaintiff American Express National Bank (“Plaintiff”) and Defendant’s reply.  The Court has also reviewed all relevant court records. 

Defendant states in his declaration, which was submitted under penalty of perjury, that he became aware of this lawsuit on May 22, 2026 when he received a Memorandum of Costs (MC-012).  Defendant’s Motion was filed days later on May 29, 2026. 

The Court notes that Default was entered by the Clerk on July 2, 2025.  Default Judgment was entered by the Clerk on July 19, 2025.  

The Court notes that Proof of Service of Summons by a registered process server by personal service was filed with the Court on April 15, 2025 creating a presumption of service under Evidence Code section 647.  Defendant’s declaration, which was signed under penalty of perjury, states that he was not personally served with the Summons and Complaint, or any other documents in this case until May 2026.  Although Defendant’s driver’s license, which is attached to his declaration, identifies the same address as the address where Defendant was purportedly served, Defendant states that the physical description of the person served does not match his physical description. 

The proof of service filed in this case describes the person served as “Gender: Male; Age: 36-40; Skin Tone: White; Hair: Brown; Eye Color: Brown; Height: 6 ft to 6ft 3 in; Weight 231-250 lbs.”  Defendant states under penalty of perjury that he has green eyes and blonde hair, and thus, he does not match the description provided by the process server.

Here, the Court finds that Defendant has rebutted the presumption of service and that there was no actual notice. “Actual notice,” within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, means genuine knowledge by the defendant, and has been strictly construed.  Relief under the statute is liberally granted to defendants so that cases may be resolved on their merits.  Ellard v. Conway (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 540, 547-548.     

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, the Court GRANTS the Motion and vacates and sets aside the default judgment.  The Court also vacates and sets aside the Writ of Execution that was issued by the Court on May 28, 2026.  The Court grants Defendant 20 days leave to file a responsive pleading.

The Court encourages the parties to discuss Defendant’s request to arbitrate.  The Court continues Defendant’s Motion to Stay for further briefing and hearing on October 1, 2026 at 10:30 a.m. in Department Y.   The Court notes that the Complaint did not attach a copy of the agreement between the parties.  The Court requests that Plaintiff submit a copy of the agreement if it opposes Defendant’s request to arbitrate.  To the extent the parties agree to arbitrate, the Court requests that Defendant take this motion off calendar

The Court also notes that the Defendant’s Motion to Tax Costs, which is set for hearing on July 7, 2026, appears to be moot.   At Defendant’s request, the Court will take that motion off calendar or deny it without prejudice as moot.

Clerk to give notice.

Case Number: 25NWLC13098    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC13098: LVNV FUNDING LLC vs SEAN HOBBS

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed the Motion to Dismiss (“Motion”) filed by Defendant Sean Hobbs (“Defendant”).  Although it appears that Defendant’s Motion was served on counsel for Plaintiff LVNV Funding LLC (“Plaintiff”), no opposition has been filed by Plaintiff.

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s Complaint should be dismissed with prejudice on the following grounds: (1) Plaintiff has failed to comply with the California Debt Buyer Act; (2) Plaintiff’s assignment evidence violates the Rule of Completeness; (3) Plaintiff’s account information contains material misrepresentations; and (4) Plaintiff cannot establish standing. 

Defendant cites Evidence Code section 356, Civil Code section 1788.52 and Code of Civil Procedure 430.10 in support of his request for dismissal with prejudice.  However, none of the cited statutes provide a basis for a dismissal of this action with prejudice.   As such, Defendant’s Motion is denied without prejudice.

The current trial date of February 3, 2027 remains on calendar.

Case Number: 25NWLC23190    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC23190: CROWN ASSET MANAGEMENT LLC vs ADRIAN MARTINEZ

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed the moving papers filed by Plaintiff Crown Asset Management LLC (“Plaintiff”) in support of its Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings.

The Court notes that on May 11, 2026, the date set for trial in this matter, counsel advised that this case had settled and based on that representation, the Court granted a trial continuance from May 11, 2026 to August 5, 2026.  Rather than submit a stipulation regarding settlement as expected by the Court, Plaintiff has filed this motion without properly meeting and conferring with Defendant Adrian Martinez (“Defendant”).

Code of Civil Procedure section 439 requires parties to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or video conference at least 5 days before filing a motion for judgment on the pleadings.  The parties must discuss whether the issues to be raised by the motion can be resolved to avoid court intervention, and the moving party must file a declaration detailing this compliance. 

Here, Plaintiff attempted to meet and confer with Defendant in July 2025 – almost a year ago.  Plaintiff has also failed to provide a copy of the meet and confer letter referenced in the supporting attorney declaration.  The Court finds that the attorney declaration submitted by Plaintiff does not demonstrate that that Plaintiff has satisfied the meet and confer requirement set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 439.

The Court will grant a continuance to cure the defect in the meet and confer process and requests that Plaintiff file a supplemental declaration pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 439 detailing its meet and confer efforts. 

Plaintiff’s motion will be continued to July 31, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Dept. Y.

The current trial date of August 5, 2026 remains on calendar.

Plaintiff to give notice.

Case Number: 25NWLC46505    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC46505: CAPITAL ONE, N.A. vs LARRY CLARK, JR

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed the Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment filed by Defendant Larry Clark Jr. (“Defendant”) on May 19, 2026.  The Court has also reviewed all relevant court records.  No opposition has been filed by Plaintiff Capital One, N.A. (“Plaintiff”).

The Court notes that Default was entered by the Clerk on October 30, 2025.  Default Judgment was entered by the Clerk on November 19, 2025.  

The Court notes that Proof of Service of Summons by a registered process server by personal service was filed with the Court on October 28, 2025 creating a presumption of service under Evidence Code section 647.  Defendant’s declaration, which was signed under penalty of perjury, states that he became aware of this lawsuit after judgment had been entered.  He also states that he did not have actual notice of this lawsuit before judgment was entered and acted promptly to seek relief from the Court.  

Although the address on the proof of service matches the address on Defendant’s motion, the Court finds that Defendant has rebutted the presumption of service and that there was no actual notice.  “Actual notice,” within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, means genuine knowledge by the defendant, and has been strictly construed.  Relief under the statute is liberally granted to defendants so that cases may be resolved on their merits.  Ellard v. Conway (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 540, 547-548.  

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b), the Court may also set aside any judgment that was obtained by mistake or surprise.  Here, even if there was actual notice, Defendant did not respond to the Complaint prior to the entry of judgment due to mistake or surprise.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 473(b) and 473.5, the Court GRANTS the Motion and sets aside and vacates the default judgment.  The Court also vacates and sets aside the Writ of Execution issued by the Court on April 10, 2026. 

The Court grants Defendant 20 days leave to file a responsive pleading.

Clerk to give notice.

Case Number: 25NWLC57014    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC57014: WELLS FARGO BANK, NA vs JULIE A CHEN, AN INDIVIDUAL

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed the moving papers filed by Plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (“Plaintiff”) in support of its Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, including the meet and confer letter dated May 19, 2026.  No opposition has been filed.

Pursuant to California Evidence Code sections 452 and 453, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s request for the Court to take judicial notice of:

  1. Plaintiff’s Complaint filed on September 29, 2025;
  2. The Court’s Order Deeming Plaintiff’s Requests for Admission as Admitted entered on May 18, 2026; and
  3. Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted, and the Requests for Admission submitted in support of that motion, filed on March 16, 2026.

The Court finds the Request for Admissions admitted cannot reasonably be controverted at this stage of the proceedings.  The Request for Admissions are tantamount to Defendant admitting the causes of action alleged by Plaintiff.

Plaintiff’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is GRANTED as the complaint states facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action and the Answer in conjunction with the admissions deemed admitted does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense.

The Court orders Judgment in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant Julie Chen for the principal sum of $6,736.08 plus costs in the amount of $285.00, for a total Judgment of $7,021.08.

The Proposed Judgment will be signed by the Court.

Case Number: 25NWLC64139    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC64139: JPMORGAN CHASE BANK N.A. vs NATASHA L BARBEE

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed the moving papers filed by Defendant Natasha Barbee (“Defendant”).  No opposition was filed by Plaintiff JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (“Plaintiff”).  The Court takes judicial notice of the Complaint filed on October 27, 2025 and the attached Cardmember Agreement.

Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2 provides that a court shall order arbitration if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate exists and that a party to the agreement refuses to arbitrate.  Once arbitration is compelled, the court must stay the action pending the outcome of the arbitration pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.4.

The Court notes that Defendant has elected to resolve Plaintiff’s claims by binding arbitration and has made this request to Plaintiff by filing this motion pursuant to the terms of the Cardmember Agreement.    

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1281.2 and 1281.4, Defendant's Motion GRANTED.  The proceedings are stayed pending arbitration with AAA or JAMS as identified in the agreement. 

The Court advances and vacates the current trial date of October 2, 2026.  The Court sets a Status Conference re Status of Arbitration on October 2, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. in Department Y.

Plaintiff to give notice.
