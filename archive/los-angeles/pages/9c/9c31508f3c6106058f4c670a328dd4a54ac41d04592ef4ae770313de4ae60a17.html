CASE:                              Rodney Richard, et al. v. The S Avenue Trust, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 24STCV16089

 

 

PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION PURSUANT TO CIVIL PROCEDURE CODE SECTION 377.31

     

 

MOVING PARTY:               Petitioner Lanisha Sharon Sims, as Successor in Interest of the Estate of Plaintiff Latonia Green

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      No opposition

 

 

I.         BACKGROUND

 

            On June 27, 2026, Plaintiffs, Latonia Green (“Green”), Rodney Richard, and Irani Springer, commenced this habitability against Defendants, The S Avenue Trust, 6 Angels LLC, and BMC Cub LLC. 

 

            On January 26, 2026, Ms. Green passed away.

 

            On July 1, 2025, Petitioner Lanisha Sharon Sims filed this motion for an order substituting herself as Ms. Green’s successor in interest. 

                             

            The motion is unopposed.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

            “A pending action or proceeding does not abate by the death of a party if the cause of action survives.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.21.)  “On motion after the death of a person who commenced an action or proceeding, the court shall allow a pending action or proceeding that does not abate to be continued by the decedent’s personal representative or, if none, by the decedent’s successor in interest.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.31.)  

 

            The person who seeks to commence an action or proceeding or to continue a pending action or proceeding as the decedent’s successor in interest shall execute and file an affidavit or a declaration under penalty of perjury under the laws of this state stating: (1) The decedent’s name, (2) The date and place of the decedent’s death, (3) “No proceeding is now pending in California for administration of the decedent’s estate,” (4) If the decedent’s estate was administered, a copy of the final order showing the distribution of the decedent’s cause of action to the successor in interest, (5) Either of the following, as appropriate, with facts in support thereof: (A) “The affiant or declarant is the decedent's successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) and succeeds to the decedent's interest in the action or proceeding”; (B) “The affiant or declarant is authorized to act on behalf of the decedent's successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) with respect to the decedent's interest in the action or proceeding”; (6) “No other person has a superior right to commence the action or proceeding or to be substituted for the decedent in the pending action or proceeding,” and (7) the statements are true, under penalty of perjury.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.32.)  

 

            A certified copy of the decedent’s death certificate shall be attached to the affidavit or declaration.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 377.32, subd. (c).) 

 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

            Upon review of the papers, the court finds the Declaration of Lanisha Sharon Sims does not comply with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 377.32.  Petitioner merely states she is Ms. Green’s next of kin.  (See Sims Decl., ¶ 4.)  There are no facts provided to support the assertion that Petitioner is Ms. Green’s successor in interest.

             

IV.       CONCLUSION 

 

The motion is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDUCE. 

 

Moving party to give notice.

CASE:                         Jane Doe K.P., et al. v. Los Angeles Unified School District, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                     24STCV33997

 

 

MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

 

I.         BACKGROUND

           

On July 28, 2026, Cindy K. Suh of Slater, Slater, Schulman, LLP, counsel for Plaintiff, Jane Doe J.A., filed this Motion to be Relieved As Counsel.¿ 

¿ 

¿           The motion is unopposed.¿ 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD  

¿ 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 (Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel) requires (1) notice of motion and motion to be directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion and declaration on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) the proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel—Civil form (MC-053)).¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

The court has discretion to allow an attorney to withdraw, and such a motion should be granted provided that there is no prejudice to the client, and it does not disrupt the orderly process of justice.¿ (See Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

III.      DISCUSSION

Plaintiff’s counsel seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for the following reasons: “It has become unreasonably difficult for this office to carry out Plaintiff’s representation effectively under CRPC 3-700(C)(1)(d).  Additionally, there has been a breakdown in the attorney-client relationship (Estate of Falco v. Decker (1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004,1014.).”  (Form MC-052.)   

Absent a showing of resulting prejudice, an attorney’s request for withdrawal should be granted.¿ (People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 406.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

Upon review, the court finds the Motion complies with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.¿  

¿ 

IV.       CONCLUSION¿ 

¿ 

Accordingly, the unopposed Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel is GRANTED and effective upon the filing of the proof of service of this signed order upon Plaintiff. ¿¿¿ 

Counsel to give notice.¿

CASE:                         Carlos Moz v. Solaris Trading Corporation, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV20856

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO QUASH OVERBROAD SUBPOENA ISSUED BY

DEFENDANT TO J & J TIRES AND WHEELS, INC. AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS IN THE AMOUNT OF $2,800.00

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Carlos Moz

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant Solaris Trading Corporation

 

 

I.         BACKGROUND

 

            On July 15, 2025, Plaintiff, Carlos Moz, commenced this action against defendants Solaris Trading Corporation dba Grand American Tires (“Solaris” or “Defendant”) and Stefan Bostanian for FEHA violations, wage and hour violations, and wrongful termination.

 

On April 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants which removed all but the wage and hour claims for failure to pay wages due (Labor Code §§ 201, 1182.12, 1194, 1194.2), failure to pay minimum wages (Labor Code §§ 1182.12, 1194, 1194.2, 1197), failure to pay overtime compensation (Labor Code §§ 510, 1194), failure to provide rest breaks (Labor Code §§ 226.7, 512), and failure to provide itemized wage and hour statements (Labor Code §§ 226 et seq.). 

 

            On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed this motion to quash Solaris’s subpoena issued to Plaintiff’s subsequent employer, J & J Tires and Wheels, Inc. (“J & J”).  Plaintiff seeks sanctions against Solaris and its counsel of record.

 

            On September 3, 2026, Solaris filed an opposition.

 

            On September 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

As an initial matter, Defendant’s motion was not timely filed.  Pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d), the court declines to consider Defendant’s opposition.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court's own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands such as unreasonable violations of the right of privacy.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1.)¿¿ 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿ 

In making an order pursuant to motion made under¿subdivision (c) of Section 1987¿or under¿Section 1987.1, the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.”¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 1987.2.)¿ 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

            Plaintiff moves to quash the subpoena to J & J on the grounds it “(1) constitutes an impermissible fishing expedition, (2) seeks to impinge on Plaintiff’s and third parties’ privacy rights, and (3) seeks documents which are not directly relevant to this action ….”  (Mot., p. 2:22-24.)  The court agrees in considerable part with Plaintiff’s third argument.  The subpoena seeks the following information:

 

PURSUANT TO THE ATTACHED SUBPOENA, PLEASE PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING DOCUMENTS, WRITINGS AND ELECTRONICALLY STORED INFORMATION PERTAINING FROM JANUARY 1, 2022 THROUGH THE PRESENT REQUESTED BELOW:

 

1. Mr. Moz’s resume;

2. Mr. Moz’s curriculum vitae;

3. Mr. Moz’s personnel file;

4. Mr. Moz’s independent contractor file;

5. Mr. Moz’s applications for employment;

6. Mr. Moz’s applications for an independent contractor relationship;

7. Mr. Moz’s earnings records;

8. Background checks of Mr. Moz;

9. All w-2s issues to Mr. Moz;

10. All 1099s issued to Mr. Moz;

11. Mr. Moz’s termination records;

12. All contracts entered into between J & J Tires and Carlos Moz;

13. resumes;

14. payroll records;

15. contracts;

16. job descriptions for all positions worked;

17. timesheets;

18. unemployment claims;

19. response to unemployment claims;

20. termination of employment;

21. complaints of failure to pay wages;

22. complaints of unpaid overtime;

23. complaints of failure to provide meal periods;

24. complaints of failure to provide rest periods;

25. reasonable accommodations;

26. requests for accommodation;

27. work restrictions;

28. medical leaves to seek treatment;

29. doctor’s authorizations to leave work;

30. doctor's orders to be off work;

31. doctor's releases to return to work;

32. disabilities;

33. training;

34. vocational assessments;

35. all communications with Mr. Moz pertaining to job assignments and/or referrals.

 

(Carvajal Decl., ¶ 7, Ex. 1.)  Following the dismissal of Plaintiff’s FEHA and wrongful termination claims, only Plaintiff’s wage and hour claims remain.  On its face, the subpoena does not seek any information relevant to Plaintiff’s for failure to pay wages due, failure to pay minimum wages, failure to pay overtime compensation, failure to provide rest breaks, and failure to provide itemized wage and hour statements, or any defenses thereto.  Instead, the subpoena appears tailored to discover information regarding claims that are no longer at issue (e.g., termination of employment, reasonable accommodations, requests for accommodation).  The subpoena does not seek relevant information and is therefore overbroad.  Having so found, the court will quash the subpoena.

           

IV.       CONCLUSION

           

The motion to quash is GRANTED.  Defendant and its counsel of record are ordered to pay sanctions in the sum of $1,260 within 30 days of this order.

Case Number: 25STCV26901    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 16, 2026                          TRIAL DATE:  January 3, 2028

                                                          

CASE:                         Sean Christopher Sabo v. Luciano Lagunas Guevarra, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV26901

 

 

PLAINTIFF SEAN CHRISTOPHER SABO’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL DEFENDANTS EVANS & SON, INC.’S AND LUCIANO LAGUNAS GUEVARRA’S VERIFIED RESPONSES WITHOUT OBJECTIONS TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES, REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

PLAINTIFF SEAN CHRISTOPHER SABO’S MOTIONS FOR AN ORDER THAT THE TRUTH OF ANY MATTERS SPECIFIED IN REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION, SET ONE TO DEFENDANTS EVANS & SON, INC. AND LUCIANO LAGUNAS GUEVARRA BE DEEMED ADMITTED, AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

 

PLAINTIFF SEAN CHRISTOPHER SABO’S MOTIONS TO QUASH OR MODIFY DEPOSITION SUBPOENA FOR BUSINESS RECORDS DIRECTED TO CUSTODIAN OF RECORDS OF MEMORIAL CARE MEDICAL GROUP

 

            This is a motor vehicle negligence action.  Before the court are eleven motions filed by Plaintiff Sean Christopher Sabo: (1) six motions to compel Defendants, Evans & Son, Inc. and Luciano Lagunas Guevarra, to provide verified responses without objections to Plaintiff’s first set of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Request for Production of Documents; (2) two motions to deem the truth of any matters specified in Plaintiff’s Requests for Admission, Set One, admitted against Defendants; (3) three motions to quash Defendants’ subpoenas issued to the custodian of records of Memorial Care Medical Group.  The court begins by addressing together the motions to compel and motions to deem admitted.

 

I.         DISCUSSION RE MOTIONS TO COMPEL AND DEEM ADMITTED

 

            A.  Background

                       

On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed the motions to compel and motions to deem admitted.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendants and their counsel of record. 

 

On September 3, 2026, Defendants filed an omnibus opposition.

            On September 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed an omnibus reply. 

            The court notes that the motions are mostly moot.  Defendants served verified responses on June 5, 2026.  (Morales Decl., ¶ 6; Khaefi Decl., ¶ 9.)  The court proceeds to consider only the request for sanctions.

B.  Legal Standard¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

If the court finds that a party has unsuccessfully made or opposed a motion to compel responses, the court “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(c); 2031.300(c).) With respect to untimely responses to admissions requests, sanctions are mandatory.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(a).)    

 

Sanctions may also be imposed against counsel if a party seeking sanctions against an attorney offers sufficient evidence of a misuse of the discovery process, and the attorney fails to demonstrate that he or she did not recommend that conduct. (Kwan Software Engineering, Inc. v. Hennings (2020) 58 Cal.App.5th 57, 81 (Kwan Software).) 

           

            C.  Application

 

The court will award sanctions.  It is undisputed Plaintiff properly served Defendants with the at-issue discovery on February 18, 2026, and that Plaintiff provided Defendants with three extensions to respond.  After the last extension, responses were due on April 27, 2026, yet Defendants did not provide any responses until after the filing of these motions.  Given this background, sanctions are warranted.  In the context of admissions requests, they are mandatory.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(a).)   

 

Defendants argue it would be unjust to impose sanctions because “Defense counsel and defendants were dealing with extraordinarily busy work schedules and defense counsel was preparing for a long cause trial which went for it on May 8, 2026 and concluded on June 15, 2026. The responses required consultation, investigation and legal research which took time. As the court and parties are aware, proposed responses have to be reviewed and approved by the clients (sic)  Despite, these challenges, responses were served on June 5, 2026 a little more

than two months after they were originally due.”  (Opp., p. 4:19-24.)  The argument is not well taken.  The original deadline to provide discovery responses was seven weeks before Defense counsel’s long cause trial began.  Defendants nor their counsel identify any persuasive reason for their failure to comply with their discovery obligations in a timely fashion.  Further, Defense counsel does not meet their burden to show they did not counsel discovery abuse.  Accordingly, sanctions are imposed against each defendant and their counsel of record in the reduced sum of $3,520.00.¿¿¿ 

           

            D.  Conclusion

 

The motions are Moot.  Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is Granted.  Each defendant and their counsel of record are ordered to pay, jointly and severally, sanctions in the sum of $3,520 within 30 days of this order.¿ 

 

II.        DISCUSSION RE MOTIONS TO QUASH

 

            A.  Background

 

            On July 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed three motions to quash, modify, or limit the scope of subpoenas that Defendants directed to the custodian of records for (i) Memorial Care Medical Group; (ii) Memorial Care Medical Group, Radiology; (iii) Memorial Care Medical Group, Billing.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendants and their counsel of record. 

 

            On September 9, 2026, Defendants filed a combined opposition.[1]

 

            On September 10, 2026, Plaintiff filed an objection to Defendants’ combined opposition.

 

Because the motions to quash raise the same substantive issues, the court addresses those motions together.     

 

            B.  Legal Standard

If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court's own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands such as unreasonable violations of the right of privacy.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1.)¿¿ 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿ 

In making an order pursuant to motion made under¿subdivision (c) of Section 1987¿or under¿Section 1987.1, the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.”¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 1987.2.)¿ 

            C.  Application

 

            For the time period from 06/03/2021 to present, the subpoenas seek the following records:

Including, but not limited to, any and all medical records of treatment rendered at Memorial Care Orange Coast Medical Center, 18111 Brookhurst Street, Fountain Valley, California 92708-6728.

All documents and records in any format or method including, but not limited to, all medical records, intake forms, patient completed forms and/or documents, correspondence, all office records and reports, lab reports, pharmacy and prescription records, physical therapy records, sign-in sheets, all descriptions of exercises prescribed, documentation which indicate date, and time of patient’s appointments, insurance documents, all radiology reports and readings, and any other documents maintained pertaining to the care, treatment and examination of the patient.

…

Including, but not limited to, any and all Films, records of treatment rendered at Memorial Care Coverage Medical Center 18111 Brookhurst Street, Fountain Valley, California 92708-6728.

COPIES of all original x-rays, films, CT scans, MRIs and any other scan or images taken and/or maintained, including a comprehensive list of all dates and body parts of all films, CT scans, MRIs and all other images or scans provided. All images and films must be provided on CD if possible.

…

Including, but not limited to, any and all Billing records of treatment rendered at Memorial Care Orange Coast Medical Center, 18111 Brookhurst Street, Fountain Valley, California 92708-6728.

All itemized statements of the billing charges and/or consolidated statement of benefits, with diagnostic and procedure codes including CPT and ICD-9/ICD-10 coding and all HCFA, UB08 and UB92 bills, to include the total charges private or governmental; any amounts written off by the provider, and any amounts that are the patient’s responsibility and explanation of benefits, payment history, records of any liens, and insurance billing or payment information, “emergency room physicians bills and “radiology billing from all sources, to include any computer billing or billing stored in any format and payment software that contains said information.

Please note: If Emergency Room (ER) physicians billing and/or Radiology billing is maintained by a separate department or third party billing entity, you are requested to provide the full name, address, phone number, email address and any additional contact information necessary to facilitate a records request.

Plaintiff argues the subpoenas are overbroad because “the documents and things identified in the subject deposition subpoena for business records are not limited to conditions of Plaintiff which are tendered in this case, and the category of documents is not reasonably particularized, and not properly limited as to time, even with respect to conditions of Plaintiff that are the subject of his present claims.”  (Mot., p. 9:2-5.)  After reviewing the subpoenas, the court finds that they are partly overbroad.  The subpoenas are not limited to Plaintiff’s claimed injuries.  The court will modify the subpoenas accordingly.  However, the court finds the temporal scope is reasonable and declines to modify the time period.  

 

The court acknowledges that it is difficult, if not, impracticable, to limit the scope of the subpoenas to Plaintiff’s claimed injuries. However, there is a workable solution.  In addition to, and in furtherance of limiting the scope of the subpoenas, the court will allow Plaintiff the “first look” at the produced documents and set deadlines around redactions (if necessary) and motion practice (if necessary).  In this way, the court balances the intrusion into Plaintiff’s privacy with Defendants’ right to defend against Plaintiff’s claims.  

 

            D.  Conclusion

 

The motions to quash are GRANTED IN PART. The subpoenas are limited to documents related to Plaintiff’s claimed injuries.  Plaintiff will have first look of the documents.  Within 10 days of the production, Plaintiff must redact the documents, as necessary, and serve the redacted documents to Defendant.  Plaintiff is to retain an unredacted copy of the production. 

 

Within 10 days of service of the redacted documents, Defendant is to file any motions, as necessary, regarding the redactions.   

 

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

 

III.      DISPOSITIONS

 

1.  The motions to compel and motions to deem admitted are Moot.  Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is Granted.  Each defendant and their counsel of record are ordered to pay, jointly and severally, sanctions in the sum of $3,520 within 30 days of this order.¿ 

 

2.  The motions to quash are Granted In Part.  The subpoenas are limited to documents related to Plaintiff’s claimed injuries.  Plaintiff will have first look of the documents.  Within 10 days of the production, Plaintiff must redact the documents, as necessary, and serve the redacted documents to Defendant.  Plaintiff is to retain an unredacted copy of the production. 

 

Within 10 days of service of the redacted documents, Defendant is to file any motions, as necessary, regarding the redactions.   

 

Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is Denied.

 

Plaintiff to give notice, unless waived.

CASE:                         Dorothy Caronna v. Déjà Vu Services, Inc.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STLC04848

 

 

DEFENDANT DEJA VU SERVICES, INC.’S MOTION FOR SANCTIONS

PURSUANT TO C.C.P. § 128.5(g)

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND

 

            Plaintiff Dorothy Caronna (“Plaintiff”) performed as a dancer at the Déjà Vu Showgirls at 7350 Coldwater Canyon Ave in North Hollywood.  Plaintiff alleges that she was forced to engage in lap dances with violent customers under threat of termination and suffered sexual battery throughout her employment.   Plaintiff also alleges that defendant Déjà Vu Services, Inc. (“Defendant”) stole her wages.  

 

            Before the court are two motions: (1) Defendant’s motion for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5(g), and (2) Plaintiff’s motion for leave to amend.  The court addresses these motions in turn.

 

I.         DISCUSSION RE MOTION FOR SANCTIONS

 

On October 14, 2025, Plaintiff filed the operative Second Amended Judicial Council Form Complaint (“SAC”) against Defendant for (1) general negligence; (2) sexual battery; (3) intentional infliction of emotional distress; and (4) fraud.

 

On January 6, 2026, Defendant filed a demurrer and motion to strike portions of the SAC.

 

On May 12, 2026, the court sustained in part the demurrer without leave to amend.

 

            On June 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for sanctions for Defendant’s purported failure to discover its demurrer had been rejected by the clerk and which caused a delay in the proceedings.

 

            On August 12, 2026, the court denied Plaintiff’s motion for sanctions.

 

Defendant’s Motion for Sanctions

On July 10, 2026, Defendant filed this motion for sanctions for Plaintiff’s failure to withdraw her June 12, 2026 motion for sanctions.

 

On July 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

            On September 9, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

            B.  Analysis

           

Defendant seeks sanctions against Plaintiff for her refusal to withdraw her June 12, 2026 sanctions motion.  Plaintiff’s sanctions motion sought $100,000 for (what Plaintiff now characterizes as) Defendant’s negligent conduct in failing to discover the rejection of Defendant’s demurrer filing.  As a result, Defendant argues it incurred expenses in filing an opposition to Plaintiff’s sanctions motion and appearing at the hearing. 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5, subdivision (a) provides, “A trial court may order a party, the party’s attorney, or both, to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by another party as a result of actions or tactics, made in bad faith, that are frivolous or solely intended to cause unnecessary delay.”  Section 128.5 further provides, “If the alleged action or tactic is the making or opposing of a written motion or the filing and service of a complaint, cross-complaint, answer, or other responsive pleading that can be withdrawn or appropriately corrected, a notice of motion shall be served as provided in Section 1010, but shall not be filed with or presented to the court, unless 21 days after service of the motion or any other period as the court may prescribe, the challenged action or tactic is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5, subd. (f)(1)(B).)

 

Here, the court finds that Defendant’s motion for sanctions is meritorious.  As discussed in the court’s September court found Plaintiff’s sanctions motion to be procedurally and substantively defective. (See Minute Order, 08/12/26.)  Stated another way, Plaintiff’s motion was frivolous.  Indeed, the relief requested therein (sanctions for $100,000) was simply not tailored to address the alleged conduct (Defendant’s alleged delay tactic by not discovering the rejection of its demurrer to the SAC).  Plaintiff does not raise any arguments to support a contrary finding.  Instead, Plaintiff mainly rehashes arguments she raised in connection to her sanctions motion.  Given the foregoing, and Defendant’s satisfaction of the 21-day safe harbor provision (see Critchlow Decl. ¶ 19, Exs. O, P), the court finds sanctions against Plaintiff are warranted.

 

C.  Conclusion

 

Defendant’s motion for sanctions is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to pay sanctions in the reduced sum of $900 within 45 days of this order.

 

II.        DISCUSSION RE MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND

 

            A.  Background

 

            On July 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for leave to amend the prayer for relief in her complaint.  Plaintiff seeks leave to request $250,000,000 for emotional distress, pain and suffering, $250,000,000 in punitive damages, and $100,000 for fraud damages.

 

            On September 2, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

 

            On September 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

            B.  Analysis

 

            After reviewing the moving papers, opposition, and reply, the court will deny Plaintiff’s motion without prejudice.  A plaintiff cannot state the amount demanded for a claim of punitive or emotional damages.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 425.10(b); see also Austin v. Regents of Univ. of California (1979) 89 Cal.App.3d 354, 359 [“Plaintiff sought leave to amend his pleading to allege damages in addition to those originally pleaded resulting from his alleged distress. Denial of that request was proper. Under subdivision (b) of section 425.10 of the Code of Civil Procedure, no dollar amount of damages may be stated in an action for personal injuries.”].)  Accordingly, Plaintiff cannot state in the complaint a specific amount sought for emotional distress damages or punitive damages.

 

            However, as to Plaintiff’s request to add $100,000 for fraud damages, leave is warranted.  Code of Civil Procedure section 425.10(a) requires that “[i]f the recovery of money or damages is demanded, the amount demanded shall be stated.” 

 

            C.  Conclusion

 

            The motion for leave to amend is GRANTED IN PART, DENIED IN PART.  Within 10 court days of this order, Plaintiff is ordered to serve and filed a Third Amended Complaint which specifies the damages sought for her fraud claim, omits the amount demands on her claims for emotional distress damages and punitive damages, and omits the sexual battery claim.

 

III.      DISPOSITIONS

 

            1.  Defendant’s motion for sanctions is Granted. Plaintiff is ordered to pay sanctions in the reduced sum of $900 within 45 days of this order.

 

            2.  Plaintiff’s motion for leave to amend is Granted In Part, Denied In Part. Within 10 court days of this order, Plaintiff is ordered to serve and filed a Third Amended Complaint which specifies the damages sought for her fraud claim, omits the amount demands on her claims for emotional distress damages and punitive damages, and omits the sexual battery claim.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice, unless waived.

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 16, 2026                                      TRIAL DATE:  Not set

                                                          

CASE:                         James Townsend v. City of Los Angeles

 

CASE NO.:                 26STCV12002

 

 

DEFENDANT CITY OF LOS ANGELES’S DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF JAMES TOWNSEND’S FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant City of Los Angeles

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      Plaintiff James Townsend

 

 

            Plaintiff James Townsend (“Townsend” or “Plaintiff”) was employed by defendant City of Los Angeles (“City” or “Defendant”) as a Commanding Officer of the Mission Area in the Los Angeles Police Department (“LAPD”).  In 2024, Towsend was terminated following an investigation finding he had ordered the removal of photos which had evidentiary value to Internal Affairs.  Townsend alleges that the City retaliated against him because the City believed Townsend might disclose criminal misconduct by police officers or severe instances of noncompliance with LAPD policy, for engaging in the protected activity of supporting and advancing internal exposure of unlawful “ghost stop” practices within the Mission Gang Enforcement Detail and challenging the evidentiary basis of the LAPD’s allegations into Townsend’s conduct.

 

I.        INTRODUCTION

 

           On April 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against Defendant for whistleblower protection, intentional infliction of emotional distress, and defamation.

 

            On July 20, 2026, Defendant filed this demurrer to the second and third causes of action.

 

            On September 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

 

            On September 9, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

 

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿ (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)¿ When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.¿ (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)¿ “Because a demurrer challenges defects on the face of the complaint, it can only refer to matters outside the pleading that are subject to judicial notice.”¿ (Arce ex rel. Arce v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 556.) 

 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

            A.  Second Cause of Action for Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress                                   

            Plaintiff concedes he cannot maintain this cause of action against City.  (See Opp., pp. 2:18, 6:14-15; see also Gov. Code, § 815.3(a).)  Accordingly, the demurrer to the second cause of action is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

            B.  Third Cause of Action for Defamation

 

            City argues the defamation claim fails because (1) it is not grounded on a statute; (2) it is barred by litigation and official proceeding privileges; (3) it is barred by the statute of limitations. Each of these arguments are meritorious.

 

First, Government Code section 815 states, in relevant part: “Except as otherwise provided by statute: (a) A public entity is not liable for an injury, whether such injury arises out of an act or omission of the public entity or a public employee or any other person.” The Legislative Committee Comment to Section 815 states: “This section abolishes all common law or judicially declared forms of liability for public entities....” (Legis. Com. com., 32 West's Ann. Gov. Code (1995), foll. § 815, p. 167.)  Here, the Complaint does not identify a statutory basis to support his defamation claim.

Second, the litigation privilege, codified at Civil Code section 47, “bars a civil action for damages for communications made ‘[i]n any … (2) judicial proceeding, (3) in any other official proceeding authorized by law, or (4) in the initiation or course of any other proceeding authorized by law and reviewable pursuant to [statutes governing writs of mandate] ….’” (Civ. Code, § 47(b).)  An internal investigation is an “official proceeding authorized by law.” (Jeffra v. California State Lottery (2019) 39 Cal.App.5th 471, 482; Laker v. Board of Trustees of California State University (2019) 32 Cal.App.5th 745, 770 [“The ‘official proceeding’ privilege has been interpreted broadly to protect communication to or from governmental officials which may precede the initiation of formal proceedings.”]; Green v. Cortez (1984) 151 Cal.App.3d 1068, 1073 [“police investigation” into alleged misconduct by a police officer is an “official proceeding authorized by law”]).  Here, Plaintiff’s defamation claim is premised upon the dissemination within the LAPD of the allegedly false statement that Plaintiff knowingly ordered the removal of items with potential evidentiary value.  (Complaint, ¶ 62.)  Critically, the statements were made during and in connection to an internal personnel investigation.  (Complaint, ¶¶ 61-68.) 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the third cause of action is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.

IV.       CONCLUSION

 

            The demurrers are Sustained.  Leave to amend is Granted as to the third cause of action only.

 

            Plaintiff is ordered to serve and file the First Amended Cross-Complaint within 15 days of this order.
