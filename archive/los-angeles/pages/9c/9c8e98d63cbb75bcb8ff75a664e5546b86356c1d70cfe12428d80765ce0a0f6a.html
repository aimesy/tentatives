[Tentative] Order RE: MOTION FOR ATTORNEY FEES

 

 

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                Plaintiff Norman Taylor & Associates, APC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Defendant Genesis Motor America erroneously sued as Hyundai Motor America

 

The Court considered the moving papers, opposition, and reply filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

            This is a standard Song-Beverly consumer warranty action.

            On December 11, 2024, Plaintiff Norman Taylor & Associates, APC (“Plaintiff”) brought this action against Defendants Genesis Motor America erroneously sued as Hyundai Motor America (“Defendant”) and Glendale Hyundai (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for (1) violation of the Song-Beverly Act – breach of express warranty, (2) violation of the Song-Beverly Act – breach of implied warranty, (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act – section 1793.2, and (4) negligent repair. The causes of action arise out of Plaintiff’s purchase of a 2023 Genesis G80 AWD 3.5T Sport Prestige (the “Vehicle”) for eventual sale to retail buyers.

            On May 5, 2025, the parties filed notice of settlement of the entire case.

            On May 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for attorneys’ fees.

            On July 9, 2026, Defendant filed its opposition.

            On September 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

EVIDENTIARY OBJECTIONS

            Plaintiff’s Objections to the Declarations of Donna Hooper and Soheyl Tahsildoost:

            Overruled: 1-12.

LEGAL STANDARD

            Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d), permits a prevailing buyer under the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act to recover costs, expenses, and attorneys' fees reasonably incurred in connection with the commencement and prosecution of the action.

The lodestar method is the primary means of calculating a reasonable fee award. (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 818-819.) The Court begins with the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate and may adjust the resulting figure as appropriate. The Court retains broad discretion to reduce excessive, duplicative, or otherwise unreasonable billing. (Wilkerson v. Sullivan (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 443, 448; Horsford v. Board of Trustees of California State University (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 395.)

DISCUSSION

            Plaintiff moves for attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses in the amount of $30,358.50.

A.    Prevailing Party

Defendant does not dispute that Plaintiff is the prevailing party. Accordingly, Plaintiff is entitled to recover reasonable attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses pursuant to Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d).

B.     Timeliness

Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys’ fees is untimely. This case was dismissed on November 21, 2025, and the notice of dismissal was served on November 24, 2025. The instant motion was not filed until May 13, 2026. Defendant argues that Plaintiff had 60 days from service of the notice of dismissal to file the instant motion, pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1702, subdivision (b)(1).

“A notice of motion to claim attorney's fees for services up to and including the rendition of judgment in the trial court-including attorney's fees on an appeal before the rendition of judgment in the trial court-must be served and filed within the time for filing a notice of appeal under rules 8.104 and 8.108 in an unlimited civil case….” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1702, subd. (b)(1).) “[A] notice of appeal must be filed on or before the earliest of: (A) 60 days after the superior court clerk serves on the party filing the notice of appeal a document entitled "Notice of Entry" of judgment or a filed-endorsed copy of the judgment, showing the date either was served; (B) 60 days after the party filing the notice of appeal serves or is served by a party with a document entitled "Notice of Entry" of judgment or a filed-endorsed copy of the judgment, accompanied by proof of service; or (C) 180 days after entry of judgment.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.104, subd. (a)(1).)

Defendant attaches a copy of the Court’s November 24, 2025 entry of dismissal and a copy of the electronic service email of the entered request for dismissal served on Defendant reflecting the service date of November 24, 2025. (Hooper Decl., ¶ 14, Exh. D.) Applying the plain language of rule 8.104, the November 24, 2025 service of the entry of dismissal would trigger the deadline for filing a motion for attorneys’ fees, requiring the motion for attorneys’ fees to be filed by the 60-day deadline prescribed in rule 8.104(a)(1)(B).

Plaintiff’s counsel declares that Plaintiff did not receive notice of entry of dismissal. (Wirtz Decl., ¶ 8.) However, the notice of dismissal states that Plaintiff filed the request for dismissal on November 21, 2025 and that it was entered the same day. The Court notified counsel of the entry of dismissal on November 24, 2025.

Counsel for Plaintiff served the notice of dismissal on Defendant on November 24, 2025. Based on these facts, Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys’ fees is untimely.

Accordingly, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys’ fees.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Plaintiff Norman Taylor & Associates, APC’s motion for attorneys’ fees.

Moving party is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

David Flores, an individual,

                              vs.

 

Luis Enrique Flores Martinez, Defender Private Security, LLC, Risen International Group, Inc., and DOES 1-10,

 

                              Defendants.

25NNCV07078

[Tentative] Order RE:

 

MOTION TO DEEM ADMITTED REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION, SET ONE

 

MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION, SET ONE

AND RELATED CROSS-ACTION.

 

MOVING PARTY:                Plaintiff David Flores

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       None.

The Court considered the moving papers filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

            On October 6, 2026, Plaintiff David Flores (“Plaintiff”) brought this action against Defendants Luis Enrique Flores Martinez (“Defendant”), Defender Private Security, LLC (“Defender”), and Risen International Group, Inc. (“Risen”), alleging causes of action for (1) assault, (2) battery, (3) negligent training, (4) negligent supervision, (5) negligence, and (6) negligent hiring. Plaintiff alleges that he suffered injuries after Defendant slammed him into the ground in the lobby of The Alhambra Hotel.

            On November 12, 2025, Defendant filed his answer to Plaintiff’s complaint.

            On December 2, 2025, Risen filed its answer and a cross-complaint against Defendant and Defender, alleging causes of action for (1) total indemnity, (2) partial indemnity, (3) express indemnity, and (4) declaratory relief.

            On December 11, 2025, the Court entered default as to Defender only.

            On June 10, 2026, the Court dismissed the entire complaint with prejudice as to Risen only.

            On July 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motions to deem admitted Requests for Admission, Set One (the “RFAs”), and to compel Defendant’s responses to Requests for Production, Set One (the “RFPs”).

            No opposition was filed.

LEGAL STANDARD

A.    Requests for Admission

A party must respond to¿requests for admission¿within 30 days after service of such requests. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)¿When¿a party¿fails to¿respond¿timely, the propounding party may¿seek¿a Court order¿deeming¿the¿requests for admission¿admitted. (Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2033.280.) An untimely responding party waives all objections, including privilege, unless they¿subsequently¿serve responses in substantial compliance with the Civil Discovery Act, and they¿demonstrate¿that their failure is the result of mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd.¿(a); § 2033.280, subd.¿(a).)¿ 

For untimely responses to¿requests for admission,¿the Court shall deem the requests for admission admitted unless the¿responding party serves a proposed code compliant response prior to the hearing. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd.¿(c).)¿

B.     Requests for Production

“[A]¿party may propound a supplemental demand to inspect, copy, test, or sample any later acquired or discovered documents, tangible things, land or other property, or electronically stored information in the possession, custody, or control of the party on whom the demand is made.”¿(Code Civ. Proc.,¿§¿2031.050, subd.¿(a).)¿“Within 30 days after service of a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the party to whom the demand is directed shall serve the original of the response to it on the party making the demand.”¿(Code Civ. Proc.,¿§¿2031.260, subd.¿(a).)¿

“If a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to¿serve¿a timely¿response to it¿…¿The¿party making the demand may move for an order compelling response to the¿demand.”¿(Code Civ. Proc.,¿§¿2031.300, subd. (b).)¿“The party to whom the demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed waives any objection to the demand, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product.”¿(Code Civ. Proc.,¿§¿2031.300, subd.¿(a).)¿¿ 

DISCUSSION

            Plaintiff requests the Court issue an order deeming admitted the RFAs and compelling Defendant’s responses to the RFPs.

            Plaintiff served RFAs and RFPs on Defendant on December 12, 2025. (Farber Decls., ¶ 2, Exhs. A.) Defendant’s responses were due on or before January 19, 2026, accounting for five additional days for mail service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1013, subd. (a).) On January 23, 2026, after Defendant did not timely respond to the RFAs and RFPs, Plaintiff’s counsel sent him a letter requesting to meet and confer and warning that Plaintiff would file motions to compel. (Farber Decls., ¶ 2, Exhs. B.) On March 4, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel sent Defendant a second letter. (Farber Decls., ¶ 3, Exhs. C.) Plaintiff’s counsel has also attempted to reach Defendant by telephone and email, but Defendant has not responded. (Farber Decls., ¶ 4, Exhs. D.) Defendant has not filed an opposition to the instant motions.

            Given Defendant’s failure to provide responses to the RFAs and RFPs, the Court grants Plaintiff’s motions to deem admitted the RFAs and to compel Defendant’s responses to the RFPs.

SANCTIONS

            Plaintiff requests that the Court award monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff against Defendant in the amount of $1,184.20 for the motion to deem admitted RFAs and $1,102.20 for the motion to compel responses to the RFPs.

            When a request for monetary sanctions is concurrently filed with a motion to compel responses, “[t]he court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the¿reasonable expenses,¿including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd.¿(a).) Additionally, “If a monetary sanction is authorized by any provision of this title, the court shall impose that sanction unless it finds that one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanction unjust.” (Ibid.) Additionally, Code of Civil Procedure¿section¿2023.030, subdivision¿(a) provides that the Court may impose a monetary sanction on a party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of¿that conduct. Misuse of the discovery process includes¿“[f]ailing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery.”¿(Code Civ. Proc.,¿§¿2023.010, subd.¿(d).)

            Defendant failed to provide responses or file an opposition, and the Court does not find substantial justification or the existence of circumstances that make the imposition of sanctions unjust.

            Plaintiff’s counsel cites her hourly rate as $820.00 per hour and her second-year associate’s hourly rate as $345.00 per hour. (Farber Decls., ¶ 6.) The Court finds awarding the requested sanctions for preparation of each motion as excessive.  The two motions are similar and contain nearly identical attorney declarations.

            Therefore, the Court elects to award attorney’s fees against Defendant in the reduced amount of $495.40, comprised of one hour of attorney work at Plaintiff’s counsel’s second year associate’s hourly rate of $345.00 per hour, plus the filing fees and costs of service by mail associated with each motion.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff David Flores’ motions to deem admitted Requests for Admission, Set One, against Defendant Luis Enrique Flores Martinez.

The Court GRANTS Plaintiff David Flores’ motion to compel Defendant Luis Enrique Flores Martinez’s responses to Requests for Production, Set One.

The Court awards sanctions in the reduced amount of $495.40 against Defendant, payable to counsel for Plaintiff within twenty (20) days of this order.

Moving party is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[Tentative] Order RE: DEMURRER AND MOTION TO STRIKE PORTIONS OF PLAINTIFF’S COMPLAINT

 

 

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                Defendants California Roofline, Inc. and Yovel Tubul

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Plaintiff Leron Inc.

The Court considered the moving papers, opposition, and reply filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

            On April 22, 2026, Plaintiff Leron Inc. (“Plaintiff”) brought this action against Defendants California Roofline Inc. and Yovel Tubul (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for (1) breach of contract, (2) negligence, (3) breach of implied warranty, and (4) intentional misrepresentations. The causes of action arise out of an alleged verbal agreement to perform construction services between the parties.

            On July 14, 2026, Defendants filed the instant demurrer and motion to strike.

            On August 28, 2026, the Court related this matter to Los Angeles Superior Court case, California Roofline Inc., a California corporation vs. Leron Inc., a California corporation, et al., case no. 26NNCV01511 (the “First Action”), within the meaning of California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a) and deemed the First Action to be the lead case.

            On September 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

            On September 8, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

            Defendants request that the Court take judicial notice of the following:

1.      The Complaint filed by Defendant California Roofline Inc. in the First Action

2.      The Answer filed by Plaintiff in the First Action on July 14, 2026.

3.      The Cross-Complaint filed by Plaintiff in the First Action on July 14, 2026.

The Court grants Defendants’ requests for judicial notice pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d).

LEGAL STANDARD

            A party may object to a complaint or cross-complaint; the party may file a demurrer, an answer, or both. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 430.30.) The demurrer may be filed within 30 days after service of the complaint or cross-complaint unless extended by stipulation or court order. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 430.40, subd. (a).) 

As a general matter, in a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Id.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) 

Before filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to for purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41.)

DISCUSSION

A.    Meet and Confer

Defendants’ counsel sent Plaintiff’s counsel a meet and confer letter on June 10, 2026. (Shem-Tov Decl., ¶ 3.) However, the parties were unable to meet and confer before the deadline to file a response to the complaint because Plaintiff’s counsel was traveling. (Shem-Tov Decl., ¶ 4.) As of the filing of this demurrer, the parties have not been able to reach an agreement regarding the demurrer and motion to strike. (Shem-Tov Decl., ¶ 6.)

The Court finds that Defendants made a good-faith effort to meet and confer before filing the demurrer. Accordingly, the Court will address the merits of the demurrer. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(4).)

B.     Demurrer to the Entire Complaint as Uncertain

Defendants demur to Plaintiff’s complaint in its entirety as uncertain.

A party may demur to a complaint on the grounds that it is uncertain, ambiguous, or unintelligible. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (f).) A complaint must contain "[a] statement of the facts constituting the cause of action, in ordinary and concise language." (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.10, subd. (a)(1).) 

Demurrers for uncertainty are disfavored and will be sustained only where the pleading is so incomprehensible that the defendant cannot reasonably respond. (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695, as modified Aug. 13, 2019.) As courts have recognized, a demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed because ambiguities and lack of specificity can generally be clarified through discovery. (Khoury v. Maly's of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.) 

Defendants argue the complaint is uncertain because it does not distinguish between the two defendants. Defendants argue that the complaint attempts to overcome this failure through alter ego, agency, and aiding and abetting theories, but the underlying allegations do not support those claims.

Plaintiff argues that the complaint sufficiently apprises Defendants of Plaintiff’s claims against them and properly pleads the allegations. (Opp., 7:25-28; 8:1-2.) Plaintiff contends that Defendants are attempting to hold Plaintiff to the standard of proving its allegations, not whether the complaint is properly pled. (Opp., 8:3-4.)

The Court that the complaint adequately apprises Defendants of the nature of the claims asserted against them and the factual basis for those claims. The allegations are sufficiently clear to permit Defendants to understand the claims and formulate a responsive pleading. To the extent Defendants seek additional details pertaining to the alter ego, agency, and aiding and abetting theories, such matters concern the specificity of the allegations rather than the intelligibility of the pleading. A demurrer for uncertainty is not the proper vehicle to obtain evidentiary detail, particularly where the complaint is otherwise understandable and discovery is available to clarify any ambiguities.

Accordingly, the Court OVERRULES Defendants’ demurrer to the complaint on the ground of uncertainty.

C.     Demurrer to the Entire Complaint as Improperly Splitting the Dispute Which Belongs in the First Action

Defendants demur to Plaintiff’s complaint in its entirety on the grounds that it seeks separate prosecution of a dispute that belongs in the First Action and is therefore barred.

“Except as otherwise provided by statute, if a party against whom a complaint has been filed and served fails to allege in a cross-complaint any related cause of action which (at the time of serving his answer to the complaint) he has against the plaintiff, such party may not thereafter in any other action assert against the plaintiff the related cause of action not pleaded.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.30, subd. (a).) A related cause of action is “a cause of action which arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause of action which the plaintiff alleges in his complaint.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.10, subd. (c).)

“The compulsory cross-complaint statute is designed to prevent ‘piecemeal litigation.’” (Align Technology, Inc. v. Tran (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 949, 959.) “[T]he compulsory cross-complaint statute … must be liberally construed to effectuate its purpose.” (Ibid.)  “The related cause of action must be one that was in existence at the time of service of the answer; otherwise, the failure to assert it in the prior litigation is not a bar under the statute.” (Id. at p. 960 [internal citations omitted].) A plaintiff is generally prevented from enforcing a single cause of action in separate, concurrent lawsuits against the same party. (Colebrook v. CIT Bank, N.A. (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 259, 263.)

Defendants argue that the compulsory cross-complaint statute is implicated here because Plaintiff asserts the same claims in its cross-complaint in the First Action. Defendants argue that the First Action already covers the underlying dispute and asserting identical claims in a separate, later-filed action is forbidden by Code of Civil Procedure section 426.30.

The Court agrees. The complaint in this action and the cross-complaint in the First Action assert the same causes of action. The allegations in Plaintiff’s cross-complaint in the First Action are the same paragraph-by-paragraph allegations that Plaintiff pleaded in the complaint in this action. The First Action is ongoing and has not reached a final determination on the merits. Therefore, the Court finds that the instant action arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences and Plaintiff may not assert the same causes of action against Defendants in a separate action.

Thus, the Court SUSTAINS Defendants’ demurrer to the entirety of Plaintiff’s complaint. Accordingly, the Court finds that Defendants’ motion to strike is moot.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court SUSTAINS Defendants California Roofline, Inc. and Yovel Tubul’s demurrer to Plaintiff Leron Inc.’s complaint in its entirety.

Considering the Court’s sustaining of Defendants’ demurrer, the Court finds that Defendants’ motion to strike is moot.

Moving party is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
