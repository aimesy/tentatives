1.       Madrid’s Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions Admitted as to Defendant Dan Reichman

Legal Standard

“Any party may obtain discovery . . . by a written request that any other party to the action admit the genuineness of specified documents, or the truth of specified matters of fact, opinion relating to fact, or application of law to fact. A request for admission may relate to a matter that is in controversy between the parties.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.010.)¿ “Within 30 days after service of requests for admission, the party to whom the requests are directed shall serve the original of the response to them on the requesting party, and a copy of the response on all other parties who have appeared ....”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)

If a party to whom request for admissions are served fails to provide a timely response, the party to whom the request was directed waives any objections, including based on privilege or the work product doctrine. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (a).) The requesting party can move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the request be deemed admitted, as well as for monetary sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) The court shall issue this order unless the party to whom the request was made serves a response in substantial compliance prior to the hearing on the motion. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) Unlike a motion to compel further discovery responses, a motion to deem RFA admitted does not have any meet and confer requirements.

It is mandatory that the court “impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) on the party or attorney, or both, whose failure to serve a timely response to requests for admission necessitated this motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)

Discussion

Madrid moves for an order deeming his requested matters admitted. Madrid also requests sanctions in the amount of $510.00 against Reichman and his counsel of record.

Madrid’s counsel represents as follows:

Madrid propounded the Requests for Admissions, Set One (“RFAs”) on Reichman on February 23, 2026. (Nunez Decl., ¶ 4, Ex. A.) Madrid did not receive either a response or a request for an extension by March 27, 2026. (Id., ¶ 5.) On April 9, 2026, after Reichman’s counsel requested a 30-day extension three days prior, Madrid’s counsel agreed to a brief extension. (Id., ¶¶ 5-6, Exs. B-C.) Reichman has failed to respond to the RFAs as of July 8, 2026. (Id., ¶ 9.)

Reichman’s counsel states that the motion is moot because Reichman has served verified, Code-compliant responses to the RFAs. (Kandarian-Stein Decl., ¶ 10, Exs. D-E.)

In reply, Madrid argues that the responses are not Code-compliant because Reichman waived every objection by failing to timely respond and yet Reichman provided objection-only responses to certain discovery requests. (Reply at pp. 3-4.) Madrid’s argument as to the sufficiency of the responses, however, is more appropriate for a motion to compel further rather than a motion to compel or deem admitted.

The Court also notes that Reichman’s service of the verifications for the discovery responses with his opposition to this motion is sufficient because they were served prior to the hearing.

Accordingly, the motion to deem the content of Madrid’s Request for Admission, Set One, admitted is DENIED as moot, except as to sanctions.

Sanctions

Madrid requests sanctions in the amount of $510.00 (i.e. 0.5 hours for this Motion and 1 hour for Reply) at a rate of $300/hour. (Nunez Decl., ¶ 10.) Madrid also requests $60.00 in costs. (Id.)

In opposition, Reichman’s counsel states that Reichman’s failure to respond timely was not intentional: the associate attorney who was responsible for handling this matter left the firm during the relevant time period and failed to bring the outstanding discovery to counsel’s attention. (Kandarian-Stein Decl., ¶ 4.) In addition, Madrid’s electronic service did not include the correct address designated by opposing counsel for receipt of service and litigation-related communications. (Id., ¶¶ 5-8.) Once the outstanding discovery was brought to counsel’s attention, Reichman promptly responded. (Id., ¶ 9.)

The Court finds Reichman’s argument unpersuasive. The Proof of Service for both the RFAs and this motion were served to Eservice-GKS@g3pmlaw.com, which is listed in the Court’s records as the relevant email address for Reichman. (Nunez Decl., Ex. A.) Counsel was also notified about the late responses back in April, and asked for an extension, indicating that the firm was having trouble getting a hold of Reichman and noting the associate’s departure; the email exchange omits the purported e-service issue. (Nunez Decl., Exs. B-E.) Counsel offers no explanation for the delay in response between April and August.

As to Madrid’s request, the Court finds both the rate and requested hours to be reasonable. Thus, the Court grants Madrid’s request for sanctions against Reichman and his counsel of record in the sum of $510.00, including costs. Sanctions are payable within 30 days from the date of the notice of ruling.

 

2.       Madrid’s Motion to Compel Dan Reichman’s Responses to Form & Special Interrogatories [Set One]

Legal Standard

If a party to whom interrogatories were directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order to compel responses without objections.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.) Failure to timely serve responses waives objections to the requests. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).)¿A party moving to compel discovery responses under this statutory provision is not required to meet and confer prior to filing the motion.¿(See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b); see also Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411 [citing Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 906 for the proposition that “meet and confer” requirement “did not apply when propounding party sought order compelling responses to interrogatories and sanctions for responding party's failure to respond ‘within the statutorily permitted time’”].)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

If the court finds that a party has unsuccessfully made or opposed a motion to compel responses, the court “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)

Discussion

Madrid moves for an order compelling Reichman to provide verified responses to its First Set of Form & Special Interrogatories. Madrid also requests sanctions in the amount of $510.00 against Reichman and his counsel of record.

Madrid’s counsel represents as follows:

Madrid propounded the first set of Form and Special Interrogatories (“FROGs” & “SROGs”) on Reichman on February 23, 2026. (Nunez Decl., ¶ 4, Exs. A-B.) Madrid did not receive either a response or a request for an extension by March 27, 2026. (Id., ¶ 5.) On April 9, 2026, after Reichman’s counsel requested a 30-day extension three days prior, Madrid’s counsel agreed to a brief extension. (Id., ¶¶ 5-6, Exs. C-F.) Reichman has failed to respond to the FROGs and SROGs as of July 8, 2026. (Id., ¶ 9.)

Reichman’s counsel states that the motion is moot because Reichman has served verified, Code-compliant responses to the FROGs and SROGs. (Kandarian-Stein Decl., ¶ 10, Exs. A-B, E.)

In Reply, Madrid argues that the responses are not Code-compliant because Reichman waived every objection by failing to timely respond and yet Reichman provided objection-only responses to certain discovery requests. (Reply at pp. 3-4.) Madrid’s argument as to the sufficiency of the responses, however, is more appropriate for a motion to compel further rather than a motion to compel.

The Court also notes that Reichman’s service of the verifications for the discovery responses with his opposition to this motion is sufficient because they were served prior to the hearing.

Accordingly, the motion to compel Reichman’s verified responses to Madrid’s Form and Special Interrogatories, Set One, is DENIED as moot, except as to sanctions.

Sanctions

Madrid requests sanctions in the amount of $510.00 (i.e. 0.5 hours for this Motion and 1 hour for Reply) at a rate of $300/hour. (Nunez Decl., ¶ 10.) Madrid also requests $60.00 in costs. (Id.)

For substantially the same reasons as set forth in connection with Motion #1, the Court grants Madrid’s request for sanctions against Reichman and his counsel of record in the sum of $510.00, including costs. Sanctions are payable within 30 days from the date of the notice of ruling.

Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) Failure to timely serve responses waives objections to the requests. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).)¿A party moving to compel discovery responses under this statutory provision is not required to meet and confer prior to filing the motion.¿(See Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b); see also Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411 [citing Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 906 for the proposition that “meet and confer” requirement “did not apply when propounding party sought order compelling responses to interrogatories and sanctions for responding party's failure to respond ‘within the statutorily permitted time’”].)

If the court finds that a party has unsuccessfully made or opposed a motion to compel responses, the court “shall impose a monetary sanction . . . unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)

In Reply, Madrid argues that the responses are not Code-compliant because MII waived every objection by failing to timely respond and yet MII provided objection-only responses to certain discovery requests.
