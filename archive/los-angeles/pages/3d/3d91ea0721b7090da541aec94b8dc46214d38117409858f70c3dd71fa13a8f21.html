LEAVE TO AMEND

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant, City of Los Angeles acting by and through the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power

RESPONDING PARTY: Unopposed/Plaintiffs, William Stan Spry and Heidi Spry

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion for Leave to Amend Answer

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiffs William Stan Spry and Heidi Spry sued Defendant Los Angeles Department of Water and Power after improvements to the road and drainage leading to the Hollywood Reservoir allegedly caused rainwater to be directed, redirected, and to flood toward and into Plaintiffs’ property. Plaintiffs assert claims for inverse condemnation, private nuisance, and negligence.

 

RULING: Granted.

Defendant moves for leave to amend its answer to add an affirmative defense for failure to join necessary parties. The basis for the amendment is Defendant’s discovery that the new owner of Plaintiffs’ property posted a permit on the front door about mold remediation. Defendant believes the mold may be related to the events involved in the instant action. Plaintiffs filed a “conditional” non-opposition to the motion, referencing their opposition to the withdrawn motion to join necessary party and their pending sanctions motion. Defendant’s motion is granted.

 

A motion for leave to amend must comply with the requirements set forth in California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, which states as follows:

 

(a)        Contents of motion

A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:

(1)               Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;

(2)               State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and

(3)               State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.

 

(b)        Supporting declaration

A separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify:

(1)        The effect of the amendment;

(2)        Why the amendment is necessary and proper;

(3)        When the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and

(4)               The reasons why the request for amendment was not made

earlier.

 

Dilatory delays and prejudice to the opposing parties is a valid ground for denial. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.) Prejudice exists where the amendment would require delaying the trial, resulting in loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation such as an increased burden of discovery. (Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, 486-88.)

 

Leave to amend is generally liberally granted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a); Mesler v. Bragg Management Co. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 290, 296.) The Court will not generally consider the validity of the proposed amended pleading in ruling on a motion for leave, instead deferring such determinations for a demurrer or motion to strike, unless the proposed amendment fails to state a valid claim as a matter of law. (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal. App.3d 1045, 1048; California Casualty Gen. Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1985) 173 Cal.App.3d 274, 280-81 disapproved of on other grounds by Kransco v. American Empire Surplus Lines Ins. Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 390.)

 

Defendant is granted leave to amend. Newly associated counsel discovered the basis for amendment on March 10, 2026 when it conducted a site inspection at the home and saw the new owner had posted a permit about mold remediation. [Abbott Decl. ¶¶ 10, 15.] Trial is more than 5 months away and there is no indication that amendment prejudices Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs’ prior opposition to the withdrawn motion and their sanctions motion are immaterial to disposition of the instant motion. Defendant’s motion is therefore granted.

 

Defendant to file a separate copy of the amended answer within 10 days of this order.

 

Defendant to give notice.

Dept. 617

DEMURRER

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants, Danielle Davis, Ronald Hodge, Sr., and Ronald Hodge, Jr.

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Kathy Collins, as the administrator of the Estate of Everett Bell

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

On July 1, 2025, Plaintiff Kathy Collins filed the instant action as the administrator of her father, Everett Bell’s, estate. She alleges that Defendant Ronald Hodge, Sr. unduly influenced her elderly father to sign a power of attorney and Hodge Sr., with the assistance of Defendants Danielle Davis, Ronald Hodge, Jr., Janie Hodge, and Christina Lara, appropriated Bell’s assets for their and their companies’ benefit. This includes transfers to Defendant Paving The Way Foundation (PTW). Plaintiff further alleges concealment of Bell’s assets through Defendant Sandy Paradise Recovery LLC (Paradise). On September 12, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint for: (1) Breach of Fiduciary Duty; (2) Breach of Loyalty; (3) Conversion; (4) Violation of Penal Code Section 496; (5) Elder Abuse; (6) Fraud; and (7) Accounting.

 

RULING: Sustained in part.

Defendants Davis, Hodge Sr., and Hodge Jr. demur to each cause of action in Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint, arguing that Plaintiff fails to sufficiently state her claims.[1] Plaintiff opposes the demurrer, maintaining the sufficiency of her allegations. Defendants’ demurrer is sustained with leave to amend as to the breach of fiduciary duty, breach of loyalty, elder abuse, and accounting claims only as to Davis and Hodge Jr. The demurrer to the conversion claim is sustained with leave to amend as to all demurring Defendants. The demurrer to the Penal Code section 496 claim is overruled.

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading “by raising questions of law.” (Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.) “In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.) The court “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .” (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.) In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated. (Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) “If the complaint states a cause of action under any theory, regardless of the title under which the factual basis for relief is stated, that aspect of the complaint is good against a demurrer.” (Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title Guaranty Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26, 38.)

 

First Cause of Action: Breach of Fiduciary Duty

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s breach of fiduciary duty claim fails because she does not allege breach of a fiduciary duty owed by Davis and Hodge Jr. Plaintiff responds that Davis and Hodge Jr.’s liability is premised on their aiding and abetting Hodge Sr.’s alleged breach of fiduciary duty.

 

“Liability may ... be imposed on one who aids and abets the commission of an intentional tort if the person (a) knows the other’s conduct constitutes a breach of duty and gives substantial assistance or encouragement to the other to so act or (b) gives substantial assistance to the other in accomplishing a tortious result and the person’s own conduct, separately considered, constitutes a breach of duty to the third person.” (Casey v. U.S. Bank Nat. Assn. (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 1138, 1144.)

 

The demurrer is sustained as to Davis and Hodge Jr. The precise breach of fiduciary duty alleged against Hodge Sr. is his wrongful “taking, concealing and/or disposing of hundreds of thousands of dollars of assets held by Mr. Bell.” [FAC ¶ 50.] (Casey, supra, 127 Cal.App.4th at p. 1149 [court must first identify the breach of fiduciary duty for which plaintiff seeks to hold defendant liable].) Most of Plaintiff’s allegations regarding Davis and Hodge Jr.’s assistance are conclusory as they do not explain how such assistance occurred. [See, e.g., id.  ¶¶ 24, 27-29, 31-32.] The only specific allegations of their aiding and abetting are Hodge Jr.’s appointment with an estate planning attorney to transfer certain properties Bell was allegedly pressured to invest in to LLCs and the transfer of assets from Defendant Hodge Enterprise LLC, which Davis co-owned, to Paradise. [Id. ¶¶ 36-37, 44.] But there are no allegations that any of the properties were transferred or that any of Bell’s assets were ever transferred to Enterprise. There are thus no allegations of Davis and Hodge Jr.’s substantial assistance of Hodge Sr.’s breach of fiduciary duty. Because there is a reasonable possibility that Plaintiff can amend her complaint to sufficiently allege Davis and Hodge Jr.’s substantial assistance, she is granted leave to amend.

 

Second Cause of Action: Breach of Loyalty

Plaintiff’s breach of loyalty claim is duplicative of her breach of fiduciary duty cause of action and the demurrer to this claim is sustained for the same reasons. Plaintiff is similarly granted leave to amend.   

 

Third Cause of Action: Conversion

The demurrer to Plaintiff’s conversion claim is sustained because the claim is barred by the statute of limitations. The statute of limitations for conversion is 3 years. (Code Civ. Proc., § 338, subd. (c)(1).) It is “triggered by the act of wrongfully taking property.” (Bono v. Clark (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 1409, 1433.) The complaint indicates alleged conversion of funds in 2016 and possibly in 2018. [See FAC ¶¶ 20-23, 25-28, 34.] Plaintiff’s filing of her conversion claim in 2025 is therefore untimely. The 2024 sale of the Comstock Property and concealment of assets do not bring Plaintiff’s claim within the limitations period because conversion only applies to personal property (Munger v. Moore (1970) 11 Cal.App.3d 1, 7) and the concealment is distinct from and occurred after the wrongful taking of funds. And while an exception exists where “a fiduciary has concealed the material facts giving rise to the cause of action,” Plaintiff’s complaint does not state when she discovered the misappropriation of Bell’s funds. (Bono, supra, 103 Cal.App.4th at p. 1433.) [FAC ¶ 42.] Because there is a reasonable possibility that Plaintiff can allege acts of conversion within the statutory period and/or plead facts invoking application of the discovery rule, she is granted leave to amend.  

 

Fourth Cause of Action: Violation of Penal Code Section 496

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s cause of action for violation of Penal Code section 496 fails because her allegations of Defendants’ acts are conclusory and fail to state a civil cause of action.

 

Penal Code section 496, subdivision (a), makes it a crime for “[e]very person who buys or receives any property that has been stolen or that has been obtained in any manner constituting theft or extortion, knowing the property to be so stolen or obtained, or who conceals, sells, withholds, or aids in concealing, selling, or withholding any property from the owner, knowing the property to be so stolen or obtained . . . .” Any person injured by a violation of subdivision (a) “may bring an action for three times the amount of actual damages, if any, sustained by the plaintiff, costs of suit, and reasonable attorney’s fees.” (Pen. Code, § 496, subd. (c).) Theft includes fraudulently appropriating property entrusted to a person. (Id. § 484, subd. (a).)

 

The demurrer to the Penal Code claim is overruled. Plaintiff sufficiently alleges that Hodge Sr. appropriated property entrusted to him via the power of attorney. [See, e.g., FAC ¶ 23.] Plaintiff further alleges that Davis and Hodge Jr. received this property, knowing that Hodge Sr. was the power of attorney and aware of his appropriation scheme. [Id. ¶¶ 24, 51.] While these facts may be insufficient to plead Davis and Hodge Jr.’s assistance in the appropriation, they still show that they received the funds knowing they were misappropriated. This is sufficient to state a Penal Code section 496 claim against them. The demurrer to this claim is therefore overruled.   

 

Fifth Cause of Action: Elder Abuse

The demurrer to the elder abuse claim is overruled as to Hodge Sr. but sustained as to Davis and Hodge Jr. Defendants contend that Plaintiff’s elder abuse claim is conclusory. Plaintiff’s cause of action is predicated on the alleged financial abuse of Bell. Financial abuse occurs when a person “[t]akes, secretes, appropriates, obtains, or retains real or personal property of an elder or dependent adult for a wrongful use or with intent to defraud, or both,” or assists in doing so. (Welf. & Inst. Code, § 15610.30, subd. (a)(1)-(2).) Here, Plaintiff alleges that Hodge Sr. appropriated Bell’s funds “to pay for jewelry, trips to casinos, real property and ‘donations’ to PTW” (i.e., for a wrongful purpose). [FAC ¶ 23.] But, as described before, the allegations regarding Davis and Hodge Jr.’s assistance in the appropriation are conclusory. Even if liability is sought to be imposed on them based on their alleged receipt of the appropriated funds, the complaint is devoid of specific allegations of their wrongful or intended use of the funds. Because there is a reasonable possibility that Plaintiff can cure this defect, she is granted leave to amend.

 

Sixth Cause of Action: Fraud

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s fraud cause of action fails because she fails to allege her claim with requisite particularity. Because this claim is only asserted against Hodge Sr., Davis and Hodge Jr. do not have standing to challenge it. (See Carter v. Prime Healthcare Paradise Valley LLC (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 396, 410 [“Allegations of misconduct directed against one defendant, however, do not state a cause of action against another defendant against whom the allegations of misconduct are not directed”].) The issue is thus whether Plaintiff sufficiently alleges Hodge Sr.’s fraudulent conduct.

 

“The elements of fraud, which give rise to the tort action for deceit, are (a) misrepresentation (false representation, concealment, or nondisclosure); (b) knowledge of falsity (or ‘scienter’); (c) intent to defraud, i.e., to induce reliance; (d) justifiable reliance; and (e) resulting damage.” (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 638.) Fraud must be pled with specificity; conclusory allegations are insufficient. (Id. at p. 645.) This requires “pleading facts which show how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered.” (Ibid.)

 

The demurrer to the fraud claim is sustained. The basis for Plaintiff’s fraud claim is Hodge Sr.’s alleged misrepresentation to her that Bell had no assets at the time of his death in 2021. [FAC ¶ 87.] But other than the alleged misrepresentation, Plaintiff’s remaining allegations in support of her claim merely recite the elements and fail to present ultimate facts. [Id. ¶¶ 88-93.] Because there is a reasonable possibility that Plaintiff can cure this pleading defect, she is granted leave to amend.

 

Seventh Cause of Action: Accounting

Defendants contend that Plaintiff’s accounting claim fails because of the lack of fiduciary duty owed by Davis and Hodge Jr. and Plaintiff’s failure to allege a need for an accounting.

 

“A cause of action for an accounting requires a showing that a relationship exists between the plaintiff and defendant that requires an accounting, and that some balance is due the plaintiff that can only be ascertained by an accounting.” (Teselle v. McLoughlin (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 156, 179.) A fiduciary relationship is not necessary; “[t]he right to an accounting can arise from the possession by the defendant of money or property which, because of the defendant’s relationship with the plaintiff, the defendant is obliged to surrender.” (Id. at pp. 179-80.) But “[s]ome underlying misconduct on the part of the defendant must be shown to invoke the right to this equitable remedy.” (Prakashpalan v. Engstrom, Lipscomb & Lack (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 1105, 1137.) “An action for accounting is not available where the plaintiff alleges the right to recover a sum certain or a sum that can be made certain by calculation.” (Teselle, supra, 173 Cal.App.4th at p. 179.)

 

The demurrer to the accounting claim is sustained only as to Davis and Hodge Jr. Plaintiff alleges a fiduciary relationship between Bell and Hodge Sr. via the power of attorney as well as a series of transactions by Hodge Sr. involving Bell’s property such that the amount due “cannot be ascertained without an accounting of the receipts and disbursements of the transactions . . . .” [FAC ¶ 98.] However, no relationship is alleged between Hodge Jr. and Bell or between Davis and Bell sufficient to give rise to an accounting claim against them. Because there is a reasonable possibility that Plaintiff can amend her complaint to cure these defects, she is granted leave to amend.  

 

Plaintiff is granted 30 days’ leave to amend. Plaintiff may only add facts in support of the successfully challenged claims. (Harris v. Wachovia Mortgage, FSB (2010) 185 Cal.App.4th 1018, 1023.) If Plaintiff declines or fails to file an amended complaint, Defendants shall answer the operative complaint within 10 days of the lapsed deadline. Any new causes of action outside the scope of the order may be addressed via a motion to strike.

 

Defendants to give notice.

 


[1] Defendants also demur on grounds of uncertainty. Because the First Amended Complaint sufficiently apprises Defendants of the issues they are being asked to meet, the demurrer on uncertainty grounds is overruled. (Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, fn. 2.)

DEMURRER

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants, Janie Hodge, Christina Lara, and Paving the Way Foundation

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Kathy Collins, as the administrator of the Estate of Everett Bell

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

On July 1, 2025, Plaintiff Kathy Collins filed the instant action as the administrator of her father, Everett Bell’s, estate. She alleges that Defendant Ronald Hodge, Sr. unduly influenced her elderly father to sign a power of attorney and Hodge Sr., with the assistance of Defendants Danielle Davis, Ronald Hodge, Jr., Janie Hodge, and Christina Lara, appropriated Bell’s assets for their and their companies’ benefit. This includes transfers to Defendant Paving The Way Foundation (PTW). Plaintiff further alleges concealment of Bell’s assets through Defendant Sandy Paradise Recovery LLC (Paradise). On September 12, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint for: (1) Breach of Fiduciary Duty; (2) Breach of Loyalty; (3) Conversion; (4) Violation of Penal Code Section 496; (5) Elder Abuse; (6) Fraud; and (7) Accounting.

 

RULING: Sustained.

Defendants Janie Hodge, Christina Lara, and PTW demur to the first through fifth and seventh causes of action in Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint. They argue that the claims are time-barred and insufficiently pled. Plaintiff opposes the demurrer, maintaining her claims are timely and sufficiently pled. Defendants’ demurrer is sustained with leave to amend.

 

Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s claims is sustained because they are barred by the statute of limitations. The statute of limitations for breach of fiduciary duty is 4 years (3 years if the breach is fraudulent). (American Master Lease LLC v. Idanta Partners, Ltd. (2014) 225 Cal.App.4th 1451, 1479.) The limitations period is 1 year for the Penal Code section 496 claim and 3 years for the conversion claim. (Prudential Home Mortgage Co. v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1236, 1242 [“[T]he settled rule in California is that statutes which provide for recovery of damages additional to actual losses incurred, such as double or treble damages, are considered penal in nature [citations], and thus governed by the one-year period of limitations stated in section 340, subdivision (1)”]; Code Civ. Proc., § 338, subd. (c)(1).) And the statute of limitations for the financial elder abuse and accounting claims are 4 years. (Welf. & Inst. Code, § 15657.7; Code Civ. Proc., § 343.) The complaint identifies the appropriation, conversion of funds, and obtainment of converted funds occurred in 2016 and 2018. [FAC ¶¶ 20-28, 34.] Plaintiff did not file these claims until 2025 and they are thus untimely.

 

The sale of the Comstock Property in 2024 and Plaintiff’s allegations concerning her eventual discovery of Hodge Sr. and Defendant’s alleged scheme do not make the claims timely. The complaint indicates that the appropriation, conversion, and obtainment of funds occurred well before sale of the Comstock Property and Plaintiff does not explain how its sale, at a time when Hodge Sr. held title, makes her claims timely. (Badie v. Bank of America (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 779, 784-85 [“When [a party] fails to raise a point, or asserts it but fails to support it with reasoned argument and citations to authority, we treat the point as waived”].) Plaintiff also cannot take advantage of the discovery rule because she does not state when she discovered the misappropriation of Bell’s funds. [FAC ¶ 42.] (Czajkowski v. Haskell & White, LLP (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 166, 174-75.) Because there is a reasonable possibility that Plaintiff can amend her complaint to allege actionable acts within the limitations period and/or facts invoking application of the discovery rule, she is granted leave to amend.

 

Plaintiff is granted 30 days’ leave to amend. Plaintiff may only add facts in support of the successfully challenged claims. (Harris v. Wachovia Mortgage, FSB (2010) 185 Cal.App.4th 1018, 1023.) If Plaintiff declines or fails to file an amended complaint, Defendants shall answer the operative complaint within 10 days of the lapsed deadline. Any new causes of action outside the scope of the order may be addressed via a motion to strike.

 

Defendants to give notice.
