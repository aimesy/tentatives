Case Number: 20STCV22944    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 529

CULOTTI v. THE CITY OF LOS ANGELES, ET AL.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL INSPECTION

 

Date of Hearing:          7/22/2026                               Trial Date:       10/12/2026

Department:               45                                            Case No.:         20STCV22944

 

Moving Party:             Defendant City of Los Angeles

Responding Party:       Plaintiff Elaine Culotti

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is an eminent domain action arising out of a public construction project causing water intrusion, inundation, and land slippage on the real property located at 17520 Revello Drive, Los Angeles, CA 90272 (the “Culotti Property”) owned by Plaintiff Elaine Culotti (“Plaintiff”). On June 17, 2020, Plaintiff filed the operative Complaint against Defendants the City of Los Angeles (the “City”); the People of the State of California acting by and through the Department of Transportation, erroneously sued as State of California Department of Transportation (“CDT”); and DOES 1 through 40 (collectively, “Defendants”), asserting causes of action for: (1) Inverse Condemnation; (2) Dangerous Condition of Public Property; (3) Nuisance; and (4) Trespass.

 

On August 7, 2020, the City and CDT filed their separate Answers to the Complaint.

 

On June 5, 2026, the City filed the instant Motion to Compel Inspection. On July 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition. On July 15, 2026, the City filed a Reply. On July 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Sur-Reply.

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Defendant City of Los Angeles’ Motion to Compel Inspection is DENIED.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A party may demand that any other party produce and permit the party making the demand, or someone acting on the demanding party’s behalf, to inspect and to photograph, test, or sample “any tangible things that are in the possession, custody, or control of the party on whom the demand is made” and “electronically stored information in the possession, custody, or control of the party on whom demand is made.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.010, subds. (c), (e).)

 

A party who receives a demand for production, i.e., a demand for inspection, copying, testing, etc. must respond separately to each individual item requested with either a statement indicating compliance, a statement that the party lacks the ability to comply, or an objection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).)

 

A party may then move for a further response to requests for production of documents where an answer to the requests are evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

 

Notice of the motion must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).)

 

Finally, California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1345 requires that all motions or responses involving further discovery contain a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses. (Cal. Rules Ct., Rule 3.1345, subd. (a)(3).)¿¿

 

PRELIMINARY MATTERS

 

As a preliminary matter, the Court notes that Plaintiff filed a sur-reply in response to the City’s reply brief on the grounds that it contains new evidence. (Plf. Sur-Reply at 2:1-10.) “It is true, of course, that ‘[t]he general rule of motion practice…is that new evidence is not permitted with reply papers.’” (Golden Door Properties, LLC v. Superior Court (2020) 53 Cal.App.5th 733, 774.) “However, a recognized exception is for points ‘strictly responsive’ to arguments made for the first time in the opposition.” (Id.)The moving papers and opposition both raise arguments regarding whether there is good cause to have Plaintiff’s devices forensically searched. (Mot. at 2:9-14; Opp. at pp. 6-7.) The declaration filed in support of the reply brief cites to Plaintiff’s production of the Assignment Agreement and is offered to support the City’s assertion that forensic examination is warranted, thus it is responsive to the arguments made in the opposition. Nevertheless, Plaintiff does not object to  Exhibits F and G, which were submitted on reply.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Here, the City moves for an order compelling Plaintiff to permit the inspection of her cloud-based email servers and technological devices including desktop computers, laptops, email accounts, mobile telephones, and associated storage media  on the grounds that the native electronic version of the Assignment Agreement and all related communications are relevant to the parties’ claims and defenses. Specifically, the City contends that Plaintiff’s standing to sue is at issue in this case, thus the Assignment Agreement is the cornerstone of determining whether Plaintiff has any cognizable interest in the claims raised in the Complaint. Furthermore, the City asserts that based on Plaintiff’s deposition testimony the authenticity and provenance of the Assignment Agreement is also at issue.

 

As an initial matter, the Court notes that Plaintiff does not dispute the relevance of the Assignment Agreement. However, Plaintiff contends that the motion should be denied because she has already agreed to produce the Assignment Agreement in its native electronic format with all associated metadata in response to the City’s concurrently served Requests for Production of Documents (Set Six). (Ho Decl., ¶4.) On the other hand, the City argues that Plaintiff’s offer to produce the native file of the Assignment Agreement does not moot the present motion because only an independent forensic examination can verify the authenticity of the Assignment Agreement. (Reply at 5:6-12.)

 

In support of its contention, the City cites to Ellis v. Toshiba America Information Systems, Inc. (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 853, which held that a court’s inspection orders of electronically stored information were reasonable and necessary. (Ellis v. Toshiba America Information Systems, Inc. (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 853, 881.) This instant case is distinguishable from Ellis, wherein the attorney was seeking a large fee request and had deleted all original electronic billing records, which called into question the authenticity and/or accuracy of her billing records. (Id.) Similarly, in Electronic Funds Solutions, LLC v. Murphy (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 1161, the defendants had violated an order of inspection and stipulated order by producing electronic hard drives, which had been “wiped” by the “Data Eraser” software. (Electronic Funds Solutions, LLC v. Murphy (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1170.) There is no indication that Plaintiff in this instant case has deleted the original electronic version of the Assignment Agreement and/or its associated metadata. In fact, Plaintiff’s counsel avers that none of the metadata of the native electronic file of the Assignment Agreement produced on July 14, 2026 was stripped, removed, modified or altered in any way before it was produced. (Ho. Suppl. Decl., ¶4, Ex. F.) The City has also stated that it was willing to avoid motion practice upon stipulation that the document produced on July 14, 2026 is the earliest version of the Assignment Agreement at issue. (Id., ¶5, Ex. A; Sozio Reply Decl., ¶6.) Plaintiff, by and through her counsel of record has done so. (Ho. Suppl. Decl., ¶4.)

 

Accordingly, the City fails to demonstrate that good cause exists to compel inspection of the Assignment Agreement by a forensic examiner at this time.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant City of Los Angeles’ Motion to Compel Inspection is DENIED.

 

 


Case Number: 21STCV17214    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 529

TERPEN INC., ET AL. v. MEMORY BUSS, ET AL.

 

MOTION FOR ATTORNEY FEES, costs, and prejudgment interest

 

Date of Hearing:          7/22/2026                               Trial Date:       Held on 1/6/26

Department:               45                                            Case No.:         21STCV17214

 

Moving Party: Plaintiffs Terpen Inc., Jairo Arrieta, Joel Arrieta, and Juan Carlos Diaz

Responding Party:  Unopposed

 

BACKGROUND

 

On May 6, 2021, Plaintiffs Terpen Inc., Jairo Arrieta, Joel Arrieta, and Juan Carlos Diaz filed a complaint against Defendants Memory Buss, Arcturian Light, LLC and Pleiadian Light, LLC. Plaintiffs allege this case arises from Defendant Memory Buss fraudulently inducing Terpen, a non-Accredited Investor, to invest $1.5 million in exchange for promised ownership in Defendants. Furthermore, plaintiffs allege Buss and other Defendants owe the Plaintiffs over $600,000 in unpaid wages for bringing the premises occupied by Defendants into legal compliance and other work expressly requested by Buss.

 

On May 11, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion for Attorney Fees, Costs, and Prejudgment Interest. The matter came for hearing on June 22, 2026; the motion was granted in part, wherein Plaintiffs were awarded $398,486.03 in attorney fees and $7,750.86 in costs, and continued for Plaintiffs to provide the statutory interest calculation via supplemental declaration. On July 9, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their Supplemental Declaration re Interest Calculation.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Plaintiffs Terpen Inc., Jairo Arrieta, Joel Arrieta, and Juan Carlos Diaz’s Motion for Pre-Judgment Interest is GRANTED in the amount of $1,091,121.40.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Code of Civil Procedure Section 3287, subdivision (a) provides that "[a] person who is entitled to recover damages certain, or capable of being made certain by calculation, and the right to recover which is vested in the person upon a particular day, is entitled also to recover interest thereon from that day. . .”¿¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 3287, subd. (a).)

 

“ ‘Damages are deemed certain or capable of being made certain within the provisions of subdivision (a) of section 3287 where there is essentially no dispute between the parties concerning the basis of computation of damages if any are recoverable but where their dispute centers on the issue of liability giving rise to damage.’ [Citations].” (Duale v. Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 718, 729.)

 

ANALYSIS

 

Here, Plaintiff seeks prejudgment interest in the amount of $1,091,121.40 from May 6, 2021 to April 27, 2026 as follows:

·         $819,141.36 for Plaintiff Terpen, Inc., based on the $1,645,495.86 awarded at trial, calculated at a daily interest rate of $450.8208 [$1,645,495.86 x 0.10 (10%)/365] multiplied by 1,817 days;

·         $92,402.85 for Plaintiff Jairo Arrieta, based on the $185,619.38 awarded at trial, calculated at a daily interest rate of $50.8546 [$185,619.38 x 0.10/365] multiplied by 1,817 days;

·         $80,909.04 for Plaintiff Joel Arrieta, based on the $162,530.55 awarded at trial, calculated at a daily interest rate of $44.5289 [$162,530.55 x 0.10/365] multiplied by 1,817 days; and

·         $98,668.16 for Plaintiff Juan Carlos Diaz, based on the $198,205.17 awarded at trial, calculated at a daily interest rate of $54.3028 [$198,205.17 x 0.10/365] multiplied by 1,817 days.

 

Accordingly, the court grants the request for prejudgment interest in the amount of $1,091,121.40.

 

CONCLUSION

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs Terpen Inc., Jairo Arrieta, Joel Arrieta, and Juan Carlos Diaz’s Motion for Pre-Judgment Interest is GRANTED in the amount of $1,091,121.40, to be divided between the parties as set forth above.  The judgment should reflect the allocation between the plaintiffs.

 


Case Number: 25STCV30393    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 529

Fatima pacheco v. nashat n. metri et al.

 

Motion for issue Sanctions

 

Date of Hearing:            July 22, 2026                                    Trial Date:         April 12, 2027

Department:                    529                                                        Case No.:           25STCV30393

 

Moving Party:                 Plaintiff Fatima Pacheco

Responding Party:        Defendant Nashat N. Metri

 

BACKGROUND

 

This action arises out of plaintiff’s lease of an allegedly uninhabitable and unpermitted converted garage from defendants. On October 16, 2025, plaintiff Fatima Pacheco filed a complaint against defendants Nashat N. Metri, Ebitsam Metri, and Does 1-10, asserting eight causes of action:

 

1.      Negligence

2.      Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability

3.      Unlawful and Unfair Business Practices or Acts in Violation of Business and Professions Code § 17200 et seq.

4.      Intentional Misrepresentation

5.      Violation of California Civil Code § 1940.2

6.      Violation of California Civil Code § 789.3

7.      Violation of California Civil Code § 1942.4

8.      Violation of the Pasadena Fair and Equitable Housing Charter Amendment (PMC § 1801 et seq.)

 

Plaintiff alleges that she entered into a verbal rental agreement to lease a converted garage from defendants in August 2024 for $1,500 per month. (Compl., ¶¶ 11, 13.) Plaintiff alleges she relied on defendants’ assurances that they would make all necessary repairs and improvements to the dilapidated structure to make it suitable for habitation. (Compl., ¶ 12.) However, upon moving in, plaintiff alleges she discovered the property was still unsafe and uninhabitable. (Compl., ¶ 14.)

 

On June 17, 2026, plaintiff filed the instant motion for issue sanctions based on defendant’s alleged willful failure to comply with the court’s Order dated March 25, 2026. On July 10, 2026, defendant filed a declaration in response to plaintiff’s motion.

 

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

1.      Plaintiff Fatima Pacheco’s Motion for Issue Sanctions is GRANTED in part. Plaintiff’s request for issue sanctions is denied. Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,300.00 is granted.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

Plaintiff Fatima Pacheco moves the court for an order imposing sanctions against defendant Nashat N. Metri based on defendant’s willful failure to obey the court’s March 25, 2026 order compelling further verified responses, without objection, to Form Interrogatories – General, Set One, Nos. 15.1 and 17.1. Plaintiff seeks an order (1) prohibiting defendant from supporting his general denial and affirmative defenses and from introducing evidence to dispute the 24 Requests for Admission he denied; and (2) establishing the matters that were the subject of those denied Requests for Admission. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (a), plaintiff also seeks monetary sanctions against defendant in the amount of $2,300.00.

 

Procedural Violations

 

Though defendant’s declaration in response is not titled as an opposition to plaintiff’s motion for sanctions, the court will exercise its discretion to treat it as such, just as the court may properly treat a motion based upon its purpose rather than its title when the title is incorrect.

(Sole Energy Co. v. Petrominerals Corp. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 187, 193; see also Glasser v. Glasser (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 1004, 1008 n.3 [“Although not expressly labeled as filed in support of the opposition, the declaration is dated September 25, 1996, and hearing on the motion was set for October 9, 1996. From these facts, we deduce that it was filed in support of the opposition.”].) Substance over form is the vital inquiry, and the court will consider the declaration by defendant filed July 10, 2026 as an opposition.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Misuses of the discovery process include, but are not limited to . . . Disobeying a court order to provide discovery.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subd. (g).) If a party fails to comply with a court order compelling discovery responses or attendance at a deposition, the court may impose monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2025.450, subd. (h) [depositions]; 2030.290, subd. (c) [interrogatories]; 2031.300, subd. (c) [demands for production of documents]; 2033.290, subd. (e) [requests for admission]).

 

“To the extent authorized by the chapter governing any particular discovery method or any other provision of this title, the court, after notice to any affected party, person, or attorney, and after opportunity for hearing, may impose the following sanctions against anyone engaging in conduct that is a misuse of the discovery process:

 

(a) The court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct . . .

 

(b) The court may impose an issue sanction ordering that designated facts shall be taken as established in the action in accordance with the claim of the party adversely affected by the misuse of the discovery process. The court may also impose an issue sanction by an order prohibiting any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process from supporting or opposing designated claims or defenses.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030.)

 

An express finding of willfulness is not required. (See, e.g., Aghaian v. Minassian (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 603, 620 [“Here, the court issued the sanctions pursuant to section 2023.030, subdivisions (b) and (c). Neither provision requires willfulness, much less an express finding of such.”].) “In the context of sanctions against a party for willful refusal to furnish discovery, it has been held that the responding party has the burden of proving the lack of willfulness of his failure to perform.” (Corns v. Miller (1986) 181 Cal.App.3d 195, 201.)

 

Discussion

 

On March 25, 2026, the court granted plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories – General, Set One, Nos. 15.1 and 17.1. (Hermansen Decl., ¶¶ 3–4.) The Minute Order states: “The defendant has to provide discovery responses to plaintiffs' counsel within 30 days. Sanctions in the amount of $1,725 are payable to the plaintiff within 45 days.” (Minute Order (03/25/2026), at p. 1.)

 

Metri did not comply with the order, and on April 22, 2026, he filed a Motion for Reconsideration and to Vacate Sanctions. (Hermansen Declaration., ¶ 6.) On May 27, 2026, the court denied the motion for reconsideration and stated: “Defendant is ordered to comply with the March 25, 2026 Order.” (Minute Order (05/27/2026), at p. 2.)  “As of the date of this declaration, Metri has served no further responses to Form Interrogatory Nos. 15.1 or 17.1, has paid none of the $1,725 in court-ordered sanctions, and has not contacted my office. He has offered no explanation for his noncompliance.” (Hermansen Decl., ¶ 8.)

 

Thus, defendant has not complied with two separate court orders, which constitutes misuse of the discovery process.

 

“Appellant's disobedience of the court's discovery order was an abuse of discovery for which the court was authorized to impose sanctions delineated in 2023, subdivision (b). Section 2023, subdivision (b) permits (1) a monetary sanction, (2) an issue sanction, (3) an evidence sanction, (4) a terminating sanction or (5) a contempt sanction.

 

Here the trial court imposed an issue sanction under section 2023, subdivision (b)(2) which provides, ‘[t]he court may impose an issue sanction ordering that designated facts shall be taken as established in the action in accordance with the claim of the party adversely affected by the misuse of the discovery process. The court may also impose an issue sanction by an order prohibiting any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process from supporting or opposing designated claims or defenses.’

 

In choosing among its various options for imposing a discovery sanction, a trial court exercises discretion, subject to reversal only for manifest abuse exceeding the bounds of reason.”

 

(Kuhns v. State of California (1992) 8 Cal.App.4th 982, 988 [citations omitted].)

 

In his declaration filed in response/opposition to the motion for issue sanctions, defendant explains that he was diagnosed with a traumatic brain injury and is still under the care of his treating physician at this time. (Metri Decl., ¶¶ 1–2.) Metri declares that he cannot meaningfully participate in court proceedings due to this injury. (Metri Decl., ¶ 3.) A letter from defendant Metri’s doctor is attached to the declaration, corroborating his claims of injury.

 

Given defendant’s injuries, the court does not find that imposing issue sanctions at this time would be just. However, the court will issue monetary sanctions for defendant’s repeated violation of the court’s orders.

 

Plaintiff requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,300.00, which constitutes three hours of time spent preparing this motion and one hour to attend the hearing at an hourly rate of $575. (Hermansen Decl., ¶ 14.) The court finds this amount reasonable.

 

 

CONCLUSION

 

1.      Plaintiff Fatima Pacheco’s Motion for Issue Sanctions is GRANTED in part. Plaintiff’s request for issue sanctions is denied. Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,300.00 is granted.  Monetary sanctions are payable within 30 days.

 

 


Case Number: 26STCV11993    Hearing Date: July 22, 2026    Dept: 529

Shanna dunson v. sparks la sports, llc

 

Motion to disqualify

 

Date of Hearing:        July 22, 2026                                      Trial Date:       Not Set

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        26STCV11993

 

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Shanna Dunson

Responding Party:     Defendant Sparks LA Sports, LLC

 

BACKGROUND

 

On April 14, 2026, Plaintiff Shanna Dunson filed a complaint against Defendant Sparks LA Sports, LLC for Race Discrimination [FEHA], Family Responsibilities Discrimination (FEHA), Retaliation [FEHA] Retaliation (Labor Code §1102.5), Failure to Prevent Discrimination and/or Retaliation [FEHA], Violation of Labor Code § 970, and Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public Policy.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Plaintiff Dunson’s Motion to Disqualify Counsel is DENIED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Request for Judicial Notice: Granted.

 

The court takes judicial notice of the existence of the pleading and court order but cannot consider the content of any pleading or finding of fact for purposes of ruling on the subject motion.

 

Standard

 

The court may exercise control “in furtherance of justice, the conduct of its ministerial officers, and of all other persons in any manner connected with a judicial proceeding before it, in every matter pertaining thereto.”¿¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 128, subd. (a)(5).)¿ This authority necessarily includes disqualifying an attorney.¿(Metro-Goldwyn Mayer, Inc. v. Tracinda Corp. (1995) 36 Cal.App.4th 1832, 1837–1838)¿”The issue of disqualification¿’ultimately involves¿a conflict between the clients’ right to counsel of their choice and the need to¿maintain¿ethical standards of professional responsibility.’”¿(Id. at p. 1838.)

 

“Motions to disqualify counsel present competing policy considerations. On the one hand, a court must not hesitate to disqualify an attorney when it is satisfactorily established that he or she wrongfully acquired an unfair advantage that undermines the integrity of the judicial process and will have a continuing effect on the proceedings before the court. On the other hand, it must be kept in mind that disqualification usually imposes a substantial hardship on the disqualified attorney's innocent client, who must bear the monetary and other costs of finding a replacement…” (Gregori v. Bank of America (1989) 207 Cal.App.3d 291, 300; see Oaks Management Corporation v. Superior Court (2006) 145 Cal.App.4th 453, 467.)

 

“‘[D]isqualification is proper as a prophylactic measure to prevent future prejudice to the opposing party from information the attorney should not have possessed’; an affirmative showing of existing injury from the misuse of privileged information is not required. (Citation.) A trial court, however, may not order disqualification ‘“simply to punish a dereliction that will likely have no substantial continuing effect on future judicial proceedings.”’” (McDermott Will & Emery LLP v. Superior Court (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 1083, 1120.)

 

Advocate Witness Rule

 

California recognizes the attorney advocate witness role as a basis of disqualification. “A lawyer shall not act as an advocate in a trial in which the lawyer is likely to be a witness unless: (1) the lawyer's testimony relates to an uncontested issue or matter; (2) the lawyer's testimony relates to the nature and value of legal services rendered in the case; or (3) the lawyer has obtained informed written consent1 from the client. If the lawyer represents the People or a governmental entity, the consent shall be obtained from the head of the office or a designee of the head of the office by which the lawyer is employed.” (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 3.7(a).) The purpose of the rule itself is to “prevent fact finder confusion regarding whether an advocate-witness's statement is to be considered proof or argument.” (Doe v. Yim (2020) 55 Cal.App.5th 573, 582.)

 

Plaintiff moves for disqualification of Defense counsel, including the two lawyers applying for pro hac vice admission, attorneys William Tarnow and Alissa Griffin. Plaintiff alleges associates and/or partners of the law firm, Neal, Gerber & Eisenberg LLP, interviewed Plaintiff and conducted an internal investigation on behalf of their client following submission of Plaintiff’s employment complaints regarding a hostile work environment to Defendant’s Director of Human Resources and the office of general counsel for the National Basketball Association (NBA). Plaintiff alleges being “required to sign an ‘interview statement’” at which time it was disclosed the statement constituted attorney client privileged information only subject to waiver by Defendant. [Comp., ¶¶ 13-20.] The interview included identification of witnesses, and examples of discriminatory conduct. [Id., ¶¶ 21-22.]

 

Plaintiff contends attorney William Tarnow conducted the interview, and Sonya Rosenberg directed the investigation. Attorney Alissa Griffin also advised Defendant on the steps leading to the subject complaint. Plaintiff moves on grounds of undue prejudice due to the pre-litigation conduct.

 

Defendant challenges both the factual basis and broad scope seeking disqualification of the entire firm. Defendant denies any substantial contact between attorneys Tarnow and Griffin, and Plaintiff. Tarnow only sent an “administrative in nature” email to Plaintiff, and Griffin never communicated with Plaintiff in any way. Defendant identifies attorneys Rosenberg and Chertok, as responsible for the investigation, and denies any necessity of calling Tarnow or Griffin as potential witnesses.

 

In addition to the complaint, Plaintiff’s counsel submits a declaration, with certain exhibits including the complaint to the NBA league office, investigation email from the NBA, email from Defendant whereby attorney Tarnow was cc’d, an email from Tarnow whereby Tarnow requests an interview and identifies both Sonya Rosenberg and Ben Cherok as the responsible parties for the investigation, the interview statement without any actually identified attorney, and a notice of a pending follow-up to the  investigation from Sonya Rosenberg. [Declaration of Debra Lauzon, Ex. 1-5.]

 

Defendant submits no declaration in support of any representations made regarding handling counsel. The motion, including the declaration of attorney Lauzon confirm the involvement of attorney Rosenberg. Defendant identifies attorney Chertok instead of Griffin as involved in the investigation, which also conforms to the exhibits submitted with the motion. The court also finds the email from attorney Tarnow administratively routine without any sign of engagement into the claim(s).

 

Accepting the undisputed existence of the involvement of the Neal, Gerber & Eisenberg LLP firm in the pre-litigation investigation of the claim(s) leading to termination and the filing of the subject complaint, at a minimum, the circumstances support consideration of the application of the rule to at least the two attorneys involved in the pre-litigation investigation. (Doe v. Yim, supra, 55 Cal.App.5th at pp. 583-584.)

 

Plaintiff submits no evidence establishing any involvement of Griffin. The motion apparently preemptively names Griffin as part of the greater effort to disqualify the entire firm.

 

The direct participation of Rosenberg and Chertok leads to the first step of consideration for disqualification: the threat of prejudice caused by the necessity of attorneys Rosenberg and Chertok to appear as witnesses at trial. The existence of the interview statement and investigation report while a likely part of the trial, reliance on testimony from counsel remains an unfounded position for purposes of establishing a direct concern with prejudice.  In other words, Plaintiff lacks any showing that the source of said items exclusively depends on the testimony of the two attorneys as opposed to other means of admission. (Smith, Smith & Kring v. Superior Court (Oliver) (1997) 60 Cal.App.4th 573, 581.)

 

The policy for disqualification fundamentally requires a sufficient showing of potential, actual prejudice. The threat must be practical rather than speculative and for purposes of gaining a tactical advantage and improperly excluding a client from utilizing counsel of their choice. (Id. at pp. 580-581; see Lopez v. Lopez (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 412, 425.) “‘Speculative contentions of conflict of interest cannot justify disqualification of counsel.’” (Smith, Smith & Kring v. Superior Court (Oliver), supra, 60 Cal.App.4th. at p. 582.)

 

The motion also lacks any acknowledgment regarding the right of Defendant to waive the attorney client privilege as provided in section 3.7(a)(3), which provides a specific exemption from disqualification upon approval by the client (e.g. Defendant) The interview statement in and of itself is identified as Defendant privileged work product. Plaintiff presents no dispute to this categorization and instead appears to suggest prejudice from Plaintiff’s responses.

 

The exemption reflects an acceptance of the ostensible disadvantage of counsel both testifying and advocating as part of the greater preference for allowing a client to choose preferred counsel. While integrity of the judicial process remains paramount, the challenging party must also still address this standard. (Lyle v. Superior Court (1981) 122 Cal.App.3d 470, 482-483.) Notwithstanding the waiver by Defendant, and only considering Plaintiff’s position that certain conveyed information prejudices the prosecution of the claims, the position still lacks support.

 

Nothing in the motion or declaration of counsel identifies any information unique to the action that would not otherwise readily become part of shared information via discovery, including deposition. The policy supports a superseding finding in favor of disqualification where confusion and veracity become the paramount concern. The practical positions relied upon in the motion fall short of this standard in that nothing in the motion establishes a potential scenario where all information comes through any associate of the law firm and every firm employee becomes instantly discredited simply by association. The motion does not support such a sweeping finding.

 

The motion is therefore denied based on insufficient legal argument and evidence in support of disqualification other than the existence of partial involvement of certain counsel in the pre-litigation investigation. The motion fails to establish a broad encompassing basis for disqualification of the entire firm, and rejection of any potential severance of involvement of the pre-litigation attorneys from trial, even if Plaintiff established their presence as absolutely integral to the trial. The involvement of proposed pro hac vice counsel presents an even more attenuated connection given the lack of any evidence of the attorneys’ substantive involvement in any pre-litigation actions.
