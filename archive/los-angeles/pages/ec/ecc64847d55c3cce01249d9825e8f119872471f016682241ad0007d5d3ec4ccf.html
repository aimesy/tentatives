DEPARTMENT A15 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

SUBMITTING ON A TENTATIVE RULING

A tentative ruling is not the final ruling of the Court. The Court may adopt, modify, or withdraw the tentative ruling at the scheduled hearing. Tentative rulings are not invitations or opportunities to file further documents related to the hearing. No such document will be considered by the Court.

If all parties wish to submit the matter without oral argument:

1. The parties must confer and confirm that every party agrees to submit.

2. One party—not each party separately—must send a single joint email to ATPdeptA15@lacourt.ca.gov no later than 8:00 a.m. on the date scheduled for the hearing.

3. The email must copy all other counsel and self-represented parties and use this subject line:

SUBMISSION — [CASE NUMBER] — [CASE NAME] — [HEARING DATE]

4. The email must state: “All parties have conferred and agree to submit the matter on the Court’s tentative ruling without oral argument. No party intends to appear. This email copies all counsel and self-represented parties.”

The email must identify the sender’s name, the party represented, and must include a telephone number for the sender.

Do not telephone the courtroom to submit on a tentative ruling. Do not send separate submission emails. Court staff will not acknowledge or confirm receipt.

If every party does not agree to submit, do not send a submission email. If all parties have not agreed to submit, any party wishing to be heard may appear at the scheduled hearing without giving advance notice to the Court.

A submission does not signify agreement with the tentative ruling. It means only that the parties do not request oral argument.

No party may submit another party’s position without that party’s express agreement.

If all parties submit and the tentative does not expressly require an appearance, the parties need not appear. The Court’s final ruling will be reflected in the minute order.

Emails are not part of the official court record. Do not use the Court's email address for any purpose other than submitting on a tentative ruling or as ordered by the Court. Do not copy the Court on emails among the attorneys, parties, or others. Do not use the Court's email address to ask questions regarding a case. 

The Court will not respond to emails submitted through the department's email address.

Inappropriate use or misuse of the Court's email address or violation of these or other rules may result in sanctions, including blocking receipt of emails by the violating sender, after the first misuse or violation. 

Case Number: 23AVCV01238    Hearing Date: September 7, 2026    Dept: A15

PRINCETON LYRIC CARTER v. JOHN A. FABER et al.

Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement (CCP 877.6)




DISPOSITION

Defendants John A. Faber, M.D. and Amen Clinics Los Angeles, P.C.’s motion for determination of good faith settlement is GRANTED. The settlement with plaintiff Princeton Lyric Carter, a minor appearing through his guardian ad litem, Melanie Martin, individually and as successor in interest to Aaron Charles Carter, is determined to have been made in good faith under Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6.

The determination bars any other joint tortfeasor or co-obligor from further claims against the settling defendants for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault. (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (c).) It does not dismiss any pleading or adjudicate claims outside that statutory scope.

The settlement remains subject to court approval of the minor’s compromise. This ruling does not grant that approval or authorize distribution of the settlement proceeds.

REASONS

This wrongful death and survival action concerns allegations that defendants negligently prescribed or dispensed controlled substances that contributed to Aaron Charles Carter’s death. The settling defendants dispute negligence and causation. (Mann Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) The court continued the motion from September 1 to September 8, 2026, and permitted supplemental evidence. (Minute Order, Sept. 1, 2026, p. 1.) The supplemental declaration now discloses the settlement amount. (Supp. Mann Decl., ¶ 4.)

In evaluating good faith, the court considers the estimated recovery and the settling parties’ potential responsibility, the settlement amount and allocation, the discount appropriate to settlement, the settling defendants’ financial condition and insurance, and any collusive or fraudulent conduct prejudicing nonsettling parties. The evaluation rests on information available when the settlement was reached. (Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499.)

When the motion is unopposed, a brief motion stating the grounds for good faith, supported by a declaration describing the case, may suffice; a complete evidentiary presentation addressing every factor is not required. (City of Grand Terrace v. Superior Court (1987) 192 Cal.App.3d 1251, 1261.) The party asserting lack of good faith bears the burden on that issue. (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (d).)

Mann declares that the parties participated in mediation on March 24, 2026, continued negotiations through the mediator, and reached a settlement on April 20, 2026. Amen Clinics’ professional liability insurer agreed to pay an amount identified in Mann's supplemental declaration in exchange for dismissal with prejudice, and Faber agreed to waive recovery of costs in exchange for dismissal with prejudice. (Supp. Mann Decl., ¶ 4.) Mann further declares that he disclosed the settlement amount to counsel for the nonsettling defendants, that those defendants have not opposed the motion, and that the negotiations were at arm’s length and free of collusion or fraud. (Supp. Mann Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.)

The supplemental declaration supplies the previously undisclosed monetary term. The court considers that payment obligation and Faber’s cost waiver together as the settlement presented by the two moving defendants. The declarations describe the disputed liability, the settlement terms, and the circumstances of negotiation. No opposition or evidence disputing the good faith of this settlement has been submitted. On this unopposed record, the showing is sufficient to support a determination of good faith. (City of Grand Terrace, supra, 192 Cal.App.3d at p. 1261.)

The court does not adopt defendants’ estimates of damages or comparative fault as factual findings or resolve the merits of the underlying negligence and causation disputes. The determination is limited to the good faith of the settlement described in Mann’s supplemental declaration, with the requirement of separate minor’s compromise approval preserved.
