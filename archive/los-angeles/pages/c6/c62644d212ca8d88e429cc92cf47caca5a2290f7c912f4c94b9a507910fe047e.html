ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On July 30, 2021, Plaintiff Karina Lepe Tenorio (“Plaintiff”), as an individual and on behalf of all others similarly situated, filed a Complaint Under the Labor Code Private Attorneys General Act (“PAGA”) against Defendants Joya Food Enterprises, Inc.; Canelo Enterprises, Inc.; Gonzalez Management Company, Inc.; Jalos Food Enterprises, Inc.; Santa Isabel Enterprises, Inc.; Zevada Enterprises, Inc.; and Zixta Enterprises, Inc. (“Defendants”) and Does 1-10.

 

On September 7, 2021, Defendants filed an Answer to the Complaint.

 

On May 18, 2026, the parties’ counsel reported to the Court that a settlement agreement was almost complete. (5/18/26 Order.)

 

            On July 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Court Approval of the Parties’ PAGA Settlement (the “Motion”).

 

            On July 21, 2026, the Court, in pertinent part, advanced and continued the hearing on the Motion from October 14, 2026, to September 17, 2026. (7/21/26 Order.)

 

On July 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed and served Notice of Advanced Hearing on the Motion.

 

To date, no Opposition to the Motion has been filed.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

Given that the pertinent PAGA notices in this matter were sent to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency (LWDA) prior to June 19, 2024, the Court applies certain PAGA requirements in effect prior to the new requirements established in 2024 by S.B. 92 and A.B. 2288.

  

Former Labor Code section 2699(l)(2) stated: “The superior court shall review and approve any settlement of any civil action filed pursuant to this part [Labor Code Private Attorneys General Act of 2004 (“PAGA”)].  The proposed settlement shall be submitted to the [Labor and Workforce Development Agency (“LWDA”)] at the same time that it is submitted to the court.”

 

An application for approval of such a settlement must demonstrate that the proposed settlement is fair, adequate, and reasonable to all those affected by it. ¿(Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 549; Nordstrom Com. Cases (2010) 186 Cal.App.4th 576, 579.)

 

In determining whether a settlement falls within the parameters of what may be considered fair, adequate, and reasonable, courts regularly rely on “ ‘ “the strength of the case for plaintiffs on the merits, balanced against the amount offered in settlement.” ’ ” (Kullar v. Foot Locker Retail, Inc. (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 116, 130, 131-132.) Other important indicia of fairness include arms’-length negotiations, experienced counsel, and an adequate investigation of the claims. (Id., at pp. 129-130.)

 

Formerly, seventy-five percent of all PAGA penalties must be paid to the LWDA.  (See former Lab. Code, § 2699(i) [“Except as provided in subdivision (j), civil penalties recovered by aggrieved employees shall be distributed as follows: 75 percent to the Labor and Workforce Development Agency for enforcement of labor laws and education of employers and employees about their rights and responsibilities under this code, to be continuously appropriated to supplement and not supplant the funding to the agency for those purposes; and 25 percent to the aggrieved employees.”].) Courts have discretion to approve settlements that do not allocate any penalty amount.  (Nordstrom Com. Cases, supra, 186 Cal.App.4th at p. 589). 

 

Former Labor Code § 2699(g)(1) provided, in relevant part: “Any employee who prevails in any action shall be entitled to an award of reasonable attorney’s fees and costs . . . .”  (Lab. Code, § 2699(g)(1).)

 

III.            Discussion

 

Plaintiffs move for approval of the parties’ PAGA settlement.

 

The Agreement

 

Here, Plaintiff provides evidence of Plaintiff’s counsel’s experience and states that, after engaging in discovery and extended arms-length negotiations, the parties were able to reach a settlement of Plaintiff’s PAGA claims.  (Motion, Perez Decl.) The parties executed the PAGA Settlement Agreement in May 2026.  (Motion, Perez Decl., Exh. 1.)  Pursuant to the settlement agreement, the parties agreed that Defendants would pay a total of $2,735,000 to settle the PAGA claims.  (Motion, Perez Decl., Exh. 1, ¶ 10.)  The parties have agreed to a proposed distribution of the gross settlement as follows: (1) not more than $911,666 for attorney’s fees; (2) not more than $75,000 for costs and expenses; (3) $30,000 for settlement administration costs given there are approximately 10,300 aggrieved employees; (4) $10,000 for Plaintiff’s service payment and $5,000 to Plaintiff’s counsel; and (5) the PAGA Payment—i.e., the amount remaining from the gross settlement amount after deducting attorney’s fees, litigation costs, settlement administration costs, and Plaintiff’s service payment—for PAGA penalties wherein 75 percent would be paid to the LWDA and 25 percent would be paid to all aggrieved employees.  (Motion, Perez Decl., Exh. 1, ¶¶ 10-14.)

 

As to the $2,735,000 gross settlement amount, Plaintiff provides evidence that Plaintiff’s counsel believes the settlement is fair, reasonable, and adequate, and the settlement was a result of the parties’ acceptance of a mediator’s proposal. (Motion, Perez Decl., ¶¶ 10-11.) Further, the Motion states that Defendants’ exposure would be approximately $59.3 million. (Motion, p. 15.)

 

With respect to the PAGA Payment, the Court finds that the distribution substantially complies with former Labor Code section 2699(i), which required 75 percent of the amount to be paid to the LWDA and 25 percent of the amount to be paid to the aggrieved employees, which here consists of “all persons who were employed by Defendants in California as non-exempt, hourly-paid employees at any time from May 25, 2020 through March 5, 2026.” (Motion, Perez Decl., Exh. 1.)  The estimated PAGA Payment is approximately $1,306,540.99 paid to the LWDA and $435,513.66 paid to the Aggrieved Employees.  (Motion, p. 8.) Plaintiffs also understand there to be about 592,474 pay periods. (Motion, p. 14.)

 

The Court finds the PAGA Payment amounts satisfactorily reflect 75 percent of the net settlement amount to be paid to LWDA and 25 percent of the net settlement amount to be paid to the Aggrieved Employees. Further, these amounts match the amounts stated in the Proposed Order accompanying the Motion. (Proposed Order, p. 3.)

 

As to the terms of the release, the Court finds the general release under the PAGA Settlement Agreement is fair and reasonable under the statute.  The release as to the PAGA aggrieved employees is appropriately limited to “any and all Settled Claims during the Settlement Period. Aggrieved Employees, other than Plaintiff, will not be deemed to have released any individual wage and hour claims by virtue of this Settlement.” (Motion, Perez Decl., Exh. 1, ¶ 19.) 

 

The Settlement Agreement also provides for a separate, more encompassing release for Plaintiff herself and allots her an extra $10,000 and her counsel an extra $5,000 separately from the rest of the Settlement Agreement. (Motion, Perez Decl., Exh. 1, ¶ 13.)

            Given these deficiencies, the Court is unable to find the fees reasonable based on the evidence presently before the Court.

 

 Plaintiff’s counsel provides counsel’s declaration to support the requested costs and expenses in this matter. (Motion, Perez Decl.) Plaintiff requests $50,979.35 in costs and fees, reflecting $409.26 in arbitration fees; $4.75 in copying, printing & scanning and fax fees; $3,222.18 in court fees, courier fees, filing and service of process fees; $8,052.67 in court reporter, transcripts, and depositions fees; $7,060.00 in Fountain Consulting fees; $2,586.25 in private investigator fees; $28,250.00 in mediation fees; and $1,394.24 in research service fees (PACER, Westlaw, etc.). (Motion, pp. 22-23, Perez Decl., p. 11.)

Plaintiffs’ Service Payment

 

As to Plaintiff’s service payment of $10,000, Plaintiff Karina Lepe Tenorio provided a declaration setting forth the actions Plaintiff Lepe Tenorio undertook in litigating this action, including engaging in legal conferences with counsel; reviewing the claims presented in the instant action; discussing details of Plaintiff’s employment at Defendants with counsel; preparing written discovery responses; being deposed and reviewing the deposition transcript; and reviewing the subject settlement. (Motion, Lepe Tenorio Decl., ¶¶ 1-15.)

 

The Court finds Plaintiff’s service payment of $10,000 to be reasonable.

LWDA Service Requirement

 

As to the LWDA service requirement, Plaintiff submitted evidence demonstrating Plaintiff’s counsel submitted a copy of the Settlement Agreement to the LWDA on July 14, 2026. (Motion, 7/14/26 Notice, Exh. 1.)

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On December 17, 2025, the jury entered a verdict in favor of Plaintiffs Derrick R. Upchurch and Sari M. Upchurch (“Plaintiffs”) and against BC Design & Development Inc. and Bradley Alan Couch (“Moving Defendants”).

 

On June 8, 2026, the Court granted in part and denied in part Defendants’ Motion Seeking (A) to Offset the Jury’s Award by the Amounts of Pre-Verdict Settlements Plaintiffs Received From Alleged Joint Tortfeasors and (B) Addressing the Impact of BC Design’s 998 Offer. (6/8/26 Order.)

 

On July 20, 2026, Judgment on Special Verdict was entered.

 

            On July 28, 2026, notice of entry of Judgment was served.

 

            On August 12, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Intention to Move for New Trial.

 

            On August 24, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion for New Trial (the “Motion”) and request for judicial notice.

 

            On August 26, 2026, the Court set and served notice of hearing on the Motion.

 

            On September 3, 2026, Defendants filed an Opposition to the Motion.

 

            On September 10, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Reply.

 

II.              Request for Judicial Notice

 

In conjunction with the Motion, Plaintiffs request the Court take judicial notice of 12/1/25 rulings on motions in limine and the 7/20/26 Judgment on Special Verdict in this matter. (See Motion, RJN, p. 2, Exhs. 1-2.)

 

The Court GRANTS the request for judicial notice pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d).

 

III.            Legal Standard

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 657, a motion for a new trial may be granted if there is any:¿¿ 

 

¿           1. Irregularity in the proceedings of the court, jury or adverse party, or any order of the court or abuse of discretion by which either party was prevented from having a fair trial.

            …

5. Excessive or inadequate damages.

6. Insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, or the verdict or other decision is against law.

7. Error in law, occurring at the trial and excepted to by the party making the application.

¿ 

            (Code Civ. Proc., § 657, subds. (1), (5)-(7).)

 

Further :

 

When a new trial is granted, on all or part of the issues, the court shall specify the ground or grounds upon which it is granted and the court’s reason or reasons for granting the new trial upon each ground stated.

 

A new trial shall not be granted upon the ground of insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, nor upon the ground of excessive or inadequate damages, unless after weighing the evidence the court is convinced from the entire record, including reasonable inferences therefrom, that the court or jury clearly should have reached a different verdict or decision.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 657.)

 

            A motion for new trial “shall be heard and determined by the judge who presided at the trial; provided however, that in case of the inability of such judge or if at the time noticed for hearing thereon he is absent from the county where the trial was had, the same shall be heard and determined by any other judge of the same court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 661.)

 

In the context of a nonjury trial, a trial court “may, on such terms as may be just, change or add to the statement of decision, modify the judgment, in whole or in part, vacate the judgment, in whole or in part, and grant a new trial on all or part of the issues, or, in lieu of granting a new trial, may vacate and set aside the statement of decision and judgment and reopen the case for further proceedings and the introduction of additional evidence with the same effect as if the case had been reopened after the submission thereof and before a decision had been filed or judgment rendered. Any judgment thereafter entered shall be subject to the provisions of sections 657 and 659.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 662.)

This power is slightly curtailed when a new judge—i.e., a judge who did not preside over the nonjury trial—hears the new trial motion.  Specifically, “ ‘[It] has been repeatedly held that the court retains power to amend or change the conclusions of law so as to point to a different judgment, and to enter a judgment different from the first announced, and that this power continues until the entry of the judgment and such change may be made by a judge other than the one who tried the cause.’ ”  (Kelly v. Sparling Water Co. (1959) 52 Cal.2d 628, 633, emphasis in original.)  

“The right to a new trial is purely statutory, and a motion for a new trial can be granted only on one of the grounds enumerated in the statute.” (Fomco, Inc. v. Joe Maggio, Inc. (1961) 55 Cal.2d 162, 166.) Since new trial motions are “ ‘creatures of statute,’ ” the “ ‘ “procedural steps for making and determining such a motion are mandatory and must be strictly followed,” ’ ” or else the court acts in excess of jurisdiction. (Maroney v. Iacobsohn (2015) 237 Cal.App.4th 473, 484-485.)¿

 

IV.           Discussion

 

A.    Timeliness

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 659 states:

 

(a) The party intending to move for a new trial shall file with the clerk and serve upon each adverse party a notice of their intention to move for a new trial, designating the grounds upon which the motion will be made and whether the same will be made upon affidavits or the minutes of the court, or both, either:

(1) After the decision is rendered and before the entry of judgment.

(2) Within 15 days of the date of serving notice of entry of judgment by the clerk of the court pursuant to Section 664.5, or service upon them by any party of written notice of entry of judgment, or within 180 days after the entry of judgment, whichever is earliest; provided, that upon the filing of the first notice of intention to move for a new trial by a party, each other party shall have 15 days after the service of that notice upon them to file and serve a notice of intention to move for a new trial.

(b) That notice of intention to move for a new trial shall be deemed to be a motion for a new trial on all the grounds stated in the notice. The times specified in paragraphs (1) and (2) of subdivision (a) shall not be extended by order or stipulation or by those provisions of Section 1013 that extend the time for exercising a right or doing an act where service is by mail.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 659.)

 

Here, Plaintiffs filed a notice of intention to file the new trial motion on August 12, 2026, and judgment was served on July 28, 2026. Thus, the Motion is timely.

 

B.    Parties’ Arguments

 

Plaintiffs move for new trial pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 657, subdivisions (1), (5)-(7) on the grounds that there was irregularity in the proceedings of the court, jury or adverse party, or any order of the court or abuse of discretion by which either party was prevented from having a fair trial; inadequate damages; insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, or the verdict or other decision is against law; and error in law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 657, subds. (1), (5)-(7).) Specifically, Plaintiffs state the damages award is inadequate and not supported by the weight of the evidence as shown by the jury’s “wholesale” adoption of Defendants’ expert, McCormick’s, calculation “to the penny” (Motion, pp. 7-8); the Court abused its discretion by excluding impeachment evidence of Couch’s “contractor-licensing disciplinary matters from 1989, 1991, and 1994 … and prohibiting his prior inconsistent statements about the matter from reaching the jury” (Motion, pp. 8-9); the evidence was insufficient to support the jury’s finding that Couch’s negligence was not a substantial factor in causing Plaintiff’s harm; and the “jury’s finding that Plaintiffs would not have behaved differently if the statutory violations underlying the concealment claims had been disclosed is unsupported.” (Motion, pp. 10-11, capitalization omitted.)

 

In support of the Motion, Plaintiffs submit the judicially noticed materials discussed above as well as a declaration from Plaintiff’s counsel; copies of Trial Exhibits 140 and 142; and copies of Trial Exhibit 381 and McCormick’s trial testimony in the Reporter’s Transcript. (Motion, Stemple Decl., Exhs. A-B.)

 

In Opposition, Defendants state that the Motion should be denied in its entirety as the jury had ample and clear evidence to support its damages award, and Plaintiffs are merely dissatisfied with the lower judgment amount; the Court properly exercised its discretion in excluding Couch’s decades-old disciplinary matters that “had nothing to do with the claims in this case”; the jury’s award was supported by expert evidence, and the jury was entitled to reject Plaintiffs’ expert’s calculation; Plaintiffs’ critiques regarding the defense expert McCormick were presented to and rejected by the jury; case law relied upon by Plaintiffs to support their arguments are distinguishable from the instant matter; and the jury “was entitled to find that Plaintiffs failed to prove reliance and causation on the misrepresentation and concealment claims. (Opp., pp. 1-9.) Finally, Defendants state that Plaintiffs have not met their burden to show that the jury clearly should have reached a different result. (Opp., p. 9.)

 

In Reply, Plaintiffs state that the jury’s adoption of McCormick’s “estimate confirms the damages award is against the weight of the evidence” and implores the Court to act as the “thirteenth juror” and evaluate the two experts’ testimony to find that the jury clearly should have awarded a figure substantially higher than $366,440.76. (Reply, pp. 2-4.) Further, Plaintiffs state Defendants’ characterization of the evidence is unsupported, and Plaintiffs emphasize that the disciplinary evidence regarding and cross-examination of Couch’s inconsistent statements excluded by the Court was error. (Reply, pp. 4-8.) Finally, Plaintiffs emphasize that the jury’s finding that Couch’s negligence was not a substantial factor in causing Plaintiff’s damages is factually inconsistent, and the jury had no grounds to reject Sari Upchurch’s uncontradicted testimony that “had she known of the statutory down payment limitation, she would have paid a smaller amount and questioned Couch.” (Reply, pp. 9-10.) Plaintiffs file an additional declaration and exhibits in support of the Reply.

 

C.    Analysis

 

            As an initial matter, the Court first highlights that the instant Court, Judge Karine Mkrtchyan, was not the Court that presided over the trial in this matter.  “ ‘[It] has been repeatedly held that the court retains power to amend or change the conclusions of law so as to point to a different judgment, and to enter a judgment different from the first announced, and that this power continues until the entry of the judgment and such change may be made by a judge other than the one who tried the cause.’ ”  (Kelly, supra, 52 Cal.2d at p. 633, emphasis in original.)  

 

            Turning to the merits of the Motion, the Court does not find that Plaintiffs have demonstrated an order granting a new trial is warranted.

 

            First, the damages award is supported by expert testimony and is not clearly inadequate. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 657, subds. (5)-(6).) Plaintiffs provide no legal authority mandating that the jury must have “split the difference between the competing experts, discount either figure, or otherwise show independent adjustment” to arrive at their damages award (see Motion, pp. 7-8), and the evidence presented by Plaintiffs demonstrate that the jury award pulled expressly from Defendants’ expert testimony and calculation. (See Motion, pp. 7-8, Stemple Decl., Exh. C, RJN, Exh. 2.) Further, the Court does not find that the record before it demonstrates that the jury clearly should have credited Plaintiffs’ expert instead of Defendants’, especially as the Motion does not provide any evidence of demeanor or manner of testifying regarding the parties’ experts. (See Meiner v. Ford Motor Co. (1971) 17 Cal.App.3d 127, 140-141 [“ ‘ “The best and most accurate record is like a dehydrated peach; it has neither the substance nor the flavor of the fruit before it was dried.” It resembles a pressed flower.’ [Citation.] ‘The cold record cannot give the look or manner of the witnesses; their hesitations, their doubts, their variations of language, their precipitancy, their calmness or consideration. A witness may convince all who hear him testify that he is disingenuous and untruthful, and yet his testimony when read, may convey a most favorable impression. [Citation.] ‘There are many factors aiding in a reasonable conclusion which are presented to the trier of facts in the first instance and not available to one going over the cold record. There is what might be called the “feel” of the case. This embraces a consideration of the witnesses, the manner in which they testify and their general attitude in the court room’ ”].)

 

            Further, the Court does not find that the legal authority relied upon by the Plaintiffs is either binding or persuasive for the Court to find otherwise. (See ibid.; Mercer v. Perez (1968) 68 Cal.2d 104.) Indeed, Plaintiffs’ cases are factually distinguishable from the instant circumstances.

 

As the Second District Court of Appeal has upheld, and as this Court so too follows: this Court has “no power to act as a super juror and substitute its personal opinion for that of the jurors…. To do otherwise would impoverish our system of justice.” (See Huy Fong Foods, Inc. v. Underwood Ranches, LP (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 1112, 1126.)

 

            Next, the Court does not find that the Court’s exclusion of impeachment evidence regarding Couch was legal error. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 657, subd. (7).) The Court provided the following reasoning for excluding the evidence of Couch’s disciplinary actions: “The 1989, 1991, and 1994 matters raised by Plaintiffs are excluded under Evidence Code 352. They are remote in time and of little relevance to the issues here. That Defendant testified at his deposition inaccurately about these matters does not sufficiently tip the balance under Evidence Code 352.” (Motion, RJN, Exh. 1.) The Court finds the Court’s rationale and the exclusion of the evidence itself comports with the discretion afforded under Evidence Code section 352 and the appropriate factors to be considered. (See Evid. Code, § 352.)

 

            Third, the Court does not find that the evidence is insufficient to support the jury’s finding that Couch’s negligence was not a substantial factor in causing Plaintiffs’ harm and, moreover, Plaintiffs fail to point to any evidence in the record to support that the Court find otherwise. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 657, subd. (6); Motion, p. 10.)

 

The Court reiterates that “ ‘[t]he cold record cannot give the look or manner of the witnesses; their hesitations, their doubts, their variations of language, their precipitancy, their calmness or consideration. A witness may convince all who hear him testify that he is disingenuous and untruthful, and yet his testimony when read, may convey a most favorable impression. [Citation.] ‘There are many factors aiding in a reasonable conclusion which are presented to the trier of facts in the first instance and not available to one going over the cold record. There is what might be called the “feel” of the case. This embraces a consideration of the witnesses, the manner in which they testify and their general attitude in the court room.’ ” (Meiner, supra, 17 Cal.App.3d at pp. 140-141.) The Court does not find that the record before the Court demonstrates that the jury clearly should have found Sari Upchurch’s testimony dispositive of this issue. Rather, the jury could have found Sari Upchurch’s testimony regarding this issue lacking in some way, for instance in credibility, and made the determination it did in the Special Verdict.

 

            Additionally, the Court notes that “[t]he purpose of Code of Civil Procedure section 657 is not to give the trial court permission to run roughshod over a party's constitutional right to jury determination. Instead, the purpose is to allow the trial court to grant a new trial on those rare occasions when the jury's verdict is so at odds with any reasonable view of the evidence that judicial intervention is required to avoid a manifest miscarriage of justice.” (See Huy Fong Foods, Inc., supra, 66 Cal.App.5th at p. 1126.)

 

            For all these reasons, the Court does not find that Plaintiffs have shown that a new trial is warranted. Thus, the Motion is DENIED.

 

V.             Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiffs Derrick R. Upchurch and Sari M. Upchurch’s Motion for New Trial is DENIED.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On July 24, 2025, Plaintiffs Veronica Alvarado, Salvador Gomez, Osvaldo Edgar Alvarado, Daniel Alvarado, Cary Alvarado, Fidel Alvarado, and minors Oralani Alavarado Gomez and Veronica Mia Alvarado by and through their guardian ad litem Veronica Alavarado (“Plaintiffs”) filed the instant action against Defendant North Beverly Development Inc. (“Defendant”) and Does 1-50.

 

On April 13, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendant and Does 1-50, alleging causes of action for Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability, Breach of Statutory Warranty of Habitability, Breach of the Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment, Negligence, and Violation of Tenant Anti-Harassment Ordinance.

 

On May 20, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer.

 

On August 12, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint (the “Motion”).

 

            No Opposition was filed.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50 provides: 

 

(a) A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint. 

 

(b) Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial. 

 

(c)¿A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint except one filed¿within¿the time¿specified in subdivision (a)¿or¿(b).¿ Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action. 

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50.)

 

A “party who fails to plead a cause of action subject to the requirements of this article, whether through oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause, may apply to the court for leave to amend his pleading, or to file a cross-complaint, to assert such cause at any time during the course of the action. The court, after notice to the adverse party, shall grant, upon such terms as may be just to the parties, leave to amend the pleading, or to file the cross-complaint, to assert such cause if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith.  This subdivision shall be liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of causes of action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.50, emphasis added.) 

 

“The legislative mandate is clear. A policy of liberal construction of section 426.50 to avoid forfeiture of causes of action is imposed on the trial¿court. A motion to file a cross-complaint at any time during the course of the action must be granted unless bad faith of the moving party is demonstrated where forfeiture would otherwise result. Factors such as oversight, inadvertence, neglect, mistake or other cause, are insufficient grounds to deny the motion unless accompanied by bad faith.” (Silver Organizations Ltd. v. Frank¿(1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 98-99 (Silver Organizations).) “ ‘ “Bad faith,” is defined as “[t]he opposite of ‘good faith,’ ” generally implying or involving actual or constructive fraud, or a design to mislead or deceive another, or a neglect or refusal to fulfill some duty or some contractual obligation, not prompted by an honest mistake . . . , but by some interested or sinister motive[,] . . . not simply bad judgment or negligence, but rather . . . the conscious doing of a wrong because of dishonest purpose or moral obliquity; . . . it contemplates a state of mind affirmatively operating with furtive design or ill will.  [Citation.]”  [Citations.]’  [Citation.] ” (Id., at p. 100.)

 

A cross-complaint is compulsory if it is transactionally related to the subject matter of the complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., § 426.10.) “To be considered a compulsory cross-complaint, a related cause of action must have existed at the time of the service of [the] answer to [the] complaint.” (Crocker National Bank v. Emerald (1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 852, 864.) The late filing of a motion for leave to file a compulsory cross-complaint “absent some evidence of bad faith is insufficient evidence to support denial of the motion.” (Silver Organizations, supra, 217 Cal.App.3d at p. 101.) “Permission to file a permissive cross-complaint is solely within the trial court’s discretion.” (Crocker, supra, 221 Cal.App.3d at p. 864.)

 

III.            Discussion

 

Defendant seeks leave of court to file a compulsory Cross-Complaint to assert causes of action against the adult Plaintiffs named in the FAC that “arise from the same tenancy, occupancy, property conditions, access history, recovery of possession, and related events placed at issue by Plaintiffs.” (Motion, pp. 1-2, 7-9, Meshekow Decl., Kellener Decl.) Further, the “motion followed recent discovery that materially clarified the factual bases and scope of the proposed claims. The motion also followed the concrete risk that settlement performance could conclude the entire action before the claims were filed. Acting on those developments is diligence, not bad faith.” (Motion, pp. 7-8; see Motion, Kellener Decl.) Finally, the Motion asserts that granting leave to file the Cross-Complaint promotes judicial economy and will not prejudice Plaintiffs on the following grounds:

 

The proposed adult Cross-Defendants are already parties. The claims concern the same premises, documents, witnesses, and period already in dispute. No deposition has been completed. Plaintiffs will retain every procedural and substantive defense, including the right to answer, demur, move to strike, conduct discovery, and challenge proof at trial. The Court may address any scheduling consequence through ordinary case-management orders. The proposed Cross-Complaint also avoids duplication. It excludes the minor Plaintiffs, excludes amounts already reduced to judgment against Salvador Gomez and Osvaldo Alvarado, and expressly disclaims liability based on the filing of this action, discovery responses, settlement communications, governmental complaints, or other protected petitioning activity. The fraud claim is limited to alleged pre-surrender concealment and direct tenancy and property-management communications. Granting leave does not prevent Plaintiffs from completing their settlement or dismissing their Complaint.

 

(Motion, p. 8.) The Motion is accompanied by a Proposed Cross-Complaint. (See Motion, Cross-Compl.)

 

The Court finds Defendants’ proposed Cross-Complaint contains compulsory claims. (Motion, Proposed Cross-Compl.) The Cross-Complaint’s causes of action arise out of the same “transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences” as the causes of action in Plaintiffs’ FAC – specifically, the residential lease contract between the adult parties in the instant action. (Motion, Meshekow Decl., Kellener Decl.; FAC.; Code Civ. Pro., § 426.10; Crocker, supra, 221 Cal.App.3d at p. 864.)

 

As the Motion is unopposed, does not appear to be made in bad faith, and is brought due to material learned during discovery, the Motion is GRANTED. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 426.50, 428.50; Silver Organizations Ltd., supra, 217 Cal.App.3d at pp. 98-99; Crocker, supra, 221 Cal.App.3d at p. 864.)  

 

IV.           Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Defendant North Beverly Development, Inc.’s unopposed Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint is GRANTED.

 

Defendant is to file and serve the Cross-Complaint within 5 days of this order.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On March 4, 2026, Plaintiff IRP Grace Apartments, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed a Complaint for Unlawful Detainer against Defendant Vlad Khinich (“Defendant”). (Compl., p. 1.)

 

On April 3, 2026, Defendant, proceeding pro se, filed an Answer.

 

On May 12, 2026, the Court called the Non-Jury Trial and noted there was neither appearance nor communication by or for Defendant on this date. (5/12/26 Order.) The Court proceeded with the Court Trial and entered judgment for Plaintiff for past rent due in the amount of $42,024.52 and holdover damages of $16,038.00 for a total of $58,062.52. (5/12/26 Order, pp. 1-2.) The Court further ordered: “Restitution and possession of the premises located at 732 S SPRING ST APT 920, LOS ANGELES, CA 90014 is granted for plaintiff(s) The Rental Agreement under which the property is held is hereby cancelled. Lease Agreement under which the property is held is hereby forfeited. Plaintiff is to submit a proposed judgment within 5 days.” (5/12/26 Order.)

 

On May 28, 2026, the Court issued Judgment in this matter and issued Notice of Entry of Judgment to Plaintiff’s counsel. (5/28/26 Order; Clerk’s Cert. of Service.)  

 

On July 13, 2026, the Court granted Defendant’s Ex Parte Application for stay of execution of Default judgment until hearing on Defendant’s first motion to set aside. (7/13/26 Order.) The Court “orders the execution on the Judgment stayed, pending the ruling on the Motion to Set Aside/Vacate the Default Judgment scheduled for 7/30/2026.” (7/13/26 Order.)

 

On July 30, 2026, the Court denied Defendant’s first motion to set aside and vacated the prior stay. (7/30/26 Order.)

 

On July 31, 2026, Defendant filed the instant second Motion to Set Aside Judgment and Any Default, and to Quash Any Writ of Possession/Execution (the “Motion”).

 

            No Opposition has been filed.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

“The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

 

The general underlying purpose of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) is to promote the determination of actions on their merits.  (Even Zohar Const. & Remodeling, Inc. v. Bellaire Townhouses, LLC (2015) 61 Cal.4th 830, 838-839.)  

 

“The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d), emphasis added.)¿

 

An order is considered “void on its face only when the invalidity is apparent from an inspection of the judgment roll or court record without consideration of extrinsic evidence.” (Pittman v. Beck Park Apartments Ltd. (2018) 20 Cal.App.5th 1009, 1021.)¿ “There is no time limit to attack a judgment void on its face.” (Ibid.) However, if “the invalidity is not apparent [by an inspection of the judgment or the judgment roll], it may be set aside by motion, if such motion is filed within a reasonable time, i. e., not longer than the time set forth in Code of Civil Procedure, section 473.” (City of Los Angeles v. Morgan (1951) 105 Cal.App.2d 726, 730.) Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) contains no express time limit, and the California Supreme Court recently abrogated the rule that Section 473, subdivision (d) is subject to the two-year limit set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5. (California Capital Ins. Co. v. Hoehn (2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, 225-226 [“We hold that a section 473(d) motion to vacate a judgment that is void for lack of proper service is not subject to the judicially imposed two-year limitation”].)  

 

III.            Discussion

 

Defendant moves to set aside the final judgment in this matter under Code of Civil Procedure sections 473, subdivisions (b) and (d).  Defendant contends the final judgment should be set aside pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) because Defendant lacked notice of the trial due to not receiving Plaintiff’s counsel’s emails. Defendant contends the final judgment should be set aside pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) on the grounds that the three-day notice accompanying the Complaint in this matter greatly overstates the rent due and is thus void as a matter of law. (Motion, pp. 2, 5-7.)

 

            Here, the Court finds the Motion’s contentions regarding Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) – namely, Defendant’s failure to appear at trial due to lack of notice – were previously addressed by the Court in ruling on the first motion to set aside judgment filed by Defendant. (See 7/30/26 Order.) While Defendant repackages the argument as focused on Defendant’s lack of receipt of emails regarding the trial, the Court’s prior order found Defendant received proper notice by mail of the Trial. (See 7/30/26 Order.) As the Court previously ruled on the substantive merits of this portion of the instant Motion, the Court finds the instant request is an attempt to have the Court reconsider the prior ruling absent a proper motion for reconsideration. Thus, the Motion is DENIED as to the request for relief pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).

 

            As to Defendant’s contentions regarding Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) and voidness of the three-day notice attached to the Complaint, the Court finds these assertions are meritless and insufficiently supported. First, the three-day notice attached to the Complaint does not appear void on its face given that the three-day notice does not provide the monthly amount owed for rent; thus, the Court is unable to determine from the face of the three-day notice attached to the Complaint that the total amount of rent owed listed is incorrect and the notice is void as a matter of law. (See Compl., Exh. A.) Further, the Court finds that Defendant’s self-serving declaration alone lacks credibility and is insufficient extrinsic evidence to demonstrate the three-day notice is void as a matter of law. Indeed, Defendant’s only evidence submitted to demonstrate the three-day notice is void in this matter is a self-serving declaration. (See Motion, Khinich Decl.) Defendant fails to provide any other supporting evidence, such as rent payments or invoices, demonstrating that the monthly rent for the subject premises in this matter did not total the amount listed in the Complaint’s three-day notice at the time the notice was served.

 

Moreover, the alleged overstatement of the 3-day notice would have been an affirmative defense had Defendant appeared.  Thus, this argument does not explain Defendant’s failure to appear at trial and is not a proper basis for the set aside of default judgment.

 

The Court acknowledges that Defendant is self-represented. Self-represented litigants are still “held to the same standards as attorneys.” (Kobayashi v. Superior Court (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 536, 543.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Motion is DENIED.

 

IV.           Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Defendant Vlad Khinich’s Motion to Set Aside Judgment and Any Default, and to Quash Any Writ of Possession/Execution is DENIED.
