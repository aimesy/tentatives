FOX CAPITAL GROUP, INC. v. CHURA SERVICES, INC., et al.  

CASE NO.:  22NWCP00018

HEARING:  Thursday, September 10, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Non-Party BLUE WHITE CLEANERS & LAUNDERS, INC.’s “MOTION TO VACATE OR MODIFY AFFIDAVIT OF IDENTITY AND ORDER, QUASH/RECALL/MODIFY WRITE AND LEVY, ORDER RELEASE/RETURN OF FUNDS, AND AWARD FEES AND COSTS” is GRANTED. The Affidavit of Identity and Order entered on September 16, 2022, is hereby vacated as void. The Court orders all funds levied pursuant to that order released. The Court grants the request for attorney’s fees and costs, but the moving party must file a properly noticed motion to seek those fees and costs.

 

Moving Party to give notice.  

 

Background

 

On January 18, 2022, Plaintiff FOX CAPITAL GROUP, INC. (“Plaintiff”) commenced this action against three (3) defendants, CHURA SERVICES, INC., CRYSTALLINE ENTERPRISES INC., and SUN JOO KIM (collectively, “Original Defendants”), by filing an application for entry of a New York judgment based on California’s Sister State Money Judgments Act (Code Civ. Proc., § 1710.10 et seq.).

The same day, Plaintiff obtained a judgment of $71,391.38 against the Original Defendants.

 

On September 16, 2022, Plaintiff filed an Affidavit of Identity, stating that the the Original Defendants were also known by the following names: “Blue White Cleaners & Launders Inc., a California corporation dba Blue White Cleaners adba Blu White Cleaners fka Chura Services, Inc. dba Blue White Cleaners; Crystalline Enterprises, Inc. dba Crystalline Enterprises.” (Affidavit of Identity, p. 1, Item 2.) The Court approved the affidavit and entered an order stating that those names may be added to any writ of execution or abstract of judgment.

 

Thereafter, Writs of Execution were issued against “Blue White Cleaners & Launders Inc. a California corporation dba Blue Blue White Cleaners adba Blu White Cleaners fka Chura Services, Inc. dba Blue White Cleaners.”

 

On June 16, 2026, BLUE WHITE CLEANERS & LAUNDERS INC. (“Blue White”) filed the instant motion “for orders [1] vacating or modifying the September 16, 2022 Affidavit of Identity and Order as to Blue White; [2] quashing, recalling, or modifying the April 10, 2026 Writ of Execution and May 18, 2026 Notice of Levy as to Blue White and any Blue White account or property; [3] ordering release or return of any funds levied from Bank of America accounts ending in 6596, 5030, and 5701; [4] prohibiting future enforcement against Blue White based on the identity order; and [5] awarding attorney's fees and costs under Code of Civil Procedure section 699.510(e).” (Motion, pp. 1:27-2:6.)

 

On August 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed its opposition.

 

On September 2, 2026, Blue White filed its reply.

 

Legal Standard

 

Under California’s Enforcement of Judgments Law (EJL) (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 680.101 to 724.260), a judgment creditor may seek an order adding a judgment debtor’s additional names to a judgment.

 

To do so, the judgment creditor must file with the clerk of the court an “Affidavit of Identity.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 680.135 [“‘Affidavit of Identity’ means an affidavit or declaration executed by a judgment creditor, under penalty of perjury, that is filed with the clerk of the court in which the judgment is entered at the time the judgment creditor files for a writ of execution or an abstract of judgment”].)

 

The affidavit must “set forth the case name and number, the name of the judgment debtor stated in the judgment, the additional name or names by which the judgment debtor is known, and the facts upon which the judgment creditor has relied in obtaining the judgment debtor’s additional name or names.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 680.135.)

 

“If a person who is not the judgment debtor has property erroneously subject to an enforcement of judgment proceeding based upon an affidavit of identity, the person shall be entitled to the recovery of reasonable attorney’s fees and costs from the judgment creditor incurred in releasing the person’s property from a writ of execution, in addition to any other damages or penalties to which an aggrieved person may be entitled to by law, including Division 4 (commencing with Section 720.010).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 699.510, subd. (e).)

 

Discussion

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) (“Section 473(d)”): “The court …may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”

 

Blue White moves to vacate Plaintiff’s Affidavit of Identity and Order pursuant to Section 473(d), arguing that the order was void because Blue White is a separate corporation from any of the judgment debtors.

 

In support of the motion, Blue White’s owner/operator, Yi Sook Kim, attests to the following facts. Blue White “is a California corporation formed in 2006. [the company] has operated for many years as a dry cleaning and laundry business at 7734 Greenleaf Avenue in Whittier, California.” (Motion, Kim Decl., 2.) Plaintiff’s characterization of Blue White as “an alias, dba, alternate dba, or former name of [Chura Services Inc.]” is false. (Id. at 3, 9.) “Search results from the California Secretary of State website show that at some time

between 2018 and 2021, a person named Sun Joo Kim became the sole officer, director, and agent for service of process of [Chura Services Inc.]. To [her] understanding, those filings list an Orangethorpe Avenue address in Buena Park and identify the business as wholesale, not as Blue White's dry cleaning/laundry business.” (Id. 4.) She is “not Sun Joo Kim and [she] do[es] not know anyone named Sun Joo Kim.” (Id. 5.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that: “Because Chura and Blue White are functionally identical, sharing the exact same ownership, management, location, and business operations, the Affidavit of Identity properly identifies the asset holder.” (Opposition, p. 2:6-9.) Specifically, “[b]oth Chura Services, Inc. and Blue White Cleaners & Launders Inc. operate the exact same line of business, a laundry and dry cleaning operation, out of the identical physical storefront location: 7734 Greenleaf Ave., Whittier, CA 90602.” (Id. at p. 2:16-18.) “Yisook Kim is the registered Agent for Service of Process, and previous President for Chura Services, Inc. (listing 7734 Greenleaf Ave.). Simultaneously, Yisook Kim is the registered Agent for Service of Process, Chief Executive Officer (under the name YiSook Jane Kim), and previous President for Blue White Cleaners & Launders Inc. (listing 7734 Greenleaf Ave.).” (Id. at p. 2:20-24.) “Blue White was initially filed on January 6, 2006. However, it fell completely delinquent and corporate compliance was abandoned from 2008 until late 2019. Right as Blue White sat delinquent, Yisook Kim formed ‘Chura Services, Inc.’ on December 13, 2018.” (Id. at p. 2:25-27.) “On February 1, 2022, Chura Services, Inc. was officially Forfeited by the state. Exactly fifteen days later, on February 16, 2022, Yisook Kim suddenly filed an updated corporate Statement of Information for the long-delinquent Blue White, installing herself as CEO and Sehwan Kim as Secretary.” (Id. at p. 3:3-6.)

 

The Court finds Plaintiff’s arguments unpersuasive for the following reasons.

 

“‘[A] person [or entity] may adopt any name in which to prosecute business, and may sue or be sued in such a name.’ [Citation.]” (The Rossdale Group, LLC v. Walton (2017) 12 Cal.App.5th 936, 945; see also Cabrera v. McMullen (1988) 204 Cal.App.3d 1, 4 (Cabrera) [“a person may sue or be sued in any name in which he or she is known and recognized”].)

 

However, “[t]he designation of ‘DBA’ or ‘doing business as’ simply indicates [a corporation] operates under a fictitious business name. (See Bus. & Prof.   Code, § 17900 et seq. [regulating fictitious business names].) …. … ‘“The designation [DBA] … is merely descriptive of the person or corporation who does business under some other name. Doing business under another name does not create an entity distinct from the person operating the business.” [Citation.] The business name is a fiction, and so too is any implication that the business is a legal entity separate from its owner.’ [Citations.]” (Pinkerton’s, Inc. v. Superior Court (1996) 49 Cal.App.4th 1342, 1348.)

 

To invoke the DBA designation for an entity or person, there must be evidence that the person or entity has adopted that name or is known by that name. For example, in the personal injury case Cabrera, there was “no dispute that [the] plaintiff adopted ‘Bonfilio’ as his alias because the plaintiff admitted during his deposition that he had taken his brother Bonfilio's name as his own. (Cabrera, supra, 204 Cal.App.3d at pp. 3, 4.)

 

Here, there is no such evidence, and Plaintiff has not cited any law in support of its argument that the sharing of ownership, management, location, and business operations satisfies the requirements for DBA designation. As Blue White argues, Plaintiff appears to be invoking the alter ego doctrine and/or successor liability theory. (See Troyk v. Farmers Group, Inc. (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 1305, 1341 [“‘“Under the alter ego doctrine, … when the corporate form is used to perpetuate a fraud, circumvent a statute, or accomplish some other wrongful or inequitable purpose, the courts will ignore the corporate entity and deem the corporation's acts to be those of the persons or organizations actually controlling the corporation, in most instances the equitable owners. [Citations.] The alter ego doctrine prevents individuals or other corporations from misusing the corporate laws by the device of a sham corporate entity formed for the purpose of committing fraud or other misdeeds. [Citation.]’ [Citation]”]; CenterPoint Energy, Inc. v. Superior Court (2007) 157 Cal.App.4th 1101, 1120 [“[A] successor company has liability for a predecessor's actions if: (1) the successor expressly or impliedly agrees to assume the subject liabilities (which has never been argued here respecting the unregulated businesses), (2) the transaction amounts to a consolidation or merger of the successor and the predecessor, (3) the successor is a mere continuation of the predecessor, or (4) the transfer of assets to the successor is for the fraudulent purpose of escaping liability for the predecessor's debts”].)

 

For those reasons, the Court grants Blue White’s motion to vacate the Affidavit of Identity pursuant to Section 473(d).

 

Blue White’s owner testifies that $45,368.98 has been withdrawn from the company’s bank accounts pursuant to a levy from this case. (Motion, Kim Decl., 7.) Plaintiff argues that “[r]eleasing the funds would cause irreparable harm to the Judgment Creditor.” (Opposition, p. 8:3-5 [bold emphasis removed].) However, Plaintiff has not cited any law in support of that argument. In addition, the Court has found that the Affidavit of Identity and Order void. As such, there is no basis for prohibiting the return of the levied funds to Blue White.

 

Accordingly, the Court grants the company’s request for an order releasing the levied funds, prohibiting future enforcement against Blue White based on the Affidavit of Identity order, and granting Blue White reasonable attorney’s fees and costs that incurred in releasing the levy from the writs of execution. Pursuant to Blue White’s request, the Court reserves jurisdiction to determine those fees and costs after the levied funds have been released.

 

Conclusion

 

Non-Party BLUE WHITE CLEANERS & LAUNDERS, INC.’s “MOTION TO VACATE OR MODIFY AFFIDAVIT OF IDENTITY AND ORDER, QUASH/RECALL/MODIFY WRITE AND LEVY, ORDER RELEASE/RETURN OF FUNDS, AND AWARD FEES AND COSTS” is GRANTED. The Affidavit of Identity and Order entered on September 16, 2022, is hereby vacated as void. The Court orders all funds levied pursuant to that order released. The Court grants the request for attorney’s fees and costs, but the moving party must file a properly noticed motion to seek those fees and costs.

Case Number: 22NWCV00691    Hearing Date: September 10, 2026    Dept: R

MELENDEZ-RODRIGUEZ v. CITISTAFF SOLUTIONS

CASE NO.:  22NWCV00691

HEARING:  Thursday, September 10, 2026, at 9:30 AM

 

Defendant CitiStaff Solutions, Inc.’s Motion to Quash Subpoena served on Everest National Insurance Company is GRANTED. 

 

Moving Party to give notice. 

 

Background

 

This sexual assault/wrongful termination action was filed by Plaintiff Alba Melendez-Rodriguez (“Plaintiff”) against Defendants CitStaff Solutions, Inc., Camino Real Foods, Inc., and Edy Ernesto Castillo Reyes, on August 10, 2022.

 

Defendant now moves for an order to quash the deposition subpoena issued by Plaintiff to Everest National Insurance Company. The motion is unopposed.

 

Motion to Quash 

 

“If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b),… may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person. (CCP §1987.1(a).) 

 

Defendant contends that the subpoenas demand sweeping corporate testimony and the production of broad and sensitive records concerning CitiStaff’s insurance policies together with a decade of insurer’s claims files, a complete claims history report reaching back to January 1, 2018, every payment, defense costs, and settlement that has eroded policy limits, all coverage and reservation of rights correspondence, and every claim relating to co-Defendant Edy Ernesto Castillo Reyes over the past ten years. Thus, Defendant argues the subpoena is overbroad, invasive, and disproportionate.

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2017.210

 

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2017.210 provides, in relevant part, “[a] party may obtain discovery of the existence and contents of any agreement under which any insurance carrier may be liable to satisfy in whole or in part a judgment that may be entered in the action or to indemnify or reimburse for payments made to satisfy the judgment. This discovery may include the identity of the carrier and the nature and limits of the coverage. A party may also obtain discovery as to whether that insurance carrier is disputing the agreement's coverage of the claim involved in the action, but not as to the nature and substance of that dispute.”¿

 

The subpoena at issue seeks:

 

(1) any and all policies in effect from April 1, 2021 through and including June 1, 2021 (the incident-period);

(2) any umbrella/excess policies;

(3) all documents concerning any claim, lawsuit, or indemnity or defense demand under the policy over the past ten years;

(4) a comprehensive claims-history report from January 1, 2018 to the present;

(5) documents evidencing exhaustion, reduction, or impairment of limits;

(6) records of all payments, defense costs, indemnity payouts, and settlements from January 1, 2018 to the present;

(7) all coverage, reservation-of-rights, and denial correspondence; and

(8) all claims relating to Castillo Reyes over the past ten years.

 

(Defendant’s Ex. A.) 

 

The California Supreme Court held that section 2017.210 reaches only the existence and contents of liability policies, "not the assets of the insurance companies," and that nothing in the common law, legislative history, or statutory text authorizes "broad discovery of the financial health of the liability insurer or its ability to meet its contractual obligations under its policies." (Catholic Mutual Relief Society v. Superior Court (2007) 42 Cal.4th 358, 373-374.)¿¿ 

 

The Court finds that the subpoena is overbroad and Plaintiff must limit her request to documents related to the availability and extent of CitiStaff’s insurance coverage.  The subpoena seeks claims files, a claims-history report, limit-erosion detail, and a running tally of every payment, defense cost, and settlement.

 

Further, as to request 8, other-claims discovery requires a threshold showing of similarity: without a showing that other claims “were or might have been even remotely similar in nature,” the request is an unauthorized fishing expedition. (Mead Reinsurance Co. v. Superior Ct. (1986) 188 Cal. App. 3d 313, 320.)

 

Lastly, corporations have no constitutional privacy right — article I, section 1 protects “people” only — and the corporate right to privacy is a lesser, non-fundamental right. (SCC Acquisitions, Inc. v. Superior Ct. (2015) 243 Cal. App. 4th 741, 755.) Whether discovery infringes it is resolved by a balancing test weighing relevance and likelihood of leading to admissible evidence against the corporate privacy interest, with doubts about relevance resolved in favor of discovery. (Id.) Here, the relevance of the insurance files is weak as the lawsuit involves employment law/personal injury. Further, Plaintiff has failed to file an opposition to show how the information at issue is relevant. Thus, the overbroad requests would infringe on corporate privacy interests.

 

Accordingly, the motion to quash is granted.

 

As to the request for monetary sanctions, Defendant failed to seek sanctions in the notice of motion and thus, the request is denied. (See CCP section 2023.040.)
