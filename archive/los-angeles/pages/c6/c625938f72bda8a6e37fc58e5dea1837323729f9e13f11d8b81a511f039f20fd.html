Case Number: 25PSCV01303    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

DEFENDANT G.I. TRUCKING COMPANY dba ESTES WEST’S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR TERMINATING SANCTIONS AGAINST PLAINTIFF JORGE ALBERTO BALDERRAMA is DENIED.

Background

 

This is an employment case. Plaintiff JORGE ALBERTO BALDERRAMA alleges the following against Defendant G. I. TRUCKING COMPANY, DBA ESTES WEST, DBA ESTES EXPRESS: Plaintiff was employed by Defendant for over 9.5 years. On or about February 11, 2025, Plaintiff learned the pallet jack was missing and reported that to his employer; the following day, he was ordered leave the premises. On February 14, 2025, Plaintiff was terminated due to alleged “low performance” but that he had been described as a reliable worker. Plaintiff alleges that he was terminated due to his age and national origin.

 

On April 14, 2025, Plaintiff filed suit asserting the following causes of action (COAs):

 

1. Breach Of Employment Contract;

2. Wrongful Termination;

3. Failure To Pay Wages;

4. Age Discrimination;

5. National Origin Discrimination;

6. Retaliation; And

7. Infliction Of Emotional Distress

 

On May 15, 2025, Defendant filed its answer.

 

On January 7, 2026, the parties attended an IDC with respect to Defendant’s discovery requests. There was no appearance by Plaintiff’s Counsel. With that, the court set an “Order to Show Cause Re: Why the Court Should Not Issue Monetary Sanctions Against Counsel for Plaintiff for their Failure to Appear at the IDC on 01/07/2026 and/or Sanctions in the Form of

Dismissal for Lack of Prosecution….”

 

On January 12, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motions.

 

On April 2, 2026, the court granted Defendant’s Motion to Compel Special Interrogatories and Hearing on Motion to Compel Request for Production of Documents. Responses and sanctions, per the signed proposed orders, were due within 14 calendar days of the signing of the order. Of note, the motions were not opposed and Plaintiff’s counsel submitted on the tentative ruling.

 

On April 30, 2026, the instant motion was filed. As with the other motions, this motion is not opposed by Plaintiff.

 

Discussion

 

Defendant makes this request on the ground that this court has inherent authority to dismiss this action with prejudice against Plaintiff because Plaintiff has ignored various court orders and/or in the alternative, terminating sanctions are also warranted based on Plaintiff’s misuse of the discovery process under California Code of Civil Procedure (“CCP”) § 2023.010. The court notes there is no opposition filed to this motion. While that may create an inference that the motion is meritorious, “dismissal is always a drastic remedy to be employed only in the rarest of circumstances.” (Stephen Slesinger v. Walt Disney Co. (2007) 155 Cal.App.4th 736, 764, italics original (Slesinger).) Accordingly, as the court must act within the “bounds of reason” (id. at p. 765), even absent argument by plaintiff, this court must examine the circumstances of the case prior to issuing terminating sanctions. Doing just that, the court denies the motion.

 

Defendant cites to various authority but strongly relies upon Slesinger.[1] Slesinger, however, militates against granting the motion. In Slesinger, the plaintiff sued the Walt Disney Company (Disney) alleging that Disney failed to pay certain royalties under its licensing agreement with Plaintiff. (Id. at p. 740.) Plaintiff hired an investigator to obtain Disney documents and to do so, the investigator who “obtained the documents by breaking into an uncertain number of Disney office buildings and secure trash receptacles, and by trespassing onto the secure facility of the company with which Disney had contracted to destroy its confidential documents.” (Ibid, emphasis added.) Of note, Plaintiff concealed the investigator's activities from Disney and the court for years. (Ibid.) After a five-day hearing (in a case that spanned over ten years), the trial court found no lesser sanctions aside from terminating sanctions were warranted. Aside from illicitly obtaining documents, the trial court determined that certain documents were also altered by Plaintiff and that Plaintiff chose not to keep certain records. (Id. at pp. 755-756.) In short, Plaintiff’s conduct was “so egregious that no remedy short of terminating sanctions can effectively remove the threat and adequately protect both the institution of justice and [Disney] from further [Plaintiff] abuse.” (Id. at p. 756.)

 

Here, none of the facts above are present. The court does not disagree with Defendant that Plaintiff has failed to obey with a court 4/2/26 order requiring responses and sanctions by served/paid by 4/17/27; that during a more than 8-month time period Plaintiff’s counsel never responded to any of Defendant’s meet and confer efforts; that Plaintiff failed to attend a Court-ordered IDC; that Plaintiff failed to contribute to and/or file Joint IDC Statement beforehand; and that Plaintiff failed to oppose Defendant’s motions to compel further discovery responses. But the court disagrees that such failures trigger terminating sanctions. As framed by the Slesinger court, “The essential requirement is to calibrate the sanction to the wrong.” (Id. at p. 755.) Here, the wrong can fairly be characterized as a lack of interest in prosecuting the case, but not as deliberate and egregious, which is required of the conduct. (Id. at p. 764.) And Defendant’s very own citation to Knight v. Knight v. Pacific Gas & Elec.Co. (1960) 178 Cal.App.2d 923 supports this court’s conclusion that dismissal by way of terminating of sanctions is inappropriate for a plaintiff’s mere failure to prosecute a case. Instead, as in Knight, other procedures such as motion to dismiss for the failure to prosecute under CCP section 583 is more appropriate.

 

All in all, “dismissal is always a drastic remedy to be employed only in the rarest of circumstances.” (Id. at p. 764, italics added.) Unlike Slesinger wherein there was a breadth of intentionally false representations and deliberate indifference to the truth which strongly influenced both court’s decisions to dismiss the case prejudice, that is completely lacking here.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion is denied.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 


[1] To the extent that Defendant relies upon Lyons v. Wickhorst (1986) 42 Cal.3d 91, that case equally does not support Defendant’s position nor is it particularly instructive. In Lyons, the issue was whether “a trial court exceed its authority when it dismisses a plaintiff's action with prejudice because no evidence was presented at a court-ordered arbitration?” (Id. at p. 913.) The appellate court answered in the affirmative. But that opinion largely turned on a party's nonparticipation in the judicial arbitration process and how the Legislature via its 1984 amendment to section 128.5 chose not to provide for dismissal as a sanction if a party refuses to participate but instead it authorized a court to impose additional costs and attorney fees on the errant party or his attorney. Case Number: 25PSCV02837    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

(1)   CROSS-DEFENDANT SVO ENTERPRISE, LLC’S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF ASUNCION CRUZ MARTINEZ’S RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS (SET 1) WITHOUT OBJECTION; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS is MOOT/GRANTED re: sanctions in a reduced amount.

(2)   CROSS-DEFENDANT SVO ENTERPRISE, LLC’S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION FOR ORDER ESTABLISHING PLAINTIFF ASUNCION CRUZ MARTINEZ’S ADMISSIONS OR, ALTERNATIVELY, TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF’S RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION (SET 1) WITHOUT OBJECTION; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS is MOOT/ GRANTED re: sanctions in a reduced amount.

(3)   CROSS-DEFENDANT SVO ENTERPRISE, LLC’S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF ASUNCION CRUZ MARTINEZ’S RESPONSES TO SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES (SET 1) WITHOUT OBJECTION; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS is MOOT/ GRANTED re: sanctions in a reduced amount.

(4)   CROSS-DEFENDANT SVO ENTERPRISE, LLC’S NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF ASUNCION CRUZ MARTINEZ’S RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES (SET 1) WITHOUT OBJECTION; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS is MOOT/ GRANTED re: sanctions in a reduced amount.

 

The motions are moot as after the filing of the motions, on July 2, 2026, Plaintiff served responses and thereafter on 7, 2026 Plaintiff served a verification for the responses. As for sanctions, they are granted in a reduced, total amount of $500.

 

Background

 

This is a negligence case wherein the plaintiff alleges that on August 30, 2023, while plaintiff was handling packages, he fell through an opening in the floor causing him to fall down one story.

 

On August 5, 2025, Plaintiff Asuncion Cruz Martinez filed suit against Defendant Gateway San Gabriel, Inc. (“Gateway”) for (1) Negligence and Premises Liability.

 

On September 29, 2025, Plaintiff filed an amendment to the complaint naming RREEF CPIF San Gabriel Properties, LLC (“RREEF”) as Doe 1.

 

On October 29, 2025, RREEF filed its answer and a general denial and that same day filed a cross-complaint (CC) against SVO Enterprises, LLC (“SVO) for:

 

1) Equitable Indemnity

2) Total Indemnity

3) Contribution

4) Express Indemnity

5) Breach Of Written Contract

6) Declaratory Relief: Duty To

Defend

7) Declaratory Relief: Duty To

Indemnify

8) Declaratory Relief

Contractual Duties

 

On December 4, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed Gateway.

 

On April 29, 2026, the parties attended an IDC.

 

On April 30, 2026, the instant motions were filed.

 

On July 23, 2026, oppositions were filed.

 

On July 29, 2026, a reply was filed.

 

Discussion

 

The court’s ruling will be brief as there is no (probing) legal issue to adjudicate but a seeming disagreement between the attorneys. SVO agrees that its motions are moot only insofar as they sought an order compelling Plaintiff’s response to the Discovery since the discovery was provided but not moot as to sanctions. After a review of the parties’ arguments, the court finds neither party has acted with the requisite level of professionalism and courtesy to warrant sanctions.

 

The timeline, as gathered by the moving papers and not otherwise disputed by Plaintiff, is that on January 19, 2026, SVO served its Discovery to Plaintiff by e-mail to the only counsel identified by Plaintiff (Plaintiff’s responses were therefore due by February 20, 2026); on February 26, 2026, SVO sends follow-up e-mail to Plaintiff’s counsel in the same e-mail chain concerning whether Plaintiff will respond to the Discovery;[1] Plaintiff’s counsel responds the same day stating that he “did not get” the Discovery and that he had not agreed to accept service by e- mail, and that SVO would have to re-serve the Discovery to a “handling” attorney;[2] SVO maintained that pursuant to Cal. Rule of Court 2.251(c)(3) service was proper.

 

Plaintiff maintains that SVO's electronic transmission had not been received and that it did not constitute valid service under the circumstances “because the discovery was not directed to the attorney or recipients identified for discovery-related communications.” (Opp. p. 3; see also opp. 4; see also Aghabala Decl., p. 2 [“After receiving the February 26, 2026 email I immediately responded to SVO's counsel informing him of our Firm's policy that we only agree to electronic service if the handling attorney and his or her assistants are included in the email. I also advised him that I had not received his January 19, 2026 email, advised him to serve the handling attorney and his assistant and stated that "Once the handling attorney and his assistants are properly served, we will gladly respond to the discovery and cooperate with you firm in every way."]; see also Reply p. 3:2-3 [“Plaintiff completely fails to show that SVO’s e-mail service of its Discovery was improper justifying his refusal to respond.”].)

 

For emphasis then, Plaintiff is not disputing that electronic service was permissible but that the right attorney and his/her assistants were not included to constitute proper electronic service.[3] But Plaintiff’s attorney of record was the only counsel for Plaintiff known to SVO. In fact, it appears Counsel Aghabala is the handling attorney as his declaration is provided in support of the opposition and furthermore the email exchanges between the attorneys does not seem to provide the name of a different handling attorney.

 

That leads to the issue of sanctions. The issue was minor/technical, yet it took until well after the filing of the motions for Plaintiff to respond.  As set forth above and in the moving papers, Plaintiff’s refusal to accept SVO’s service was not grounded in law. (See Reply p. 5, citing Doe v. United State Swimming, Inc. (2011) 200 Cal.App.4th 1424, 1434 [“the phrase ‘substantial justification’ has been understood to mean that a justification is clearly reasonable because it is well-grounded in both law and fact.”]; see also Golf & Tennis Pro Shop, Inc. v. Sup.Ct. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 127, 139.) In light of these facts, Plaintiff has presented no “substantial justification” or “other circumstances” that make the imposition of sanctions unjust.

 

That said, exercising its discretion and considering the totality of the circumstances (e.g., Plaintiff’s participation in the IDC), the court reduces the total sanction award to a total of $500 for all motions.[4]

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motions are moot as to compelling responses/granted in a reduced amount as to sanctions.
