DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

MOLAVINE WIDITORA, 

 

         vs.

 

RUSSELL GOMEZ.

 Case No.:  24STCV22589

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  September 23, 2026

 

Moving Defendant Russell Gomez’s demurrer to Plaintiffs Molavine Widitora’s and Cynthia Widitora’s first amended complaint is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

          Moving Defendant Russell Gomez (“Gomez”) (“Moving Defendant”) demurs to Plaintiffs Molavine Widitora’s (“Molavine”) and Cynthia Widitora’s (“Cynthia”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) first amended complaint (“FAC”).  (Notice of Demurrer, pgs. 1-2; C.C.P. §§430.10 et seq., 337.15, 338.)

 

Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, the moving party must meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading to attempt to reach an agreement that would resolve the objections to the pleading and obviate the need for filing the demurrer.  (C.C.P. §430.41, emphasis added.)

Moving Defendant’s counsel declares that he met and conferred telephonically with Plaintiffs’ counsel on August 21, 2025.  (See Decl. of Peto ¶3.)  Moving Defendant’s counsel’s declaration is sufficient under C.C.P. §430.41.  Accordingly, the Court will consider the instant demurrer.

Request for Judicial Notice

Moving Defendant’s 5/22/26 request for judicial notice of (1) the Permit Application Status History for 4430 N. Richard Circle, Los Angeles, California 90032 (“Subject Property”) (D-RJN, Exh. 1); and (2) Certificate of Occupancy (D-RJN, Exh. 2) is granted.

Moving Defendant’s 5/22/26 request for judicial notice of the Complaint filed in this action is denied because the Court does not need to take judicial notice of filings on the instant docket.

 

          Background

          Plaintiffs filed their initial Complaint on September 4, 2024, against Moving Defendant and Non-moving Defendants Liel Construction, Inc. (“Liel Construction”) and Michael Angel Oviedo (“Oviedo”) (collectively, “Non-moving Defendants”) (collectively, “Defendants”) alleging three causes of action: (1) negligence [against all Defendants]; (2) violation of standards for residential construction [against all Defendants]; and (3) breach of contract [against Moving Defendant Gomez].

          Plaintiffs filed the operative FAC on February 11, 2025, against Defendants alleging the same three causes of action.

          Moving Defendant filed the instant demurrer on May 22, 2026.  Plaintiffs filed their opposition on September 4, 2026.  Moving Defendant filed his reply on September 16, 2026.

 

Summary of Demurrer

Moving Defendant demurs to the FAC in its entirety and each cause of action alleged therein on the grounds that the pleading is barred by the applicable statutes of limitation and repose.  (Demurrer, pg. 1; C.C.P. §430.10(e).)  Moving Defendant also demurs to the FAC in its entirety and each cause of action alleged therein on the grounds that the entire FAC is uncertain, ambiguous, and unintelligible.  (Notice of Demurrer, pg. 1; C.C.P. §430.10(f).)

 

Legal Standard

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.)  A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable.  (See Donabedian v. Mercury Insurance Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].)  For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law.  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital District (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

 

Failure to State a Claim

Entire FAC

C.C.P. §337.15(a) provides,

No action may be brought to recover damages from any person, or the surety of a person, who develops real property or performs or furnishes the design, specifications, surveying, planning, supervision, testing, or observation of construction or construction of an improvement to real property more than 10 years after the substantial completion of the development or improvement for any of the following:

 

(1)     Any latent deficiency in the design, specification, surveying, planning, supervision, or observation of construction or construction of an improvement to, or survey of, real property.

(2)     Injury to property, real or personal, arising out of any such latent deficiency.

 

(C.C.P. §337.15(a).)

          C.C.P. §337.15(g) provides,

(g) The 10-year period specified in subdivision (a) shall commence upon substantial completion of the improvement, but not later than the date of one of the following, whichever first occurs:

 

(1)   The date of final inspection by the applicable public agency.

(2)   The date of recordation of a valid notice of completion.

(3)   The date of use or occupation of the improvement.

(4)   One year after termination or cessation of work on the improvement.

 

(C.C.P. §337.15(g), emphasis added.)

Moving Defendant demurs to the entire FAC on the basis that it is barred by the 10-year statute of repose to sue for latent defective construction in C.C.P. §337.15(a).  (Demurrer, pgs. 3-4.)  Moving Defendant argues Plaintiffs’ final inspection was on August 20, 2014, and therefore the statute of repose, which is “absolute” and cannot be extended, ran on August 20, 2024.  (D-RJN, Exhs. 1, 2.)  The Court agrees. This entire lawsuit, filed on September 2, 2024, is time-barred as a matter of law.

Plaintiff’s argument in opposition that pursuant to C.C.P. §337.15(f), C.C.P. §337.15(a) is not controlling is unavailing.  C.C.P. §337.15(f) provides, “this section shall not apply to actions based on willful misconduct or fraudulent concealment.”  (C.C.P. §337.15(f).)  Plaintiff’s initial Complaint, filed beyond the ten-year statute of repose on September 4, 2024, did not allege a cause of action for willful misconduct or fraudulent concealment.  Plaintiff’s initial complaint merely alleges the legal conclusion on information and belief that Defendants “engaged in willful misconduct and fraudulent concealment by failing to disclose the DEFICIENCIES.”  (Complaint ¶15.)

If a statute of limitations appears on the face of a complaint, the plaintiff must plead around it.  (Czajkowski v. Haskell & White, LLP (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 166, 174-175 “[A] demurrer which merely states that the cause of action set forth in the complaint is on its face barred by the statute of limitations is sufficient to raise that defense.”]; Williams v. International Longshoremen’s & Warehousemen’s Union (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 84, 87; see also Bainbridge v. Stoner (1940) 16 Cal.2d 423, 431-432.)

Here, the Complaint and FAC both attach a copy of the purchase agreement acknowledging Plaintiffs bought the Property from Moving Defendant 10 years ago.  Therefore, it appears from the face of the Complaint that the first cause of action for negligence is barred by the three-year statute of limitations.  (Civ. Code §338(b).)  Similarly, the third cause of action for breach of contract is barred by the four-year statute of limitations.  (Civ. Code §337(1).)  The FAC does not state when Plaintiffs learned of the alleged negligence or breaches of contract, so it fails to plead around the blatant defect and fails to state facts sufficient to establish a cause of action. (C.C.P. §430.10(e).)

Accordingly, Moving Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ entire FAC is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

 

          Conclusion

Moving Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiffs’ first amended complaint is sustained with 20 days leave to amend.

DEPARTMENT 729

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

JUDE HALAWI, 

 

         vs.

 

VVV GLOBAL ENT., et al.

 Case No.:  25STCV22013

 

 

 

 Hearing Date:  September 23, 2026

 

Defendants VVV Global Ent., LLC d/b/a Miss USA and Miss Teen USA’s, Laylah Loiczly’s, and Elyse Sevi’s motion for judgment on the pleadings of Plaintiff Jude Halawi’s complaint is granted with 20 days leave to amend as to each cause of action.

 

Moving Defendants VVV Global Ent., LLC d/b/a Miss USA and Miss Teen USA (“VVV Global”), Laylah Loiczly (“Loiczly”), and Elyse Sevi (“Sevi”) (collectively, “Pageant Defendants”) move for judgment on the pleadings of Plaintiff Jude Halawi’s (“Halawi”) (“Plaintiff”) complaint (“Complaint”), and each cause of action therein plead, on the basis it fails to state sufficient facts to constitute a cause of action against Pageant Defendants and the allegations and claims for relief fail to sufficiently allege liability against Pageant Defendants.  (Notice Motion, pg. 2; C.C.P. §438.)

 

Background

Plaintiff filed his operative Complaint on July 25, 2025, against Pageant Defendants and Non-moving Defendants M & C Hotel Interests, Inc., (d/b/a Millennium Biltmore Hotel Los Angeles) (“M&C Hotel Interests”), S&S Labor Force Inc, (d/b/a JRM Private Security) (“S&S Labor Force”), Matthew Lopez (“Lopez”), Renato Basile Entertainment, Inc. (“RB Entertainment”), Renato Basile (“Basile”), Jimmy Wu (“Wu”), Stuart Howe (“Howe”), and Brenda Canales (“Canales”) (collectively, “Non-moving Defendants”), alleging ten causes of action: (1) Violation of Civil Code §52.1 [against all Defendants]; (2) Violation of Civil Code §51.7 [against all Defendants]; (3) Violation of Civil Code §51.5 [against Defendants VVV Global, M&C Hotel Interests, RB Entertainment, and S&S Labor Force]; (4) Violation of Civil Code §51 [against all Defendants]; (5) Assault [against all Defendants]; (6) Battery [against all Defendants]; (7) False Arrest [against all Defendants]; (9) Negligence, including Negligent Hiring, Retention, Supervision and/or Training [against all Defendants]; and (10) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress [against all Defendants].

On June 2, 2026, Pageant Defendants filed the instant motion.  On September 10, 2026, Plaintiff filed his opposition.  On September 16, 2026, Pageant Defendants filed their reply.

 

Legal Standard

“A motion for judgment on the pleadings may be made at any time either prior to the trial or at the trial itself.”  (Stoops v. Abbassi (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 644, 650; see also Korchemny v. Piterman (2021) 68 Cal.App.5th 1032, 1055 [“common law ground for a motion for judgment on the pleadings is identical to the statutory ground”].)

The grounds for a motion for judgment on the pleadings must appear on the face of the challenged pleading or be based on facts the court may judicially notice.  (C.C.P. §438(d); Tung v. Chicago Title Co. (2021) 63 Cal.App.5th 734, 758-759.)

Where the motion is based on matters the court may judicially notice (under Evidence Code §§452, 453), such matters must be specified in the notice of motion or supporting points and authorities.  (Compare C.C.P. §438(d) with Saltarelli & Steponovich v. Douglas (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 1, 5 [taking judicial notice of matters in parties’ exhibits in “nonstatutory” motion for judgment on the pleadings].)

 

          Meet and Confer

Before filing a motion for judgment on the pleadings, the moving party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to the motion for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the motion.  (C.C.P. §439(a), emphasis added.)  A declaration must be filed with the motion regarding the results of the meet and confer process.  (C.C.P. §439(a)(3).)

Pageant Defendants’ counsel’s declaration states that on January 23, 2026, he met and conferred with Plaintiff’s counsel telephonically and the parties were unable to reach an agreement.  (See Decl. of Wahl ¶3.)  Pageant Defendants’ counsel’s declaration is proper pursuant to C.C.P. §439.  Therefore, the Court will consider Pageant Defendants’ motion.

 

Failure to State a Claim

Entire Complaint as Alleged Against Defendant Loiczly

Plaintiff alleges Defendant Loiczly (also known as Laylah Rose) was at all relevant times the president and chief executive of Miss USA.  (Complaint ¶8.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendant Loiczly is a resident of Florida who was doing business in Los Angeles County.  (Complaint ¶8.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendant Loiczly was responsible for the direction, supervision and management of the Miss Teen USA Pageant, including pageant security personnel, and was directly responsible for causing the false arrest and use of non-consensual and excessive force against Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶8.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that each Defendant is, and at all times mentioned was, the agent, employee, representative, successor and/or assignee of each other Defendant.  (Complaint ¶9.)  Plaintiff alleges each Defendant, in doing the acts, or in omitting to act as alleged in this Complaint, was acting within the scope of his or her actual and apparent authority, or the alleged acts and omissions of each Defendant as agent subsequently were ratified and adopted by each other Defendant as principal.  (Complaint ¶9.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that each individual Defendant was in some way responsible for the torts alleged in this complaint.  (Complaint ¶9.)

Pageant Defendants argue the entire Complaint is devoid of any factual allegations against Defendant Loiczly in her individual capacity, and therefore all claims against her should be dismissed.  (Motion, pgs. 9-10.)  Pageant Defendants’ argument is well taken.  While Plaintiff does allege that Defendant Loiczly was “the president and chief executive of [Defendant VVV Global],” Plaintiff fails to allege any facts pertaining to Defendant Loiczly.

With regard to conduct alleged against Defendant Loiczly in her capacity as “the president and chief executive of Miss USA,” Plaintiff also fails to allege facts stating the relationship she had with Defendant M&C Hotel Interests; Plaintiff does not allege VVV Global and M&C Hotel Interests are alter egos of each other.  The mere allegation that each entity ratified the other’s conduct is conclusory and fails for lack of factual allegations.

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion for judgment on the pleadings of Plaintiff’s Complaint as alleged against Defendant Loiczly individually and in her capacity as the president and chief executive of Miss USA is granted with 20 days leave to amend.

Violation of Civil Code §52.1 (Bane Act) (1st COA)

“[T]o state a cause of action under section 52.1 there must first be violence or intimidation by threat of violence. Second, the violence or threatened violence must be due to plaintiff’s membership in one of the specified classifications set forth in Civil Code section 51.7 or a group similarly protected by constitution or statute from hate crimes.”  (Gabrielle A. v. County of Orange (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 1268, 1290.)

Plaintiff alleges that he is of Lebanese descent.  (Complaint, ¶1.)  He alleges that he was attending a six-week long jury trial at the Stanley Mosk Courthouse in Los Angeles starting June 24, 2024, and on or about June 23, 2024, Plaintiff checked in as guest at the Biltmore Los Angeles Hotel and remained as a guest for approximately five weeks until this incident.  (Complaint ¶20.)  Plaintiff alleges that as a long-term guest of the Biltmore Hotel, he had gotten to know many of the hotel’s employees and representatives, and he was known to them as of July 29, 2024.  (Complaint ¶20.)

Plaintiff alleges that on July 29, 2024, Plaintiff returned to the Biltmore Hotel from the trial he was attending, in professional attire.  (Complaint ¶21.)  Plaintiff alleges that he attempted to enter the Biltmore Hotel at the Olive Street entrance, as he had in the past; however, a security guard, who appeared to be an agent of Miss USA, blocked his entrance and without providing additional information told Plaintiff that he could not enter.  (Complaint ¶21.)  Plaintiff alleges that the guard asked Plaintiff what he was doing there.  (Complaint ¶21.)  Plaintiff alleges that he responded that he was staying at the Biltmore and heading to his room.  (Complaint ¶21.)  Plaintiff alleges that the guard firmly denied him entrance and before finally informing Plaintiff that the entrance was closed for a private event.  (Complaint ¶21.)  Plaintiff alleges that the guard told him to go around to the other entrance.  (Complaint ¶21.) 

Plaintiff alleges that after he entered the Biltmore from its Grand Avenue entrance and as he crossed the lobby, he saw several people running toward him, and heard words to the effect of “that’s him!”  (Complaint ¶22.)  Plaintiff alleges that he was confused and asked what was happening.  (Complaint ¶22.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendant Sevi said in a threatening manner words to the effect of “you’re going to see what’s going to happen to you!”  (Complaint ¶22.) 

Plaintiff alleges that shortly afterward one of the pageant security guards, whom Plaintiff was told by pageant officials was Defendant Matthew Lopez, blocked Plaintiff’s path and told him that he was not going anywhere.  (Complaint ¶23.)  Plaintiff alleges that when he asked the guard to explain why and what was going on, the guard refused.  (Complaint ¶23.)  Plaintiff alleges he was simply threatened by taunts indicating that he was going to be punished for some unknown reason.  (Complaint ¶23.)  Plaintiff alleges that as he attempted to pass, the guard grabbed him.  (Complaint ¶23.) 

Plaintiff alleges the same security guard, who Plaintiff is informed and believes was Defendant Matthew Lopez and was employed by Defendant S&S, held Plaintiff’s hands and arms tightly and painfully and pushed Plaintiff toward where Defendant Lopez wanted him.  (Complaint ¶24.)  Plaintiff alleges that he continued to ask what was happening, as he did not understand who these people were or what was happening to him.  (Complaint ¶24.)  Plaintiff alleges that Biltmore Hotel staff observed Plaintiff being grabbed, seized, and knowingly allowed him to be forcefully detained.  (Complaint ¶24.)  Plaintiff alleges that he began calling out to Biltmore Hotel personnel to please help him and that he was a hotel guest and to call the police.  (Complaint ¶24.) 

Plaintiff alleges that he was held against his will by non-Biltmore security personnel with the cooperation of the Biltmore Hotel, whose employees recognized him as a guest.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges that at one point, while he was being forcibly detained, a Biltmore employee approached him and asked him if he was a guest there, to which he replied yes.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges that the employee obtained Plaintiff’s identification and again confirmed that he was a hotel guest, even though this was already known to other Biltmore employees.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges that even after the others present, including the pageant security guards and other agents of Miss Teen USA, heard Biltmore personnel confirm that Plaintiff was a hotel guest, they refused to let go of him.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges that given that he was an invited and paying guest at the Biltmore Hotel, the hotel and its employees and agents owed him a heightened duty of care to prevent foreseeable harm to him by third parties and to intervene once they observed Plaintiff being assaulted, battered and/or unlawfully detained.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges that, instead, they assisted in Plaintiff’s detention, blocked Plaintiff’s path, surrounded him, and allowed him to be forcibly detained against his will and physically injured.  (Complaint ¶25.) 

Plaintiff alleges that Defendant Lopez physically held Plaintiff for an extended period of time with such force that it hurt Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that while he was being physically held against his will, he was shown an image of a man on a cell phone and asked words to the effect of “is this you?”  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that the person on the cell phone photograph clearly was not Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that he was stunned.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that he asked the people around him, including Defendants Sevi, Lopez, and several Biltmore employees, how they could think that this person was him.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that nonetheless, Defendants continued to cause Plaintiff to be physically restrained for several more minutes.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that all the while, he was repeatedly pleading with Biltmore employees to help him, but they ignored his cries for help.  (Complaint ¶26.) 

Plaintiff alleges that during his detention, Defendants Sevi, Loiczly, Basile, and Lopez, and persons acting on Miss USA’s behalf never asked Plaintiff who he was, for his identification or other information, or gave him any explanation as to what was happening to him.  (Complaint ¶27.)

Plaintiff alleges that long after they recognized Plaintiff as a guest and heard his pleas for help, Biltmore Hotel personnel finally informed Defendant Lopez and the other Defendants affiliated with the Miss Teen USA Pageant that Plaintiff was not the person they sought.  (Complaint ¶28.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendant Lopez still would not release Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶28.)  Plaintiff alleges that although Defendant Lopez was acting as their agent on behalf of Defendant S&S in providing security for the Miss Teen USA Pageant, Defendants Sevi, Loiczly, and Basile did not authorize, direct or ask Defendant Lopez to release Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶28.) 

Plaintiff alleges that although he was still being detained, he managed to call his brother, who is a criminal defense lawyer in Los Angeles, and asked for his immediate help.  (Complaint ¶29.)  Plaintiff alleges that his brother, who is also of Lebanese descent, headed to the Biltmore as soon as he received the call for help.  (Complaint ¶29.) 

Plaintiff alleges that shortly before his brother arrived, the pageant Defendants finally let go of Plaintiff, but Plaintiff was still not free to leave. (Complaint ¶30.)  Plaintiff alleges that while Plaintiff was attempting to address what had occurred, a person working for the Miss Teen USA Pageant chastised Plaintiff and falsely denied that Miss USA security had wrongfully detained him.  (Complaint ¶30.)  Plaintiff alleges that instead of owning up to their grievous misconduct and apologizing to Plaintiff, the Pageant Defendants covered up the facts and attempted to deflect blame for their own malfeasance.  (Complaint ¶30.)

Plaintiff alleges that after his release, he and his brother were told that the Miss USA pageant believed Plaintiff to be some person who allegedly posed a threat to a girl or girls participating in the pageant, who Plaintiff understands to have been the different man in the photograph he had seen.  (Complaint ¶31.)  Plaintiff alleges that an employee of the Biltmore Hotel also acknowledged to Plaintiff’s brother that Plaintiff’s detention had violated protocol.  (Complaint ¶31.)  Plaintiff alleges that, nonetheless, instead of taking responsibility for their wrongful actions, Defendants misrepresented what happened in an attempt to obfuscate and cover up their illegal conduct and despicable actions towards Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶31.) 

Plaintiff alleges that he was able to go to his room and obtain his belongings.  (Complaint ¶32.)  Plaintiff alleges that as he approached his room, he observed two Biltmore security guards standing outside his room, whom he had gotten to know during his lengthy stay, and asked them how they could have let this happened.  (Complaint ¶32.)  Plaintiff alleges that they replied by indicating that they were not allowed to do anything to help him, even though they knew he was a guest and had done nothing wrong. (Complaint ¶32.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that on or about July 29, 2024, Defendants and each of them, including DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, interfered, and/or attempted to interfere, by threats, intimidation, coercion, and/or violence with Plaintiff’s rights, including, without limitation, the right to be free from bodily harm or restraint, the right to be free from personal insult and injury to personal relations, the right to be free from discrimination based on race, religion, color and/or national origin or his perceived race, religion, color, and/or national origin, the right to be from excessive force and as provided for by Civil Code Sections 43, 51, 51.5, and 51.7, Penal Code Sections 236, 240 and 242 and Article 1, Sect. 1 of the California Constitution and U.S. Constitution.  (Complaint ¶37.)  Plaintiff alleges that at all relevant times, Defendants acted with intent to violate Plaintiff’s rights or with reckless disregard of his rights.  (Complaint ¶37.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendants, and each of them, engaged in the acts alleged herein and/or condoned, permitted, authorized, directed, approved, and/or ratified the conduct of their officers, managers, staff, employees, agents, and other representatives, and are therefore vicariously liable for the wrongful conduct of their employees and agents.  (Complaint ¶38.) 

Plaintiff alleges that the individual Defendants’ unlawful acts against Plaintiff were committed in the course and scope of their agency and employment with the entity Defendants, which are vicariously liable for the acts and omissions of their agents and employees.  (Complaint ¶39.)  Plaintiff alleges the Biltmore Defendants condoned and ratified the unlawful acts of Defendant Lopez and other Doe Defendants, who were acting as de facto and/or ostensible agents of the Biltmore Defendants.  (Complaint ¶39.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and legal result of the acts and omissions of Defendants, including DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, Plaintiff has suffered physical injury and physical sickness, loss of earnings and earnings capacity, loss of enjoyment of life, pain and suffering, disfigurement, humiliation, fear, anxiety, torment, degradation, embarrassment, shock, loss of self-esteem, medical expenses, severe mental and emotional distress, attorneys’ fees and other pecuniary and non-pecuniary losses.  (Complaint ¶40.)  Plaintiff alleges he has sustained serious and lasting injuries, which could result in permanent disability, the nature and extent of which has yet to be determined.  (Complaint ¶40.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that he will continue to incur medical expenses in the future, the amount of which is not presently ascertained.  (Complaint ¶40.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendants and their managing agents, officers, and/or directors committed the acts alleged herein maliciously, fraudulently, and oppressively, with the wrongful intention of injuring Plaintiff and acted with an improper and evil motive amounting to malice or oppression, and in conscious disregard of the rights, welfare, and safety of Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶42.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants and their managers, officers, and/or directors authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct of their employees or had advance knowledge of the unfitness of their employees and employed them with a conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others and/or are personally guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.  (Complaint ¶42.)

Plaintiff fails to allege the conduct complained of was due to his membership in one of the specified classifications set forth in Civil Code section 51.7 or a group similarly protected by constitution or statute from hate crimes.  Plaintiff’s Complaint merely alleges that “after his release, he and his brother were told that the Miss USA pageant believed Plaintiff to be some person who allegedly posed a threat to a girl or girls participating in the pageant, who Plaintiff understands to have been the different man in the photograph he had seen.”  (Complaint ¶31.)  Plaintiff’s Complaint also states that Plaintiff is a Muslim man of Lebanese descent, but the Complaint makes no allegation that he was detained by the Biltmore Hotel’s staff because of his religion or ancestry; the allegation is that he was a “victim of racial profiling.”  (See Complaint ¶4.)

Plaintiff also fails to allege facts stating the scope of Pageant Defendant’s control over M&C Hotel Interest’s and S&S Labor Force’s employees; Plaintiff’s Complaint acknowledges this ambiguity: “although Defendant Lopez was acting as their agent on behalf of Defendant S&S in providing security for the Miss Teen USA Pageant, Defendants Sevi, Loiczly, and Basile did not authorize, direct or ask Defendant Lopez to release Plaintiff.”  (Complaint ¶28.)  Plaintiff does not allege any conduct by Pageant Defendants that meets the definition of threats, intimidation, coercion, and/or violence.  All factual allegations of such conduct are alleged against Defendant Lopez, Defendant S&S, and M&C Hotel Interest.

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion is granted as to the 1st cause of action with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Violation of Civil Code §51.7 (Ralph Act) (2nd COA)

“Under the Ralph Act, a plaintiff must establish the defendant threatened or committed violent acts against the plaintiff or their property, and a motivating reason for doing so was a prohibited discriminatory motive, or that [defendant] aided, incited, or conspired in the denial of a protected right.”  (Gabrielle A. v. County of Orange (2017) 10 Cal.App.5th 1268, 1291.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants wrongfully deprived Plaintiff of his right to be free from any violence, or intimidation by threat of violence, committed against his person because of his race, religion, color, and/or national origin or his perceived race, religion, color, and/or national origin in violation of Cal. Civ. Code §51.7.  (Complaint ¶44.) 

Plaintiff alleges that he is a Muslim man of Lebanese decent and alleges on information and belief that Defendants assaulted and battered him and threatened and intimidated and physically restrained his freedom of movement because of his race, religion, color, and/or national origin or his perceived race, religion, color, and/or national origin.  (Complaint ¶44.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that his race, religion, color, and/or national origin, or his perceived race, religion, color, and/or national origin, was a motivating factor in Defendants’ unlawful treatment of him.  (Complaint ¶44.)

Plaintiff alleges that the individual Defendants’ unlawful acts against him were committed in the course and scope of their agency and employment with the entity Defendants, which are vicariously liable for the acts and omissions of their agents and employees.  (Complaint ¶45.)  Plaintiff alleges that the Biltmore Defendants condoned and ratified the unlawful acts of Defendant Lopez and other Doe Defendants, who were acting as de facto and/or ostensible agents of the Biltmore Defendants.  (Complaint ¶45.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and legal result of the acts and omissions of Defendants, including DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, Plaintiff has suffered physical injury and physical sickness, loss of earnings and earnings capacity, loss of enjoyment of life, pain and suffering, disfigurement, humiliation, fear, anxiety, torment, degradation, embarrassment, shock, loss of self-esteem, medical expenses, severe mental and emotional distress, attorneys’ fees and other pecuniary and non-pecuniary losses.  (Complaint ¶46.)  Plaintiff alleges that he has sustained serious and lasting injuries, which could result in permanent disability, the nature and extent of which has yet to be determined.  (Complaint ¶46.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that he will continue to incur medical expenses in the future, the amount of which is not presently ascertained.  (Complaint ¶46.)

Plaintiff fails to allege facts demonstrating that the violence committed against him was motivated by ethnic/racial/national or religious animus. Plaintiff merely alleges, “While Mr. Halawi was being physically held against his will, he was shown an image of a man on a cell phone and asked words to the effect of ‘is this you?’ The person on the cell phone photograph clearly was not Mr. Halawi. Mr. Halawi was stunned. Mr. Halawi asked the people around him, including Defendants Sevi, Lopez, and several Biltmore employees, how they could think that this person was him.”  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff also alleges, “[a]fter Mr. Halawi’s release, he and his brother were told that the Miss USA pageant believed Mr. Halawi to be some person who allegedly posed a threat to a girl or girls participating in the pageant, who Plaintiff understands to have been the different man in the photograph he had seen.”  None of these allegations provide a factual basis that states the violence committed against Plaintiff was motivated by ethnic/racial/national or religious animus; Plaintiff’s Complaint essentially alleges that fact that Plaintiff was detained because staff believed he was a person who posed a threat to girls in the pageant.  Plaintiff does not allege that the reason presented to him was pretext for discrimination.

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion is granted as to the 2nd cause of action with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Violation of Civil Code §51.5 (Discrimination in Business Dealings) [against Defendant VVV Global Ent.] (3rd COA)

“[T]he analysis under Civil Code section 51.5 is the same as the analysis we have already set forth for purposes of the [Unruh Civil Rights] Act.” (Semler v. General Electric Capital Corp. (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1380, 1404.)

“No business establishment of any kind whatsoever shall discriminate against, boycott or blacklist, or refuse to buy from, contract with, sell to, or trade with any person in this state on account of any characteristic listed or defined in subdivision (b) or (e) of Section 51, or of the person’s partners, members, stockholders, directors, officers, managers, superintendents, agents, employees, business associates, suppliers, or customers, because the person is perceived to have one or more of those characteristics, or because the person is associated with a person who has, or is perceived to have, any of those characteristics.”  (Civ. Code §51.5(a).)

“The plain language of section 51 does not prohibit only those acts of discrimination which are routinely committed or are a matter of policy. Obviously, a plaintiff will have an easier burden of persuasion if he can provide evidence that the defendant routinely discriminates against the class to which he belongs. Such an allegation is not necessary, however, to plead a violation of section 51. Whether real parties did discriminate in this case is a factual question and it cannot be determined as a question of law from the facts pleaded.”  (Jackson v. Superior Court (1994) 30 Cal.App.4th 936, 942.)

“To state a claim under the Unruh Civil Rights Act, a plaintiff must allege the defendant is a business establishment that intentionally discriminates against and/or denies plaintiff full and equal treatment of a service, advantage, or accommodation based on plaintiff’s protected status.”  (Liapes v. Facebook, Inc. (2023) 95 Cal.App.5th 910, 922.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendant VVV Global Ent. d/b/a Miss USA, is a business establishment within the meaning of Civ. Code §51.5.  (Complaint ¶49.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendant VVV Global Ent. d/b/a Miss USA discriminated against Plaintiff because of his race, color, ancestry, religion, and/or national origin or their perception of these characteristics as alleged herein; and denied him full and equal accommodations, advantages, facilities, privileges, or service because of his race, religion, color, and/or national origin or his perceived race, religion, color, and/or national origin.  (Complaint ¶51.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and legal result of the acts and omissions of Defendant, Plaintiff has suffered physical injury and physical sickness, loss of earnings and earnings capacity, loss of enjoyment of life, pain and suffering, disfigurement, humiliation, fear, anxiety, torment, degradation, embarrassment, shock, loss of self-esteem, medical expenses, severe mental and emotional distress, attorneys’ fees and other pecuniary and non-pecuniary losses.  (Complaint ¶52.)  Plaintiff alleges that he has sustained serious and lasting injuries, which could result in permanent disability, the nature and extent of which has yet to be determined.  (Complaint ¶52.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that he will continue to incur medical expenses in the future, the amount of which is not presently ascertained.  (Complaint ¶52.)

Plaintiff fails to allege facts that Defendant VVV Global Ent. intentionally discriminated against and/or denied plaintiff full and equal treatment of a service, advantage, or accommodation based on plaintiff’s protected status.  Plaintiff merely asserts legal conclusions that he was discriminated against “because of his race, color, ancestry, religion, and/or national origin or their perception of these characteristics as alleged herein.”

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion is granted as to the 3rd cause of action with 20 days leave to amend.

Assault [against all Defendants] (5th COA)

“The essential elements of a cause of action for assault are: (1) defendant acted with intent to cause harmful or offensive contact, or threatened to touch plaintiff in a harmful or offensive manner; (2) plaintiff reasonably believed she was about to be touched in a harmful or offensive manner or it reasonably appeared to plaintiff that defendant was about to carry out the threat; (3) plaintiff did not consent to defendant’s conduct; (4) plaintiff was harmed; and (5) defendant’s conduct was a substantial factor in causing plaintiff’s harm.”  (So v. Shin (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 652, 668-669.)

Plaintiff alleges that after he entered the Biltmore from its Grand Avenue entrance and as he crossed the lobby, he saw several people running toward him, and heard words to the effect of “that’s him!”  (Complaint ¶22.)  Plaintiff alleges he was confused and asked what was happening.  (Complaint ¶22.)  Plaintiff alleges Defendant Sevi said in a threatening manner words to the effect of “you’re going to see what’s going to happen to you!”  (Complaint ¶22.)

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and legal result of the acts and omissions of Defendants, including DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, Plaintiff has suffered physical injury and physical sickness, loss of earnings and earnings capacity, loss of enjoyment of life, pain and suffering, disfigurement, humiliation, fear, anxiety, torment, degradation, embarrassment, shock, loss of self-esteem, medical expenses, severe mental and emotional distress, attorneys’ fees and other pecuniary and non-pecuniary losses.  (Complaint ¶64.)  Plaintiff alleges he has sustained serious and lasting injuries, which could result in permanent disability, the nature and extent of which has yet to be determined.  (Complaint ¶64.)  Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that he will continue to incur medical expenses in the future, the amount of which is not presently ascertained.  (Complaint ¶64.)

False Arrest [against all Defendants] (7th COA)

“The crime of false imprisonment is defined by Penal Code section 236 as the ‘unlawful violation of the personal liberty of another.’ The tort is identically defined. As we recently formulated it, the tort consists of the ‘nonconsensual, intentional confinement of a person, without lawful privilege, for an appreciable length of time, however short.’ That length of time can be as brief as 15 minutes. Restraint may be effectuated by means of physical force, threat of force or of arrest, confinement by physical barriers, or by means of any other form of unreasonable duress.”  (Fermino v. Fedco, Inc. (1994) 7 Cal.4th 701, 716, internal citations omitted.)

“[T]he critical question as to causation in intentional torts is whether the actor’s conduct is a substantial factor in bringing about the type of harm which he intended from his original act.”  (Null v. City of Los Angeles (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 1528, 1536, n.6, internal citations omitted.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that on July 29, 2024, Defendants intentionally and unlawfully – without lawful privilege, and without any reasonable or probable cause, and without any justification – caused Plaintiff to be forcibly seized Plaintiff against his will and caused him to be arrested.  (Complaint ¶71.)  Plaintiff alleges the individual Defendants’ acts of false arrest against him were committed in the course and scope of their agency and employment with the entity Defendants, which are vicariously liable for the acts and omissions of their agents and employees.  (Complaint ¶71.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendant Matthew Lopez was at all relevant times acting as an agent of Miss USA as a security guard at the Biltmore Hotel in Los Angeles. Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendant Lopez was acting at the behest of and/or in the course and scope of his agency and/or employment with Defendants S&S, Miss USA, Loiczly, Sevi, RBE and Basile and with the authorization and permission of the Biltmore Hotel.  (Complaint ¶12.)

Plaintiff alleges that after he entered the Biltmore from its Grand Avenue entrance and as he crossed the lobby, he saw several people running toward him, and heard words to the effect of “that’s him!”  (Complaint ¶22.)  Plaintiff alleges he was confused and asked what was happening.  (Complaint ¶22.)  Plaintiff alleges Defendant Sevi said in a threatening manner words to the effect of “you’re going to see what’s going to happen to you!”  (Complaint ¶22.)

Plaintiff alleges that shortly afterward one of the pageant security guards, whom Plaintiff was told by pageant officials was Defendant Matthew Lopez, blocked Plaintiff’s path and told Plaintiff that he was not going anywhere. (Complaint ¶23.)  Plaintiff alleges that when he asked the guard to explain why and what was going on, the guard refused.  (Complaint ¶23.)  Plaintiff alleges that he was simply threatened by taunts indicating that he was going to be punished for some unknown reason.  (Complaint ¶23.)  Plaintiff alleges that as he attempted to pass, the guard grabbed him.  (Complaint ¶23.) 

Plaintiff alleges that the same security guard, who Plaintiff is informed and believes was Defendant Matthew Lopez and was employed by Defendant S&S, held Plaintiff’s hands and arms tightly and painfully and pushed Plaintiff toward where Defendant Lopez wanted him.  (Complaint ¶24.)  Plaintiff alleges he continued to ask what was happening, as he did not understand who these people were or what was happening to him.  (Complaint ¶24.)  Plaintiff alleges Biltmore Hotel staff observed Plaintiff being grabbed, seized, and knowingly allowed him to be forcefully detained.  (Complaint ¶24.)  Plaintiff alleges he began calling out to Biltmore Hotel personnel to please help him and that he was a hotel guest and to call the police.  (Complaint ¶24.) 

Plaintiff alleges was held against his will by non-Biltmore security personnel with the cooperation of the Biltmore Hotel, whose employees recognized him as a guest.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges that at one point, while he was being forcibly detained, a Biltmore employee approached him and asked him if he was a guest there, to which he replied yes.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges the employee obtained Plaintiff’s identification and again confirmed that he was a hotel guest, even though this was already known to other Biltmore employees. (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges even after the others present, including the pageant security guards and other agents of Miss Teen USA, heard Biltmore personnel confirm that Plaintiff was a hotel guest, they refused to let go of him.  (Complaint ¶25.)  Plaintiff alleges the hotel and its employees and agents assisted in Plaintiff’s detention, blocked Plaintiff’s path, surrounded him, and allowed him to be forcibly detained against his will and physically injured.  (Complaint ¶25.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendant Lopez physically held him for an extended period of time with such force that it hurt Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that while he was being physically held against his will, he was shown an image of a man on a cell phone and asked words to the effect of “is this you?”  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that the person on the cell phone photograph clearly was not Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that he was stunned and asked the people around him, including Defendants Sevi, Lopez, and several Biltmore employees, how they could think that this person was him.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants continued to cause Plaintiff to be physically restrained for several more minutes.  (Complaint ¶26.)  Plaintiff alleges that all the while, he was repeatedly pleading with Biltmore employees to help him, but they ignored his cries for help.  (Complaint ¶26.) 

Plaintiff alleges that during his detention, Defendants Sevi, Loiczly, Basile, and Lopez, and persons acting on Miss USA’s behalf never asked Plaintiff who he was, for his identification or other information, or gave him any explanation as to what was happening to him.  (Complaint ¶27.) 

Plaintiff alleges that long after they recognized Plaintiff as a guest and heard his pleas for help, Biltmore Hotel personnel finally informed Defendant Lopez and the other Defendants affiliated with the Miss Teen USA Pageant that Plaintiff was not the person they sought.  (Complaint ¶28.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendant Lopez still would not release Plaintiff, although Defendant Lopez was acting as their agent on behalf of S&S in providing security for the Miss Teen USA Pageant, Defendants Sevi, Loiczly, and Basile did not authorize, direct or ask Lopez to release Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶28.)

Plaintiff alleges that although he was still being detained, he managed to call his brother, who is a criminal defense lawyer in Los Angeles, and asked for his immediate help.  (Complaint ¶29.)  Plaintiff alleges his brother, who is also of Lebanese descent, headed to the Biltmore as soon as he received the call for help.  (Complaint ¶29.) 

Plaintiff alleges that shortly before Plaintiff’s brother arrived, the pageant Defendants finally let go of Plaintiff, but he was still not free to leave.  (Complaint ¶30.)  Plaintiff alleges that while he was attempting to address what had occurred, a person working for the Miss Teen USA Pageant chastised Plaintiff and falsely denied that Miss USA security had wrongfully detained him.  (Complaint ¶30.)  Plaintiff alleges that instead of owning up to their grievous misconduct and apologizing to Plaintiff, the Pageant Defendants covered up the facts and attempted to deflect blame for their own malfeasance. (Complaint ¶30.) 

Plaintiff fails to allege facts rather than legal conclusions regarding Defendant Lopez’s agency relationship with Pageant Defendants.

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion as to the 7th cause of action is granted with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Negligence- Negligent Hiring, Retention, Supervision, and/or Training [against all Defendants] (8th COA)

“The elements of a cause of action for negligence are well established. They are ‘(a) a legal duty to use due care; (b) a breach of such legal duty; [and] (c) the breach as the proximate or legal cause of the resulting injury.’”  (Ladd v. County of San Mateo (1996) 12 Cal.4th 913, 917.)

“To establish negligent supervision, a plaintiff must show that a person in a supervisorial position over the actor had prior knowledge of the actor’s propensity to do the bad act.”  (Z.V. v. County of Riverside (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 889, 902.)

Plaintiff alleges Defendants, and each of them, owed a duty of care towards Plaintiff in which they were required to act reasonably towards Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶76.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants, and each of them, breached their duty of care towards Plaintiff, resulting in Plaintiff’s damage.  (Complaint ¶76.)  

Plaintiff alleges that the individual Defendants’ negligent acts towards Plaintiff were committed in the course and scope of their agency and employment with the entity Defendants, which are vicariously liable for the acts and omissions of their agents and employees.  (Complaint ¶77.) 

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendants VVV Global Ent. d/b/a Miss USA, M & C Hotel Interests, Inc. d/b/a/ Millennium Biltmore Hotel Los Angeles, Renato Basile Entertainment, S&S Labor Force Inc,, Elyse Sevi, Laylah Loiczly, Jimmy Wu, Stuart Howe, Brenda Canales, Ron Basile and other named unknown supervisors negligently hired, retained, supervised, trained, assigned, and failed to discipline their employees and agents, including Defendant Matthew Lopez and DOES 1-40, inclusive, involved in the incident.  (Complaint ¶78.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants’ negligence was a direct and legal cause of injury to Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶78.)

Plaintiff alleges on information and belief that Defendants, and DOES 1-50, inclusive, and other supervisors knew, or in the exercise of reasonable care should have known that Plaintiff had done nothing wrong and that there was no basis to forcibly detain him yet nonetheless caused and allowed Plaintiff to be wrongfully detained and injured.  (Complaint ¶79.)

Plaintiff fails to allege facts rather than legal conclusions regarding Defendant Lopez’s agency relationship with Pageant Defendants.   Plaintiff fails to allege facts that Pageant Defendants were in a supervisorial position over Defendant Matthew Lopez and had prior knowledge of the Defendant Matthew Lopez’s propensity to do the bad act alleged.

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion as to the 8th cause of action is granted with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress [against all Defendants] (9th COA)

“A cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress exists when there is ‘(1) extreme and outrageous conduct by the defendant with the intention of causing, or reckless disregard of the probability of causing, emotional distress; (2) the plaintiff's suffering severe or extreme emotional distress; and (3) actual and proximate causation of the emotional distress by the defendant's outrageous conduct.’ A defendant’s conduct is ‘outrageous’ when it is so ‘extreme as to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized community.’ And the defendant's conduct must be ‘intended to inflict injury or engaged in with the realization that injury will result.’”  (Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, 1050-1051.)

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants engaged in outrageous conduct towards Plaintiff with the intention to cause, or with reckless disregard for the probability of causing, Plaintiff to suffer severe emotional distress, and with wanton and reckless disregard for the injurious result to Plaintiff.  (Complaint ¶82.)  Plaintiff alleges the individual Defendants’ unlawful acts against Plaintiff were committed in the course and scope of their agency and employment with the entity Defendants, which are vicariously liable for the acts and omissions of their agents and employees.  (Complaint ¶82.) 

Plaintiff alleges that as a direct and proximate cause of aforementioned despicable acts of Defendants, Plaintiff has suffered severe emotional distress.  (Complaint ¶83.)  Plaintiff alleges that Defendants’ acts were intentional and done with malicious and oppressive intent.  (Complaint ¶83.)

Plaintiff fails to allege facts rather than legal conclusions regarding Defendant Lopez’s agency relationship with Pageant Defendants.  Plaintiff’s allegation that Defendant Sevi said, “you’re going to see what’s going to happen to you!” (Complaint ¶22) does not exceed the bounds of civilized society and does not rise above “mere insults, indignities, threats, annoyances, petty oppressions, or other trivialities.”

Accordingly, Pageant Defendants’ motion as to the 9th cause of action is granted with 20 days leave to amend.

 

Conclusion

Pageant Defendants’ motion for judgment on the pleadings to Plaintiff’s Complaint is granted with 20 days leave to amend.
