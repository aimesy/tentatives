Case Number: 23BBCV01916    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: B

Hon. Victor Avila, Dept B 

MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL RESPONSES; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

Hearing Date: 07/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 23BBCV01916 / Albert Valle v. Eduard Barakyozyan, et al.

Moving Party: Plaintiff Albert Valle

Responding Party: Defendant Adrine Isayan

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: DENIED

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is a personal injury case. On May 15, 2023, Plaintiff Albert Valle (Plaintiff) allegedly suffered third-degree burns after he tripped on debris, miscellaneous items, and/or an exposed concrete slab at 9867 Milbur Dr., Sun Valley, CA 91352 (the Property) and hot tar spilled on him. (Angulo Decl., ¶ 4.) Defendant Adrine Isayan (Defendant) allegedly owned, leased, occupied, or controlled the Property. (Ibid.)

On August 18, 2023, Plaintiff filed his Complaint. On September 18, 2025, Plaintiff propounded Requests for Production of Documents, Set Four (RPDs) on Isayan. (Angulo Decl., ¶ 10.) After receiving two extensions, Isayan failed to respond to discovery requests. (Ibid.)

On February 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel verified responses to the RPDs. On July 10, 2026, the Court granted Isayan’s motion for relief from waiver of objections. On July 20, 2026, Isayan filed an opposition. No reply has been filed as of July 28, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom requests for production of documents are directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) The party also waives the right to make any objections, including one based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to production of documents other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.020, subd. (a), 2031.300.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300; Sinaiko, supra, 148 Cal.App.4th at 411.)

 

DISCUSSION           

Request for Production of Documents

Plaintiff contends that Defendant failed to timely respond to the RPDs on or by the statutory deadline. (Mot., p. 5.)

Here, Plaintiff’s counsel served Defendant with the RPDs by electronic service on September 18, 2025. (Angulo Decl., ¶ 10, Exh. A.) Responses were initially due on October 20, 2025; however, Plaintiff’s counsel granted two back-to-back extensions requested by Defendant bringing the new deadline to November 19, 2025. (Angulo Decl., ¶ 10.) As of February 9, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel had not received any responses, despite giving Defendant several extensions by request and as a professional courtesy. (Ibid.)

However, the Court’s July 10, 2026, order granted Defendant relief from waiver as to the RPDs and ordered the court to serve substantive responses. (See 7.10.26 Minute Order) On March 6, 2026, Defendant served Plaintiff with responses to the four sets of RPDs. (Zhou Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. B.)

Since the court subsequently granted relief due to mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect and Defendant has responded to the RPDs, the motion is DENIED as moot.

Sanctions

The Court shall impose sanction on the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to compel responses to interrogatories or RPD. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300(c), 2030.290(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a) [the court can award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party seeking to compel discovery even though no opposition was filed, the opposition was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed].)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

Since Plaintiff’s motion to compel is denied as moot, the request for monetary sanctions is also DENIED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff Albert Valle’s motion to compel initial responses to RPDs and request for sanctions is DENIED.

MOVING PARTY to provide notice of this order.

 


Case Number: 23BBCV02069    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: B

Motion for Reconsideration

Hearing Date: 7/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 23BBCV02069 / Tutkhalyan v. Los Angeles Metro. Transportation Authority, et al.

Moving Party: Defendant Los Angeles Metro. Transportation Authority

Responding Party: Plaintiff Karen Tutkhalyan

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: DENY

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This case arises from a January 9, 2023, incident in which plaintiff Karen Tutkhalyan (Plaintiff), a passenger on a Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority bus, alleges that the bus driver accelerated abruptly before Plaintiff had an opportunity to sit or stabilize himself, causing him to fall and suffer significant injuries. The complaint, filed September 8, 2023, alleges causes of action for negligence and strict liability against Defendant Los Angeles Metropolitan Transportation Authority (Defendant).

 

On June 29, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion requesting the Court reconsider its June 12, 2026, Order granting Plaintiff’s motion to compel the deposition of Defendant’s Person Most Qualified (PMQ). On July 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On July 24, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“When an application for an order has been made to a judge, or to a court, and refused in whole or in part, or granted, or granted conditionally, or on terms, any party affected by the order may, within 10 days after service upon the party of written notice of entry of the order and based upon new or different facts, circumstances, or law, make application to the same judge or court that made the order, to reconsider the matter and modify, amend, or revoke the prior order. The party making the application shall state by affidavit what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order or decisions were made, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1008.) 

The legislative intent was to restrict motions for reconsideration to circumstances where a party offers the court some fact or circumstance not previously considered, and some valid reason for not offering it earlier. (See Gilberd v. AC Transit (1995) 32 Cal.App.4th 1494, 1500.)

A party seeking reconsideration of a prior order based on “new or different facts, circumstances or law” must provide a satisfactory explanation for failing to present the information at the first hearing, i.e., a showing of reasonable diligence. (Garcia v. Hejmadi (1997) 58 Cal.App.4th 674, 690.)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Defendant moves the court to reconsider and modify the Court’s June 12, 2026, Order granting Plaintiff’s renewed motion to compel further responses of Defendant’s PMQ. (Mot., p. 2.) Defendant argues that the March 10, 2026, stipulation entered into by the parties extended subsequent discovery only to topics agreed to, these topics allegedly did not include producing PMQ in categories 5, 7, 9, or 19. (Mot., p. 7.) Defendant argues that these are the categories in which the Court ordered Defendant to produce their PMQ for further testimony on June 12, 2026. (Mot., p. 8.) Defendant argues that allowing Plaintiff to compel examination in these categories would effectively rewrite the parties’ discovery agreement. (Ibid.) Thus, Defendant asks the Court to revoke the June 12, 2026, Order as to categories 5, 7, 9, and 19. (Mot., p. 9.)

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the Court already litigated this question in the June 12, 2026, Order. (Opp., p. 2.) Plaintiff argues that Defendant attempts to present a new argument upon a closer reading of a document already on record and that the motion presents no new or different facts. (Ibid.) Further, Plaintiff argues that the Court already found that Defendant agreed to produce their PMQ for the categories now in dispute. (Mot., p. 4.) Lastly, Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s own conduct, in producing the PMQ pursuant to the June 12, 2026, Order, refutes their own motion. (Mot., p. 5.)

In reply, Defendant argues that section 1008, which governs reconsideration, governs the parties not the Court’s authority to revisit its own order. (Reply, p. 3.) Further, Defendant narrows the grounds for reconsideration to only Category 19. (Reply, p. 4.) Defendant also states that they have complied with the Order in dispute pending hearing, and that their compliance does not refute their motion. (Reply, p. 6.)

Here, the applicable standard requires that a motion to reconsider be “based upon new or different facts, circumstances, or law.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1008.) Further, the intent of the legislature was to “restrict motions for reconsideration to circumstances where a party offers the court some fact or circumstance not previously considered.” (Gilberd, supra, 32 Cal.App.4th at 1500.) Defendant brings the instant motion based on stipulations already considered in the June 12, 2026, Order. (See June 12, 2026, Minute Order, pp. 3-4.) Defendant presents no new facts or circumstances which would justify reconsideration. Thus, Defendant does not satisfy the requirements set forth under section 1008.  

            Accordingly, the motion is DENIED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant Los Angeles Metro. Transportation Authority’s motion for reconsideration is DENIED.

MOVING PARTY is to give notice.

MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

Hearing Date: 7/31/26¿ 

CASE NO./NAME: 24NNCV03125 / BriteCap Financial, LLC v. Grand Slam Trucking, LLC, et al.

Moving Party: Defendants’ Counsel

Responding Party: None

Notice: Sufficient¿ 

Ruling: GRANTED

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

BACKGROUND

Attorney Jeffrey P. Boykin (Counsel) represents defendants (1) Barry Evans, an individual; (2) Grand Slam Trucking, LLC; and (3) Evan Rippey, an individual (Defendants) in this breach of contract action. On May 4, 2026, Counsel filed motions to be relieved as counsel for each of these defendants. Counsel cites a breakdown in the attorney client relationship between Counsel and all Defendants.

No opposition has been filed as of July 23, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

The court may order that an attorney be changed or substituted at any time before or after judgment or final determination upon request by either client or attorney and after notice from one to the other. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court¿(1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Council Form MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a)), MC-052 (Declaration) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(c)), and MC-053 (Proposed Order) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e)). The proposed order must specify all hearing dates scheduled in the action or¿proceeding, including the date of trial, if known. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).)¿¿¿ 

Further, the requisite forms must be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).) The court may delay the effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) A motion to withdraw will not be granted where withdrawal would prejudice the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant¿(1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)

 

DISCUSSION

Counsel has filed the notice of motion to be relieved as counsel on form MC-051 regarding all Defendants. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a)), a declaration on form MC-052 regarding all Defendants (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(c)), and proposed order on form MC-053 regarding all Defendants. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) Counsel’s declarations and the proposed orders identify the upcoming hearing for the instant motion, but no further hearings in the action. (Boykin Decl., ¶ 5; Proposed Order, ¶ 7.)

            Counsel declares that there has been a breakdown in the attorney client relationship. (Boykin Decl., ¶ 2.) Counsel declares that, as a result, Defendants have been presented with substitution of attorney forms and repeatedly refused to sign them. (Ibid.)

            The¿Court finds that Counsel has set forth sufficient grounds to be relieved as counsel for Defendants. Also, the motion complies with the requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362. Given that trial is not set and Defendant has not opposed the motion, the Court finds that withdrawal at this stage of litigation will not prejudice Defendant. Defendant has not filed an opposition contending otherwise.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Attorney Jeffrey P. Boykin’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED.

Case Number: 24NNCV06180    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: B

Hon. Victor Avila, Dept B 

Motion to Amend Judgment

Hearing Date: 7/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 24NNCV06180/ Aerofund Financial, LLC v. Builders First Source LLC

Moving Party: Plaintiff Aerofund Financial, LLC

Responding Party: Special Appearing Proposed Debtor Builders FirstSource-Dallas, LLC

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: DENY without prejudice.  

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is a nonpayment action. On November 27, 2024, plaintiff Aerofund Financial (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against defendants Builders First Source, LLC (Defendant) and DOES 1-20 alleging nonpayment assigned invoices and accounts stated. On January 29, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed DOES 1-20 from the action. On February 21, 2025, judgment was entered in Plaintiff’s favor.

On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to amend the judgment and name Builders FirstSource-Dallas LLC (Builders-Dallas) as the alter ego of defendant Builders First Source, LLC (Defendant). (Mot., pp. 1-2.) On July 20, 2026, specially appearing party Builders-Dallas filed an opposition. On July 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

            Plaintiff requests judicial notice of Exhibit: (2) California entities search for “Builders First Source LLC”. The request is granted. (Evid. Code, § 452(d).)

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“Under Code of Civil Procedure section 187, ‘the trial court has jurisdiction to modify a judgment to add additional judgment debtors. [Citation.]’” (Wolf Metals Inc. v. Rand Pacific Sales Inc (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 698, 703 (Wolf Metals Inc.).) 

As a general rule, “a court may amend its judgment at any time so that the judgment will properly designate the real defendants.” (Favila v. Pasquarella (2021) 65 Cal.App.5th 934, 942.)  Judgments may be amended to add additional judgment debtors on the ground that a person or entity is the alter ego of the original judgment debtor.” (Ibid.) “Amendment of a judgment to add an alter ego ‘is an equitable procedure based on the theory that the court is not amending the judgment to add a new defendant but is merely inserting the correct name of the real defendant.” (Ibid.) In addition, even if all the formal elements necessary to establish alter ego liability are not present, an unnamed party may be included as a judgment debtor if “the equities overwhelmingly favor” the amendment and it is necessary to prevent an injustice.’” (Ibid.)

Additionally, “where the successor corporation is a mere continuation and hence liable for the acts of its predecessor … a motion pursuant to the procedural mechanism of section 187 enables the court to consider disregarding the corporate entity … in order to add an additional judgment debtor.” (McClellan v. Northridge Park Townhome Owners Ass'n (2001) 89 Cal.App.4th 746, 754-755 (McClellan).)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Alter-Ego

Alter ego exists where (1) unity of interest and ownership eliminates separateness and (2) an inequitable result would follow if the acts are treated as those of the corporation alone. (Mesler v. Bragg Mgmt. Co. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 290, 300; Sonora Diamond Corp. v. Superior Court (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 523, 538.) Factors include commingling of funds, centralized accounting, disregard of formalities, and one entity paying the debts of another. (Associated Vendors, Inc. v. Oakland Meat Co. (1962) 210 Cal.App.2d 825, 838-40.)

            Plaintiff argues that Builders-Dallas paid the invoices issued by Defendant and Builders-Dallas operates Defendant’s pseudo bank account by paying all its bills. (Mot., p. 3.) Plaintiff further argues that payment of one affiliate's invoices or liabilities by another affiliated entity is classic evidence of commingling and lack of corporate separateness, which directly supports the unity-of-interest requirement for alter-ego liability. (Mot., p. 4.) Further, Plaintiff argues that an inequitable result would follow if Builders -Dallas is allowed to avoid liability through corporate formalities despite operating as a single enterprise. (Ibid.)

            In opposition, Builders-Dallas argues that Plaintiff’s motion is not a simple name correction but instead seeks to impose judgment on a third-party who was not held liable on the underlying claim. (Opp., p. 1.) Further, Builder-Dallas argues that imposing the judgment on them would violate due process in that their rights were not represented in the underlying litigation. (Opp., p. 2.) Builders-Dallas argues that Plaintiff shows only that they footed some bills for Defendant, but does not provide evidence of common ownership, domination, misuse of the corporate form, or disregard for the separateness of the entities. (Ibid.) Builder-Dallas argues that most importantly Plaintiff has not established that the amendment would not violate due process. (Ibid.)

            In reply, Plaintiff argues that Builders-Dallas is not an unrelated third-party because they are the real entity behind Defendant. (Reply, p. 8.) Plaintiff argues that at the time the transaction at issue occurred, Defendant entity did not yet exist, but Builders-Dallas did exist as an entity and paid all the bills for Defendant’s operational name, Builders First-Source. (Ibid.) Plaintiff reiterates that Builders-Dallas is the operational bank account behind Defendant and paid every invoice at issue in the instant action. (Reply, pp. 5-6.) Plaintiff argues that adding Builders-Dallas as an additional judgment debtor simply names the real entity behind Defendant. (Reply, p. 9.)

            Here, Plaintiff provides a copy of an entity search for “builders first search” showing that the earliest initial filing date is August 25, 2024. (RJN, Exh. 2.) Moreover, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that when trying to obtain judgment funds, they discovered that Defendant does not have their own bank account. (Laney Decl., ¶ 3.) Attached to Plaintiff’s counsel’s declaration, not as an exhibit, is a copy of a business organization inquiry from the Texas secretary of state which identifies Builders-Dallas as existing as early as 2003. (Laney Decl., p. 3.) This is the only evidence presented by Plaintiff to establish that Builders-Dallas is the alter ego of Defendant. Plaintiff has not provided sufficient evidence to establish that Builders-Dallas is the alter-ego of Defendant. For example, the court in Pan Pacific Sash & Door Co. v. Greendale Park, Inc. found alter ego liability where the moving party provided evidence that the corporations in question both had the same stockholders, directors and officers, occupied the same premises as their offices, and had common employees; both were without substantial capital; each loaned money to the other; and each received the benefit of the other supporting a finding of true unity of interests. (Pan Pacific Sash & Door Co. v. Greendale Park, Inc. (1958) 166 Cal.App.2d 652, 655-60.) Here, Plaintiff has not provided such evidence. The Court cannot impose judgment on a debtor without sufficient evidence of alter-ego liability.

            Accordingly, the motion is DENIED, without prejudice.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff’s Motion to Amend Judgment is DENIED, without prejudice.  

MOVING PARTY is to give notice.


Case Number: 25NNCV01328    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: B

Hon. Victor Avila, Dept B 

Motion to Compel Mental Examination

Hearing Date: 7/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 25NNCV01328 / Vazquez v., Costco Wholesale Corp., et al.

Moving Party: Defendant Costco Wholesale Corporation

Responding Party: Plaintiff Elizabeth Vazquez

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: GRANT

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is a personal injury action. Plaintiff Elizabeth Vazquez (Plaintiff) alleges that on March 11, 2023, an employee of Costco Wholesale Corporation (Defendant), Anthony Vasquez, injured her at a Costco Warehouse in Monterey Park while she was sitting in an electric scooter. Employees Anthony Vasquez and Brian Rixner allegedly bumped into Plaintiff while loading a TV box into another customer’s vehicle. On February 28, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint against Defendant, Brian Rixner, Anthony Vasquez, and DOES 1-100.

            On June 29, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to compel Plaintiff to submit to a mental examination. On July 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On July 24, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

The Court has the authority to compel a mental examination of a party. Code of Civil Procedure section 2032.310(a) provides that any party seeking to obtain discovery by a mental examination “shall obtain leave of court.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.310(a).) A motion for a mental examination “shall specify the time, place, manner, conditions, scope, and nature of the examination, as well as the identity and the specialty, if any, of the person or persons who will perform the examination”. (Ibid.) The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under¿Section 2016.040.” (Id. § 2032.310(b).)  Any grant of a motion for a mental examination must be based on “good cause.” (Id. § 2032.320(a).)  Good cause requires “that the party produce specific facts justifying discovery and that the inquiry be relevant to the subject matter of the action or reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.” (Vinson v. Superior Court (1987) 43 Cal.3d 833, 840.)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Meet and Cofer

            Defendant’s counsel declares to have met and conferred with Plaintiff’s counsel on June 16 and 26, 2026 in an effort to arrange the mental examination at issue. (Damon Decl., ¶ 11.) Defendant’s counsel declares that despite these efforts, no agreement was reached. (Ibid.) Plaintiff does not dispute that a meet and confer took place.

            Accordingly, the parties have satisfied the meet and confer requirement.

Relevant to the Action and Good Cause Exists

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff has placed her mental condition in controversy by seeking recovery for emotional or psychological injury. (Mot., p. 5.) Defendant cites Vinson v. Superior Court which held that by asserting a causal link between mental condition and defendant’s conduct, the plaintiff had put her mental condition in controversy. (Ibid.) Moreover, Defendant argues that good cause exists for the mental examination because it is directly relevant to disputed issues. (Mot., p. 6.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff has made allegations surrounding her mental state which Defendant has no way to evaluate other than a mental examination. (Ibid.)

            In opposition, Plaintiff argues that she has not placed her mental condition in controversy and seeks only emotional distress damages that naturally accompany physical injuries. (Opp., p. 7.) Further, Plaintiff argues that Defendant has not shown good cause for a mental examination and has not explained why a less intrusive discovery method would be inadequate. (Opp., pp. 5, 7.) Plaintiff maintains that a less intrusive method, interrogatories, offers sufficient insight into her psychological and mental conditions. (Opp., p. 5.)

            In reply, Defendant argues that Plaintiff has placed her mental condition squarely in controversy through several discovery responses. (Reply, p. 2.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff has described her injuries through multiple interrogatories as ongoing “significant mental health symptoms” and described having anxiety, sleep disturbances, fear of being attacked, depression, irritability, and fear of public spaces. (Reply, p. 3, ¶¶ A-B.) Further, Defendant argues that Plaintiff purportedly incurred $7,910.50 in psychological therapy charges and claims to need ongoing treatment psychotropic medication as a result of her mental distress. (Reply, pp. 3-4, ¶¶ C-F.) Accordingly, Defendant argues that Plaintiff has placed her mental condition in controversy and good cause exists for seeking mental examination. (Reply, pp. 6.)

            Here, Plaintiff has claimed that Defendant has caused her to suffer mental and emotional ailments. (Damon Decl., Exh. A-B.) The Court in Vinson has held that when these allegations are made, a plaintiff has put their mental condition in controversy. (Vinson v. Superior Court (1987) 43 Cal.3d 833, 839.) Where a plaintiff alleges ongoing symptoms such as anxiety, sleeplessness, and fear, good cause exists for a mental examination to test the nature and extent of those very injuries. (Vinson, supra, 43 Cal.3d at 839–840.) Here, Plaintiff has made similar claims in interrogatory responses and depositions. (Damon Decl., Exh. A-B.) Further, in her Complaint, Plaintiff alleges that she has suffered “mental anguish” and “emotional distress” as a direct result of Defendant’s conduct. (Comp., ¶¶ 27-28.) Thus, Plaintiff has placed her mental state in controversy, and good cause exists for seeking mental examination.

Scope

            Defendant purports that the single mental examination will be performed by Gregg M. Baringoldz, Ph.D., a licensed psychologist, in Long Beach, California, on July 31, 2026, at 9:00am. (Mot., p. 7.)           Defendant purports that the examination will consist of a comprehensive personal history questionnaire, a clinical psychology interview, and standard psychological/diagnostic tests. (Ibid.) Further, Defendant purports that the examination will be limited to an inquiry into the mental and emotional conditions Plaintiff has placed in controversy. Thus, Defendant argues they provide the requisite specific information.

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that an eight-hour mental examination is unsafe, unduly burdensome, and unnecessary considering Plaintiff’s physical limitations and current mental conditions. (Opp., p. 6) Plaintiff argues that she is a wheelchair user with strict time restrained dieting and medication plans and as a result is unable to accommodate such a long examination window. (Opp., pp. 5-6.) Moreover, Plaintiff argues that Defendant has not provided a rationale for each standard psychological/diagnostic test requested. (Opp., p. 6.) In reply, Defendant does not directly address Plaintiff’s arguments regarding scope.

Here, a motion for a mental examination “shall specify the time, place, manner, conditions, scope, and nature of the examination, as well as the identity and the specialty, if any, of the person or persons who will perform the examination. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2032.310(a).) Defendant has identified the requisite information as well as the specific tests to be performed during the examination. (Damon Decl., ¶ 10.) Thus, Defendant has sufficiently identified the limited scope of the mental examination specific to the issues in controversy.

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant Costco Wholesale Corporation’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff to Submit to Mental Examination is GRANTED.

MOVING PARTY is to give notice and file a proposed order consistent with this ruling.


Case Number: 25NNCV02498    Hearing Date: July 31, 2026    Dept: B

MOTION TO BE RELIEVED AS COUNSEL

Hearing Date: 7/31/26¿

Moving Party: Defendants’ Counsel

Responding Party: None

Ruling: GRANTED

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

BACKGROUND

Attorney Jeffrey D. Nadel, Esq. (Counsel) represents defendants (1) Paramjit Kaur; and (2) Narinder Singh (Defendants) in this breach of contract action. On July 2, 2026, Counsel filed motions to be relieved as counsel for each of the defendants. Counsel cites a breakdown in the attorney client relationship between Counsel and Defendants.

No opposition has been filed as of July 23, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

The court may order that an attorney be changed or substituted at any time before or after judgment or final determination upon request by either client or attorney and after notice from one to the other. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) “The determination whether to grant or deny a motion to withdraw as counsel lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Manfredi & Levine v. Superior Court¿(1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1128, 1133.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

An application to be relieved as counsel must be made on Judicial Council Form MC-051 (Notice of Motion and Motion) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a)), MC-052 (Declaration) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(c)), and MC-053 (Proposed Order) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e)). The proposed order must specify all hearing dates scheduled in the action or¿proceeding, including the date of trial, if known. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).)¿¿¿ 

Further, the requisite forms must be served on the client and all other parties who have appeared in the case. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).) The court may delay the effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) A motion to withdraw will not be granted where withdrawal would prejudice the client. (Ramirez v. Sturdevant¿(1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)

 

DISCUSSION

Counsel has filed the notice of motion to be relieved as counsel on form MC-051 regarding both Defendants. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a)), a declaration on form MC-052 regarding both Defendants (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(c)), and proposed order on form MC-053 regarding both Defendants. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) Counsel’s declarations and the proposed orders identify the upcoming hearing in the action. (Nadel Decl., ¶ 5; Proposed Order, ¶ 7.)

            Counsel declares that there has been a breakdown of trust and communication in the attorney-client relationship and disagreement on strategy. (Nadel Decl., ¶ 2.) Counsel declares that elements of the written retainer agreement have not been honored. (Ibid.) Moreover, Counsel declares that, as a result, Defendants have been presented with substitution of attorney forms and efforts have not been successful. (Ibid.)

            The¿Court finds that Counsel has set forth sufficient grounds to be relieved as counsel for Defendants. Also, the motion complies with the requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362. Given that trial is not set and Defendant has not opposed the motion, the Court finds that withdrawal at this stage of litigation will not prejudice Defendant. Defendant has not filed an opposition contending otherwise.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Attorney Jeffrey D. Nadel, Esq.’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED.

MOVING PARTY is to give notice.

Motion to Tax Costs

Hearing Date: 7/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 25NNCV03845/ Narek Sargsian v. Volvo Car USA, LLC et al.

Moving Party: Defendant Volvo Car USA, LLC

Responding Party: Plaintiff Narek Sargsian

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: GRANT in part.

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is a lemon law action. On June 5, 2025, plaintiff Narek Sargsian (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against defendants Volvo Car USA, LLC (Defendant), Rusnak/Pasadena, and DOES 1 through 30, inclusive, alleging fraud, breach of written and implied warranty pursuant to the Madnuson-Moss Warranty Act, breach of written and implied warranty pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act, violations of Business & Professions Code, negligence, and strict liability. On November 4, 2025, the Parties resolved the claims and entered a settlement agreement (Agreement).

On April 22, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to strike or tax costs. On July 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On July 21, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 1032(b) provides that a prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs. Section 1033.5 itemizes costs that are recoverable. Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5(c) states, in relevant part: “Any award for costs shall be subject to the following: (1) Costs are allowable if incurred, whether or not paid. (2) Allowable costs shall be reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation rather than merely convenient or beneficial to its preparation. (3) Allowable costs shall be reasonable in amount. (4) Items not mentioned in this section . . . may be allowed . . . in the Court’s discretion.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032(b).)

            Code of Civil Procedure section 1032(a)(4) allows for other costs and expenses to be recoverable when expressly authorized by law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032(a)(4).)

            California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(1) requires that a motion to tax costs be filed within 15 days of service of the cost bill. However, the rule does clarify an exception under the Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6. (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 31700(b)(1).) Under that section, duty to “make any response within any period or on a date certain after the service of the document…shall be extended after service by electronic means by two court days”. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6(a)(3).) California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(2) provides, “[u]nless objection is made to the entire cost memorandum, the motion to strike or tax costs must refer to each item objected to by the same number and appear in the same order as the corresponding cost item claimed on the memorandum of costs and must state why the item is objectionable.” (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 31700(b)(2).)

            “If the items in a cost memorandum appear proper, the verified memorandum is prima facie evidence the expenses were necessarily incurred by the’ prevailing party. To controvert this evidence, the burden is on the objecting party to present evidence showing the contrary.” (Whatley-Miller v. Cooper (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 1103, 1115.) “[A] party’s ‘mere statements in the points and authorities accompanying its notice of motion to strike cost bill and the declaration of its counsel are insufficient to rebut the prima facie showing [that the costs were necessarily incurred].” (Jones v. Dumrichob (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 1258, 1266.)

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Defendant objects to the amounts Plaintiff claims regarding (1) filing and motion fees, (2) fees for electronic filing or service, (3) costs listed as “other”, and (4) expert fees. (Mot., p. 4.) In opposition, Plaintiff argues that filing of the Memorandum of Costs constitutes prima facie evidence of the reasonableness of expenses and costs incurred litigating the action. (Opp., p. 3.) Plaintiff argues that the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act authorizes recovery of costs and expenses in this action. (Opp., p. 4.) Further, Plaintiff argues that Defendant does not present any evidence or binding authority to overcome that showing. (Opp., 3.)

Filing and Motion Fees and Electronic Filing or Service Fees

            Plaintiff seeks $723.05 in filing and motion fees (Item 1). (Memo. of Costs, p. 1, ¶ 1.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff does not provide competent proof supporting the claimed filing and motion fees or a showing of necessity. (Mot., p. 5.) Further, Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s worksheet does not provide documentation for corresponding costs and includes improper costs not actually incurred. (Ibid.) Plaintiff seeks $22.50 in electronic filing or service fees (Item 14). (Memo. of Costs, p. 1, ¶ 14.) Defendant argues that Plaintiff provides no competent evidence demonstrating the statutory basis or need for these costs. (Mot., p. 6.)

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that each filing in Item 1 and 14 was mandated by statute, required by California rules of court, compelled by local practice, or expressly contemplated by the Agreement. (Opp., p. 5.) Plaintiff argues that each filing was necessary to pursue the action and filing fees are expressly recoverable under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5(a)(1). (Ibid.) Further, Plaintiff argues that each cost was incurred, and the anticipated Request for Dismissal (Worksheet, p. 4, ¶ 1.4) is required to be filed pursuant to the terms of the Agreement. (Opp., p. 6.) In reply, Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s reliance on the position that the filings were required is overbroad. (Reply, p. 4.) Further, Defendant argues that not all costs have been actually incurred. (Ibid.)

            The memorandum of costs is prima facie evidence of reasonableness regarding costs and expenses. (Whatley-Miller, supra, 212 Cal.App.4th at 1115.) Here, Defendant presents no evidence to the contrary. Defendant only challenges the costs sought by Plaintiff by alleging that they are unsupported. Nonetheless, in their opposition, Plaintiff provides invoices for all documents filed in this action. (Margarian Decl., Exh. A-H.) Moreover, the terms of the Agreement require that Plaintiff file a Request for Dismissal, so that filing fee will be incurred pursuant to its terms. (Margarian Decl., Exh. K, p. 3.) While Defendant has not overcome Plaintiff’s showing of reasonableness, as of July 27, 2026, the Court notes that Plaintiff has not filed a reply to their separate motion for attorney’s fees making that anticipated fee not actually incurred.

Thus, the Court will tax Item 1 fees by $23.67 to reflect $699.38 in filing and motion fees actually incurred. The Court will not tax Item 14 fees as Defendant has not overcome Plaintiff’s prima facie showing of reasonableness.

“Other” Costs

Plaintiff seeks $95.00 in “other” costs labeled as “AppearMe Service Fee”. (Memo. of Costs, p. 1, ¶ 16; Worksheet p. 3, ¶ 16.) Defendant argues that this is not an enumerated cost under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5 and that in order to recover this cost Plaintiff must satisfy a necessity and reasonableness test, which they do not. (Mot., p. 6.) Further, Defendant argues that Plaintiff does not provide competent documentation or explanation demonstrating reasonable necessity for this fee. (Ibid.)

            In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the “AppearMe Service Fee” was a necessary cost incurred to secure a required court appearance on a date when Plaintiff’s counsel was engaged in a simultaneous hearing for a different matter. (Opp., 8.) Further, Plaintiff argues that this cost is recoverable as a cost necessary to conduct litigation pursuant to section 1033.5(c)(4). (Ibid.) In reply, Defendant argues that this cost is not recoverable litigation cost and argues that a scheduling conflict should not be Volvo’s cost to reimburse. (Reply, p. 6.)

            Here, Defendant provides no substantive evidence to challenge Plaintiff’s prima facie showing of reasonableness. Defendant only argues that it is unreasonable. Defendant provides no evidence or case law to support their position. Nonetheless, in opposition, Plaintiff provides an invoice for the appearance fee cited as “other” costs. (Margarian Decl., Exh. J.)

Thus, Defendant has not overcome Plaintiff’s showing of reasonableness and these costs will not be taxed.

Expert Fees

            Plaintiff seeks $2,683.40 in expert witness fees and inspection fees for an individual identified as David Gurul. (Memo. of Costs, p. 1, ¶ 8; Worksheet p. 3, ¶ 8(b); Worksheet, p. 4, ¶ 8(b).) Defendant argues that Plaintiff does not identify the statutory mechanism authorizing recovery of expert fees for this action. (Mot., pp. 6-7.) Further, Defendant argues that Plaintiff does not provide an invoice or any other competent evidence to show that these costs were actually incurred, and reasonable, or were necessary. (Mot., p. 7.) Lastly, Defendant argues that the timing of this cost and the amount in proportion to other litigation costs is unreasonable. (Ibid.) 

            In opposition, Plaintiff argues that “[u]nder Cal. Civ. Code § 1794(d), a prevailing buyer is entitled to recover all “costs and expenses” reasonably incurred in connection with the prosecution of a Song-Beverly action.” (Opp., p. 7.) Plaintiff argues that [c]ourts have repeatedly held that this provision encompasses expert and inspection costs, even where such costs might not otherwise be recoverable under Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 1033.5”. (Ibid.) Plaintiff further argues that Defendant’s reliance on section 1033.5(b)(1) is misplaced because the Song-Beverly Act independently authorizes recovery for expert witnesses. (Ibid.) In reply, Defendant argues that alternatively, expert fees should be taxed because Plaintiff has not shown this amount was reasonably necessary. (Reply, p. 4.)

            Here, Plaintiff cites Jensen v. BMW of North America, Inc. which held that in enacting the Song-Beverly Act, the legislature chose to permit the prevailing buyer to recover both costs and expenses to broaden the scope of recovery. (Jensen v. BMW of North America, Inc. (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 112, 138.) Further, the Court in Jensen explicitly stated that “the Legislature amended section 1794 to provide for the recovery of “costs and expenses…[t]he legislative history indicates the Legislature exercised its power to permit the recovery of expert witness fees by prevailing buyers under the Act.” (Jensen, supra, 35 Cal.App.4th at 138.) Thus, Plaintiff has shown that expert fees are recoverable under the Song-Beverly Act.

In their reply, Defendant argues that the fees should be taxed because Plaintiff has not shown reasonableness; however, Plaintiff has made a prima facie showing of reasonableness in filing the memorandum of costs. Plaintiff’s counsel declares that at the time inspection was conducted, the expert witness was reasonably necessary to the litigation even though the matter ended up resolved prior to trial. (Margarian Decl., ¶ 16.) Plaintiff also provides the invoice of the expert witness reflecting reasonable hours expended and an itemized list of fees sought. (Margarian Decl., Exh. I.) Defendant does not provide evidence to argue that these fees were not reasonably necessary as Plaintiff alleges.

Thus, Defendant has not overcome Plaintiff’s showing of reasonableness and these costs will not be taxed.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant Volvo Car USA, LLC’s Motion to Strike/Tax Costs is GRANTED IN PART. Electronic filing fees are taxed in the amount of $23.67 only.

MOVING PARTY is to give notice.

MOTION TO DEEM REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION ADMITTED; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

Hearing Date: 07/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 25NNCV06250 / Jordyn Green-Brown v. Brady Trucking, Inc., et al.

Moving Party: Defendant Brady Trucking, Inc.

Responding Party: Plaintiff Jordyn Green-Brown

Notice: Sufficient 

Ruling: Continue

 

NOTICE:

The Court requests oral argument on the following issues: the legal and factual basis for imposing sanctions on Plaintiff’s former counsel, who was relieved prior to this hearing, and whether Plaintiff’s request for a continuance of the Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted should be granted considering her recent loss of counsel.

 

BACKGROUND

This is a personal injury case arising from a motor vehicle accident that occurred on November 3, 2023. On September 5, 2025, Plaintiff Jordyn Green-Brown (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against defendants Brady Trucking, Inc. (Defendant), John Doe, and DOES 1 to 50, inclusive alleging personal injury, property damage, and general negligence. On December 29, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

On June 3, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to compel requests for admission (RFAs) admitted. On July 10, 2026, the Court granted Vanessa Marie Fantasia’s motion to be relieved as Plaintiff’s counsel. On July 20, 2026, Plaintiff’s former counsel filed a response out of abundance of caution, due to having not yet received the signed July 10th order. On July 24, 2026, Defendant filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)  “If a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response…(a) [that party] waives any objection to the requests, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product…” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (a).) “The requesting party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction under Chapter 7.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) 

Sanctions are mandatory against the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to deem RFA as admitted. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280(c).) The Court shall impose sanction on the party, the person, or the attorney whose failure to serve a timely response to the request necessitated the motion to compel responses to interrogatories or RPD. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300(c), 2030.290(c); see also Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1348(a) [the court can award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party seeking to compel discovery even though no opposition was filed, the opposition was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed].)

“A request for a sanction shall, in the notice of motion, identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought, and specify the type of sanction sought. The notice of motion shall be supported by a memorandum of points and authorities and accompanied by a declaration setting forth facts supporting the amount of any monetary sanction sought.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.040.)

 

DISCUSSION           

Defendant contends that Plaintiff failed to timely respond to the RFAs on or by the statutory deadline. (Mot., p. 1.)

Here, Defendant’s counsel served Plaintiff with the RFAs by electronic service on December 29, 2025. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 2.) Defendant granted three extensions at the request of Plaintiff, bringing the due date to April 7, 2026. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 3.) On April 1, 2026, Plaintiff served Defendant responses to the RFAs without verifications. (Marquez Decl., ¶ 4.) On April 27, 2026, Defendant requested verifications from Plaintiff and provided additional time to provide the signed verifications. (Ibid.) Defendant argues that because Plaintiff has failed to serve verified responses, Plaintiff has not properly responded to the RFAs. (Mot., p. 2.)

Plaintiff’s former counsel does not substantively oppose the motion but instead requests continuance, in the interest of justice, due to the status of Plaintiff’s representation. (Resp., p. 2.) Further, Plaintiff’s former counsel argues that sanctions are moot because counsel has withdrawn from representation. (Resp., p. 3.) Alternatively, Plaintiff’s former counsel argues the discussion of sanctions should be deferred until Plaintiff retains new counsel. (Ibid.)

In reply, Defendant argues that the hearing should not be continued because Plaintiff should not benefit from her misconduct and failure to comply with discovery. (Reply, p. 3.) Further, Defendant argues that sanctions are not moot because the conduct of Plaintiff’s former counsel necessitated the instant motion. (Reply, p. 4.)

Since Plaintiff’s counsel has withdrawn from representation, and Plaintiff is not currently represented by counsel, the Court continues the instant hearing in the interests of justice.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant Brady Trucking, Inc.’s motion to deem RFAs admitted is CONTINUED.

MOVING PARTY to provide notice of this order.

CASE NO./NAME: 25NNCV07072 / Harvey Olen Quinn, by and through his successor in interest, Beverly Rodriguez vs. Valley Post-Acute and Rehab, LLC, et al.

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

BACKGROUND

This is an elder abuse case. Harvey Olen Quinn by and through his successor in interest, Beverly Rodriguez (Plaintiff) moves the Court for an order to compel Defendant Valley Post-Acute and Rehab, LLC (Valley Post-Acute) to serve responses to the Requests for Production of Documents, Set Three (“RPDs”). As of July 29, 2026, no opposition has been filed.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

A party must respond to requests for production within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom requests for production are directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).)

The party also waives the right to make any objections, including one based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to interrogatories other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2024.020, subd. (a), 2030.290.)

“The court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.030, subd. (a).) Misuse of the discovery process includes: “(d) failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.010, subd. (d).) Reasonable expenses under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (a) include

the time spent in researching and preparing the motion, as well as court time and travel time

spent in connection with the motion. (Ghanooniv. Super Shuttle (1993) 20 Cal.App.4th 256, 262.)

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

            Pursuant to July 10, 2026, Minute Order, the Court has already granted a motion filed by Plaintiff compelling Valley Post-Acute’s responses to RPD Set Three. This motion is a duplicative request to compel responses.

Accordingly, the motion is DENIED as moot and the request for sanctions is also DENIED.  

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Responses to RPD, Set Three is DENIED.

Motion to Bifurcate

Hearing Date: 7/31/26

CASE NO./NAME: 25NNCV08186 / Loyd v. Pasadena Highlands, et al.

Moving Party: Defendants Pasadena Highlands, 625 Investments, LLC dba Pasadena Highlands, and Wellquest Living, LLC

Responding Party: Plaintiff Sylvia Loyd

Notice: Sufficient

Ruling: GRANT in part.

 

NOTICE

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

Parties must appear on all other matters scheduled for this case.

BACKGROUND

This is an elder abuse action. On November 18, 2025, plaintiff Sylvia Loyd (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against Pasadena Highlands, 625 Investments, LLC dba Pasadena Highlands, and Wellquest Living, LLC, and DOES 1-50 (collectively Defendants) alleging causes of action for elder abuse and neglect, negligence, breach of contract, and willful misconduct. Trial is set for September 8, 2026.

On July 2, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion to bifurcate trial into three phases. On July 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. No reply has been filed as of July 28, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

Courts may order a separate trial of any cause of action or any separate issue in furtherance of convenience or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (b).)  The Court may also order the trial of an issue to precede the trial on another issue.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 598.)

Additionally, Evidence Code¿§320 provides that “[e]xcept¿as otherwise provided by law, the court in its discretion shall regulate the order of proof.” “Under these provisions, trial courts have broad discretion to determine the order of proof in the interests of judicial economy.” (Grappo v. Coventry Financial Corp. (1991) 235 Cal.App.3d 496, 504.)¿¿Further, “[t]he statutory provisions for severance and separate trials (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 598, 1048) are not limited to the separate trial of a ‘cause¿of action’ but also authorize a separate trial of any¿‘issue.’”¿ (American Motorists Insurance¿Co. v. Superior Court¿(1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 864, 872.)¿¿

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Defendant seeks an order bifurcating trial in the instant action as follows: (Phase 1) Alter-Ego; (Phase 2) Liability; (3) Punitive Damages. (Mot., p. 5.) Defendant seeks this order in the interest of justice and the judicial economy to efficiently manage the trial which they argue involves complex litigation. (Mot., p. 6.)

Plaintiff does not oppose the motion. (Opp., p. 2.) However Plaintiff argues that she does not assert an alter-ego theory in this case, as such Phase 1 proposed by Defendant is unnecessary. Plaintiff does not oppose the motion to bifurcate issues of liability and punitive damages provided the same jury hears both issues. (Opp., pp. 2-3.)

The Court has reviewed the Complaint and although the term “alter-ego” is used, Plaintiff does not assert liability against any party under an alter-ego theory. (Comp., ¶ 5 (“Plaintiff is informed and believes that WELLQUEST LIVING, LLC is a limited liability company…and at all times mentioned herein was the management company, operator, joint venture, partner, and/or alter-egos of PASADENA HIGHLANDS”).) As such, the issue of liability is alleged against and encompassing of all named Defendants. (Comp., ¶¶ 46-54.) Plaintiff cites Rivera v. Sassoon which states “the reason for requiring the same jury to try the liability and punitive damage phases of the case is that "[p]unitive damages are not simply recoverable in the abstract. They must be tied to oppression, fraud or malice in the conduct which gave rise to liability in the case.” (Rivera v. Sassoon (1995) 39 Cal.App.4th 1045, 1048.) Although the court in Rivera the court held that the implementation of two alternate jurors did not upset this requirement as set forth in Code for Civil Procedure section 3295, the court is persuaded by the position that the two issues are dependent on one another and should be evaluated by the same triers of fact.

Thus, the Court holds that Plaintiff’s position is reasonable and grants unopposed bifurcation as to Phase 2 and Phase 3 on the condition that the same jury evaluate both issues. Since Plaintiff does not assert liability based on an alter-ego theory, the court denies bifurcation as to Phase 1 because it is a non-issue.

            Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED in part.  

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Defendant’s Motion for Bifurcation is GRANTED in part.

MOVING PARTY is to give notice and submit a proposed order consistent with this ruling.

The Court is not requesting oral argument on this matter/motion.  The Court is guided by California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1308(a)(1) whereby notice of intent to appear is requested.  Unless the Court directs argument in the Tentative Ruling, no argument is requested and any party seeking argument should notify all other parties and the court by 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing of the party’s intention to argue as to this matter/motion.  The tentative ruling will become the ruling of the court if no argument is received.   Notice may be given either by email at [email protected] or by telephone at (818) 260-8422.

BACKGROUND

This is a personal property dispute action. On January 7, 2026, Plaintiff VW Credit Inc. dba VW Credit Leasing Ltd (Plaintiff) filed a Complaint against defendants GG Motorsport, Inc. (Motorsport); California Department of Motor Vehicles; and DOES 1-10 inclusive asserting causes of action for (1) claim and delivery of personal property; (2) conversion; (3) quiet title; and (4) declaratory relief. On May 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion seeking an order authorizing service of summons to Motorsport via electronic mail.

No opposition has been filed as of July 23, 2026.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

“If no provision is made in this chapter or other law for the service of summons, or if a plaintiff, despite exercising reasonable diligence, has been unable to effect service of the summons by any of the methods authorized under this chapter, the court in which the action is pending may, upon motion, direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice to the party to be served, including by electronic mail or other electronic technology, and that proof of such service be made as prescribed by the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(1).) This requires a plaintiff to “set forth facts detailing all attempts to serve the defendant by each of the methods prescribed by statute.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(2).)

 

DISCUSSION                                                                                              

Plaintiff argues that they have exercised reasonable diligence to attempt service through authorized methods but have not been able to personally serve Motorsport at its principal place of business or the residential address of its owner. (Mot., p. 2.) For this reason, Plaintiff moves the court to authorize service via electronic mail. (Ibid.)

First, Plaintiff attempted to serve Motorsport at its business address, but the premises was vacant. (Mot., p. 3.) Per an onsite manager, Plaintiff argues that Motorsport moved off the premises three years ago. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. D; Mot., p. 3.) Subsequently, Plaintiff attempted to serve Motorsport’s sole owner and agent at their residential address but was unsuccessful. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. E; Mot., p. 3.) Plaintiff seeks to serve Motorsport using a business email found on an invoice issued by Motorsport prior to the commencement of this action. (Mot., p. 4.) Plaintiff’s counsel declares that they have communicated with Motorsport’s sole owner via email before and received an immediate response. (Krugliak Decl., ¶ 9.) Thus, Plaintiff’s counsel declares that this is the most likely method for providing notice of the summons, complaint, and other related documents. (Ibid.)

Here, efforts to personally serve Motorsport have not been exhausted. (Krugliak Decl., Exh. E.) Although Motorsport’s principal place of business may be outdated, Plaintiff has obtained the residential address of its owner or agent and can attempt service at that location. Moreover, section 413.30 states that the Court can authorize alternative methods of service if a party “has been unable to effect service of the summons by any of the methods authorized under this chapter”. (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30, subd. (a)(1).) Plaintiff has not attempted service by mail, which is authorized under this chapter of the Code of Civil Procedure, and Plaintiff has attempted service at the residence of Motorsport’s sole owner only once.

Further, under section 413.30 the court is authorized “to direct that summons be served in a manner that is reasonably calculated to give actual notice”, this doesn’t necessarily mean electronic service. (Code Civ. Proc., § 413.30(a)(1).) Upon a showing of reasonable diligence, “the court may make an order that the service be made upon the corporation by delivering by hand to the Secretary of State, or to any person employed in the Secretary of State’s office in the capacity of assistant or deputy” when an agent cannot be served. (Corp. Code, § 1702 (a).)

However, as stated, Plaintiff has not exhausted efforts to personally serve Motorsport’s sole owner. Accordingly, the motion is DENIED.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

Plaintiff VW Credit Inc.’s Motion to Authorize Electronic Service is DENIED.  

MOVING PARTY is to give notice.
