Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Case Name: Bushey v. 534 East Foothill LLC, et al.

Case No.: 19STCV27951

Matter: Motion to Tax Costs

Moving Party: Defendants 534 East Foothill LLC, Hamid Hamedi and Lili Hamedi

Responding Party: Plaintiffs Dan Bret Bushey and Brianna Molden 

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is denied.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On March 30, 2026, Plaintiffs Dan Bret Bushey and Brianna Molden filed a memorandum of costs in the amount of $61,209.21.

On April 29, 2026, Defendants 534 East Foothill LLC, Hamid Hamedi and Lili Hamedi filed a Motion to Tax Costs.

A motion to tax costs must be filed within 15 days of the memorandum of costs or the costs will be entered on the judgment. (Cal Rules of Court, Rule 3.1700(b).)

Here, the Motion was filed well after the April 16, 2026, deadline to do so. Therefore, the Motion to Tax Costs is denied. 

Moving party to give notice.




Case Number: 25STCV24668    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: June 30, 2026

Case Name: Rosamond Ranch, LP v. Robison, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV24668

Matter: Motion for Leave to File FAC

Ruling: The Motion is granted.

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On August 21, 2025, Plaintiffs Rosamond Ranch, LP, Calitex, LLC, Elias Shokrian, and Derek Boisse filed the operative Complaint for (1) legal malpractice, (2) breach of contract, and (3) breach of fiduciary duty. 

Plaintiff now seeks leave to file a first amended complaint in order to (a) remove previously dismissed defendants; (b) clarify specific factual allegations regarding the entry of the default judgment in the underlying action; and (c) plead damages with greater specificity by identifying a claim for lost rent in the amount of $2,226,794.44, and aligning the prayer for relief among the remaining defendants.

The Court may, in the furtherance of justice, and upon any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 473, 576.)  In general, California courts liberally exercise discretion to permit amendment of pleadings in light of a strong policy favoring resolution of all disputes between parties in the same action.  (Nestle v. Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 939; Morgan v. Superior Court (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.)  “[T]here is a strong policy in favor of liberal allowance of amendments.”  (Mesler v. Bragg Management Co. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 290, 296.)  Pursuant to this policy, requests for leave to amend generally will be granted unless the party seeking to amend has been dilatory in bringing the proposed amendment before the Court, and the delay in seeking leave to amend will cause prejudice to the opposing party if leave to amend is granted.  (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490; Higgins v. Del Faro (1981) 123 Cal.App.3d 558, 564-565.)  The decision on a motion for leave is directed to the sound discretion of the trial court.  (See generally Weil & Brown, Cal. Practice Guide: Civil Procedure Before Trial (The Rutter Group 2014) ¶¶ 6:637 et seq.)

The proposed amendments do not alter the foundational facts or introduce entirely new, unexpected causes of action. The clarification of factual details and the specific itemization of damages stem directly from the core malpractice claim.

While Defendants argue that the timeline will be impermissibly squeezed if Plaintiffs' motion for trial preference is subsequently granted, that procedural argument is premature for the purposes of this motion. Standing alone, this case is relatively early in its procedural life cycle relative to the standard trial track. Because the underlying allegations remain consistent and the amendments merely specify damages and clean up the pleadings, denying leave to amend outright would run contrary to California's liberal amendment policy.

The arguments regarding compressed discovery schedules and statutory summary judgment deadlines are properly deferred to, and harmonized at, the upcoming hearing on Plaintiffs' Motion for Trial Preference on July 2, 2026. Accordingly, the motion for leave to amend is granted.

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 26STCV05329    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: June 30, 2026

Case Name: Luburic v. Ralph Lauren Retail, Inc., et al.

Case No.: 26STCV05329

Matter: Motion to Compel Arbitration

Ruling: The Motion is denied.

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is an employment matter.

Defendant Ralph Lauren Retail, Inc seeks to compel arbitration of this matter.

“[O]n petition of a party to an arbitration agreement alleging the existence of a written agreement to arbitrate a controversy and that a party to the agreement refuses to arbitrate that controversy, the court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1281.2; see also EFund Capital Partners v. Pless (2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 1311, 1320 [the language in section 1281.2 compelling arbitration is mandatory].)  The right to compel arbitration exists unless the court finds that the right has been waived by a party’s conduct, other grounds exist for revocation of the agreement, or where a pending court action arising out of the same transaction creates the possibility of conflicting rulings on a common issue of law or fact.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1281.2(a)-(c).)

“On a petition to compel arbitration, the trial court must first determine whether an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists. Because the existence of the agreement is a statutory prerequisite to granting the petition, the petitioner bears the burden of proving its existence by a preponderance of the evidence. The party seeking arbitration can meet its initial burden by attaching to the petition a copy of the arbitration agreement purporting to bear the respondent's signature.”  (Bannister v. Marinidence Opco, LLC (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 541.)  The party seeking to compel arbitration must also “plead and prove a prior demand for arbitration and a refusal to arbitrate under the agreement.”  (Mansouri v. Superior Court (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 633, 640-641.)

Plaintiff argues the agreement is unconscionable. 

“ ‘[U]nconscionability has both a “procedural” and a “substantive” element,’ the former focusing on ‘ “oppression” ’ or ‘ “surprise” ’ due to unequal bargaining power, the latter on ‘ “overly harsh” ’ or ‘ “one-sided” ’ results. [Citation.] ‘The prevailing view is that [procedural and substantive unconscionability] must both be present in order for a court to exercise its discretion to refuse to enforce a contract or clause under the doctrine of unconscionability.’ [Citation.] But they need not be present in the same degree.... [T]he more substantively oppressive the contract term, the less evidence of procedural unconscionability is required to come to the conclusion that the term is unenforceable, and vice versa.”  (Armendariz v. Found. Health Psychcare Servs., Inc. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 83, 114.)

Considering the agreement is an adhesion contract, the Court finds the following provisions unconscionable: (a) one-sided injunctive relief in court for Defendant; (b) unjustified requirement of “strict confidence” for all filings; (c) the requirement that alters PAGA standing: “you may pursue claims and seek remedies as a representative of the California Labor & Workforce Development Agency, but only based on alleged violations of law personally suffered by you”; and (d) the requirement that is clearly inimical to EFAA: “if you wish to bring other claims against the Company in addition to a Sex Harassment claim [ ], then all such claims, including the Sex Harassment claim, and, if applicable, the aforementioned retaliation claim, must be brought in arbitration pursuant to this Agreement.”

It is irrelevant that Plaintiff does not assert a sexual harassment claim. Indeed, “we must analyze the circumstances as they existed ‘at the time [the contract] was made’ to determine if gross unfairness was apparent at that time.”  (Am. Software, Inc. v. Ali (1996) 46 Cal.App.4th 1386, 1392.)

The agreement is entrenched and permeated with unconscionable terms that cannot and should not be severed.

Because the agreement is unconscionable and invalid, the Motion to Compel Arbitration is denied.

Moving party to give notice.
