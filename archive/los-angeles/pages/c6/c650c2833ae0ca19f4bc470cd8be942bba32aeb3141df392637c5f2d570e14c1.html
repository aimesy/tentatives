LEGAL STANDARD:

 

“ ‘The right to recover any of the costs of a civil action “is determined entirely by statute.” ’¿[Citation.]¿“ ‘[I]n the absence of an authorizing statute, no costs can be recovered by either¿party.” ’¿[Citation.] ‘Section 1032 governs the award of costs of trial court litigation.’ [Citation.]” (Charton v. Harkey¿(2016) 247 Cal.App.4th 730, 737.)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿ 

“Except as otherwise¿expressly¿provided by statute, a prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs in any action or proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (b).) (Emphasis added.) Code of Civil Procedure section 1032, subdivision (a) defines a “prevailing party” as “[1] the party with a net monetary recovery, [2] a defendant in whose favor dismissal is entered, [3] a defendant where neither plaintiff nor defendant obtains any relief, and [4] a defendant as against those plaintiffs who do not recover any relief against that defendant.”¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿ 

Costs are allowable if incurred,¿whether or not¿paid. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (c)(1).) Costs must also be “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation rather than merely convenient or beneficial to its preparation” and must be reasonable in amount. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (c)(2)-(3).)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

A prevailing party claiming costs must file and serve a memorandum of costs either (1) within 15 days after the date of service of a notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5, (2) 15 days after the service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal, or (3) within 180 days after entry of judgment, whichever is first. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700, subd. (a).)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿ 

Any motion to strike or tax costs must be served and filed 15 days after service of the memorandum, plus an¿additional¿5 days if served by mail or 2 days if served electronically. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700, subd. (b)(1).) “Unless objection is made to the entire cost memorandum, the motion to strike or tax costs must refer to each item objected to by the same number and appear in the same order as the corresponding cost item claimed on the memorandum of costs and must state why the item is objectionable.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700, subd. (b)(2).)¿ 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

  1. Prevailing Party¿ 

 

As provided by statute, a¿prevailing party¿“includes the party with a net monetary recovery, a defendant in whose favor a dismissal is entered, a defendant where neither plaintiff nor defendant obtains any relief, and a defendant as against those plaintiffs who do not recover any relief against that defendant.”¿(Chaparral Greens v. City of Chula Vista¿(1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 1132, 1152¿[citing CCP § 1032(a)).)¿¿ 

 

Here, the parties do not dispute that Intervenors are the prevailing party for purposes of this motion.

 

Accordingly, Intervenors are prevailing parties.

 

      ii.            Reasonable Costs¿ 

Item 2: Jury Fees

 

Moving Parties contend this is improper because the court determined the parties did not timely post jury fees. Otherwise, the requested amount exceeds the $150 posted amount by $11.39.

 

Intervenors argue the fees are expressly recoverable per statute and the amount includes the court’s electronic filing fee plus the jury fee deposit. They add that it does not matter that they waived jury trial to recover the fees.

 

Moving Parties reiterate their argument. They also argue that the court should reduce the amount by 50% to remove Huang’s share.

 

Jury fees are recoverable costs. (CCP § 1033.5(a)(1).)

 

The notice of posting jury fees was filed on May 17, 2021 on behalf of Huang and Intervenors. (May 17, 2021 Notice of Posting Jury Fees.) Moving Parties are correct that Huang cannot recover her fees. Their argument to reduce this fee by 50% is well taken. However, Intervenors sufficiently demonstrated that the full amount was $161.39 due to the deposit amount, filing fee, and convenience fee imposed by the electronic filing provider. (Declaration of Aaron McKwon (McKown Decl.), Exhibit C.)

 

Accordingly, the costs in Item 2 are taxed 50% to $80.70.

 

Item 5: Service of Process

 

Moving Parties contend this is unrelated to claims against 1804 Bannister but to abandoned claims against ABC Green Farms (a third party).

 

Intervenors argue that the process fees were to issue third party subpoenas ($256.25) and serve chambers copies of documents ($132.85). However, they concede the cost should be taxed by $298.65 due to lack of receipts.

 

Moving Parties reply that the court should tax the $132.85 associated with Huang but concede Intervenors can recover $265.25 for third-party subpoena service.

 

Service of process fees are recoverable. (CCP § 1033.5(a)(4)(B).)

 

Here, these costs are recoverable, in part. First, the court has never required courtesy copies delivered to chambers. Therefore, the $132.85 to delivery courtesy copies were not reasonable or necessary for the litigation. The court otherwise accepts the parties’ concession as to the remaining costs.

 

Accordingly, the court taxes $298.65 conceded by Intervenors and $132.85 in courtesy copy costs for a total of $431.50.

 

Item 11: Court Reporter Fees

 

Moving Parties contend this is not rooted to a hearing or otherwise explain how the fees are established by statute.

 

Intervenors argue they mistakenly included some court reporter fees in Item 15 instead of Item 11.

 

Moving Parties reply that Intervenors cannot recover these fees because the parties had agreed to share in these costs.

 

CCP § 1033.5(a)(11) allows for “[c]ourt reporter fees as established by statute.” That statute is Gov. Code § 68086. (Gov. Code § 68086(c).) If there is no official court reporter provided by the court, a party is free to obtain a private official court reporter.[1] (Gov. Code § 68086(d)(2).)  

 

Transcripts of court proceedings not ordered by the court are not recoverable. (CCP § 1033.5(b)(5).) 

 

a.       Agreement of Parties re Apportionment of Costs

When “the parties agree to share costs during litigation, the courts will enforce those agreements as written under” provided the written agreement is unambiguous and there is no provision to allow recovery of an apportioned fee to a prevailing party. (Anthony v. Li (2020) 47 Cal.App.5th 816, 824.) However, there is no evidence of a written agreement let alone an agreement that unambiguously indicates the prevailing party can or cannot recover their apportioned costs. Therefore, Anthony does not apply to this matter.

 

b.      Reasonableness

To start, there is significant confusion as to what costs Intervenors seek. They admit to typographic errors where they included some costs in Item 15 that belong here or in other categories. In sum, Intervenors seek to recover court reporter fees for trial reporting in the amount of $4,831.25. (Opp. 6:20-24.) This represents 9 days of trial (8 full days and one half-day) and one extra-time fee. (Ibid.) This is reasonable.

 

Therefore, the court declines to tax these costs.

 

Item 12: Interpreter Fees

 

Moving Parties contend there is no explanation supporting this request and, to the extent is exceeds an interpreter for Shen’s deposition, is not allowed.

 

Intervenors argue the interpreter fee includes a prorated fee for trial and the cost to interpret various trial exhibits.[2]

 

Moving Parties reply that these costs are not allowed by statute, are otherwise unidentified costs, and are unreasonable ($60 per page of translation).

 

The statute allows for recovery of “[c]ourt interpreter fees for a qualified court interpreter authorized by the court for an indigent person represented by a qualified legal services project, as defined in Section 6213 of the Business and Professions Code, or a pro bono attorney, as defined in Section 8030.4 of the Business and Professions Code.” (CCP § 1033.5(a)(12).)

 

Here, the interpreter fees are not authorized under this provision because no one was an indigent party or a pro bono attorney. However, the fees are recoverable pursuant the statute.

 

“Items not mentioned in this section and items assessed upon application may be allowed or denied in the court’s discretion.” (CCP § 1033.5(c)(4).) 

 

It is undisputed that an interpreter were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation due to witnesses and exhibits being in Mandarin.

 

Therefore, the court DENIES the motion to tax these items.

 

Item 15: Other

 

Moving Parties contend there is no evidence supporting the $31,571.44 in “other” costs or how they are recoverable under CCP § 1033.5.

 

Intervenors argue these costs include RealTime transcription used by the court during trial, Intervenor’s portion of the court report’s daily parking for trial, court-ordered mediation costs, hotel costs, conference room costs, parking, and meals for counsel who all reside out of Los Angeles County. However, they concede that the court should tax $2,875.45 of these costs.

 

Moving Parties reply that there is still no explanation for these costs, the parties agreed to share some of these costs (court reporter/RealTime transcript), agreement to share the mediation fee, and Invervenors’ decision to hire non-local counsel does not give him the right to recover their accommodation fees. Additionally, the accommodation fees are unreasonable because they did not need a conference room, valet parking, or wine, or for weekend hotel stays.

 

“Items not mentioned in this section and items assessed upon application may be allowed or denied in the court’s discretion.” (CCP § 1033.5(c)(4).) 

 

Here, the costs are reasonable in part. First, as noted above, there is no evidence of a written agreement expressly excluding apportioned costs from recovery. Second, the court rejects Moving Parties’ argument that counsel’s lodging during trial was not reasonable. However, the court agrees that Intervenors’ request for $44 in wine is unreasonable. (McKown Decl., Exhibit J.)

 

Therefore, the court taxes $44.00 from this category.

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS the motion to tax costs, in part, in the amount of $556.20.

1.      Motion to tax costs is GRANTED, in part, in the amount of $556.20.
