ANALYSIS:

           

            Patriot Bank sued Teresita Foster for breach of contract.  Foster answered.  The parties then settled and stipulated that Foster would pay $556.00 on January 30, 2025, make 22 monthly payments of $556.00 starting in February 2025, and to make a final payment of $557.00 in December 2026.  The Court dismissed this action without prejudice and retained jurisdiction to enforce the stipulation pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6.  Foster made payments up to August 2025, but has failed to pay since then.  Bank therefore moves to set aside the dismissal and enter judgment against Foster pursuant to the stipulation.  Foster has not opposed the motion.

 

            Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, subdivision (a), allows a court to enter judgment pursuant to the terms of a written, signed settlement.  If the parties agree, a court may dismiss the case without prejudice and retain jurisdiction to enforce the settlement until full performance is made. (Ibid.) 

 

            The stipulation between Bank and Foster is valid and enforceable under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6.  In the event of breach, the stipulation provides that Foster is liable for $25,672.65, plus $508.00 in costs, minus any payments Foster made before breaching.

 

            Bank requests a judgment of $21,732.65 against Foster, reflecting $25,672.65 plus $508.00 in court costs, less $4,448.00 for payments received from Foster.  That amount is supported by Bank’s motion, stipulation, and memorandum of costs.

 

            The Court therefore GRANTS Bank’s motion to set aside the dismissal entered on February 7, 2025 and enters judgment pursuant to the stipulation in the amount of $21,732.65 against Foster.

 

            Bank is to give notice.
