DEPARTMENT F43 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25CHCV02035    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 09-17-26

Case # 25CHCV02035, Patino, et al. v. Ford Motor Company, et al.

Trial Date: N/A

 

MOTION FOR ATTORNEY FEES AND COSTS

 

MOVING PARTIES:  Plaintiffs Alison Patino and Jeffrey Patino

RESPONDING PARTIES:  Defendants Ford Motor Company and AutoNation Ford Valencia

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order awarding plaintiffs $22,328.50 in attorney fees, 0.3 lodestar enhancement of $6,698.55, and $2,546.53 in costs incurred in litigating this matter.

 

RULING:  Motion is granted in the reduced amount of $24,439.03: $21,892.50 in attorney fees and $2,546.53 in costs.  The court denies the request for a lodestar multiplier.

 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On June 12, 2025, plaintiffs Alison Patino and Jeffrey Patino (Plaintiffs) filed this lemon law action, concerning issues with a new 2024 Ford Maverick vehicle.  Plaintiffs asserted action four causes of action under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act against defendants Ford Motor Company and AutoNation Ford Valencia.  Ford filed an answer on July 11, 2025, and AutoNation filed an answer on July 22, 2025.

 

On January 27, 2026, the parties settled the matter when Plaintiffs accepted Defendants’ Section 998 offer for a repurchase of Plaintiffs’ vehicle plus reasonable attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses pursuant to Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d).  Plaintiffs filed a notice of settlement on January 28, 2025.  (1/28/2026 Notice of Settlement.)  Plaintiffs subsequently filed motions to enforce the settlement agreement and for sanctions on May 26, 2026.  The motions were withdrawn on July 1, 2026, after Ford sent the settlement check on June 9, 2026.

 

On July 27, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion for $22,328.50 in attorney fees, a lodestar enhancement of $6,698.55, and $2,546.53 in costs.  Defendants filed an opposition on September 3, 2026, and Plaintiffs replied on September 10, 2026.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Awarding Attorney Fees

 

A prevailing party is entitled to recover its attorneys’ fees when authorized by contract, statute, or law.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10); Civ. Code, § 1717, subd. (a).)  “A successful party means a prevailing party, and [a party] may be considered prevailing parties for attorney’s fees purposes if they succeed on any significant issue in litigation which achieves some of the benefit the parties sought in bringing suit.”  (Bowman v. City of Berkeley (2005) 131 Cal.App.4th 173, 178.)  If a buyer prevails in a Song-Beverly action, then the buyer is allowed to recover attorney fees.  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).)

 

Because Plaintiffs are vehicle “buyers” and this matter settled their favor, Plaintiffs are the prevailing parties entitled to attorney fees pursuant to Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d).

 

1.  Hourly Rates

 

Plaintiffs request hourly rates of $350 to $525 for attorneys, $250 for paralegals, and $150 for legal assistants.  (Declaration of Jorge L. Acosta ¶¶ 6-7, 9-14 [Lemon Pros, LLP].)

 

Defendants oppose the attorney hourly rates, contending Plaintiffs present no admissible evidence of the prevailing rate for attorneys conducting non-contingent litigation of the same type on a lemon law matter in Southern California.  (Opp. at p. 4:12-25.)  According to Defendants, the only evidence is a copy of the United States Consumer Law Attorney Fee Survey Report from 2017-2018, which is hearsay, and cherry-picked rulings on fee motions.  Defendants also challenge the paralegal and legal assistant hourly rates and insists the court lower the rates to $150 and $100, respectively.  (Opp. at p. 5:10-16.)

 

Defendants present no evidence to support reducing the paralegal and legal assistant rates.

 

“The reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work.”  (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.)  The moving party bears the initial burden of establishing the prevailing market “through its own affidavits.”  (MBNA Am. Bank, N.A. v. Gorman (2006) 147 Cal.App.4th Supp. 1, 13.)  Once the moving party presents competent evidence of prevailing rates, the burden shifts to the opposing party.  The opposing party may present rulings which concern hourly rates for comparable work from the relevant legal market.  (Bronshteyn v. Dept. of Consumer Affairs (2025) 114 Cal.App.5th 537, 543, 546-547;  Baer v. Tedder (2025) 115 Cal.App.5th 1139, 1160-1162.)  The court must engage in an objective analysis “to determine the prevailing rate in the community for comparable professional legal services[.]”  (Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 156, citation omitted.)  The court may “consider the attorney’s skill as reflected in the quality of the work, as well as the attorney’s reputation and status.”  (MBNA Am. Bank, N.A., supra, 147 Cal.App.4th Supp. at p. 13.)

 

After reviewing the moving papers, the opposing papers, and the reply, the court finds that requested billing rates are reasonable, with the exception of attorney Christopher Urner’s requested $525 “contingency” rate.  However, because Mr. Urner is lead counsel in this matter and a partner at The Lemon Pros, LLP, the court finds the $525 hourly rate is reasonable according to what the court expects would be charged as a non-contingent rate for an attorney with Mr. Urner’s experience, qualifications, and title.

2.  Billing Entries

 

Plaintiff requests fees for 2.8 hours of paralegal work, 11.3 hours of legal assistant work, and 54.2 hours of attorney work, including 6.9 hours for the instant motion.

 

The Song-Beverly Consumer Act only entitles Plaintiffs to attorney fees for “actual time expended.”  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d).)  This means an analysis as to whether the fees and costs sought are reasonable under the circumstances is required.  (Levy v. Toyota Motor Sales, USA, Inc. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 807, 816.)  “A prevailing buyer has the burden of ‘showing that the fees incurred were ‘allowable,’ were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation and were ‘reasonable in amount.’”  (Hanna v. Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC (2019) 36 Cal.App.5th 493, 507, citations omitted.)  A verified fee bill is prima facie evidence that the services listed were necessarily incurred.  (Hadley v. Krepel (1985) 167 Cal.App.3d 677, 682.)  If a fee request is opposed, “[g]eneral arguments that fees claimed are excessive, duplicative, or unrelated do not suffice.” (Premier Med. Mgmt. Sys. v. Cal. Ins. Guarantee Ass’n (2008) 163 Cal.App.4th 550, 564.)  Once an objection to the fees has been raised, then plaintiff’s counsel bears the burden of showing that the fees were reasonably necessary, and the court has the discretion to reduce fees awarded.  (En Palm, LLC v Teitler Family Trust (2008) 162 Cal.App.4th 770, 775.)

 

In determining the reasonableness of fees, courts look to the factors from Church of Scientology v. Wollersheim (1996) 42 Cal.App.4th 628, disapproved on other grounds by Equilon Enters. v. Consumer Cause, Inc. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 53, 68 fn.5.  The factors from Wollersheim are (1) the amount of money involved in the litigation; (2) the nature of the litigation and its difficulty and the intricacies and importance of the litigation; (3) the skill required and employed in handling the litigation, the necessity for skilled legal training and ability in trying the case, and counsel’s education and experience in the particular type of work involved; (4) the attention given to the case; (5) the success of the attorneys efforts; and (6) the time consumed by the litigation.  (Id.)

 

Defendants ask the court to reduce the hours requested for clerical and administrative tasks, for written discovery served during a discovery stay imposed by Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(e), for filing a standard complaint, for filing frivolous motions to enforce the settlement and for sanctions, and for anticipated hours related to this motion.  (Opp. at pp. 6:8-7:11, 8:1-7 [requesting a 13.8-hour reduction].)

 

In response, Plaintiffs assert Ford fails to properly support its arguments by citing specific billing entries.  (Reply at pp. 2:13-4:14.)  According to Plaintiffs, the discovery was served because Plaintiffs filed their complaint before Section 871.26 went into effect.  Although Plaintiffs served the discovery after Section 871.26 went into effect, Plaintiffs did not know Defendants had opted-in until after the discovery was served and withdrew the discovery thereafter.  (Reply at pp. 6:4-7:10.)  Furthermore, Defendants’ own conduct, failing to provide any date to return the vehicle, caused the six-month delay that resulted in the motions for enforcement and sanctions.  (Reply at pp. 7:12-8:2.)  After receiving the requested information, Plaintiffs were able to serve the final paperwork and withdrew the motions.  Thus, all hours were reasonably incurred.

 

After reviewing the parties’ arguments, the supporting evidence, and the law concerning Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(e), the court reduces the requested fees for attorney Jorge L. Acosta by 0.1 hours, attorney Christopher Urner by 0.6 hours, and attorney Michael Saeedian by 1.3 hours.[1]  The court also reduces paralegal Celeste Luppino’s hours by 0.1 hours and legal assistant Daisey Hernandez’s hours by 3.3 hours.  Several billing entries include clerical tasks, and the hours billed for the discovery requests that were drafted on July 24, 2025, were not reasonably incurred.  The court understands that Plaintiffs believed filing the Complaint before July 1, 2025, meant this matter is beyond the scope of Section 871.26.  However, Plaintiffs’ own billing records demonstrate Ford gave Plaintiffs notice of opting in concerning Section 871.26 on July 14, 2025, and Plaintiffs admit the final “opt-in” list was available sometime in June of 2025.  As such, the court strikes 4.2 hours related to drafting the discovery on July 24, 2025.  The court also strikes some time entries because the time billed appears excessive based on attorney experience.

 

Finally, the court notes that Plaintiffs requests 1.4 hours of attorney work at a rate of $350 per hour for an individual named Alexandra Sloan.  (Urner Decl., Exh. A at p. 11.)  Because Plaintiffs provide no information about Ms. Sloan’s credentials, qualifications, and title, the court does not award any attorney fees for Ms. Sloan.

3.  Lodestar Enhancement

 

After determining an appropriate lodestar figure, the court may determine whether that figure should be adjusted with a positive or negative multiplier.  (Doppes v. Bentley Motors, Inc. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 967, 997.)  Whether a multiplier or negative multiple is appropriate depends on several factors, including (1) the risks presented by the litigation;  (2) the novelty and difficulty of the legal and factual issues involved;  (3) the results obtained on behalf of the plaintiff;  and (4) the skill exhibited by counsel.  (Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 556.)  “The ‘results obtained’ factor can properly be used to enhance a lodestar calculation where [1] an exceptional effort produced [2] an exceptional benefit.”  (Graham v. DaimlerChrysler Corp. (2005) 34 Cal.4th 553, 582.)

 

With the revised attorney fee amount, the 0.3x enhancement is $6,567.75.

 

Plaintiffs contend the court should apply the multiplier due to the delay in settlement payment, expertise in this matter resulting in excellent recovery for Plaintiffs, and counsel’s risk of taking this matter on contingency.  (Mot. at p. 14:2-12.)  In opposition, Defendants contend there was no novelty to Plaintiffs’ claims, no special skill was displayed, and there was no actual uncertainty of prevailing the merits because Ford offered to repurchase the vehicle at the outset of the case.  (Opp. at pp. 7:27-28, 8:1-7.)  Defendants suggest that the court consider adjusting the fee award downward and reducing counsel’s hourly rates.

 

The court finds Plaintiffs’ arguments do not support applying any multiplier because this matter was settled in less than one year and the matter presented no complex or novel legal issues.  Any delay caused by Defendants is included in the lodestar amount based on the emails between counsel concerning the surrender and counsel’s communications with Plaintiffs.

 

Therefore, the court denies Plaintiffs’ request to apply a multiplier.

 

 

            B.  Costs and Expenses

 

Plaintiffs seek $2,546.53 in costs and expenses incurred in litigating this matter.  (7/27/2026 Mem. of Costs.)  Defendants do not challenge this request.

 

“A prevailing party who claims costs must serve and file a memorandum of costs within 15 days after the date of service of the notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk[.]  The memorandum of costs must be verified by a statement of the party, attorney, or agent that to the best of his or her knowledge the items of cost are correct and were necessarily incurred in the case.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(a)(1).)  Under the law, the court presumes a verified memorandum of costs is correct.  However, a party may contest the costs that a prevailing party seeks.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1034 subd. (a).)  The challenging party has the burden of demonstrating that those costs are unreasonable or unnecessary.  (Adams v. Ford Motor Co. (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 1475, 1486.)

 

Because the request is unopposed, the court grants Plaintiffs’ request for $2,546.53 in costs.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Plaintiffs’ motion is granted in the reduced amount of $24,439.03: $21,892.50 in attorney fees and $2,546.53 in costs.  The court denies the request for a lodestar multiplier.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.

Case Number: 25CHCV02194    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 09-17-26

Case # 25CHCV02194, Tahmasian v. Costco Wholesale Membership, Inc., et al.

Trial Date: 10-30-28

 

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiff Arlet Tahmasian

RESPONDING PARTY:  Defendant Costco Wholesale Corporation, Inc.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order compelling defendant Costco to serve further verified responses to plaintiff second set of form interrogatories numbers 16.3-16.6 and 17.1 and imposing $1,460 in monetary sanctions against defendant and its counsel, jointly and severally.

 

RULING:  Motion is continued.  The parties are ordered to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference and to file a joint status report by a date to be set by the court.

 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On June 23, 2025, plaintiff Arlet Tahmasian (Plaintiff) filed this personal injury action against defendants Costco Wholesale Membership, Inc. (Costco), Trey Spivey, and Josh Trainor, asserting negligence and premises liability causes of action.  Defendant Spivey was dismissed on October 1, 2025.  Plaintiff alleges that on April 1, 2024, she was a customer at defendant Costco’s retail store in Pacoima, CA when she slipped and fell on a slippery substance on the floor and sustained bodily injuries.  According to Plaintiff, defendants’ employees negligently maintained the store and caused her to incur medical and related expenses and suffer property damages and loss of earnings.  Costco filed an answer on July 30, 2025.

 

On April 17, 2026, Plaintiff electronically served Costco with her second set of form interrogatories.  (Declaration of Arthur Mouradyan ¶ 3, Exh 1.)  Costco served responses to the form interrogatories and Plaintiff’s second set of request for admissions.  (Id. ¶ 4, Exhs. 2, 3.)  Plaintiff’s counsel sent defense counsel a “meet and confer” letter on June 5, 2026, requesting further responses to several form interrogatories.  (Id. ¶ 6, Exh. 4.)  Defense counsel responded on June 23, 2026, and served further verified responses on July 23, 2026.  (Id. ¶¶ 7-8, Exhs. 5, 6.)  No further responses have been served.  (Id. ¶ 9.)

 

On August 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel defendant Costco’s further verified responses to her second set of form interrogatory numbers 16.3, 16.4, 16.5, 16.6, and 17.1.  Costco filed an opposition on September 3, 2026, seeking sanctions and presenting a copy of unverified further responses that were served on September 3, 2026.  Plaintiff replied on September 10, 2026.

 

 

 

ANALYSIS and MEET AND CONFER

 

A motion to compel further responses to form interrogatories must include a declaration stating facts showing a “reasonable and good faith attempt” to resolve the issues mentioned in the motion before filing.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2016.040, 2030.300, subd. (b)(1).)  Section 2016.040 requires the meet and confer to occur by “in person, by telephone, or by videoconference.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a).)  In Department F43, meet and confer means in person or via phone, not by letter or email.  (Department F43 Courtroom Information, p. 2.)

 

The court has reviewed the moving papers and finds the parties have failed to adequately meet and confer according to Section 2016.040 and this court’s Information Bulletin.  At most, counsel exchanged two letters, with defense counsel stating its willingness to meet and confer by telephone.  (Mouradyan Decl., Exh. 5 at p. 5 [defense counsel’s June 23, 2026, letter].)  However, Plaintiff presents no facts indicating an attempt to contact or meet with defense counsel by phone, videoconference, or in person.  Furthermore, Plaintiff does not indicate any meet and confer attempts after Costco served its supplemental responses on July 23, 2026.

 

Therefore, the court continues the motion and orders the parties to meet and confer in person, by phone, or by video conference and to file a joint statement of remaining issues by a date to be set by the court.  The joint statement should briefly describe the matters in dispute, followed by Plaintiff’s arguments, then defendant Costco’s arguments.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

1.  The parties are ordered to conduct a meaningful meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference.

 

2.  The parties shall file a joint statement of the remaining issues as described above.  The format should be as follows: The parties should recite the specific discovery requests at issue, followed by the moving party’s statement of why it should be compelled, followed by the opposing party’s statement of why it should not be compelled.  To the extent that an argument is repeated for a subsequent request, the party shall simply refer to the section where the argument was previously made.

 

3. The deadline for the joint statement will be set at the hearing on this motion.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.
