Case Number: 25PSCV00141    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

(1)   DEFENDANT COVINA 023 – WOOD 206, LP’S MOTION TO STRIKE PORTIONS OF PLAINTIFFS’ THIRD AMENDED COMPLAINT is TBD/likely DENIED due to lack of facts re: ratification.

 

(2)   DEFENDANT COVINA 023 – WOOD 206, LP’S DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFFS’ THIRD AMENDED COMPLAINT is OVERRULED.

 

Background

 

This is a habitability case wherein the plaintiffs (various families) allege their units had some of the following nuisance, untenantable, and substandard conditions: cockroaches, electrical surge, low water pressure, mold, clogged bathroom sink, and deteriorated kitchen cabinets.

 

On January 14, 2025, Plaintiffs CLAUDIA PEÑA, an individual and as Guardian ad Litem for RUBEN PRADO, a minor child, AURORA PRADO, a minor child, and JOSIAH PRADO, a minor child; RICHARD PRADO, an individual; IDALIA JIMENEZ, an individual; ANDREW ALDERETE, an individual; DAVID ARNOLD, an individual; HELEN LEWIS, an individual; and BIANCA ARNOLD filed suit against Defendants COVINA 023 – WOOD 206, LP, a California limited partnership; PAMA IV PROPERTIES, INC., a California Corporation; REGENCY MANAGEMENT XLIX, INC. dba REGENCY MANAGEMENT INC. (collectively, “Defendants”) for:

 

1.     Breach Of Implied Warranty Of Habitability;

2.     Tortious Breach Of Implied Warranty Of Habitability;

3.     Negligence; And

4.     Private Nuisance

 

On March 26, 2025, Plaintiffs, additionally with Plaintiffs JESUS GARCIA an individual; and MANUEL RODRIGUEZ filed a first amended complaint (FAC) adding more COAs for the following seven:

 

1.     Breach Of Implied Warranty Of Habitability;

2.     Tortious Breach Of Implied Warranty Of Habitability;

3.     Negligence;

4.     Private Nuisance;

5.     Intentional Infliction Of Emotional Distress;

6.     Violation Of Business And Professions Code, Section 17200

7.     Unjust Enrichment

 

 

On May 19, 2025, Defendants filed their answer.

 

On June 20, 2025, a stipulation for leave to file a second amended complaint (SAC) was filed but rejected by the court clerk.

 

On November 7, 2025, a motion for leave to file a SAC was filed.

 

On February 9, 2026, the court granted the motion which additional tenants and additional units. More specifically, Plaintiffs seek to add twenty-eight (28) plaintiffs—bringing the total to 27 adults and 13 children and encompassing eight units—and adding an 8th COA for constructive eviction.

 

On February 18, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their second amended complaint (SAC) for:

 

1.     Breach Of Implied Warranty Of Habitability;

2.     Tortious Breach Of Implied Warranty Of Habitability;

On March 20, 2026, Defendant COVINA 023 – WOOD 206, LP (“Covina”) filed a demurrer which was sustained on 4/21/26 with leave to amend.

On July 9, 2026, DEFENDANTS PAMA IV PROPERTIES, INC., AND REGENCY MANAGEMENT XLIX, INC. dba REGENCY MANAGEMENT INC.'S filed a ‘NOTICE OF JOINDER IN DEFENDANT COVINA 023- WOOD 206, LP'S DEMURRER [AND MTS] TO PLAINTIFFS' THIRD AMENDED COMPLAINT.’

 

On July 28, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition.

 

On August 3, 2026, Covina filed a reply.

 

Discussion

 

Previously, the court sustained the demurrer with leave to amend solely on the grounds that of uncertainty i.e., that the SAC failed to plead which Defendant was responsible for what alleged conduct. Comparing the SAC to the 3AC, more is pled about the purported relationship between Defendants. The 3AC alleges that: “Covina 023 was and is the owner of the Property and, in connection therewith, authorized and ratified the tortious and other wrongful conduct described herein through one or more managing agents or employees”; that “Defendant Pama IV Properties, Inc. ("Pama IV Properties Inc.") …has directly supervised the day-to-day operations of Covina 023”; and that “Defendant Covina 023 employs Defendant Regency Management, as the management company for the Property and has authorized Defendant Regency Management to act on Defendant Covina 023’s behalf on all matters related to the Property, including the responsibility for management and maintenance of the Property including the complaints made by tenants and the uninhabitable conditions at the Property, but has failed and refused to remedy those uninhabitable conditions despite his ability and authority to do so.” (See generally 3AC pp. 3-7.) In short, the basis of allegations against the moving party Covina 023 is that it was and is the owner of the Property and, in connection therewith, authorized and ratified the tortious and other wrongful conduct described herein through one or more managing agents or employees (i.e., PAMA IV Properties Inc. and Regency Management).

 

With that, the defect previously identified has been cured. To the extent that in reply, Defendant attempts to raise the issue, that is improper and will be disregard.[1] (Reply p. 7, Header ‘C’ [“The TAC Alleges No Conduct by This Defendant.”].)

 

Now, in this demurrer, Defendant argues that the IIED fails because “Plaintiffs have pled zero conduct on the part of Defendants. Instead, Plaintiffs’ TAC makes general allegations of repeated notice to Defendants of various conditions and alleges that those conditions continued. Given that Plaintiffs must allege conduct so extreme as “to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized community,” Plaintiffs’ Complaint is fatally defective in its assertion of this cause of action.” (Demurrer pp. 6-7.)

 

The court disagrees, and Defendant’s reliance upon non-habitability cases are inapposite. For example, Defendant cites to Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, but that case someone making lewd comments and demanding sexual favors in return for working to approve release of funds from a trust. Defendant’s citation to Potter v. Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. (1993) 6 Cal.4th 965 to set the standard is equally unavailing as that case involved a toxic exposure case brought by four landowners living adjacent to a landfill who as a result of defendant Firestone's practice of disposing of its toxic wastes at the landfill were subjected to prolonged exposure to certain carcinogens.

 

The most cited case in habitability cases is Stoiber v. Honeychuck (1980) 101 Cal.App.3d 903, which is the case relied upon by Plaintiffs. While Stoiber may not present identical facts like Defendant is requiring, the purpose of that case is to show that IIED is a permissible COA in habitability cases. In Stoiber, regarding the condition of the subject premises, the plaintiffs alleged that “numerous defective and dangerous conditions were in existence, including, but not limited to leaking of sewage from the bathroom plumbing; defective and dangerous electrical wiring; structural weaknesses in the walls; deteriorated flooring; falling ceiling; leaking roof; dilapidated doors; broken windows; and other unsafe and dangerous conditions.” (Id. at p. 912.) Additionally, the county’s health department issued a notice to vacate and demolish the subject premises based upon the alleged conditions, a heavy cockroach infestation. (Ibid.) The court’s opinion regarding the IIED COA begins at page 921 and briefly goes through two cases—Newby v. Alto Rivera Apartments (1976) 60 Cal.App.3d 288 and Aweeka v. Bonds (1971) 20 Cal.App.3d 278—to hold that IIED permissible.[2] The court’s reasoning? Because “[w]hether this is so under the present allegations, presents a factual question it cannot be said as a matter of law that appellant has not stated a cause of action.” (Id. at p. 922.) Defendant, here, however, is asking for the court to make a factual determination.

 

And here, the facts are enough. Plaintiffs clearly allege the problems and the request for repairs and the timeline. For example, since 2018, unit 6 has been making complaints to managers about a cockroach infestation; since 2017, mold in the bathroom; and since 2024, cracks in the ceiling. (3AC pp. 7-8.) The same goes for the family in Unit 23 who since 2015 made complaints about mold; throughout the tenancy made complaints a leak; and since 2015 an inoperable heater. (3AC.) So, in short, for years there have been verbal and written complaints to managers about habitability concerns that management has repeatedly ignored. (3AC p. 2:11-13; 15:6-7; see also ¶ 73.)

 

To the extent that in reply defendant argues that the foregoing is a ‘legal conclusion,’ it is unclear how so. (Reply p. 5.) to the extent that Defendant argues “An unkept promise to repair is the ordinary currency of a habitability dispute,” the relevancy of the argument is unclear but perhaps to show Defendant was ignoring requests and that such conduct is “ordinary.” (Reply p. 5:26-28.)

 

All in all, Defendant has presented no instructive authority to suggest the allegations are insufficient for an IIED COA.

 

As for the motion to strike punitive damages, that request is TBD.

 

Defendant argues that “Allegations of delay, failure to repair, or inadequate maintenance, without more, do not establish malice, oppression, or fraud under Civil Code section 3294.” (Reply p. 8:8-10.) But Defendant conclusively states as much without citation to authority. To the contrary, as noted in opposition, a conscious disregard for the safety of others may constitute the malice required to sustain a claim for punitive damages. (Taylor v. Superior Court, 24 Cal.3d 890; G. D. Searle & Co. v. Superior Court, 49 Cal.App.3d 22.) "In order to justify an award of punitive damages on this basis, the plaintiff must establish that the defendant was aware of the probable dangerous consequences of his conduct, and he willfully and deliberately failed to avoid those consequences.” (Opp. p. 6, quoting Blegen v. Superior Court (1981) 125 Cal.App.3d 959, 963.) And while Defendant maintains that “the Opposition never does is identify a fact, a person, an act, a date, a statement, that would permit a trier of fact to find that this Defendant engaged in despicable conduct with a willful and conscious disregard of Plaintiffs’ rights,” it does. The Defendant treats the 3AC as if it merely states there were some problems and that the plaintiffs relayed said problems to management. But Plaintiffs plead more. Plaintiffs lay out when concerns were made about what and to whom. The court is unaware of authority that Plaintiffs must present essentially a log of the exact statements made to the name of the employee made and what time and date those statements were made. If anything, such facts are presumably within the knowledge of Defendant. 

 

To the extent that Defendant argues “TAC does not identify any such individual or allege specific facts describing conduct by a managing agent that would support punitive damages against a corporate defendant,” the motion does not meaningfully analyze ratification.

 

To the extent that Defendant argues “the TAC alleges defendant-specific conduct distinguishing Defendant from the other named entities in support of punitive damages,” as with above, the motion does not meaningfully address ratification.

 

Notwithstanding, the MTS likely will be granted because of insufficient facts regarding Civil Code Section 3294, Subdivision (b). The statute provides that where the defendant is an entity, punitive damages may not be awarded unless the oppression, fraud, or malice was on the part of an officer, director, or managing agent, or was authorized or ratified by one, or unless such a person had advance knowledge of the unfitness of an employee and employed that person with a conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others, that is true. In reply, Defendant argues that Plaintiffs did not address that argument, but in fairness, they could not because it was raised in the reply. That said, in fairness, the court will allow the parties to present an oral argument as to the foregoing and issue a ruling thereafter. During the hearing, Plaintiffs must present facts that would satisfy this statute.

 

Thus, while the court is inclined to deny the motion to strike punitive damages, it is TBD upon Civil Code Section 3294, Subdivision (b).

 

Finally, the issue of filing a fourth amended complaint remains. In opposition, Plaintiffs state that due to a clerical error, the Third Amended Complaint did not include all Plaintiffs to whom Defendants had previously stipulated. Indeed, as noted in Reply, it appears Plaintiffs are attempting to introduce new plaintiffs into the action via opposition e.g., “Castro Family” at Unit 14, the “Sanchez Family” at Unit 18, the “Lagunas Family” at Unit 26, and the “Martinez Family” at Unit 15. (Opp., pp. 2–4.) Yet, none of those individuals, families, or units appears anywhere in the TAC. Defendant does seem to oppose the request but that “any order be limited to the addition of the omitted Plaintiffs and expressly preserve Defendant’s right to respond to the amended pleading as authorized by statute.” (Reply p. 9.) The court finds Defendant’s reasonable and will inquire as to how the parties intend on proceeding.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer to the IIED COA is OVERRULED; MTS is likely to be denied.

 

 

 

 


[1] The demurrer is brief, and the arguments/analysis section go towards whether the IIED alleges extreme and outrageous conduct. The demurrer does not mention an “improper lumping” of defendants.

[2] In Aweeka, the property had major leaking problems and the backdoor needed repair; in seeming response to the plaintiff’s request to have the defects fixed or else they would deduct the cost of repairs from their rent, the landlord served a notice indicating the tenants’ rent would be increased. In finding an IIED COA permissible, the Aweeka court “stressed the significance of the relationship between the parties in determining whether liability should be imposed. The authorities indicate that landlords have been held liable for abuse of their position.” (Id. at pp. 281-282.) Equally here, there is a landlord-tenant relationship.

 


Case Number: 26STCV02001    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

DEFENDANTS’ PETITION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF TRACI BLACKMON- BHAGAT TO ARBITRATE AND TO STAY ACTION PENDING ARBITRATION is GRANTED; the case is STAYED such that the court need to address the other motions. The court requests Defendant file a proposed order.

 

Background

 

This is an employment case regarding various plaintiffs.

 

On January 20, 2026, Plaintiffs TRACI BLACKMON-BHAGAT; TAMRELLE COLLINS;  PALOMAR; and CHERYL WILSON filed suit against Defendants HASA, INC., a California corporation; CHRIS BRINK; GARY BARANCIK; ALMA BUNCH for:

 

1. VIOLATION OF UNRUH ACT (CIVIL CODE §51)

2. VIOLATION OF BANE ACT (CIVIL CODE § 52.1)

3. 4. GENDER VIOLENCE (CIVIL CODE§ 52.4) SEXUAL HARASSMENT (CIVIL CODE § 51.9)

5. SEXUAL ASSAULT

6. SEXUAL BATTERY (CIVIL CODE §1708.5)

7. UNFAIR BUSINESS PRACTICES (BUSINESS & PROFESSIONS CODE § 17200)

8. INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS

9. CONSTRUCTIVE FRAUD (CIVIL CODE §1573)

10. NEGLIGENCE

11. NEGLIGENCE PER SE - CONDUCT IN VIOLATION OF MANDATED REPORTING LAWS

12. NEGLIGENT HIRING, TRAINING, SUPERVISION, AND RETENTION;

13. NEGLIGENT FAILURE TO WARN, TRAIN OR EDUCATE

14. ASSAULT

15. BATTERY

16. PREMISES LIABILITY

17. INTENTIONAL MISREPRESENTATION

18. VICARIOUS LIABILITY

 

On June 4, 2026, the instant petition/motion was filed.

 

On June 25, 2026, an opposition was filed.

 

On August 4, 2026, a memorandum of points and authorities was filed.

 

On August 19, 2026, a reply was filed.

 

On August 26, 2026, the court held its hearing on the matter.

 

Discussion

 

As stated in the court’s posted ruling and during the hearing, the court continued the matter to afford Plaintiffs an opportunity to file a supplemental brief in light that the memorandum of points and authorities was filed after the notice of the motion was filed. To date, as of September 22, 2026, no such filing has been made by Plaintiffs, and Defendant also acknowledges that much. (See 9/21/26 Reply 2: 1-3 [“The Court continued the original August 26, 2026 hearing specifically to afford Plaintiff Traci Blackmon-Bhagat an opportunity to file a further opposition to Defendants’ Petition to compel arbitration. Plaintiff declined to do so.”].)

 

As indicated in the court’s 8/26/26 ruling, there was an already pending arbitration before AAA, Case No. 01-26-0003-3029 such that the court was uncertain as to what there is for this court to compel. (During the hearing, Plaintiffs’ counsel represented that there is no arbitration case; no supplemental brief, despite the opportunity, was filed to explain how so.) As further explained by Defendant, Emily R. Boyle was appointed as the arbitrator in Traci Blackmon-Bhagat v. Hasa, Inc., AAA Case No. 01-26- 0003-3029. [McLoughlin Decl. ¶ 5; Ex. 6]. In the AAA case, Plaintiff not asked Arbitrator Boyle to halt the arbitration, has not argued the AAA proceeding should not move forward, and has not raised any objection to the arbitrator’s jurisdiction or regarding the existence, scope, or validity of the Agreement. [McLoughlin Supp. Decl. ¶ 3]. Instead, as explained by Defendant, “Plaintiff and her counsel have refused to participate in the ongoing arbitration proceeding” hence why “Hasa seeks relief from the Court – an order staying this civil action in its entirety and an order directing Plaintiff to arbitrate her claims in this action in the pending AAA proceeding.” (Supp. Brief p. 2.)

 

The court does so. The only argument raised in the very brief opposition is that Plaintiffs, individually and collectively, are excluded from being compelled to participate in arbitration because the Complaint consists of claims of sexual assault, sexual battery, sexual discrimination, and sexual harassment, pursuant to the Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Harassment Act. However, as argued in reply and no supplemental brief was filed despite given the opportunity to do so, that argument fails for two reasons.

 

First, Section 402(a) is a provision of the Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”). It applies only to cases and arbitration motions controlled by the FAA. But Defendants do not move to compel arbitration under the FAA. Instead, Defendants seek enforcement of Plaintiff’s Agreement exclusively under the California Arbitration Act. Second, even if the FAA applied, Plaintiff Blackmon-Bhagat does not plead any sexual-harassment or sexual-assault, which is the threshold requirement to invoke section 402(a). Plaintiff’s individualized allegations concern regulatory complaints, retaliation, termination, and a nonsexual confrontation with Human Resources; the references to sexual harassment are directed at other co-workers and do not supply any facts alleging that Plaintiff Blackmon-Bhagat herself was subjected to any sexually harassing conduct.

 

To the extent that Plaintiff Blackmon-Bhagat may argue she was subject to sexual harassing conduct (because she actually doesn’t argue that), there is one sentence in her declaration at paragraph 3 that states the following: “In approximately February to March 2024, I, Gary Barancik (Director of EHS), Edgar Solorzano (EHS Manager), and Lori Ann (Quality Supervisor), were at the Saugus facility. After co-worker Lori Ann left the office, Gary Barancik stated, “Mr. Brink is going to have an oreo sex sandwich with you and Lori Ann.”

 

Defendant filed evidentiary objections to this declaration on 9/21 and the court sustains all. Even assuming that the court accepts Plaintiff’s out-of-court statements regarding the alleged conduct (i.e., exception to hearsay/statement of party opponent), Plaintiff has not explained, again despite the opportunity file supplemental briefing, how such allegations satisfy the severe or pervasive standard necessary to constitute actionable sexual harassment under California law. (See e.g., Lyle v. Warner Bros. Tel. Prod. (2006) 38 Cal. 4th 264, 282 [“For instance, we agree “ with the United States Supreme Court that, to prevail, an employee claiming harassment based upon a hostile work environment must demonstrate that the conduct complained of was severe enough or sufficiently pervasive to alter the conditions of employment and create a work environment that qualifies as hostile or abusive to employees because of their sex.”], italics original); Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, 1042-1043 [“Taken into account must be the surrounding circumstances, such as the frequency of the discriminatory conduct; its severity; whether it is physically threatening or humiliating, or a mere offensive utterance; and whether it unreasonably interferes with an employee's work performance. [] Thus, simple teasing, offhand comments, and isolated incidents (unless extremely serious) will not amount to discriminatory changes in employment conditions. Thus, simple teasing, offhand comments, and isolated incidents (unless extremely serious) will not amount to discriminatory changes' in employment conditions.”], internal citations omitted.)

 

Thus, the court rejects Plaintiff’s attempt to invoke section 402 of the FAAA.

 

Accordingly, the court grants Defendants’ petition, compels Plaintiff to submit her claims to the pending AAA arbitration and, as requested by Hasa throughout its moving papers, stays further proceedings in this action.

 

Conclusion

 

Based upon the foregoing, the motion is granted.
