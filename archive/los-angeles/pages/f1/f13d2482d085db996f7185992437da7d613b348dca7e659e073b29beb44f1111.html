DEPARTMENT S25 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

Imed Hsini v. Mohsen Ghariani, et al.
LASC Case No. 24LBCV00194
Hearing Date: September 11, 2024
Request for Default Judgment by Court
 
 
In preparation for the September 11, 2024 default judgment hearing, the court has identified several concerns with Plaintiff’s request: 
 

  1. Request for Entry of Default was entered on May 9, 2024 which is less than 30 days after service of the summons and complaint to Defendant Danielle Canissa and CDW Logistics, LLC. (C.C.P. §587);
 
  1. Plaintiff checked the wrong box and should have checked “Court Judgment” in the “Request For” section on the 1st page. (See 7/29/2024 Request for Entry of Default/Judgment);
 
  1. No DOEs dismissal filed;
 
  1. No attached/adequate and/or authenticated exhibits provided with Plaintiff’s Declaration. Exhibits should demonstrate, e.g. costs for title and insurance for two vehicles, power of attorney, agreement entered into with Defendants to purchase trucks and provide logistic and shipping services to clients;
 
  1. No contractual or statutory basis provided by Plaintiff as to recovery of attorney fees. Also, attorney fees requested is more than what is allowed in a contract for recovery of reasonable attorney fees under Local Rule of the Court, rule 3.214(a);  
 
  1. Prayer for complaint does not specify damages claimed;
 
  1. No notice of punitive damages as required under Code Civ. Proc., § 425.11, subd. (c).  Appears Plaintiff may be waiving punitive damages based upon proposed judgment;
 
  1. Proposed Judgment (UD-100) incorrect calculations as to attorney fees and prejudgment interest calculations.

Background 

On February 20, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants Saeid Mohtashami, DDS and Does 1 to 30, alleging a claim for dental malpractice.   

 

On June 24, 2024, Defendant Saeid Mohtashami, DDS filed an answer.  On August 27, 2024, Plaintiff filed a Doe Amendment, substituting Doe 1 with Philip Konopa, DDS.  On October 28, 2024, Defendant Philip Konopa, DDS filed an answer.  On September 16, 2025, the Court denied Defendant’s motion for summary judgment.  (September 16, 2025 Minute Order.)   

 

On May 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to be relieved as counsel.   

 

Legal Standard 

An attorney in an action¿may be changed¿at any time before or after judgment or final determination upon the order of the court,¿upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. (2).)¿ California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires: 

 

  1. Notice of motion and motion¿to be directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel Civil form (MC-051)); 

  1. A declaration¿stating in general terms, and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney client relationship, why a motion under CCP §284(2), is brought instead of filing a consent under CCP §284(1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel Civil form (MC-052)); 

  1. Service of the notice¿of motion and motion and declaration on¿all other parties¿who have appeared in the case; and 

  1. The¿proposed order¿relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney's Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel Civil form (MC-053)). 

 

The motion should be denied if withdrawal would¿prejudice the client.¿ (Rules Prof. Conduct, Rule 3-700(A)(2); Weil & Brown, Cal. Prac. Guide: Civ. Pro. Before Trial, 9:385.2 (2008);¿1 Witkin Cal. Pro., Attorneys §72 (2008).) ¿The motion should be denied if it will¿cause undue delay in the proceeding or cause injustice.  (Mandell v. Superior Court, (1977) 67 Cal.App.3d 1, 4.) ¿The determination whether to grant or deny an attorney’s motion to withdraw as counsel of record lies¿within the sound discretion¿of the trial court, having in mind whether such withdrawal might work an injustice in the handling of the case.¿ (Lempert v. Superior Court¿(2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 1161, 1173.)¿ A breakdown in the attorney-client relationship (or personality clash) is grounds for withdrawal.  (Estate of Falco¿(1987) 188 Cal.App.3d 1004, 1014.)  An attorney is not limited to withdrawing from a case for cause and may withdraw when withdrawal can be accomplished without undue prejudice to the client.  (Ramirez v. Sturdevant¿(1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.) 

 

Discussion 

Plaintiff counsel’s notice of motion and motion to be relieved as counsel is made on the proper Judicial Council of California form MC-051.  The notice of motion and motion complies with Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(a).   

 

Plaintiff’s counsel provided a declaration stating that: “[t]here has been a breakdown in the attorney client relationship in that counsel and client cannot agree on certain critical issues, such that it is impossible to continue with the representation of client.”  (Duke Decl., ¶ 2.)  The declaration is on the proper Judicial Council of California form MC-052 and complies with Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(c).   

 

As for service, Plaintiff’s counsel has provided proper proofs of service for the notice of motion and motion and the declaration to the client and Defendant’s counsel.  Service complies with Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).  

 

The proposed order has been prepared on the correct Judicial Council of California form MC-053 and has been lodged with the court with the moving paper.  The proposed order complies with Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).

Case Number: 24LBCV01737    Hearing Date: June 25, 2026    Dept: S25

Motion:  Plaintiff, Daisy Leah Rosas Garcia, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem, Fernando Rosas’ Petition to Approve Minor’s Compromise  

Moving Party:  Plaintiff, Daisy Leah Rosas Garcia, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem, Fernando Rosas (“Plaintiff”) 

Responding Party:  None 

 

Tentative Ruling 

 

The Court has reviewed the proposed settlement and finds that the settlement itself is fair and reasonable.  The minor claimant here has agreed to dismiss her claims; Defendants Magella Medical Group, Inc. will pay $130,000.00 and Long Beach Memorial Medical Center, Brittany File, M.D., Xiang Feng, M.D., and Kaneshiro, D.O., will pay $130,000.00; a total of $260,000.00 will be paid to minor claimant.  (Pet. ¶ 10.a, Attachment 13a [Hemesath Declaration] ¶ 6(f).)  The amount of attorney’s fees requested, representing 33 percent of the gross settlement to minor claimant, is $83,164.60.  (Pet. ¶ 13.a, Attachment 13a ¶ 24(d).)  The Claimant, through her guardian agreed to a 33 percent contingency fee arrangement; attorney fees are dictated under MICRA’s Business and Professions Code section 6146.  (Pet., Attachment 13a ¶¶ 22, 24, Attachment 17a [Retainer Agreement] ¶ 6.)    

The petition seeks to use $245.22 of the settlement proceeds to pay for medical expenses.  (Pet. ¶ 12, Attachment 12b(4)(c), Attachment 13a ¶ 27.)  The petition further seeks $7,986.07 for expenses (other than medical expenses) that have been incurred or advanced.  (Pet. ¶ 13.b, Attachment 13a ¶ 24(d), Attachment 13b.) Claimant will receive $168,604.11 after payment of all fees and expenses.  The Court finds that Plaintiff’s counsel has set sufficient facts breaking down and explaining the legal fees and costs incurred in litigating this matter.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.955(b).)   

 

Plaintiff, DAISY LEAH ROSAS GARCIA, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem, Fernando Rosas’ Petition to Approve Minor’s Compromise is GRANTED.
