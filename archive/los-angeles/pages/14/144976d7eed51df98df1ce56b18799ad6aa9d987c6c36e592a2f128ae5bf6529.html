Summary of Ruling:   The Petition is granted. 

Defendant has filed a Petition to Confirm an arbitration award dated February 17, 2026, along with an Amended Final Award of Costs dated March 27, 2026.  Defendant has complied with the requirements for seeking confirmation of an arbitration award, as noted below. 

Defendant has complied with Code of Civil Procedure section 1285, which requires that the petition name as respondents all parties to the arbitration and any other persons bound by the arbitration award.  Defendant has also complied with section 1285.4(a) of the Code of Civil Procedure, by attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement; section 1285(b), by stating the names of the arbitrator, Honorable Craig Kamansky, Ret. (See Petition at No. 6); and section 1285(c), by attaching a copy of the arbitration award (Attachment 8(¿)). 

Defendant served and filed the Petition to Confirm on April 3, 2026, not later than four years after the date of service of a signed copy of the award on the Plaintiff, as required by section 1288.  And Defendant waited at least 10 days after service of the signed copy of the award on Plaintiff before serving and filing the Petition to Confirm, in compliance with section 1288.4. 

The Court, upon reviewing the submitted Petition and concurrently filed documents, finds that the Petition meets the requirements of Code Civ. Proc. § 1280 et seq. and, there being no reason to correct or vacate the award, confirms the award as made. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 1286.) The Court intends to sign and file Defendant/Petitioner Citistaff Solutions Inc.'s proposed order. 


Case Number: 25STCV25867    Hearing Date: June 29, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 06/29/2026

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV25867

CASE NAME:                        ABLE INVESTMENT SERVICES, LLC vs CHRISTAIN COPELIN                                                                                            

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff Able Investment Services, LLC

OPPOSING PARTY:             Defendant Christian Copelin

PROCEEDING:                      Motion for Summary Judgment

MOVING PAPERS:               06/16/2026

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff Able Investment Services, LLC has established as a matter of law that Defendant is not in possession on the Property.  As against Defendant, Plaintiff is entitled to immediate possession of the Property.  Therefore summary adjudication is granted in favor of Plaintiff as to that issue.  The claim for damages is reserved for a subsequent trial. 

 

BackgrounD

 

On September 03, 2025, Plaintiff Able Investment Services, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed its Complaint against Defendant Christian Copelin (“Defendant”), alleging one cause of action for Unlawful Detainer.

 

Plaintiff alleges that a foreclosure was initiated on a loan by Plaintiff to SCLW Holdings, owned by and under the control of Avis Copelin (“Avis”)[1], secured by the real property located at 440 Troy Drive, Hollywood, CA 90068 (the “Property”). Plaintiff further alleges that on or about February 11, 2025, it became the owner of the Property following a non-judicial trustee sale of the Property (the “Foreclosure Sale”), in which Plaintiff was the successful bidder. Plaintiff contends that Defendant, the son of Avis, moved onto the Property on or about February 9, 2025 under Avis’ direction to frustrate the foreclosure of the Property and remains in possession of the Property. Plaintiff seeks possession of the Property and damages in the amount of $283.33 per diem for rent for each day from February 11, 2025, during which Defendants have been in possession of the Property until the date upon possession of the Property is delivered to Plaintiff.

 

On September 5, 2025, a Notice of Unlawful Detainer (Eviction) was given by the clerk to (1) Defendant, and (2) all occupants.

 

On September 29, 2025, Defendant filed his Answer, which he amended the same day.

 

On January 20, 2026, the Court issued an order setting the following dates: (1) Initial Status Conference scheduled for 01/29/27 at 8:30 AM in Department 37 (now Department 413) at Stanley Mosk Courthouse; (2) Final Status Conference scheduled for 03/30/27 at 08:30 AM in Department 37 (now Department 413) at Stanley Mosk Courthouse; and (3) Jury Trial scheduled for 04/06/27 at 10:00 AM in Department 37 at Stanley Mosk Courthouse. The Court noted that Plaintiff had not posted jury fees and ordered Plaintiff to post jury fees by January 29, 2026 or waive jury.

 

On June 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed (1) an Ex Parte Application seeking an order to advance its hearing on its Motion for Summary Judgment which was set for August 21, 2026, and (2) its Motion for Summary Judgment.

 

On June 17, 2026, Plaintiff’s Ex Parte Application was granted and the hearing on the Motion for Summary Judgment was advanced to June 29, 2026 at 08:30 AM in Department 413 (formerly Department 37) at Stanley Mosk Courthouse.

 

Copelin filed an Opposition on June 26, 2026. 

 

LEGAL STANDARD – SuMMary judgment (Unlawful Detainer)

 

“The procedures governing a motion for summary judgment in an unlawful detainer action are streamlined (e.g., separate statements are not required under Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (s), but such a motion ‘shall be granted or denied on the same basis as a motion under § 437c, Code Civ. Proc., § 1170.7. Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1351’ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1170.7). (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1351.)” (Borden v. Stiles (2023) 92 Cal.App.5th 337, 344-45.)

 

The function of a motion for summary judgment or adjudication is to allow a determination as to whether an opposing party cannot show evidentiary support for a pleading or claim and to enable an order of summary dismissal without the need for trial. (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co.¿(2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843.) Code Civ. Proc.¿§437c(c) “requires the trial judge to grant summary judgment if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to¿any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”¿ (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp.¿(1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1110, 1119.)¿ “The function of the pleadings in a motion for summary judgment is to delimit the scope of the issues; the function of the affidavits or declarations is to disclose whether there is any triable issue of fact within the issues delimited by the pleadings.”¿ (Juge¿v. County of Sacramento¿(1993) 12 Cal.App.4th 59, 67 [citing¿FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima¿(1991) 231 Cal. App. 3d 367, 381-382].)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿ 

As to each claim as framed by the complaint, the party moving for summary judgment must satisfy the¿initial¿burden of proof by presenting facts to negate an essential element, or to¿establish¿a defense. (Code Civ.¿Proc.¿§437c(p)(2);¿Scalf¿v. D. B. Log Homes, Inc.¿(2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1510, 1520.) Courts “liberally construe the evidence in support of the party opposing summary judgment and resolve doubts concerning the evidence in favor of that party.” (Dore v. Arnold Worldwide, Inc.¿(2006) 39 Cal.4th 384, 389.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿ 

Once the defendant has met that burden, the burden shifts to the opposing party to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action or a defense thereto. To¿establish¿a triable issue of material fact, the party opposing the motion must produce substantial responsive evidence. (Sangster v.¿Paetkau¿(1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 151, 166.)¿

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff provides evidence that Defendant has denied that he was a tenant or in possession of the Property. (See Pl.’s UMF No. 15; Decl. Snyder ¶ 5; Exhs. L-M.) Plaintiff further provides that (1) a California statutory Prejudgment Claim of Right to Possession was served on the occupants of the Property on March 26, 2026, and again on April 17, 2026, in accordance with Code Civ. Proc. §§ 415.46 and 1174.3, and (2) the Prejudgment Claim of Right to Possession has not been returned or filed with the Court; and (3) because of this, any third party is now cut off from asserting any right to possession in this proceeding. (See Pl.’s UMF No. 16; Decl. Snyder ¶ 5; Exhs. N-O.)  In the Opposition, Copelin unequivocally acknowledges that he is not and have never been in possession or an occupant of or tenant on the Property.  That fact is undisputed. 

 

“The basic elements of unlawful detainer for nonpayment of rent … are (1) the tenant is in possession of the premises; (2) that possession is without permission; (3) the tenant is in default for nonpayment of rent; (4) the tenant has been properly served with a written three-day notice; and (5) the default continues after the three-day notice period has elapsed.” (KB Salt Lake III, LLC v. Fitness Internat., LLC (2023) 95 Cal.App.5th 1032, 1045.)

 

Plaintiff has further provided the recorded documents regarding ownership (see Requests for Judicial Notice), and documents regarding the Prejudgment Claim of Right to Possession (see Exhs. Exhs. N-O.) The Prejudgment Claim of Right to Possession form allows an occupant, not a party to the lawsuit, to seek to be inserted into the lawsuit if it is filed within ten days of its service. (Code Civ. Proc. § 1174.25.)

 

Here, Plaintiff has met its initial burden by negating an essential element of Unlawful Detainer (i.e. possession). The burden shifts to Defendant.

 

Defendant has not disputed the fact that he is not in possession of the Property.  He argues that, as a result an element of the unlawful detainer – that defendant is in possession of the Property – has not been established.  However, the Court is not precluded from making the finding that Plaintiff has the right to possession of the Property as against Copelin. 

 

The Opposition makes other arguments regarding disputes over Plaintiff’s title to the Property, failure to attach required documents to the Complaint, and other arguments that the Opposition asserts relate to protections afforded to occupants or residential property following foreclosure, none of those arguments apply to Defendant, because he it is established as a matter of law that he is not an occupant of the Property. 

 

Plaintiff has provided the legal standard for the Court to rule on possession without ruling on the amount of damages. (See Mtn. at 6:16-7:20 [citing Northrop Corp. v. Chaparral Energy, Inc. (1985) 168 Cal.App.3d 725; Coyne v. De Leo (2018) 26 Cal.App.5th 801; and Hiona v. Superior Ct. (2020) 48 Cal. App. 5th 866.) The Court declines to rule on the issue of damages as the evidence presented shows: (1) that Defendant was not in possession of the Property, and (2) the loan was secured by Avis, not Defendant.

 

Conclusion¿

 

Plaintiff Able Investment Services, LLC has established as a matter of law that Defendant is not in possession on the Property.  As against Defendant, Plaintiff is entitled to immediate possession of the Property.  Therefore summary adjudication is granted in favor of Plaintiff as to that issue.  The claim for damages is reserved for a subsequent trial.
