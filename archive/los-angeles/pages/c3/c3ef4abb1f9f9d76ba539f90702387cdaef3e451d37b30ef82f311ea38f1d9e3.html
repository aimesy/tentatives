DEMURRER

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants, Alpine F&B Corporation, Pocono Foods Corp., and Jay Jeong Park

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Kimnori USA, Inc.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

On or about March 17, 2023, Plaintiff Kimnori USA, Inc. entered into an “OEM Manufacturing Agreement” with Defendant Alpine F&B Corporation for the production of seaweed food products. A month later, Alpine’s President, Defendant Jay Jeong Park, informed Plaintiff’s officers that he also owned and controlled Defendant Pocono Foods Corp. Plaintiff alleges Park represented that Pocono would assume the same terms of the OEM contract for any goods ordered and delivered to Pocono. Park further guaranteed the obligations of both Alpine and Pocono.

 

Plaintiff claims that despite manufacturing and delivering its products to Alpine and Pocono, Defendants have refused to pay, resulting in an outstanding sum of $93,564. On July 31, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint for: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of Oral Contract; (3) Breach of Guarantee; (4) Fraud; and (5) Quantum Meruit.

 

RULING: Overruled.

Defendants demur to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint, arguing that Plaintiff fails to sufficiently state its causes of action and that its claims are uncertain. They further contend that the second through fifth causes of action suffer from a misjoinder of parties. Plaintiff opposes the demurrer, maintaining the sufficiency of its allegations and denying any uncertainty or misjoinder of parties. Defendants’ demurrer is overruled.

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading “by raising questions of law.” (Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.) “In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.) The court “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .” (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.) In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated. (Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) “If the complaint states a cause of action under any theory, regardless of the title under which the factual basis for relief is stated, that aspect of the complaint is good against a demurrer.” (Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title Guaranty Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26, 38.)

 

“A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly’s of Cal., Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616; Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, fn. 2 [“[U]nder our liberal pleading rules, where the complaint contains substantive factual allegations sufficiently apprising defendant of the issues it is being asked to meet, a demurrer for uncertainty should be overruled or plaintiff given leave to amend”].)

 

First Cause of Action: Breach of Contract

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s breach of contract cause of action fails because it does not allege when it delivered the goods to Alpine and when Alpine breached the contract. They further contend that Plaintiff’s damages claim is uncertain because it seeks the same amount of damages against all Defendants, despite the alleged existence of separate contracts.

 

The elements of a breach of contract cause of action are: “(1) existence of the contract; (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance; (3) defendant’s breach; and (4) damages to plaintiff as a result of the breach.” (Miles v. Deutsche Bank Nat. Trust Co. (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 394, 402.)

 

Plaintiff sufficiently alleges performance, breach, and damages. Defendants’ attack on the absence of allegations regarding time of performance and breach apparently rests on a statute of limitations argument. But this is immaterial because any breach must have occurred after the contract was formed in March 2023 and Plaintiff filed its action in 2024, within the 4-year limitations period for written contracts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 337, subd. (a).) Plaintiff’s damages allegations are also sufficient because it seeks the outstanding balance due on the contracts against all Defendants based on alter-ego and single enterprise theories. (Leek v. Cooper (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 399, 411; Tran v. Farmers Group, Inc. (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 1202, 1219.) Plaintiff alleges Alpine and Pocono are “affiliated companies” controlled by Park and that Park “used these entities interchangeably or as shells to avoid liability and to commit fraud.” [FAC ¶¶ 10, 25, 42.] Defendants’ demurrer to this claim is thus overruled.     

 

Second Cause of Action: Breach of Oral Contract

Defendants demur to the breach of oral contract cause of action based on the sham pleading doctrine. This doctrine applies where “a party files an amended complaint and seeks to avoid the defects of a prior complaint either by omitting the facts that rendered the complaint defective or by pleading facts inconsistent with the allegations of prior pleadings.” (Owens v. Kings Supermarket (1988) 198 Cal.App.3d 379, 384.) “In these circumstances, the policy against sham pleading permits the court to take judicial notice of the prior pleadings and requires that the pleader explain the inconsistency. If he fails to do so the court may disregard the inconsistent allegations and read into the amended complaint the allegations of the superseded complaint.” (Id. at p. 384.) Defendants argue that the allegations in the First Amended Complaint are inconsistent with those in the original complaint because the initial complaint only alleges Pocono was a party to the oral contract while in the amended complaint Plaintiff claims that Park was also a party to it.    

 

The allegations in the First Amended Complaint and the original complaint are not inconsistent. Plaintiff adds in the amended complaint that Park “on behalf of himself and POCONO, orally agreed and promised to be bound by the same terms of the OEM CONTRACT.” [FAC ¶ 26.] At most, this allegation further elaborates on Park’s alleged oral promises to Plaintiff that he would be “personally responsible for all obligations of POCONO.” [Compl. ¶ 10; FAC ¶ 10.] Defendants’ demurrer to this claim is therefore overruled.

 

Third Cause of Action: Breach of Guarantee

Defendants contend that Plaintiff’s breach of guarantee cause of action fails because it does not allege a written guaranty.

 

“A special promise to answer for the debt, default, or miscarriage of another” must be in writing and signed by the party to be charged. (Civ. Code, § 1624, subd. (a)(2).) An exception exists, however, “[w]here the promise is upon a consideration beneficial to the promisor, whether moving from either party to the antecedent obligation, or from another person.” (Id. § 2794, subd. (4).) This so-called “main purpose rule” applies “whenever the leading and main object of the promisor is not to become surety or guarantor of another but to subserve some purpose or interest of his own . . . .” (Farr & Stone Ins. Brokers, Inc. v. Lopez (1976) 61 Cal.App.3d 618, 622.) “[T]he important question is whether the promise is only an assumption of another’s liability or a new and primary obligation of the promisor.” (Id. at p. 622.)

 

The main purpose rule is applicable to Park’s alleged guarantee of Pocono’s obligations under the oral contract. Plaintiff sufficiently alleges that the main purpose of Park’s guarantee was to advance his own pecuniary interest as President of Pocono [FAC ¶¶ 4, 10, 33-35.] Defendants’ demurrer to the breach of guarantee cause of action is thus overruled.

 

Fourth Cause of Action: Fraud

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s fraud claim fails because it does not allege “how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered.”

 

“The elements of fraud, which give rise to the tort action for deceit, are (a) misrepresentation (false representation, concealment, or nondisclosure); (b) knowledge of falsity (or ‘scienter’); (c) intent to defraud, i.e., to induce reliance; (d) justifiable reliance; and (e) resulting damage.” (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 638.) Fraud must be pled with specificity; conclusory allegations are insufficient. (Id. at p. 645.) This requires “pleading facts which show how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered.” (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff has pled its fraud claim with requisite particularity. It alleges that on or about April 2023, Park orally represented to Plaintiff’s officers, Kyungsun Hong and Sarah Hong, that he owned and controlled Pocono and that if Plaintiff “fulfilled orders issued by POCONO, both POCONO and PARK himself, individually, would be bound by the same terms and conditions contained in the OEM Manufacturing Agreement previously executed between PLAINTIFF and ALPINE under the OEM CONTRACT.” [FAC ¶¶ 10, 38-39.] These allegations sufficiently state the circumstances and details of Park’s alleged misrepresentations to Plaintiff. Defendants’ particularity argument is therefore unavailing.

 

To the extent Defendants challenge the fraud claim on the basis of the independent tort principle (see Demurrer at p. 10), this argument also fails. Where, as here, it is alleged that “a contract is secured by fraudulent representations, the injured party may elect to affirm the contract and sue for the fraud.” (Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 41; Lazar, supra, 12 Cal.4th at p. 645; Campbell v. Birch (1942) 19 Cal.2d 778, 791; see also Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 841-43 [economic loss rule does not bar fraudulent inducement claim].) The independent tort principle is thus no bar to Plaintiff’s fraud claim and Defendants’ demurrer is overruled.

 

Fifth Cause of Action: Quantum Meruit

Defendants’ demurrer to the quantum meruit common count is overruled. Defendants contend that Plaintiff fails to state a count for quantum meruit because the count only applies to the rendition of services, not the provision of goods. While the correct count for “the reasonable value of goods sold and delivered” is quantum valebant (SwiftAir, LLC v. Southwest Airlines Co. (2022) 77 Cal.App.5th 46, 51, fn. 2.), the mere mislabeling of the count is not a reason to sustain the demurrer where it otherwise pleads a cognizable theory of recovery. (Quelimane, supra, 19 Cal.4th at p. 38.) The Court also declines to consider Defendants’ argument that Plaintiff cannot pursue a quasi-contractual theory of recovery due to the alleged existence of a contract between the parties because this point was first raised in reply. (Maleti v. Wickers (2022) 82 Cal.App.5th 181, 227-28.) Defendants’ demurrer to the common count is therefore overruled.

LEAVE TO FILE CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants, Peter Roald Dion-Kindem and Gabrielle Dion-Kindem

RESPONDING PARTY: Unopposed/Plaintiff, Claudia Bretado

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Leave to File Cross-Complaint

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff Claudia Bretado sued Defendants Peter Roald Dion-Kindem and Gabrielle Dion-Kindem for negligence after a car accident between the parties. On December 12, 2025, Plaintiff requested dismissal of the entire action, which was entered on the same day. The dismissal was vacated on April 6, 2026.

 

RULING: Off Calendar

Defendants move for leave to file a cross-complaint in indemnity against Oscar Alejandro Leon Cerada. The basis for the amendment is the revelation through discovery that Plaintiff was involved in an accident with Cerada two days after the accident at issue, she settled her claims against Cerada, and the alleged injuries and damages from that accident overlap with those claimed here. Because Defendants already filed their cross-complaint on December 22, 2025 before trial was set, they need not seek leave. (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (b).) The instant motion is therefore taken off calendar.

 

This order in no way precludes Cerada from seeking to strike the cross-complaint if it is shown that the settlement between Plaintiff and Cerada was a “good faith” settlement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (c).)

 

Defendants to give notice.

STAY

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendant, County of Los Angeles

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Jennifer Donnell

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion to Stay

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

Plaintiff Jennifer Donnell sued Defendant County of Los Angeles, alleging that she was demoted due to her age and her reporting of misconduct at the Probation Department. She asserts multiple causes of action under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), among other claims.

 

RULING: Granted.

Defendant moves to stay the instant action for 6 months pending the outcome of Plaintiff’s Writ of Mandate. The Writ comes after the Civil Service Commission denied hearing on Plaintiff’s appeal of her reduction from Bureau Chief at the Probation Department to Lieutenant at the Public Defender. Defendant argues the decision on the Writ could potentially bar Plaintiff’s suit. Plaintiff opposes the motion, contending a stay is not warranted because the underlying administrative proceeding concerns a narrow civil service rule and will have no impact on the claims and remedies here. Because the outcome of the administrative proceeding could narrow the issues in the instant case, Defendant’s motion is granted and the action is stayed.  

 

“Trial courts generally have the inherent power to stay proceedings in the interests of justice and to promote judicial efficiency.” (Freiberg v. City of Mission Viejo (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1484, 1489; see OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho (2019) 8 Cal.5th 111, 141 [“[A] court ordinarily has inherent power, in its discretion, to stay proceedings when such a stay will accommodate the ends of justice”]; Cooper v. Superior Court In and For Los Angeles County (1961) 55 Cal.2d 291, 301; Bailey v. Fosca Oil Co. (1963) 216 Cal.App.2d 813, 817.)

 

A stay is warranted here whether the Writ court merely reverses the Commission’s decision for reconsideration or proceeds to decide the merits of the appeal itself. The civil service rule at issue is Rule 25. [Chang Decl. ¶ 3, Ex. B.] That rule prohibits discrimination against an employee based on certain classifications such as race and age as well as “other non-merit factors.” (Los Angeles County Code section 5, rule 25.01.) If either the Commission on reversal or the Writ court decides that Plaintiff’s reduction was due to legitimate reasons, those findings are binding here and have collateral estoppel effect. (George v. California Unemployment Ins. Appeals Bd. (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 1475, 1486-88.) The decision would shape litigation on the issue of reasons for demotion. Therefore, the prudent course is to stay the instant action pending resolution of Plaintiff’s administrative claim. Defendant’s motion is thus granted.

 

Defendant represents that the hearing on the writ is scheduled for December 4, 2026. The Court stays the action until the following court day on December 6 and sets an OSC re: Status of Writ for that date. The parties will be required to update the Court on the status of the writ proceeding. Depending on the update, the Court will either lift the stay and set new hearing dates or continue the stay.

 

Defendant to give notice.

TRANSFER/CHANGE VENUE

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants, Hyundai Motor Company and Supernal LLC  

RESPONDING PARTY: Plaintiff, Diana Cooper

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Motion to Change Venue/Transfer the Action to Orange County Superior Court

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

In or around 2020, Hyundai Motor Group established Defendant Supernal LLC for the development of its eVTOL aircraft. Plaintiff Diana Cooper, an experienced aviation and policy expert, was recruited to help lead Supernal’s strategy, regulation, and commercialization efforts. Plaintiff alleges Supernal began to marginalize her after she warned it of safety and regulatory risks and raised concerns about gender discrimination in the company. Her access to senior leadership was restricted, she was excluded from key leadership discussions at Hyundai Motor Company’s (HMC) Korea headquarters, and her leadership authority was dismantled. Plaintiff further alleges that she went on medical leave due to the emotional harm caused by derailment of her career and that she was wrongfully terminated while on leave. On May 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint asserting multiple causes of action under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), among other claims.

 

RULING: Granted.

Evidentiary Objections: The Court only rules on objections material to disposition of the motion. Objection No. 1 to the Declaration of Chapman C. Chan is overruled.

 

Defendant HMC, joined by Supernal, moves to transfer the instant action to Orange County Superior Court. It argues that venue is improper in Los Angeles County under the FEHA’s mandatory venue statute and the general corporate venue statute. Plaintiff opposes the motion, maintaining the propriety of venue in Los Angeles County. Because the FEHA venue statute compels venue in Orange County, HMC’s motion is granted and the action is transferred.

 

“Except as otherwise provided by law and subject to the power of the court to transfer actions or proceedings as provided in this title, the superior court in the county where the defendants or some of them reside at the commencement of the action is the proper court for the trial of the action. If the action is for injury to person or personal property or for death from wrongful act or negligence, the superior court in either the county where the injury occurs or the injury causing death occurs or the county where the defendants, or some of them reside at the commencement of the action, is a proper court for the trial of the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 395, subd. (a).)

 

“Except as otherwise provided in Section 396a, if an action or proceeding is commenced in a court having jurisdiction of the subject matter thereof, other than the court designated as the proper court for the trial thereof, under this title, the action may, notwithstanding, be tried in the court where commenced, unless the defendant, at the time he or she answers, demurs, or moves to strike, or, at his or her option, without answering, demurring, or moving to strike and within the time otherwise allowed to respond to the complaint, files with the clerk, a notice of motion for an order transferring the action or proceeding to the proper court, together with proof of service, upon the adverse party, of a copy of those papers. Upon the hearing of the motion the court shall, if it appears that the action or proceeding was not commenced in the proper court, order the action or proceeding transferred to the proper court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 396b, subd. (a).)

 

“Venue is determined based on the complaint on file at the time the motion to change venue is made.” (Brown v. Superior Court (1984) 37 Cal.3d 477, 482.) In a FEHA action, venue is proper in the county in which: (1) “the unlawful practice is alleged to have been committed”; (2) “the records relevant to the practice are maintained and administered”; or (3) “the aggrieved person would have worked . . . but for the alleged unlawful practice . . . .” (Gov. Code, § 12965, subd. (c)(3).) “[I]f the defendant is not found within any of these counties, an action may be brought within the county of the defendant’s residence or principal office.” (Ibid.) “[T]he special provisions of the FEHA venue statute control in cases involving FEHA claims joined with non-FEHA claims arising from the same facts.” (Brown, supra, 37 Cal.3d at p. 487.)

 

Venue is determined here based on Plaintiff’s original complaint because it was the complaint on file at the time HMC filed its motion to transfer. The special provisions of the FEHA venue state govern because Plaintiff asserted FEHA claims and her FEHA and non-FEHA claims all arose from the alleged adverse conditions of her employment. The question is thus where venue is proper under the statute.

 

Venue is proper in Orange County under the FEHA venue statute. Orange County is where the unlawful practices are alleged to have been committed because Plaintiff alleged in her complaint that the “injuries and damages suffered by Plaintiff were directly caused by Defendants’ wrongful conduct originating from and occurring within Orange County.” [Compl. ¶¶ 21-22.] The records relevant to the practice are maintained in Orange County because Brandy Wamala, Sr. Manager of Total Rewards and HR Operations at Supernal, states in her declaration that personnel files and documents relating to Plaintiff’s employment are located in Irvine. [Wamala Decl. ¶ 9.] And Plaintiff would have worked in Orange County but for the alleged unlawful practice because she pled in her complaint that she performed her work in Irvine and Wamala testifies that Plaintiff’s job duties are performed at Supernal’s headquarters in Irvine. [Compl. ¶ 24; Wamala Decl. ¶ 6.] Venue is therefore mandatory under three of the statutory criteria.

 

Plaintiff’s argument that venue is proper via the venue selection clause in the Confidential Information, Intellectual Property Assignment, and Arbitration Agreement with Supernal is unavailing. While the agreement provides for venue in Los Angeles County for employment-related claims not subject to arbitration [Chan Decl. ¶ 2, Ex. A, p. 9; see Compl. ¶ 21], “agreements fixing venue in some location other than that allowed by statute are a violation of [public] policy” and are unenforceable. (Alexander v. Superior Court (2003) 114 Cal.App.4th 723, 731-32.) As explained above, here, the FEHA venue statute mandates venue in Orange County, not Los Angeles County. HMC’s motion is therefore granted.

 

The action is ordered transferred to Orange County Superior Court. All scheduled items on calendar are vacated.

 

HMC to give notice.
