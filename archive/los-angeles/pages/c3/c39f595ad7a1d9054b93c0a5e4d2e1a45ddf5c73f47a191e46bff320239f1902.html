Tentative Ruling

Barbara Burrell, et al. v. Essex Management, et al., Case No. 25SMCV03450

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to

Hearing Date: September 24, 2026

Background

Plaintiffs Barbara Burrell and Sky’s Gourmet Marketplace sue Defendants Essex Management Corporation, Essex Marina City Club, Marina City Club Condominium Association, Property Management Professionals, the County of Los Angeles, and Does 1-350 for fourteen causes of action arising out of their lease to the premises at 4344 Promenade Way, Unit 202 and Suite 001, Marina Del Rey, California. Plaintiffs initially also made claims against the Essex Defendants’ attorneys, Kimball, Tirey, & St. John (“KTS”).

On August 29, 2025, Defendant KTS filed an Anti-SLAPP motion. On November 10, 2025, the Court granted KTS’s Anti-SLAPP on November 10, 2025.

On March 13, 2026, the Court sustained the Essex Defendant’s demurrer and the County of Los Angeles’s demurrer.

On May 13, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiffs’ Motion for Reconsideration and amended its March 13 ruling on the demurrer to correct errors caused by citations to an outdated version of the Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Tenant Protection Ordinance (“LA RSTPO”).

On July 14, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s Motion for Sanctions based on the citations to the outdated LA RSTPO in the demurrer papers.

On September 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Second Amended Complaint.

On May 12, 2026, Plaintiff Sky’s Gourmet Marketplace (“Plaintiff”) filed this Motion to Compel Further Responses and Production of Documents Requested in the Notice of Deposition of Susan Bowie. On August 24, 2026, the Court held an IDC and ordered Defendant’s counsel to file and serve supplemental responses by September 7, 2026. On September 17, 2026, the Essex Defendants filed a declaration stating that it served compliant responses on September 10, 2026. On September 17, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply arguing that the supplemental responses are not code-compliant.

Motion to Compel Further Responses

The propounding party may file motions to compel further responses to requests for production if it believes the responses received are (1) evasive, (2) incomplete, or (3) if the objections raised are meritless or overly general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a 

The burden is on the moving party to “set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).)  This burden “is met simply by a fact-specific showing of relevance.” (TBG Ins. Servs. Corp. v. Superior Court (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 443, 448.) In response to a motion to compel further responses, the respondent bears the burden of justifying any objections. (Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255.) 

Here, the Essex Defendants produced supplemental discovery responses on September 10, 2026, mooting this motion to compel further responses. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.310(c) [“Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, . . . the demanding party waives the right to compel a further response to this demand.”].)  If Plaintiff wishes to contest the validity of the Essex Defendants’ supplemental responses, it must bring a new motion.

Although the motion is mooted by the supplemental responses, the Court reminds the parties that where a statement that a party will comply with a production demand must state whether the production will be in whole or in part, “and that all documents or things in the demanded category that are in the possession, custody, or control of that party and to which no objection is being made will be included in the production.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.220.) Moreover, an objection must (1) identify, with particularity, any document or tangible thing falling within any category of item in the demand to which the objection is being made, and (2) “set forth clearly the extent of, and the specific ground for, the objection.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.240.) If the objection is based on privilege, the response must provide sufficient information to evaluate the merits of the claim, including, if necessary, a privilege log. (Ibid.)

Sanctions

Although the service of supplemental responses moots the motion to comel, it does not moot the issue of sanctions incurred in bringing the motion. “[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.310(h).)

Plaintiff seeks sanctions for 6.5 hours preparing this motion at its attorney’s hourly rate of $750 per hour. (Hakimfar Decl., ¶ 17.) Plaintiff attests that its attorney spent an additional 4.5 hours on this motion after it was filed, but does not request additional fees for this time. (Supplemental Hakimfar Decl., ¶ 14.) The Essex Defendants do not oppose the request for sanctions. After review, the Court agrees that 6.5 hours on this issue is reasonable, given the IDC and the meet and confer process. However, the Court believes that $750 per hour is excessive for a landlord/tenant dispute. Instead, the Court will award Plaintiff fees at $400/hour, which is consistent with the rates in matters of this type. As such, the Court Plaintiff $2,600 in sanctions.

Conclusion

The Motion to Compel Further Responses is DENIED as Moot.

Plaintiff is awarded $2,600 in sanctions.


Case Number: 25SMCV03803    Hearing Date: September 24, 2026    Dept: P

Tentative Ruling

Anthony Jones v. Velocity Commercial Capital, LLC, et al. Case No. 25SMCV03803

Vivimei LLC’s Demurrer

Hearing Date: September 24, 2026

Background

Anthony Jones, in pro per, sues Velocity Commercial Capital, LLC; U.S. Bank Trust company, N.A., as trustee for the Velocity Commercial Capital Loan Trust 2022-2; McCalla Raymer Leibert Pierce, LLC; Nabeel Zuberi; Vivimei, LLC; and Gaymond Lee for (1) quiet title, (2) cancellation of instruments, (3) declaratory relief, and (4) unfair business practices.

Plaintiff challenges the validity of Defendant Vivimei’s title to property located at 2704 Strongs Drive, Venice, CA 90210. (Second Amended Complaint, ¶¶ 12-17.) Plaintiff alleges that the property should have been sold to him because he made a down payment and subsequently offered a $1.6 million loan he secured in September 2024 to Velocity, which allegedly ignored Plaintiff’s offer and sold the property to Defendant Vivmei at foreclosure. (Id. ¶ 17.) 

On February 26, 2026, the Court sustained Vivimei’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s initial complaint with leave to amend. Plaintiff subsequently filed a First Amended Complaint. The parties then stipulated to leave to amend, and on May 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Second Amended Complaint.

On June 25, 2026, Vivimei filed this demurrer.

On July 21, 2026, Plaintiff retained counsel and filed a substitution of attorney.

On September 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Declaration of Bahram Madaen, seeking leave to amend to file a third amended complaint to clarify the grounds for Plaintiff’s claim of ownership over the subject property.

Given that Vivimei’s demurrer is in large part based on the fact that Plaintiff’s claim to title in the Second Amended Complaint is unclear and because Plaintiff only recently retained counsel, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s counsel’s request for leave to amend.

Conclusion

Plaintiff’s request for leave to amend is GRANTED.

Vivimei’s demurrer is MOOT.

Plaintiff is ordered to file a Third Amended Complaint within ten (10) days.
