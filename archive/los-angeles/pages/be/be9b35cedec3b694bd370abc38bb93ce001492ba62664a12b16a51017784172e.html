BACKGROUND 

This is a medical malpractice case.  On August 18, 2017, Plaintiff Arden Fletcher, who was then 2 years old, was seen for a well-child visit by her pediatrician, Defendant Dr. Paria Hassouri.  At that visit, Plaintiff's parents expressed concern regarding a curvature in Plaintiff's back which was also noted by her school.  Plaintiff's parents also expressed concern that she “waddles”.  

Later, on May 23, 2018, Plaintiff was seen by Dr. Hassouri for another well child visit. Plaintiff's parents again expressed concern about the curvature in Plaintiff's back. Dr. Hassouri referred Plaintiff to Defendant Dr. Robert Bernstein, a pediatric orthopedist.  

Plaintiff alleges that both doctors were negligent in failing to obtain radiologic imaging and as a result, they negligently failed to diagnose and treat Plaintiffs condition - bilateral hip dysplasia.  As a result, Plaintiff alleges she has suffered severe, serious, and permanent injuries.   Her complaint also seeks damages relating to a loss of earning capacity in the future as well as past and future medical expenses. 

This hearing is on Dr. Bernstein’s motion to continue the trial date from September 14, 2026 to November 16, 2026.  Dr. Bernstein requests the approximately two-month continuance because of the unavailability of trial counsel and a retained expert.  Dr. Bernstein’s expert, Dr. Vidyadhar Upasani, will be out of the country from September 10, 2026 through October 10, 2026.  Trial counsel (Michael Gonzalez) is scheduled for a preferential trial in another case, originally set for August 19, 2026 but recently continued to September 9, 2026 which is expected to last 15-20 days.   

LEGAL STANDARD 

Rule 3.1335(a) states that “[a] party seeking to advance, specially set, or reset a case for trial must make this request by noticed motion or ex parte application under the rules in chapter 4 of this division.” Rule 3.1335(b) also says that “[t]he request may be granted only upon an affirmative showing by the moving party of good cause based on a declaration served and filed with the motion or application.” 

DISCUSSION 

There have been several continuances in this case.  Trial was originally scheduled for July 21, 2025.  The parties then stipulated to continue trial to May 18, 2026.  On May 18, the parties again stipulated to a further continuance of trial to September 14, 2026.   

Dr. Bernstein’s counsel participated in the conference where trial was continued to September 14, 2026.  There is no explanation as to what has changed between the May 18 status conference and the filing of the motion to continue, such that Dr. Bernstein’s retained expert suddenly became unavailable.   

As to counsel’s unavailability, trial was set in this case on May 18, 2026.  The trial date in the other case (Case No. 25STCV27943) was set on December 18, 2025.  Counsel therefore should have known there would be a conflict and not stipulate to a trial date of September 14, 2026, at the May 18 hearing. 

Notwithstanding, trial in the other case has now been rescheduled to September 9, 2026.  It is estimated to last 15-20 days which means that it will run into the September 14, 2026 trial date set for this case.  This was done by the Court in Case No. 25STCV27943 on August 19, 2026, which was after the trial setting conference in this case, and therefore, is a new change that warrants a continuance.   

CONCLUSION 

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Defendant Robert Bernstein’s motion to continue trial.  Trial is continued to November 16, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.  Counsel for both parties are cautioned not to schedule vacation or other commitments that would conflict with the November 16 trial date.  The parties must also ensure that their experts or other persons they deem necessary for the trial do not schedule conflicting commitments. 

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

PROCEEDINGS: REQUEST FOR ENTRY OF DEFAULT JUDGMENT 

MOVING PARTY: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.   

RESPONDING PARTY: Authenticated Ventures, LLC; James Andrews 

 

BACKGROUND 

This is a breach of contract case.  On January 11, 2023, Defendant Authenticated Ventures, LLC (“Borrower Defendant”) entered into a Business Line of Credit and Security Agreement in favor of Plaintiff JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A., memorializing a revolving loan not to exceed $90,000.00 (the “Loan”). On the same date, Defendant James Andrews (“Guarantor Defendant”) entered into a Guaranty Agreement in consideration of the Loan (the “Guaranty”).  

Borrower Defendant has failed to pay under the terms of the Contract and is in default. Guarantor Defendant has failed to cure the default of the Borrower Defendant under the Guaranty. Accordingly, Guarantor Defendant is in default.  

As of June 23, 2026, Plaintiff is owed a principal balance of $90,512.11, plus late fees and costs of $582.74, for a total of $91,094.85. While additional sums have accrued, including $5,209.43 in interest and substantial attorneys’ fees and costs, Plaintiff is not seeking recovery of such amounts, and seeks judgment solely for the outstanding principal and late charges and costs. 

Plaintiff substitute served Defendants on April 29, 2026, by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint with “Ari Doe” at a private mailbox located at a UPS store.  The address of the store is 324 S. Beverly Dr., Beverly Hills, CA 90212.  Defendants did not respond.   

On June 9, 2026, following Plaintiff’s filing of a Request for Entry of Default, the Clerk of the Court entered default against Defendants.  This hearing is on Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment.   

REQUEST FOR RELIEF 

Default judgment against Defendants for a total of $91,094.85, which is comprised of: (1) $90,512.11 as the “demand of complaint”, and (2) $582.74 for loan fees and costs.   

 

ANALYSIS 

 

There are several defects in Plaintiff’s proofs of service.  Plaintiff served Defendants via a commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA).  Section 415.20(c) provides that substitute service may be effectuated on an individual by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint with a¿CMRA¿ “if the¿only¿address reasonably known¿for the person … is a private mailbox obtained through a commercial mail receiving agency.” (§ 415.20, subd. (c), italics¿added.)  Here, Plaintiff has not indicated how it came to conclude that there is no other address for Defendants, including whether it ran a search for a different address.   

Further, California law requires the CMRA to place a notice or copy of the documents in the customer’s mailbox within 48 hours and to send the documents by first-class mail¿within five days after receipt¿to the customer’s address. (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17538.5, subd. (d)(1); see also¿§ 415.20, subd. (c).)  There is no evidence that indicates whether these statutory requirements were met.  Plaintiff did not submit any declaration from a UPS store employee.  

Accordingly, because there is no proper service, the Court has no personal jurisdiction over Defendants, and any default or default judgment against them would be void.   

CONCLUSION AND ORDER 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff’s Request for Default Judgment is DENIED.  The Court sets an order to show cause why the case should not be dismissed for failure to obtain default judgment for December 9, 2026 at 8:30 a.m.
