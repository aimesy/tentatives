Case Number: 25STCV20111    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: June 24, 2026

Case Name: Amar v. FCA US LLC, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV20111 

Matter: Motion to Compel

Moving Party: Plaintiff Amar

Responding Party: Defendant FCA US LLC

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is a lemon law action. 

Plaintiff Lauren Amar seeks to compel initial disclosures from Defendant FCA US LLC.

Defendant contends that Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 does not explicitly require a written, verified pleading or response, but merely the physical production of documents. The Court rejects this narrow interpretation.

Under the statutory framework governing initial disclosures, a party must provide an authenticated statement of compliance to ensure completeness. Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.090(a)(5), which informs the mechanics of mandatory disclosures, explicitly requires that initial disclosures be verified by a written declaration of the party or signed by counsel. A compliance framework relies on formal verification to prevent selective withholding and to guarantee that a diligent search was executed. While FCA notes it eventually served unverified written disclosures on June 10, 2026, in an abundance of caution, it must formalize this with a proper statutory verification signed under penalty of perjury by an authorized representative or signed by counsel of record.

Section 871.26 establishes an expedited discovery regime for Song-Beverly consumer warranty actions, making specific categories of upfront disclosures mandatory without awaiting a formal discovery request. Plaintiff argues that FCA’s production of the vehicle's warranty transaction history is filtered and incomplete. Section 871.26(h)(7) explicitly mandates the production of the "entire warranty transaction history for the motor vehicle." This includes automated system entries such as AutoPay claims and "Trouble Not Found" (TNF) diagnostic claims submitted under Labor Operation Codes (LOP) 85-41-XX-XX or the 08-19-XX-40 series. These entries constitute recorded diagnostic and repair attempts germane to a Song-Beverly claim (see Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785). FCA is ordered to produce a complete, unfiltered DealerCONNECT report and transaction history containing these fields to the extent they exist for this VIN.

Under sections 871.26(h)(8), (9), and (10), the manufacturer must disclose recalls, field actions, Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs), and Informational Service Bulletins (ISBs) reasonably related to the alleged nonconformities. FCA has produced TSBs and certain recall records but has apparently produced no ISBs. FCA must explicitly clarify in its verified written response whether any responsive ISBs exist and, if so, produce them.

Section 871.26(h)(13) requires the production of service manuals reasonably related to the vehicle's alleged nonconformities. FCA objects to producing its Warranty Administration, Dealer Policy, Customer Assistance Center, and Service Manuals without a protective order, claiming they are commercially sensitive. To the extent a protective order has now been entered, FCA must fully produce the relevant manual segments within the 10-day compliance window.

In sum, the Motion is granted. Production/responses are to be provided within 10 days. No sanctions.

Moving party to give notice.



Case Number: 26STCV00750    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: June 24, 2026

Case Name: Rose v. Artiano Shinoff, APC, et al.

Case No.: 26STCV00750

Matter: Motion to Lift Stay of Discovery

Moving Party: Plaintiff Joseph Rose

Responding Party: Defendants Artiano Shinoff, APC, Gil Abed, Paul Carelli IV, and Maurice

Bumbu

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is denied.

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA 

CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On March 25, 2026, Plaintiff Joseph Rose filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”). Plaintiff alleges that evidence subject to a protective order from his underlying action (22STCV37608) against LAUSD was improperly disclosed and weaponized in the Jane Doe K.D. v. LAUSD case (22TRCV01592), and conversely, restricted evidence from the K.D. case was imported back into 22STCV37608. Plaintiff asserts that this illicit exchange of discovery was orchestrated to manipulate litigation strategies and gain an unfair collateral advantage in both proceedings. Plaintiff’s causes of action target Defendants’ representations to the court and their litigation strategy in attempting to settle these cases. 

On April 27, 2026, Defendants Artiano Shinoff, APC, Gil Abed, Paul Carelli IV, and Maurice Bumbu filed an anti-SLAPP Motion as to the FAC.

Plaintiff now seeks to lift the stay of discovery and conduct discovery “into when Defendants learned of the correct Darryl Charles Lott identity, when Defendants learned that Plaintiff’s case involved a mistaken-identity issue, whether confidential materials from Plaintiff’s case were used in the K.D. case, and whether communications exist between Defendants and Slater Slater Schulman LLP concerning these issues.”

Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(g) provides, “All discovery proceedings in the action shall be stayed upon the filing of a notice of motion made pursuant to this section. The stay of discovery shall remain in effect until notice of entry of the order ruling on the motion. The court, on noticed motion and for good cause shown, may order that specified discovery be conducted notwithstanding this subdivision.”

In order to establish “good cause” within the meaning of Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(g), the plaintiff must show that “a defendant or a witness possesses evidence needed by the plaintiff to establish its probability of prevailing on a claim.”  (1-800 Contacts, Inc. v. Steinberg (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 568, 572).  Whether good cause has been shown is within the discretion of the Court.  (See Lafayette Morehouse, Inc. v. Chronicle Publ'g Co. (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 855, 868.) 

“[T]he fact evidence necessary to establish the plaintiff's prima facie case is in the hands of the defendant or a third party goes a long way toward showing good cause for discovery. But it is not the only factor. The trial court should consider whether the information the plaintiff seeks to obtain through formal discovery proceedings is readily available from other sources or can be obtained through informal discovery.  The court should also consider the plaintiff's need for discovery in the context of the issues raised in the SLAPP motion.”  (The Garment Workers Ctr. v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 1156, 1162.)

Good cause has not been shown for lifting the stay on discovery. This is because it appears that the FAC is largely subject to the litigation privilege and/or presents defective pleading.

Therefore, the Motion is denied.

Moving party to give notice.

Case Name: Santamaria v. Magic Mountain LLC, et al.

Case No.: 26STCV10542

Matter: Motion to Strike 

Moving Party: Defendants Magic Mountain LLC and Six Flags Entertainment

Corporation

Responding Party: Plaintiff Elena Santamaria

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion to Strike is denied.

Moving parties to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA 

CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is an employment action.

Defendants Magic Mountain LLC and Six Flags Entertainment Corporation now seek to strike the Complaint’s references to punitive damages.

Motions to strike are used to challenge defects in the pleadings not subject to demurrer.  Any party may move to strike the whole or any part of a pleading within the time allotted to respond to the pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 435(b)(1).)  The grounds for a motion to strike shall appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the Court is required to take judicial notice.  (Id. § 437(a).)  The Court may strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading, and strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.  (Id. § 436.)  An “irrelevant” matter includes any “demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint or cross-complaint.”  (Id. § 431.10(b)(3), (c); see also Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1036-1042.)

Punitive damages are available when the plaintiff establishes oppression, fraud, or malice by clear and convincing evidence.  (Civ. Code § 3294(a).)  

Malice means “conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.”  (Civ. Code § 3294(c)(1).)  In this context, despicable conduct is conduct considered “so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people.”  (Scott v. Phoenix Schools, Inc. (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 702, 715, internal quotation marks omitted.)  Oppression means “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights.”  (Civ. Code § 3294(c)(2).)  Fraud means “an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.”  (Id. § 3294(c)(3).)

The Complaint goes beyond mere boilerplate assertions, detailing a deliberate, continuous course of conduct where corporate supervisors repeatedly disregarded Plaintiff’s safety and rights. Specifically, Plaintiff alleges that management ignored severe sexual harassment and battery complaints for weeks, actively suppressed a report of physical violence by instructing her not to contact Human Resources or the police, and ultimately forced her into a retaliatory "take it or face termination" transfer that required her to work side-by-side with her known sexual harasser. These specific acts, combined with the allegation that a Human Resources representative stated the transfer "had already been signed off on," provide a sufficient factual basis at the pleading stage to infer malice, oppression, and corporate ratification or authorization by a managing agent. 

Therefore, the Motion to Strike is denied. An answer is to be filed within 10 days.

Moving party to give notice.
