Analysis:

 

On November 21, 2023, Plaintiff Progressive West Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Destiny C. Erazo-Lopez (“Defendant”). Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered Defendant’s default on April 28, 2025 and default judgment on May 9, 2025.

However, the Motion does not include a proof of service showing it was served on Plaintiff, or that Plaintiff was given notice of the hearing date. The statute for service of motion mandates that at least 16 court days’ notice must be provided. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).)

 

Accordingly, the Court continues the hearing so the Motion may be heard with proper notice to the other side.

 

Defendant Destiny C. Erazo-Lopez’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is continued to 09/14/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/10/2026, Defendant Destiny C. Erazo-Lopez is to file proof of service of the Motion and the Notice of the New Hearing Date. Failure to do so may result in the Motion being denied.

Case Number: 25STLC02051    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Baldwin Mader Law Group’s Motion to Continue Trial is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On March 14, 2025, Plaintiff Baldwin Mader Law Group (“Plaintiff”) filed this action for breach of contract against Defendant Jerry Ostrin (“Defendant”).

 

On June 11, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Continue Trial seeking a continuance of “at least three months.” (Motion, p.3.)

 

On June 15, 2026, the Court on its own motion issued an order continuing the trial date from September 11, 2026 to November 25, 2026, on account of the Court being unavailable on September 11, 2026. (Minute Order, 06/15/26.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for a trial continuance of three months pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court rule 3.1332, which provides “[a]lthough continuances of trials are disfavored, each request for a continuance must be considered on its own merits. The Court may grant a continuance only on an affirmative showing of good cause requiring the continuance.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (c).) Grounds for a continuance include the unavailability of trial counsel because of death, illness, or other excusable circumstances. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (c)(3).)

 

The court may look to the following factors in determining whether a trial continuance is warranted: (1) proximity of the trial date; (2) whether there was any previous continuance of trial due to any party; (3) the length of the continuance requested; (4) the availability of alternative means to address the problem that gave rise to the motion; (5) the prejudice that parties or witnesses will suffer as a result of the continuance; and (6) whether trial counsel is engaged in another trial.  (See generally, Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subd. (d)(1)-(11).) Additional factors for the court to consider include: a party’s excused inability to obtain essential testimony, documents, or other material evidence despite diligent efforts; whether all parties have stipulated to a continuance; and any other fact or circumstance relevant to the fair determination of the motion or application. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1332, subds. (c), (d).)

 

The Court denies the Motion as moot because the requested continuance has already been granted.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Baldwin Mader Law Group’s Motion to Continue Trial is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC00094    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Diamond Environmental Services, LP’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

 

Plaintiff Nakisha Lee is ordered to serve responses to the Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Defendant’s requests for sanctions are denied.

 

 

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

Defendant Diamond Environmental Services, LP (“Defendant”) served Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, on Plaintiff Nakisha Lee (“Plaintiff Lee”) on April 23, 2026. (Motions, Ghomi Decl., Exh. A.) Despite a meet and confer effort, to date, Plaintiff Lee has not served verified responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶¶6-8 and Exh. 2) Defendant filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions on June 25, 2026. The Motions were served on Plaintiff Lee through Attorney Sergei N. Gladkov, Esq. by electronic mail the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Further, the motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Based on Plaintiff Lee’s failure to timely respond to the propounded discovery, Defendant is entitled to an order compelling service of verified responses to the discovery requests without objections.

 

Defendant moves for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030. In City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, the Supreme Court observed:

 

It is already well-established that a court may not rely on [Code of Civil Procedure] section 2023.030 to override the limitations prescribed by any other applicable sanctions provision in the [Civil Discovery] Act. A court may invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 only when confronted with an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision. And where it invokes that authority, it is constrained by the long-settled rules generally governing the imposition of discovery sanctions under the Act.

 

(City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 74-75.) The moving statutes, Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300, only make sanctions available when a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); § 2031.300, subd. (c).) Plaintiff Lee filed no opposition to the instant Motions and cannot be sanctioned under sections 2030.290 or 2031.310. Nor has Defendant shown that Plaintiff Lee’s failure to serve discovery responses is “an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision.” Therefore, the Court declines to invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Diamond Environmental Services, LP’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.


Plaintiff Nakisha Lee is ordered to serve responses to the Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Defendant’s requests for sanctions are denied.

 

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

 

 


Case Number: 26STLC00700    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Zhiwei Chen’s Motion to Vacate Ex Parte Order Dated June 23, 2026 is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Zhiwei Chen (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendant Five Points Towing, LLC (“Defendant”) on February 2, 2026. Defendant filed its Answer on March 12, 2026. On May 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed (1) Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories and Requests for Production. The discovery motions were set for hearing on June 25, 2026.

 

On June 18, 2026, Defendant filed an Ex Parte Application for Order Continuing Hearing on Motions, which the Court denied on June 23, 2026. (Minute Order, 06/23/26.) The Court ruled that it would consider Defendant’s arguments at the hearing on the Motions. (Ibid.) Plaintiff filed a Supplemental Notice with respect to the discovery motions and an opposition to the Ex Parte Application on June 22, 2026.  Plaintiff filed this Motion on June 25, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to vacate the Court’s order dated June 23, 2026 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).  Section 473, subdivision (b), however, only “relieve[s] a party or the party’s legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against the party . . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b), emphasis added.) The order dated June 23, 2026 was not taken against Plaintiff. Instead, the Court denied Defendant’s request to continue the hearing date on Plaintiff’s discovery motions. The Motion does not demonstrate how this denial operated against Plaintiff’s interests. Relief under section 473, subdivision (b) is not available to Plaintiff for this order.

 

Conclusion

 

Therefore, Plaintiff Zhiwei Chen’s Motion to Vacate Ex-Parte Order Dated June 23, 2026 is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC01900    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Guhan Santhappan’s Motion for Summary Adjudication is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On March 19, 2026, Plaintiff Guhan Santhappan(“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for return of personal property and declaratory relief. (Compl., p. 1.) Defendant filed a Response to Complaint on April 15, 2026. On May 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Summary Adjudication. Defendant filed an opposition on June 2, 2026 and Plaintiff replied on June 29, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is not accompanied by a memorandum of points and authorities, as required by the California Rules of Court. “The memorandum must contain a statement of facts, a concise statement of the law, evidence and arguments relied on, and a discussion of the statutes, cases, and textbooks cited in support of the position advanced.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1113(b).) Indeed, a party’s failure to provide a memorandum as required by the Rule is an “admission that the [request] is without merit and cause for its denial.” (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1113(a), (b); In re Marriage of Falcone & Fyke (2012) 203 Cal.App.4th 964, 976.)

 

Plaintiff filed a document entitled “Notice of Motion and Motion,” which appears to be solely a notice of motion, as it is less than one page long and includes no attached memorandum, nor does it reference any legal authority. (Notice of Motion and Motion, pp. 1-2.)  Without citation to legal authority and an explanation of how it applies to the evidence, the Court cannot award Plaintiff summary adjudication.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Guhan Santhappan’s Motion for Summary Adjudication is denied.

Case Number: 26STLC03135    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Doddy Rafieha’s Demurrer to the Complaint is overruled.

 

Defendants Abbyson Living LLC and Doddy Rafieha are to file and serve an Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this ruling.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 7, 2026, Plaintiff Captain Pallets, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendants Abbyson Living LLC (“Defendant Abbyson”) and Doddy Rafieha (“Defendant Rafieha”). Defendants Rafieha filed the instant Demurrer to the Complaint on June 25, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 9, 2026 and Defendant Rafieha replied on July 20, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Allegations in the Complaint

 

The Complaint alleges causes of action for breach of contract and common counts against both Defendants, and includes an attachment that alleges the existence of “a unity of interest and ownership between” Defendants such that their individuality and separateness have ceased to exist. (Compl., Attachment, p. 1:13-16.) Defendant Abbyson is the alter ego of Defendant Rafieha because “there is and has been a commingling of Doddy Rafieha’s assets with that of Abbyson Living LLC, and Doddy Rafieha has used and continues to use corporate funds and assets as if they were his own.” (Id. at p. 1:16-19.) Defendant Abbyson has failed to follow the corporate formalities required to remain an entity distinct from Defendant Rafieha. (Id. at p, 1: 20-22.) Finally, “adherence to the fiction of the separate existence of Abbyson Living LLC as an entity distinct from Doddy Rafieha would permit abuse of the corporate privilege and would promote injustice.” (Id. at p. 1:22-24.)

 

Demurrer to Complaint

 

Defendant Rafieha demurs to the Complaint for failure to allege facts sufficient to state either cause of action, on account of inadequate alter ego allegations. (Demurrer, p. 4:6-15.) The Demurrer is accompanied by a meet and confer declaration as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. (Demurrer, Bamford Decl., ¶2.)

 

“To recover on an alter ego theory, a plaintiff need not use the words “alter ego,” but must allege sufficient facts to show a unity of interest and ownership, and an unjust result if the corporation is treated as the sole actor.” (Leek v. Cooper (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 399, 415.) These facts include commingling of funds, failure to maintain corporate formalities, inadequate capitalization, or use of corporate assets for personal purposes. (Sonora Diamond Corp. v. Superior Court (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 523, 538.) The Complaint alleges the ultimate facts of commingling of funds, failure to maintain corporate formalities, and use of corporate assets for personal purposes by Defendant Rafieha.

 

As pointed out by Plaintiff’s opposition, these ultimate fact allegations are held to be sufficient to allege alter ego liability. (Rutherford Holdings, LLC v. Plaza Del Rey (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 221, 236 [“Defendants argue that Rutherford failed to allege specific facts to support an alter ego theory, but Rutherford was required to allege only ‘ultimate rather than evidentiary facts.’”].) Defendant Rafieha’s reading of Rutherford Holdings, LLC incorrectly requires Plaintiff to allege particular alter ego factors, such as the individual defendant dominated and controlled the corporate entity, that the entity operated as a mere shell and conduit for the individual's personal affairs, that the entity was inadequately capitalized. That is not what the case requires, it simply points to those factors as the ones alleged to support the alter ego claim in that case. No particular set of alter ego factors is required; here, Plaintiff alleges commingling of funds, failure to maintain corporate formalities, and use of corporate assets for personal purposes to demonstrate unity of interest and ownership. This is sufficient.

 

Regarding the facts required to allege an unjust result, the Complaint alleges that adherence to the corporate fiction in this case would “permit abuse of the corporate privilege and promote injustice.” This is sufficient to allege facts showing an injustice or an inequitable result, as explained in Rutherford Holdings:

 

Rutherford alleged that … recognizing the separate existence of PDR would promote injustice. These allegations mirror those held to pass muster in First Western Bank & Trust Co. v. Bookasta (1968) 267 Cal.App.2d 910, 915–916, 73 Cal.Rptr. 657. As in First Western, “[a]ssuming these facts can be proved, [Caswell] ... may be held liable ... under the alter ego principle.” (Id. at p. 916, 73 Cal.Rptr. 657.)

 

(Rutherford Holdings, 223 Cal.App.4th at 235–236, citing First Western Bank & Trust Co. v. Bookasta (1968) 267 Cal.App.2d 910, 915–916.) As the Demurrer points out, without alter ego liability, the Complaint does not support either cause of action against Defendant Doddy Rafieha. The invoices attached to the Complaint are billed only to Defendant Abbyson, with nothing to show personal liability on behalf of Defendant Rafieha. (Compl., Exh. A.) The alter ego allegations, however, are sufficient to support liability against Defendant Rafieha, as well.

 

Therefore, the Demurrer to the Complaint is overruled.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Doddy Rafieha’s Demurrer to the Complaint is overruled.

 

Defendants Abbyson Living LLC and Doddy Rafieha are to file and serve an Answer to the Complaint within 20 days of this ruling.

Case Number: LAM17K07112    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Judgment Creditor Delbert Cannon’s Motion for Assignment Order is Granted.

 

The proposed order filed on 04/21/2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

ANALYSIS:  

 

On June 2, 2017, Plaintiff Delbert Cannon (“Judgment Creditor”) filed this action against Defendants Panya Patee (“Judgment Debtor Panya Patee”), Thong Kam Patee (“Judgment Debtor Thong Patee”), and Becky Thi (“Judgment Debtor Thi”). On May 30, 2018, the Court entered default judgment in favor of Judgment Creditor and against Judgment Debtor for the total amount of $5,145.00. (Judgment, 05/30/18, ¶6.) The Court renewed the judgment on August 22, 2025. On April 15, 2026, Judgment Debtor Panya Patee was sworn, examined, and discharged. (Minute Order, 04/15/26.) 

 

Judgment Creditor filed the instant Motion for an Assignment Order on April 21, 2026. Judgment Creditor served the Motion on Judgment Debtors by first-class mail on the same date. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure, section 708.510, subdivision (a), the moving statute, the Court may order the judgment debtor to assign to the judgment creditor or to an appointed receiver all or part of a right to payment due or to become due. The types of payments that can be assigned include wages due from the federal government if not subject to a withholding order, rents, commissions, royalties, patent or copyright payments, and insurance policy loan value. (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.510, subd. (a).)

 

Relevant factors the Court may take into consideration when making an assignment order include the judgment debtor’s reasonable requirements if they are a natural person, payments the judgment debtor is required to make to satisfy other judgments and wage assignments, the amount remaining due on the judgment, and the amount to be received in satisfaction of the right to payment that may be assigned.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 708.510, subd. (c).) Construing all the applicable statutes together, the “assignment order” contemplated by Code of Civil Procedure, section 708.510, et seq. must include a court order that assigns a right to payment outright (not simply an order directing the judgment debtor to do so). 

 

Here, Judgment Creditor seeks an order assigning rental income earned by Judgment Debtor Panya Patee with respect to the property located at 2415 Pacific Avenue, Long Beach, California. Judgment Debtor Panya Patee testified at the judgment debtor examination that Judgment Debtor Patee receives $2,500.00 a month in rent from lessee Suphaphorn Charukulvanich. (Motion, Mehrban Decl., ¶2.) Assignment orders may include rents received by the Judgment Debtor.  (Code Civ. Proc., 708.510(a)(2).)  Based on the foregoing in-court testimony, Judgment Creditor has shown that Judgment Debtor Panya Patee receives rent payments to which Judgment Creditor may be granted a right of assignment to enforce the judgment. 

 

In determining whether to order assignment or the amount of assignment, the Court may consider various factors including the requirements of the judgment debtor and other payments the judgment debtor is required to make.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.510(c).)  Judgment Debtor failed to oppose the Motion and therefore provided no relevant information for the Court to consider.  Accordingly, the Court grants the motion for an assignment order in the full amount sought, to the point that the judgment is satisfied.

 

Conclusion

 

Judgment Creditor Delbert Cannon’s Motion for Assignment Order is Granted.

 

The proposed order filed on 04/21/2026 is signed and entered this date.
