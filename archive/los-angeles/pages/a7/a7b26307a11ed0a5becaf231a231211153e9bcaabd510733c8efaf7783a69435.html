Case Number: 23STCV11406    Hearing Date: August 28, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: August 28, 2026

Case Name: Dechter v. Massman, et al.

Case No.: 23STCV11406 

Matter: Motions to Compel Further Responses (2x)

Moving Party: Plaintiff Bradley J. Dechter

Responding Party: Defendants Ronald Massman and DHX–Dependable Hawaiian Express, 

Inc. 

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motions are granted in part.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On May 19, 2023, Plaintiff Bradley J. Dechter filed a Complaint.  On October 9, 2023, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants Ronald Massman and DHX–Dependable Hawaiian Express, Inc. (“DHX”) for (1) breach of oral contract, (2) breach of oral contract, (3) fraud, and (4) unjust enrichment.  Plaintiff alleges that he is entitled to an interest in DHX due to his contributions, but that his ownership interest has been refused.  

Plaintiff now seeks to compel further responses to its requests for production (RFPs) from Defendants.


  1. Responses indicating nonexistence

The Motion is granted as to the many responses in which Defendants indicated that no documents exist. First, the objections lack merit. Second, a response should be provided as though the "acquisition" refers to the asset purchase. The objection to this phrasing is evasive.


  1. Co-owned entities term

The Motion is granted as to RFP nos. 15, 16, 19. The objection to the term “co-owned entities” lacks merit. A response should be provided based on Defendants’ best understanding of the term. If the parties had met and conferred, a reasonable definition could also have been agreed upon.


  1. Financial Records, Corporate Minutes, etc.

The Court temporally narrows these financial and corporate documents to 2018 through 2022. Querying an existing general ledger database to extract standard monthly/quarterly financial statements or comparative reports constitutes translating ESI into a "reasonably usable form" pursuant to CCP § 2031.280(d)–(e), not creating new documents (Person v. Farmers Ins. Group of Cos. (1997) 52 Cal.App.4th 813, 818). Defendants conceded they hold a database capable of generating these standard statements. 


  1. Lease related RFPs

Plaintiff's allegations regarding below-market or artificially controlled lease rates directly implicate breach of fiduciary duty and financial management theories. Historical lease terms, actual payment ledgers, and internal communications regarding property leases are directly relevant to establishing baseline obligations, rent allocation methods between entities, and corporate governance practices over time.

Requests seeking lease documents and payment records dating back to 2013 or 2019 are reasonably tailored to show multi-year financial arrangements, corporate inter-entity transfers, and baseline metrics before and after key corporate transactions (West Pico Furniture Co. v. Super. Ct. (1961) 56 Cal.2d 407, 417).

Defendants' proposed compromise—limiting production to post-lease-term general ledger excerpts and communications—unreasonably restricts discovery to a self-selected evidentiary subset. Plaintiff is entitled to inspect the underlying lease agreements, payment records, and non-privileged communications within Defendants' possession, custody, or control.


  1. Privilege Log & Protective Order

If a privilege is asserted, then a privilege log must be provided. Confidentiality concerns can be addressed via the Court’s model protective order.


In sum, the Motions are granted in part. Further responses without objections are to be provided within 20 days. Financial and corporate documents are temporally narrowed to 2018 through 2022. Lease documents must be provided from 2013/2019 until the present.

Moving party to give notice.

Case Name: Harris v. 111 W 7 Owner, LLC, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV27169

Matter: Motion to Strike

Moving Party: Defendant 111 W 7 Owner, LLC

Responding Party: Plaintiff Tamara Harris

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion to Strike is denied.

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is a landlord-tenant dispute. 

Defendant 111 W 7 Owner, LLC now seeks to strike the Complaint’s references to punitive damages.

Motions to strike are used to challenge defects in the pleadings not subject to demurrer.  Any party may move to strike the whole or any part of a pleading within the time allotted to respond to the pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 435(b)(1).)  The grounds for a motion to strike shall appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the Court is required to take judicial notice.  (Id. § 437(a).)  The Court may strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading, and strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.  (Id. § 436.)  An “irrelevant” matter includes any “demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations of the complaint or cross-complaint.”  (Id. § 431.10(b)(3), (c); see also Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1036-1042.)

Punitive damages are available when the plaintiff establishes oppression, fraud, or malice by clear and convincing evidence.  (Civ. Code § 3294(a).)  

Malice means “conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.”  (Civ. Code § 3294(c)(1).)  In this context, despicable conduct is conduct considered “so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people.”  (Scott v. Phoenix Schools, Inc. (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 702, 715, internal quotation marks omitted.)  Oppression means “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights.”  (Civ. Code § 3294(c)(2).)  Fraud means “an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.”  (Id. § 3294(c)(3).)

“[M]alice does not require actual intent to harm. (Id. at p. 895, 157 Cal.Rptr. 693, 598 P.2d 854.) Conscious disregard for the safety of another may be sufficient where the defendant is aware of the probable dangerous consequences of his or her conduct and he or she willfully fails to avoid such consequences. (Id. at pp. 895–896, 157 Cal.Rptr. 693, 598 P.2d 854.) Malice may be proved either expressly through direct evidence or by implication through indirect evidence from which the jury draws inferences.”  (Angie M. v. Superior Ct. (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1228.)

The Motion is denied as to punitive damages because Plaintiff alleges sufficient facts to indicate malice with regard to Defendant’s intentional failure to do repairs at the subject premises.  (See Smith v. David (1981) 120 Cal.App.3d 101, 112.)  

Moving party to give notice.
