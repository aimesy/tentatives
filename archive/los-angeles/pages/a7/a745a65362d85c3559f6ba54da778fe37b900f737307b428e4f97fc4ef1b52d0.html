DEPARTMENT N LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24SMCV02812    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE ORDER

Plaintiffs Benjamin Berenji and Matthew Berenji’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Defendant Apex Security Group, Inc.’s Employee Bruce Nance and Production of Documents is GRANTED. The parties shall meet and confer regarding the deposition of Bruce Nance to occur within sixty (60) days of entry of this order.

Plaintiffs Benjamin Berenji and Matthew Berenji’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is DENIED.

Plaintiffs Benjamin Berenji and Matthew Berenji to give notice.

REASONING

Plaintiffs Benjamin Berenji and Matthew Berenji (“Plaintiffs”) move the Court for an order compelling Bruce Nance, an employee of Defendant Apex Security Group, Inc. (“Defendant”), to appear for an in-person deposition at Plaintiffs’ counsel’s office, and to produce records requested in the deposition notices issued to those witnesses, within 20 days of entry of this order, with the document production to occur at least one hour before the date and time of his deposition. Plaintiffs argue that they have been attempting to take Nance’s deposition since August 2025, but Defendant has failed to produce the witness, despite identifying him as a witness to the subject incident that is the subject of this action. 

The Court finds that Plaintiffs are entitled to take the deposition of Bruce Nance, who was identified by Defendant as its employee who was a witness to the subject incident. (Mot., Gage Decl. ¶ 2.) Plaintiffs provide evidence that they have noticed Nance’s deposition, and the deposition has not gone forward, nor have new dates been provided. (Mot., Gage Decl. ¶¶ 3-24.) Defendant states in the opposition that the deposition was proposed to go forward on July 20, 2026, but Plaintiffs contend that they are skeptical this deposition will go forward on that date. Accordingly, Plaintiffs Benjamin Berenji and Matthew Berenji’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Defendant Apex Security Group, Inc.’s Employee Bruce Nance and Production of Documents is GRANTED. The parties shall meet and confer regarding the deposition of Bruce Nance to occur within sixty (60) days of entry of this order.

“If a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (g)(1).) The Court opts not to impose monetary sanctions here, as Defendant has proposed a date for deposition, and the Court will not issue sanctions based on speculation that the deposition will not go forward. Thus, Plaintiffs Benjamin Berenji and Matthew Berenji’s Request for Monetary Sanctions is DENIED. However, the Court again advises the parties to continue attempting to resolve discovery disputes informally without motion practice, as the Court may impose monetary sanctions as to other motions if proper.

Case Number: 25SMCV06536    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu’s Motion to Transfer Venue to Santa Clara County is GRANTED.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 399, all required costs and fees of transfer shall be paid by Plaintiff Jerry Kowal and Plaintiff’s counsel within five (5) days after service of notice of the transfer order.

Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu’s Request for Attorney Fees is DENIED.

Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu to give notice.

Plaintiff alleges in the complaint that venue is proper in Los Angeles because the acts and omissions giving rise to his claims occurred in Los Angeles, and at least one defendant resides or maintains a business presence here. (Compl. ¶ 9.) Notably, neither the complaint nor the opposition to this motion shed any light on which defendant is alleged to reside or maintain a business presence in Los Angeles, and Defendants evidence shows that all Defendants are residents of Santa Clara County. (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶¶ 3, 6, 7.) Moreover, the present action is one properly classified as a “mixed action.” “In a mixed action, a plaintiff alleges two or more causes of action each of which is governed by a different venue statute. Or, two or more defendants are named who are subject to different venue standards.” (Brown v. Superior Court(1984) 37 Cal.3d 477, 488.) “In cases with mixed causes of action, a motion for change of venue must be granted on the entire complaint if the defendant is entitled to a change of venue on any one cause of action.” (Ibid.)

Where even one claim is “personal and transitory,” the defendants are entitled to a change of venue, regardless of how many other causes of action in the complaint may not be subject to a venue change. (Rosen v. Kessler (1956) 145 Cal.App.2d 676, 683.) The complaint makes clear that Plaintiff’s claims for breach of contract and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing are person and transitory, such that venue is only proper as to those claims in the county where at least one defendant resides. (Code Civ. Proc., § 395, subd. (a) [“[e]xcept as otherwise provided by law and subject to the power of the court to transfer actions or proceedings as provided in this title, the superior court in the county where the defendants or some of them reside at the commencement of the action is the proper court for the trial of the action”].) Further, Plaintiff chose to bring this action against both corporate defendants and an individual defendant, and it is well established that “when a plaintiff brings an action against several defendants, both individual and corporate, in a county in which none of the defendants reside, an individual defendant has the right to change venue to the county of his or her residence,” even if the corporate defendant may be sued elsewhere. (Brown v. Superior Court, supra, 37 Cal.3d at p. 484, fn. 6.) 

Plaintiff does not dispute any of these arguments. Instead, he focuses on arguing that Defendants forfeited their right to a transfer because of their delay and litigation conduct. This argument is not well taken. Defendants were entitled to change their mind on filing a motion to compel arbitration, just as they were entitled to decline Plaintiff’s request that they waive their right to file such a motion if he stipulated to a transfer of this action. This motion was filed less than four months after this action was filed, which does not support a conclusion that Defendants delayed in so moving. They were also entitled to a stay while this motion was pending, and they were not required to participate in discovery in the meantime. Thus, Plaintiff’s arguments against transferring this action are unavailing. Further, Defendants’ motion papers make clear that 31 out of 37 employees of the corporate entities reside in Santa Clara (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶ 12), the documents are located in Santa Clara (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶ 11), and the purported events at issue in this action likely occurred in Santa Clara (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶¶ 10-14).  

For these reasons, the Court finds it is proper to transfer this action to Santa Clara County. Accordingly, Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu’s Motion to Transfer Venue to Santa Clara County is GRANTED. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 399, all required costs and fees of transfer shall be paid by Plaintiff Jerry Kowal and Plaintiff’s counsel within five (5) days after service of notice of the transfer order. Insofar as Defendants request an award of attorney fees against Plaintiff’s counsel for filing this motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 396b, subdivision (b), the Court lacks a basis to conclude that this action was filed here in bad faith, particularly because Plaintiff offered to stipulate to change of venue, even if the terms of that stipulation were not accepted; thus, Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu’s Request for Attorney Fees is DENIED.

Case Number: 25STCV36594    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu’s Motion to Transfer Venue to Santa Clara County is GRANTED.

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 399, all required costs and fees of transfer shall be paid by Plaintiff Andrea Berry and Plaintiff’s counsel within five (5) days after service of notice of the transfer order.

Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu to give notice.

Plaintiff Andrea Berry (“Plaintiff”) alleges in the complaint that venue is proper in Los Angeles because the acts giving rise to her claims occurred in Los Angeles, and she worked in Los Angeles County. (Compl. ¶ 9.) Defendants’ evidence shows that all Defendants are residents of Santa Clara County. (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶¶ 5-7.) 

Moreover, the present action is one properly classified as a “mixed action.” “In a mixed action, a plaintiff alleges two or more causes of action each of which is governed by a different venue statute. Or, two or more defendants are named who are subject to different venue standards.” (Brown v. Superior Court(1984) 37 Cal.3d 477, 488.) “In cases with mixed causes of action, a motion for change of venue must be granted on the entire complaint if the defendant is entitled to a change of venue on any one cause of action.” (Ibid.)

Plaintiff has alleged three causes of action under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), and Code of Civil Procedure section 12965, subdivision (a)(4), states that such claims “shall be brought in a county in which the department has an office, in a county in which unlawful practices are alleged to have been committed, in the county in which records relevant to the alleged unlawful practices are maintained and administered, in the county in which the person claiming to be aggrieved would have worked or would have had access to public accommodation, but for the alleged unlawful practices, in the county of the defendant’s residence or principal office, or, if the civil action includes class or group allegations on behalf of the department, in any county in the state.” Plaintiff alleges that she worked remotely in Los Angeles, even though Defendants were based in Santa Clara, such that Los Angeles is the proper venue. The FEHA claims, however, are based on conduct that occurred via remote communication, in Las Vegas, and in Texas. (Compl. ¶¶ 83-97.) Insofar as Plaintiff argues that Defendant Liu may be personally liable for the FEHA claims, she has not alleged those claims against him, focusing instead on “corporate defendants,” which the Court must assume does not include an individual defendant like Liu. 

Plaintiff chose to bring this action against both corporate defendants and an individual defendant, and it is well established that “when a plaintiff brings an action against several defendants, both individual and corporate, in a county in which none of the defendants reside, an individual defendant has the right to change venue to the county of his or her residence,” even if the corporate defendant may be sued elsewhere. (Brown v. Superior Court, supra, 37 Cal.3d at p. 484, fn. 6.) The Court finds that Santa Clara was the proper venue for the FEHA claims given Defendants’ residence, and Plaintiff has not shown a basis to keep this action in Los Angeles. Further, Defendants’ motion papers make clear that 31 out of 37 employees of the corporate entities reside in Santa Clara (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶ 17), and the documents are located in Alameda County (Mot., Liu Decl. ¶ 11). For these reasons, the Court finds it is proper to transfer this action to Santa Clara County. Accordingly, Defendants Sliver VR Technologies, Inc., Theta Labs, Inc., and Mitchell Liu’s Motion to Transfer Venue to Santa Clara County is GRANTED. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 399, all required costs and fees of transfer shall be paid by Plaintiff Andrea Berry and Plaintiff’s counsel within five (5) days after service of notice of the transfer order.

Case Number: 26SMCV00693    Hearing Date: July 15, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Jessica L. Moran and Plante Huguenin Lebovic Kahn LLP’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant/Cross-Complainant BMB Commercial Corp. is GRANTED.

Counsel has asserted a valid reason for withdrawal, i.e., the attorney handling this case retired from the firm, and no other attorney at the firm is available to continue the representation of Defendant/Cross-Complainant BMB Commercial Corp. in this action. (Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(10).) Counsel has provided the required forms under rule 3.1362 of the California Rules of Court and served the clients and opposing parties with the same. There being good cause to grant counsel’s request to withdraw, Jessica L. Moran and Plante Huguenin Lebovic Kahn LLP’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel for Defendant/Cross-Complainant BMB Commercial Corp. is GRANTED. The ruling granting counsel’s motion to withdraw shall become effective upon filing of proof service of the ruling upon Defendant/Cross-Complainant BMB Commercial Corp. Defendant/Cross-Complainant BMB Commercial Corp. is reminded that a corporation cannot represent itself in litigation and must be represented by licensed counsel. (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1145.)
