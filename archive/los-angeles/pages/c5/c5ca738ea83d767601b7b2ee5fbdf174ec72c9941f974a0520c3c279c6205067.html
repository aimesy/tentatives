            For the reasons stated below, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint.

Background

This is a motor vehicle negligence case. Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant is Jabari Whitfield. Defendants are Karen and Ulises Arias. Plaintiff alleges that on March 8, 2024, he was driving through an intersection when Defendants negligently made a left-hand-turn, crashing their vehicle into Plaintiff. At the time of the accident, Defendants had a passenger in their car – Angel Sandoval.

On January 3, 2025, Plaintiff filed suit against Defendants, alleging motor vehicle and general negligence.

On February 13, 2025, Defendants, along with their passenger, Angel Sandoval, filed a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff, alleging that Plaintiff was the negligent driver, and injured all three of them when he ran a red light through the intersection.

Now, Plaintiff moves for leave to file a Cross-Complaint against Cross-Complainants Karen Arias, Ulises Arias, and Angel Sandoval, alleging: 1) indemnity, 2) apportionment of fault, 3) comparative fault, and 4) declaratory relief.

            Discussion

Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant moves under CCP § 426.50 for leave to file a cross-complaint against Defendants for indemnity, apportionment of fault, and comparative fault. (Mot. for Leave, p. 2:2-8.)

Under CCP § 426.50, “[a] party who fails to plead a cause of action subject to the requirements of this article, whether through oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause, may apply to the court for leave to amend his pleading, or to file a cross-complaint, to assert such cause at any time during the course of the action. The court, after notice to the adverse party, shall grant, upon such terms as may be just to the parties, leave to amend the pleading, or to file the cross-complaint, to assert such cause if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith.” (CCP § 426.50 [emphasis added].)

            Under this statute the Court must grant leave to file a cross-complaint, so long as the party who failed to plead acted in good faith. Here, Plaintiff asserts that, as a result of  counsel’s excusable neglect, they failed to file this proposed cross-complaint when they answered the first Cross-Complaint. (Mot. for Leave, p. 4:7-8.) Plaintiff also argues that this failure was in good faith, as, at the time they filed their Answer to the Cross-Complaint, they did not have all the facts – namely, the traffic collision report and the deposition testimony of cross-complainants. (Id. p. 5:12-14.) The court agrees that there is no evidence Plaintiff acted in bad faith in bringing this proposed Cross-Complaint.

The Court finds no evidence that granting this motion will prejudice Cross-Complainants. First, this motion is unopposed. (Notice of Non-Opposition, 7/31/26.) Second, the trial date for this case is scheduled is not until  November 23, 2026. Third, the proposed Cross-Complaint only raises claims relating to the levels of fault in the same underlying accident. (Foster Decl. Ex. A, p. 1.) Therefore, it is unlikely that additional discovery will be necessary if this motion is granted.  The Court finds there is no evidence that this amendment will prejudice Cross-Complainants.

For these reasons, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint.

            IT IS SO ORDERED.

             For the reasons stated below, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion to Amend Judgment to Correct Name of Judgment Debtor.

Background

This is a loan repayment case. Plaintiff is Jason Franco, and Defendants are RCFO, LLC, and its owner Alex Camacho. On November 24, 2023, Plaintiff Jason Franco loaned RFCO $200,000 at a 12% interest rate. Defendant Alex Camacho was the guarantor of that loan. The loan came due on June 24, 2024. Plaintiff demanded repayment, and Defendants have yet to pay.

On July 31, 2025, this Court granted default judgment against Defendants RFCO, LLC and Alex Camacho. Plaintiff was awarded $243,992.40.

Now, Plaintiff moves to amend this default judgment to correct Defendant Alex Camacho’s name to Alejandro Jose Camacho.

            Discussion

Plaintiff moves to amend this judgment to correct a clerical error with the default judgment. (Mot. to Amend, p. 4:3-9.)

Under CCP § 473(d), “[t]he court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (CCP § 473(d).)

Here, Plaintiff states that Defendant Alex Camacho used this name to do business, but his true and correct name is Alejandro Jose Camacho. (Janssen Decl. ¶¶ 2-4.) In support of this argument, Plaintiffs provide a copy of the California Secretary of State Statement of Information for Defendant RFCO, LLC. (Janssen Decl. Ex. A.) This document has “Alejandro Jose Camacho” listed as its manager and CEO of RFCO, LLC. (Ibid.) Plaintiff also provides a copy of the original promissory note, which is signed by “Alex Camacho” in his capacity as CEO of Defendant RFCO, LLC. This evidence establishes that named Defendant Alex Camacho’s true and correct name is Alejandro Jose Camacho.

Under California law, “a court may at any time amend its judgment so that the latter will properly designate the real defendants.” (Mirabito v. San Francisco Dairy Co. (1935) 8 Cal.App.2d 54, 57.) Thus, this Court can amend the underlying judgment to correct Defendant’s name.

This motion is unopposed. Thus, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion to Amend Judgment.

Conclusion

The Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion to Amend Judgment.

            IT IS SO ORDERED.

             The Court CONTINUES this motion to September 14, 2026. The parties are ordered to meet and confer in-person or by telephone in a good faith attempt to resolve or narrow the issues raised by this demurrer.  

Background

This is a commercial landlord/tenant dispute. Defendant Jimhown Kwok is the owner and operator of Defendant Yummy Donuts, Inc., a small donut shop located at 7555 Balboa Boulevard. In 2012, Defendants entered into a 10-year commercial lease with Plaintiffs Tanya Kalaydjian, Nadya Donikian, Sona Dagliyan Survivors’ Trust, and Sona Dagliyan Living Trust.

On October 1, 2022, Defendants exercised their right to extend their lease by an additional ten years. According to Plaintiffs, the extension option in the lease stated that the new rent, “shall be the prevailing market rate, but in no case less than the rent paid in the last term of the lease, which increases measured using the cost-of-living index, CPI-U for Los Angeles, Riverside, and Orange County.”

The parties now dispute exactly how much Defendants owe in yearly rent. Since October, 2022, Defendants have paid $5,200 per year in rent. Meanwhile, Plaintiffs contend that Defendants owe upwards of $7,200 a year.

On October 23, 2025, Plaintiffs filed suit against Defendants for breach of contract and requested declaratory relief.

On April 7, 2026, Defendants (herein Cross-Complainants) Yummy Donuts, Inc. and Jimhorn Boon Kwok filed a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiffs, alleging seven causes of action: 1) fraudulent inducement; 2) negligent misrepresentation; 3) breach of written contract; 4) conversion; 5) IIED; 6) declaratory relief; and 7) violation of BPC § 17200.

Now, Plaintiffs demur to all seven causes of action alleged in Cross-Complainants’ Cross-Complaint, asserting that they all fail to allege sufficient facts.

            Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, the demurring party shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (CCP § 430.41(a) [emphasis added].) A declaration must be filed with a demurrer regarding the results of the meet and confer process. (CCP § 430.41(a)(3) [emphasis added].)

Here, Plaintiffs/Cross-Defendants provide the declaration of attorney Richard Kolber, who states only that, “[a]fter reviewing the Cross-Complaint, I sent Cross-Complainants’ counsel a meet and confer communication stating that the causes of action asserted therein were deficient, and I asked counsel to withdraw or amend the claims. Cross-Complainants’ counsel stated he would not amend the Cross- Complaint or withdraw the deficient allegations and causes of action.” (Kolber Decl. ¶ 2 [emphasis added].)

This declaration is inadequate to meet Cross-Defendants’ meet and confer obligations. Cross-Defendants appear to have only sent a single email, and make no showing of a conversation in person or by telephone.

The Court believes that a robust meet and confer is a crucial part of litigation process, as it obligates the parties to communicate and make a genuine effort to narrow the scope of their dispute. Thus, the Court CONTINUES this motion September 14, 2026. The parties are ordered to properly meet and confer in-person or by telephone and discuss in good faith the issues raised by the demurrer. If the parties can resolve their differences, the parties shall take the hearing on the demurrer off calendar.

Conclusion

The Court CONTINUES the hearing on the demurrer to September 14, 2026. The parties are ordered to properly meet and confer in-person or by telephone for at least one hour. 

            IT IS SO ORDERED.
