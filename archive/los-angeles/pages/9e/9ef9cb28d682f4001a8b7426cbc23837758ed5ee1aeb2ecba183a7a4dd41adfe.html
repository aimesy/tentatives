TENTATIVE RULING

August 6, 2026

23SMCV04492 (Van Vu v. Dar)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

 

            On September 21, 2023, plaintiff Hoang Van Vu (Plaintiff) filed the complaint against defendant Murtaza Iqbal Dar (Defendant).

 

            On June 6, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Strike the Answer of Defendant and to Impose Terminating Sanctions. The Court summarized Defendant’s conduct in the Order Granting Plaintiff’s Motion to Strike Answer of Defendant Murtaza Iqbal Dar and to Impose Terminating Sanctions as follows:

 

Defendant repeated his claim that he has sent documents to the Court, complained that he had ‘wasted two hours’ on his day off because he appeared for the hearing remotely at 8:30 am. Regarding the Plaintiff's motion and the Court's OSC, Defendant did not file an opposition or response. Defendant simply repeated his argument that he should not have to respond to discovery or appear in Court because ‘thirteen months ago he offered to pay the plaintiff $10,000 a month and that should have resolved the case, but the Plaintiff's counsel never responded.

 

(6/6/25 Minute Order, Ex. J).

 

            On December 4, 2025, the Judgment was entered.

           

INSTANT MOTION

 

             On March 12, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion to set aside the Judgment. Plaintiff filed an opposition, and Defendant filed a reply.

 

DISCUSSION

 

            “The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or the party’s legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against the party through the party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 473, subd. (b).)

            Here, Defendant seeks an order vacating the Judgment entered in this action on December 4, 2025. Defendant contends that the Judgment should be set aside because it “arose from procedural misunderstanding by a self-represented litigant rather than intentional refusal to participate in the litigation process.” (Mtn., 3:5-6).

            The Court finds no basis to set aside/vacate the Judgment. The Court is not persuaded by Defendant’s argument that there was a “procedural misunderstanding of discovery procedures” because Defendant was self-represented. (See Burnete v. La Casa Dana Apartments (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1262, 1267-1271 [Parties are not entitled to relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, merely based upon being a self-represented litigant].) Defendant’s conduct in repeatedly failing to fulfill his discovery obligations or comply with the Court’s orders does not constitute mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect within the meaning of Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b).

            Accordingly, Defendant’s motion to set aside/vacate is DENIED.


Case Number: 24SMCV04064    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: O

TENTATIVE RULING

August 6, 2026

24SMCV04064 (Hunter v. Israels)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            On August 21, 2024, plaintiff James Hunter (Plaintiff) filed the Complaint against defendant Sam Israels (Defendant), alleging four causes of action: battery; assault; false imprisonment; and interference with Plaintiff’s civil rights under the Bane Act.

 

            On May 6, 2025, the Court ruled on Defendant’s anti-SLAPP motion, granting in part, and denying in part.

 

            On January 9, 2026, this Court entered a minute order awarding Defendant $30,560.42 in attorney’s fees pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16(c).

           

INSTANT MOTIONS

 

             On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to “lift stay, reopen discovery, and set trial date.” No opposition has been filed.

 

            On February 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for “Stay of Enforcement (Stay of Execution) of January 9, 2026 Attorney Fee Order; Request for Waiver or Reduction of Undertaking.” No opposition has been filed.

 

            On March 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for “Protective Order Regarding Post-Judgment Enforcement Discovery; Request for Interim Stay and Order Vacating April 16, 2026 Inspection Demand.” No opposition has been filed.

 

DISCUSSION

 

A.    Motion to Lift Stay, Reopen Discovery, and Set Trial

 

            Plaintiff seeks orders to lift the stay, reopen discovery, and set a trial date. The Court finds no basis for the relief requested. With respect to a stay, Plaintiff argues that “fundamental fairness demands lifting the stay” and cites only the Court’s inherent authority to provide for the orderly conduct of proceedings under Code of Civil Procedure Section 128(a)(3). This does not adequately support Plaintiff’s position. With respect to discovery, Plaintiff generally cites Code of Civil Procedure section 2024.050 without providing a valid basis for the relief requested or addressing subdivisions (b)(1)-(4). (See Code Civ. Proc., 2024.050.) Lastly, with respect to the trial date, Plaintiff cites California Rules of Court, rule 3.1332, which pertains to the continuance of a trial date, which is not what Plaintiff is requesting.

 

            Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED.

 

B.     Motion for Stay of Enforcement of January 9, 2026 Fee Order

 

            Plaintiff requests orders staying enforcement of the January 9, 2026 order awarding attorney’s fees and waiving, or reducing undertaking. Plaintiff cites Code of Civil Procedure sections 128, 916, 917.1, 918, and 995.240. Plaintiff also contends that the Court has discretion to waive the undertaking for an indigent litigant under Sarkany v. West (2022) 82 Cal.App.5th 801. The Court finds that a stay is appropriate with respect to the January 9, 2026, order because Plaintiff has filed an appeal regarding same. (See Notice of Appeal filed 2/23/26; Code Civ. Proc., § 916(a) [“the perfecting of an appeal stays proceedings in the trial court upon the judgment or order appealed from or upon the matters embraced therein or affected thereby, including enforcement of the judgment or order, but the trial court may proceed upon any other matter embraced in the action and not affected by the judgment or order.”].) Since Plaintiff requests a reduction of the undertaking in the alternative, the Court does not address Plaintiff’s arguments regarding same.

 

            Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is GRANTED.

 

C.    Motion for Protective Order, Stay, and Order Vacating April 16, 2026 Judgment Regarding the Debtor Inspection Demand

 

            Plaintiff seeks an order staying enforcement discovery and vacating the scheduled inspection date (April 16, 2026) pending determination of Plaintiff’s Motion to Stay Execution and appellate proceedings concerning the attorney-fee order. The Court finds no basis for the relief requested by Plaintiff. Plaintiff generally cites Code of Civil Procedure sections 2017.020, 2030.090, 2031.060, 708.020, 708.030. Plaintiff has not shown that the burden, expense, or intrusiveness of the discovery clearly outweighs the likelihood that the information sought will lead to admissible evidence. Nor has Plaintiff shown unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden and expense. Plaintiff’s general privacy arguments are unsupported. Lastly, Plaintiff moves to “vacate” an inspection demand set for April 16, 2026, pending determination of Plaintiff’s motion to stay execution and appellate proceedings concerning the attorney-fee order. This request is moot given that the date of inspection has passed. Plaintiff also fails to provide any valid basis for the relief, and a motion to vacate is not the appropriate procedure.

 

            Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED.


Case Number: 24SMCV04490    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: O

TENTATIVE RULING

August 6, 2026         

24SMCV04490 (United Security Forces, LLC v. Security Industry Specialists, Inc.)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            On September 17, 2024, plaintiff United Security Forces, LLC (Plaintiff or USF) filed the complaint against defendant Security Industry Specialists, Inc. (Defendant or SIS) alleging four causes of action for breach of contract; account stated; open book account; and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

 

            On April 1, 2026, Defendant filed the cross-complaint against Plaintiff alleging two causes of action for breach of contract and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

 

            Trial is set for July 12, 2027.

                       

INSTANT MOTION

 

             On June 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for leave to file a first amended complaint (FAC). Defendant filed an opposition, and Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

             The Court finds that Plaintiff has satisfied the meet and confer requirement. (Mesa Decl. ¶ 15.)

 

DISCUSSION

 

            “A court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).) The court’s discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendments of the pleadings. (Nestle v. City of Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 939.)¿“If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend and where the refusal also results in a party being deprived of the right to assert a meritorious cause of action or a meritorious defense, it is not only error but an abuse of discretion.” (Morgan v. Superior Court (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.) Courts may grant a motion to amend as late as the time of trial and even postpone the trial if necessary to the furtherance of justice. (Honig v. Financial Corp. of America (1992) 6 Cal. App. 4th 960, 965.)

 

            Under Rule 3.1324, subdivision (a) of the California Rules of Court, a motion to amend a pleading shall (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) state what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, subd. (a).)

 

            Under Rule 3.1324, subdivision (b) of the California Rules of Court, a separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324, subd. (b).)

 

            Here, in support of the instant motion for leave to file a FAC, Plaintiff has provided the declaration of counsel, David D. Mesa (Mesa). Attached to the Mesa Declaration is a copy of the proposed FAC. (See Mesa Decl., ¶ 2, Ex. A.) Mesa explains that the FAC seeks to accomplish five objectives:

(a) add Apple Inc. (“Apple”) as a defendant;

(b) add four new causes of action against Apple (quantum meruit, unjust enrichment, intentional interference with contractual relations, and negligent interference with prospective economic advantage);

(c) update the damages allegations to reflect the full scope of unpaid invoices, which have increased from the originally pled $3,244,973.98 to in excess of $6,500,000;

(d) add factual allegations concerning SIS’s invoice practices and Apple’s involvement in the billing and payment relationship; and

(e) expand the fictitiously named Doe defendants from Does 1 through 20 to Does 1 through 40, with Does 1 through 20 directed at the existing claims against SIS and Does 21 through 40 directed at the new claims against Apple.

(Id. ¶ 2.)

            Plaintiff states that, when the Complaint was filed, it did not know the full extent of Apple’s direct involvement in causing or contributing to USF’s nonpayment. Mesa declares that, “[a]t the time USF filed the original Complaint in September 2024, USF knew that SIS was not paying its invoices but did not know the full extent of Apple’s involvement in and contribution to the nonpayment. USF’s contractual relationship was with SIS, and USF initially believed this was a straightforward contract dispute between USF and SIS. [¶] Beginning in late 2024 and continuing through 2025, USF obtained documents and information through investigation, discovery and its subpoena to Apple that revealed the full scope of Apple’s involvement.” (Id. ¶ 8, 9.)

 

            Plaintiff argues that the amendment will not prejudice Defendant because trial is not set to begin until July 12, 2027, discovery is ongoing, no dispositive motions have been filed or heard, and there is ample time for Defendant and Apple to conduct any additional discovery necessitated by the amendment and to prepare for trial without delaying the trial date.

 

            In opposition, Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s delay in seeking amendment is unexplained and that Plaintiff groups together investigation, discovery, subpoena responses, invoice summits, and alleged operational directives without identifying when any particular document was received, when any witness supplied material information, or why the pleading could not have been amended months earlier.

 

            Defendant further argues that the proposed amendment would also include claims that are facially questionable, including a quantum meruit theory despite Plaintiff’s allegation that Defendant, not Apple, requested and contracted for the services and interference claims premised largely on Apple’s exercise of rights under its own contract with Defendant.

 

            In reply, Plaintiff reiterates that the claims against Apple were not known at the time of filing and continued to emerge through early 2026. Plaintiff also argues that Defendant has failed to show prejudice. Lastly, Plaintiff states that Defendant’s arguments concerning the merits of the claims are premature.

 

            The Court finds that leave to amend is appropriate. Plaintiff has complied with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324 by providing the Mesa Declaration, which attaches a redlined copy of the proposed amendment and specifies the effect of the amendment. (See Mesa Decl. ¶ 2-9.) The declaration explains that the amendment is necessary and proper, “because they will allow Plaintiff to recover all damages it has suffered from the wrongful acts of SIS and Apple in a single action, rather than requiring Plaintiff to initiate a separate proceeding against Apple on the same set of operative facts.” (Id. ¶ 3.) Additionally, counsel attests that the facts giving rise to the amendment were not discovered until after the complaint was filed, after conducting discovery. (Id. ¶ 11-14.)

 

            The Court finds no prejudice exists. As Plaintiff points out, trial is not set to begin until July 12, 2027, discovery is ongoing, and no dispositive motions have been filed or heard.

 

            The Court notes that it does not address the merits or the sufficiency of the pleading of the new claims. Defendant’s arguments concerning those matters are premature at this stage.

 

            Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file a FAC is GRANTED. Plaintiff has thirty (30) days to amend.
