DEPARTMENT I LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

If the parties wish to submit on the tentative ruling and avoid a court appearance on the matter, the moving party must contact the opposing party and all other parties who have appeared in the action and confirm that each will submit on the tentative ruling. Please call the court no later than 4:30 p.m. on the court day before the hearing, leave a message with the court clerk at (310) 260-3629 advising her that all parties will submit on the tentative ruling and waive hearing, and finally, serve notice of the Court's ruling on all parties entitled to receive service. If any party declines to submit on the tentative ruling, then no telephone call is necessary, and all parties should appear at the hearing.

Case Number: 24SMCV03769    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: I

This is an application by plaintiff to continue the trial date.  The trial had been set for September 28, 2026.  But on June 18, 2026, the court on its own motion continued the matter to October 7, 2026—a week later.  The court asked the parties to let it know quickly if this posed a problem.  Plaintiff’s counsel has a conflict on October 8.  The court, frankly would have had more sympathy for this had it been brought to the court’s attention later in June or July.  The court does not know why plaintiff waited two months to say anything.

 

Plaintiff also notes that defendants are allegedly not providing discovery and that motions to compel have been filed.  Those motions are not set to be heard until October 16, 2026, at the earliest.

 

The court is a bit troubled.  The case was filed back in February 2023.  The court is not sure why it is that discovery is up against the deadline.  The court also still wonders about the delay in letting the court know of the conflict.

 

Even so, the court will inquire.  If the court can accommodate the parties, it will do so.  The trial date was changed on the court’s own motion to accommodate the court’s own travel plans.  The court will not prejudice the parties or destroy already-set plans for that.  Judges are entitled to a vacation now and again, but so are attorneys.

 


Case Number: 26SMCV03065    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: I

The court will GRANT the application to extend the time to file an SMS until August 31, 2026.  The current deadline is at least arguably about to pass, but plaintiff filed a FAC on August 9.  Frankly the requested relief is so minor that the court was tempted just to say GRANTED and be done with it.  However, the court will provide a bit of explanation.

 

Plaintiff opposes the motion on the basis that there is no exigency and that any emergency there might have been is a self-inflicted wound in that defendants could have filed earlier if they were so inclined.  Plaintiff also contends that there is nothing new under the sun in the FAC, so that is not really a good reason to extend the time.

 

Here it the court’s view.  The court often sees such motions filed after the 60 day time period.  And they are often granted.  Whether to consider an untimely SMS is a matter of discretion, not jurisdiction.  Where an SMS has merit, the court often will exercise its discretion to consider the matter, at least if the untimeliness is not over long.  (For example, if it had been 6 months rather than two weeks, the court might deny the SMS out of hand.)  And the court finds it commendable that defendants seek permission rather than forgiveness.  So the court’s general view is to allow the extra time.

 

The matter has also been somewhat complicated by the preliminary injunction issue.  Plaintiff seeks a preliminary injunction to compel defendants to lift the ban on her being a patron at certain businesses at the hotel.  Defendants oppose that injunction.  To that end, there has been some discovery and a lot of briefing.  That is a confounding factor.  An early SMS would have frozen discovery, an issue with which the court would have to deal in the context of a preliminary injunction motion.  The court will hardly fault defendants for not going that route.  Second, defendants’ time was of necessity divided.  In the typical case, very little is going on for the first 60 days of the case, and defendants can consider whether to file an SMS at their leisure.  That was just not the case here.  And finally, there is the FAC.  The court has not studied the FAC with great care, and it is unclear whether the FAC would re-start the clock as to some of the grounds for the SMS.  Better not to have to deal with that additional procedural issue.

 

On balance, the court sees no harm in granting the application.  The court also notes that the exigency is that if this were brought on noticed motion, defendants would have to either file the SMS timely (thereby mooting the motion) or take the risk that the court will strike it on the procedural ground.  That is enough exigency for this a timing request.

 

The application is GRANTED.  Defendants have until August 31, 2026, to file an SMS.  Opposition and reply will be per Code.  The court does not intend to hear argument.
