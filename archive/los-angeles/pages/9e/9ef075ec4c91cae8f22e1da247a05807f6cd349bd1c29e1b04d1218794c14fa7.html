DEPARTMENT P LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23NWCV02293    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: P

MASIAH SMITH, ET AL. v. DEON QUININE, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  23NWCV02293 / 23STCV20707

HEARING:  08/13/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#4

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff Maria Liliana Ramirez’s motion to set aside the voluntary dismissal of City is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Maria Liliana Ramirez (Plaintiff) moves to set aside the voluntary dismissal of Defendant City of Los Angeles (City) and reinstate City as a defendant.

 

Background

 

On July 26, 2023, Plaintiff Masiah Smith, a minor through her guardian ad litem Jalia Stephens filed a personal injury action against Defendants Deon Quinine, Los Angeles Metropolitan Transit Authority (Metro), Maria Lilliana Ramirez, and Does 1 through 10. The form complaint alleges that on December 22, 2022, at Long Beach Ave., 10 feet South of Vernon Ave, Defendants collided at a railroad intersection. The complaint asserts a single cause of action for motor vehicle.

 

On August 29, 2023, in case no. 23STCV20707, Plaintiff Maria Liliana Ramirez filed a personal injury action against Metro, City of Los Angeles (City), County of Los Angeles, Deon Quinine, and Does 1 through 20. The complaint alleges that on December 22, 2022, on Long Beach Avenue & E. Vernon Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90058, “[a]fter waiting a proper amount of time, Plaintiff's vehicle proceeded into the intersection of E. Vernon Avenue, when the Metro Rail owned by Defendants [Metro], City of Los Angeles, and/or County of Los Angeles, and driven by Defendant Deon Quinine, and caused a collision with Plaintiff's vehicle, resulting in injuries and damages to Plaintiff.” The complaint asserts two causes of action: (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

 

On November 1, 2023, Metro filed a cross-complaint against Maria Lillliana Ramirez, Roes 1 to 30, and Roe Companies 31 through 60, inclusive. The cross-complaint asserts five causes of action: (1) equitable indemnity, partial or comparative indemnity, (2) declaratory relief, (3) equitable comparative contribution, (4) apportionment of fault based on comparative negligence and/or comparative fault, and (5) property damage.

 

On May 28, 2024, in case no. 23STCV20707, Plaintiff filed a Request for Dismissal to dismiss City without prejudice. The Court granted the Request for Dismissal on May 30, 2024.

 

On December 14, 2024, the complaint in case no. 23NWCV02293 was dismissed without prejudice. (Order, 12/19/24.)

 

On September 12, 2025, the Court granted Metro’s motion to consolidate this action with case no. 23STCV20707. This action was deemed the lead case.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by adding or striking out the name of any party, or by correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect; and may, upon like terms, enlarge the time for answer or demurrer. The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).)

 

Discretionary relief is available to “relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) An application for discretionary relief “shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed…and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) Mandatory relief is available “whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney's sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect…unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for relief from the voluntary dismissal of City on May 28, 2024, and to reinstate City as a defendant in this action. Plaintiff moves pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subd. (a)(1) and (b), Code of Civil Procedure section 389, and the Court’s inherent equitable powers. This motion is unopposed.

 

Plaintiff argues that City misled Plaintiff into voluntarily dismissing it by representing that City is not a proper defendant because it does not own or operate the Metro bus or rail services. (Rabi Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.)

 

Here, relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) is not available as it is untimely. A motion for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) “shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) Plaintiff moved to dismiss City on May 28, 2024, and the Court entered City’s dismissal on May 30, 2024. (Rabi Decl., ¶ 4, Exh. B.) Plaintiff moved for this relief over two years after the dismissal, well over the six-month deadline. The Court does not have authority to grant discretionary relief. (Aldrich v. San Fernando Valley Lumber Co. (1985) 170 Cal.App.3d 725, 735, n. 3 [“The six-month time limit for granting relief under section 473 is jurisdictional and relief cannot be granted under section 473 if the application for such relief is instituted more than six months after the entry of the judgment, order or proceeding from which relief is sought.”].)

 

Plaintiff alternatively moves for relief pursuant to the Court’s inherent powers. However, the Court does not have inherent authority to set aside a voluntary dismissal. The Court’s jurisdiction to set aside a voluntary dismissal is limited to its jurisdiction under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). (Basinger v. Rogers & Wells (1990) 220 Cal.App.3d 16, 21-22.) “Numerous cases categorically state the trial court “ ‘ “is without jurisdiction to act further in the action” (citation)’ once a voluntary dismissal is filed.” (Id., at p. 21, n. 10 [“Section 473, not ‘inherent power,’ governs motions to vacate voluntary dismissals entered within six months of the motion.”].)

 

Plaintiff cites to Kulchar v. Kulchar (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471-72 to argue that the Court can set aside the dismissal order on the basis of extrinsic fraud. “Under certain circumstances a court, sitting in equity, can set aside or modify a valid final judgment.” (Id., at p. 470.) “Extrinsic fraud usually arises when a party is denied a fair adversary hearing because he has been ‘deliberately kept in ignorance of the action or proceeding, or in some other way fraudulently prevented from presenting his claim or defense.’” (Ibid.) While Kulchar does not arise out of a voluntary dismissal, the Court considers this argument.

 

Plaintiff contends that City’s representation in its demurrer created a false impression that City had no involvement in the subject incident and did not control any relevant infrastructure at the collision location. The meet and confer letter, provided by Plaintiff, shows that Defendant stated that “[t]he City does not own, manage, operate, or otherwise control bus or rail services provided by LACMTA, and did not own, manage, operate, or otherwise control bus or rail services provided by LACMTA or LACMTA's premises on the date of the alleged incident upon which this lawsuit is premised.” (Rabi Decl., ¶ 3, Exh. A.) Plaintiff contends that through subsequent discovery, Plaintiff learned that City controlled traffic signals at the relevant intersection.

 

Here, the Court does not find that this representation constitutes extrinsic fraud. Rather, the Court finds that this constitutes intrinsic fraud. “Courts deny relief… when the fraud or mistake is ‘intrinsic’; that is, when it ‘goes to the merits of the prior proceedings, which should have been guarded against by the plaintiff at that time.’” (Id., at p. 472-73; see also Heyman v. Franchise Mortgage Acceptance Corp. (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 921, 926 [“Where the defrauded party failed to take advantage of liberal discovery policies to fully investigate his or her claim, any fraud is intrinsic fraud.”].) In Kulchar, relied upon by Plaintiff, the Supreme Court of California reversed an order granting relief when a party and her attorney did not investigate facts and chose to rely on an opposing party’s representations. (Id., at p. 474.) Similarly, here, Plaintiff does not explain why she could not have verified Defendant’s representations regarding the bus or rail services. Therefore, even if the Court is permitted to use its inherent powers to set aside a voluntary dismissal, under the facts presented here, Plaintiff does not establish extrinsic fraud.

 

To the extent that Plaintiff moves for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a)(1), this is an improper basis for relief. A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (a)(1) is a motion for leave to amend which is an independent motion with separate requirements.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to set aside the voluntary dismissal of City is DENIED.

 


Case Number: 23NWCV03581    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: P

SEDRIC MOORE v. L’ABRI MANAGEMENT, INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.: 23NWCV03581

HEARING:  08/13/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#5

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Defendants L’Abri Management, Inc., Ronald A, Kolar, and Edgl Has’s motion for terminating sanctions is GRANTED. This action is dismissed with prejudice.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendants L’Abri Management, Inc., Ronald A, Kolar, and Edgl Has (Defendants) move for an order issuing terminating sanctions in its favor and against Plaintiff Sedric Moore (Plaintiff).

 

Background

 

On November 6, 2023, Plaintiff filed this action against Defendants L’Abri Management, Inc. (L’Abri Management); Ronald A, Kolar, individually, and as CEO of Defendant; Edgl Has, Vivianna Escamilla, and Does 1 to 10. This action arises out of alleged instances of harassment, discrimination, and hostile living conditions faced by Plaintiff during Plaintiff’s tenancy at 2525 Carson Street, Unit No. 6, Lakewood, CA 90712, County of Los Angeles (Subject Property). (Complaint, ¶¶ 23-34.) The complaint asserts the following causes of action: (1) Breach of Warranty of Quiet Enjoyment, (2) Negligence, (3) Constructive Eviction, (4) Harassment, and (5) Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress.

 

On February 18, 2025, the Court granted L’Abri Management’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories, set one, special interrogatories, set one, and requests for production of documents, set one (Order, 2/18/25).

 

On October 9, 2025, the Court granted L’Abri Management’s motion to compel further responses to form interrogatories, set one, special interrogatories, set one, and requests for production of documents, set one (10/9/2025 Order).

 

This is a continuation. On February 26, 2026, this motion first came on for hearing. At the hearing, “[t]he Court confirm[ed] that Plaintiff’s counsel, Gary Saunders, has been ineligible to practice law since 11/30/2025.” (Order, 2/26/26.) Plaintiff appeared to request a continuance to obtain new counsel, and the Court continued the hearing to May 28, 2026. (Order, 2/26/26.)

 

On May 28, 2026, this motion came on for hearing again. The Court continued this motion to today’s date at defense counsel’s request based on an issue with notice. (Order. 5/28/26.)

 

Legal Standard

 

If a party fails to comply with a court order compelling discovery responses or attendance at a deposition, the court may impose monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, subd. (g); 2030.290, subd. (c); 2023.030; R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486, 495.) Ultimate discovery sanctions are justified where there is a willful discovery order violation, a history of abuse, and evidence showing that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with discovery rules. (Van Sickle v. Gilbert (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1495, 1516.) “[A] penalty as severe as dismissal or default is not authorized where noncompliance with discovery is caused by an inability to comply rather than willfulness or bad faith.” (Brown v. Superior Ct. (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 701, 707.)

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (d), “[t]he court may impose a terminating sanction by one of the following orders:

 

(1) An order striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings of any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process.

(2) An order staying further proceedings by that party until an order for discovery is obeyed.

(3) An order dismissing the action, or any part of the action, of that party.

(4) An order rendering a judgment by default against that party.”

 

“The trial court may order a terminating sanction for discovery abuse ‘after considering the totality of the circumstances: [the] conduct of the party to determine if the actions were willful; the detriment to the propounding party; and the number of formal and informal attempts to obtain the discovery.’” (Los Defensores, Inc. v. Gomez (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 377, 390, quoting Lang v. Hochman (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 1225, 1246.)

 

Discussion

 

Defendants move for terminating sanctions against Plaintiff. Specifically, Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the 10/9/2025 Order and other violations of the Civil Discovery Act warrant an order of dismissal with prejudice in favor of Defendants.

 

This motion is unopposed. The Court notes that this motion has been continued twice. On February 26, 2026, when this motion first came on for hearing, Plaintiff appeared and confirmed that Plaintiff’s counsel, Gary Saunders, has been ineligible to practice law since November 30, 2025. (Order, 2/26/26.) The Court continued the motion to permit Plaintiff to retain new counsel. (Order, 2/26/26.) On May 28, 2026, when this motion came on for a second time, the Court continued the hearing again based on defense counsel’s representation that timely notice of the prior continuance was only provided to Plaintiff’s counsel and not plaintiff. (Order, 5/28/26.) The notice included a representation that Plaintiff was seeking new counsel and was to comply with all outstanding orders and discovery at least 30 days prior to the hearing. (Order, 5/28/26.) The Court then continued the hearing to today’s date and instructed the parties “to apprise the Court of any relevant updates via declaration or briefing prior to the hearing.” (Order, 5/28/26.)

 

There have been no new filings except a Notice of Non-Opposition filed by Defendants indicating that they have not received any opposition from Plaintiff as of August 4, 2026. The Court proceeds to rule on this motion.

 

Defendants recount the following: on February 8, 2024, Defendants served Plaintiff with initial written discovery. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 3.) On February 18, 2025, the Court granted Defendant’s motion to compel discovery responses to the subject discovery requests. (Order, 2/18/25.) On April 7, 2025, Plaintiff served untimely responses with objections to the subject requests, form interrogatories, set one, special interrogatories, set one, and requests for production, set one. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 7.) The majority of the responses said “[d]iscovery is continuing” and the objections were in violation of the 2/28/25 Order. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 7.) Defendants moved for further responses. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 11.) Defendants did not receive any responses to their meet and confer efforts and Plaintiff only opposed the motions the day before, blaming the failure to respond earlier on lack of communication and administrative staff turnover. (Hoffman Decl., ¶¶ 7-11.) This is the same justification Plaintiff provided for their initial motions to compel. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 11.)

 

During the October 9, 2025 hearing on the motions to compel further responses, “Wayne Quint appeared for counsel for record on behalf of Plaintiff. Mr. Quint informed the Court that counsel of record, Gary Saunders, was determined to be no longer eligible to practice law by the California State Bar. Mr. Quint stated that he would be working on the firm’s cases.” (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 12.) The Court granted L’Abri Management’s motions to compel further responses and ordered Plaintiff and his counsel to provide further responses pay sanctions in the amount of $2,620.00 within 30 days of the 10/9/25 Order. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 13.)

 

After Mr. Quint introduced himself to defense counsel via email, Mr. Quint did not respond to meet and confer efforts. (Hoffman Decl., ¶¶ 14-16.) Defendants emailed Mr. Quint, Mr. Saunders, firm staff, and their firm’s general litigation email address on December 8, 2025, stating that the further responses and monetary sanctions are overdue and providing an extension. (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 16.) Defense counsel “received a message from Microsoft Outlook stating that the email could not be sent to Mr. Quint’s email address, as it was inactive, or to Ms. Barreto’s email address, as it did not exist.” (Hoffman Decl., ¶ 17.) As of the date of this motion, Defendants have not received any communications from Plaintiff.

 

The Court finds that Defendants show Plaintiff engaged in repeated, willful violations of Court orders and the discovery process. The subject discovery has been pending for nearly two years, since April 30, 2024. Plaintiff responded with objections in violation of the 2/18/25 Order and failed to provide further responses in violation of the 10/9/25 Order. Willfulness is apparent through Plaintiff’s counsel’s failure to meet and confer and failure to comply with Court Orders, despite representing to the Court and opposing counsel that Plaintiff will comply. Despite claiming that Plaintiff’s firm experienced staffing issues in the late oppositions to prior discovery motions, Plaintiff failed to request any extensions from Defendants nor appraise the Court or Defendants of any difficulties.

 

Outside of Plaintiff’s counsel, Plaintiff has again failed to timely oppose this motion. It has been over five months since Plaintiff appeared in this Court, represented that he sought to retain new counsel, and was instructed to comply with the pending discovery orders. Moreover, the issuance of monetary sanctions has not influenced Plaintiff’s participation in discovery, as Plaintiff has also not paid any monetary sanctions.

 

“If a plaintiff wishes to avail himself of the California courts, he owes a duty to comply with its rules, such as the rules of discovery. To hold otherwise, would permit a litigant to disappear and not be obliged to comply with our discovery statutes.” (Cornwall v. Santa Monica Dairy Co. (1977) 66 Cal.App.3d 250, 253.)

 

Defendants’ motion for terminating sanctions is GRANTED. This action is dismissed with prejudice.

 

 


Case Number: 24NWCV00372    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: P

JAIME ROBLES, ET AL. v. PIH HEALTH WHITTIER, ET AL.

CASE NO.:  24NWCV00372 

HEARING:  08/13/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#6

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Petitioner Ethmeshell Escobar’s petition for approval of compromise of action is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Petitioner Ethmeshell Escobar (Petitioner) moves for approval of compromise of action.

 

Background

 

On February 1, 2024, Plaintiffs Jaime Robles (Claimant), a minor, by and through his Guardian Ad Litem, Ethmeshell Escobar; Ethmeshell Escobar, individually; and Rogelio Robles, individually (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed this medical malpractice action against Defendants PIH Health Whittier Hospital; PIH Health Women’s Center; Brent James Gray, M.D.; Jung M. Park, M.D.; John H. Sanchez, M.D.; Leslie M. Gonzalez, M.D.; and Does 1 to 250 (Defendants). The complaint alleges Defendants were negligent in the labor and delivery of Jamie Robles including “negligent failure to provide informed consent,…negligent failure to timely anesthetize, deliver, treat and care for minor Plaintiff’s intolerance of labor, as well as fetal distress causing profound neurological and bodily injuries” to Jamie Robles. (Complaint, ¶¶ 17-20.) The complaint also alleges Defendants were negligent in ignoring Ethmeshell Escobar’s requests for a cesarean section resulting in a delay in delivering Jamie Robles. (Complaint, ¶ 29.)

 

The complaint asserts four causes of action: (1) negligence as to Jamie Robles, (2) negligence as to Ethmeshell Escobar, (3) negligent infliction of emotional distress, and (4) loss of consortium.

 

On February 21, 2025, Plaintiffs filed Requests for Dismissal as to Jung M. Park, M.D. and John H. Sanchez, M.D. On July 21, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a Request for Dismissal as to Leslie M. Gonzalez, M.D.

 

On December 10, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case.

 

Legal Standard

 

A Petition for Compromise of Disputed Claim of Minor must conform with Code of Civil Procedure section 372 and Probate Code sections 2504 and 3600 et seq.  Such a petition “must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Moving papers should include the Petition of Guardian Ad Litem for Compromise of Disputed Claim of Minor (Petition) on Form MC-350 and an Order Approving Compromise of Minor’s Claim (Order) Form MC-351.

 

Discussion

 

Petitioner files this Petition on behalf of Claimant, a minor, age three, to approve this settlement. Petitioner provides a Petition on Form MC-350 and Proposed Order on Form MC-351. This Petition is unopposed.

 

          Allocation

 

PIH Health Whittier Hospital will pay $100,000 and Brent James Gray, M.D. will pay $400,000 to Plaintiffs in exchange for release of all claims, including wrongful death, Plaintiffs assert against them. (Pet., ¶ 10; Attach.,10c.) Of the total settlement proceeds, Defendants are paying $100,000 to Petitioner for her medical negligence and emotional distress claims, reflecting 20% of the total recovery. (Pet., ¶ 11.)

 

Claimant will receive $251,354.14. (Pet., ¶ 16.)

 

          Medical Expenses

 

The total medical expenses paid is $195,569.62. (Pet., ¶ 12a.) The Petition indicates that none of the medical expenses will be reimbursed from the proceeds. (Pet., ¶ 12a.) The Petition also indicates that there are no statutory or contractual liens for payment of Claimant’s medical expenses. (Pet., ¶ 12b.)

 

          Attorney’s Fees & Costs

 

The Petition seeks approval of $123,801.30 in attorney’s fees. (Pet., ¶ 13; Attach., 13a.) This represents 33%, as reflected in the retainer agreement signed by Petitioner. (Attach., 13b.) The Petition also seeks $24,844.56 in litigation expenses as supported in a client cost ledger. (Pet., ¶ 13.) The Petition indicates that Plaintiff’s counsel additionally expects to be paid $33,000 by Petitioner. (Pet., ¶ 17f.)

 

Based on the declaration of counsel and information provided in this Petition, the Court finds this amount reasonable.

 

          Disposition of Settlement Funds

 

The Petitioner requests that the disposition of the proceeds be invested in a single-premium deferred annuity, subject to withdrawal only on authorization of the Court. Petitioner provides the terms of the annuity stating that Prudential Assigned Settlement Service Corp. will provide for the following Periodic Payments to be made by The Prudential Insurance Company of America, rated A+ by A.M. Best. (Attach., 18b(3).)

 

          Additional Orders

 

The Petition also requests that an annuity for the benefit of Jaime Robles in the amount of $251,354.14 be promptly funded by Defendant Brent James Gray, M.D. with The Prudential Insurance Company of America, and that the balance of the settlement funds be paid within twenty days of Court approval of this Petition. Proof of Purchase of annuity to be filed with the Court thereafter.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds the Petition fair and reasonable.

 

Petitioner Ethmeshell Escobar’s petition for approval of minor’s compromise is APPROVED. The Court will sign the Proposed Order concurrently filed with the Petition. 

 

 


Case Number: 26NWCP00255    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: P

STATE BAR OF CALIFORNIA v. ALMAREZ, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 26NWCP00255 

HEARING: 08/13/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

State Bar of California’s verified petition for assumption of jurisdiction over John Pamintuan Alamarez Jr. is GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

This OSC Re: why the Court should not assume jurisdiction over Respondent’s practice is made pursuant to the Court’s ruling on July 7, 2026.

 

Background

 

On June 23, 2026, State Bar of California (State Bar) filed a verified petition (Petition) for assumption of jurisdiction over the unauthorized law practice of John Pamintuan Alamarez Jr. aka John P. Alamarez, John Alamarez, Domingo P. Alamarez, Domingo P. Alamarez Jr. dba Immigration Law Services, and Immigration Services Law Office (Respondent) and an injunction. On July 7, 2026, the Court granted State Bar’s ex parte application to set an OSC Re: why the Court should not assume jurisdiction over Respondent’s practice. The Court also granted interim orders for assumption of jurisdiction. (Order, 7/7/26.)

 

On July 10, 2026, State Bar filed Proofs of Service reflecting service of the Petition, ex parte application and related filing, and order granting the ex parte application upon Respondent via personal service and mail.

 

Legal Standard

 

Business and Professions Code section 6125 provides that “[n]o person shall practice law in California unless the person is an active licensee of the State Bar.” “Any person advertising or holding himself or herself out as practicing or entitled to practice law or otherwise practicing law who is not an active licensee of the State Bar, or otherwise authorized pursuant to statute or court rule to practice law in this state at the time of doing so, is guilty of a misdemeanor.” (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126, subd. (a).)

 

Business and Professions Code section 6126.3, subdivision (b) provides that: “The State Bar…may make application to the superior court…for assumption by the court of jurisdiction over the practice… the State Bar shall be permitted to intervene and to assume primary responsibility for conducting the action.” The application “shall be verified, and shall state facts showing all of the following:

 

(1) Probable cause to believe that the facts set forth in subdivision (a) of Section 6126 have occurred.

(2) The interest of the applicant.

(3) Probable cause to believe that the interests of a client or of an interested person or entity will be prejudiced if the proceeding is not maintained.” (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126.3, subd. (c).)

 

“The application shall be set for hearing, and an order to show cause shall be issued directing the person to show cause why the court should not assume jurisdiction over the practice as provided in this section.” (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126.3, subd. (d).)

 

“If the court finds that the facts set forth in subdivision (a) of Section 6126 have occurred and that the interests of a client or an interested person or entity will be prejudiced if the proceeding provided herein is not maintained, the court may make an order assuming jurisdiction over the person’s practice pursuant to this section. If the person to whom the order to show cause is directed does not appear, the court may make its order upon the verified application or upon such proof as it may require.” (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126.3, subd. (e).)

 

Discussion

 

State Bar petitioners for an order of assumption of jurisdiction over Respondent based on his unauthorized practice of law. This Petition is unopposed.

 

State Bar puts forth the declaration of Colie Dillon, the lead investigator for State Bar (Investigator Dillon), assigned to the complaint filed by Attorney Tajsha Thomas (Attorney Thomas) against Respondent. (Dillon Decl., ¶¶ 2-3.) Attorney Thomas’s complaint alleged that Attorney Thomas’s father passed away on September 25, 2024, and he left behind The Thomas Firm which is located at the same address that Respondent’s business is located now. (Dillon Decl., Exh. 1.) The complaint alleges Respondent induced Attorney Thomas to return to the firm, and once Attorney Thomas returned, she saw that Respondent was engaged in the unauthorized practice of law and his true intent in urging her to join the firm was so he could use her law license in carrying out his conduct. (Dillon Decl., Exh. 1.) Attorney Thomas alleged that she personally witnessed Respondent holding himself out as an attorney to clients and court clerks, providing legal advice, and collecting attorney’s fees. (Dillon Decl., Exh. 1.)

 

Investigator Dillon recounts his investigation of the complaint. Investigator Dillon searched the State Bar database of licensed California attorneys and found no licensed attorneys with Respondent’s name or aliases. (Dillon Decl., ¶ 25.) Investigator Dillon reviewed letters prepared by Respondent on letterhead stating “Divina Law Office” with a photo of Respondent listing him as "John P. Alamarez, JD Law Administrator" of the "Divina Law Office Immigration Law" located at "17127 Pioneer Blvd Suite E-1, Aliesia CA 90701" with the telephone number (562) 278-2475. (Emphasis in original.) The card also listed "Attorney Marianne B. Divina, ESQ Attorney-At-Law." (Dillon Decl., ¶ 6.) Investigator Dillon learned from Attorney Divina that she never authorized Respondent using her name and that she had previously employed and terminated Respondent. (Dillon Decl., ¶ 8.) Additionally, Investigator Dillon learned that the letter obtained by Investigator Dillon, which showed Respondent representing that his client was Allgelita Vinluan Labit and requesting the status on a survivor benefit claim that he had previously submitted her behalf, evidenced the unauthorized practice of law because Labit was never Respondent or Attorney Divina’s client, but rather, a client of the deceased lawyer Jack Richard Thomas. (Dillon Decl., ¶ 6.)

 

On May 8, 2026, Investigator Dillon was informed that Respondent may be associated with an out-of-state attorney Marlon Wijesekara (Attorney Wijesekara), licensed in New York and Washington D.C. (Dillon Decl., ¶¶ 10-11.) Investigator Dillon communicated with Attorney Wijesekara and learned that Respondent was using Attorney Wijesekara’s name without his consent and that he had never employed Respondent at any capacity. (Dillon Decl., Exh. 12.) Attorney Wijesekara told Respondent to remove all signage and to refrain from using his name. (Dillon Decl., Exh. 12.)

 

Investigator Dillon also discussed his investigation of the premises of Respondent’s business address. In June of 2026, a Google Maps search of the business showed signage stating "IMMIGRATION LAW THOMAS LAW OFFICE." (Dillon Decl., ¶ 17, Exh. 13.) Investigator Dillon received photographs of the business taken on April 15, 2026, showing the same signage with the name "THOMAS" substituted with the name "Wijesekara.” (Dillon Decl., Exh. 14.) In June of 2026, Investigator Sandra Hernandez and counsel Kyuhee Han conducted exterior-only site visits of the business address and confirmed that Respondent’s office is open, operating, and advertising immigration services to the public. (Hernandez Decl., ¶ 5; Han Decl., ¶ 5.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that State Bar establishes that Respondent engaged in the unauthorized practice of law. The practice of law “includes legal advice and counsel, and the preparation of legal instruments and contracts by which legal rights are secured, although such matter may or may not be pending in a court.” (State Bar of California v. Superior Court (1929) 207 Cal. 323, 335.) “ ‘The cases uniformly hold that the character of the act, and not the place where it is performed, is the decisive element, and if the application of legal knowledge and technique is required, the activity constitutes the practice of law.’” (Benninghoff v. Superior Court (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 61, 68.)

 

Here, State Bar shows that Respondent advertised himself as providing legal services and actually provided legal services to the public in California. Respondent conducted client consultations, communicated with parties on behalf of clients, and charged legal fees. The Court therefore finds probable cause to believe that Respondent advertised and held himself out as practicing law and provided immigration legal services. (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126, subd. (a).)

 

The Court further finds that the State Bar has an interest in protecting the public from the unauthorized practice of law. (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126.3, subd.) State Bar shows that the interests of the clients and the administration of justice will be prejudiced if this proceeding is not maintained as Respondent has misrepresented himself as qualified to provide legal services or attempted to align himself with the work of attorneys without the consent or knowledge of those attorneys. “Two public policies underlie the strictures against the unlicensed practice of law. First, attorneys must be licensed so that the public is protected from being advised and represented by persons who are not qualified to practice law. [Citation.] Second, the litigation of cases by unlicensed attorneys threatens the integrity of the judicial process itself.” (Russell v. Dopp (1995) 36 Cal.App.4th 765, 773.) The Court finds that State Bar establishes that the public remains at risk of being victimized by Respondent if this action is not taken to cease the unauthorized practice of law.

 

Accordingly, State Bar’s petition is GRANTED. The Court will sign the Proposed Permanent Order for State Bar to assume jurisdiction over Respondent’s unauthorized practice of law with the orders necessary to assume jurisdiction as provided in the Proposed Order. (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6126.3, subd. (e).)

Case Number: 26NWCV02419    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: P

Ceja, et al. v. Salguero,

26NWCV02419

This motor vehicle-personal injury action was filed on July 21, 2026. Plaintiffs Salvador Rodríguez Ceja, Graciela Gonzalez Vargas, and Adriana Rodríguez Gonzalez (Plaintiffs) now apply ex parte for the fifth time for a nunc pro tunc order to backdate the filing date of their complaint.

Counsel for Plaintiffs James A. Allaire states: “July 1, 2026, my office e-filed the Summons and Complaint in this lawsuit via One-Legal… On July 2, 2026, we received electronic confirmation that the documents were successfully submitted. The confirmation status stated: ‘E-Filing under Court Clerk Review.’” (Allaire Decl., ¶ 3.) Mr. Allaire states that on July 7, 2026, the filing was rejected due to a clerical mismatch in the names. (Allaire Decl., ¶ 7.)

 

“The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) “[A] paper is deemed filed when it is deposited with the clerk with directions to file the paper.” (Rojas v. Cutsforth (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 774, 778.) When a filing has an insubstantial defect, “the clerk should file the complaint and notify the attorney or party that the perceived defect should be corrected at the earliest opportunity.” (Id., at p. 777.) Examples of such defects include failing to submit a form required by local rules and listing the wrong courthouse or division. (Id., at p. 777-778.)

 

Here, Plaintiffs provide a confirmation by OneLegal reflecting an attempted filing by Plaintiffs to OneLegal, a court-approved filing service, at 5:35 PM on 7/1/2026. (Allaire Decl., Exh. A.) Plaintiffs attest that this action was timely filed with a Court-approved filing company, the action was not given a case number upon rejection, and that the delay in re-filing was due to delays in getting the rejections and inadvertence. (Allaire Decl., ¶ 3-9.)

Accordingly, Plaintiffs’ ex parte application is GRANTED. This action is nunc pro tunc deemed filed on July 2, 2026.
