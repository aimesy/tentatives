HEARING DATE:                 September 14, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   24STCV07322 (Related/Consolidated: 26AVCV00316)

CASE NAME:                        Gloria Lopez v. KMC Landscaping Services, Inc., et al

MOVING PARTY:                Plaintiff Gloria Lopez

OPPOSING PARTY:             KMC Landscaping Services, Inc.

PROCEEDING:                      MOTION FOR TRIAL PREFERENCE

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff’s motion for trial setting preference is denied.                         

Background

Case No. 24STCV07322

 

Plaintiff Gloria Lopez (“Lopez”) filed this action on March 22, 2024, against Defendant KMC Landscaping Services, Inc. (“KMC Landscaping”). The Complaint asserts causes action for: (1) Failure to Pay Wages; (2) Failure to Provide Meal and Rest Period; (3) Failure to Pay Minimum Wage; (4) Failure to Pay Overtime Wages; (5) Failure to Pay Wages Upon Termination; (6) Failure to Issue Accurate Itemized Wage Statement; (7) Unlawful/Unfair Business Practices; (8) Failure to Reimburse; (9) Wrongful Termination; (10) Retaliation; and (11) Failure to Deliver Personnel File.

 

On June 25, 2026, the Court related Case Nos. 24STCV07322 and 26AVCV00316 and designated Case No. 24STCV07322 as the lead case; on July 14, 2026, the Court ordered Case Nos. 24STCV07322 and 26AVCV00316 consolidated for all purposes and designated Case No. 24STCV07322 as the lead case.

 

Case No. 26AVCV00316

 

On March 5, 2026, KMC Landscaping filed a complaint against Lopez and Does 1-10, asserting causes of action for (1) Fraud/Intentional Misrepresentation, (2) Negligent Misrepresentation, (3) Breach of Duty of Loyalty, (4) Unjust Enrichment/Restitution and (5) Conversion.

 

On June 25, 2026, the Court related Case Nos. 24STCV07322 and 26AVCV00316 and designated Case No. 24STCV07322 as the lead case; on July 14, 2026, the Court ordered Case Nos. 24STCV07322 and 26AVCV00316 consolidated for all purposes and designated Case No. 24STCV07322 as the lead case.

 

Lopez moves this Court for an order specially setting the action for trial pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 36, subdivision (d) and/or (e). Trial in this matter is set for November 3, 2026. KMC Landscaping and Cohick filed an opposition to the motion.

 

Motion for trial preference

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Code of Civil Procedure, subdivision (d) provides as follows:

 

In its discretion, the court may also grant a motion for preference that is accompanied by clear and convincing medical documentation that concludes that one of the parties suffers from an illness or condition raising substantial medical doubt of survival of that party beyond six months, and that satisfies the court that the interests of justice will be served by granting the preference.

 

Code of Civil Procedure, subdivision (e) provides as follows:

 

Notwithstanding any other provision of law, the court may in its discretion grant a motion for preference that is supported by a showing that satisfies the court that the interest of justice will be served by granting this preference.

 

DISCUSSION

 

On August 6, 2026, Lopez filed an Ex Parte Application for an Order Granting Trial Preference Under Code of Civil Procedure Sections 36(d) and/or 36(e), or in the Alternative Setting a Hearing Date and Order Shortening Time for a Noticed Motion for Preference (“Ex Parte Application”); on August 7, 2026, the Court denied the Ex Parte Application without prejudice to a noticed motion and scheduled a hearing on the motion for September 14, 2026.  

 

Lopez argues that she is entitled to trial preference because she has a substantial interest in this action and because her medical condition is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudice to that interest. As the sole plaintiff asserting workplace claims arising from Defendants' alleged misconduct, Plaintiff maintains that no co-party can vindicate her interests if this litigation is delayed. She contends that her recent diagnosis of Grade 3, node-positive breast cancer, coupled with active oncology care at City of Hope, satisfies the standard for preference under Code of Civil Procedure section 36(e). (Code Civ. Proc., § 36(e); Lopez Decl. ¶¶ 3–12; Exs. A–F.)

 

Further, Plaintiff argues that the course of treatment now before her makes an ordinary trial setting untenable. She faces a staging workup for possible distant metastasis, endocrine therapy, potential chemotherapy, genetic testing, and surgical decision-making. On these facts, Plaintiff contends that an open-ended or remote trial setting, imposed while she remains in active cancer care, would force her to choose between her medical treatment and meaningful participation in her own trial. She further argues that such delay risks the deterioration of her ability to testify and to assist counsel, thereby prejudicing the very interest section 36(e) is designed to protect.

 

Next, Plaintiff argues that the medical documentation attached as Exhibits A through F establishes, by clear and convincing evidence, the seriousness of her illness. That documentation reflects poorly differentiated Grade 3 invasive ductal carcinoma, a clinical stage of cT2N1, axillary nodal involvement, and oncology concern for distant metastatic risk warranting PET/CT imaging and brain MRI. (Exs. A–F.) On this record, Plaintiff contends that the seriousness of her condition and the burden of her ongoing care together compel preference.

 

Plaintiff argues in the alternative that, to the extent the Court concludes that the "substantial medical doubt of survival beyond six months" factor under section 36(d) requires an express physician attestation beyond the City of Hope records, Plaintiff does not rest solely on that subdivision. She also seeks preference under section 36(e), and also requests an order shortening time so that a noticed preference motion — accompanied by additional treating-physician declaration — can be heard on an expedited calendar.

 

Defendants argue that the motion suffers from a straightforward statutory defect: none of the submitted medical records concludes that Plaintiff's illness raises a substantial medical doubt that she will survive beyond six months. Defendants acknowledge what the records do show. Defendants contend that the evidence of test results and further diagnostic procedures planned, which were offered by Plaintiff, while serious, do not meet the showing required under section 36(d). A risk of distant metastasis, a pending staging workup, and a discussion of potential therapies do not establish, clearly and convincingly, that there is substantial medical doubt of Plaintiff's survival beyond six months. Defendants point out that the submitted documentation contains no prognosis, no life-expectancy opinion, and no treating-physician declaration addressing the statutory standard.

 

Defendants’ assertion regarding the deficiency of Plaintiff’s showing under section 36(d) is correct.  Plaintiff did not submit evidence supporting the showing required with her Ex Parte Application and offered no further evidence thereafter. 

 

Plaintiff also has not provided sufficient grounds to support her request for trial preference under the independent ground pursuant to section 36(e).  The trial is set for a date 57 days out.  Plaintiff has not shown that an earlier trial date is needed.   

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s motion for trial setting preference is denied.

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Standard for Approving Minor’s Compromises – Court approval is required for all settlements of a minor’s claim or that of a person lacking the capacity to make decisions.  (Prob. Code, §§ 2504, 3500, 3600 et seq.; Code Civ. Proc., § 372; see Pearson v. Superior Court (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1333, 1337.)

 

“[T]he protective role the court generally assumes in cases involving minors, [is] a role to assure that whatever is done is in the minor’s best interests . . .[I]ts primary concern is whether the compromise is sufficient to provide for the minor’s injuries, care and treatment.”  (Goldberg v. Superior Court (1994) 23 Cal.App.4th 1378, 1382.)  

 

A petition for court approval of a compromise or covenant not to sue under Code of Civil Procedure section 372 must comply with California Rules of Court Rules 7.950, 7.951, and 7.952.  The petition must be verified by the petitioner and contain a full disclosure of all information that has “any bearing upon the reasonableness” of the compromise or the covenant.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.)  The person compromising the claim on behalf of the minor or person who lacks capacity, and the represented person, must attend the hearing on compromise of the claim unless the court for good cause dispenses with their personal appearance.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.952(a).)

 

An order for deposit of funds of a minor or person lacking decision-making capacity and a petition for the withdrawal of such funds must comply with California Rules of Court Rules 7.953 and 7.954.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1384; see also Super. Ct. L.A. County, Local Rules, rules 4.115-4.118.)

·       Jorge settled with Defendants for $50,000.00, with $12,206.62 used to pay for attorneys’ fees, and $1,173.52 used to pay non-medical expenses, leaving a balance of $36,619.86 for Jorge. Petitioner proposes the net proceeds be deposited into a blocked account on behalf of Jorge.

Case Number: 26STCP02244    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 Monday September 14, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   26STCP02244

CASE NAME:                        Lauzon Paluch, APC v. Sophia Carlsson

MOVING PARTY:                Lauzon Paluch, APC

OPPOSING PARTY:             None

PROCEEDING:                      motion to compel arbitration

RULING SUMMARY:          Petitioner’s motion to compel arbitration and stay the action is GRANTED.  The request that the Court appoint an arbitrator is denied without prejudice.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Status of Arbitration for _____________, 2026, at 8:30AM.                                                                                                                                     

Background

 

On June 15, 2026, Petitioner Lauzon Paluch (“Petitioner”) filed a petition to compel binding arbitration and for Court appointment of an arbitrator.

 

Petitioner claims respondent Sophia Carlsson (“Carlsson”) entered into a written contract with Petitioner to perform legal services for Carlsson in connection with her dissolution matter.

 

Petition to compel arbitration

 

I.          Legal Standard

 

“California law reflects a strong public policy in favor of arbitration as a relatively quick and inexpensive method for resolving disputes.¿ To further that policy, Code of Civil Procedure, section 1281.2 requires a trial court to enforce a written arbitration agreement unless one of three limited exceptions applies.¿ Those statutory exceptions arise where (1) a party waives the right to arbitration; (2) grounds exist for revoking the arbitration agreement; and (3) pending litigation with a third party creates the possibility of conflicting rulings on common factual or legal¿ issues.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.2; Acquire II, Ltd. v. Colton Real Estate Group (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 959, 967.)¿ Similarly, public policy under federal law favors arbitration and the fundamental principle that arbitration is a matter of contract and that courts must place arbitration agreements on an equal footing with other contracts and enforce them according to their terms.¿ (AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion (2011) 563 U.S. 333, 339.) 

¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

In deciding a motion or petition to compel arbitration, trial courts must first decide whether an enforceable arbitration agreement exists between the parties and then determine whether the claims are covered within the scope of the agreement.¿(Omar v. Ralphs Grocery Co. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 955, 961.)¿ The opposing party has the burden to establish any defense to enforcement.¿ (Gatton v. T-Mobile USA, Inc. (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 571, 579 [“The petitioner ... bears the burden of proving the existence of a valid arbitration agreement and the opposing party, plaintiffs here, bears the burden of proving any fact necessary to its defense.”].)¿¿¿¿¿ 

 

II.        Discussion

 

Petitioner moves to compel arbitration and for Court appointment of an arbitrator. Carlsson did not oppose this motion.

 

            A.        Defendant’s Initial Burden

 

First, Petitioner represents it gave Carlsson a “Notice of Right to Arbitrate” pursuant to Business & Professions Code section 6200-6206. (See Petition, Paragraph 3, Exh. B)

 

The moving party bears the burden of producing ‘prima facie evidence of a written agreement to arbitrate the controversy.’” (Gamboa v. Northeast Community Clinic (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 158, 165 (Gamboa).) “The moving party ‘can meet its initial burden by attaching to the [motion or] petition a copy of the arbitration agreement purporting to bear the [opposing party's] signature.’” (Id. at p. 165.) “Alternatively, the moving party can meet its burden by setting forth the agreement's provisions in the motion.” (Ibid.)

 

Petitioner meets its initial prima facie burden by presenting a signed retainer agreement that contains an arbitration agreement that Carlsson signed and dated November 6, 2026. (Petition., Ex. A.)  “Under the Gamboa framework, the moving party is entitled to a presumption, in the first instance, that the agreement to arbitrate is authentic.” (Kinder v. Capistrano Beach Care Center, LLC (2023) 91 Cal.App.5th 804, 815.)

 

Here, the Court finds that Petitioner has met its burden and there is a valid arbitration agreement between the parties. Further, the claims here are regarding unpaid legals fees arising from the contractual relationship between the parties.  The claims are covered by the arbitration agreement.  Petitioner presented evidence that it served respondent Carlsson with a Notice of Client’s Right to Arbitrate.  No evidence has been presented that, within 30 days after service by Petitioner of a Notice of Client’s Right to Arbitrate, respondent Carlsson elected to proceed under the State Bar fee arbitration procedures or filed a request for fee arbitration.  And respondent Carlsson did not oppose this motion. The Court grants Petitioner’s motion to compel binding arbitration.

 

The Court cannot appoint an arbitrator, because the arbitration agreement Paragraph 20 (A)(3) in part states:  

 

“Any party to the arbitration may request ADR to identify panels of retired or former jurists with at least two (2) years of family law judicial experience to sit as an arbitrator of the matters submitted for arbitration and the arbitrator determining the submitted matters shall be selected from such panels pursuant to ADR Rules.  If an arbitrator cannot be selected pursuant to the ADR rules, then the Superior Court of Los Angeles County shall choose an impartial arbitrator whose decision shall be final and conclusive on all parties.”  

 

No evidence has been presented as to the provisions of the ADR Rules for selection of an arbitrator, or that as to what efforts have been made to seek appointment of an arbitrator pursuant to those rules. 

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner’s motion to compel arbitration and stay the action is GRANTED.  The request that the Court appoint the arbitrator is DENIED without prejudice.  The Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Status of Arbitration for _____________, 2026, at 8:30AM.
