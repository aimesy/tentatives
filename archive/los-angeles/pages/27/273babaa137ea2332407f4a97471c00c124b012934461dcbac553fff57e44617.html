[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

 

PLAINTIFF ROBERT M. COHEN dba THE LAW OFFICES OF ROBERT M. COHEN’S MOTION TO COMPEL ARBITRATION

 

Background

On May 29, 2024, Robert M. Cohen, dba The Law Offices of Robert M. Cohen, (“Cohen”) filed a complaint against Defendant Carle Ebert Pierose (“Pierose”), asserting four causes of action for (1) breach of written contract (fee services agreement), (2) account stated, (3) open book account, and (4) common counts.

On April 23, 2026, Pierose filed a motion for leave to file compulsory cross-complaint.  On May 4, 2026, Cohen filed an opposition asking the Court to delay any ruling on the motion for leave to file a compulsory cross-complaint until after it ruled on Cohen’s petition to compel arbitration pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2 and to stay the lawsuit pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1281.4.  On May 7, 2026, Pierose filed a reply. 

On May 11, 2026, Cohen filed the instant petition to compel arbitration.  As of September 14, 2026, no opposition or reply has been filed.

On July 27, 2026, Pierose’s motion for leave to file compulsory cross-complaint was continued to September 17, 2026, the same date Cohen’s petition to compel arbitration was to be heard.  Pierose’s motion for leave to file compulsory cross-complaint was continued to September 17, 2026 for scheduling purposes only and the merits would not be heard on that date.

Legal Standard

In a motion to compel arbitration, the moving party must prove by a preponderance of evidence the existence of the arbitration agreement and that the dispute is covered by the agreement. The burden then shifts to the resisting party to prove by a preponderance of evidence a ground for denial (e.g., fraud, unconscionability, etc.). ((Rosenthal v. Great Western Fin. Securities Corp. (1996) 14 Cal.4th 394, 413-414); (Hotels Nevada v. L.A. Pacific Center, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 754, 758.)

Generally, on a petition to compel arbitration, the court must grant the petition unless it finds either (1) no written agreement to arbitrate exists; (2) the right to compel arbitration has been waived; (3) grounds exist for revocation of the agreement; or (4) litigation is pending that may render the arbitration unnecessary or create conflicting rulings on common issues. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.2; Condee v. Longwood Management Corp. (2001) 88 Cal.App.4th 215, 218-219.)

“California has a strong public policy in favor of arbitration and any doubts regarding the arbitrability of a dispute are resolved in favor of arbitration.” ((Coast Plaza Doctors Hospital v. Blue Cross of California (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 677, 686.) “This strong policy has resulted in the general rule that arbitration should be upheld unless it can be said with assurance that an arbitration clause is not susceptible to an interpretation covering the asserted dispute.” ((Ibid. [internal quotations omitted].) This is in accord with the liberal federal policy favoring arbitration agreements under the Federal Arbitration Act (“FAA”), which governs all agreements to arbitrate in contracts “involving interstate commerce.” (9 U.S.C. section 2, et seq.; (Higgins v. Superior Court (2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 1238, 1247.) Although the parties disagree on whether the FAA applies to the instant case, regardless, the analysis under California law and the FAA is the same. “Thus, under both the FAA and California law, ‘arbitration agreements are valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract.’” (Higgins v. Superior Court, supra, 140 Cal.App.4th at p. 1247, citing Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services, Inc. (2000) 24 Cal.4th 83, 98.)

Discussion

Cohen presents an applicable arbitration agreement to his complaint.  The Retainer Agreement with Defendant Pierose contains an express binding arbitration provision at paragraphs 9 and 10.  (Petition, ¶17, Ex. A, ¶¶9 and 10.)  The arbitration provision applies to “each and every controversy between the parties hereto regarding payment of attorney fees owed by you to the Law Offices of Robert M. Cohen.  Any claim of non-performance or breach of this agreement, any claim concerning its validity or enforceability, and any claim for malpractice or professional negligence, fraud, misrepresentation, breach of fiduciary duty or refund of fees paid against any lawyer performing services pursuant to the terms hereof, or in connection with the terms hereof, shall be referred to mandatory and binding arbitration.”  (Petition, Ex. A, ¶9.)  Cohen sent Pierose a Notice of Client’s Right to Fee Arbitration on May 31, 2024.  (Id. at ¶8.)  Cohen did not file the complaint for payment of fees until December 4, 2025.  (Id. at ¶9.) 

Plaintiff establishes an applicable arbitration agreement.  Plaintiff satisfies its burden as moving party with undisputed, admissible evidence of an applicable arbitration provision in the Retainer Agreement with Defendant.  Defendant did not oppose.

Cohen’s Petition to Compel Arbitration is granted. 

Cohen also asks that the Court stay this action pending completion of arbitration pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.4.  “If a court of competent jurisdiction, whether in this State or not, has ordered arbitration of a controversy which is an issue involved in an action or proceeding pending before a court of this State, the court in which such action or proceeding is pending shall, upon motion of a party to such action or proceeding, stay the action or proceeding until an arbitration is had in accordance with the order to arbitrate or until such earlier time as the court specifies.”  (Code Civ. Proc., §1281.4.)

Cohen’s entire complaint is ordered to arbitration.  As such, the entire action is stayed pending completion of arbitration and Pierose’s motion for leave to file cross-complaint is placed off calendar.

Conclusion

Plaintiff Cohen’s Petition to Compel Arbitration and Request to Stay Action Pending Completion of Arbitration is granted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 1281.2 and 1281.4. The Court sets an arbitration status conference on September 16, 2027 at 10 a.m. in Dept. 508.

Cohen is ordered to give notice of this Order.

DEFENDANT STARSHIP, LLC DBA ROCKSTAR SUSHI’S MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

           

            Background

Plaintiff Dreamstation, Inc. (Plaintiff) filed this breach of contract and fraud action against defendants Starship, LLC dba Rockstar Sushi (Defendant) and Rockstar Original on February 5, 2026. Defendant answered on March 12, 2026. Trial is set for May 17, 2028.

On August 11, 2026, Defendant filed the instant motion for leave to file a cross-complaint asserting causes of action against Plaintiff for breach of contract and fraudulent concealment. Plaintiff opposes, arguing Defendant unduly delayed before filing its motion and Defendant’s causes of action alleged in the proposed cross-complaint have no merit.

 

Legal Standard

A defendant against whom a complaint has been filed may file a cross-complaint setting forth “Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (b).)

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 428.50, the defendant must file this cross-complaint “at any time before the court has set a date for trial.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subd. (b).) If the defendant fails to file the cross-complaint within this timeframe, he must obtain leave of court. (Id., § 428.50, subd. (c).) The court may grant leave “in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.” (Ibid.)

In other words, the Court may grant a motion for leave to belatedly file a cross-complaint if it finds the causes of action in the cross-complaint arise from the same transaction or occurrence as the causes of action in the complaint, and that doing so would be in the interests of justice. “Permission to file a permissive cross-complaint is solely within the trial court's discretion.” (Crocker Nat. Bank v. Emerald (1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 852, 864.

 

            Discussion

The motion is granted.

Defendant’s proposed cross-complaint arises from the same sales arrangement as Plaintiff alleged in the complaint. Undue delay is not grounds to deny leave to amend unless there is prejudice to Plaintiff; this case is set for trial in May 2028, and Plaintiff identifies no specific prejudice it will suffer except the resources expending litigating Defendant’s cross-complaint. This argument and Plaintiff’s objection to the merits of Defendant’s cross-complaint requests that the Court adjudicate the merits of the proposed causes of action prematurely. Plaintiff contends Defendant has contradicted its own claims in its discovery responses; this is not a basis to deny leave to file the cross-complaint.

 

Conclusion

Defendant’s Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint is GRANTED.

Defendant is ordered to provide notice of this Order and file its Cross-Complaint within 10 days of the date of this Order.

DEFENDANT GRANT SHENON ALMARAZ, A PROFESSIONAL LAW CORPORATION’S MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE COMPULSORY CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

           

            Background

Plaintiffs Luminosity Entertainment LLC, MMJ Productions LLC, Elie K. Samaha, and Peter M. Hoffman (Plaintiffs) filed this legal malpractice action on March 24, 2026 against defendants David M. Almaraz and Grant Shenon Almaraz (Defendants). Defendants answered on May 12, 2026, correcting the identity of defendant Grant Shenon Almaraz to “Grant Shenon Almaraz, a Professional Law Corporation” (Almaraz PLC). A trial date has not been set.

On June 18, 2026, Almaraz PLC filed the instant motion for leave to file a compulsory cross-complaint for unpaid attorneys’ fees against Plaintiffs and new cross-defendants Geno Taylor, and individual, and GVN Releasing LLC (together “Cross-Defendants”).

Almaraz PLC served its motion on Plaintiffs’ counsel by electronic mail on June 18, 2026. Plaintiffs filed no opposition.

 

Legal Standard

            A defendant against whom a complaint has been filed may file a cross-complaint setting forth “Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.” ((Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subd. (b).)

“A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint.” (Id., § 428.50(a).) “Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.” (Id., subd. (b).) If the defendant fails to file the cross-complaint within this timeframe, he must obtain leave of court. (Id., subd. (c).) The court may grant leave “in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.” (Ibid.)

In other words, the Court may grant a motion for leave to belatedly file a cross-complaint if it finds the causes of action in the cross-complaint arise from the same transaction or occurrence as the causes of action in the complaint, and that doing so would be in the interests of justice. “Permission to file a permissive cross-complaint is solely within the trial court's discretion.” ((Crocker Nat. Bank v. Emerald (1990) 221 Cal.App.3d 852, 864.)

 

            Discussion

The unopposed motion is granted. The causes of action in the proposed cross-complaint arise from the same transaction or occurrence as the causes of action in the complaint. This case is less than six months old and no trial date has been set. Plaintiffs filed no opposition demonstrating cognizable prejudice.


Conclusion

Defendant Grant Shenon Almaraz, a Professional Law Corporation’s Motion for Leave to File Compulsory Cross-Complaint is GRANTED.

Moving Defendant is ordered to provide notice of this Order and separately file its proposed cross-complaint within 10 days of this Order.
