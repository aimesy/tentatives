DEPARTMENT I LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

If the parties wish to submit on the tentative ruling and avoid a court appearance on the matter, the moving party must contact the opposing party and all other parties who have appeared in the action and confirm that each will submit on the tentative ruling. Please call the court no later than 4:30 p.m. on the court day before the hearing, leave a message with the court clerk at (310) 260-3629 advising her that all parties will submit on the tentative ruling and waive hearing, and finally, serve notice of the Court's ruling on all parties entitled to receive service. If any party declines to submit on the tentative ruling, then no telephone call is necessary, and all parties should appear at the hearing.

Case Number: 23SMCV06010    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: I

This is an application by defendant to delay the due date for her brief, and, presumably, argument and resolution as well.  One issue she raises are exhibits 21-27, which she claims (and the plaintiff does not dispute) were served on her on July 7 at about 9 am.  Defendant states that this is improper.  The court disagrees.  First, exhibit 21 was a deed that only became relevant because a few days earlier, defendant had submitted—for the first time—a deed of trust in her favor on the property.  Had defendant submitted that at any point in the last 2 years, the court would agree with the defense that exhibit 21 comes too late.  But because defendant’s deed was submitted at the last second, defendant will not be heard to complain that plaintiff’s response was submitted late as well.  As to other documents, the court can look.  Plaintiff stated that these were impeachment exhibits.  If truly used for that purpose and that purpose only, then there is nothing improper about presenting them late—indeed, they need not be presented ahead of time at all.  If they go to defendant’s last minute document, then they are proper.  If they go to other things, then the objection is well taken and the court will not consider them.  As to Exhibit 22, the court will look.  If the deed is to a different property, then it is of very little, if any, relevance and the court agrees it likely ought not have been admitted or presented at all.  As to the Loshin letter, the court can accept that the letter was written and sent, but it remains hearsay as to the truth of the matters within it.  Finally, as to the lis pendens, the court will do this.  The court will ASSUME the authenticity of the document (which is all the purple stamp really proves).  If this turns out to be a pivotal document, the court will give defendant an opportunity to get the formal document.  For now, though, defendant ought to assume that the document will be considered.

 

As to resting, the court is not sure what to do.  The court asked whether defendant had more witnesses to call.  Defendant said no.  As to the documents, they were not admitted.  That is what it means when there are no more witnesses and no one moves the admission of documents.  However, if defendant wants to argue that the documents are crucial, and if they make a difference, the court will at least hear the argument to try and figure out whether to allow the documents to be admitted and to allow plaintiff an opportunity to rebut them.

 

Defendant also claims that there is a medical issue.  Defendant contends that she was in the ER on Thursday, July 8 and as a result of medical issues, could not write the brief.  The court will hear the medical evidence.  However, this case is now tried and it needs to be resolved.  The court is not in a rush to justice, but it seems that at every turn the court is met with a delay or a request for a delay.  Plaintiff was willing to forego briefing and move straight to argument—the briefing was ordered at defendant’s request.  This court’s problem is that it is going on medical leave.

 

What the court might do is to have the matter resolved solely by closing briefs with no argument.  That will allow the parties to brief the issues.  The court returns from leave on August 3 (but only in chambers).  If there is full briefing, the court can rule from chambers, which might be a fair compromise.


Case Number: 24SMCV02574    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: I

The court is not likely to rule today.  However, the court provides some questions for purposes of the argument.

 

First, what it the parties’ take on the effect of Bonni. 

 

Second, the court’s view is that in order to resolve the motion, it needs to consider the specific allegations as against each moving party.  In other words, there might be many allegations that are background, or are relevant to the case against the UC.  But the court believes that the only ones pertinent to this motion are those against the moving defendants.  The court would like to know if anyone disagrees.

 

Third.  From plaintiff, what are the specific allegations upon which you rely that, if true, would support your claims as to the causes of action against the individual defendants?

 

Fourth.  How do the incorporation by reference allegations play into that?

 

Fifth.  What about the IIED cause of action.  How should the court interpret the charging allegations there?  While the court can agree that as to causes of action with specific elements (like battery) only the charging allegations going to the elements are pertinent, can the same be said of IIED?

 

Sixth.  What is the effect of the conspiracy cause of action?  Of course, civil conspiracy is not a tort, and thus it ought not be a cause of action at all.  But does that series of allegations have any effect on extending liability or the charging allegations beyond that to which they otherwise would apply?

 

Seventh.  What exactly is plaintiff’s specific evidence to support each cause of action.  And what is defendant’s contrary evidence or objection to make that evidence inadmissible?  If that evidence stands, why would plaintiff not have established “minimal merit,” which is all plaintiff need do at this stage?  Or does defendant believe that a higher showing is required, and, if so, on what authority?

 

Eighth.  Does anyone really believe that plaintiff’s motion for sanctions ought to be decided today?  The court believes not.


Case Number: 24SMCV03128    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: I

This is a motion for summary judgment.  It is based on the theory that defendant has tacitly admitted the truth of plaintiff’s allegations.  More specifically, plaintiff served requests for admission, but there was no response.  This motion followed.  While the motion was pending, plaintiff brought a motion to deem the RFAs admitted, which was granted unless the defense had responded before the hearing.  (The court still does not know if defendant had responded).  However, that was months after the motion was brought and cannot therefore form the basis for the motion.  Absent the admissions, there is really nothing to support the motion and the motion is, therefore, DENIED.
