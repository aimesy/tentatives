MOTION (1) TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT AND DEFAULT JUDGMENT and (2) to quash service of summons

 

 

MOVING PARTY:           Defendant Avrohom Light

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  Plaintiffs Donald E. Reith and Midwest Construction, LLC

Motion (1) to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment and (2) to Quash Service of Summons

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

EVIDENTIARY OBJECTIONS 

The court does not address the objections to the evidence submitted in support of the opposition to this motion, because the items to which those objections are asserted are not material to the court’s ruling.

 

 

REQUESTS FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

The court declines to rule on the requests for judicial notice accompanying this motion because they are not material to the court’s ruling. (Gbur v. Cohen (1979) 93 Cal.App.3d 296, 301 (“[J]udicial notice . . . is always confined to those matters which are relevant to the issue at hand.”].)

DISCUSSION

Specially appearing defendant Avrohom Light (“Defendant”) moves (1) to set aside the default and default judgment entered in favor of plaintiffs Donald E. Reith and Midwest Construction, LLC (“Plaintiffs”) and against Defendant as void pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), and (2) to quash service of summons on Defendant pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10. 

The court finds that Defendant has met his burden to show that the default and default judgment should be set aside as void under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d).

“The court . . . may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) A default judgment “entered against a defendant who was not served with a summons in the manner prescribed by statute is void.” (Dill v. Berquist Construction Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 1426, 1444; see also Code Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (b) [permitting service at a person’s dwelling, residence, or place of business].)

Defendant states that the default judgment is void because Defendant did not use the address at which substituted service was made as his residence, place of business, or mailing address, such that substituted service was invalid. (Light Decl. ¶ 3.) Defendant’s categorical denial is sufficient to rebut the presumption of valid service created by Plaintiffs’ Proof of Service filed October 14, 2022. (Evid. Code, § 647.) Plaintiffs’ attack on Defendant’s credibility in other matters is not evidence contradicting his sworn statements under penalty of perjury. The court therefore must set aside this default judgment as void under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d).  

          For the same reasons discussed above, the court also grants Defendant’s motion to quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1).) 

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendant Avrohom Light’s motion (1) to set aside default and default judgment as void, and (2) motion to quash service of summons on Defendant.

The court orders that (1) the default of defendant Avrohom Light entered on October 26, 2022, and the default judgment entered against defendant Avrohom Light on June 26, 2023, are set aside, and (2) service of summons on defendant Avrohom Light as stated in the proof of service filed on October 14, 2022, is quashed.

The court sets a Case Management Conference in this action on November 3, 2026, at 8:30 a.m.  

The court orders defendant Avrohom Light to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories - General

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   No timely response

Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories - General

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. The court did not consider defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority’s notice of nonopposition, filed August 6, 2026, because it was not timely filed.   

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez moves the court for an order (1) compelling defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority (“Defendant”) to serve responses to Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories -General, Set 1, and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Plaintiff and against Defendant.

The court grants Plaintiff’s motion because (1) Plaintiff has shown that Defendant did not serve timely responses to their Form Interrogatories, and (2) Defendant did not submit opposition papers or other evidence with the court establishing that responses have since been served on Plaintiff.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b); Kasis Decl., Ex. 1 [proof of service of Plaintiff’s Form Interrogatories on counsel for Defendant] and ¶¶ 4-11.)

          The court grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)  “The court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed . . . or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.”  (Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 3.1348(a).) The court finds that $750 (1.5 hours x $500 hourly rate)is a reasonable amount of sanctions to impose against Defendant in connection with this motion.

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez’s motion to compel responses to Form Interrogatories - General, set 1.

          Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290, the court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority to serve on plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez full and complete verified answers, without objections and which comply with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.220-2030.250, to plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez’s Form Interrogatories - General, Set 1, within 20 days of the date of service of this order.

          The court orders defendant Los Angeles Services Homeless Authority to pay monetary sanctions to plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez in the amount of $750.00 within 30 days of the date of service of this order.

 

 

 

 

The court orders plaintiff Jennifer Vazquez to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOtion to Compel Arbitration

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant Collective Hub, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:   Plaintiff Katherine Wang

Motion to Compel Arbitration

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant Collective Hub, Inc. (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order (1) compelling plaintiff Katherine Wang (“Plaintiff”) to submit her claims alleged in the Complaint to binding arbitration and (2) staying this action pending completion of arbitration.

1.     Applicability of Federal Arbitration Act

“The party asserting the FAA applies to an agreement has ‘the burden to demonstrate FAA coverage by declarations and other evidence.’” (Evenskaas v. California Transit, Inc. (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 285, 292 [internal citation omitted].) “[W]hen an agreement provides that its ‘enforcement’ shall be governed by the FAA, the FAA governs a party’s motion to compel arbitration[.]” (Victrola 89, LLC v. Jaman Properties 8 LLC (2020) 46 Cal.App.5th 337, 346.)

The court finds that Defendant has met its burden to show that the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. § 1 et seq.) (the “FAA”) governs this motion because the arbitration agreement that is the subject of this motion states that, “[a]ny arbitration hereunder will be pursuant to the Federal Arbitration Act (the ‘FAA’) and California law, unless California law conflicts with the FAA in which case the FAA shall govern.”  (Thompson Decl., Ex. A [PDF page 15].)

2.     Existence of Agreement to Arbitrate

“‘ “The party seeking to compel arbitration bears the burden of proving the existence of an arbitration agreement, while the party opposing the petition bears the burden of establishing a defense to the agreement’s enforcement.” ’”¿ (Beco v. Fast Auto Loans (2022) 86 Cal.App.5th 292, 302.)¿ To determine the existence of an arbitration agreement, the court uses “a three-step burden-shifting process.”¿ (Iyere v. Wise Auto Group (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 747, 755.)¿ “The arbitration proponent must first recite verbatim, or provide a copy of, the alleged agreement.¿ [Citations.]¿ A movant can bear this initial burden ‘by attaching a copy of the arbitration agreement purportedly bearing the opposing party’s signature.’”¿ (Ibid. [internal citations omitted].)¿ “If the movant bears its initial burden, the burden shifts to the party opposing arbitration to identify a factual dispute as to the agreement’s existence . . . .”¿ (Ibid.)¿ If the opposing party meets its burden to “submit sufficient evidence to create a factual dispute” as to the existence of the agreement, the burden shifts back to the arbitration proponent, who retains the ultimate burden of proving its existence by a preponderance of the evidence.¿ (Ibid.; Gamboa v. Northeast Community Clinic (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 158, 165-166.)¿¿¿ 

First, the court finds that Defendants have met their burden to provide a copy of an alleged agreement to arbitrate this controversy.

Defendant has submitted a copy of a “Terms and Conditions” Agreement belonging to TriNet, Defendant’s “Professional Employer Organization,” which operated Defendant’s payroll system and provided human resource services. (Thompson Decl. Ex. A.) The Terms and Conditions Agreement states that the employee agrees to arbitrate any claims arising out of or relating to the employee’s employment by TriNet or a TriNet client (here, Defendant). (Thompson Decl., Ex. A [PDF page 15].) Defendant has also submitted a separate record of Plaintiff’s electronic signature for that agreement dated April 4, 2022. (Thompson Decl. Exh. B.)  Plaintiff does not deny accepting the Terms and Conditions.

The court further finds that Defendant has shown that the Arbitration Agreement encompasses Plaintiff’s claims because it requires arbitration of any claim between Plaintiff and her employer.  (Thompson Decl., Ex. A [PDF page 15].) Plaintiff also does not deny that the Arbitration Agreement encompasses her claims.

 For the reasons set forth above, the court finds that Defendant has proven that an agreement to arbitrate this controversy exists.

3.     The Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act (EFAA)

Plaintiff challenges the Arbitration Agreement as unenforceable under the Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021 (EFAA; 9 U.S.C. §§ 401-402).

Pursuant to 9 United States Code section 402(a), “at the election of the person alleging conduct constituting a sexual harassment dispute or sexual assault dispute . . . , no predispute arbitration agreement or predispute joint-action waiver shall be valid or enforceable with respect to a case which is filed under Federal, Tribal, or State law and relates to the sexual assault dispute or the sexual harassment dispute.”  (9 U.S.C. § 402, subd. (a).) When application of the EFAA is based on a “sexual harassment dispute,” courts have construed this language as requiring the plaintiff to demonstrate that the pleadings are sufficient to state a claim for a sexual harassment.  (See Liu v. Miniso Depot CA, Inc. (2024) 105 Cal.App.5th 791, 799 fn. 2 [noting federal district court decisions requiring sexual harassment claims be capable of surviving a motion to dismiss under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6)]; see also Quilala v. Securitas Security Services USA, Inc. (2025) 117 Cal.App.5th 75, 86 [analyzing whether plaintiff’s allegations are sufficient to state a cause of action for sexual harassment].)

The court finds that Plaintiff has adequately alleged a claim for sexual harassment such that the EFAA applies.

First, claims for sexual harassment based on sex, gender, and pregnancy, as alleged here, fall within the scope of the EFAA. “The term ‘sexual harassment dispute’ means a dispute relating to conduct that is alleged to constitute sexual harassment under applicable Federal, Tribal, or State law.”  (9 U.S.C. § 401(4).)  For the purposes of Government Code section 12940, subdivision (j), “‘harassment’ because of sex includes sexual harassment, gender harassment, and harassment based on pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions. Sexually harassing conduct need not be motivated by sexual desire.”  (Gov. Code, § 12940, subd. (j)(4)(C).)  Thus, harassment based on pregnancy is sexual harassment under California state law and therefore qualifies as a “sexual harassment dispute” under the EFAA. The language of these statutes is not ambiguous, and Defendant’s attempt to narrow the scope of these provisions through extrinsic materials is not well-taken.

Second, Plaintiff’s allegations in her Complaint are sufficient to state a cause of action for sexual harassment under FEHA.

“[T]he prohibition against sexual harassment includes protection from a broad range of conduct, [including] the creation of a work environment that is hostile or abusive on the basis of sex.”  (Kruitbosch v. Bakersfield Recovery Services, Inc. (2025) 114 Cal.App.5th 200, 211 [quotations and citations omitted].) “To prevail on a claim of hostile work environment under FEHA, an employee must show he or she was subjected to harassing conduct that was (1) unwelcome, (2) because of sex or gender, and (3) sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter the conditions of her employment and create an abusive work environment.” (Wawrzenski v. United Airlines, Inc. (2024) 106 Cal.App.5th 663, 692.)  “The working environment must be evaluated in light of the totality of the circumstances. These may include the frequency of the discriminatory conduct; its severity; whether it is physically threatening or humiliating, or a mere offensive utterance; and whether it unreasonably interferes with an employee’s work performance.  The required level of severity or seriousness varies inversely with the pervasiveness or frequency of the conduct.” (Bailey v. San Francisco Dist. Attorney’s Office (2024) 16 Cal.5th 611, 628.)

Here, Plaintiff alleges a pattern of hostility and abuse based on her pregnancy of sufficient frequency to constitute pervasive harassment.  Plaintiff alleges that, once she revealed her pregnancy to her supervisor, Hooman Radfar, he began downplaying her contributions to the company, excluding Plaintiff from meetings and removing Plaintiff from Defendant’s organizational chart. (Complaint ¶ 11.) Plaintiff also alleges that a prior supervisor, Patrick Bragg, told her that her employment was “vulnerable” when she informed him of her pregnancy. (Complaint ¶ 9.) Crucially, Plaintiff also alleges that she was subjected to a pattern of demeaning comments from Defendant’s Vice President of Engineering, Chintan Shah. (Complaint ¶ 12.) Taken collectively, these are allegations of pervasive hostility to Plaintiff on the basis of her pregnancy. Because of the frequency and number of these incidents, the threshold of severity to allege harassment is reduced, and Plaintiff’s allegations meet that reduced threshold.

          For the reasons stated above, the court finds that the EFAA applies to this action. Because Plaintiff has exercised her option under the EFAA to refuse arbitration, the court denies Defendant’s motion to compel arbitration.  

ORDER

The court DENIES defendant Collective Hub, Inc’s motion to compel arbitration.

The court orders plaintiff Katherine Wang to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion to Dismiss for Failure to Pay Transfer Fees

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendants Toyota Motor Sales U.S.A., Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response

Motion to Dismiss for Failure to Pay Transfer Fees

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition was filed.  

DISCUSSION

Defendant Toyota Motor Sales U.S.A., Inc. (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order dismissing this action for failure to pay transfer fees, and for sanctions for Defendant and against plaintiff Mark Bayaoa (“Plaintiff”).

Upon a court order transferring an action, any costs and fees must be paid within 30 days after service of notice of the transfer order. (Code Civ. Proc., § 399, subd. (a).) If costs and fees are not paid, “then, within 30 days after notice of finality of the order of transfer, the court on a duly noticed motion by any party may dismiss the action without prejudice to the cause on the condition that no other action on the cause may be commenced in another court before satisfaction of the court’s order for costs and fees.” (Ibid.)

On November 20, 2025, the court filed an order on the stipulation by the parties to transfer this action to the Superior Court for the County of Kern, with Plaintiff to pay all court fees and costs associated with the transfer of the action. (Stipulation and Order, filed November 20, 2025.) To date, the action has not been transferred, and the transfer fees have not been paid. (Ely Decl. ¶ 3.) The court therefore dismisses this action for failure to pay transfer fees.

The court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions.

 

ORDER

The court GRANTS defendant Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc.’s motion to dismiss.

The court orders that this action is dismissed without prejudice as of the date of this order. The court further orders that no other action on this cause may be commenced in any other court before payment of all transfer fees and costs is made.

The court orders defendant Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
