DEPARTMENT S27 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS



Case Number: 23LBCV00750    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Judgment Creditor Kinecta Federal Credit Union filed a complaint against Judgment Debtor Hector Reyna alleging Judgment Debtor defaulted on a loan, thereby leaving an outstanding balance of $17,558.29, and on a revolving credit account (credit card), with an outstanding balance of $10,39518. On April 25, 2023, Judgment Creditor filed a complaint for 1. Breach of Unsecured Written Agreement 2. Common Count 3. Breach of Agreement 4. Open Book Account, and 5. Account Stated. 

 

On December 7, 2023, the clerk entered Judgment Debtor’s default. On December 8, 2023, the Court entered a default judgment for $32,375.60. On April 22, 2026, the clerk issued a writ of execution, which led to judgment debtor filing a claim of exemption, and the setting of the hearing by Judgment Creditor.

 

2.     Prior Hearing

The Court was originally scheduled to hear this claim of exemption on 8/11/26.  Prior to the hearing, the Court issued a tentative ruling setting forth the applicable law governing claims of exemption, and then analyzing the present claim.  The Court found the judgment debtor had failed to provide any documentation supporting the claim of exemption, and continued the hearing to require him to do so.  Specifically, the Court ordered him to email copies of his supporting documentation to Judgment Creditor for review and to file copies of the documentation with the Court.

 

3.     Continued Hearing

On 9/04/26, Judgment Creditor filed a supplemental memorandum in support of its opposition to the claim of exemption.  Judgment Creditor indicates it has received no emailed documentation for review.  The Court’s file reveals that no supporting documentation has been filed either.  CCP §706.050(a)(1) permits a judgment creditor to levy twenty percent of an individual’s disposable earnings for the week by way of an earnings withholding order.  §706.011 defines “disposable earnings” as the portion of an individual’s earnings that remain after deducting all amounts required to be withheld by law. 

 

The Court finds Judgment Debtor failed to support his claim of exemption, and permits Judgment Creditor to execute on the earnings withholding order to the full extent of the law. 

 

Judgment Creditor is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV01883    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: S27

 

  1. Background Facts

Plaintiff, Deborah A. Gustafson filed this action against Defendant, American Honda Motor Co., Inc. for violations of the Song-Beverly Act, breach of implied warranty, and fraudulent inducement – concealment. 

 

  1. Motion for Summary Adjudication

a.     Initial Note

Defendant’s notice of motion and separate statement do not comply with CRC 3.1350(b) and (h), which require the notice of motion to set forth each issue to be adjudicated and the separate statement to repeat the issues verbatim.  The Court finds, in connection with this particular motion, that Plaintiff can reasonably understand Defendant is seeking adjudication of the fourth and fifth causes of action on statute of limitations grounds, and therefore the Court will rule on the motion despite the defect.  As the Court has previously admonished Defense Counsel, this defect may be grounds for denial in the future in Department S27. 

 

b.     Analysis

Defendant moves for summary adjudication of the fourth (breach of implied warranty of merchantability) and fifth (fraud – concealment) causes of action in Plaintiffs’ complaint.  Plaintiffs failed to timely file opposition papers. 

 

Recently, in Mandell-Brown v. Novo Nordisk Inc. (2025) 109 Cal.App.5th 478, 508, the Court of Appeals considered a situation where a plaintiff failed to file an opposing separate statement prior to the hearing on the defendant’s motion.  The Mandell-Brown Court noted, “Thus, if a plaintiff opposing summary judgment fails to file a separate statement, and the trial court reviews the moving papers and concludes the motion is not deficient on its face, it has discretion under subdivision (b)(3) to grant the motion, without first undertaking a detailed analysis of the supporting evidence to determine if a prima facie showing has been made as to one or more of the elements of each claim."

 

The Court has reviewed the moving papers and finds they are not deficient on their face.  In light of the lack of opposition and an opposing separate statement, the Court grants the motion.

 

Recently, in 24LBCV02791 and myriad other cases, Department S27 has admonished Plaintiff’s attorney as follows:

The Court notes that there have been numerous motions for summary judgment and/or adjudication filed by Honda in various cases without opposition.  The Court asks Plaintiff’s attorney to dismiss any case or cause of action the plaintiff does not intend to pursue.  Requiring the Court to rule on unopposed motions is not an efficient use of judicial resources.

 

Despite this admonishment, Plaintiff has neither dismissed the causes of action nor opposed the motion.  Again, the motion is granted.

 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV01965    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff, Maria Guadalupe Guerrero Villasenor filed this action against Defendants, Julio Jonatan Oliva Benavides and JJ Oliva Transport, Inc. for damages arising out of an automobile accident.  Plaintiff filed her complaint on 7/08/25. 

 

The Court heard an OSC re: proof of service on 10/01/25 and continued it to 12/05/25.  On 12/05/25, the Court continued the matter to 1/21/26.  On 1/21/26, the Court imposed monetary sanctions and continued the OSC re: proof of service to 3/26/26.  Prior to 3/26/26, Plaintiff filed an application to serve the summons and complaint by publication.  On 3/26/26, the Court imposed further monetary sanctions, continued the OSC re: proof of service to 5/20/26, and set an OSC re: dismissal for 5/20/26.  On 5/20/26, Plaintiff appeared at the hearing through her attorney and requested dismissal of the action without prejudice.    

 

2.     Motion to Vacate Dismissal

a.     Relief Sought by Plaintiff

On 6/12/26, Plaintiff filed this motion to vacate the dismissal, contending it was entered as a result of her attorney’s mistake or excusable neglect in failing to have the summons and complaint served by publication, which was not completed because the staff member handling the service took an unexpected medical leave. 

 

Plaintiff contends “the Court” dismissed the action on 5/20/26.  The Court’s minute order indicates Plaintiff requested dismissal.  To the extent the Court dismissed the action on its own motion, the dismissal was entered without any statutory or other authority for dismissing the action.  CCP §583.420(a)(1) permits the Court to dismiss an action if the summons and complaint are not served within two years after the action is filed.  §583.410 also permits the Court to dismiss an action if the case is not brought to trial within two years after filing.  There are various other statutes that permit dismissal of an action, none of which apply here.  Pursuant to Lyons v. Wickhorst (1986) 42 Cal.3d 911, 915-917, the Court’s powers to dismiss absent a statutory basis are extremely limited, and can only be applied in sham actions or where a delay of less than the statutory period has been prejudicial to the other party.  Because the dismissal was entered without statutory or other basis, to the extent the Court dismissed the action, the dismissal is void and the Court vacates the dismissal. 

 

To the extent Plaintiff dismissed the action, the Court finds the dismissal is not void.  Plaintiff’s attorney, however, adequately explains that the failure to serve Defendants was due to excusable neglect at the law firm, as the staff member tasked with serving Defendants by publication took unexpected medical leave.  To the extent Plaintiff dismissed the action, the motion to vacate the dismissal is granted per the mandatory relief provision in CCP §473(b).

 

b.     Future Hearings

Plaintiff has not, to date, filed proof of service of the summons and complaint on Defendants.  The Court sets an OSC re: proof of service for November 4, 2026.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.
