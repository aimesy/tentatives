DEPARTMENT M LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

Dept. M issues tentative rulings in many, but not all motion hearings. There is no set time at which tentatives are posted. Please do not call the staff to inquire if a tentative will be posted. 

If parties are satisfied with the ruling, parties may submit on the tentative. However, if an opposing party does not submit, they will be permitted to argue. Please check with the other side before calling the courtroom to submit. The staff does not keep track of which parties submitted and which did not, so please do not ask. 

If a matter is also a scheduling hearing (CMC, TSC, OSC etc) an appearance is still required even if a party submits on the tentative ruling.

Michael Thomas, et al. v. Rafael Ramos Robles, et al.

22STCV24812

1.      Michael Thomas’ Motion for Leave to Serve a Late Expert Designation

 

TENTATIVE RULING

     

      Michael Thomas’ Motion for Leave to Serve a Late Expert Designation is denied without prejudice.

 

Background

 

Plaintiffs filed their Complaint on August 2, 2022. Plaintiffs allege the following facts. Plaintiffs were involved in a motor vehicle collision with Defendants.

 

Motion to Leave to Serve a Late Expert Designation

 

Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.710 states:

(a) On motion of any party who has failed to submit expert witness information on the date specified in a demand for that exchange, the court may grant leave to submit that information on a later date.

(b) A motion under subdivision (a) shall be made a sufficient time in advance of the time limit for the completion of discovery under Chapter 8 (commencing with Section 2024.010) to permit the deposition of any expert to whom the motion relates to be taken within that time limit. Under exceptional circumstances, the court may permit the motion to be made at a later time.

(c) The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.

 

Code Civ. Proc., § 2034.720 states:

The court shall grant leave to submit tardy expert witness information only if all of the following conditions are satisfied:

(a) The court has taken into account the extent to which the opposing party has relied on the absence of a list of expert witnesses.

(b) The court has determined that any party opposing the motion will not be prejudiced in maintaining that party's action or defense on the merits.

(c) The court has determined that the moving party did all of the following:

(1) Failed to submit the information as the result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.

(2) Sought leave to submit the information promptly after learning of the mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.

(3) Promptly thereafter served a copy of the proposed expert witness information described in Section 2034.260 on all other parties who have appeared in the action.

(d) The order is conditioned on the moving party making the expert available immediately for a deposition under Article 3 (commencing with Section 2034.410), and on any other terms as may be just, including, but not limited to, leave to any party opposing the motion to designate additional expert witnesses or to elicit additional opinions from those previously designated, a continuance of the trial for a reasonable period of time, and the awarding of costs and litigation expenses to any party opposing the motion.

 

First, and most fundamentally, Plaintiff has failed to attach a proof of service with this motion. Thus, Plaintiff has failed to establish that Defendants were served with this motion. Second, Plaintiff failed to submit a meet and confer declaration as required by CCP § 2034.710. Third, Plaintiff failed to show that he complied with CCP § 2034.720(c)(3).

 

Thus, for the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to serve late expert witness designation is denied without prejudice.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

Luis F. Vargas v. Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC, et al.

25TRCV01104

1.      Luis F. Vargas’ Motion to Compel Further Responses to Form Interrogatories

2.      Luis F. Vargas’ Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories

3.      Luis F. Vargas’ Motion to Compel Further Responses to Request for Production of Documents

4.      Luis F. Vargas’ Motion to Compel Further Responses to Request for Admissions

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Luis F. Vargas’ Motions to Compel Further Responses to Form and Special Interrogatories, Request for Production of Documents, and Request for Admissions are granted, in part, and denied, in part.

 

Background

 

Plaintiff filed the Complaint on January 29, 2025. This is a “Lemon Law” case brought by Plaintiff concerning a 2023 Land Rover Range Rover Velar. Plaintiff alleges that the vehicle suffers from widespread defects including electrical and engine defects. Defendant was unable to repair their vehicle within a reasonable number of attempts. Plaintiff further alleges that Defendant knew that the vehicle suffered from the prevalent defects but nevertheless refused to repurchase the vehicle—a willful violation of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (“Song-Beverly Act”).

 

Motions to Compel Further Responses to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, Requests for Production of Documents, and Request for Admissions

 

Where responses to interrogatories have been served but the requesting party believes that they are deficient because the answers are evasive or incomplete, or, because an objection is without merit, that party may move for an order compelling a further response.  CCP § 2030.300(a).  Notice of the motion must be given within 45 days after service of verified responses in question, or any verified supplemental responses.  CCP § 2030.300(c).  The motion must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration in compliance with CCP § 2016.040.  CCP § 2030.300(b).

 

Where responses to requests for admissions have been served but requesting party believes that they are deficient because the answers are evasive or incomplete, or, because an objection is without merit, that party may move for an order compelling a further response.  CCP § 2033.290(a).  The motion must be made within 45 days after service of verified responses in question, or any verified supplemental responses.  CCP § 2033.290(c).  The motion must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration in compliance with CCP § 2016.040.  CCP § 2033.290(b).

 

A party responding to an inspection demand shall respond to each demand with one of the following: a statement the party will comply with the demand, a representation the party lacks the ability to comply with the demand, or an objection.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).)  A response to an inspection demand may be inadequate because it is evasive or incomplete; contains an incomplete statement of compliance; an inadequate, incomplete, or evasive representation of inability to comply; or meritless or overly general objections to a demand.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

 

If a demanding party believes the responding party responded inadequately, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)  “Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the demanding party and the responding party have agreed in writing, the demanding party waives any right to compel a further response to the demand.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (c).)   

 

Meet and Confer

 

            Plaintiff set forth meet and confer declarations in substantial compliance with CCP 2030.300(b), § 2031.310(b)(2), and 2033.290(b).  (Declarations, Astrid Souto).

 

            Motion to Compel

 

Plaintiff seeks further responses to Form Interrogatories 15.1 and 17.1.

 

Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to Form Interrogatories 15.1 and 17.1 is granted. First, and most fundamentally, Plaintiff contends that no verifications were served in connection with the responses. Unverified responses are tantamount to no responses at all. Appleton v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 632, 636.

 

In any event, even had verifications been served, the responses are evasive, incomplete, non-responsive, and/or Defendant has failed to justify its objections. Other than the response to sub-part (a) of interrogatory 15.1 in connection with the general denial, the factual responses that were required in sub-part (a) are conclusory. The responses to the other sub-parts are incomplete and not fully responsive to the interrogatories.

 

Plaintiff seeks further responses to Special Interrogatories 3 to 5, 7, 8, 10, and 13.

Special Interrogator (“SI”) 3: “Please describe in detail YOUR rules, policies, and procedures (if any) for complying with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as of the year 2023.”

 

SI 4: “Please describe in detail the rules, policies, and procedures (if any) that YOUR authorized repair facilities must follow in order to insure YOUR compliance with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as of the year 2023.”

 

SI 10: “If YOU contend YOU are under no obligation to repurchase or replace the SUBJECT VEHICLE in accordance with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act, please state all facts upon which YOU rely upon for this contention.”

 

SI 13: “Please state how many cumulative days YOUR records show that the SUBJECT VEHICLE was out of service by reason of any defect or condition complained of by Plaintiff.”

 

Plaintiff’s motion is granted as to special interrogatories 3, 4, 10, and 13. Defendant’s responses consist of simply a series of objections. However, Defendant failed to provide any evidence in its opposition that could justify and provide merit for the objections. The substantive response to interrogatory 13 essentially tells Plaintiff to look at a document. The response is non-responsive to the interrogatory. The substantive response to interrogatory 10 refers Plaintiff to Defendant’s response to form interrogatory 15.1. However, as already noted above, the response to form interrogatory 15.1 is insufficient. The substantive response to interrogatory 13 also simply tells Plaintiff to look at repair orders that are being produced. Again, this response is non-responsive to the interrogatory.

 

SI 5: “Please IDENTIFY each of YOUR agents or employees responsible for implementing YOUR rules, policies, and procedures for complying with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as of the year 2023.”

 

The motion is denied as to special interrogatory 5. The interrogatory is overly broad and vague. Perhaps a more specific tailored interrogatory that focuses on the vehicle in question would have been less broad and would have mandated a response. As currently worded, the interrogatory potentially encompasses numerous individuals throughout the numerous departments of Defendant’s corporate infrastructure.

 

SI 7: “Yes or no, prior to the filing of this lawsuit, did YOU have a policy in place to be informed of repeat repair visits from anyone other than the Plaintiff?”

 

SI 8: “If your answer to No. 7 above was yes, describe YOUR policy in detail.”

 

The motion is denied as to special interrogatories 7 and 8. The interrogatories are vague and ambiguous. First, it is unclear how overarching and sweeping this interrogatory is meant to be because as currently worded the interrogatory would apply to every single individual living on the planet other than Plaintiff. For example, without a clear definition of “repeat repair visit,” it is unclear if Plaintiff is seeking information as to all vehicles of any kind owned by anyone which was taken into for repair. The term “being informed” is also somewhat vague and unclear.

 

 

Plaintiff seeks further responses to Request for Production of Documents (“RFP” or “Request[s]”) Nos. 1, 11, 12, 14, and 15.

 

RFP 1: “All DOCUMENTS bearing vehicle identification number ("VIN") 1C4RJYB65N8748403.”

 

The motion is denied. The request is overly broad as the request appears to seek potentially every document in existence with respect to this vehicle without any regard for how it is intertwined with the facts of this case.

 

RFP 11: “All DOCUMENTS evidencing or setting out YOUR rules, policies, or procedures (if any) for complying with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as of the year 2023.”

 

RFP 12: “All DOCUMENTS evidencing or setting out the rules, policies, and procedures (if any) applicable to YOUR authorized repair facilities to further YOUR compliance with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as of the year 2023.”

 

RFP 14: “All written agreements between YOU and any of YOUR authorized repair facilities to which the SUBJECT VEHICLE has been presented concerning, reflecting, or memorializing rules, policies, or procedures regarding the inspection, service, diagnosis, analysis, evaluation, or repair of vehicles as of the year 2023.”

 

RFP 15: “All written agreements between YOU and any of YOUR authorized repair facilities to which the SUBJECT VEHICLE has been presented concerning, reflecting, or memorializing any rules, policies, or procedures to further YOUR compliance with the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as of the year 2023.”

 

Plaintiff’s motion is granted as to requests 11, 12, 14, and 15. The requests are designed to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence in this action. To the extent that Defendant may be withholding documents based on a privilege, Defendant is required to produce a privilege log.

 

Plaintiff seeks further responses to Request for Admissions (“RFA”) 3, 9, 13, and 15.

 

As to requests 3, 9, 13, and 14, the motion is granted.

 

RFA 3: “Admit YOUR authorized repair facility took more than a combined total of 30 days to attempt to repair the SUBJECT VEHICLE.”

 

RFA 9: “Admit YOU have failed to conform the SUBJECT VEHICLE to warranty within two (2) repair attempts.”

 

RFA 13: “Admit that YOU reviewed claims for reimbursement submitted to YOU by YOUR authorized repair facility for repairs performed on the SUBJECT VEHICLE.”

 

As to requests 3, 9, and 13, Defendant’s responses consist of simply a series of objections. Then, Defendant responded essentially stating that discovery is on-going. The responses fail to comply with CCP § 2033.210 et seq. In addition, Defendant failed to provide any evidence in its opposition that could justify and provide merit for the objections.

 

RFA 14: “Admit that YOU request charge-backs to YOUR authorized repair facilities for reimbursement claims if YOU determine that a repair that was claimed under YOUR express warranty should not have been covered under YOUR express warranty.”

 

As to request 14, Defendant’s response consists of simply a series of objections. Then, Defendant responded with a vague response stating: “Subject to and without waiving the foregoing objections, JLRNA responds: To the extent JLRNA understands this request, JLRNA admits subject to certain conditions and circumstances.” However, this response fails to comply with CCP 2033.220(b) which states: “Each answer shall: (1) Admit so much of the matter involved in the request as is true, either as expressed in the request itself or as reasonably and clearly qualified by the responding party. (2) Deny so much of the matter involved in the request as is untrue. (3) Specify so much of the matter involved in the request as to the truth of which the responding party lacks sufficient information or knowledge.”

 

Defendant is ordered to serve further verified responses to form interrogatories 15.1 and 17.1, special interrogatories 3, 4, 10, and 13, requests for production of documents 11, 12, 14, and 15, and requests for admissions 3, 9, 13, and 14 within 20 days of this date.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

Janet Kama v. Armine Pogosyan, et al.     

25TRCV03660

1.      Armine Pogosyan’s Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint        

 

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

            Armine Pogosyan’s Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint is granted.

 

            Background

 

            Plaintiff filed the Complaint on October 24, 2025. Plaintiff alleges the following facts. Defendant threw a clipboard at Plaintiff injuring her wrist. Plaintiff set forth a sole cause of action for Negligence.  

 

            Motion for Leave to File Cross-Complaint

 

            Pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. § 428.10(b)(1): “A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint or cross-complaint may file a cross-complaint setting forth either or both of the following: Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him. . .” The instant cross-complaint is compulsory since it “arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause of action which the plaintiff alleges in his complaint.” Code Civ. Proc., § 426.10.

 

            Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50 states:

            “(a) A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint.

            (b) Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.

            (c) A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint except one filed within the time specified in subdivision (a) or (b). Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.”

 

            Pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. § 426.50: “A party who fails to plead a cause of action subject to the requirements of this article, whether through oversight, inadvertence, mistake, neglect, or other cause, may apply to the court for leave to amend his pleading, or to file a cross-complaint, to assert such cause at any time during the course of the action. The court, after notice to the adverse party, shall grant, upon such terms as may be just to the parties, leave to amend the pleading, or to file the cross-complaint, to assert such cause if the party who failed to plead the cause acted in good faith. This subdivision shall be liberally construed to avoid forfeiture of causes of action.”

 

            “A policy of liberal construction of section 426.50 to avoid forfeiture of causes of action is imposed on the trial court.  A motion to file a cross-complaint at any time during the course of the action must be granted unless bad faith of the moving party is demonstrated where forfeiture would otherwise result.  Factors such as oversight, inadvertence, neglect, mistake or other cause, are insufficient grounds to deny the motion unless accompanied by bad faith.”  Silver Organizations Ltd. v. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 98–99.  Mere delay in filing the proposed Cross-Complaint is not sufficient to deny a motion absent substantial injustice and prejudice.  Foot's Transfer & Storage Co. v. Superior Court (1980) 114 Cal.App.3d 897, 903–904. Actual bad faith must be demonstrated.

 

            Defendant moves for leave to file a Cross-Complaint on the grounds that the proposed causes of action arise out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as Plaintiff’s Complaint, and, thus, the granting of leave to file the cross-complaint is mandatory pursuant to CCP § 426.10. Defendant contends that the causes of action are related to the Complaint which seeks damages for personal injuries. Defendant seeks leave to file a Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff as well as proposed Cross-Complainants Radnet Managed Imaging Services, Inc. and DOES 1 to 30.

 

            The interest of justice supports granting leave to file the Cross-Complaint to allow all disputes between all parties to be adjudicated in one action.  There is no showing that moving Defendant acted in bad faith in making this motion. There is no showing that granting the motion will cause Plaintiff substantial prejudice and injustice.

 

            Therefore, as there is no showing that Defendant acted in bad faith in making this motion, Defendant’s motion for leave to file a Cross-Complaint is granted.

            Defendant is directed to file and serve the proposed Cross-Complaint as to all parties who have already appeared in this action within five days of this date.

 

            As to Radnet Managed Imaging Services, Inc. an OSC re: dismissal for failure to serve Cross-Complaint is set for ____________.

 

            Defendant is ordered to give notice of this ruling.
