Case Number: 25STCV32859    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 517

DEMURRER

Pending before the Court is the demurrer of Defendants Aykem, LLC, Gennady Mazin, and West LA LLC to the third and fourth causes of action in the First Amended Complaint of Plaintiff Valley 5 LLC.  Both of those causes of action are for cancellation of a written instrument based on fraud.  Aykem and Mazin are named Defendants in the third cause of action. West LA LLC is a  named Defendant in the fourth cause of action.  Valley 5 LLC asserts that all three Defendants participated in a fraud orchestrated by Defendant Alexander Mirsky.

The Court is sustaining with leave to amend the demurrer of Aykem and Mazin with respect to the third cause of action.  There are no allegations in the First Amended Complaint of any fraudulent representation that Aykem and Mazin made to Valley 5 LLC.  Nor are the 
allegations in the First Amended Complaint sufficient to attribute Mirsky's purported fraud to Aykem and Mazin.  In particular, there is no allegation that Mirsky was an agent or representative of Aykem and Mazin.   By contrast, the Court is overruling the demurrer of West LA LLC with respect to the fourth cause of action.  The First Amended Complaint alleges that Mirsky controls West LA LLC.  The allegations of fraudulent representations that Mirsky made to Valley 5 LLC are sufficient to have those representations attributed to West LA LLC.  

 VALLEY 5 LLC'S MOTION FOR RELIEF FROM WAIVER OF OBJECTIONS AND AYKEM LLC'S MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER DISCOVERY RESPONSES

At the initial hearing on these motions, the Court indicated that it was inclined to deny Valley 5 LLC's motion for relief from its waiver of objections to Aykem LLC's requests for productions ("RFPs") and special interrogatories ("SROGs") and grant Aykem LLC's motions to compel further responses to the RFPs and SROGs.  The Court is adhering to that tentative ruling.  Valley 5 LLC's motion for relief from waiver of objections should be denied because Valley 5 LLC did not, after missing the deadline for responding to the RFPs and SROGs, provide responses that substantially comply with applicable requirements of the Code of Civil Procedure, which is necessary to obtain such relief. Thus, as requested in Aykem's motions, Valley 5 LLC must provide further responses to the RFPs and SROGs without any objections.  Valley 5 LLC shall provide those further responses within 21 days of this order.  The Court is, however, denying Aykem, LLC's request in its motions for an order imposing monetary sanctions against Valley 5 LLC in the form of attorney's fees that Aykem, LLC incurred in connection with the motions.  In the Court's view, Valley 5 LLC was substantially justified in providing responses that contained objections even though the Court has concluded that relief from the waiver of the objections is unavailable.  Thus,monetary sanctions are unwarranted.

Case Number: 26STCV08866    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 517

Plaintiff JMJ Creative, Inc. ("JMJ") sued a corporate entity, Defendant Sisly Corporation (“Sisly), as well as two individuals, Defendants Jeff Namki Choi (“Choi”) and Young Jaw Rafael Lee Shin (“Shin”).  In its operative First Amended Complaint (“the FAC”), JMJ alleges that it entered into a series of contracts with Sisly for JMJ’s sale to Sisly of certain goods, and that there is an unpaid balance on some of those goods.  JMJ further alleges that Sisly initiated purchase orders from JMJ but that Sisly unilaterally cancelled the orders.  As to Choi and Shin, the FAC alleges that they are the owners and shareholders of Sisly and are the alter egos of Sisly.  The FAC asserts causes of action  against Sisly, Choi, and Shin for breach of contract, common  count, and account stated.

Pending before the Court is the demurrer of Choi and Shin (collectively, “the Individual Defendants”) to all of the causes of actions in the FAC.  The Court is sustaining the demurrer with leave to amend.   JMJ shall file and serve a Second Amended Complaint within 21 days of this order. The case management conference that is scheduled for September 21, 2026 is vacated.  It is reset for February 16, 2027 at 8:30 a.m.

The Individual Defendants’ primary argument in their demurrer is that the allegations in the FAC are insufficient to impose liability on them for Sisly’s actions on an alter ego theory.  The Court agrees.

The general rule that is officers and shareholders of a corporation are not personally liable for the alleged wrongdoings of the corporation.  (Sonora Diamond Corp. v. Superior Court (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 523, 538.)  To establish personal liability of individual defendants for corporate wrongs based on an alter ego theory, the plaintiff must demonstrate (1) a unity of interest of ownership between the individuals and the corporation such that the separate personalities of the corporation and the individuals do not exist in reality, and (2)

 

and a resulting injustice if the corporation is treated as the sole responsible actor.   (Ibid; see also Leek v. Cooper (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 399, 415.) 

In the FAC, JMS asserted that there is a unity of interest between Sisly and the Individual Defendants and that an injustice would result if Sisly’s corporate veil is not pierced so as to permit the imposition of personal liability on the Defendants.  But JMS alleged no specific facts to support that assertion.  Under alter ego case caselaw, such facts could include, for example, allegations that Sisly was inadequately capitalized, existing as a mere shell and conduit for the Individual Defendants’ affairs, who used the corporation's assets as their own, and that corporate formalities were not observed.  (Rutherford Holdings, LLC v. Plaza Del Rey (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 221, 235.)  As the Individual Defendants pointed out in their demurrer, no allegations of this kind were made in the FAC.  JMJ failed to respond to this argument in its opposition to the demurrer.  It simply fell back on the broad assertion of alter ego liability in the FAC.  That does not cut it.

All of the causes of action in the FAC against the Individual Defendants depend on the alter ego theory.  Because the allegations in the FAC are insufficient to establish alter ego liability, each cause of action against the Individual Defendants fails.


Case Number: BC716949    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 517

 The Court is denying the Defendants' motion for summary judgment, or in the alternative, summary adjudication.  Through the declaration of its principal, Pinkal Jogani, Plaintiff has presented evidence that shows the presence of disputed issues of material fact that preclude the entry of summary judgment or summary adjudication for the Defendants.  The Court is overruling the Defendants' evidentiary objections to the Jogani declaration.
