The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with these motions.

MOTION TO COMPEL ANSWERS AT DEPOSITION AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS 

Defendant Dave & Buster’s Inc. (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order (1) compelling plaintiff Stewart Marroquin’s (“Plaintiff”) deposition answers and (2) that Plaintiff pay the sum of $2,620 in sanctions for misuse of the discover process.

The court grants Defendant’s motion to compel answers to questions 1, 3, 5-7, and 9 as set forth in Defendant’s separate statement, filed May 27, 2025.

The court denies the motion to compel an answer to question 2 because the subject of this question is addressed in question 1.

The court denies the motion to compel an answer to question 4 because Defendant has not shown that Defendant included a request to produce Plaintiff’s ID in the notice of deposition.

The court denies the motion to compel an answer to question 8 because Defendant has not cited any authority to support its position that Plaintiff was required to sign any papers during his deposition.

The court grants Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,760.00 ($200/hour x 8.5 hours + $60 filing fee) against Plaintiff.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (j).)

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

            Defendants Dave & Buster’s, Inc. and Christian Singer (“Defendants”) move the court for an order (1) compelling Plaintiff to provide further responses to Defendants’ Special Interrogatories, Set One and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,800.00 against Plaintiff.

            The court denies Defendants’ motion to compel further response to Special Interrogatory no. 1 because the interrogatory seeks information protected by the right to privacy.  (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 552.) 

            The court grants Defendants’ motion to compel further response to Special Interrogatory no. 16 in part, limited to the time period October 1, 2019 to the present.

            The court denies Defendants’ request for sanctions because, in light of the mixed results of the ruling, the court finds that the circumstances presented would make the imposition of sanctions unjust.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (d).)

MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO REQUEST FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

Defendants Dave & Buster’s, Inc. and Christian Singer (“Defendants”) move the court for an order (1) compelling Plaintiff to provide further responses to Defendants’ Request for Production of Documents, Set One and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,800.00 against Plaintiff.

The court denies Defendants’ motion to compel further response to Request for Production no. 15 because the request (1) is overbroad since it includes information and documents that are neither related to the subject matter involved in this action nor appear reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence, and (2) seeks documents and information protected by the right to privacy.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2017.010; Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 552.)       

The court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions because Defendant’s motion is denied.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).)

ORDER

The court grants in part defendant Dave & Buster’s, Inc.’s motion to compel answers at deposition and request for monetary sanctions as follows.  

The court orders plaintiff Stewart Marroquin to answer, at a deposition to be taken by counsel for defendant Dave & Buster’s, Inc., questions 1, 3, 5-7, and 9 set forth in the “Separate Statement in Support of Defendant Dave & Buster’s, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff Stewart Marroquin’s Deposition Answers” filed by defendant Dave & Buster’s, Inc. on May 27, 2025, and reasonable follow-up questions within 30 days of service of this order. 

            The court orders plaintiff Stewart Marroquin to pay monetary sanctions to defendant Dave & Buster’s, Inc. in the amount of $1,760.00 within 30 days of the date of service of this order.

The court grants in part defendants Dave & Buster’s, Inc. and Christian Singer’s motion to compel further responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One and monetary sanctions as follows.

            Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.300, the court orders plaintiff Stewart Marroquin to serve on defendants Dave & Buster’s, Inc. further, a full and complete verified answer, without objections, to defendants Dave & Buster’s, Inc. and Christian Singer’s Special Interrogatory no. 16, which complies with Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.210-2030.250, within 30 days of the date of service of this order.¿      

            The court denies defendants Dave & Buster’s, Inc. and Christian Singer’s motion to compel further responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One and monetary sanctions.

            The court orders defendant Dave & Buster’s, Inc. to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

DEFENDANT GENERAL MOTORS, LLC’S motion FOR COMPLIANCE PURSUANT TO C.C.P.  871.26; REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant General Motors LLC        

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Plaintiff Arcelia Diaz

Motion for Compliance Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Sanctions

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

Although the opposition was untimely filed and served, the court will consider it because Defendant General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) will not be prejudiced and was able to file a reply.

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

            The court denies Defendant’s request for judicial notice of Exhibit A attached to the Declaration of Kaitlyn Lasater.

 

DISCUSSION

Defendant moves for an order (1) for compliance requiring plaintiff Arcelia Diaz (“Plaintiff”) to provide the complete production of documents and written disclosure of information required under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivisions (b) and (f) and (2) imposing monetary sanctions against Plaintiff’s attorneys of record, Strategic Legal Practices, APC, in the sum of $1,500.00.

The court grants the motion for compliance pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 as to the incidental damage information because Plaintiff refuses to provide these documents.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subds. (b), (f)(5).)  Although Plaintiff asserts that these documents are in Defendant’s possession, Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 does not allow the plaintiff to merely state that these documents are in the defendant’s possession.  Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 states that “plaintiff shall provide the following documents” without qualification or exception.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subds. (f), (g).)

            The court denies the motion for compliance pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 as to the sales contract, repair orders, and insurance information because Plaintiff timely provided the sales contract, “Retail Installment Sale Contract – Simple Finance Charge (with Arbitration Provision)” and multiple repair orders on December 24, 2025, and Plaintiff stated that the disclosure regarding whether the motor vehicle has been involved in a collision or accidents reported to insurance prior to the nonconformity is not applicable and, thus, Plaintiff need not provide insurance information.  (Declaration of Deepali Shah, Ex. 1.)  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subds. (f)(1), (4), (g)(7).)  Because Defendant filed its responsive pleading, the Demurrer, on October 29, 2025, the deadline to produce the documents was December 29, 2025.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (b).)

            The court grants Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,500.00 against Strategic Legal Practices, APC because Plaintiff has not shown good cause for not producing documents regarding incidental damages.  (Code. Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (j)(1).)

 

 

ORDER

            The court grants defendant General Motors LLC’s motion for compliance and sanctions as follows.  

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivisions (b) and (f)(5), the court orders plaintiff Arcelia Diaz to produce documents detailing all underlying claimed incidental damages.

The court orders Strategic Legal Practices, APC to pay monetary sanctions to defendant General Motors LLC in the amount of $1,500 within 15 business days of the date of service of this order.  

            The court orders defendant General Motors LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

defendant prime/park labrea titleholder, llc’s motion to strike portions of plaintiffs’ complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTIES:              Defendant Prime/Park LaBrea Titleholder, LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTIES:    Plaintiffs Kristen Taylor-Ladd and Meagan Donovan

Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant Prime/Park LaBrea Titleholder (“Defendant”) moves the court to strike from plaintiffs Kristen Taylor-Ladd (“Taylor-Ladd”) and Meagan Donovan’s (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) Complaint all allegations purporting to state a basis for punitive or exemplary damages and the corresponding punitive/exemplary damages request in the Prayer.

The court grants Defendant’s motion to strike because Plaintiffs have failed to allege facts establishing that Defendant’s conduct was authorized by an officer, director, or managing agent of Defendant.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 436; Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (b) [“With respect to a corporate employer, the advance knowledge and conscious disregard, authorization, ratification or act of oppression, fraud, or malice must be on the part of an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporation”].)  Plaintiffs only allege that Defendant’s head maintenance worker, Defendant’s workers, and Defendant’s property management team ignored Plaintiffs, dismissed Plaintiffs, or incorrectly marked repairs as completed, but fail to allege that any of these individuals were an officer, director, or managing agent of Defendant.  (Compl., ¶¶ 25, 28, 29, 32, 33.)

ORDER

            The court grants defendant Prime/Park LaBrea Titleholders, LLC’s motion to strike portions of the Complaint alleging punitive or exemplary damages and the corresponding punitive/exemplary damages request in the Prayer with leave to amend.

The court grants plaintiffs Kristen Taylor-Ladd and Meagan Donovan 20 days leave to file an amended complaint to cure the defects regarding the punitive or exemplary damages allegations set forth in this order.  

            The court orders defendant Prime/Park LaBrea Titleholders, LLC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
