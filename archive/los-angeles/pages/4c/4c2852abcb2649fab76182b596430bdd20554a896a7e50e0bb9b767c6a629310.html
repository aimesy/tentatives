Case Number: 25NWCV00012    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: C

REYES vs CLASSIC EXTERIORS & CONSTRUCTION

CASE NO.:  25NWCV00012

HEARING: 7/27/26 @ 9:30 AM

 

#19

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Defendants RYAN WINTERS and CHRIS KRANES’s Unopposed Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED in part without leave to amend and OVERRULED in part.  Defendants Ryan Winters and Chris Kranes to answer within 10 days. 

 

Moving party to give NOTICE.

 

 

Defendants RYAN WINTERS and CHRIS KRANES (Defendants) demur to Pro Per Plaintiff STELLA REYES’s (Plaintiff) First Amended Complaint (FAC) on the grounds that it is uncertain, ambiguous, and unintelligible. Additionally, Defendants demur to the FAC on the grounds that each cause of action is barred by the applicable statutes of limitation.  Defendants contend that they were first named in the First Amended Complaint on November 26, 2025, but the conduct alleged against them occurred in 2020.

 

No opposition as of July 24, 2026. 

 

 

BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff filed this breach of contract lawsuit against Defendant Classic Exteriors & Construction on January 3, 2025.  By way of reference, the Complaint alleges that Plaintiff is the owner of 16240 Shady Valley Lane, Whittier, CA 90603.  Between July 2020 and August 2020, Plaintiff entered into a series of eight contracts with Defendant to make various repairs to the Property.  Defendant began work on site at the subject Property on July 27,2020 and stopped working at the subject property on August 17, 2020.  The Defendant did not complete the work that was promised to Plaintiff.  Furthermore, Defendant left the work incomplete and it was substandard.  Defendant no longer answered the phone.  Defendant failed to contact the Plaintiff to complete the work promised.  Defendant went out of business on December 23, 2020.  Plaintiff received an invoice from a collection agency, Service Finance Company, LLC., on behalf of Defendant on or about October 09, 2024 demanding the amount of $21,656.03. The due date on the invoice was on December 23, 2020.

 

On November 20, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff leave to amend the Complaint to add the individual owners, officers, and/or agents of Classic Exteriors & Construction, Inc. who personally committed the acts of fraud and financial elder abuse alleged in the original Complaint.  The corporation was dissolved on December 30, 2020, but Plaintiff sought leave to name the owners and officers of the corporation who engaged in fraudulent acts before the corporation’s dissolution.

 

The operative First Amended Complaint was filed on November 26, 2025.  Plaintiff alleges that Ryan Winters, Chris Kranes, and Kelli Wilkins personally engaged in the fraud and elder financial abuse, and these individuals may be liable personally as owners, officers, or agents of the corporation. (FAC, ¶ 4.) Plaintiff dismissed Kelli Wilkins on May 5, 2026. 

 

Plaintiff asserts the following causes of action:

1.    Breach of Contract

2.    Breach of Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing  

3. Fraud

4. Financial Elder Abuse

5. Unfair Business Practices

6. Negligence

7. Unjust Enrichment

8. Declaratory Relief

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (CCP §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732, internal citations omitted.) 

“Liberality in permitting amendment is the rule, if a fair opportunity to correct any defect has not been given.” (Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1227.) It is an abuse of discretion for the court to deny leave to amend where there is any reasonable possibility that plaintiff can state a good cause of action. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) The burden is on plaintiff to show¿in what manner¿plaintiff can amend the complaint, and¿how¿that amendment will change the legal effect of the pleading. ¿(Id.) 

DISCUSSION

 

First through Seventh Causes of Action

 

Defendants demur to the FAC on the grounds that each cause of action is time barred because they were first named in the FAC on November 26, 2025, but the conduct alleged against them occurred in 2020. 

 

Contract-based claims are governed by Code of Civil Procedure section 337, which provides for a four-year limitations period for written agreements; section 339 subjects oral contracts to a two-year limitations period. Fraud, unjust enrichment sounding in fraud, and negligence causing property damage are all subject to a three-year statute of limitations. (Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 338, subds. (b), (d).) UCL claims are subject to a four-year limitations period. (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17208.) As are elder abuse claims. (Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code §15657.7.) Breaches of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing are subject to a two-year limitations period. (Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 339(1).)

 

Under the delayed discovery rule, the statute of limitations begins to run when the plaintiff suspects or should suspect that her injury was caused by wrongdoing. (Metabyte, Inc. v. Technicolor S.A. (2023) 94 Cal.App.5th 265, 281.) As previously noted, the original Complaint alleged that “Defendant began work on site at the subject Property on July 27,2020 and stopped working at the subject property on August 17, 2020.” (Complaint, ¶ 16, italics added.) The FAC contains no such allegation, but it does state the following:

 

“Each cause of action from the original Complaint is realleged against all Defendants: Classic Exteriors, Winters, Kranes, and Wilkins. This includes: breach of contract, breach of implied covenant, fraud, elder abuse, unfair business practices, negligence, unjust enrichment, and declaratory relief. The allegations are updated to state that Winters, Kranes, and Wilkins personally made misrepresentations, directed the fraudulent scheme, and benefited from funds obtained from Plaintiff.” (FAC, p. 3.)

 

To the extent that the FAC incorporates the factual allegations in the original Complaint, the first through seventh causes of action are time-barred because statute of limitations began to run as early as August 17, 2020, when Defendants stopped working on the Property, and no later than December 23, 2020, when Classic Exteriors & Constructions, Inc. dissolved.  The lawsuit was filed on January 3, 2025, after the four-year statute of limitations had already expired. 

 

Even if the FAC superseded the original Complaint, which is the general rule, Plaintiff does not explain why the Court should disregard the allegations in the original Complaint relevant to discovery of wrongdoing. Allegations in the original pleading that rendered it vulnerable to demurrer cannot simply be omitted without explanation in the amended pleading. (Vallejo Develop. Co. v. Beck Develop. Co. (1994) 24 Cal.App.4th 929, 946 (discussing sham pleading doctrine).) Thus, Plaintiff is barred from asserting that the statute of limitations was tolled.

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the first through seventh causes of action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend. 

 

8th CAUSE OF ACTION: Declaratory Relief

 

To qualify for declaratory relief, an action must present two essential elements: (1) a proper subject of declaratory relief, and (2) an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to the rights or obligations of a party. Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 1060. The “actual controversy” language in the declaratory judgment statute encompasses a probable future controversy relating to the legal rights and duties of the parties, but it does not embrace controversies that are conjectural, anticipated to occur in the future, or an attempt to obtain an advisory opinion from the court. Cal. Civ. Proc. Code § 1060. (Lee v. Silveira (2016) 6 Cal.App.5th 527.)

 

Plaintiff alleges she “received an invoice from a collection agency, Service Finance Company, LLC., on behalf of Defendant on or about October 09, 2024 demanding the amount of $21,656.03. The due date on the invoice was on December 23, 2020.” (Complaint, ¶ 19.) Plaintiff seeks “Declaratory relief determining that Plaintiff is not liable for any additional charges or invoices demanded by Defendants.” (FAC, p. 4.) The Court finds that Plaintiff alleges sufficient facts to state a cause of action for declaratory relief.

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the eighth cause of action is OVERRULED.  Defendants Chris Kranes and Ryan Winters to answer within 10 days.

Case Number: 25NWCV04567    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: C

LEE, et al. vs  LOYD JONES, JR.

CASE NO.:  25NWCV04567

HEARING: 7/27/26 @ 9:30 AM

 

#20

TENTATIVE RULING

 

      I.          Defendant FRANCISCO RAMOS (sued herein as DOE 1)’s Unopposed Demurrer is SUSTAINED with 20 days leave to amend.  

 

    II.          The Case Management Conference is ADVANCED from August 5, 2026 to today and CONTINUED to October 15, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. in Dept. SE-C. 

 

Moving party to give NOTICE.

 

 

Defendant FRANCISCO RAMOS, DOE 1, (Ramos) generally demurs to Plaintiffs MEN JA LEE and SOO YEON LEE’s (collectively “Plaintiffs”) Complaint on the grounds that it is uncertain, ambiguous, and unintelligible as to Ramos.

 

No opposition filed as of July 24, 2026. 

 

Background

 

Plaintiffs allege that on September 6, 2025, WOON TEAK LEE (Decedent) went for a walk in his own neighborhood. At approximately 6:46 a.m Decedent was walking westbound along the north curbline of 166th Street near Graystone Avenue, a quiet residential street in the City of Norwalk. (Complaint, ¶ 10.) Decedent was attacked by Defendant LOYD JONES JR.’s two Rottweiler dogs. (Complaint, ¶ 11.)  Emergency medical services rushed Decedent to Saint Francis Medical Center. He was admitted to the Intensive Care Unit in critical condition. (Complaint, ¶ 13.)  On September 7, 2025, at approximately 6:36 a.m., Decedent was pronounced dead. (Complaint, ¶ 15.) 

 

Plaintiffs assert the following causes of action for:

1. STRICT LIABILITY [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- DOG BITE STATUTE (CIV. CODE § 3342)

2. STRICT LIABILITY [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- DANGEROUS PROPENSITIES (COMMON LAW)

3. NEGLIGENCE [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- FAILURE TO PROPERLY RESTRAIN DOGS

4. NEGLIGENCE [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- FAILURE TO MAINTAIN ADEQUATE ENCLOSURES

5. NEGLIGENCE [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- FAILURE TO WARN OF DANGEROUS PROPENSITIES

6. NEGLIGENCE [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- NEGLIGENT KEEPING OF DANGEROUS ANIMALS

7. PREMISES LIABILITY [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- DANGEROUS CONDITION ON PROPERTY

8. NEGLIGENCE PER SE [WRONGFUL DEATH AND SURVIVAL ACTION] -- VIOLATION OF ANIMAL CONTROL LAWS

9. INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS [SURVIVAL ACTION]

10. NEGLIGENT INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS [SURVIVAL ACTION]

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (CCP §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732, internal citations omitted.) 

“Liberality in permitting amendment is the rule, if a fair opportunity to correct any defect has not been given.” (Angie M. v. Superior Court (1995) 37 Cal.App.4th 1217, 1227.) It is an abuse of discretion for the court to deny leave to amend where there is any reasonable possibility that plaintiff can state a good cause of action. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) The burden is on plaintiff to show¿in what manner¿plaintiff can amend the complaint, and¿how¿that amendment will change the legal effect of the pleading. ¿(Id.) 

Discussion

 

Doe 1 Amendment

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 474 allows a plaintiff to name fictitious defendants, or “Does.” The complaint must state a cause of action against each Doe defendant. (Pacific Coast Refrigeration, Inc. v. Badger (1975) 52 Cal.App.3d 233, 249–250.) It must allege that the plaintiff is ignorant of the Doe defendant's name. (Code Civ. Proc., § 474.) Moreover, the plaintiff must actually be ignorant of the Doe defendant's name, i.e., “ignorant of the facts giving rise to a cause of action against that defendant.” (Fuller v. Tucker (2000) 84 Cal.App.4th 1163, 1170.) When the plaintiff discovers a Doe defendant's true name, he or she must amend the complaint accordingly. (Code Civ. Proc., § 474; see Jonson v. Weinstein (1967) 249 Cal.App.2d 954, 957.) Provided these requirements are satisfied, the amendment is deemed to “relate[ ] back” to the filing date of the original complaint for purposes of the statute of limitations. (Fireman's Fund Ins. Co. v. Sparks Construction, Inc. (2004) 114 Cal.App.4th 1135, 1143.)

 

Here, while Plaintiffs may amend the complaint to include Ramos, Plaintiffs must also allege sufficient facts to provide Ramos with notice as to what the claims asserted against him are and why.  (Davaloo v. State Farm Ins. Co. (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 409, 415.) Plaintiffs fail to allege that Ramos owned, harbored, or kept the dogs; had any duty to restrain or contain them; had knowledge of any dangerous propensity; controlled, owned, or maintained any premises where the attack occurred; or engaged in any act or omission giving rise to liability. Indeed, Plaintiffs allege:

 

At all times relevant hereto, Defendant LOYD JONES JR. was the owner, keeper, harborer, and person in control of two (2) Rottweiler dogs that attacked and killed Decedent WOON TEAK LEE.

 

(Complaint, ¶ 8.)

 

The true names and capacities of Defendants DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, are unknown to Plaintiffs at this time. Plaintiffs will seek leave to amend this Complaint to state the true names and capacities when ascertained

 

(Complaint, ¶ 9.)

 

Plaintiffs make no allegations against the Doe Defendants. Moreover, Plaintiffs’ amendment fails to make any additional factual allegations as to Ramos. In demurrer, Ramos contends that “Plaintiffs subsequently identified Defendant RAMOS as a Doe Defendant based on the alleged claim by Defendant LOYD JONES JR. that Defendant RAMOS permitted Defendant LOYD JONES JR. to use his property to house his dogs.” (Dem., p. 14: 16-18.) These allegations are absent from the Complaint, defendant Jones’ Answer, and the Doe 1 Amendment to the Complaint. Therefore, there is an absence of allegations against Ramos specifically and Doe Defendants generally that could be attributed to Ramos.

 

Accordingly, the demurrer is SUSTAINED with 20 days leave to amend.


Case Number: 26NWCV01167    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: C

ALTERO, INC. V. GREENField, et al.

CASE NO.:  26NWCV01167

HEARING:  7/27/26 @ 9:30 AM

#21

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Pro Per Defendant ADAM LUJAN GREENFIELD’s Motion to Set Aside Default is DENIED.

Moving Party to give NOTICE.

 

Pro per Defendant ADAM LUJAN GREENFIELD (Defendant) moves for relief from default pursuant to CCP § 473 subd., (b).

Background

This unlawful detainer complaint was filed on April 2, 2026 and concerns commercial property located at 6026 S. Atlantic Blvd., Maywood, CA 90270 (the “Property”).  Plaintiff ALTERO, INC. alleges that on or about March 3, 2026, Plaintiff acquired ownership of the Property by purchasing the commercial building in which the Property is located at a nonjudicial foreclosure sale. The Property is a unit located within a commercial building. (Complaint, ¶ 9.) Defendants ADAM LUJAN GREENFIELD, an individual; MR. C’S BARBER SHOP, a business entity unknown; and ALL UNKNOWN OCCUPANTS, TENANTS, AND SUBTENANTS are currently in possession of, and are occupying, the Property, or claim a right to possess or occupy the Property, without any right to do so. No occupant of the Property has provided Plaintiff with a copy of any lease or rental agreement. (Id. at ¶ 10.) Defendant ADAM LUJAN GREENFIED is the former foreclosed owner of the Property. (RFJN Ex. 8.)

Defendant’s default was entered on May 4, 2026.  Default judgment has not been entered. 

The Court notes that Plaintiff has filed five other unlawful detainer actions regarding the same building and against the same parties.  Plaintiff alleges that Defendant is in unlawful possession of five other units in the building. (Ostayan Decl., ¶ 4.) In each case, Defendant has filed identical motions to set aside default.  Case Nos. 26NWCV01166 and 26NWCV01172 are pending in Dept. SE-L.  Case No. 26NWCV01177 is pending in Dept. SE-P.  In these three cases, Defendant’s motions to set aside default have yet to be heard.  Case Nos. 26NWCV01169 and 26NWCV01176 are pending in Dept. SE-R.  In these two cases, Defendant’s motions to set aside default were granted by Judge Gasdia on July 6, 2026. 

Plaintiff filed a Notice of Related Case on July 3, 2026 which has not been ruled upon by Dept. SE-L. 

Request for Judicial Notice

Plaintiff’s Request for Judicial Notice is GRANTED.

Legal Standard

Discretionary and mandatory relief is available to parties from “judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her.” (CCP § 473(b).) Discretionary relief is available under the statute as “the court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. (CCP § 473(b).) Alternatively, mandatory relief is available when “accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” (CCP § 473(b).) Under this statute, an application for discretionary or mandatory relief must be made no more than six months after entry of the judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding from which relief is sought.  (CCP § 473(b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.)

“‘[W]hen relief under section 473¿is¿available, there is a strong public policy in favor of granting relief and allowing the requesting party his or her day in court.” (Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal.4th 975, 981-982.)

Discussion

Plaintiff’s Request for Entry of Default was entered on May 4, 2026. Defendant filed this Motion to Set Aside Default on May 15, 2026, well within the six months allowed by CCP § 473. Thus, there is a strong public policy in favor of granting Defendant’s Motion to Set Aside Default.

Defendant contends that he was under impression and believing in good faith that the attorney that he had hired, Chris Brooks, to represent him in the case had taken the necessary steps to file the Answer or any other pleadings in his defense. To Defendant’s detriment the attorney he hired failed to file an answer or any other pleadings in the case, exposing Defendant to default judgment. It was only when Defendant received the Notice of Default that he learned that his attorney had failed to file an answer on his behalf. (Motion, Exh. A-B.)

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Defendant did not hire Chris Brooks as his attorney, nor has Brooks provided any attorney’s affidavit of fault. Instead, Defendant appears to have hired HFCI, LLC, which is not a law firm, per its website, and is not registered with the California Secretary of State. As noted in the signature block of “Chris Brooks Customer Service, HFCI LLC,” Brooks does not purport to be an attorney. (Motion, Ex. B.) Indeed, as shown on the California State Bar Website, the only attorney by that name practicing in California is an attorney named Christopher Lemmel Brooks who is employed at the CA Dept of Tax and Fee Administration in Sacramento.

The Court finds that defendant has failed to demonstrate mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.  Defendant asserts he was not aware of the six lawsuits filed against him until after default was entered on May 4, 2026. (Greenfield Decl., ¶ 8.) Yet, he acknowledges service of the summons and complaint for each case on April 17, 2026. (Id. at ¶ 2.) The Court rejects Defendant’s assertion that he hired Chris Brooks as an attorney to file an answer. Chris Brooks does not hold himself out as an attorney in his correspondence to Defendant.  In the signature block for his email to Defendant on May 4, 2026, Brooks indicates his job title is “client services.” (Greenfield Decl., ¶ 3., Ex. A.) In the signature block for another (undated) email, Brooks indicates his job title is “customer service.” (Greenfield Decl., ¶ 6., Ex. B.) This Court is aware that Dept. R ruled it was reasonable that Defendant, acting in pro per, believed Brooks or another individual working at HFCI LLC would file a responsive pleading on his behalf. (Case Nos. 26NWCV01169 and 26NWCV01176, 7/6/26 Minute Orders.) This Court is unable to reach the same conclusion.  In this Court’s view, any belief Defendant may have held that Brooks is an attorney is unreasonable.  Even now, after no answer was filed on Defendant’s behalf, Defendant still maintains that Brooks is an attorney. (See, Greenfield Decl., ¶ 3 (“I hired Chris Brooks, Esq. to represent me on this matter.”) Defendant’s claimed ignorance is simply not credible.  Because Defendant has not filed an attorney affidavit of fault, relief is discretionary, not mandatory. In the exercise of its discretion, this Court finds that Defendant has failed to demonstrate mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect.

Accordingly, Defendant’s Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment is DENIED.
