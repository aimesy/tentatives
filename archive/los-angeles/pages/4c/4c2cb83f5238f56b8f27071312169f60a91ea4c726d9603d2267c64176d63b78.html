DEPARTMENT 731 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV09038    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 731

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PETITIONS FOR APPROVAL OF COMPROMISE OF CLAIM FOR MINORS



  1. Background Facts

Plaintiffs sue Defendant Manual Regalado pursuant to an April 10, 2026, Complaint alleging unhabitable rental conditions from Defendant, Plaintiff’s landlord, resulting in injuries to Plaintiff, supporting seven causes of action.

On March 3, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a notice of settlement, noting a conditional settlement with an expected dismissal of June 1, 2026.

On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff Jose Luis Galindo Vargas, as parent and guardian ad litem, filed petitions for approval of the settlement as relates to minor Plaintiffs, Brianna Iris Galindo Benitez, Kimberly Jasmine Galindo Benitez, and Ashley Galindo (for ease of reference hereafter referred to by first names; no disrespect is intended). The petitions were set for hearing on May 21, 2026.

On May 21, 2026, the Court denied the petitions without prejudice based on an errata filing by Plaintiffs on May 19, 2026.

That same day, Plaintiff Jose Luis Galindo Vargas filed amended petitions for Brianna and Kimberly Galindo Benitez—though the record fails to reflect the same for Ashley Galindo.

The two amended minor Plaintiffs’ petitions are now before the Court.

 

  1. Petitions to Approve Minors’ Compromise

a.     Legal Standard on Petitions to Approve Minors’ Compromise

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 372, any compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment made for a minor or adult with a disability must be approved by the Court. (See also Prob. Code § 3600, subd. (b) [a compromise or covenant for a disputed claim or damages, money, or other property of a minor or person who lacks legal capacity is valid only after it has been approved by the superior court].) A petition for court approval of a compromise of a minor or disabled adult’s compromise or judgment of a pending action or proceeding to which this person is a party must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise, covenant, settlement, or disposition. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950; see also Cal. Rules of Court, rules 7.950.5-7.955.) The petition is generally submitted on a completed Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Minor or Person with a Disability (Judicial Council form MC-350). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) If the Court is satisfied that the compromise, covenant, settlement, or disposition is in the best interest of the person, then the Court should approve the same. (See Pearson v. Superior Court (2012) 202 Cal.App.4th 1333, 1338.)

b.     Discussion

A review of the petition shows that it meets all the requirements in California Rules of Court, rules 7.950 to 7.955. The record reflects that the amended petitions filed for Brianna and Kimberly:

(1) Are filed on verified Civil Form MC-350EX and contain a full disclosure of all information that bears upon the reasonableness of the compromise, covenant, settlement, or disposition (see, e.g., Petitions at §§ 10, 11, 15, 16, 17, attachs. 11b.(5), 17a, 17e);

(2) Show none of the circumstances prohibiting expedited approval of these petitions (cf. Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.5, subd. (a));

(3) Show the name, state bar number, law firm, and business address of the attorney (§ 17.b.), to-date compensation (§ 17.c.), insurer involvement (§ 17.d.), representation of other parties in this action (§ 17.e., attach. 17.e.), expected fees (§ 17.f., attach 11b.(5)), and representation terms (§ 17.a.(2), attach 17a.);

(4) Need not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 7.952 as good cause exists to dispense with the need for their personal appearance or the personal appearance of their parent/guardian ad litem;

(5) Need not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 7.953 as the Court has not ordered deposit of funds;

(6) Need not satisfy California Rules of Court, rule 7.954 as no withdrawal of funds are at issue; and

(7) Show reasonable fees charged to the minors here (§ 16 [roughly 24%]) (§ 16, attach 11b.(5)).

Moreover, nothing before the Court indicates that the settlement is not in the children’s best interests, where the parents in the best position to use the remaining settlement proceeds to further the economic welfare and wellbeing of these minors. (Attach. 11b.(5).)

c.     Conclusion

Plaintiffs’ petitions are GRANTED.

Petitioner Jose Luis Galindo Vargas is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV07343    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 731

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION AND

MOTION FOR COMPLIANCE WITH

DISCOVERY PROCESS AND THE

COURT’S MARCH 24, 2026 ORDER

COMPELLING PLAINTIFF’S DEPOSITION

WITH SANCTIONS




  1. Background Facts

Plaintiffs Ricardo Campos Sanchez and Marlene Romero sue Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. (“Defendant”), and Does 1 through 10 pursuant to a March 14, 2025, Complaint alleging lemon law claims related to Plaintiffs’ purchase of a 2020 Honda Insight manufactured and/or distributed by Defendant.

Before the Court is Defendant’s un-opposed motion to compel compliance with this Court’s March 24, 2026, order imposing monetary sanctions on Plaintiff Ricardo Sanchez Campos, which Defendant argues have not been paid to date. Defendant has filed a notice of non-opposition noting the absence of opposition from Plaintiffs.

///

///

  1. Motion to Compel Compliance with Court Order

The Court finds in favor of Defendant in part and in favor of Plaintiff Ricardo Sanchez Campos in part.

The Court finds merit in Defendant’s request for an order directing Plaintiff Ricardo Sanchez Campos to comply with the Court’s March 24, 2026, order, which ruled that several discovery motions directed at this plaintiff were moot but nevertheless imposed monetary sanctions on him in the amount of $2,000.00, to be paid directly to Defendant’s counsel within 30 days. (Motion, pp. 4-5; see 3/24/26 Minutes, p. 1.) Every California court has the inherent and statutory authority to provide for the orderly conduct of proceedings before it and to control its process and orders so as to make them conform to law and justice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128, subds. (a)(3), (a)(8).) Here, conforming the facts before the Court to justice necessitates ordering Plaintiff Richard Sanchez Campos to comply with this Court’s March 24th order. Defendant’s motion is thus GRANTED as to this requested relief.

However, Defendant’s motion is DENIED as to the imposition of either further monetary sanctions or terminating sanctions. As for monetary sanctions, such sanctions orders are enforceable as money judgments unless the court orders otherwise; instead, the remedy to enforce payment of monetary sanctions is to obtain and levy a writ of execution on assets of the debtor. (Newland v. Superior Court (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 608, 615.) Sanctions cannot be imposed for failure to pay previously ordered sanctions. (Ibid.) As for terminating sanctions, such remedy is disproportionate to the violation here, which is a mere failure to pay money rather than a violation substantively affecting Defendant’s ability to develop its denials and defenses.

a.     Conclusion

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s motion is thus:

(1) GRANTED as to ordering Plaintiff to remit sanctions previously ordered by this Court, which the Court ORDERS Plaintiff to remit within fourteen (14) days of service this order; and

(2) DENIED as to monetary and terminating sanctions.

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV08666    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: 731

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION TO COMPEL SANCTIONS PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 871.26

  1. Background Facts

Plaintiff, who brings this action seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle, brought this motion seeking an order that compels Defendants to comply with initial disclosure obligations and granting sanctions against Defendant pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26.

Defendant has filed an opposition, which argues that this motion has been mooted by since-made disclosures, and that sanctions should be denied because, according to Defendant, the parties were working on settlement and put a pause on initial disclosures when Plaintiff filed this motion.

Because no reply appears in the record, there are no papers from Plaintiff to dispute Defendant’s mootness and good cause arguments.

///

///

///

  1. Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures; Request for Sanctions

Plaintiff’s motion seeks disclosures and sanctions from Defendant for failure to comply with its initial disclosure requirements under section 871.26.

After review, the Court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion in full.

Initial-disclosure procedures exist for specific causes of action, including procedures described in Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivisions (b) and (f) to (h) for civil actions seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.20, et seq.

Regarding disclosures, Defendant has provided evidence of disclosures, the extent and nature of which are not addressed here. (See Opposition, Sweeney Decl., ¶¶ 4-13, Exs. A-B.) Separately, Plaintiff has failed to file a reply, for which reason the Court can accept that the issue of whether Defendant has complied with its initial disclosure obligations is moot.

As for sanctions, though Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (j), mandates that this Court impose sanctions for a party’s failure to make initial disclosures, those sanctions can be withheld where the non-producing party shows good cause. Here, Defendant argues that the parties were engaged in settlement negotiations, for which reason Defendant was under the impression that disclosures were paused, and once Defendant received notice of motion, it made disclosures and thus brought itself into compliance with the Code. (Opposition, Sweeney Decl., ¶¶ 4-13, Exs. A-B.) Again, no reply exists to dispute this position. Sanctions consequently fail under a showing of good cause.

Plaintiffs’ motion is accordingly DENIED.

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.
