DEPARTMENT 408 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV24937    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: 408

Plaintiff M.O.S.’ Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint is GRANTED.

 

I.                BACKGROUND  

 

On September 25, 2024, Plaintiff M.O.S., a minor by and through his guardian ad litem, Moieshai Carter (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District (“Defendant”) alleging causes of action for:

 

1.     Negligence, Negligent Hiring, Training, Retention, and/or Supervision of Unfit Employees;

2.     Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress;

3.     Breach of Mandatory Statutory Duties Which Proximately Caused Plaintiff’s Sexual Assault and Battery; and

4.     Breach of Mandatory Statutory Duties Which Proximately Caused Plaintiff Additional Damages Separate and Distinct From His Sexual Assault And Batter.

 

On February 13, 2025, the court granted Defendant’s Motion to Strike portions of Plaintiff’s complaint with leave to amend.

 

On February 24, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”).

 

On March 26, 2025, Defendant filed an answer to Plaintiff’s FAC.

 

On June 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion for Leave to File a Second Amended Complaint. On July 22, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. On July 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD 

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(a)(1), “[t]he court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).)¿Amendment may be allowed at any time before or after commencement of trial. (Code Civ. Proc., § 576.) “[T]he court’s discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendment of the pleadings. The policy favoring amendment is so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified.” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428 [internal citations omitted].) “If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend[.]”¿ (Morgan v. Sup. Ct. (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.) Prejudice includes “delay in trial, loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation.” (Solit v. Tokai Bank, Ltd. New York Branch (1999) 68 Cal.App.4th 1435, 1448.)¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

A motion to amend a pleading before trial must include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, subd. (a).) The motion must also state what allegations are proposed to be deleted or added, by page, paragraph, and line number.¿(Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, subd. (a).) Finally, a separate supporting declaration specifying the effect of the amendment, why the amendment is necessary and proper, when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered, and the reason the request for amendment was not made earlier must also accompany the motion. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, subd. (b).)¿¿¿¿ 

 

III.          DISCUSSION  

 

Plaintiff seeks leave to file a second amended complaint to include additional causes of action under Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act; Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act; standalone retaliation; sexual battery under California Civil Code section 1708.5; and violations of the California Education Code, along with a prayer for punitive damages against individual defendants. (Motion, at p. 3.) Plaintiff argues that leave to amend is to be liberally granted and there will be no prejudice to Defendant as the proposed amendments arise from the same facts at issue. (Id., at pp. 5-7.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that amendment would be fatal as Plaintiff seeks to add punitive damages allegations and the new causes of action without meeting the Government Claims Act claim presentation requirement set forth in Government Code  section 911.2. (Opp., at p. 2.) Defendant also argues that allowing amendment will further complicate the issues, unnecessarily delay this action, and unduly prejudice Defendant in that it will necessitate the filing of a Demurrer and Motion to Strike followed by new discovery. (Ibid.)

 

The court finds that Plaintiff complies with the requirements of¿Rule 3.1324 by providing a copy of the proposed second amended complaint, identifying the changes to be made, and a supporting declaration stating why the amendment is necessary. (Diggs Decl., ¶¶ 2-7, Exh. A.) The court also finds that any prejudice to Defendant would be minimal as there is ample time to contest the amended pleading to raise any dispositive arguments as to the new causes of action asserted.

 

Accordingly, the motion is granted.

 

IV.          CONCLUSION

 

Plaintiff M.O.S.’ Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint is GRANTED.


Case Number: 26STCV08812    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: 408

1.               Defendant Encompass Insurance Company’s Demurrer to Plaintiffs Marine Chabukhyan and Nataly Zohrabyan’s Complaint is OVERRULED as to the second cause of action.  Defendant is ordered to file an Answer within 20 days from the date of this order.

 

2.               Defendant Encompass Insurance Company’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs Marine Chabukhyan and Nataly Zohrabyan’s Complaint is DENIED, in part, and GRANTED, in part.

 

I.                BACKGROUND


On March 18, 2026, Plaintiffs Marine Chabukhyan and Nataly Zohrabyan, a minor by and through Guardian Ad Litem Marine Chabukhyan (“Plaintiffs”), filed a complaint against Defendant Encompass Insurance Company (“Defendant”) alleging causes of action for:

 

1.               Breach of Contract; and

2.               Breach of The Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing.

 

On May 8, 2026, Defendant filed this Demurrer and Motion to Strike to Plaintiffs’ complaint. On June 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed oppositions. On June 22, 2026, Defendant filed replies.

 

II.             DEMURRER

 

A.             Legal Standard

 

          “The party against whom a complaint or cross-complaint has been filed may object, by demurrer or answer as provided in Section 430.30, to the pleading on any one or more of” various grounds listed in statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10.)

 

          When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not the evidence or facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pled or implied factual allegations. (Ibid.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)

 

          Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.)

 

B.              Discussion

 

Defendant demurs to the second cause of action for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing alleged in Plaintiffs’ complaint pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e), on the grounds that Plaintiffs fail to state sufficient facts to constitute such cause of action.[1]

 

          The law implies a covenant of good faith and fair dealing in every contract, including insurance policies. (Wilson v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2007) 42 Cal.4th 713, 720.) “[T]he essence of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing is that [t]he insurer must refrain from doing anything that will injure the right of the insured to receive the benefits of the [insurance] agreement, the terms and conditions of which define the duties and performance to which the insured is entitled.” (Brandwein v. Butler (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1485, 1514-1515.)

 

To establish the insurer's “bad faith” liability, the insured must show that the insurer has (1) withheld benefits due under the policy, and (2) that such withholding was “unreasonable” or “without proper cause.” (Gruenberg v. Aetna Ins. Co. (1973) 9 Cal.3d 566, 573-574.) The actionable withholding of benefits may consist of paying less than due; and/or unreasonably delaying payments due. (Neal v. Farmers Ins. Exchange (1978) 21 Cal.3d 910, 921; Waller v. Truck Ins. Exchange (1995) 11 Cal.4th 1, 36.)

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiffs fail to allege facts demonstrating that Defendant acted unreasonably in denying benefits to support a claim for bad faith. (Demurrer, at pp. 5-6.)

 

The complaint alleges that, on February 13, 2024, Defendant issued a Personal Insurance Policy to Plaintiff Marine Chabukhyan and Taron Zohrabyan which provided, among other things, $100,000.00 per person or $300,000.00 per incident in excess uninsured and underinsured motorist bodily injury coverage. (Complaint, ¶ 7.) On April 11, 2024, Plaintiffs were involved in a motor vehicle collision caused by a motorist who failed to maintain automobile liability insurance. (Id., ¶ 12.) Plaintiffs sustained injuries, sought medical care and treatment, and incurred damages. (Ibid.) The complaint further alleges that as the at-fault motorist failed to maintain liability insurance, Plaintiffs’ damages were to be covered under the terms of the insurance policy with Defendant. (Id., ¶ 13.) Plaintiffs allege that Defendant has wrongfully and intentionally withheld benefits due under the insurance contract, including denying benefits under the insurance contract and unreasonably delaying payment of benefits due without proper cause. (Id., ¶¶ 23-24.)

 

The court finds that Plaintiffs sufficiently allege that Defendant’s conduct was unreasonable or without proper cause to support a claim for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

 

Accordingly, the demurrer is overruled as to the second cause of action.

 

III.          MOTION TO STRIKE

 

A.             Legal Standard


Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 436, “the court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) The grounds for a motion to strike must “appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437.)


B.              Discussion


Defendant moves to strike Plaintiffs’ request for punitive damages, injunctive relief, and attorney fees under Insurance Code section 1619 from the complaint. (Motion, at p. 4.)

 

1.               Punitive Damages 

 

Defendant argues that Plaintiffs fail to allege sufficient facts to support a request for punitive damages making conclusory allegations that Defendant’s acted with malice, oppression or fraud. (Id., at pp. 6-8.) Defendant also argues that Plaintiffs’ request for punitive damages fails as the bad faith claim fails. (Id., at p. 6.)

 

Punitive damages may be awarded in an action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract upon clear and convincing evidence that a defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice. (Civ. Code § 3294, subd. (a).)

 

“Malice” is defined as “conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.” (Civ. Code § 3294, subd. (c)(1).) “Oppression” is defined as “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights.” (Civ. Code § 3294, subd. (c)(2).) “Fraud” is defined as “an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.” (Civ. Code § 3294, subd. (c)(3).)

 

The court finds that Plaintiffs provide sufficient facts to support a request for punitive damages. The complaint alleges that Defendant wrongfully withheld benefits due under the insurance policy at issue; Defendant failed to conduct a fair and objective investigation into Plaintiffs’ claim; Defendant unreasonably delayed payment of benefits; Defendant misrepresented the terms of the insurance contract; and Defendant failed to communicate with Plaintiff. (Complaint, ¶¶ 23-24.) The complaint also alleges that these acts were done with the knowledge, approval, and ratification of Defendant sufficient to comply with Civil Code section 3294(b). (Id., ¶ 30.)

 

Accordingly, the motion is denied as to punitive damages.

 

2.               Injunctive Relief

 

Defendant argues that as Plaintiffs have an adequate remedy at law in pursuing a claim for breach of contract, Plaintiffs’ prayer for injunctive relief for a permanent injunction enjoining Defendant from engaging in each of the unlawful practices, policies, usages and customs alleged  is improper and should be stricken. (Motion, at p. 8.)

 

The court notes that Defendant fails to provide persuasive authority finding that Plaintiffs’ request for injunctive relief is per se improper and subject to strike.

 

Accordingly, the motion is denied as to the request for injunctive relief.

 

3.               Attorney’s Fees

 

Attorney’s fees are recoverable only when authorized by contract, statute, or law. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10).)¿

 

          Plaintiffs seek attorney’s fees pursuant to Insurance Code section 1619. (Complaint, Prayer for Relief, ¶ 7.) Insurance Code section 1619 applies to “any action against a nonadmitted foreign or alien insurer upon a contract of insurance issued or delivered in this State”. (Ins. Code, § 1619.)

 

          Defendant argues that Plaintiffs fail to allege that Defendant is a nonadmitted foreign or alien insurer for Section 1619 to apply. (Motion, at p. 9.) Defendant further contends that it is licensed in California making Section 1619 improper. (Ibid.)

 

          The complaint alleges that Defendant is “an Illinois corporation, authorized under the laws of the State of California to do business as an insurer in the State of California.” (Complaint, ¶ 2.) The court finds that such allegations defeat Plaintiffs’ request for attorney’s fees under Section 1619. (Ins. Code, § 24 [admitted means entitled to transact insurance business in this state].)

 

Accordingly, the motion is granted as to the request for attorney’s fees under Section 1619.

 

IV.          CONCLUSION


1.               Defendant Encompass Insurance Company’s Demurrer to Plaintiffs Marine Chabukhyan and Nataly Zohrabyan’s Complaint is OVERRULED as to the second cause of action. Defendant is ordered to file an Answer within 20 days from the date of this order.

 

2.               Defendant Encompass Insurance Company’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs Marine Chabukhyan and Nataly Zohrabyan’s Complaint is DENIED, in part, and GRANTED, in part.


[1]                  In opposition, Plaintiffs argue that Defendant failed to sufficiently meet and confer prior to filing this demurrer. (Opp., at pp. 2-3.) An insufficient meet and confer process is not grounds to overrule or sustain a demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(4).) The court exercises its discretion to consider the demurrer on the merits despite any alleged procedural deficiencies.

1.     Plaintiff Jamal J. Savage’s Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint is GRANTED. 

2.     Defendant Insomnia Cookies, LLC’s Demurrer to Plaintiff Jamal J. Savage’s First Amended Complaint is OVERRULED, as moot.

 

I.                BACKGROUND 

 

On March 19, 2026, Plaintiff Jamal J. Savage (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint against Defendant Insomnia Cookies, LLC (“Defendant”) alleging causes of action for:

 

1.     Disability Discrimination;

2.     Retaliation; and

3.     Wrongful Termination.

 

On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleging cause of action for:

2.     Failure to Accommodate;

3.     Failure to Engage in Interactive Process;

4.     Retaliation;

On June 2, 2026, Defendant filed this Demurrer to Plaintiff’s FAC. On July 30, 2026, Defendant filed a Notice of Non-Opposition.

 

On July 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed this Motion for Leave to Amend to File a Second Amended Complaint. On July 22, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. As of July 30, 2026, no reply has been filed.

B.         Discussion

 

Plaintiff seeks leave to file a second amended complaint to include additional allegations to clarify issues of venue, employment relationship, work location, job duties, Plaintiff’s disability, accommodation history, protected activity, adverse actions, separation from employment, and administrative exhaustion. (Motion, at p. 1.) Plaintiff contends that that the proposed amendment address the deficiencies identified in Defendant’s Demurrer and meet and confer efforts as to Plaintiff’s FAC. (Id., at p. 2.) Plaintiff argues that leave to amend is liberally granted and that Defendants will suffer no meaningful prejudice from the amendment. (Id., at pp. 1-2.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues that Plaintiff failed to meaningfully meet and confer prior to filing this motion for leave to amend which resulted in Defendant being forced to file a demurrer to Plaintiff’s FAC. (Opp., at pp. 2-3.) Defendant also argues that it would be prejudiced by Plaintiff’s conduct. (Id., at pp. 3-4.)

 

The court finds that Plaintiff complies with the requirements of¿Rule 3.1324 by providing a copy of the proposed second amended complaint, identifying the changes to be made, and a supporting declaration stating why the amendment is necessary. (Savage Decl., ¶¶ 2-9, Exhs. 1-2.) The court also finds that any prejudice to Defendant would be minimal as there is ample time to contest the amended pleading. To the extent that Defendant challenges the legal viability of Plaintiff’s claims or claims that an arbitration agreement applies to Plaintiff’s claims, Defendant is free to raise such argument through an appropriate motion.

 

Accordingly, the motion is granted.

B.         Discussion

 

Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s FAC pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e) and (f), on the grounds that Plaintiff does not state facts sufficient to constitute the causes of action and the pleading is uncertain.

IV.          CONCLUSION 

 

1.     Plaintiff Jamal J. Savage’s Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint is GRANTED.
