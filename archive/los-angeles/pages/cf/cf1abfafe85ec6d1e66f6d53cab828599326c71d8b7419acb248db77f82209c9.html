MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiffs Hovhannes Vardanyan and Ohan Melkonyan

 

RESPONDING PARTIES:    Defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and LAD-V, LLC

Motion for Attorney’s Fees, Costs, and Expenses

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs Hovhannes Vardanyan and Ohan Melkonyan (“Plaintiffs”) move the court for an order awarding attorney’s fees, costs, and expenses pursuant to the settlement agreement between Plaintiffs and defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and LAD-V, LLC in the total amount of $67,720.80 in fees and $3,746.16 in costs and expenses.

First, the court finds—and Defendants do not dispute—that Plaintiff is entitled to recover attorney’s fees and costs from Defendants pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act and the parties’ settlement.  (Civ. Code, § 1794, subd. (d) [“If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action”].) ¿ 

Second, the court finds that Plaintiffs have established, as to the attorney’s fees incurred to commence and prosecute this action and to prepare the pending fee motion, a lodestar of $37,475.00.

“[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate. . . . .¿ The reasonable hourly rate is that prevailing in the community for similar work.¿ The lodestar figure may then be adjusted, based on consideration of factors specific to the case, in order to fix the fee at the fair market value for the legal services provided.”¿ (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095 (internal citations omitted); Reck v. FCA US LLC (2021) 64 Cal.App.5th 682, 691 [“To determine a reasonable attorney fee award, the trial court applies the lodestar method”].)¿ “[T]he verified time statements of the attorneys, as officers of the court, are entitled to credence in the absence of a clear indication the records are erroneous.”¿ (Horsford v. Board of Trustees of California State Univ. (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 359, 396.)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

The court finds that the reasonable hourly rates for the legal staff that performed work in this action are as follows: (1) $650 for attorney Margarian, (2) $550 for attorney Kimball, and (3) $150 for Plaintiffs’ counsel’s legal assistant, Elmira Oganyan.  (Margarian Decl., ¶¶ 12-16; Kimball Decl., ¶¶ 2-4, 10.)

The court has considered (1) the billing records of Plaintiffs’ legal staff, and (2) the objections to specific entries in Defendants’ opposition papers.  (Margarian Decl., Ex. A,; Opp., pp. 7:15-11:18.)  The court finds that Plaintiff’s legal staff reasonably expended a total number of 63.10 hours (46.9 hours for Margarian, 11.4 hours for Kimball, and 4.8 hours for Oganyan) to commence and prosecute this action and to prepare the pending motion. 

The court therefore finds that Plaintiffs have established a lodestar figure of $37,475.00 ((46.9 hours x Margarian’s $650 hourly rate = $30,485.00) + (11.4 hours x Kimball’s $550 hourly rate = $6,270.00) + (4.8 hours x Oganyan’s $150 hourly rate = $720.00).

Third, after considering the factors the court may consider in determining whether to adjust the lodestar, the court finds that the lodestar figure should not be adjusted by applying a multiplier.  (Margarian Decl., ¶ 25; Kimball Decl., ¶ 15; Mikhaeilpoor v. BMW of North America, LLC (2020) 48 Cal.App.5th 240, 248 [setting forth factors delineated by the Supreme Court that the trial court may consider in adjusting the lodestar].)¿¿

Fourth, the court declines to address Plaintiffs’ request for costs and expenses because they are the subject of Defendant’s motion to strike or tax costs filed on October 15, 2025, set to be heard on September 2, 2026.  

ORDER

            The court grants plaintiffs Hovhannes Vardanyan and Ohan Melkonyan’s motion for attorney’s fees, costs and expenses in part as follows.

Pursuant to Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d), the court orders that plaintiffs Hovhannes Vardanyan and Ohan Melkonyan shall recover from defendants Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. and LAD-V, LLC attorney’s fees in the total amount of $37,475.00.

            The court orders plaintiffs Hovhannes Vardanyan and Ohan Melkonyan to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

(1)   ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: WHETHER THE COURT SHOULD (1) ALLOW PLAINTIFFS JANE DOE 1 AND JANE DOE 2 TO PROCEED UNDER A PSEUDONYM, AND (2) REQUIRE PLAINTIFFS JANE DOE 1 AND JANE DOE 2 TO AMEND THEIR COMPLAIN TO STATE THEIR REAL NAMES

(1)  Order to Show Cause Re: Whether the Court Should (1) Allow Plaintiffs Jane Doe 1 and Jane Doe 2 to Proceed Under a Pseudonym, and (2) Require Plaintiffs Jane Doe 1 and Jane Doe 2 to Amend their Complain to State Their Real Names

Demurrer to Complaint

(2) Defendant Pegasus Real Estate Advisory LLC’s Demurrer to Plaintiffs’ Complaint

The court considered “Plaintiff’s Response to the Court’s Order to Show Cause Re: ‘Jane Doe’ Status” in connection with the order to show cause hearing.

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this demurrer.

ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: WHETHER THE COURT SHOULD (1) ALLOW PLAINTIFFS JANE DOE 1 AND JANE DOE 2 TO PROCEED UNDER A PSEUDONYM, AND (2) REQUIRE PLAINTIFFS JANE DOE 1 AND JANE DOE 2 TO AMEND THEIR COMPLAINT TO STATE THEIR REAL NAMES

            The court finds that plaintiffs Jane Doe 1 and Jane Doe 2 (“Plaintiffs”) have met their burden to show that there is an overriding interest that will likely be prejudiced without the use of a pseudonym and it would not be feasible to protect the interest with less impact on the constitutional right of access.  (Department of Fair Employment and Housing v. Superior Court of Santa Clara County (2022) 82 Cal.App.5th 105, 111-112 [“Before a party to a civil action can be permitted to use a pseudonym, the trial court must conduct a hearing and apply the overriding interest test: A party’s request for anonymity should be granted only if the court finds that an overriding interest will likely be prejudiced without use of a pseudonym, and that it is not feasible to protect the interest with less impact on the constitutional right of access.  In deciding the issue the court must bear in mind the critical importance of the public’s right to access judicial proceedings.  Outside of cases where anonymity is expressly permitted by statute, litigating by pseudonym should occur only in the rarest of circumstances”] [internal quotation marks and citation omitted]; Roe v. Smith (2025) 116 Cal.App.5th 227, 239 [agreeing that “trial courts faced with a motion to proceed pseudonymously should apply the overriding interest test outlined in NBC Subsidiary and California Rules of Court, rule 2.550(d)”].) 

            The court finds that (1) Plaintiffs have an overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access to the record because the sexual assault and harassment allegations fall within the category of highly sensitive and private matters, Plaintiffs fear public stigmatization, humiliation, and embarrassment for being a victim of sexual harassment and assault, Plaintiffs fear personal information such as sensitive medical information will be revealed, and defendant Benn Schecter has appeared at their homes and their parents’ homes; (2) the fear of stigmatization, humiliation, and embarrassment and for their safety supports Plaintiffs’ request for anonymity; (3) if Plaintiffs’ identities are revealed, there is a substantial probability that their privacy and safety interests will be prejudiced; (4) allowing Plaintiffs to proceed under a pseudonym is a narrow way of addressing the issue as defendants Pegasus Real Estate Advisory LLC and Benn Schecter (collectively “Defendants”) are aware of Plaintiffs’ identities[1]; and (5) no less restrictive means exist to maintain Plaintiffs’ anonymity.  (Doe 1 Decl., ¶¶ 2, 4-6; Doe 2 Decl., ¶¶ 2, 4-6; Zhang Decl., ¶ 1-2, Ex. A [Plaintiffs’ Confidential Information Form served on Defendants on June 15, 2026].)  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.550(d).)

            Thus, the court will allow Plaintiffs to proceed under pseudonyms in this action.  

DEMURRER

Defendant Pegasus Real Estate Advisory LLC (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order sustaining its demurrer on the ground that each cause of action is uncertain due to Plaintiffs’ unauthorized and improper use of pseudonyms and on the ground that the first and second causes of action fail to allege facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to each cause of action on the ground of uncertainty because, as discussed above, the court is issuing an order allowing Plaintiffs to use pseudonyms in this action.  In addition, the Complaint is not uncertain as it is neither ambiguous nor unintelligible. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (f).)  It is clear that the Complaint makes allegations regarding defendant Benn Shecter’s (“Shechter”) sexual harassment of Plaintiffs, Defendant’s failure to prevent sexual harassment, and wage and hour claims.  (Compl., ¶¶ 10-17.)  (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695, [“[D]emurrers for uncertainty are disfavored, and are granted only if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond”] [internal citations and quotations omitted].)

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the first cause of action for sexual harassment because Plaintiffs have alleged facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action for quid pro quo harassment since Plaintiffs have alleged that (1) the quid pro quo harassment by defendant Shechter included both explicitly sexual words and unwanted physical touching of sensitive body parts, which Plaintiffs protested and (2) that Plaintiffs’ terms of employment were made contingent by words and/or defendant Shecter’s conduct, on Plaintiffs’ acceptance of defendant Shechter’s sexual advances and/or conduct, and defendant Shechter started treating Plaintiffs poorly after they rebuffed his sexual advances.  (Compl., ¶¶ 10, 11, 13.)  (Mogilefsky v. Superior Court (1993) 20 Cal.App.4th 1409, 1414 [“To state a cause of action on this theory, is it sufficient to allege that a term of employment was expressly or impliedly conditioned upon acceptance of a supervisor's unwelcome sexual advances”].)

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the second cause of action for failure to prevent sexual harassment because Plaintiffs have alleged facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action for failure to prevent sexual harassment since Plaintiffs allege that (1) they were subjected to harassment by defendant Shechter, (2) Defendant failed to take all reasonable steps necessary to prevent sexual harassment in the workplace from occurring since Defendant had prior knowledge of defendant Shechter’s sexual harassment of women prior to Plaintiffs’ complaints, and (3) as a result of Defendant’s conduct, Plaintiffs have suffered damage.  (Compl., ¶¶ 10-14, 32-33.)  (Caldera v. Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation (2018) 25 Cal.App.5th 31, 43–44 [“When a plaintiff seeks to recover damages based on a claim of failure to prevent ... harassment ... she must show three essential elements: 1) plaintiff was subjected to ... harassment ...; 2) defendant failed to take all reasonable steps to prevent ... harassment ...; and 3) this failure caused plaintiff to suffer injury, damage, loss or harm”] [citation omitted, emphasis in original].)[2]   

ORDER

            The court orders that plaintiffs Jane Doe 1 and Jane Doe 2 may proceed under pseudonyms in this action.

The court overrules defendant Pegasus Real Estate Advisory LLC’s demurrer to plaintiffs Jane Doe 1 and Jane Doe 2’s Complaint.

            The court orders plaintiffs Jane Doe 1 and Jane Doe 2 to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
