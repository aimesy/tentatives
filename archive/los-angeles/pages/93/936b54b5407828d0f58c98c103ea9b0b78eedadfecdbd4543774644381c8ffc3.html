DEPARTMENT 735 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV20885    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 735

Shahrokhi v. Lozano, et al.

Defendant Lozano’s Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories from Plaintiff;

Defendant Lozano’s Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories from Plaintiff;

Defendant Lozano’s Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents from Plaintiff

 

BACKGROUND 

This action arises from a motor vehicle accident. On May 1, 2025, Plaintiff Azita Shahroki filed the operative first amended complaint against Defendants Jesse Lozano, State Farm LLC and Hertz Global Holdings, Inc., asserting causes of action for negligence and negligent entrustment.

LEGAL STANDARD

When timely responses to interrogatories are not received, “[t]he party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling response to the interrogatories.”  (CCP § 2030.290(b).)

“If a party to whom a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed fails to serve a timely response to it. . .  [t]he party to whom the demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling is directed waives any objection to the demand, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product. . . .  The party making the demand may move for an order compelling response to the demand.”  (CCP § 2031.300(a)–(b).)

DISCUSSION

Defendant Lozano moves to compel Plaintiff’s responses to sets one of form interrogatories, special interrogatories and request for productions of documents. Defendant served the subject discovery on August 27, 2025. After receiving several extensions, Plaintiff’s responses were due on November 20, 2025. As of the filing of the motions, responses had not been received.

In opposition, Plaintiff represents that responses were served on June 9, 2026. Plaintiff asserts that Plaintiff’s counsel offered to pay the requested sanctions amount for each motion while serving responses in exchange for Defendant taking these motions off calendar. Plaintiff states that Defendant’s counsel refused to take the motions off calendar.

In reply, Defendant asserts that Plaintiff’s responses are not code-compliant because they contain objections in spite of their untimeliness. Defendant requests the Court order Plaintiff to provide responses without objections and award sanctions.

As responses have been served, the motions are denied as moot. If Defendant takes issue with the content of the responses, the remedy is to meet and confer and file a motion to compel further responses thereafter. The Court declines to award sanctions to either party. As Plaintiff failed to serve timely responses, Plaintiff is not entitled to sanctions. Defendant failed to take these motions off calendar after responses were served and after Plaintiff offered to pay the sanctions. After receipt of responses and the sanctions offer, there was nothing left for the Court to resolve, and the motions should have been taken off calendar.

CONCLUSION 

The Court denies Defendant’s motions to compel responses to discovery.

The Court denies the parties’ requests for sanctions.

Defendant shall give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV13858    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 735

Rodriguez v. Lifeline Ambulance, et al.

Defendant Falck Mobile Health Corp.’s Demurrer to Complaint

 

BACKGROUND 

On May 12, 2025, Plaintiff Tony Olimon Rodriguez filed a complaint against Defendants Lifeline Ambulance, et al., asserting causes of action for (1) negligent misrepresentation; (2) intentional misrepresentation; (3) battery; (4) personal injury; (5) violation of civil rights; (6) conspiracy against civil rights; (7) violation of RICO Act; (8) conspiracy to participate in violation of RICO Act; (9) civil conspiracy; (10) conversion; (11) respondeat superior negligence; (12) IIED; and (13) declaratory relief.

Plaintiff alleges “[o]n or about May 12, 2024, Defendant Dennis Quang and Tanner DePadua who were Police officers for the Garden Grove City Police Department arrived on scene at a traffic collision. Defendants Quang and DePadua subsequently forced Plaintiff Rodriguez into a Falck Transport vehicle, and instructed the Falck employees to inject Plaintiff Rodriguez.” Plaintiff alleges “thereafter, on or about May 12, 2024, Plaintiff Tony Olimon Rodriguez was forced by defendants to be injected by nonconsensual drugs by UC.I. Medical Center's Staff and others.” Plaintiff alleges Defendants’ “ulterior purpose of injecting Plaintiff Tony Olimon Rodriguez with the drugs was to fabricate evidence against Plaintiff Rodriguez to later use in support of a DUI Criminal Case. Additionally, Defendants and DOES wrongfully drugged Plaintiff Rodriguez to earn a profit for Clinical Drug Trials, and other Scientific Research.”

DISCUSSION

Defendant Falck Mobile Health Corp. demurs to each cause of action in the complaint on the ground that Plaintiff has failed to allege sufficient facts against Defendant. As stated, Plaintiff alleges that he was involved in a motor vehicle accident and “Defendants Quang and DePadua subsequently forced Plaintiff Rodriguez into a Falck Transport vehicle, and instructed the Falck employees to inject Plaintiff Rodriguez.” There are no other facts alleged against Falck in the complaint.

The facts alleged against Falck do not show a misrepresentation for the fraud causes of action, do not show any taking of property for conversion, do not support causes of action for RICO violations or conspiracy, and do not show intent to cause emotional distress. Plaintiff does not allege any facts under the causes of action for battery (which is completely absent from the complaint except for in the caption), respondeat superior negligence, violation of civil rights or declaratory relief. Plaintiff does not mention Defendant Falck’s alleged actions under the cause of action for “personal injury.” It is also unclear what cause of action Plaintiff is alleging under the “personal injury” claim, i.e. battery or negligence. Plaintiff has failed to allege any cause of action against Defendant Falck.

CONCLUSION 

The Court sustains Defendant’s demurrer to the complaint. Plaintiff to file and serve a first amended complaint within 30 days of notice of ruling. Defendant to file and serve a response within 30 days thereafter.

Defendant shall give notice.


Case Number: 26STCV06969    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: 735

Wiles, et al. v. Patel, et al.

Defendants’ Motion to Strike Punitive Damages

 

BACKGROUND 

On March 3, 2026, Plaintiffs Adam Wiles and Lewsi US HoldCo, LLC filed a complaint against Defendants Amit Patel and Sklar Kirsh, asserting causes of action for (1) legal malpractice; (2) breach of fiduciary duty; (3) intentional misrepresentation; (4) negligent misrepresentation; (5) constructive fraud; (6) conspiracy; (7) aiding and abetting breach of fiduciary duty; and (8) UCL violations.

DISCUSSION

Defendants move to strike the claims for punitive damages on the ground that Plaintiffs have not alleged Defendants have engaged in malice, oppression, or fraud. Punitive damages are available in noncontract cases where the defendant is guilty of “oppression, fraud, or malice.”  (Civil Code § 3294(a).)  Conclusory allegations are insufficient to support a claim for punitive damages.  However, the language the moving party seeks to strike must be read not in isolation, but in the context of the facts alleged in the entire complaint.

Here, Plaintiffs allege Defendants carried out a multi-year scheme to defraud their clients Plaintiffs Adam Wiles and Lewsi US Holdco, LLC. Plaintiffs allege Defendant Patel knowingly engaged in a scheme to divert $22.5 million of Plaintiffs’ funds (all without Plaintiffs’ knowledge) into a failed and now-defunct real estate investment in which Patel had invested $600,000 of his own money. Plaintiffs allege Patel concealed this conflict of interest from Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs learned of this misconduct only after they retained different counsel to investigate suspected malfeasance years later. And when confronted, Plaintiffs allege Patel compounded his misconduct by making fraudulent misrepresentations, exploiting the trust inherent in the attorney-client relationship, and fabricating documents to cover his tracks. This is sufficient to allege Defendants engaged in fraudulent conduct and thus sufficient to support a claim for punitive damages.

CONCLUSION 

The Court denies Defendants’ motion to strike punitive damages.

Defendants shall give notice.
