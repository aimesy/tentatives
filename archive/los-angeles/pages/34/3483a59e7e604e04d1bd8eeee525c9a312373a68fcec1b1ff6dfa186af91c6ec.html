[Tentative] Order RE: MOTION TO FILE UNDER SEAL

 

 

 

AND RELATED CROSS-ACTION.

 

MOVING PARTIES:             Defendants and Cross-Complainants Welltower OP LLC, Welltower TRS Holdco LLC, Welltower Victory II TRS LLC, and Welltower Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       None.

The Court considered the moving papers filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

            On December 31, 2025, Plaintiffs Marie D’Angelo and Greg D’Angelo (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) brought this action against Defendants Burbank Subtenant LP dba Ivy Park at Burbank, Oakmont Management Group LLC, Welltower OP LLC, Welltower Victory II OPCO LLC, Welltower TRS Holdco LLC, Welltower Victory II TRS LLC, Welltower Inc., Providence Health System – Southern California dba Providence St. Elizabeth Care Center, and Providence Health & Services (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for (1) elder neglect, (2) elder neglect (enhanced remedies), (3) negligence (custodial), (4) violation of patients’ bill of rights, (5) fraud (constructive), (6) tort per se, (7) intentional infliction of emotional distress, and (8) wrongful death.

            On June 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed two amendments to complaint for fictitious name, substituting Providence Health & Services – Washington and Providence Health System-Southern California dba Providence Saint Joseph Medical Center as Does 1 and 2, respectively.

            On June 25, 2026, Defendants and Cross-Complainants, Welltower OP LLC, Welltower TRS Holdco LLC, Welltower Victory II TRS LLC, and Welltower Inc. (collectively, “Cross-Complainants”) filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiffs, alleging causes of action for (1) declaratory relief as to status/rights, (2) declaratory relief as to interpretation of law, and (3) injunctive relief to stay irreparable harm and related cross-action. The same day, Cross-Complainants filed the instant motion to file under seal.

            As of August 25, 2026, no opposition was filed.

LEGAL STANDARD

            The trial court “‘may order that a record be filed under seal only if it expressly finds facts that establish: (1) There exists an overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access to the record; (2) The overriding interest supports sealing the record; (3) A substantial probability exists that the overriding interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed; (4) The proposed sealing is narrowly tailored; and (5) No less restrictive means exist to achieve the overriding interest.’” (McGuan v. Endovascular Techs., Inc. (2010) 182 Cal.App.4th 974, 988, quoting Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.550(d) [paragraph breaks omitted].)

DISCUSSION

            Cross-Complainants move the Court for an order allowing them to file the following exhibits to the cross-complaint under seal:

            1. Exhibit B – Sublease;

2. Exhibit C - Management Agreement between Burbank Subtenant LP dba Ivy Park at Burbank and Oak Management Group, LLC

            Cross-Complainants request this order on grounds that the records contain commercially sensitive business information and the overriding interest in sealing the records overcomes the right to the public’s access to the same.

            A company or individual’s confidential financial affairs are protected under the right to privacy guaranteed by the California Constitution. (Overstock.com, Inc. v. Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. (2014) 231 Cal.App.4th 471, 503.) “This right embraces confidential financial information in whatever form it takes, whether that form be tax returns, checks, statements, or other account information.” (Ibid. [internal quotation omitted].)

            Welltower OP LLC is a privately owned limited liability company, and Cross-Complaints are all connected business entities owned and operated in connection with one another. (Sher Decl., ¶¶ 4-11.) The sublease and management agreement have controls in place to prevent public access to these documents and are not published on any websites, with any public agency or entity, and have not previously been filed on published dockets or in open court. (Sher Decl., ¶ 12.) Only members/shareholders of Cross-Complainants are permitted to access these exhibits. (Sher Decl., ¶ 13.) Cross-Complainants contend that if competitors had access to these documents, they could gain an unfair business advantage and utilize the information to better their investment opportunities to the detriment of Cross-Complainants. (Sher Decl., ¶ 14.)

            The Court agrees with Cross-Complainants. In its review of the documents, the Court finds that they contain the identities of non-parties to the lawsuit and confidential financial information pertaining to business dealings of Cross-Complainants. The Court agrees that these entities and their business dealings with Cross-Complainants are wholly unrelated to the underlying dispute and disclosure would implicate Cross-Complainants’ right to privacy protected by the California Constitution. Likewise, the Court finds Cross-Complainants’ proposed redactions narrowly tailored to their right of privacy, omitting only financial terms and non-party information. (See Sher Decl., Exhs. B-C.) The Court also finds no overriding interest in public access warranting denial of the motion to file under seal, and notes that the motion is unopposed.

            Accordingly, the Court grants Cross-Complainants’ motion to file under seal.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Defendants and Cross-Complainants Welltower OP LLC, Welltower TRS Holdco LLC, Welltower Victory II TRS LLC, and Welltower Inc.’s motion to file under seal Exhibit B – Sublease; and Exhibit C - Management Agreement between Burbank Subtenant LP dba Ivy Park at Burbank and Oak Management Group, LLC

 

Moving party is ordered to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Order RE: CITY OF SAN GABRIEL’S PETITIONER FOR ORDER TO ABATE

MOVING PARTY:                Petitioner City of San Gabriel

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       None.

The Court
considered the moving papers filed in connection with the instant motion.

BACKGROUND

            On July 10, 2026, Petitioner
City of San Gabriel filed this petition for order to abate substandard building
and appointment of receiver against Respondents Yan Yu (“Yu”), Jia Hui Cong
(“Cong”), East West Investment, Inc. (“East West”), Sunrise 3 LLC (“Sunrise 3”),
Lancer Investments, LLC (“Lancer”), Merastar Industries LLC (“Merastar”), and
the State of California (the “State”) (collectively, “Respondents”).

            On August 19, 2026, the
Court entered default as to Merastar.

            As of August 25, 2026,
the petition is unopposed.

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

            Cong holds title to the real property located
at 904 S. Del Mar, San Gabriel, California 91776, APN 5368-027 008 (the
“Subject Property”), pursuant to
a Grant Deed recorded on February 27, 2015, as Document No. 20150219919, along
with Yu, pursuant to a Grant Deed recorded on June 1, 2021, as Document No.
20210869900. (Heath Decl., ¶ 3, Exhs. A-B.) East West holds a recorded interest
in the Subject Property pursuant to a Deed of Trust recorded on February 27,
2015, as Document No. 2015-0219920. (Heath Decl., ¶ 4, Exh. C.) Sunrise 3 holds
a recorded interest in the Subject Property pursuant to the UCC Financing
Statement recorded on February 13, 2018, as Document No. 20180147325. (Heath
Decl., ¶ 5, Exh. D.) Lancer holds a recorded interest in the Subject Property
pursuant to an Abstract of Judgment recorded on October 29, 2025, as Document
No. 20250753010. (Heath Decl., ¶ 6, Exh. E.) Alliance United Insurance Company (“Alliance”)
holds a recorded interest in the Subject Property pursuant to an Abstract of
Judgment recorded on November 20, 2017, as Document No. 20171334627. (Heath
Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. F.) Merastar is Alliance’s successor in interest pursuant to a
Certificate of Merger filed with the California Secretary of State on September
12, 2025. (Health Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. G.) The State holds a recorded interest in
the Subject Property pursuant to the Certificate of Lien recorded February 25,
2020 as Document No. 20200220830 and pursuant to the State Tax Liens recorded
on March 26, 2024 as Document No. 20240190884 and October 10, 2024 as Document
No. 20240692004. (Health Decl., ¶¶ 9-10, Exhs. H-J.)

            The Subject Property has
been subject to numerous code enforcement investigations since October 2024,
and contains the following substandard conditions: unpermitted construction in
the main dwelling, garage and rear yard of the Subject Property; rubbish,
discarded furniture and various household equipment visible from public
right-of-way and/or adjoining property; junk, trash and debris scattered over
property; lack of property maintenance; and lack of necessary permits and
business license. (Ayub Decl., ¶¶ 2-18.)

            On October 24, 2024, the
San Gabriel Police Department responded to the Subject Property and issued a first
Notice of Violation based on observations of (1) multiple violations of section
105.1 of the California Building Code (“CBC”) resulting from the Owners’
failure to obtain the required permits for the Unpermitted Construction; (2)
San Gabriel Municipal Code (“SGMC”) section 110.02 resulting from the Owners’
failure to obtain the required business license for the operation of a short
term rental property; (3) SGMC section 153.020 for performing Unpermitted
Construction in violation of the Use of Buildings and Land in Established
Zones; and (4) SGMC section 153.031 for the Owners’ use of the Subject Property
beyond the use permitted by its Single Family Residence zoning designation.
(Ayub Decl., ¶¶ 2-4, Exh. L.) The Notice of Violation directed Yu and Cong to
cease short-term rental of the Subject Property, obtain an Investigation
Inspection Permit, and schedule an inspection by October 30, 2024. (Ayub Decl.,
¶ 4, Exh. L.)

            Yu and Cong did not
comply with the Notice of Violation and a second Notice was issued on November
6, 2024. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 5, Exh. M.) Yu and Cong remained noncompliant and  third, fourth, and fifth Notices were issued.
(Ayub Decl., ¶¶ 6-8, Exhs. N-P.) On February 26, 2025, Code Enforcement
Supervisor Aldo Ayub (“Ayub”) spoke with Yu, provided her all previously sent
notices, and granted her a two-week extension to obtain a lawyer. (Ayub Decl.,
¶ 9.)

            On March 18, 2025, Ayub
attempted to contact Yu by phone unsuccessfully. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 10.)
Subsequently, Ayub posted a Code Enforcement Notification on the Subject
Property describing the unabated code violations and directed compliance by
April 1, 2025. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 11, Exh. Q.) Ayub also issued multiple
administrative citations and compliance letters requesting inspections on Yu
and Cong for the unabated code violations. (Ayub Decl., ¶¶ 12-13. Exhs. R-S;
Heath Decl., ¶¶ 11-12, Exhs. T-U.) Ayub sent Yu and Cong a Notice of Inspection
of Property, but when Ayub arrived at the Subject Property on the listed date,
the occupants refused to allow Ayub to enter. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 14, Exh. V.)
However, from the public right-of-way, Ayub saw that the garage had been
converted into a habitable living space and observed an unpermitted structure
in the rear of the Subject Property. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 14, Exh. W.)

            On December 10, 2025,
Ayub and the City’s Building Official, Christopher Kalbaklian, three
detectives, and an officer from the San Gabriel Police Department executed an
Inspection Warrant issued on the Subject Property and found additional
unpermitted walls, an illegally added bedroom, drywall, and new flooring in the
garage, indications that interior walls had been removed and attempts had been
made to restore the garage to its original condition, a large unpermitted
structure next to the garage that was in the process of being demolished, and
construction debris. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 16, Exh. Y.)

On March 5, 2025, Ayub posted a Notice and
Order to Repair or Abate on the Subject Property. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 17, Exh. AA.) Despite
these attempts, all violations identified continue to exist and no action has
been taken to abate any of the violations. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 18.)

LEGAL STANDARD

Health and Safety Code section 17980.7, subdivision (c)(1) states that
an “enforcement agency, tenant, or tenant association or organization may seek
and the court may order, the appointment of a receiver for the substandard
building pursuant to this subdivision. In its petition to the court, the
enforcement agency, tenant, or tenant association or organization shall include
proof that notice of the petition was posted in a prominent place on the
substandard building and mailed first-class mail to all persons with a recorded
interest in the real property upon which the substandard building exists not
less than three days prior to filing the petition.” “If any building is
maintained in a manner that violates any provisions of this part, the building
standards published in the State Building Standards Code…, or any provision in
a local ordinance that is similar…, and the violations are so extensive and of
such a nature that the health and safety of residents or the public is
substantially endangered, the enforcement agency may issue an order or notice
to repair or abate pursuant to this part.” (Health & Saf. Code, § 17980.6.)
If a property owner does not address the issues in the notice within a
reasonable time, the enforcement agency “may seek and the court may order, the
appointment of a receiver for the substandard building pursuant to this
subdivision….” (Health & Saf. Code, § 17980.6, subd. (c).) A property that contains
structural hazards or inadequate sanitation that “endangers the life, limb,
health, property, safety, or welfare of the occupants of the building, nearby
residents, or the public shall be deemed and hereby is declared to be a
substandard building.” (Health & Saf. Code, § 17920.3.)

“The appointment of a receiver is a drastic remedy” such that the
Court must “carefully weigh the propriety of such appointment in exercising
[its] discretion to appoint a receiver particularly if there is an alternative
remedy.” (Barclays Bank of California v. Superior Court (1977) 69
Cal.App.3d 593, 597, quotation marks omitted.) Indeed “courts are strongly
discouraged—although not strictly prohibited—from appointing a receiver unless
the more intrusive oversight of a receiver is a necessity because other, less
intrusive remedies are either inadequate or unavailable.” (Medipro Medical
Staffing LLC v. Certified Nursing Registry, Inc. (2021) 60 Cal.App.5th
622, 628, quotation marks omitted.) “In appointing a receiver, the court shall
consider whether the owner has been afforded a reasonable opportunity to
correct the conditions cited in the notice of violation.” (Health & Saf.
Code, § 17980.7, subd. (c)(1).

DISCUSSION

            Petitioner moves for an order
appointing a receiver and to abate the allegedly substandard building.

            Petitioner, in its capacity as the
City of San Gabriel, qualifies as an enforcement agency that may seek court
order and appointment of a receiver pursuant to Health and Safety Code
section 17980.7, subdivision (c)(1). Petitioner demonstrates that that notice
was provided to Respondents by placing prominently the Notice and Order to Repair or Abate on the door to the Subject Property.
(See Ayub Decl., ¶ 17, Exh. AA.) Further, Petitioner has filed proof of service
of the petition on all Respondents.

            Petitioner demonstrates that the
Subject Property’s condition violates multiple statutory codes and local
ordinances. “Any building or portion thereof, regardless of zoning designation
or approved uses of the building” in which there exists any condition “that
endangers the life, limb, health, property, safety, or welfare of the occupants
of the building, nearby residents, or the public shall be deemed and hereby is
declared to be a substandard building.” (Health & Saf. Code, § 17920.3.) “[A]
city council may, by ordinance, declare what it deems to constitute a public
nuisance.” (City of Monterey v. Carrnshimba (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 1068,
1086.) California Building Code section 105.1 requires a person to obtain a
building permit before constructing, altering, converting, or demolishing any building.
Similarly, San Gabriel Municipal Code (“SGMC”) section 153.020 provides that
alterations, reconstruction, and enlargements of buildings must comply with
city guidelines. During the inspection, Ayub found unpermitted walls, an
illegally added bedroom, drywall, and new flooring in the garage, indications
that interior walls had been removed and attempts had been made to restore the
garage to its original condition, a large unpermitted structure next to the
garage that was in the process of being demolished, and construction debris.
(Ayub Decl., ¶ 16, Exh, Y.) Next, SGMC section 110.2 requires a business
license to operate a short-term rental. SGMC section 153.031, subdivision
(B)(1) states that accessory dwelling units shall not be operated commercially.
Despite numerous notices of violation and administrative citations, the Subject
Property continues to be used as a short-term rental and a large unpermitted
structure next to the garage existed. (Ayub Decl., ¶¶ 4-18.) SGMC section
153.031, subdivision (C)(2)(b) states that “[n]o home occupation shall generate
any traffic, parking, sewage, or water use in excess of what is normal for
residential use.” The Subject Property has rubbish, discarded furniture and
various household equipment visible from public right-of-way and/or adjoining
property, junk, trash and debris scattered over property, and a general lack of
property maintenance. (Ayub Decl., ¶ 2.) Additionally, Petitioner, a city, has
found that the Subject Property qualifies a public nuisance. Therefore, the
Subject Property exists in violation of numerous ordinances and codes and is a
substandard building within the definition of Health and Safety Code section
17920.3.

            The Court finds that Respondents
have been afforded a reasonable opportunity to correct the conditions. Ayub
issued upon the Subject Property five notices of violation, spoke with Yu
directly and provided her with physical notices, attempted to contact by phone
call, posted a code enforcement notification, issued multiple administrative
citations, a notice of property inspection, inspection warrant, and notice and
order to repair or abate prior to Petitioner bringing the instant petition.
(Ayub Decl., ¶¶ 3-17.)  

            The Court grants the petition for
order to abate substandard building and appointment of receiver. The Court has
reviewed Petitioner’s nomination of Jackson Wyche and his curriculum vitae,
qualifications, and background, and finds Wyche acceptable as a receiver for
the Subject Property.

            Petitioner requests attorneys’ fees
in connection with the instant petition. Health and Safety Code section
17980.7, subdivision (c)(11) states that the “prevailing party in an action
pursuant to this section shall be entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees and
court costs as may be fixed by the court.” Petitioner provides no metric for
the Court to determine the amount of reasonable attorneys’ fees and declines to
award attorneys’ fees on that basis.

CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Petitioner City of San
Gabriel’s petition for order to abate
substandard building and appointment of receiver.
