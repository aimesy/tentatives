DEPARTMENT 511 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV28409    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 17, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           In The Passing Lane LLC v. Lightning Motors Corporation

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV28409

 

MOTION FOR AN ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: CONTEMPT AND FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiff In The Passing Lane LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): None

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order commanding non-party Daily Direct LLC d/b/a Haulbikes to show cause as to why they should not be held in contempt for failing to comply with the court’s April 23, 2026 order and issuing monetary sanctions in the amount of $3,000.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for an order to show cause re contempt is GRANTED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On October 29, 2024, Plaintiff In The Passing Lane, LLC (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendant Lightning Motors Corporation (Defendant) with causes of action for: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of implied warranty; (3) breach of express warranty; violation of Song-Beverly Act; (5) and violation of Commercial Code. 

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff purchased a luxury custom LS-218 electric motorcycle for over $41,000, but upon delivery the vehicle arrived visibly damaged, poorly assembled, and unsafe for use. Plaintiff immediately notified Defendant, requested that the motorcycle be picked up and refunded, and repeatedly raised concerns about defects and safety risks, including those associated with lithium-ion batteries. Defendant refused to refund or retrieve the motorcycle, leaving Plaintiff with an unusable vehicle and ongoing storage and ownership expenses. 

 

On December 3, 2024, Defendant filed an answer. 

 

On February 13, 2026, Defendant filed a motion for summary judgment, or alternatively, summary adjudication (MSJ). 

 

On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel compliance with deposition subpoena for production of business records to Daily Direct LLC d/b/a Haulbikes which the court GRANTED. 

 

On April 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed an ex parte application to continue the hearing on the MSJ, trial, and all related dates which the court GRANTED in part.

 

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed another ex parte application to continue the MSJ, trial, and all related dates which the court GRANTED.

 

On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for an osc re: contempt against Daily Direct LLC dba Haulbikes and monetary sanctions of $3,000.

 

On July 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed another ex parte application to continue the MSJ and to advance the hearing date on the instant motion for an OSC re contempt which the court GRANTED in part. Plaintiff provided notice of ruling on July 27, 2026.

 

Oppositions to the motion for an osc re: contempt were due on or before September 3, 2026. As of September 14, 2026, the court has not received any oppositions.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

“If a deponent fails to answer any question or to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing under the deponent's control that is specified in the deposition notice or a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer or production.” (CCP § 2025.480(a).)

 

“If a deponent fails to obey an order entered under this section, the failure may be considered a contempt of court.” (CCP § 2025.480(k).)

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Plaintiff contends relief is warranted because Haulbikes received notice of the court’s order granting Plaintiff’s motion to compel compliance with third party subpoena, Haulbikes reached out to Plaintiff, and then stopped communicating with Plaintiff after promising on multiple occasions to produce documents.

 

Here, relief is warranted. First, Plaintiff demonstrated Haulbikes’ disobedience of the court’s April 23, 2026 order compelling compliance with third party subpoena and the imposition of sanctions. (Declaration of Saam Takaloo ¶¶ 8, 9, 10.) As such, an OSC re Contempt is proper. The court defers ruling on the additional monetary sanctions until such hearing.

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Plaintiff’s motion for an order to show cause re: contempt.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for an order to show cause re contempt is GRANTED;

2.      Moving party is to prepare a proposed order with a proposed hearing date and then personally serve the order.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV16478    Hearing Date: September 17, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 17, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Alexxandra Jarrett v. Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA, LLC

 

CASE NO.:                25STCV16478

 

DEMURRER TO THIRD AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Alexxandra Jarrett

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      Demurrer to the first, second, third, and fourth causes of action for failing to state sufficient facts to constitute a cause of action.[1]

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Demurrer to the first, second, and third causes of action are SUSTAINED without leave to amend;

2.      Demurrer to the fourth cause of action is OVERRULED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On June 6, 2025, Plaintiff Alexxandra Jarrett (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendant Mercedes Benz Financial Services USA LLC (Defendant) with causes of action for: (1) breach of contract; (2) unlawful repossession; (3) breach of peace; (4) FDCPA/CLRA; (5) conversion; (6) trespass to chattels; and (7) failure to provide required notice. 

 

On July 14, 2025, Defendant filed an answer. 

 

On September 29, 2025, Defendant filed a notice of removal to federal court. 

 

On December 15, 2025, the clerk filed a notice of remand from federal court. 

 

On January 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC) against Defendants Mercedes Benz Financial Services USA LLC (Mercedes) and A-Z Recovery (A-Z) (sometimes collectively Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) wrongful repossession/breach of the peace; (2) violation of Comm. Code §§ 9609, 9611, 9623, 9626; (3) violation of Vehicle Leasing Act – Civ. Code § 2985.8 (against Mercedes only); (4) conversion; and (5) negligence. 

 

According to the FAC, this case involves Defendants’ repossession of Plaintiff’s leased Mercedes Benz vehicle. Plaintiff alleges that A-Z repossessed the vehicle without allowing Plaintiff to retrieve her personal belongings. Plaintiff alleges that Mercedes took no steps to cure and did not follow various laws allowing Plaintiff the opportunity to make payments, recover the vehicle, or recover her belongings. 

 

On January 9, 2026, Mercedes filed a demurrer to the FAC with motion to strike which the court SUSTAINED with leave to amend and DENIED as moot. 

 

On February 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for leave to amend the FAC to file an amended complaint which the court DENIED without prejudice. 

 

On April 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Second Amended Complaint (SAC).  She added additional allegations and a cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

On May 11, 2026, Mercedes filed a demurrer to the SAC which the court SUSTAINED with leave to amend.

 

On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative Third Amended Complaint (TAC).

 

On August 10, 2026, Mercedes filed the instant demurrer. On September 4, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.[2] On September 10, 2026, Mercedes filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Meet and Confer¿ 

¿ 

Prior to filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to satisfy their meet and confer obligations pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41, and demonstrate that they so satisfied their meet and confer obligation by submitting a declaration pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41(a)(2) & (3). ¿The meet and confer requirement also applies to motions to strike. (CCP § 435.5.)¿

 

Here, Mercedes’ counsel filed a Judicial Council form CIV-140 indicating meet and confer occurred by telephone at least five days before the deadline to file the demurrer and motion to strike. (Kemp Decl. ¶ 2(a).) The requirement is therefore met. 

 

Demurrer – Sufficiency of Facts¿ 

¿ 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿(Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.¿In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice.¿(Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)¿“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. …. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”¿(Hahn¿147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true. (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.) Courts also consider exhibits attached to the complaint and incorporated by reference. (See Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94 (Frantz).)¿¿ 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

First Cause of Action – Wrongful Repossession / Breach of the Peace 

 

Mercedes contends this claim fails because they are immune from liability under Bus. & Prof. Code § 7507.13(b) because A-Z is a licensed repossession agent. Plaintiff argues she alleged Mercedes’ independent conduct. 

 

“After default, a secured party may . . . (1) Take possession of the collateral.” (Comm. Code § 9609(a).) A secured party may do so pursuant to judicial process or “[w]ithout judicial process, if it proceeds without breach of the peace.” (Id. at subd. (b).)  

 

Bus. & Prof. Code § 7507.13(b) states: 

 

The legal owner, debt collector, debtor, lienholder, lessor, lessee, or registered owner, or the agent of any of them, is not liable for any act or omission by a licensed repossession agency, or its agent, in carrying out an assignment or repossession order and is entitled to indemnity from the repossession agency for any loss, damage, cost, or expense, including court costs and attorney's fees, that the legal owner, debt collector, debtor, lienholder, lessor, lessee, or registered owner, or the agent of any of them, may reasonably incur as a result thereof. Nothing in this subdivision limits the liability of any person for his or her tortious conduct.

 

After reviewing the TAC, Plaintiff’s claims against Mercedes are still barred by Bus. & Prof. Code § 7507.13(b). As before, the central point of Plaintiffs’ complaint against Mercedes is how A-Z repossessed the vehicle. (TAC p.3:4-4:10.) She alleges Mercedes acknowledged the agent’s conduct was improper. (TAC 4:13-17.) This allegation defeats her ratification argument. As before, this cause of action is based on the repossession conduct by A-Z. Plaintiff previously alleged that A-Z is a licensed repossession company. (SAC ¶ 7.) Now she alleges they are a repossession company acting as Mercedes’ operational agent for purposes of the repossession. (TAC 2:18-19.) Plaintiff did not allege facts that demonstrate Mercede’ own tortious conduct. Finally, the court rejects Plaintiff’s argument that the non-delegable duty doctrine provides liability as that would negate the Legislature’s “clear and unequivocal” expression of providing indemnity. (See, e.g., Presbyterian Camp & Conference Centers, Inc. v. Superior Court (2021) 12 Cal.5th 493, 503 [“A statute will be construed in light of common law decisions, unless its language clearly and unequivocally discloses an intention to depart from, alter, or abrogate the common-law rule concerning the particular subject matter.”] [internal citations omitted].)

 

Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS Mercedes’ demurrer to the First Cause of Action without leave to amend. 

 

Second Cause of Action – Violation of Civ. Code §§ 2985.8, 2983.2 (Vehicle Leasing Act) 

 

Mercedes argues this statute does not afford the relief sought. Plaintiff argues a different statute does. 

 

The Vehicle Leasing Act details obligations for each party that must be included in the lease agreement. (Civ. Code § 2985.8(a).) One requirement is the following notice: “You have the right to return the vehicle, and receive a refund of any payments made if the credit application is not approved, unless nonapproval results from an incomplete application or from incorrect information provided by you.” (Id. at subd. (f).)  

 

Civ. Code § 2983.2(a) provides:

 

[A]ny provision in any conditional sale contract for the sale of a motor vehicle to the contrary notwithstanding, at least 15 days' written notice of intent to dispose of a repossessed or surrendered motor vehicle shall be given to all persons liable on the contract. The notice shall be personally served or shall be sent by certified mail, return receipt requested, or first-class mail, postage prepaid, directed to the last known address of the persons liable on the contract.

 

Civ. Code § 2983.2(b) provides:

 

Unless automatically provided to the buyer within 45 days after the disposition of the motor vehicle, the seller or holder shall provide to any person liable on the contract within 45 days after their written request, if the request is made within one year after the disposition, a written accounting regarding the disposition.

 

These provisions do not apply to a lease where the lessee will not become the vehicle’s owner at the end of the lease. (Civ. Code §§ 2981(a)(2), 2981.5.)

 

After reviewing the TAC, Plaintiff failed to state a claim. First, she alleges a lease agreement with Mercedes. (TAC 3:1-2.) Second, she alleges Mercedes provided inconsistent and unitemized reinstatement amounts verbally, never delivered written paperwork, did not start sending notices until after she filed this lawsuit, sent a  notice to her mother who resides at a different address, delayed accounting, and issued a facially defective statement. (TAC 7:26-8:11.) Plaintiff alleges that, as a result, Mercedes has waived any legal right to claim a deficiency balance against Plaintiff. (TAC 8:11.) However, it is unclear what relief Plaintiff seeks let alone damages as requested in her prayer for relief.

 
Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS Mercedes’ demurrer to the second cause of action without leave to amend. 

 

Third Cause of Action – Violation of UCC Article 2A (Comm. Code §§ 2A-525, 2A-527, 2A-528) 

 

Mercedes argues this statute does not afford the relief sought. Plaintiff argues a different statute does. 

 

After a default by the lessee under the lease contract of the type described in¿Section 2A-523(1)¿or¿2A-523(3)(a)¿or, if agreed, after other default by the lessee, the lessor has the right to take possession of the goods. If the lease contract so provides, the lessor may require the lessee to assemble the goods and make them available to the lessor at a place to be designated by the lessor which is reasonably convenient to both parties. Without removal, the lessor may render unusable any goods employed in trade or business, and may dispose of goods on the lessee's premises (Section 2A-527).

 

(U. Comm. Code § 2A-525(2).) 

 

“The lessor may proceed under subsection (2) without judicial process if it can be done without breach of the peace or the lessor may proceed by action.” (U. Comm. Code § 2A-525(3).) 

 

UCC section 2A-527 pertains to the lessor’s rights to dispose of the goods. UCC section 2A-528 pertains to the lessor’s damages for non-acceptance, failure to pay, repudiation, or other default.  

 

As before, these code sections do not provide for the relief Plaintiff seeks. They apply to the lessor’s rights.

 

Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS Mercedes’ demurrer to the third cause of action without leave to amend.  

 

Fourth Cause of Action – Trespass to Chattels/Conversion 

 

Mercedes reiterates their immunity argument here and point out that Plaintiff did not allege right to possession of the vehicle or Mercedes’ interference with or disposition of Plaintiff’s property. Plaintiff argues she sufficiently stated this claim due to Mercedes’ interference with retrieving her personal property. 

 

The elements for a claim of¿conversion¿are: “(1) the plaintiff's ownership or right to possession of the property; (2) the defendant's¿conversion¿by a wrongful act or disposition of property right; and (3) damages.” (IIG Wireless, Inc. v. Yi¿(2018) 22 Cal.App.5th 630, 650;¿Berry v. Frazier¿(2023) 90 Cal.App.5th 1258, 1271.) Intentional destruction or alteration of property may constitute¿conversion¿of property. (Martinez v. Robledo¿(2012) 210 Cal.App.4th 384, 387.) If the interference is less significant than the exercise of complete dominion or control, the proper claim is for¿trespass¿to¿personal property. (See¿Jamgotchian v. Slender¿(2009) 170 Cal.App.4th 1384, 1400-1401.) Wrongful intent is not¿required. (Mendoza v. Rast Produce Co., Inc.¿(2006) 140 Cal.App.4th 1395, 1405.)¿ 

 

Returning the property impacts damages. (Krusi v. Bear, Stearns & Co. (1983) 114 Cal.App.3d 664, 673 [“the plaintiff's damages will be reduced if the defendant returns the property or the plaintiff otherwise recovers the property.”]) So, the court rejects Mercedes’s arguments to the extent they attack the conversion claim due to return of the personal property. 

 

As before, Plaintiff has really alleged a claim of trespass to chattels/personal property against Mercedes. 

 

The elements of a claim for¿trespass¿to personal property are: (1) plaintiff owned, possessed, or had a right to property, (2) defendant's intentional interference with plaintiff's use or possession, (3) no consent by plaintiff, (4) harm, and (5) causation.¿(Intel Corp. v. Hamidi¿(2003) 30 Cal.4th 1342, 1350-1351.)¿A¿defendant may temporarily dispossess plaintiff of the property, including by blocking access to it.¿(Jamgotchian v. Slender¿(2009) 170 Cal.App.4th 1384, 1401.)¿To be actionable, defendant's interference must have caused some injury to the property or to plaintiff's rights in the property.¿(Intel Corp. supra,¿at p. 1350.)¿¿ 

 

Here, Plaintiff has sufficiently alleged a claim for trespass to chattels. Plaintiff alleged that Mercedes refused to disclose the Vehicle’s location to Plaintiff so she could retrieve her personal property, sold the Vehicle with her personal property inside, and eventually returned her property in a damaged box. (TAC 10:1-8, 15-16.) She further alleges specific damages from loss of use of her daily toll transponder of $450.00 and $50.00 in damage for the other, unidentified, physical items. (TAC ¶ 19-23.)

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES Mercedes’ demurrer to the fourth cause of action. 

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Demurrer to the first, second, and third causes of action are SUSTAINED without leave to amend;

2.      Demurrer to the fourth cause of action is OVERRULED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
