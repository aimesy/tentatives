DEPARTMENT 309 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV22258    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

JANELLE ARAMBULA

                          

         vs.

 

LOS ANGELES COUNTY DEPARMENT OF MENTAL HEALTH

 

 Case No.: 23STCV22258

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 5, 2026

 

 

Plaintiff’s motion to strike is GRANTED. 

 

On 9/15/2023, Plaintiff Janelle Arambula (Plaintiff) filed suit against Los Angeles County Department of Mental Health (Defendant). On 2/5/2024, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC), alleging: (1) discrimination; (2) harassment; (3) retaliation; (4) failure to prevent; (5) failure to provide reasonable accommodation; (6) failure to engage in good faith interactive process; (7) retaliation; and (8) retaliation.

 

On 8/5/2026, Plaintiff moved to strike costs.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff moves to strike Defendant’s Memorandum of Costs in its entirety pursuant to CCP section 1032 and Government Code section 12965(b), because unlike typical civil litigation, a defendant in a FEHA action cannot recover costs unless the court finds the lawsuit frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless.

 

            In opposition, Defendant argues that the motion is moot “Plaintiff Janelle Arambula’s motion to tax/strike costs is moot because the County of Los Angeles, an employer that prevailed at summary judgment, timely filed a motion for fees and costs, asserting that Arambula’s case was frivolous, after Arambula filed the present motion, but within the statutory deadline to move for fees and costs. The memorandum of costs was filed out of caution, so that the County could not be accused of missing the statutory deadline for seeking costs. Taking this motion off calendar will not harm Arambula in any way, as she can attack the validity of the costs sought in her opposition to the County’s motion, scheduled to be heard on September 1, 2026.” (Opp., 2: 4-11.)

 

            However, the motion set for 9/1/2026 is for attorney fees, and while it also seeks an award of costs, Plaintiff’s motion here is properly filed and Defendant is not entitled to determine that a motion is moot because it believes Plaintiff can adequately address the arguments in an opposition to another motion at a later date. Notably, Defendant’s contention that “Arambula is free to attack it in her opposition, but she cannot do so in the present motion” is not supported by any supporting legal authority. “When [a party] fails to raise a point or asserts it but fails to support it with reasoned argument and citation to authority, we treat the point as waived.” (Badie v. Bank of America (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 779, 784-785.)

 

            Defendant did not submit any substantive argument to show that Plaintiff’s claim was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless, and thus has not shown any entitlement to costs.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to strike is granted.

 

 

           

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 24STCV14630    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

WARREN HOLYFIELD, et al.

 

         vs.

 

MICHAEL HERNANDEZ, MD

 

 Case No.: 24STCV14630

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 6, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion compelling White Memorial to designate and produce one or more person(s) most knowledgeable for deposition in response to Plaintiffs’ Notice of Deposition is GRANTED, consistent with the ruling set forth below. The PMK deposition(s) is/are ordered to take place within 20 days of this order.

 

On 6/11/2024, Plaintiffs Warren and Rebecca Holyfield (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed suit against Michael Hernandez MD and Adventist Health Care Network, alleging wrongful death.

 

            On 9/23/2024, Plaintiff filed a Doe Amendment substituting White Memorial Medical Center dba Adventist Health White Memorial for DOE-1.

 

            On 6/4/2026, Plaintiffs moved for an ordering compelling White Memorial Medical Center dba Adventist Health White Memorial (AHWM) to designate and produce one or more person(s) most knowledgeable for deposition in response to Plaintiffs’ Notice of Deposition.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiffs argue that AHWM must be compelled to produce their PMK pursuant to CCP section 2025.230. In support, Plaintiffs contend:

 

On multiple occasions, Plaintiffs noticed the deposition of Defendant White Memorial Medical Center dba Adventist Health White Memorial with fourteen categories directed to Mr. Holyfield’s October 2023 admission, evaluation, treatment, suicide-risk assessment, discharge, and related hospital handling of the occurrence. AHWM refused to designate any PMK, contending that the categories are not described with reasonable particularity and that, because multiple individuals participated in the admission and some providers were physicians or physician assistants not employed by the hospital, AHWM has no person or persons most knowledgeable who can testify on its behalf.

 

            (Motion, 3: 3-11.)

 

            In opposition, AHWM argues that the deposition notice is overbroad, as the notice describes “the entirety” of Mr. Holyfield’s care and treatment. AHWM also contends that various providers were not employed by the hospital, that several individuals had already been deposed, and that Plaintiffs should identify specific caregivers from the records for individual depositions. AHWM additionally identified Dr. Hannah Heath Janeway and James Anthony Slusher, PA-C, as persons reflected in the records with respect to certain categories,

 

After review, the Court finds the fourteen categories to be sufficiently particular. As put by Plaintiff, “the fourteen categories concern one patient, one admission, and one set of alleged negligent acts and omissions by AHWM in connection with Mr. Holyfield’s October 2023 hospitalization. They address the hospital’s knowledge of the patient’s presentation, history obtained, examinations, advice and warnings, care and treatment, diagnostic testing, discharge risks, suicide-risk assessments, and related internal handling.” (Motion, 6: 4-8.)

 

Moreover, the Court is unpersuaded by AHWM’s position that no PMK exists because multiple individuals participated in the care and treatment and some were not hospital employees. Section 2025.230 does not require Plaintiffs to identify which individual employee or contractor knows each detail and then depose them separately before obtaining an entity deposition. Instead, the statute requires the entity to designate and produce those witnesses “most qualified” to testify on its behalf to the extent of information known or reasonably available to the deponent. If one witness cannot cover all topics, the entity may designate multiple witnesses. AHWM has access to the hospital chart, nursing notes, ED documentation, behavioral-health documentation, consult entries, discharge records, policies and procedures, and communications reflected in or reasonably obtainable from its systems. AHWM therefore can and must prepare witnesses to testify on the noticed categories from the hospital’s institutional knowledge.

 

And, even assuming AHWM could not compel an outside physician to testify as a hospital designee, that does not excuse the hospital from designating current officers, directors, managing agents, employees, or agents who can testify to what is known or reasonably available to AHWM about those providers’ role, chart entries, orders, consults, and participation in the admission.

 

Finally, AHWM objects to producing a PMK regarding Category 13 based on Evidence Code section 1157, which protects the proceedings and records of organized peer-review committees and peer review bodies from discovery. California authority recognizes that section 1157 is limited to protected proceedings and records, and does not extend categorically to all administrative matters. Accordingly, the Court compels PMK testimony on all nonprivileged aspects of the category while allowing AHWM to preserve specific objections at deposition where privileged inquiry is posed.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion compelling AHWM to designate and produce one or more person(s) most knowledgeable for deposition in response to Plaintiffs’ Notice of Deposition is granted, consistent with the ruling set forth above.

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV24547    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

CHARLOTTE CADENAS

                          

         vs.

 

STANTON AND COMPANY, LLC

                                         

 Case No.: 25STCV24547

 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 5, 2026

 

 

Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s fourth and eighth causes of action is OVERRULED.

 

On 8/20/2025, Plaintiff Charlotte Cadenas (Plaintiff) filed suit against Stanton and Company, LLC (Defendant). On 2/25/2026, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC), alleging: (1) disability discrimination; (2) retaliation; (3) retaliation; (4) retaliation; (5) failure to accommodate; (6) failure to engage in interactive process; (7) wrongful termination; (8) violation of the California Equal Pay Act; and (9) waiting time penalties.

 

On 4/3/2026, Defendant demurred to Plaintiff’s fourth and eighth causes of action.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s fourth and eighth causes of action are insufficiently pled.

 

            After review, the Court disagrees.

 

            As to the fourth cause of action, Defendant argues a number of deficiencies including that internal personnel issues don’t constitute illegal activity, there is no causation, there were legitimate reasons for termination, and the cause of action is duplicative and uncertain. However, first, Defendants’ arguments related to illegality, causation, and legitimate termination reasons involve factual determinations not properly made at the pleadings stage. As to the remaining arguments, the Court finds that the cause of action is not uncertain and also finds no duplication. FEHA retaliation and section 1102.5 retaliation require different elements and are based on different alleged types of retaliation.

 

            As for the eighth cause of action for violation of the California Equal Pay Act (CEPA), Defendant argues that this claim is insufficiently pled because Plaintiff has not alleged specific facts regarding comparator employees.

 

CEPA prohibits the paying of lower wage rates than those paid to members of the opposite sex or of other ethnic or racial groups for substantially similar work. (Cal. Lab. Code, § 1197.5.) In order to establish a prima facie case under CEPA, a plaintiff is required to show “not only that she is paid lower wages than a male comparator for equal work, but that she has selected the proper comparator.” (Hall v. County of Los Angeles (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 318, 324) (explaining that an EPA claim fails as a matter of law where plaintiff fails to provide a basis for the use of a male colleague as a comparator).)

 

The Court previously sustained Defendant’s demurrer to this cause of action on the grounds that Plaintiff needed to allege additional facts which could provide Defendant adequate notice of the skill, effort, responsibility, or working conditions of any alleged comparator. Now, Plaintiff alleges that two named Caucasian women earned more than Ms. Cadenas, despite sharing the same title and duties as Ms. Cadenas. (FAC ¶ 3.) While Defendant argues that the claim remains deficient because “the FAC does not allege any overlap in the comparators’ employment, seniority, or length of employment to support a contemporaneous pay comparison,” these specifics require the benefit of discovery. As such, Plaintiff’s allegations are sufficient at the pleadings stage.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s demurrer to Plaintiff’s fourth and eighth causes of action is overruled.

 

It is so ordered.
