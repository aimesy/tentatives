DEPARTMENT 26 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

If you desire to submit on the Court's tentative ruling, you may do so by e-mailing Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse until the morning of the motion hearing.
 
The e-mail address is SSCDEPT26@lacourt.org
 
The heading on your e-mail should contain the case name, number, hearing date, and that you submit. The message should indicate your name, contact information, and the party you represent. Please note that if you submit on the court's tentative ruling and do not appear, another party may appear to argue and the court may change its tentative ruling after the oral argument.
 
If there are no appearances by either side and no submission on the Court's tentative ruling, the court will adopt its tentative ruling as its final ruling. The deadline for a party to withdraw a pending motion, or take it off calendar, is TWO COURT DAYS before the hearing on the motion. The court will enter its tentative ruling on motions that remain on calendar after that deadline.
 
Please note, the above e-mail address is to inform the court of your submission on the tentative ruling. All other inquiries to this e-mail will not receive a response.
 
Parties may schedule remote appearances for hearings on motions via LACourtConnect (https://mylacourt.org/laccwelcome). Remote appearances are not permitted for trial or Applications for Order for Appearance and Examination proceedings absent leave of court.

Case Number: 26STLC00099    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Malcolm Bull is awarded attorney’s fees of $3,983.50 and costs of $1,030.25, jointly and severally against Defendants Car Search USA and Westlake Services, LLC.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On January 7, 2026, Plaintiff Malcolm Bull (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Car Search USA (“Defendant Car Search”), Westlake Services, LLC dba Westlake Financial Services (“Defendant Westlake”), Merchants Bonding Company (Mutual) (“Defendant Merchants”). The Complaint alleges causes of action for (1) Violation of Consumers Legal Remedies Act, Civil Code § 1750 et seq; (2) Violation of California Business and Professions Code § 17200, et seq., Unlawful Acts or Practices; (3) Claim Against Surety; and (4) Violation of Code of Civil Procedure §§ 1281.97 & 1281.99.

 

Defendant Merchants filed its Answer on March 4, 2026; Defendant Car Search filed a General Denial on March 16, 2026.  On May 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Motion to Compel Arbitration, for Court to Pick Arbitration Forum, for an Order Confirming Defendants Car Search USA and Westlake Services, LLC are in Material Breach of the Arbitration Clause, an Order for Fees and Costs Associated with Arbitration, and Request for Stay. Defendant Car Search filed a Limited Opposition on August 4, 2026. Defendant Westlake has neither appeared in the action, nor filed an opposition to the instant Motion.

 

At the hearing on August 18, 2026, the Court granted the Motion to Compel Arbitration, for Court to Pick Arbitration Forum, for an Order Confirming Defendants Car Search USA and Westlake Services, LLC are in Material Breach of the Arbitration Clause, an Order for Fees and Costs Associated with Arbitration, and Request for Stay. (Minute Order, 08/18/26.) However, the Court continued the question of the amount of attorney’s fees and costs to be awarded and ordered the parties to file supplemental briefing. (Ibid.) Plaintiff filed a supplemental declaration on August 25, 2026, and Defendant Car Search USA filed a supplemental opposition on September 10, 2026, and on September 11, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an award of $4,883.50 in attorney’s fees and $1,030.25 in costs. Plaintiff’s request for costs is supported by the declaration of counsel, demonstrating that the costs were incurred for filing and service fees. (Motion, Sadr Decl., ¶8 and Exh. 1, p. 3.) The attorney’s fees are based on time billed at $750.00 per hour for 3.6 hours of time, $500.00 per hour for 3.8 hours of time, and 2.1 hours of paralegal time billed at $135.00 per hour. (Motion, Supp. Sadr Decl., ¶6 and Exh. 1.) The requested fees are supported by the declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel attesting to 25 years of experience in consumer protection law to justify the rate of $750.00, and 12 years of experience in the same field for the attorney who bills at $500.00 per hour. Defendant Car Search USA’s opposition argues that these rates are excessive for this type of case, based on defense counsel’s rate of $350.00 per hour. (Supp. Opp., Miller Decl., ¶2.) The Court agrees that this type of case does not warrant a billing rate of $750.00. Plaintiff’s counsel files numerous cases of this type in the Limited Civil Court and this particular action does not involve any unusual factual or legal issues. The $750.00 hourly attorney rate, therefore, will be reduced to $500.00, for a total award of $3,983.50 in fees.

 

Defendant Car Search USA also argues that the Court of Appeals has rejected treating the failure to pay arbitration fees within 30 days as an automatic forfeiture, and instead considered whether the nonpayment was willful, fraudulent, or grossly negligent before imposing harsh consequences, citing Hohenshelt v. Superior Court (2025) 18 Cal.5th 310, 346.  Hohenshelt did not apply that inquiry to the requirement to pay reasonable expenses incurred as a result of the breach; it applied it to forfeiture of the right of arbitration. (See Hohenshelt, 18 Cal.5th at 339-340 (“[S]ection 1281.99’s mandatory sanction provision . . . requires the drafting party to pay any reasonable expenses incurred by the employee or consumer as a result of its failure to timely pay arbitration fees (§ 1281.99, subd. (a)) — in essence, to make the other party whole (Civ. Code, § 3275) — whether the nonpayment was willful or not.”); see also id. at 339 (“Consistent with general contract law, a material breach is enough to trigger an award of compensatory damages, which the mandatory sanctions provision of section 1281.99, subdivision (a) provides for. But other consequences depend on the circumstances.”)  Even if the standard did apply here, the time to raise the argument would have been in response to Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel.  The only issue left for resolution in this briefing is the amount of the reasonable costs.  As the Court stated in its August 18, 2026 Minute Order, “Plaintiffs have established the material breach, as discussed above, and are entitled to an order for payment of their fees and costs as set forth in Sections 1281.97(b)(2) and 1281.99(a). The Court continues the hearing on the amount to be awarded under Section 1281.99(a) as set forth below. The arbitrator may determine the reasonable fees and costs to be awarded under Section 1281.97(b)(2).”  (Minute Order, 08/18/2026, p. 5 (emphasis added).)

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff Malcolm Bull is awarded attorney’s fees of $3,983.50 and costs of $1,030.25, jointly and severally against Defendants Car Search USA and Westlake Services, LLC.

Case Number: 26STLC02228    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Lucky Elephant Couture, LLC’s (1) Demurrer to First Amended Complaint; and (2) Motion to Strike Portions of First Amended Complaint, are placed off calendar.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Why this Action Should not be Reclassified as an Unlimited Civil Action and Transferred to the Reclassification/Transfer Desk for Collection of Fees and Reassignment is scheduled for 10/06/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

 

Analysis:

 

On March 30, 2026, Plaintiff Ali Abdulhadi (“Plaintiff”) filed this action for (1) Unruh Civil Rights Act Cal. Civ. Code § 51-53; (2) California Disabled Persons Act Cal. Civ. Code § 54.1; and (3) ADA 42 U.S.C. § 12181 et seq. The action is brought against Defendant Lucky Elephant Couture, LLC (“Defendant”). Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint alleging the same causes of action against Defendant on June 15, 2026. On August 17, 2026, Defendant filed the instant (1) Demurrer to First Amended Complaint; and (2) Motion to Strike Portions of First Amended Complaint. Plaintiff filed a joint opposition on September 8, 2026 and Defendant replied on September 15, 2026.

 

Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint prays for the following relief: (1) a declaratory judgment that Defendant was in violation of specific requirements of Title III of the ADA described above, and the relevant implementing regulations of the Unruh Civil Rights Act, California Disabled Persons Act, and ADA; (2) a preliminary and permanent injunction pursuant to section 52.1 of the California Civil Code on behalf of Plaintiff enjoining Defendant from violating the Unruh Civil Rights Act and ADA and requiring Defendant to take the steps necessary to make rontomson.com readily accessible to and usable by visually-impaired individuals; (3) an award of costs and expenses; (4) an award of statutory minimum damages of $4,000 per offense per person; and (4) attorneys’ fees and expenses.

 

The declaratory and permanent injunctive relief sought in the First Amended Complaint cannot be awarded in the Limited Civil Court. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 85, 86, 580(b)(2).) The Motion to Strike requested that the Court strike those requests for relief on this basis, but that is not the appropriate remedy when reclassification is available.  (See Mot. to Strike, p. 4:20-5:17; see also Demurrer, p. 4:2-4.)  The Court, therefore, places the hearing on the Demurrer and Motion to Strike off calendar and schedules an Order to Show Cause Re: Why this Action Should not be Reclassified as an Unlimited Civil Action and transferred to the Reclassification/Transfer Desk for Collection of Fees and Reassignment, for 10/06/2026 at 8:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 26STLC03135    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:  

           

The Court orders the parties to appear at the hearing on the Motion to discuss the discovery requests.   

 

 

Analysis:

 

On May 7, 2026, Plaintiff Captain Pallets, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendants Abbyson Living LLC (“Defendant Abbyson”) and Doddy Rafieha (“Defendant Rafieha”). The Complaint alleges causes of action for breach of contract and common counts against both Defendants. No proof of service of the Complaint and Summons was filed as to either Defendant.  Defendant Rafieha filed a Demurrer on June 25, 2026.  On July 27, 2026, the Court overruled Defendant Rafieha’s Demurrer to the Complaint, which argued that the causes of action against him were insufficient on account of inadequate alter ego allegations. (Minute Order, 07/27/26.) Defendant Rafieha filed an Answer to the Complaint on August 17, 2026.

 

On August 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Compel Further Responses and Production of Documents from Defendant Doddy Rafieha Regarding Plaintiff’s Requests for Production, Set One, Nos. 1-10; Request for Monetary Sanctions. Defendant Rafieha filed an opposition on September 9, 2026. To date, Plaintiff has not filed a proof of service of the Complaint and Summons as to Defendant Abbyson Living, LLC, nor has that defendant filed any response to the Complaint.

 

Discussion

 

Procedural Requirements

 

Notice of the motion to compel further must be given “within 45 days of service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or any specific later date to which the requesting party and the responding party have agreed in writing,” otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (c).) This time is extended based on the manner of service; service by electronic mail extends the deadline by two court days. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (a)(3)(B).) Defendant Rafieha served responses to the Request for Production of Documents, Set One, on August 7, 2026. (Motion, Sayer Decl., ¶3 and Exh. 2.) The instant Motion was timely filed and served 19 days later.

 

Second, the Motion must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1).) Plaintiff’s counsel sent an 18-page meet and confer letter to defense counsel on August 11, 2026, asking Defendant Rafieha to serve amended responses and produce documents by August 19, 2026, with respect to RFPs 1-10. (Motion, Sayer Decl., Exh. 3.) Defense counsel sent a two-page written response on August 19, 2026 briefly rejecting Plaintiff’s arguments, and stating in closing that defense counsel remained available for discussion. (Id. at Sayer Decl., Exh. 4.) The attorneys did not meet and confer further before Plaintiff filed the instant Motion one week later. This was not a sufficient meet-and-confer effort. “[T]he law requires that counsel attempt to talk the matter over, compare their views, consult, and deliberate.” (Townsend v. Superior Court (1998) 61 Cal.App.4th 1431, 1439; Clement v. Alegre (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1277, 1294.) Initiating a meet-and-confer in writing is fine, but a follow-up conversation must be held. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040(a).)  Motion practice after a single round of emails is not enough.

 

This concern is especially pertinent here because the primary basis for several of the objections to the RFPs at issue is the objection that the wrong document name was used: e.g., “bylaws”, “Articles of Incorporation”, and “stock certificates” and the request for Annual Statements of Information that did not identify the entity at issue.  (RFPs 1-2, 4-5.)  This kind of dispute should be remedied through meet-and-confer efforts.

 

The Court may nonetheless address the Motion to Compel.

 

Before entering an order on the Motion, the Court orders the parties to appear at the hearing on the Motion to discuss the discovery requests.
