DEPARTMENT A LAW AND MOTION RULINGS

INSTRUCTIONS: If the parties wish to submit on the tentative ruling and avoid a court appearance on the matter, the moving party must:

1. Contact the opposing party and all other parties who have appeared in the action and confirm that each will submit on the tentative ruling.

2. No later than 4:00 p.m. on the court day before the hearing, call the Courtroom (310-761-4302) advising that all parties will submit on the tentative ruling and waive hearing; and

3. Serve notice of the Court's ruling on all parties entitled to receive service.

If this procedure is followed, when the case is called the Court will enter its ruling on the motion in accordance with its tentative ruling. If any party declines to submit on the tentative ruling, then no telephone call is necessary, and all parties should appear at the hearing. If there is neither a telephone call nor an appearance, then the matter may either be taken off calendar or ruled on.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT MICHAEL PARK’S MOTION TO ENFORCE SETTLEMENT

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: May 14, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 09/02/2021

DISMISSAL ENTERED: 05/16/2022

 

MOVING PARTY:              Defendant Michael Park

RESPONDING PARTY:     None

1.   Background

On September 2, 2021, Plaintiffs Segero, Inc. and Kenneth Park (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) commenced this action against defendants Michael Park and Fashion Beauty Supply, Inc., alleging conversion of funds. On October 27, 2021, cross-complainant Michael Park (“Cross-Complainant”) filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiffs, alleging involuntary dissolution and breach of contract.

On May 16, 2022, pursuant to a signed stipulation, Judge Thomas D. Long dismissed this action without prejudice and retained jurisdiction to enforce the settlement pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6.

2.   Discussion

Cross-Complainant moves to vacate the dismissal entered on May 16, 2022; reinstate this action; and enter judgment against Plaintiffs. Cross-Complainant contends that Plaintiffs have breached the settlement agreement. No parties have opposed this motion.

The motion is heavily redacted, removing all information concerning the terms of the settlement agreement, the obligations that Cross-Complainant contends that Plaintiffs have breached, and an unknown amount of other information. Cross-Complainant has not moved to file the motion under seal pursuant to California Rules of Court, Rules 2.550 and 2.551, nor has Cross-Complainant even filed a notice of lodging of the unredacted papers with the court. Under Rule 2.551, “[a] record must not be filed under seal without a court order.”

The court will therefore deny Cross-Complainant’s motion to enforce the settlement agreement.

3.   Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court DENIES Cross-Complainant Michael Park’s motion to enforce the settlement agreement, without prejudice to the filing of a compliant motion.

Cross-Complainant is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT AIR DISTRIBUTION USA, INC’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH DEPOSITION SUBPOENA

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: June 25, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 08/30/2023

4th AMENDED COMPLAINT FILED: 04/20/2026

CROSS-COMPLAINT FILED: 09/19/2023

2nd AMENDED CROSS-COMPLAINT FILED: 04/08/2026

TRIAL: 11/09/2026

 

MOVING PARTY:              Cross-Complainant Air Distribution USA, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY:     Cross-Defendants Rose ML, Inc.; Sal Haddadin; Alfa Distribution, Inc.; and Corona Wholesale, Inc.

1.     Background

This is an action arising from an alleged breach of contract. The complaint alleges that defendants Air Distribution USA, Inc. and Robert Maneson (collectively, “Defendants”) entered into an agreement with plaintiff Rose ML, Inc. (“Rose”) not to sell certain products except to certain persons or under certain circumstances, and that Defendants breached this agreement.

Defendant Air Distribution USA, Inc. (“Air Distribution”) filed a cross-complaint against Rose and cross-defendants Sal Haddadin; Alfa Distribution, Inc. (“Alfa”); and Corona Wholesale, Inc. (“Corona”) (collectively, “Cross-Defendants”). In the cross-complaint, Air Distribution alleges that Alfa and Corona are alter egos of Haddadin. Air Distribution alleges that Rose and Haddadin failed to pay for $3,158,685.65 worth of tobacco products delivered to them by Air Distribution.

2.     Discussion

Air Distribution moves to compel third party Dhamir Hannona’s compliance with a deposition subpoena. Air Distribution contends that it noticed Hannona’s deposition for April 7, 2026 but that Hannona neither objected nor appeared for deposition. In opposition, Cross-Defendants contend that discovery is stayed in the instant action, including Hannona’s deposition.

Code of Civil Procedure §1987.1 provides that “[i]f a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court's own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare.”

After an anti-SLAPP motion has been filed in an action, discovery is automatically stayed as to that action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (g).) However, a cross-complaint is considered to be a separate action from the complaint. (Trinity Risk Management, LLC v. Simplified Labor Staffing Solutions, Inc. (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 995, 1004.) Here, Air Distribution’s notice of deposition, provided as Exhibit 2 to the declaration of attorney Kina Wong, indicates that the deposition was sought in connection with the allegations in the Second Amended Cross-Complaint (“SACC”)), as the deposition topics concern transfers of money or property between Cross-Defendants and Hannona. Attorney Wong declares that this issue arose because of discovery into Haddadin’s bank records, which is at issue in the SACC but not in the Fourth Amended Complaint. (Wong Decl. ¶¶ 2-3.)

Plaintiff contends that the court issued a stay on discovery on May 19, 2026.  However, the May 19, 2026 minute order on Motion to Deem Request for Admissions Admitted and on Ex Parte Application for order continuing hearing on pending discovery-related matters contains no such order imposing a blanket stay on discovery.  Instead, the hearing on the then-pending discovery motions were continued to datesafter the hearing on the Special Motion to Strike under CCP § 425.16.

As such, discovery on this issue is not automatically stayed by the filing of an anti-SLAPP motion as to the Fourth Amended Complaint on May 14, 2026.

On March 10, 2026, Air Distribution noticed Hannona’s deposition for April 7, 2026. (Wong Decl. ¶¶ 4-5.) Hannona did not object to this deposition. (Wong Decl. ¶ 6.) Hannona failed to appear for this deposition. (Wong Decl. ¶ 9.) The motion is reasonably made. Air Distribution is therefore entitled to an order compelling Hannona’s compliance with Air Distribution’s deposition subpoena, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure §1987.1, subd. (a).

Although Cross-Defendants opposed the instant motion, they did not oppose the motion on substantive grounds. As the court has determined that the motion is procedurally proper, the court will grant Air Distribution’s motion to compel Hannona’s compliance with Air Distribution’s deposition subpoena.

3.     Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court GRANTS Cross-Complainant Air Distribution USA, Inc.’s motion to compel third party Dhamir Hannona’s compliance with Air Distribution’s deposition subpoena.

The deposition shall take place on July ___, 2026, at the time and location specified in the notice of deposition, unless the parties and deponent mutually agree upon a different date and time on or before July 23, 2026.

Air Distribution is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

 

DATED: June 25, 2026

   

Hon. Elizabeth L. Bradley

Judge of the Superior Court

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT THINK TOGETHER, INC.’S MOTION TO QUASH SERVICE

 

Dept. A

 

DATE: June 25, 2026

TIME: 8:30 A.M.

 

COMPLAINT FILED: 04/25/2024

TRIAL: 08/31/2026

 

MOVING PARTY:              Defendant Think Together, Inc.

RESPONDING PARTY:     None

1.   Background

Plaintiff J.L. (“J.L.”) alleges that at all relevant times, she was a student at Enterprise Middle School, which was operated and controlled by defendant Compton Unified School District (“District”). J.L. alleges that on May 10, 2023, she was sexually, physically, and verbally assaulted by another student while on campus grounds at Enterprise Middle School. J.L. alleges that other students had reported assaults by the perpetrator prior to the incident, but that District concealed evidence relating to prior assaults and failed to adequately protect and supervise J.L.

District filed a cross-complaint against cross-defendant Think Together, Inc. (“Think Together”) for express contractual indemnity, equitable indemnity, and apportionment of fault.

On April 30, 2026, J.L. amended the First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) to substitute Think Together as a Doe defendant.

2.   Discussion

Think Together moves to quash J.L.’s service of the summons and complaint on the grounds that there has been no service of the summons and complaint and that Think Together cannot be substituted as a Doe defendant because J.L. was previously aware of Think Together’s identity. J.L. does not oppose this motion.

Under Code of Civil Procedure § 418.10, a defendant may move to quash service of summons “on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow” on the ground of lack of jurisdiction over them. Improper service of process prevents a court from exercising jurisdiction over a defendant. (Stancil v. Superior Court (2021) 11 Cal.5th 381, 392-393.) A defendant has 30 days after the service of the summons to file a responsive pleading.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 412.20, subd. (a)(3).)

There has been no proof of service of the summons and First Amended Complaint filed upon Think Together. Think Together claims in its motion that it was never served with the summons and First Amended Complaint.  Therefore, there is, as of this time, nothing to quash. The instant motion is premature. The court will therefore deny Think Together’s motion to quash service of the summons and complaint.

 

3.   Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, the court DENIES Think Together’s motion to quash service of the summons and complaint, without prejudice.

Think Together is ordered to give notice.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

  

DATED: June 25, 2026

   

Hon. Elizabeth L. Bradley

Judge of the Superior Court
