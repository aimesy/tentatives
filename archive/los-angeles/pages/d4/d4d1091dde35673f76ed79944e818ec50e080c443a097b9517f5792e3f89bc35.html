DEPARTMENT H LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24PSCV03167    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: H

Sukarieh v. City of Glendora, Case No. 24PSCV03167

 

ORDER ON MOTION FOR DETERMINATION OF GOOD FAITH SETTLEMENT

 

Plaintiff Dana Joseph Sukarieh’s Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement is GRANTED.

 

Background

Plaintiff Dana Joseph Sukarieh (“Plaintiff”) alleges that on February 24, 2024 she sustained injuries after colliding her vehicle, which had been traveling eastbound on W. Gladstone St. towards the intersection of W. Gladstone St. and S. Nearglen Ave., with an unmarked curb extension.

On February 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed two “Amendment[s] to Complaint,” wherein Alfaro Communications Construction Inc. (“Alfaro”) was named in lieu of Doe 1 and JMDiaz, Inc. (“JMDiaz”) was named in lieu of Doe 2.

On February 18, 2026, Plaintiff filed another “Amendment to Complaint,” wherein Ivan Salvatierra (“Salvatierra”) was named in lieu of Doe 3.

On February 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), asserting causes of action against City of Glendora (“City”), JMDiaz, Alfaro, Salvatierra and Does 4-20 for: (1) Inverse Condemnation, (2) Dangerous Condition of Public Property, (3) Failure to Discharge Duty Imposed by Mandatory Enactment, (4) Public Nuisance, (5) Professional Negligence, (6) Negligence, and (7) Negligence Per Se.

A Case Management Conference is set for August 19, 2026.


Legal Standard

“Any party to an action in which it is alleged that two or more parties are joint tortfeasors or co-obligors on a contract debt shall be entitled to a hearing on the issue of the good faith of a settlement entered into by the plaintiff or other claimant and one or more alleged tortfeasors or co-obligators, upon giving notice in the manner provided in subdivision (b) of Section 1005 . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (a)(1).)

“A determination by the court that the settlement was made in good faith shall bar any other joint tortfeasor or co-obligor from any further claims against the settling tortfeasor or co-obligor for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (c).)

“[T]he intent and policies underlying section 877.6 require that a number of factors be taken into account including a rough approximation of plaintiffs’ total recovery and the settlor’s proportionate liability, the amount paid in settlement, the allocation of settlement proceeds among plaintiffs, and a recognition that a settlor should pay less in settlement than he would if he were found liable after a trial.

Other relevant considerations include the financial conditions and insurance policy limits of settling defendants, as well as the existence of collusion, fraud, or tortious conduct aimed to injure the interests of nonsettling defendants. Finally, practical considerations obviously require that the evaluation be made on the basis of information available at the time of settlement.” (Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499 [emphasis added].)

Additionally, “the trial court’s good faith determination must take into account the settling tortfeasor’s potential liability for indemnity to a cotortfeasor, as well as the settling tortfeasor’s potential liability to the plaintiff.” (Far West Financial Corp. v. D&S Co. (1988) 46 Cal.3d 796, 816, fn. 16.) “If section 877.6 is to serve the ends of justice, it must prevent a party from purchasing protection from its indemnification obligation at bargain-basement prices.” (Long Beach Memorial Medical Center v. Superior Court (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 865, 876.)

The moving party’s initial evidentiary burden depends on whether the good faith of the settlement is being contested. If the nonsettling defendants do not oppose the motion on the good faith issue, a “barebones” motion which sets forth the grounds of good faith, accompanied by a declaration which sets forth a brief background of the case, is sufficient. (City of Grand Terrace v. Superior Court (1987) 192 Cal.App.3d 1251, 1261.)

When a motion for determination of good faith settlement is contested, however, the moving party must make a more specific showing under the Tech-Bilt factors. (Id. at 1261-62.) Such a showing may be made either in the original moving papers or in counter-declarations filed after the nonsettling defendants have filed an opposition challenging good faith of the settlement. (Id. at 1262.) Where good faith is contested, the showing requires competent evidence in support of “good faith.” (Greshko v. County of Los Angeles (1987) 194 Cal.App.3d 822, 834.)

“Once there is a showing made by the settlor of the settlement, the burden of proof on the issue of good faith shifts to the non-settlor who asserts that the settlement was not made in good faith.” (City of Grand Terrace, supra, 192 Cal.App.3d at 1262; Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6, subd. (d).)

 

Discussion

Plaintiff moves the Court, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 877 and 877.6, for an order determining that her settlement reached with City was entered into in good faith and barring any present or future claims against the City for equitable comparative contribution, partial or comparative indemnity, or implied non-contractual indemnity by any co-defendant, joint tortfeasor, or other person or entity arising out of the subject incident, and dismissing any such claims with prejudice.

As an initial matter, this instant motion was originally set for hearing on June 24, 2026; the day before, the Court reset the hearing date to July 17, 2026, on the following basis:

“The Court observes that the opposition was not filed until June 18, 2026, despite it being due by June 10, 2026. Moreover, a proof of service was not filed until June 18, 2026, despite one being due by June 16, 2026; as such, when the Court initially reviewed the motion, it believed the motion would be taken off calendar due to the lack of a proof of service.

Under the circumstances, the Court will continue the hearing to July 17, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. so that it is able to fully review the belatedly filed opposition and the reply brief . . .”

Notice of the continuance was provided by the clerk.

Next, the Court addresses Alfaro’s claim, raised in its opposition, that Plaintiff never served it with the motion. The Court’s June 23, 2026 minute order references that Plaintiff filed a proof of service on June 18, 2026. This proof of service reflects that Alfaro was electronically served with the motion on May 29, 2026. Additionally, Bachour has attached a copy of the Green Filing electronic service records for the May 29, 2026 as Exhibit A to his reply declaration. Regardless, Alfaro has filed a substantive opposition, which the Court is considering. “[T]he appearance of a party at the hearing of a motion and his or her opposition to the motion on its merits is a waiver of any defects or irregularities in the notice of the motion. This rule applies even when no notice was given at all.” (Tate v. Superior Court (1975) 45 Cal.App.3d 925, 930 [internal citations omitted].) Accordingly, the Court will proceed to address the merits of the motion at this juncture, as follows.

Plaintiff and City have agreed to resolve Plaintiff’s claims for $150,000.00 (i.e., $40,000.00 economic/$110,000.00 non-economic), in exchange for a release and dismissal with prejudice as to City. (Bachour Decl., ¶ 20, Exh. A).

Plaintiff’s counsel Rami Bachour (“Bachour”) represents, and/or the accompanying exhibits reflect, as follows:

            This action arises from a roadway improvement project (“Project”) commissioned by City at the intersection of W. Gladstone Street and S. Nearglen Avenue (“Intersection”). (Bachour Decl., ¶ 3.) City began the project in late 2023. (Id.) City retained JMDiaz as its engineering consultant for Project. (Id., ¶ 4). Salvatierra, a licensed civil engineer employed by JMDiaz, prepared and signed the design plans for Project, including the taper calculations governing the lane narrowing at issue in this action. (Id.) Alfaro was the construction contractor for Project and contractually responsible for designing and implementing the temporary traffic control plan during construction, including the signage, barricades, delineators, and warning measures intended to protect motorists until the permanent roadway features were complete. (Id., ¶ 5.) City, as owner of the roadway, held final approval over the design and authorized construction. (Id., ¶ 3.)

            On February 24, 2024, Plaintiff was driving eastbound on W. Gladstone Street while Project was still under construction. (Id., ¶ 6.) W. Gladstone Street had a posted speed limit of 45 miles per hour. (Id.) As Plaintiff approached Intersection, her travel lane abruptly narrowed from approximately fourteen feet to less than seven and one-half feet because of a newly constructed curb extension, which had not been painted, marked or signed. (Id.) Plaintiff struck the curb extension after being unable to merge into the adjacent lane because of another vehicle and her vehicle was declared a total loss. (Id.)

Plaintiff’s past medical specials and out-of-pocket special damages exceed $100,000.00. (Id., ¶ 9.) Plaintiff sustained the following injuries/damages as a result of the incident: (a) thyroid storm requiring multiple surgical interventions; (b) a vocal cord injury sustained when her vocal cord was nicked during a thyroid surgery, which has required corrective vocal cord filler injections and additional vocal cord surgery; (c) cervical disc protrusions at C3-C4, C5-C6, and C6-C7; (d) lumbar disc protrusions at L4-L5 and L5-S1, with the L5-S1 protrusion measuring 5.7 mm and presenting an associated annular fissure, mild left neural foraminal narrowing, and abutment on the left transiting nerve root, treated with epidural injections; and (e) property damage to her vehicle. (Id., ¶ 8.) Plaintiff’s future medical needs, which include lifelong thyroid hormone replacement therapy, continuing vocal cord treatment (including additional surgery and filler injections), continued epidural injections, and long-term pain management for her cervical and lumbar disc injuries are projected to be approximately $200,000.00 or more. (Id., ¶ 9.)

Bachour conducted the depositions of City’s Persons Most Knowledgeable (“PMK”) concerning project management, design, approval, and construction, including Maliha Ansari (“Ansari”), City’s Principal Engineer, and Steven Mateer (“Mateer”), City’s Transportation Manager. (Id., ¶ 10.) Salvatierra was also deposed over two sessions. (Id.) Salvatierra testified that the seventy-five-foot taper was calculated using a formula intended for temporary traffic control zones rather than for permanent roadway features. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 11, Exh. B, 168:6-11.) Applying the formula from the CA MUTCD applicable to permanent approach markings, Salvatierra testified that the taper for the subject roadway should have been 270 feet. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 11, Exh. B, 39:13-21.) Salvatierra testified that the contractor is responsible for the traffic control during construction and identified that contractor as Alfaro. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 11, Exh. B, 30:18-31:6.) Mateer also testified that the contractor prepares the traffic control plan and implements it. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 12, Exh. D, 87:13-88:7.) Ansari testified that she signed the plans and specifications for the Project, and confirmed that she approved the plans by electronic signature. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 13, Exh. C, 109:20-110:2.) Ansari testified that City does not independently verify taper-length calculations, explaining that the City has no city standard for taper length and instead relies on the Caltrans manual and on consultant expertise. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 13, Exh. C, 53:11-24.) Mateer explained that the curb extension and refuge island were selected because they were “the low hanging fruit” that “could [be] implement[ed] quickly to improve safety.” (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 12, Exh. D, 29:5-14.) He testified that City hires an engineering firm to design the improvement so that it “provides correct lane width” and “correct notification to drivers of any change” (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 12, Exh. D, 79:20-81:4.) Salvatierra confirmed that, as far as he could recollect, there was no instance where City directed JMDiaz to design anything that violated a standard. (Id., ¶¶ 10 and 11, Exh. B, 60:8-12.)

Bachour has provided the Court with copies of City’s written contracts with JMDiaz and Alfaro and represents that there is no agreement under which City indemnifies JMDiaz, Salvatierra, or Alfaro. (Id., ¶ 22, Exhs. E and F.) Bachour has reviewed Plaintiff’s cell phone records for the date and approximate time of the collision and represents that they reflect that no calls or texts were placed or received in the period immediately surrounding the time of the incident. (Id., ¶ 23.) He represents that Plaintiff testified that she was traveling at or below the posted speed limit at the time of the accident. (Id.)

Bachour confirms that the settlement was reached following arms-length negotiations. (Id., ¶ 20.) City’s counsel represented to him that City is insured through the Independent Cities Risk Management Authority (“ICRMA”), which provides general liability coverage and excess coverage with reinsurance, subject to a self-insured retention of $250,000.00. (Id.) 

The Court determines that Plaintiff has made an adequate showing of the settlement [FN]; as such, the burden of proof on the issue of lack of good faith shifted to Alfaro. Alfaro, in turn, has failed to provide the Court with any declarations, expert opinion, competing fault allocation, damages analysis, and/or evidence of collusion.

Alfaro complains that it became a party to this action in February 2026, after City “had completed the discovery on which this motion now relies” (Opp., 2:2-3) and that it “has had no meaningful opportunity to participate in that discovery, evaluate the City’s proportional liability, or test the assumptions underlying the settlement” (Opp., 2:3-5). Bachour, however, represents that on May 28, 2026 (weeks before Alfaro filed its June 18, 2026 opposition) his office served Alfaro’s counsel (along with all other counsel of record) with Plaintiff’s discovery responses in this action, including her responses to JMDiaz’s Requests for Admissions, Form and Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production of Documents, Sets No. One, and provided counsel access to a shared electronic folder containing Plaintiff’s document productions and the materials exchanged in this action, totaling approximately 873 megabytes. (Bachour Reply Decl., ¶¶ 7 and 8, Exhs. C and D.) 

Alfaro requests that if the Court is not inclined to deny the motion, that it continue the hearing for 90 days to permit supplemental briefing “once it has been properly served and afforded a meaningful opportunity to take discovery.” (Opp., 2:10-12.) The Court denies Alfaro’s request for a continuance. Again, Alfaro appears to have been properly served with the motion. Next, Alfaro has not propounded any interrogatory, request for production, or request for admission on Plaintiff or on City, and has not noticed any deposition in this action as of the reply filing date. (Bachour Reply Decl., ¶ 10.) At any rate, the discovery sought by Alfaro would not change the analysis, inasmuch as a good faith determination requires a “rough approximation” of plaintiff’s total recovery and City’s proportionate share on the basis of information available at the time of settlement. (Tech-Bilt, supra, 38 Cal.3d at 499.) 

The motion is granted.

 

  

[FN] Alfaro makes much of the fact that this instant motion was brought by Plaintiff, rather than by City. Alfaro’s position that Plaintiff somehow lacks standing is belied by the language of section 877.6, which expressly permits “[a]ny party” to file a motion for good faith settlement determination.


Case Number: 24PSCV04231    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: H

loanDepot.com, LLC v. Grymonprez, et al., Case No. 24PSCV04231

 

ORDER ON MOTION TO VACATE DEFAULT AND DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

Defendant Adam Grymonprez’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is DENIED.

 

Background 

Plaintiff loanDepot.com, LLC (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows: Plaintiff is the current noteholder and beneficiary of the deed of trust recorded on September 23, 2014 against the real property located at 232 W. 2nd Street in San Dimas (“subject property”); Tracy Grymonprez (“Tracy”), Adam Grymonprez (“Adam”) and Sandra K. Pratt (“Sandra”) are the current record owners. Capital One Bank (USA), National Association (“Capital One”), Discover Bank (“Discover”), Department of Housing and Urban Development (“HUD”) and Unifund CCR, LLC (“Unifund”) may claim interests in the subject property by virtue of subordinate abstract judgments and deed of trust recorded against the subject property. Additionally, Los Angeles County Tax Collector (“L.A. Tax Collector”) and Sunrun Inc. (“Sunrun”) may claim interests in the subject property by virtue of a certificate of liens and a Notice of an Independent Solar Energy Contract, respectively, recorded against the subject property. Tracy, Adam and Sandra failed to pay the monthly installment on the note due on December 1, 2023 and have failed to pay all subsequent monthly installments. 

On December 11, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting a cause of action for Judicial Foreclosure against Tracy, Adam, Sandra, Capital One, Discover, HUD, Unifund, L.A. Tax Collector, Sunrun and Does 1-10. 

On February 24, 2025, Unifund’s, L.A. Tax Collector’s, Capital One’s, Sunrun’s and Discover’s defaults were entered. 

On August 11, 2025, Plaintiff filed an “Amendment to Complaint,” wherein Randy Argo was named in lieu of Doe 1; that day, Adam’s default was entered. 

On November 4, 2025, Tracy’s default was entered. 

A Post-Mediation Status Conference/Further Case Management Conference is set for July 17, 2026.

 

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) 

“The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken. . . Notwithstanding any other requirements of this section, the court shall, whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against his or her client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) 

“The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5

“When service of a summons has not resulted in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action and a default or default judgment has been entered against the party in the action, the party may serve and file a notice of motion to set aside the default or default judgment and for leave to defend the action. The notice of motion shall be served and filed within a reasonable time, but in no event exceeding the earlier of: (i) two years after entry of a default judgment against the party; or (ii) 180 days after service on the party of a written notice that the default or default judgment has been entered.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (a).) 

“A notice of motion to set aside a default or default judgment and for leave to defend the action . . . shall be accompanied by an affidavit showing under oath that the party’s lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party’s avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect. The party shall serve and file with the notice a copy of the answer, motion, or other pleading proposed to be filed in the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (b).) 

“Upon a finding by the court that the motion was made within the period permitted by subdivision (a) and that the party’s lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party’s avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect, it may set aside the default or default judgment on whatever terms as may be just and allow the party to defend the action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (c).)   


Discussion

Adam moves the Court, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 473.5 and 473, subdivisions (b) and (d) and the Court’s inherent equitable powers, for an order vacating the entry of his default on August 11, 2025 and any default judgment.

At the outset, the Court notes that the motion originally came on calendar for hearing on June 2, 2026 and was continued to the instant date due to a notice issue. The notice issue has since been corrected, as evidenced by the proof of service filed by Adam on July 13, 2026. 

Adams asserts that, while “Plaintiff claims substituted service. . . [he] never received the summons and complaint.” (Motion, 3:4-5; see also, 3:15-17.) Adams further asserts that “Plaintiff’s substituted service was defective because: Plaintiff failed to exercise reasonable diligence before resorting to substituted service.” (Id., 4:25-26.) Although Adams has not attached the subject proof of service, the docket reflects that on January 27, 2025, the summons and complaint were substitute served on a “Gary Srymonprez – Co-Resident” on January 23, 2025 at 11:30 a.m. at 1147 W Tudor Street in San Dimas (“Tudor Street Address”). The subject proof of service is accompanied by a “Declaration of Diligence” listing six separate service attempts on four different dates at two different addresses (i.e., with the first three attempts made at 234 W 2nd St. in San Dimas and the latter three attempts made at the Tudor Street Address.

Adams’ declaration, however, is devoid of any information regarding the purported insufficiency of service; he states only that he was not personally served with the summons and complaint and that he did not learn of the “judgment” [FN] against him until his divorce attorney found it online during the first week of September 2025. (Adam Decl., 7:8-10.) 

Adam next asserts that he did not receive actual notice of this action in time to defend, again “due to defective substitute service.” Adam’s declaration, however, does not “show[ ] under oath that the party’s lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party’s avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect,” as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, subdivision (b).

Adam asserts that the default was entered as a result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. First, the motion is untimely to the extent it is based on this ground. The record reflects that Adam’s default was entered on August 11, 2025 and that the motion was filed on February 10, 2026. The record further reflects that the motion was not served on Plaintiff’s counsel until June 16, 2026, over ten months after default was entered. An application for discretionary relief must be made “within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b.) “[A]n application for relief under [section 473, subdivision (b)] is deemed to be made upon filing in court of a notice of motion and service of the notice of motion on the adverse party.” (Arambula v. Union Carbide Corp. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 333, 341 [citation omitted; emphasis theirs].) At any rate, Adam’s declaration is devoid of any facts supporting discretionary relief.

Finally, Adam asserts that “equity requires relief where a party is deprived of a fair adversarial hearing due to extrinsic mistake.” (Motion, 6:4-5.) Adam’s declaration is devoid of any facts supporting extrinsic mistake. 

The motion is denied.

 


[FN] The Court observes that default has been entered against Adam; there is no default judgment as of this date.

Case Number: 25PSCV03636    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: H

ODK Capital, LLC v. Teach Hair Inc., et al., Case No. 25PSCV03636

 

ORDER CONTINUING HEARING ON MOTION TO SET ASIDE ENTRY OF DEFAULT AND ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE RE: DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

The hearing on Defendant Crystal Perez’s Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default and the Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment is CONTINUED to August 21, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. [see below].

 

Background 

Plaintiff ODK Capital, LLC (“Plaintiff”) alleges as follows:

On June 24, 2024, Teach Hair Inc. (“THI”) entered into a Business Loan and Security Agreement and accompanying supplement (“BLSA”) in favor of Celtic Bank (“Celtic”), wherein Celtic agreed to loan monies to THI in exchange for repayment. Crystal Perez (“Perez”) contemporaneously executed a personal guaranty. Celtic subsequently assigned the BLSA to Plaintiff. TSI has failed to make payments due. 

On October 8, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint, asserting causes of action against THI and Perez for: (1) Breach of Contract and (2) Breach of Guaranty. 

On December 10, 2025, THI’s and Perez’s defaults were entered. 

An Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment is set for July 17, 2026.

 

Discussion

THI and Perez move the Court for an order, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473.5, setting aside their respective defaults. 

The Court observes that on January 30, 2026, Perez filed a four-page document entitled “Notice of Motion and Motion to Set Aside Enty of Default” (“Notice”); no hearing date was listed (although the attached reservations page identified a June 2, 2026 reserved hearing), nor was the Notice accompanied by a proof of service. On May 19, 2026, Plaintiff filed a “Response to Defendant Crystal Perez’s Notice of and Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default” (“Response”), representing therein that “Defendant’s Notice of and Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default (‘Defendant’s Notice’) is procedurally noncompliant and unsupported, and it consists only of a notice with no related filings despite making representations that it is based on a memorandum, declaration, and exhibits. The Court docket shows no such filings, nor have any been provided to Plaintiff.” (Resp., 1:25-2:4.) 

The Court’s record reflects that on May 28, 2026, Perez rescheduled the hearing on this instant motion to September 30, 2026; it does not appear that Perez ever provided notice of same. During the June 2, 2026 Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment (which Perez did not attend), the Court observed as follows:

“Defendant Crystal Perez’s motion to set aside default was initially calendared for today’s date, but Defendant Perez moved the hearing to 9/30/26. The Court sees no reason to delay the motion to September[.] On the Court’s own motion, the Hearing on Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Default (CCP 473.5) scheduled for 09/30/2026 is advanced to this date and continued to 07/17/2026 at 09:00 AM in Department H at Pomona Courthouse South.

The Court confirms, consistent with Plaintiff’s opposition, that the only filing reflected on the docket in connection with that motion is a four-page document, ‘Notice of Motion and Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default.’ The Court did receive a courtesy copy of a two-page memorandum of points and authorities, a 57 page declaration of Crystal Perez, and a one-page proposed order. However, there is no indication those documents were filed or served on Plaintiff.”

The Court, on that date, directed Perez “to file and serve those documents forthwith and by no later than June 9, 2026,” directed that opposition and reply papers be filed in compliance with Code of Civil Procedure section 1005, subdivision (b) and directed Plaintiff to give notice. 

The Court observes that Perez has not filed any of the aforementioned documents referenced in its June 2, 2026 minute order, to date; that said, the Court also notes that Plaintiff filed a proof of service on July 1, 2026, reflecting that Perez had been mail-served on June 30, 2026 from Atlanta with a “Minute Order/Notice of Hearing June 17, 2026 at 8:30 am.” [FN] Plaintiff’s notice to Perez is insufficient under the circumstances, inasmuch as it circumvented Perez’s opportunity to provide compliant documents “by no later than June 9, 2026.” The Court admonishes Plaintiff’s counsel in this regard. 

The Court will continue the hearing, to August 21, 2026, at 9:30 a.m. Perez is directed to file and serve the two-page memorandum of points and authorities, the fifty-seven page declaration of Crystal Perez, and one page proposed order identified in the Court’s June 2, 2026 minute order forthwith and by no later than July 24, 2026. Again, any opposition and reply papers are to be filed per Code. 

The Court will continue the Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment scheduled for July 31, 2026 to this same date. 

Plaintiff is to provide notice to Perez no later than five calendar days from the date of this hearing and to file proof of service of same forthwith. 


[FN] The hearing was on June 2, 2026, not June 17, 2026.
