The court awards attorney fees in the reduced, total amount of $5,042 which is payable within 10 days of this hearing.

 

Background

 

This lawsuit arises from a multi-vehicle automobile collision that occurred in January 2024.

 

On November 13, 2024, Plaintiffs ALICIA MARTINEZ and LAYLA MARTINEZ filed suit against Defendants WEST COAST TRANSPORTATION; UNITED PLUMBING INC. (“United Plumbing”); JOSE RAMIREZ LOPEZ; KEVIN FRANCIS MOORE; JOSE BELTRAN PONCE; and NATHALIE GUERRA for:

 

1.     Negligence

2.     Negligent Entrustment

3.     Negligent Hiring, Training, Retention, and Supervision

 

On January 22, 2025, United Plumbing and Moore filed their answer along with a cross-complaint (CC) for Indemnity/Contribution against WEST COAST TRANSPORTATION and JOSE RAMIREZ LOPEZ.

 

On February 1, 2025, Guerra filed her answer along with a CC for comparative indemnity, contribution, and declaratory relief against WEST COAST TRANSPORTATION, UNITED PLUMBING INC., JOSE RAMIREZ LOPEZ, KEVIN FRANCIS MOORE.

 

On February 19, 2025, UNITED PLUMBING, INC. and KEVIN FRANCIS MOORE filed their answer to Guerra’s CC.

 

On February 21, 2025, West Coast Transportation filed its answer to the complaint.

 

On April 18, 2025, (another) answer by UNITED PLUMBING, INC. and KEVIN FRANCIS MOORE was filed to Guerra’s CC.

 

On May 9, 2025, West Coast Transportation and Lopez filed their answer to Guerra’s CC.

 

On June 20, 2025, Lopez filed his answer to the complaint.

 

On June 25, 2025, West Coast Transportation and Lopez filed a CC.

 

On November 4, 2025, Ponce filed his answer to the complaint.

 

On March 12, 2026, an IDC was held.

 

On April 13, 2026, the parties attended a CMC. According to the minute order (MO), “Counsel represent that the parties intend to file a stipulation to consolidate a related case.” (No notice of related case/stipulation has been filed.)

 

On April 17, 2026, the instant motions were filed.

 

On July 7 and 8 of 2026 two oppositions were filed by MARTHA LARREGUI dba WEST COAST TRANSPORTATION to the SROGs and FROGs motions.

 

On July 10, 2026, two replies were filed.

 

Discussion

 

The court’s ruling will be brief as the motions are not motions to compel further discovery but for orders compelling initial responses. (While duplicates, the motions are directed at West Coast Transportation and Lopez.)

 

As set forth in the motions, Plaintiff duly served Defendants with the subject discovery on March 13, 2025 and July 12, 2025. As to all, Defendants requested extensions such as a request for extension made on June 11, 2025 (as to the March 13, 2025 discovery) and July 18, 2025 (as to the June 12, 2025 discovery). Whether the reason being an extension because of a mis-calendaring error or that Defense counsel advised that she had no record of the discovery in her system or emails and requested that Plaintiff re-send all discovery (re West Coast Transportation), Plaintiff gave the extensions and promptly re-served the March 13, 2025 discovery that same day.  But still no responses were served. On January 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Request for an IDC. It was not until February 25, 2026, in connection with the IDC process that Defense counsel first raised the issue

of being unable to locate and contact Defendants Martha Larregui (West Coast Transportation) and Jose Ramirez Lopez, attributing the failure to respond to difficulty reaching their clients.

 

In opposition, Defendant does not dispute the foregoing. Instead, Defendant argues they have exhibited “continuing and diligent efforts” to contact the insureds and that “Defense counsel is prepared to serve verified responses promptly upon reestablishing

contact and will comply with any reasonable deadline the Court sets.” (See e.g., FROGs Opp. p. 3:3-7.) Based thereon, Defendant argus that sanctions are unwarranted against counsel because their “active[] participation” in this case “are not the hallmarks of a party or attorney misusing the discovery process, and they make the imposition of sanctions unjust.” (Opp. p. 4:2-5.)

 

The court strongly disagrees. As even stated in Defense Counsel Lopez’s declaration, their firm began to locate and communicate with the insured defendants beginning “in or about February 2025.”[1] Yet, to date, as of July 2026, no motions to be relieved as counsel have been filed. For emphasis, despite over one year of not being able to contact the insured defendants, Defense Counsel contends that they are not “delay[ing]” the process. But continuing to represent a client who clearly has no interest in defending or participating in a case is delaying the process of litigating a case. It is inarguable then that Defendant has acted with substantial justification to prevent the imposition of sanctions.

 

With that, the court grants all the motions in their entirety. As for attorney fees, Plaintiffs’ counsel seeks one hour (1) of time expended in the research, preparation and drafting of the SROGs motions and 1.5 hours for the other motions and for each anticipates spending one hour (1) hour of time at the reasonable rate of $350.00 per hour in the preparation and presentation of this motion at the hearing, and a $61.65 filing fee/motion. The only reduction the court makes is the for one hour requested/motion in arguing. Since a consolidated argument will likely/need be presented, the court awards two hours total in preparing/presenting the motions. With that, the court awards 11 hours drafting each motion + 2 hours preparing for the hearing/argument for a total of 13 hours x $350/hour = $4,550 + $61.50 filing fee/motion for a TOTAL award of $5,042 (reduced from the requested total of $7,143.20).

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the motions are granted.

Case Number: 25PSCV03747    Hearing Date: August 4, 2026    Dept: O

Tentative Ruling

 

DEFENDANTS FERHAT HANACI’S AND FERHAT CONSTRUCTION INC.’S NOTICE OF MOTION TO STRIKE PORTIONS OF COMPLAINT AND DEMURRER AND MOTION TO STRIKE is SUSTAINED with 30 days leave to amend; the MTS is moot in part (re: punitive damages)/denied in part (re: attorney fees and jury trial).

 

Background

 

This case arises from a commercial lease agreement. Plaintiffs SOPHIA L. ARMENTA, DINA NIMRI, and MUNA HAWATMEH allege the following against Defendants FERHAT HANACI (“Hanaci”), JOHN L. FAIRE (“Faire”), COLDWELL BANKER RESIDENTIAL BROKERAGE COMPANY (“Coldwell”), FERHAT CONSTRUCTION, INC. (the “Company”): On or about April 7, 2025, Plaintiffs Sophia L. Armenta and Dina Nimri (Contracting Plaintiffs) entered into a Commercial Lease Agreement with Defendant Hanaci for a property that would be used as a "Juice Bar & related services,” and it was represented as such by Faire. (¶¶4, 5.) After signing the lease (which included three months of free rent), Contracting Plaintiffs discovered that the Property was not suitable for a juice bar without significant modifications and did not have proper health department approvals. (¶12.) Then in June 2025, at a meeting at Hanaci’s residence, Hanaci “forcefully grabbed Hawatmeh by her arm with his hand and would not let her go while he screamed at her that she was not a woman.” (¶¶17-19.) Contracting Plaintiffs allege that they have suffered damages including the loss of their security deposit, the $3,500 paid for construction plans, rent payments made during the lease period, lost business opportunity and profits, and costs associated with moving and finding an alternative business location; Hawatmeh has suffered emotional distress as have the others by “witnessing” the assault/battery on Hawatmeh. (¶¶24-26.)

 

On October 16, 2025, Plaintiffs filed suit asserting the following causes of action (COAs):

 

1. FRAUD IN THE INDUCEMENT IN VIOLATION OF CAL CIV CODE § 1572;

2. NEGLIGENT MISREPRESENTATION IN VIOLATION OF CAL CIV CODE § 1710;

3. NEGLIGENCE IN VIOLATION OF CAL CIV CODE § 1714;

4. NEGLIGENT SUPERVISION OF REAL ESTATE SALESPERSON IN VIOLATION OF CAL CIV CODE § 2338;

5. ASSAULT IN VIOLATION OF CAL PEN CODE § 240;

6. BATTERY IN VIOLATION OF CAL CIV CODE § 1708;

7. BREACH OF CONTRACT;

8. UNFAIR BUSINESS PRACTICES IN VIOLATION OF CAL BUS & PROF CODE § 17200; and

9. INTENTIONAL INFLICTION OF EMOTIONAL DISTRESS (IIED)

 

On November 17, 2026, the court denied Plaintiff’s notice of related case seeking to relate the small claims case filed by Hanaci against Plaintiffs (25WCSC01390).

 

On January 13, 2026, Defendants Coldwell and Faire filed a ‘DECLARATION OF DEMURRING OR MOVING PARTY IN SUPPORT OF AUTOMATIC EXTENSION’ indicating that “[s]ince December 16, 2025” Counsel has been attempting to meet and confer with Plaintiffs’ counsel.

 

On April 23, 2026, the court sustained the demurrer and granted the motion to strike filed by COLDWELL BANKER, RESIDENTIAL BROKERAGE COMPANY AND JOHN L. FAIRE, both without leave to amend.

 

On June 1, 2026, a notice of appeal was filed.

 

On June 17, 2026, the instant demurrer was filed with a motion strike.

 

On July 21, 2026, an opposition was filed.

 

On July 24, 2026, a reply was filed.

 

Discussion

 

Moving Defendants demur to the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 7th, and 8th COAs on various grounds (e.g., the Release and other Lease provisions do not support the allegations by Contracting Plaintiffs that Moving Parties breached the Lease). For purposes of this demurrer and MTS, the court will focus on the issue that more readily resolves the dispute: whether the complaint fails for uncertainty. And it does. As argued by moving Defendants and not otherwise addressed in opposition, the complaint lumps allegations against the various defendants making no distinctions or elaborations as to what any defendant said. (See e.g., Demurrer p. 8 [“There are four defendants named in the Complaint and Plaintiffs fail to specifically identify the specific “who, what, when, where, how” required for a fraud allegation per Robinson Helicopter. Plaintiffs lump Moving Parties into the other defendants and think that such allegation is enough. It is not and this Court should sustain this Demurrer.”].)

 

As for leave to amend, the opposition merely recites elements and allegations such that Plaintiffs do not meet their burden to demonstrate how the complaint can be properly amended. Plus, as noted in the demurrer and not otherwise addressed in opposition, it appears Plaintiff’s counsel has (again) refused to meet and confer. The court emphasizes that Plaintiffs’ counsel must meaningfully meet and confer with Defense counsel should moving Defendants file a demurrer to an amended complaint.

 

As for the motion to strike, Defendants seek to strike the award of attorney fees, punitive damages, and the jury trial request.

 

In opposition, Plaintiffs conclusively argue that the lease and applicable statutes allow for attorney fees (though none are delineated) and that Defendants’ fraudulent conduct and intentional misrepresentations justify punitive damages.

 

As for punitive damages, the MTS is moot insofar as the complaint is to be amended. As for attorney fees, the reply seems to concede that attorney fees are permissible as they are “limited to the contract” and section 31 of the lease provides that a prevailing party “shall be entitled to reasonable attorneys’ fees.” Accordingly, moving Defendants’ argument that attorney fees are not permissible is unclear such that said request is denied.

 

As for the waiver of jury trial, Plaintiffs cite to Grafton Partners v. Superior Court (2005) 36 Cal.4th 944 to support their proposition that any predispute agreement that any lawsuit between parties would be adjudicated in a court trial, and not by jury trial, was unenforceable, that is the correct holding of the case and Defendants do not address the case at all. Thus, the request to strike the jury trial is denied.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer is sustained with 30 days leave to amend and the MTS is denied in part (re: attorney fees and jury trial) and moot in part (re: punitive damages).
