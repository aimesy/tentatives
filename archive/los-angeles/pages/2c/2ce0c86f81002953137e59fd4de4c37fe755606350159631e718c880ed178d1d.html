DEPARTMENT 516 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV16865    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:            September 21, 2026   

Case Name:              Taheri v. Alfa Beta, Inc.

Case No.:                  25STCV16865

Matter:                       Motion to Deem Request for Admissions Admitted  

Moving Party:            Defendant Alfa Beta Inc. and Diana Hakimi

Responding Party:    Plaintiff Sherry Taheri

 

Tentative Ruling:     Defendant Alfa Beta Inc. and Diana Hakimi’s motion to deem Requests for Admissions is granted.

 

 

                  Plaintiff Sherry Taheri (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Alfa Beta Inc. (“Alfa”), a California Corporation, Diana Hakimi (“Hakimi”), and all persons unknown, claiming any legal or equitable right, title, estate, lien, or interest in the property described in the complaint adverse to Plaintiff’s title, or any cloud on Plaintiff’s title thereto, and Does 1-10.

 

            Defendants Alfa and Hakimi (collectively, “Defendants”) move to deem Requests for Admissions (Set One) served on Plaintiff admitted. Defendants contend that on July 2, 2026, they propounded discovery on Plaintiff. (Zadeh Decl., ¶ 3.) Defendants contend that to date Plaintiff has not served responses. (Id. at ¶ 5.)

 

            In opposition, Plaintiff confirms that she has not served responses. Moreover, Plaintiff argues that she does not reside at the address used to propound discovery. However, the address used to propound discovery, 10763 Wilkins Avenue, Los Angeles, California 90024, is the address that Plaintiff has provided the Court with the address for proper service.

 

            “An attorney or self-represented party whose mailing address, telephone number, fax number, or e-mail address (if it was provided under rule 2.111(1)) changes while an action is pending must serve on all parties and file a written notice of the change.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.200.)

 

            Plaintiff did not provide notice prior to the date of propounded discovery and has not served responses.

 

                Thus, Defendants’ motion to deem Requests for Admissions (Set One) against Plaintiff is granted.

 

            Defendants also request that the Court impose sanctions against Plaintiff in the amount of $1,860. The Court grants Defendants’ request for sanctions in the reduced amount of $500. Sanctions are to be paid on or before October 21, 2026.

 

 

 


Case Number: 26STCV22574    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             September 21, 2026

Case Name:               Gallaher v. United Parcel Service, Inc., et al.

Case No.:                   26STCV22574

Matter:                        Motion on Change of Venue

Moving Party:              Defendant United Parcel Service

Responding Party:       Plaintiff Travis Martin Gallaher

 

Tentative Ruling:      Defendant United Parcel Service’s motion to change venue is denied.

 

 

            Plaintiff Travis Martin Gallaher (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants United Parcel Service, Inc. (“UPS”), Carlos Padilla (“Padilla”), Diego Leon (“Leon”), Carlos Gutierrez (“Gutierrez”), and Does 1 through 100. The Complaint alleges eight causes of action for: (1) hostile work environment in violation of FEHA; (2) discrimination in violation of FEHA; (3) retaliation in violation of FEHA; (4) failure to prevent discrimination, harassment, or retaliation in violation of FEHA; (5) negligent hiring, supervision, and retention; (6) wrongful termination of employment in violation of public policy; (7) whistleblower retaliation (Labor Code section 1102.5); and (8) intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

            UPS moves to transfer Plaintiff’s case to Imperial County Superior Court. Plaintiff opposes.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Venue is determined based on the complaint on file at the time the motion to change venue is made. [Citations.]” (Brown v. Superior Court (1984) 37 Cal.3d 477, 482.) Code of Civil Procedure section 397, subdivision (a) provides that “[t]he court may, on motion, change the place of trial when the court designated in the complaint is not the proper court.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 397, subd. (a).) Code of Civil Procedure section 397, subdivision (c) states the court may change venue, “[w]hen the convenience of witnesses and the ends of justice would be promoted by the change.” (Id. at § 397, subd. (c).)

 

Analysis

 

            UPS moves to transfer Plaintiff’s case to Imperial County Superior Court pursuant to FEHA, on a discretionary basis, and seeks attorney’s fees.

 

            Preliminary Issue of Joinder

 

            Defendants Padilla, Leon, and Gutierrez filed a notice of joinder to UPS’ motion. While it is permissible for a co-party with identical interests to join on a motion. (See Calvert Fire Ins. Co. v. Cropper (1983) 141 Cal.App.3d 901, 905.) Here, Defendants Padilla, Leon, and Gutierrez did not provide the Court with any argument or basis to allow joinder of the motion.

            Thus, the Court denies Defendants Padilla, Leon, and Gutierrez’s motion for joinder.

 

            Motion to Change Venue

 

            Government Code section 12965, subdivision (c) states that:

 

The superior courts of the State of California shall have jurisdiction of actions brought pursuant to this section, and the aggrieved person may file in these courts. An action may be brought in any county in the state in which the unlawful practice is alleged to have been committed, in the county in which the records relevant to the practice are maintained and administered, or in the county in which the aggrieved person would have worked or would have had access to the public accommodation but for the alleged unlawful practice, but if the defendant is not found within any of these counties, an action may be brought within the county of the defendant’s residence or principal office.

 

(Govt. Code, § 12965, subd. (c)(3).)

 

            UPS contends that the only appropriate venue for Plaintiff’s case is Imperial County because Plaintiff’s workplace interactions occurred in the El Centro facility. (Mot., 5:1.) UPS also contends that all of Plaintiff’s relevant employment records are maintained in Imperial County. (Mot., 5:7-12.)

 

            However, UPS makes these allegations without any evidentiary support. In reply, UPS attempts to cure its procedural defect by providing evidence that was required when it filed its motion, but failed to do so. ““The general rule of motion practice ... is that new evidence is not permitted with reply papers.”” (Maleti v. Wickers (2022) 82 Cal.App.5th 181, 227.) The reason for not allowing new arguments in the reply papers “is based on the same solid logic applied in the appellate courts, specifically, that ‘[p]oints raised for the first time in a reply brief will ordinarily not be considered, because such consideration would deprive the respondent of an opportunity to counter the argument.’” (Jay v. Mahaffey (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1522, 1538.) Thus, UPS has not presented the Court with any evidence to support its claims.

 

            In opposition, Plaintiff provides evidence to support its claim that pursuant to  Government Code section 12965, subdivision (c), Los Angeles is the proper venue because he would have continued working in Los Angeles had it not been for the unlawful practice. Plaintiff declares that he “drove commercial semi-trucks and trailers in Los Angeles County.” (Gallaher Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) “Generally, when venue is proper in more than one county, a plaintiff has the choice of where to file the action from among the available options.” (Battaglia Enterprises, Inc. v. Superior Court (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 309, 313.) “The moving party must overcome the presumption that the plaintiff has selected the proper venue.” (Fontaine v. Superior Court (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 830, 836.) Thus, UPS has not met its burden to warrant transfer of Plaintiffs’ case.

 

            UPS also argues that Los Angeles is an inconvenient forum under Code of Civil Procedure section 397 (c), based on the convenience of witnesses. However, UPS provides no evidence to support its argument.

 

           Therefore, UPS’s motion to change venue is denied in its entirety.

 

           Plaintiff also requests that Defendant pay all necessary fees associated with the filing of its motion without any legal authority. Specifically, Plaintiff seeks an award of fees in the amount of $7,200.00 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 396b, subdivision (b). An award of fees and costs is not warranted under the circumstances here.

 

           Thus, Plaintiff’s request for sanctions is denied.

  

Conclusion

 

            Defendant United Parcel Service’s motion to change venue is denied.
