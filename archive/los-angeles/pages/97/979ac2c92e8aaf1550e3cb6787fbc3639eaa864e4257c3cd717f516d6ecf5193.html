Case Number: 23STCV11038    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 7 - Otto Perdomo v. County of Los Angeles

          Plaintiff Otto Perdomo’s Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement

Plaintiff Otto Perdomo moves to enforce his settlement agreement and enter judgment against Defendant County of Los Angeles.

Entry of Judgment

Plaintiff shows sufficient grounds to enter judgment of $46,746.55 against Defendant. Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, subdivision (a) provides in relevant part, “If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside of the presence of the court... for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.” 

The parties entered a written agreement to settle the action. (Slaughter Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. 1 (“Settlement Agreement”).) The agreement was signed by Plaintiff in April 2025, and Defendant in May 2025. (Id., at p. 7.) The agreement requires Defendant to pay 21 months of backpay at a monthly salary of $6,540 (Settlement Agreement, ¶ 1(b).) It also requires Defendant to reimburse any out-of-pocket expenses incurred after September 2022 for cafeteria plan benefits; Plaintiff was required to submit any applicable receipts for reimbursement. (Id., ¶ 1(b)(i).)

Here, Plaintiff submitted all required documentation in 2025. (Slaughter Decl., ¶ 9.) While Defense counsel informed Plaintiff’s counsel in May 2026 that the County mailed the payment via certified mail, Plaintiff did not receive it. (Id., ¶¶ 14-15.) Defendant now admits that the payment was never sent; there was a processing error, and the County is working to rectify the issue. (Flores Decl., ¶ 14.) Therefore, Defendant breached the agreement when it failed to timely pay the outstanding backpay and benefit amounts, as contemplated in the parties’ settlement agreement.

The Settlement Agreement provides that if any party breached the agreement, the Court retained jurisdiction to enforce its terms under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. (Settlement Agreement, ¶ 11.) The Court will enter judgment against Defendant for the remaining amount due to Plaintiff.

The Court notes that the parties dispute whether Plaintiff properly resigned from the County as required by the settlement agreement. Whether Plaintiff independently breached the agreement due to the way he resigned has no apparent bearing on the County’s payment obligations to him now. Defendant concedes as much in its brief. Therefore, the Court will not analyze this issue.

Attorney Fees

Plaintiff requests $4,950 in attorney fees for bringing this motion to enforce settlement. (Slaughter Decl., ¶ 20.) The Court declines to award attorney fees. The settlement agreement does not contain an attorney fee provision, and the Court declines to add one. “Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 provides a summary procedure to enforce a settlement agreement by entering judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.” (Hines v. Lukes (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 1174, 1182 [emphasis added].)

Disposition

          Plaintiff Otto Perdomo’s motion to enforce settlement agreement is granted. Plaintiff is to submit a proposed judgment forthwith.


Case Number: 24STCV30040    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 – Zainab Raheen, et al. v. Unique Homes by Caspian, Inc., et al.

Defendant Unique Homes by Caspian, Inc.’s Motions for Orders Compelling Responses to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Production

Defendant Unique Homes by Caspian, Inc. moves for orders compelling Plaintiff Zainab Raheen to provide initial responses to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production.

A party must respond to written discovery “[w]ithin 30 days after service.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.260(a) [interrogatories], 2031.260(a) [requests for production].) When the responding party fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories or requests for production, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(b) [interrogatories], 2031.300(b) [requests for production].) Failing to serve a timely response results in waiving any objections. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a), 2031.300(a).)

Plaintiff failed to serve timely responses to Defendant’s discovery requests. Defendant served form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production on Plaintiff on October 30, 2025. (Medina Decls., ¶ 3.) As of the date these motions were filed, Plaintiff had still not responded. (Id., ¶ 4; Motions, at p. 3.) There is no indication that Plaintiff served responses between the date the motions were filed and the date of the hearing.  Defendant is therefore entitled to an order compelling Plaintiff to respond to written discovery.

Sanctions

Defendant requests $9,000 in fees and $180 in costs as monetary sanctions against Plaintiff. Failing to respond to an authorized method of discovery is a misuse of the discovery process subject to monetary sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010(d).) Plaintiff failed to provide any responses by the deadline. There is no evidence that Plaintiff acted with substantial justification. Plaintiff did not file an opposition.

The Court finds sanctions are appropriate. Defendant did not, however, reasonably incur $9,000 in expenses in preparing these three motions. (Medina Decls., ¶¶ 6-7.) These motions are almost identical to each other. In addition, the Court notes that two conflicting hourly rates are presented: one at $250 per hour and another at $375 per hour. (Ibid.) The Court exercises its discretion to award counsel fees incurred drafting one of the motions at a rate of $250 per hour for four hours. This totals $1,000. The Court will award counsel one hour for attending the hearing on this motion for an additional $250. The Court will award $180 in filing costs to compensate for the $60 filing fee required for each motion. The Court will not award fees for hours requested for reviewing an anticipated opposition brief and preparing a reply; no opposition brief was filed and no reply was needed.

Thus, Defendant reasonably incurred a total of $1,250 in attorney fees and $180 in filing fees.

Disposition

Defendant Unique Homes by Caspian, Inc.’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories, special interrogatories, and requests for production are granted. Plaintiff Zainab Raheen is ordered to serve verified responses without objections within 10 days.

Plaintiff Zainab Raheen and her counsel of record are ordered jointly and severally to pay Defendant Unique Homes by Caspian, Inc. $1,430 in sanctions within 21 days.


Case Number: 26STCP03002    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 8 - Waterloo Pays, LLC v. Certain Statutorily Defined Interested Parties

          Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC’s Petition for Approval of Transfer of Structured Settlement Payment Rights

Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC petitions for approval of transfer of structured settlement rights by a payee. The court must determine whether the transfer “is fair and reasonable and in the best interest of the payee.” (Ins. Code, § 10137(a).) Specifically, the court must consider “[w]hether the financial terms of the transaction, including the discount rate applied to determine the amount to be paid to the payee, the expenses and costs of the transaction for both the payee and the transferee, the size of the transaction, the available financial alternatives to the payee to achieve the payee’s stated objectives, are fair and reasonable.” (Ins. Code, § 10139.5(b)(9).) 

Petitioner lodged its petition and supporting documents with the Court conditionally under seal. Therefore, the Court has access to all unredacted material on which to base its decision. In a declaration filed in support of Petitioner’s accompanying motion to seal, counsel claims that unredacted copies were forwarded to all interested parties. (Shafai Decl., ¶ 3.)

Payee, whose name is redacted in the public file, agreed to transfer rights to four annual payments of $31,244.61 from January 1, 2027, through January 1, 2030. (First Amended Petition, Ex. 2, p. 13.) This totals $124,978.44 in payments. Though it is unclear why, Petitioner redacted the total amount it would pay Payee in exchange for those rights. However, the Court notes that the amount is very low; the transaction would be equivalent to paying an incredibly high interest rate on a loan.

Payee states, “I intend to use this money to consolidate debt and purchase a vehicle.” (Payee Decl., ¶ 11.) Payee has the right under Insurance Code section 10139.5, subdivision (h) to have petitioner pay up to $1,500 for independent legal or financial advice about this transaction. As required, Petitioner advised Payee of that right, which the Payee waived. (Payee Decl., ¶ 15.)

Given the information before the Court, it cannot make the six express written findings as required under Insurance Code section 10139.5(a). This transfer is not fair, reasonable, or in Payee’s best interest. Payee could wait only a few months until her first annual payment of $31,244.61 and thereby receive a significant portion of what Petitioner offers Payee now. It is in Payee’s best interest not to transfer rights to these future payments for the low amount Petitioner offers.

Disposition

The Court hereby denies Waterloo Pays, LLC’s amended petition for approval of transfer of payment rights.

Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC’s Motion to Seal

Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC moves for an order sealing portions of documents filed in support of its petition for approval of transfer of structured settlement payment rights.

Specifically, Petitioner moves to seal the following:

1.     Payee’s name and address (Proof of Service to the Notice of Hearing)

2.     Payee’s name in Paragraph 1 of (First Amended Petition)

3.     Payee’s name and signature in body of Transfer Agreement (Exhibit 1)

4.     Payee’s name, address, annuity number, and purchase price on Schedule A (Exhibit 1)

5.     Financial information in Paragraphs 5.18, 5.19, 5.21, 5.22, 5.24, and 5.25 (Exhibit 1)

6.     Payee’s name and signature in body of Disclosure Statement (Exhibit 2)

7.     Financial information in the body of the Disclosure Statement (Exhibit 2)

8.     Payee’s name, address, age, dependents, employment information, and purchase price in Payee Declaration (Exhibit 3)

California Rules of Court, rule 2.550(d) provides:

A court may order that a record be filed under seal only if it expressly finds facts that establish:

(1) There exists an overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access to the record;

(2) The overriding interest supports sealing the record;

(3) A substantial probability exists that the overriding interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed;

(4) The proposed sealing is narrowly tailored; and

(5) No less restrictive means exist to achieve the overriding interest.

The Court cannot grant the full relief Petitioner seeks here. The proposed sealing is not narrowly tailored. Here, Petitioner seeks to seal its proposed purchase price and the equivalent annual interest rate on a loan. It is unclear why these items require sealing. Petitioner makes no effort in its papers to identify the overriding interest in sealing these items.

However, the Court finds good cause to seal Payee’s name, address and other identifying information given the underlying confidential settlement agreement resolved claims of sexual assault and/or abuse. Payee has an overriding privacy interest in her name and other identifying information; this interest supports sealing the record. There is a substantial probability that this privacy interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed. No less restrictive means exist.

Disposition

Petitioner Waterloo Pays, LLC’s motion to seal is granted in part. The following information is hereby sealed: Payee’s name, signature, address, annuity number, age, dependents and employment information.

As for the remaining portions—purchase price and other financial information—the moving party has two options. First, Petitioner may notify the Court within 10 days that it chooses for the unredacted records to be filed unsealed. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551(b)(6).) Second, if Petitioner does nothing within 10 days, the Court will return the paper copies of the lodged records and delete any digital copies. (Ibid.) The redacted versions of the records will remain in the Court’s public file.
