DEPARTMENT 6 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25PSCV04518    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  Joshua Lozano, et al. v. Betty Luong Wong, et al. 

Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc.’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Complaint 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court GRANTS Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc.’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Complaint. The Court STRIKES the following words from the Complaint as to Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc. without leave to amend:

1.      “own,” and “lease, entrust, maintain,” from paragraph MV-1 of the First Cause of Action-Motor Vehicle; and

2.      “managed, maintained,” and “entrusted” from paragraph GN-1 of the Second Cause of Action-General Negligence. 

            Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc. is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is an auto accident case. On December 17, 2025, plaintiffs Joshua Lozano and Lorena E. Solorzano (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed this action against defendants Betty Luong Wong (Wong), United Parcel Service, Inc. (UPS) (collectively, Defendants), and Does 1 to 75, alleging causes of action for motor vehicle and general negligence. 

On March 30, 2026, UPS filed a cross-complaint against Wong and Roes 1 through 75, alleging causes of action for comparative indemnity and apportionment of fault, total equitable indemnity, and declaratory relief. 

On May 4, 2026, Wong filed a cross-complaint against Roes 1 through 5, alleging causes of action for contribution, indemnity, and declaratory relief. On June 12, 2026, Wong dismissed the cross-complaint with prejudice. 

On March 30, 2026, UPS moved to strike portions of the Complaint. Plaintiffs did not oppose the motion. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

            “Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof, but this time limitation shall not apply to motions specified in subdivision (e).” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(2).) “The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper: (a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.” (Id., § 436.) 

DISCUSSION 

Meet and Confer 

The Court finds UPS’ efforts to meet and confer before filing the Motion to Strike sufficient. (Hernandez-Moreno Decl., ¶ 4; Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a).) 

            Admission of Vicarious Liability 

            “[A]n employer’s admission of vicarious liability for an employee’s negligent driving in the course of employment bars a plaintiff from pursuing a claim for negligent entrustment.” (Diaz v. Carcamo (2011) 51 Cal.4th 1148, 1161 (Diaz).) 

            UPS seeks to strike portions of the Complaint alleging direct negligence against UPS on the grounds that UPS admitted vicarious liability in its Answer to the Complaint and therefore cannot be liable based on negligent entrustment per Diaz. UPS then seeks to strike allegations in the motor vehicle cause of action against UPS based on owning, leasing, entrusting, or maintaining the vehicle because Plaintiffs did not allege facts showing UPS negligently owned, leased, maintained, or entrusted the vehicle. UPS also seeks to strike allegations against UPS in the general negligence cause of action that UPS managed, maintained, and entrusted the vehicle because Plaintiffs have not alleged any facts that UPS negligently managed, maintained, or entrusted the vehicle. 

            The Court agrees with UPS that its admission of vicarious liability in its Answer obviates any negligent entrustment theory of liability against UPS per Diaz. (See Answer (3/30/26), p. 2, lines 4-5; Diaz, supra, 51 Cal.4th at p. 1161.) As such, any allegations against UPS involving negligent entrustment are improper. The Court further construes Plaintiffs’ lack of opposition as a concession that UPS’ arguments on this point are meritorious. (D.I. Chadbourne, Inc. v. Superior Court (1964) 60 Cal.2d 723, 728, fn. 4 [where nonmoving party fails to oppose a ground for a motion, "it is assumed that [nonmoving party] concedes" that ground].) 

            The Court declines to address UPS’ remaining arguments. Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS the motion. The Court STRIKES the following words from the Complaint as to Defendant UPS without leave to amend:

1.      “own,” and “lease, entrust, maintain,” from paragraph MV-1 of the First Cause of Action-Motor Vehicle; and

2.      “managed, maintained,” and “entrusted” from paragraph GN-1 of the Second Cause of Action-General Negligence.[1] 

CONCLUSION 

The Court GRANTS Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc.’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiffs’ Complaint. The Court STRIKES the following words from the Complaint as to Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc. without leave to amend:

1.      “own,” and “lease, entrust, maintain,” from paragraph MV-1 of the First Cause of Action-Motor Vehicle; and

2.      “managed, maintained,” and “entrusted” from paragraph GN-1 of the Second Cause of Action-General Negligence. 

            Defendant United Parcel Service, Inc. is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order.


[1] UPS’ Notice of Motion references “Page 1, ¶ 4” and “Page 5, ¶ 6” regarding the portions it seeks to strike. (Notice, 2:1-4.) However, those paragraphs do not exist in the Complaint. Plaintiffs used a Judicial Council Form complaint, which has different pages and numbering. (See generally, Compl.) Nevertheless, the Court was able to determine from reviewing the Complaint where the disputed language was located. The Court admonishes UPS’ counsel to be more cautious going forward about such details.

Case Number: 26PSCP00118    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 6

CASE NAME:  Rolling Green, Inc., et al. v. Gabriel Contreras 

Verified Petition of Rolling Green, Inc. and Victor Zuniga for an Order Authorizing Preservation of Evidence Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 2035.010, E.T. SEQ. [sic] 

TENTATIVE RULING 

The Court summarily DENIES the Verified Petition of Rolling Green, Inc. and Victor Zuniga for an Order Authorizing Preservation of Evidence Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 2035.010, E.T. SEQ. [sic] without prejudice. 

            Petitioner is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order. 

BACKGROUND 

This is a petition to preserve evidence under Code of Civil Procedure section 2035.010, et seq., in connection with an anticipated personal injury lawsuit. On March 20, 2026, petitioners Rolling Green, Inc. and Victor Zuniga (collectively, Petitioners) filed this petition against respondent Gabriel Contreras (Respondent) for an order authorizing preservation of evidence under Code of Civil Procedure section 2035.010, et seq. Respondent has not responded to the Petition as of this tentative ruling. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

(a) One who expects to be a party or expects a successor in interest to be a party to an action that may be cognizable in a court of the state, whether as a plaintiff, or as a defendant, or in any other capacity, may obtain discovery within the scope delimited by Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 2017.010), and subject to the restrictions set forth in Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 2019.010), for the purpose of perpetuating that person's own testimony or that of another natural person or organization, or of preserving evidence for use in the event an action is subsequently filed. 

(b) One shall not employ the procedures of this chapter for purposes of either ascertaining the possible existence of a cause of action or a defense to it, or of identifying those who might be made parties to an action not yet filed. 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.010.) 

DISCUSSION 

Petitioners argue that they expect to be defendants in an anticipated personal-injury lawsuit arising from a July 24, 2025 automobile accident, but they cannot adequately defend themselves because Respondent has not produced any medical or employment records despite months of repeated requests. Petitioners contend that pre-litigation discovery is necessary to preserve evidence, including medical records, radiology films, employment data, and prior loss records, that may otherwise be lost, destroyed, or unavailable due to unknown providers and varying record-retention policies. Petitioners argue that they have diligently attempted to meet and confer since August 2025, but Respondent’s counsel has provided no records or timeline, preventing meaningful settlement discussions. Petitioners contend they meet the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 2035.010 et seq for pre-litigation discovery, including the need to prevent failure or delay of justice. 

Petitioners contend that courts routinely grant similar petitions when a respondent refuses to provide medical records. Petitioners argue that granting the Petition will promote judicial efficiency, avoid prejudice, and help facilitate early resolution of the claim. Petitioners request the Court issue an order compelling Respondent’s physical examination, production of all medical, billing, or radiology records related to Respondent’s alleged injuries in their possession, custody, or control, an order permitting Petitioners to subpoena records to any and all medical providers who may have treated Respondent based on the underlying accident, and for Respondent’s employment records in connection with any potential loss of earnings claim. 

The Court notes Petitioners have not filed a proof of service of the Petition indicating service in the same manner as a summons and complaint per Code of Civil Procedure section 2035.040, subdivision (a), or that such service was effected at least 20 days before the hearing on the Petition, per section 2035.040, subdivision (c). (Code Civ. Proc., § 2035.040, subds. (a), (c); see Proof of Service (4/1/26); Notice of Continuance (6/29/26).) Courts generally lack jurisdiction to rule on motions “that have not been properly noticed for hearing on the date in question. [Citations.]” (Diaz v. Pro. Cmty. Mgmt., Inc. (2017) 16 Cal.App.5th 1190, 1204-1205; see also Harris v. Board of Ed. of City and County of San Francisco (1957) 152 Cal.App.2d 677, 680 [“where the trial court lacks jurisdiction to make the order, as in a situation where requisite notice has not been given to the plaintiff, it may be vacated by that court at any time thereafter”]; Svistunoff v. Svistunoff (1952) 108 Cal.App.2d 638, 641 [“A sufficient notice of the hearing not having been given, the order was void”].) 

Based on the foregoing, the Court summarily DENIES the Petition without prejudice. 

CONCLUSION 

The Court summarily DENIES the Verified Petition of Rolling Green, Inc. and Victor Zuniga for an Order Authorizing Preservation of Evidence Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure § 2035.010, E.T. SEQ. [sic] without prejudice. 

            Petitioner is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling within five calendar days of this order.

Case Number: 26PSCV00600    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: 6

Plaintiff Fifth Third Bank’s Request for Entry of Default Judgment

Defendant: Minjae Kim

TENTATIVE RULING    

Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is granted in the amount of $113,225.45. The Court directs Plaintiff to submit a revised proposed judgment for the Court’s review and signature. If Plaintiff submits on this tentative ruling, Plaintiff can submit the revised proposed judgment in advance of the hearing date. 

BACKGROUND           

            This is a lending dispute. On February 19, 2026, plaintiff Fifth Third Bank, N.A., successor by merger to Comerica Bank (Plaintiff) filed this action against defendant Minjae Kim (Defendant) and Does 1 to 100, alleging causes of action for breach of guaranty, money lent, and account stated. 

            On April 21, 2026, the court clerk entered default against Defendant. 

            On May 8, 2026, Plaintiff requested entry of default judgment against Defendant. 

LEGAL STANDARD 

Code of Civil Procedure section 585 permits entry of a default judgment after a party has failed to timely respond or appear. (Code Civ. Proc., § 585.) A party seeking judgment on the default by the court must file a Request for Court Judgment and: (1) a brief summary of the case; (2) declarations or other admissible evidence in support of the judgment requested; (3) interest computations as necessary; (4) a memorandum of costs and disbursements; (5) declaration of nonmilitary status; (6) a proposed form of judgment; (7) a dismissal of all parties against whom judgment is not sought or an application for separate judgment under Code of Civil Procedure section 579, supported by a showing of grounds for each judgment; (8) exhibits as necessary; and (9) a request for attorneys’ fees if allowed by statute or by the agreement of the parties. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1800, subd. (a).) 

ANALYSIS 

Plaintiff seeks default judgment against Defendant in the total amount of $115,842.62, including $100,000.00 in damages, $9,812.50 in interest, $5,270.00 in attorney fees, $522.95 in costs, and $237.17 in late charges. The Court finds Plaintiff has provided sufficient evidence to support the damages claimed, but the attorney fee request is excessive. The available attorney fee under Local Rule 3.214 is $2,890.00, i.e., $1,890.00 plus 2% of $50,000.00 (or $1,000.00). (Local Rule 3.214.) The Court also finds no documentary evidence to showing how the $237.17 in late charges was calculated and will therefore deduct those from the judgment. (See Alfaro Decl., ¶¶ 7, 12, 17; Code Civ. Proc., § 585, subd. (b) [“The court shall hear the evidence offered by the plaintiff, and shall render judgment in the plaintiff's favor for that relief, not exceeding the amount stated in the complaint… as appears by the evidence to be just”]; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1800, subds. (a)(2), (a)(8).) The Court will therefore grant Plaintiff’s default judgment request in the amount of $113,225.45. 

CONCLUSION 

      Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s request for entry of default judgment is granted in the amount of $113,225.45. The Court directs Plaintiff to submit a revised proposed judgment for the Court’s review and signature. If Plaintiff submits on this tentative ruling, Plaintiff can submit the revised proposed judgment in advance of the hearing date.
