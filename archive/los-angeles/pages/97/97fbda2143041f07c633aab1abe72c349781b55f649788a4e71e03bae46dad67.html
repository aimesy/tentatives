Case Number: 21STCV00971    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 514

Delgado v. Ford Motor Company
21STCV00971
Defendants’ Motion to Strike Costs

Tentative Ruling

The motion to strike costs is granted.

This action was filed in 2021.  A notice of settlement was filed on April 25, 2024, and the Court dismissed this action (without prejudice) on October 2, 2024.

On March 12, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s motion for attorney’s fees.

On August 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a memorandum of costs.

On August 5, 2026, Defendants filed a motion to strike the memorandum of costs as untimely.  Plaintiff filed an opposition on September 9, and Defendants filed a reply on September 14.

In general, the “prevailing party” is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs for suit in any action or proceeding. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (b); Santisas v. Goodin (1998) 17 Cal.4th 599, 606; Scott Co. Of Calif. v. Blount, Inc. (1999) 20 Cal.4th 1103, 1108.)  Assuming the “prevailing party” requirements are met, the trial court has no discretion to order each party to bear his or her own costs of suit.  (Michell v. Olick (1996) 49 Cal.App.4th 1194, 1198; Nelson v. Anderson (1999) 72 Cal.App.4th 111, 129.)   

“A prevailing party who claims costs must serve and file a memorandum of costs within 15 days after the date of service of the notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5 or the date of service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal, or within 180 days after entry of judgment, whichever is first.” (California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700, subd. (a).)

This action was dismissed on October 3, 2024.  The memorandum of costs filed by Plaintiff in August 2026 is untimely.

On that basis, the Court GRANTS the Defendants’ motion to strike the memorandum of costs.

Moving party is ordered to give notice.


Case Number: 24STCV10296    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 514

Angel v. Fernandez
24STCV10296
Plaintiff’s Motion for Terminating Sanctions

Tentative Ruling

The motion is denied.

Background

This case arises out of allegations of fraud, breach of contract, and various other torts.

On April 24, 2024, Geraldine Angel (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against Alvin Bautista Fernandez, George W. Angel, George C. Fernandez (collectively “Defendants”), and Does 1 through 50. 

On May 29, 2024, Defendants filed an answer to the complaint and a cross-complaint against Plaintiff and Does 1 through 10.

The cross-complaint has gone through several iterations.  The currently operative cross-complaint is the Third Amended Cross-Complaint (the “TACC”) filed on November 25, 2025.

On July 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed this motion to strike the TACC as a terminating sanction.  Defendants filed an opposition on August 25, and Plaintiff filed a reply on September 15.

Trial is set for April 27, 2027.

Discussion

Plaintiff seeks a terminating sanction and/or discovery sanctions against Defendants based on the failure of Defendants to file a notice of related cases, the failure to join an indispensable party, and other grounds.

Plaintiff argues terminating sanctions are warranted because Defendants (among other things) filed to identify in discovery a related case, Case No. 24STCV31324, and failed to file a notice of related cases.

The Court denies the request to impose terminating sanctions and/or discovery sanctions against Defendants for failure to file a notice of related cases.  The duty to file a notice of related cases is on all parties to a civil action.  Even after Plaintiff became aware of the potentially related case, Plaintiff did not file a notice of related cases.  Moreover, a terminating sanction is not an appropriate sanction for the failure to file a notice of related cases, and the failure to make such a filing is not subject to discovery sanctions.

The Court also denies the request to impose terminating sanctions and/or discovery sanctions against Defendants for failure to disclose the existence of the potentially related case in discovery.  Terminating sanctions are not appropriate in these circumstances, and Plaintiff does not identify, in the notice of motion and motion, what discovery sanctions she seeks.  Moreover, if Plaintiff contends that she has been unduly prejudiced by the late disclosure of material information, she does not explain why an alternative remedy (such as a brief continuance of the trial date to conduct additional discovery) is not appropriate.

The Court does not in any way condone the failure to of any party to give complete, truthful answers under oath to interrogatories and other discovery requests.  But Plaintiff has not made a sufficient showing for the imposition of the sanctions that she seeks.

Finally, if a party in Case No. 24STCV31324 is a necessary party here, and if there is a legal and factual basis to add that party to this litigation at this time, Plaintiff may choose to do so through appropriate means.

The Court DENIES the motion of Plaintiff Geraldine Angel for an order striking the Answer to the Complaint, and dismissing the Third Amended Cross-Complaint, and other sanctions.

Moving Party is ordered to give notice.
