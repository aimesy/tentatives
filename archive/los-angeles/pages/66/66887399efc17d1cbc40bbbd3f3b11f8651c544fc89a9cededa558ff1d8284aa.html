DEPARTMENT T LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24NNCV03036    Hearing Date: July 8, 2026    Dept: T

Claire Yi He vs Sheng Li, et al.

Motion to Compel Plaintiff’s Deposition and Production of Documents

Moving Party:                         Defendant Cassix LLC

Responding Party:                   Plaintiff Claire Yi He

Tentative Ruling:                    Grant

 

BACKGROUND

 

            On April 6, 2026, Defendant Cassix LLC (“Defendant”) served a notice of deposition and notice to produce documents on Plaintiff Claire Yi He (“Plaintiff’), which noticed the taking of the in-person deposition of Plaintiff for April 29, 2026, and set forth 25 production requests.

 

            On April 16, 2026, following an exchange of email correspondence between counsel for the parties, the date of Plaintiff’s deposition was rescheduled to May 11, 2026, with all other details set forth in the original notice remaining operative.

 

            On May 6, 2026, Plaintiff served objections to the production requests. The next day, her counsel withdrew an objection to Defendant’s vague usage of term “complaint” in these requests.  In the same correspondence, Plaintiff’s counsel stated that Plaintiff would be appearing remotely for the hearing.  Following a disagreement between counsel as to whether Plaintiff could appear remotely for this deposition, defense counsel took this deposition off calendar to file the instant motion.

 

Defendant now moves to compel Plaintiff’s in-person deposition and her responses to its production requests.

 

Plaintiff opposes, and Defendant replies.  The Court exercises its discretion to consider Plaintiff’s untimely opposition.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d).)

 

MOVING PARTY POSITION

 

            Defendant asks for an order compelling Plaintiff to appear for deposition in-person and to produce the requested documents due to Plaintiff’s refusal to appear in-person and provide the requested records.  Defendant also seeks $6,010.00 in monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel of record.

 

OPPOSITION

           

            In an untimely opposition, Plaintiff contends that the motion should be denied because Plaintiff never refused to submit to discovery and because Defendant has not established any basis to compel document production under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.240.  Plaintiff argues that sanctions against her are unwarranted and also seeks $3,575.00 in monetary sanctions against Defendant and its counsel of record.

 

REPLY

 

            Defendant initially responds that Plaintiff’s opposition was late and may be disregarded by the Court.  Defendant further responds that the opposition avoids facts and law and that it does not establish that Plaintiff’s counsel acted with substantial justification in opposing the motion. 

ANALYSIS

I.                   Legal Standard

            “If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action … , without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document … described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document … described in the deposition notice.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

“A motion under subdivision (a) [above] shall comply with both of the following:

1. The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.

2. The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040, or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”

(Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450, subd. (b).)

II.                Discussion

            A.        Meet and Confer

            The Court finds that the declaration of Defendant’s counsel, Lyle R. Mink, sets forth sufficient facts showing that counsel attempted to resolve this issue in good faith after Plaintiff communicated that she would not attend the deposition as noticed.  (Mink Decl. ¶¶ 2-16.) 

            B.        Compel Deposition and Document Production

On May 7, 2026, Plaintiff informed Defendant that she would not be appearing in-person for the noticed deposition and served the following amended objection to this notice:

Plaintiff further objects to the deposition notice that required in person appearance which imposes unnecessary burden, expense, and inconvenience where the deposition can be effectively conducted remotely by videoconference. Defendant [sic] further objects to the extent the notice seeks to compel in-person appearance without good cause demonstrating that a remote deposition would be inadequate or prejudicial.

(Sun Decl. ¶ 14, Exh. C.) 

            Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.410, “[a]ny party served with a deposition notice that does not comply with Article 2 (commencing with Section 2025.210) waives any error or irregularity unless that party promptly serves a written objection specifying that error or irregularity at least three calendar days prior to the date for which the deposition is scheduled, on the party seeking to take the deposition and any other attorney or party on whom the deposition notice was served.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.410, subd. (a), emphasis added.) 

            However, none of the objections set forth by Plaintiff constitute actual error or irregularity in the notice itself, including any failure to comply with Article 2.  First, Plaintiff’s objection that Defendant must demonstrate good cause for noticing an in-person deposition is without factual or statutory basis.  Rather, Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.250 provides that, “[u]nless the court orders otherwise under Section 2025.260, the deposition of a natural person, whether or not a party to the action, shall be taken at a place that is, at the option of the party giving notice of the deposition, either within 75 miles of the deponent’s residence, or within the county where the action is pending and within 150 miles of the deponent's residence.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.250, subd. (a), emphasis added.)  Accordingly, no good cause showing is required for a deposing party to notice the deposition of a natural person at a physical location within 75 miles of a deponent’s residence.  Furthermore, as Plaintiff offers no argument or evidence establishing that the noticed Pasadena location for her deposition is not within 75 miles of Plaintiff’s residence, Plaintiff’s objections as to the unnecessary burden, expense, and inconvenience of an in-person deposition are meritless.

            Nor does Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.310 provide a deponent with the discretion to elect to attend a properly noticed deposition remotely, as the statute concerns the discretion afforded to counsel, non-deponent parties, and deposition officers to attend a deposition remotely.  (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.310, subd. (a) [allowing the deposition officer to attend a deposition at a different location than the deponent via remote means]; id., subd. (b) [providing any party or attorney of record with the discretion to be physically present at the deposition at the deponent’s location]; see, also, id., subd. (d) [“An exercise of the authority granted by subdivision (a) or (b) does not waive any other provision of this title, including, but not limited to, provisions regarding the time, place, or manner in which a deposition shall be conducted.”].) 

Moreover, in reviewing defense counsel’s declaration, the Court finds that Defendant has shown good cause for the inspection and production of each of the 25 production requests set forth in the deposition notice.  (See Mink Decl. [re Good Cause] ¶¶ 1-25.)  Although Plaintiff objected to these requests due to the purportedly vague usage of the term “complaint” throughout, it appears that this objection was withdrawn upon Defendant’s confirmation that the allegations referenced in the disputed production requests were directed to the second amened complaint, which the Court notes was evident from the plain language of the notice itself.  (Mink Decl. ¶ 2, Exh. 1, at p. 2 [“The original and copy of the 2020 Agreement described in paragraph 37 of your second amended complaint (hereafter “complaint”)”], emphasis added.)  Nor are the remaining objections to these production requests merited, as Plaintiff cites to no legal authority for her refusal to produce any original documents for inspection at deposition. 

Therefore, given Plaintiff’s meritless objections to the noticed location of her deposition and the requested documents for production, the motion to compel is granted in its entirety.

            D.        Sanctions

“If a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450, subd. (g).)

 

Defendant seeks $6,010.00 in monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel of record, which defense counsel calculates by applying his $595.00 hourly rate to 10 total hours of completed and anticipated work, plus a $60.00 filing fee.  (Mink Decl. ¶ 17.)  Given the motion’s apparent lack of complexity and the facially excessive hours of work attributed to the motion, the Court finds that a reasonable reduction of $2,975.00 is warranted, reflecting a reduction of 5 total hours of work. 

 

Therefore, as Plaintiff failed to show substantial justification in opposing the instant motion and as the circumstances underlying this motion do not make the imposition of sanctions unjust, the Court awards sanctions in the amount of $3,035.50.

RULING

Defendant Cassix LLC’s Motion to Compel Plaintiff Claire Yi He’s Deposition and Production of Documents is GRANTED.  Plaintiff shall appear for an in-person deposition at the office of Chiao & Wu within 20 days of this order, or on another date and location stipulated in writing by the parties.

The Court further GRANTS monetary sanctions in reduced amount of $3,035.50 against Plaintiff and her counsel of record, jointly and severally.  Said monetary sanctions shall be paid to counsel for Defendant within 30 days of this order.

 

 


Case Number: 25NNCV07910    Hearing Date: July 8, 2026    Dept: T

Velocity Investments LLC vs. Akop Labachyan

Motion to be Relieved as Counsel

Moving Party:                         Defendant Akop Labachyan’s Counsel

Responding Party:                   None

Tentative Ruling:                    Grant

 

BACKGROUND

 

Defendant Akop Labachyan’s (“Defendant”) counsel of record, attorney Christina Toroyan and Petoskey Law Group, PLLC (“Counsel”), move to be relieved as counsel for Defendant.

 

No opposition has been filed.

 

MOVING PARTY POSITION

 

Counsel seeks to be relieved based on a breakdown of the attorney-client relationship.

 

OPPOSITION

           

No opposition was filed.

 

REPLY


No reply was filed. 

ANALYSIS

            Counsel has filed forms MC-051 and MC-052 and has lodged with the Court a copy of the proposed order on form MC-053 as required.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362.)  Counsel states the instant motion is filed for the following reason: “Attorney and client have a conflict of interest that precludes further representation of the client. there [sic] has been a break down in the attorney client relationship that precludes further representation. Attorney is unable to represent client as a result of this breakdown and conflict. Due to the nature of the breakdown and conflict, attorney is precluded from disclosing the exact nature of the breakdown and conflict in this Declaration (California Rules of Professional Conduct 3-1700(A) - attorney withdrawal must not prejudice client) but will discuss the nature of the conflict with the Court if so required.”  (MC-052.)  The Court finds this is a valid reason to withdraw as counsel for Defendant.  (See Rules Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4).)¿¿¿ 

Counsel must serve the signed order (MC-053) within 10 days of the date of the order and file a proof of service confirming this service.  Counsel will remain the attorney of record for Defendant until Counsel files the proof of service.¿ (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).)¿¿¿ 

RULING 

Defendant’s counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED on the terms set forth above.


Case Number: 26NNCV02699    Hearing Date: July 8, 2026    Dept: T

Isla Marout, et al. vs. Hambik Bedroosian, et al. 

Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiff’s Complaint

Moving Party:                         Defendants Sidna Pilikian and Hamp Khatchadourian

Responding Party:                   Plaintiff Isla Marout, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Sarineh Danlian

Tentative Ruling:                           Grant

 

BACKGROUND

 

            On April 8, 2026, Plaintiff Isla Marout, a minor, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Sarineh Danlian (“Plaintiff’) filed a complaint against Defendants Hambik Bedroosian, Sidna Pilikian, and Hamp Khatchadourian, alleging the following causes of action: (1) negligence; and (2) strict liability.  Plaintiff alleges that on or about August 13, 2023, she was bitten by dog while lawfully located near or at 650 West Dryden Street in Glendale, California (the “Premises”). 

 

Defendants Sidna Pilikian and Hamp Khatchadourian (“Defendants”) now move to strike punitive damages allegations from the complaint. 

 

Plaintiff opposes, and Defendants reply.  The Court exercises its discretion to consider Plaintiff’s untimely opposition.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d).)

 

MOVING PARTY POSITION

 

            Defendants seek to strike Paragraph numbers 19, 26, 31, 32, and 33 and Prayer for Relief number 4 from the complaint because Plaintiff has not alleged facts sufficient to support her punitive damages claim against Defendants under Civil Code section 3294, including ultimate facts establishing malice, oppression, or fraud.

 

OPPOSITION

 

            In an untimely opposition, Plaintiff contends that the complaint adequately alleges in the form of conscious disregard of a known danger because, read as a whole, the complaint alleges that Defendants had actual prior knowledge of the dog’s vicious tendencies.  Plaintiff also argues that the negligence label of her sole cause of action against Defendants does not preclude recovery for punitive damages if the underlying facts establish their conscious disregard of a known danger.  Plaintiff also seeks leave to amend without showing how it could amend the complaint to cure any pleadings defects found by the Court.  

 

REPLY

 

            Defendants initially respond that the Court should disregard the untimely opposition filed by Plaintiff.  Defendants further respond that the opposition fails to provide any sufficient ultimate facts to support a claim for punitive damages                    

ANALYSIS

I.          Legal Standard

A.        Motion to Strike

            California law authorizes a party’s motion to strike matter from an opposing party’s pleading if it is irrelevant, false, or improper.¿ (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 435, 436(a).)¿ Motions may also target pleadings or parts of pleadings which are not filed or drawn in conformity with applicable laws, rules or orders.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 436(b).)  A motion to strike is used to address defects that appear on the face of a pleading or from judicially noticed matter but that are not grounds for a demurrer.  (Pierson v Sharp Memorial Hospital (1989) 216 Cal.App.3d 340, 342; see, also, City & County of San Francisco v Strahlendorf (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1911, 1913 [motion may not be based on a party's declaration or factual representations made by counsel in the motion papers].)  In particular, a motion to strike can be used to attack the entire pleading or any part thereof – in other words, a motion to strike may target single words or phrases, unlike demurrers.  (Warren v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. (1971) 19 Cal.App.3d 24, 40.)¿

            The Code of Civil Procedure also authorizes the Court to act on its own initiative to strike matters, empowering the Court to enter orders striking matter “at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 436.)¿ 

                        1.         Punitive Damages

            Punitive damages may be imposed where it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).)  “Malice” is conduct intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.  (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(1).)  “‘Punitive damages are proper only when the tortious conduct rises to levels of extreme indifference to the plaintiff’s rights, a level which decent citizens should not have to tolerate.’  [Citation.]”  (Lackner v. North (2006) 135 Cal.App.4th 1188, 1210.)    

            “As amended to include [despicable], the [Civil Code section 3294] plainly indicates that absent an intent to injure the plaintiff, ‘malice’ requires more than a ‘willful and conscious’ disregard of the plaintiffs’ interests.  The additional component of ‘despicable conduct’ must be found.”  (College Hospital Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704, 725.)  The statute’s reference to despicable conduct represents a “new substantive limitation on punitive damage awards.”  (Ibid.)  Despicable conduct is “conduct which is so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people.  Such conduct has been described as ‘having the character of outrage frequently associated with crime.’ [Citation.]”  (Tomaselli v. Transamerica Ins. Co. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 1269, 1287.)  There also “must be evidence that defendant acted with knowledge of the probable dangerous consequences to plaintiff’s interests and deliberately failed to avoid these consequences.”  (Flyer’s Body Shop Profit Sharing Plan v. Ticor Title Ins. Co. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 1149, 1155.) 

            A motion to strike punitive damages is properly granted where a plaintiff fails to state a prima facie claim for punitive damages, including well-pleaded allegations that defendant is guilty of oppression, fraud or malice.  (Turman v. Turning Point of Cent. California, Inc. (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 53, 63.)  “Mere negligence, even gross negligence, is not sufficient to justify such an award” for punitive damages.” (Kendall Yacht Corp. v. United California Bank (1975) 50 Cal.App.3d 949, 958.)  Moreover, conclusory allegations are not sufficient to support a claim for punitive damages.  (Brousseau v. Jarrett (1977) 73 Cal.App.3d 864, 872.)  Lastly, “[t]he mere allegation an intentional tort was committed is not sufficient to warrant an award of punitive damages.  Not only must there be circumstances of oppression, fraud, or malice, but facts must be alleged in the pleading to support such a claim.”  (Grieves v. Superior Court (1984) 157 Cal.App.3d 159, 166.)

II.        Meet and Confer

Per the declaration of Defendants’ counsel Ternier A. Wright, counsel for the parties met and conferred telephonically and were unable to resolve the issues underlying the instant motion.  (Wright Decl. ¶¶ 4-6.) 

III.       Discussion

A.                Motion to Strike

Defendants seek to strike the punitive damages allegations from the complaint, as set forth in their notice of motion.  (See Compl. ¶¶ 19, 26, 31, 32, and 33; id., Prayer for Relief ¶ 4.)  In reviewing the complaint, the Court finds that Plaintiff failed to set forth well-pleaded factual allegations establishing that Defendants acted in conscious disregard of the safety of others by allowing the subject dog to remain on the Premises prior to the subject incident.  (See id. ¶¶ 3, 4, 17, and 22)  As Plaintiff instead relies on conclusions of fact to establish Defendants’ knowledge of the subject dog’s dangerous propensities and the risk of harm it posed to others, as well as Defendants’ failure as landlords to exercise reasonable control over the dog given this knowledge, the malicious character of Defendants’ conduct is not adequately supported by the complaint.  (See Turman, supra, 191 Cal.App.4th at p. 63; cf. Taylor v. Superior Court (1979) 24 Cal.3d 890, 899 [“Although the circumstances in a particular case may disclose similar wilful or wanton behavior in other forms, ordinarily, routine negligent or even reckless disobedience of traffic laws would not justify an award of punitive damages.”].)

Thus, as Plaintiff failed to plead sufficient facts to support her claim for punitive damages against Defendants, and as Plaintiff failed to show that a reasonable possibility exists of amending the complaint to cure these deficiencies, the motion is granted without leave to amend.

RULING

Defendants Sidna Pilikian and Hamp Khatchadourian’s Motion to Strike Portions of the Complaint is GRANTED without leave to amend.
