DEPARTMENT 316 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCV32221    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 316

BACKGROUND

This case is a contract dispute. Plaintiff Town House Retail Center, L.P. alleges that it was unjustly brought into a construction contract which exposed it to significant financial and reputational risk.

On January 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed its first amended complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants Grand Mesa Partners 400 Trinity Driver Hilltop Owners LLC, Ocean Rock Capital Partners, Ocean Rock Group, Ocean Rock Holdco LLC, Ocean Rock Hilltop Asset Manager, LLC, Sierra Equity Partners, LLC, GMP 400, LLC, D. Tucker Sharp, The Georgetown Companies, Georgetown Investors 3, LLC, and Steven E. Coury. The FAC alleges six causes of action stemming from the facts alleged above.

On March 9, 2026, Defendants Grand Mesa Partners 400 Trinity Driver Hilltop Owners LLC, Ocean Rock Capital Partners, Ocean Rock Group, Sierra Equity Partner, LLC, and D. Tucker Sharp filed a demurrer to the second, third, fifth and sixth causes of action in the FAC.

On July 13, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition.

On July 23, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint is SUSTAINED.

Plaintiff is granted leave to amend and has twenty days from the Court’s order to file and serve an amended complaint.

LEGAL STANDARD

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (See Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (See Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)   

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Ibid.; see also Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245).  

DISCUSSION

I.                Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer or motion to strike, the demurring party shall meet and confer with the opposing party in person, by telephone, or by video conference. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) The demurring party must file a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(3).)

Here, the parties discussed the demurrer by telephone. (See Antoni Decl., ¶ 2.) Thus, the meet and confer requirement has been satisfied. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).)

II.             Fraud Causes of Action (Causes of Action No. 2 & 3)

The Court finds that Plaintiff failed to allege resulting damage from Defendants’ alleged fraud. Accordingly, the demurrer to this cause of action is sustained with leave to amend.

To state a cause of action for fraud, the plaintiff must allege: (1) misrepresentation by defendant; (2) defendant’s knowledge of falsity (or “scienter”);¿(3) defendant’s intent¿to defraud¿(i.e., induce reliance);¿(4) plaintiff’s justifiable¿reliance;¿and (5) resulting¿damage.¿(See Conroy v. Regents of Univ. of Cal. (2009) 45¿Cal.4th¿1244, 1255.)  

Here, Plaintiff alleges that it invested $4,263,000 to purchase a percentage interest in the subject property. (See FAC, ¶ 29.) Per the parties’ agreement, Plaintiff’s percentage interest would equal the amount of its investment against prior investments made in the subject property. (See ibid.) This percentage interest was allegedly 55%. (See id. at ¶ 34.) Plaintiff then goes on to assert facts to show Defendants’ alleged fraud, their failure to disclose Dr. Tan’s investment in the subject property, and how Defendants used this transaction to personally enrich themselves. (See id. at ¶¶ 30-37, 42-50.)

Despite these allegations, Plaintiff failed to allege any resulting damage from Defendants’ alleged fraud. Simply put, it was promised that Plaintiff would receive a percentage interest if it made an investment. Plaintiff decided to make an investment and accordingly received a 55% interest in the subject property. (See id. at ¶¶ 29, 34.) Therefore, given these allegations, Plaintiff got what was promised – a specified percentage interest in exchange for its multi-million-dollar investment. Furthermore, Plaintiff does not allege that it expected to receive a higher percentage interest, or that its money was used for other non-investment purposes. Therefore, Plaintiff does not allege any resulting damage stemming from Defendants’ alleged fraud. 

In opposition, Plaintiff contends that it sufficiently alleged resulting damage because Defendant Sharp allegedly used its funds to secure a 45% interest in the subject property. (See Opp’n, pp. 7:21-8:12; see also FAC, ¶ 30.) However, considering this and the other allegations of fraud, Plaintiff still fails to clearly allege how Plaintiff was damaged Sharp’s alleged fraud. The Court finds that Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged resulting damage from Defendants’ alleged fraudulent conduct.

Because Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged resulting damage, Plaintiff has failed to state all elements to this cause of action. (See Conroy v. Regents of Univ. of Cal. (2009) 45¿Cal.4th¿1244, 1255.) For this reason, the demurrer to this cause of action is sustained. Because there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment, Plaintiff will be granted leave to amend to provide further specificity as to what resulting damage it suffered due to the alleged fraud.

III.           Violation of Penal Code § 496 (Cause of Action No. 5)

The Court finds that Plaintiff failed to allege that Defendants’ conduct constituted theft. Accordingly, the demurrer to this cause of action is sustained with leave to amend.

To state a cause of action for receiving stolen property,¿the¿plaintiff must allege:¿(1) property was stolen or obtained in a manner constituting¿theft;¿(2)¿defendant knew¿the¿property was stolen or obtained by theft; and (3)¿defendant¿received or had possession of¿the¿stolen¿property.¿(See¿Pen. Code, § 496, subds. (a), (c).)¿In California,¿theft¿occurs when one:¿ 

feloniously steal[s], take[s],¿[carries], lead[s], or drive[s]¿away¿the¿personal property of another, or who shall fraudulently appropriate property which has been entrusted to him or her, or shall knowingly and designedly, by any false or fraudulent representation or pretense, defraud any other person of money, labor or real or personal property, or who causes or procures others to report falsely of his or her wealth or mercantile character and by thus imposing upon any person, obtains credit and¿thereby fraudulently gets or obtains possession of money, or property or obtains¿the¿labor or service of another. . . .¿ 

(Pen. Code, § 484, subd. (a).)¿ 

Theft¿requires that¿the¿defendant act with specific intent to permanently deprive¿the¿owner of¿their property. (See¿People v. Edwards¿(1992) 8 Cal.App.4th 1092, 1099.)¿ 

Here, Plaintiff alleges that Defendants’ committed theft by fraudulently inducing Plaintiff to make its $4,263,000 investment. (See FAC, ¶¶ 58, 60-61.) However, as discussed above, Plaintiff’s fraud claims do not survive demurrer because Plaintiff did not adequately allege resulting damages from Defendants’ alleged conduct. (See § II.) Plaintiff appears to have received what was promised – partial ownership in the subject property in exchange for its investment. (See FAC, ¶¶ 29, 34.) The Court does not find that Plaintiff has sufficiently alleged that Defendants committed theft when Plaintiff received what was promised to it.

For these reasons, Plaintiff has failed to sufficiently allege that Defendants committed theft. (See Pen. Code, § 484, subd. (a).)The demurrer to this cause of action is sustained. Because there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment, Plaintiff is granted leave to amend.

IV.           Removal of Defendants as Tenant in Common Agreement (Cause of Action No. 6)

This cause of action is subject to demurrer because Plaintiff is seeking injunctive relief, which is not an independent cause of action. (See City of South Pasadena v. Department of Transportation (1994) 29 Cal.App.4th 1280, 1293 [“Injunctive relief is a remedy, not a cause of action.”]; see also Shell Oil Co. v. Richter (1942) 52 Cal.App.2d 164, 168 [“Injunctive relief is a remedy and not, in itself, a cause of action, and a cause of action must exist before injunctive relief may be granted.”].)

If Plaintiff succeeds on a separate cause of action that justifies the injunctive relief it seeks, it may request it. However, the sixth cause of action is not a viable cause of action. Accordingly, the demurrer to this cause of action is sustained. To the extent Plaintiff has a basis to allege a valid cause of action that would support the requested injunctive relief as a remedy, Plaintiff may amend the complaint to include it.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint is SUSTAINED.

Plaintiff is granted leave to amend and has twenty days from the Court’s order to file and serve an amended complaint.

Case Number: 26STCV02620    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 316

BACKGROUND

This case is a property dispute. Plaintiff Carlos Comeaux, Trustee of the In Memory of the Hurdie Marshall and Ruth Collins Living Trust requests the Court issue an injunction to postpone the foreclosure sale of the subject property.

On January 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendants FCI Lender Services, Inc. and AGI Logistics Holdings LLC.

On March 4, 2026, Defendants filed a demurrer.

No opposition or reply has been filed.

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Accordingly, judgement is entered in favor of Defendants.

LEGAL STANDARD

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (See Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (See Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)   

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Ibid.; see also Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245).  

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Here, Defendants request the Court take judicial notice of four exhibits: (1) Deed of Trust, dated January 13, 2023, and recorded January 23, 2023, in the Recorder’s Office, Los Angeles County, California as Document No. 20230043969; (2) Notice of Default and Election to Sell Under Deed of Trust, dated July 3, 2025, and recorded July 8, 2025, in the Recorder’s Office, Los Angeles County, California as Document No. 20250455960; (3) Notice of Trustee’s Sale, dated October 9, 2025, and recorded October 14, 2025, in the Recorder’s Office, Los Angeles County, California as Document No. 20250707141; (4) Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale, dated November 21, 2025, and recorded December 1, 2025, in the Recorder’s Office, Los Angeles County, California as Document No. 20250856494.

The document for which judicial notice is sought were not submitted to the Court with any filing and there is no declaration authenticating them. For these reasons, the Court will not take judicial notice of these documents under Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (h).

DISCUSSION

I.                Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer or motion to strike, the demurring party shall meet and confer with the opposing party in person, by telephone, or by video conference. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) The demurring party must file a declaration detailing their meet and confer efforts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(3).)

Here, the parties discussed the demurrer by telephone. (See Wilkinson Decl., ¶ 2.) Thus, the meet and confer requirement has been satisfied. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).)

II.             Plaintiff’s Action is Moot

“A case is moot when the reviewing court cannot provide the parties with practical, effectual relief.” (In re Oliveras (2024) 103 Cal.App.5th 771, 776.) Put another way, “A case becomes moot when events “‘render[ ] it impossible for [a] court, if it should decide the case in favor of plaintiff, to grant him any effect[ive] relief.’”” (In re S.R. (2025) 18 Cal.5th 1042, 1042.)

Plaintiff’s complaint contains only one cause of action, which is for equitable relief by injunction. Plaintiff requests the Court grant an injunction to postpone the foreclosure sale on the subject property and to stop the sale deed from being recorded. (See Compl., ¶ 12, Prayer for Relief, ¶ 1.) However, as acknowledged in the Complaint, the foreclosure sale took place on November 5, 2025, approximately two months before Plaintiff pursued this action. (See id.) Thus, even if the Court ruled in Plaintiff’s favor, the Court would not be able to grant effective relief because the property is in the possession of a new legal owner. (See In re S.R. (2025) 18 Cal.5th 1042, 1042; see also In re Oliveras (2024) 103 Cal.App.5th 771, 776.)

For these reasons, this matter is moot and the demurrer is sustained.

CONCLUSION 

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Judgment is entered in favor of Defendants.
