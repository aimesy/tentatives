Case Number: 24STLC08663    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Western Funding, Inc.’s (1) Motion for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default, Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default, Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted. Defendants Alejandro Ramirez and Si Se Puede Autos, Inc.’s Answer filed on May 13, 2025 is stricken.

 

Order to Show Cause Regarding Entry of Default and Default Judgment is set for 08/10/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Trial set for August 3, 2026 is taken off calendar.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On November 22, 2024, Plaintiff Western Funding, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Alejandro Ramirez (“Defendant Ramirez”) and Si Se Puede Autos, Inc. (“Defendant Si Se Puede Autos”). Defendants filed an Answer on May 13, 2025.

 

On March 2 and 3, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions, against Defendants with respect to service of responses. (Minute Orders, 03/02/26 and 03/03/26.) The Court ordered Defendants to serve verified discovery responses within 20 days’ service of the order. (Ibid.) Notice of the ruling was mailed to Defendants on March 3 and 4, 2026. (Notices of Ruling, filed 03/03/26 and 03/04/26.)

 

On March 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motions for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default, Request for Monetary Sanctions, as to each Defendant. Plaintiff served the Motions on Defendants through Attorney Rami Bata, Esq. by electronic mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff seeks an order striking Defendants’ Answer under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, pursuant to which the Court may impose a terminating sanction by one of the following orders:

 

(1)   An order striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings of any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process.

(2)   An order staying further proceedings by that party until an order for discovery is obeyed.

(3)   An order dismissing the action, or any part of the action, of that party.

(4)   An order rendering a judgment by default against that party.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (d).) Where a party willfully disobeys a discovery order, courts have the discretion to impose terminating, issue, evidence or monetary sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, subds. (d), (g); R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486, 495.) The court should look at the totality of the circumstances in determining whether terminating sanctions are appropriate. (Lang v. Hochman (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 1225, 1246.) Ultimate discovery sanctions are justified where there is a willful discovery order violation, a history of abuse, and evidence showing that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with discovery rules. (Van Sickle v. Gilbert (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1495, 1516.) “[A] penalty as severe as dismissal or default is not authorized where noncompliance with discovery is caused by an inability to comply rather than willfulness or bad faith.” (Brown v. Sup. Ct. (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 701, 707.)

 

As set forth above, on March 2 and 3, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s discovery motions against both Defendants requiring them to serve responses within 20 days’ service of the orders. (Motions, Aphosian Decl., ¶6 and Exh. 2.) Notice of each ruling was served on Defendants on March 3 and 4, 2026. (Ibid.) As of the filing of the instant Motions, however, Defendants have not served responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶5.) 

 

The Court finds terminating sanctions are warranted for Defendants’ non-compliance with the discovery orders. Despite notice of the discovery Motions, the Court’s orders, and the instant Motions for Terminating Sanctions, Defendants have not served responses as ordered nor did Defendants file any opposition to the instant Motions to explain their wholesale failure to participate in discovery and comply with the Court’s orders. Defendants’ persistent refusal to engage in the discovery process without explanation or justification demonstrates that their compliance with the Court’s orders cannot be achieved through lesser sanctions. Although terminating sanctions are a harsh penalty, “[t]he court [is] not required to allow a pattern of abuse to continue ad infinitum.” (Mileikowsky v. Tenet Healthsystem (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 262, 280.) Accordingly, the Court strikes Defendants’ Answer. However, it does not enter their default, which is not an authorized action under the moving statute.

 

Plaintiff also moves for monetary sanctions under subdivision (a) of the moving statute, on account of Defendants’ misuse of the discovery process. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (a).) Failing to respond to an authorized method of discovery, especially in light of the Court’s orders, is a misuse under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.010. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subd. (d).) Plaintiff moves for an award of $ 1,575.77 per motion, which the Court finds excessive given that they are nearly identical and unopposed. (See Motion, Aphosian Decl., ¶8.) The Court awards Plaintiff $1,151.54 based on two hours of attorney time billed at $500.00 per hour, and the filing fees of $75.77 per Motion. (Ibid.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Western Funding, Inc.’s (1) Motion for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default, Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default, Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted. Defendants Alejandro Ramirez and Si Se Puede Autos, Inc.’s Answer filed on May 13, 2025 is stricken.

 

Order to Show Cause Regarding Entry of Default and Default Judgment is set for 08/10/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

Trial set for August 3, 2026 is taken off calendar.

Case Number: 25STLC05887    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Deere & Company’s Motion to Deem Admitted Requests for Admission and Request for Sanctions is granted. Defendant Violeta S. Salinas is ordered to pay sanctions of $485.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Deere & Company (“Plaintiff”) served Requests for Admissions, Set One, on Defendant Violeta S. Salinas on April 3, 2026. (Motion, Frueh Decl., Exhs. 1-2.) As of the filing of the instant Motion to Deem Admitted Requests for Admission and Request for Sanctions on May 20, 2026, Defendant has not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶6.) Plaintiff now moves for an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280. Plaintiff served the Motion on Defendant by first-class mail on the same day it was filed, but no opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to deem requests for admission can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Further, the motion can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to serve responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.) Due to Defendant’s failure to timely serve verified responses and resultant waiver of objections, Plaintiff is entitled to an order deeming the Requests for Admission admitted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280.)

 

The Court is required to award sanctions under the moving statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).) However, the amount sought is excessive for an unopposed motion to deem admissions admitted. Sanctions are granted against Defendant in the amount of $485.00 based on one hour of attorney time billed at $425.00 per hour, plus the $60.00 filing fee. (Motion, Frueh Decl., ¶¶7-8.)

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Deere & Company’s Motion to Deem Admitted Requests for Admission and Request for Sanctions is granted. Defendant Violeta S. Salinas is ordered to pay sanctions of $485.00 to Plaintiff’s counsel within 20 days’ service of this order.

Case Number: 26STCP01260    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner Grace Lee’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is Granted. Judgment is to be entered in the amount of $30,785.60.

 

The proposed order filed on April 2, 2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On March 9, 2026, Petitioner Grace Lee (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award against Respondent Temply Technologies, Inc. (“Respondent”). Petitioner filed a Proof of Personal Service on May 26, 2026. No response to the Petition has been filed to date.

 

Legal Standard

 

“Any party to an arbitration in which an award has been made may petition the court to confirm, correct or vacate the award.  The petition shall name as respondent all parties to the arbitration and may name as respondents any other persons bound by the arbitration award.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.) “If a petition or response under this chapter is duly served and filed, the court shall confirm the award as made, whether rendered in this state or another state, unless in accordance with this chapter it corrects the award and confirms it as corrected, vacates the award or dismisses the proceeding.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1286.)

 

A response to a petition to confirm an arbitration award, which seeks to vacate or correct the award, must be served and filed no later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the respondent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288.2.)

 

Discussion

 

Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing

 

Code of Civil Procedure, section 1290.4 requires that the petition and notice of hearing be served on Respondent “in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice” or “[i]f the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made . . . [s]ervice within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4, subds. (a), (b).)

 

The arbitration agreement is attached to the Petition, but does not provide for the manner of service for a petition to confirm an arbitration award. (Pet., Attachment 4b, ¶¶3(g).) Therefore, the Petition and Notice of Hearing must be served in the same manner as a summons. Petitioner filed a Proof of Personal Service of the Petition and Notice of Hearing demonstrating that the papers were served to Respondent’s agent on May 12, 2026. (Proof of Personal Service, filed 05/26/26, ¶¶3-5.) This satisfies the service requirement of Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4.

 

Service of the Arbitration Award (CCP § 1283.6)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1283.6 requires that “[t]he neutral arbitrator shall serve a signed copy of the award on each party to the arbitration personally or by registered or certified mail or as provided in the agreement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1283.6.) This requirement may be satisfied by service by the arbitrator, or upon proper service of the award with the petition. (See Murray v. Civil Service Employees Ins. Co. (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 796, 799-800.) The arbitration award was served with the instant Petition. (Pet., Attachment 8(c).)

 

Confirmation of the Arbitration Award

 

An arbitration award is not directly enforceable until it is confirmed by a court and judgment is entered.  (Code Civ. Proc. § 1287.6; Jones v. Kvistad (1971) 19 Cal.App.3d 836, 840.) The court must confirm the award as made, unless it corrects or vacates the award, or dismisses the proceeding.  (CCP § 1286; Valsan Partners Limited Partnership v. Calcor Space Facility, Inc. (1994) 25 Cal.App.4th 809, 818.) Code of Civil Procedure, section 1285.4 states a petition under this chapter shall:

 

a)     Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

b)     Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

c)     Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4.) The Petition complies with these requirements. It sets forth the nature of the arbitration agreement, the name of the arbitrator, and attaches a copy of the arbitration award. (Pet., ¶¶4-6 and Attachment 8(c).) The arbitrator awarded Petitioner $30,785.60, comprising $26,185.60 principal, $3,200.00 administrative fees and expenses, and $1,400.00 arbitrator compensation. (Id. at Attachment 8(c), p. 2.) Therefore, the arbitration award is confirmed in favor of Petitioner in that amount.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner Grace Lee’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is Granted. Judgment is to be entered in the amount of $30,785.60.

 

The proposed order filed on April 2, 2026 is signed and entered this date.
