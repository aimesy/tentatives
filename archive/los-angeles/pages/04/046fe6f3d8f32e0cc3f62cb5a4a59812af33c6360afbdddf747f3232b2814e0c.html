21SMCV00260 (Mcalister v. Deutsch)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            Michael Mcalister (Plaintiff or Judgment Creditor) holds a monetary judgment against Defendants Lawrence Deutsch (Lawrence) and Jacob Deutsch (Jacob) (collectively, “Defendants” or “Judgment Debtors”), which remains unsatisfied.

                       

INSTANT MOTION

 

             On February 18, 2026, non-party Ashley Deutsch (Ms. Deutsch) filed the instant motion to quash service of the order to appear for examination or for a protective order. Plaintiff filed an opposition, and Ms. Deutsch filed a reply.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Upon ex parte application by a judgment creditor who has a money judgment and proof by the judgment creditor by affidavit or otherwise to the satisfaction of the proper court that a third person has possession or control of property in which the judgment debtor has an interest or is indebted to the judgment debtor in an amount exceeding two hundred fifty dollars ($250), the court shall make an order directing the third person to appear before the court, or before a referee appointed by the court, at a time and place specified in the order, to answer concerning such property or debt. The affidavit in support of the judgment creditor's application may be based on the affiant's information and belief.

(b) Not less than 10 days prior to the date set for the examination, a copy of the order shall be:

(1) Served personally on the third person.

(2) Served personally or by mail on the judgment debtor.

…

Code Civ. Proc., § 708.120.)

            Ms. Deutsch’s motion to quash service of the order to appear for examination is GRANTED on the basis that Plaintiff has not shown that the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 708.120 have been satisfied.

            Code of Civil Procedure section 708.120 requires the creditor to establish that the third person possesses or controls property in which the judgment debtor has an interest, or owes the judgment debtor more than $250. Plaintiff offers no evidence of either circumstance. Nor does Plaintiff address either that Ms. Deutsch married the judgment debtor Jacob only recently or the premarital agreement submitted with the motion, which establishes that Ms. Deutsch and Jacob maintain separate property. Plaintiff identifies no asset of Jacob in Ms. Deutsch’s possession or control and no debt she owes him. And Plaintiff cites no authority that Ms. Deutsch’s marriage to Jacob is, by itself and in the face of her sworn declaration, sufficient to satisfy section 708.120.

            In light of the above, the Court need not consider the arguments concerning service.

            Moving party’s counsel to give notice.

25SMCV00859 (Armstrong v. Yoo, et. al.)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            On February 18, 2025, plaintiff Christopher Armstrong (Plaintiff) filed his complaint. On July 7, 2025, Plaintiff filed his first amended complaint (FAC) against Tomas Soongmin Yoo (Yoo), Jenny Jechart (Jechart), and Does 1-25, alleging eleven causes of action. Plaintiff alleges that Yoo acted as a financial advisor to Plaintiff and that Yoo’s aunt, Jechart, is also a financial advisor who manages Yoo’s trust and other assets and colluded with Yoo. (FAC ¶ 8-10.)

                       

INSTANT MOTION

 

            On June 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motions to compel Jechart’s further responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One (Nos. 5, 6, 7, 41, 43, 53, and 66) and Requests for Production, Set One (Nos. 7, 12, 18, and 28).  

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

            The Court finds that Plaintiff has satisfied the meet and confer requirement. (See Cantor Decl. ¶ 5, 7, 9, 15.)

 

DISCUSSION

 

            Here, Plaintiff seeks further responses to the identified discovery served on December 30, 2025 [Special Interrogatories] and November 18, 2025 [Requests for Production] on the basis that, despite three rounds of supplemental responses, Jechart’s responses remain deficient and not code-compliant. (Cantor Decl. ¶ 2-10.)

 

            In opposition, Jechart argues that Plaintiff’s motion should be denied because the responses are sufficient, but Jackart does not address each discovery request individually. Jackart also contends that Plaintiff’s motion is built on inconsistent meet-and-confer correspondence that does not accurately track the actual discovery served. But the Court is not persuaded and finds that the Cantor declaration satisfies the meet-and-confer requirement.

 

Special Interrogatories

 

            The Court notes that Plaintiff’s separate statement does not fully comply with the requirements of California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345, which requires Plaintiff to set forth “(1) The text of the request, interrogatory, question, or inspection demand; [and] (2) The text of each response, answer, or objection, and any further responses or answers…” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.13.345, subd. (c)(1)-(2).) Plaintiff’s separate statement at times summarizes the requests and responses rather than providing the actual text. (See Plaintiff’s Separate Statement.)

 

 

            Additionally, the Court notes that Plaintiff has failed to provide the Court with Jechart’s initial responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One. The Cantor Declaration provides the supplemental responses, but not the initial responses. (See Cantor Decl. Exhibits.) Also, since Plaintiff failed to provide the exact requests and responses verbatim in the separate statement, the Court cannot adequately determine whether the responses are deficient. The meet-and-confer letters also appear to indicate that the initial responses included objections, but, again, the initial responses have not been provided to the Court.

 

            Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One, is DENIED on procedural grounds. Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is DENIED.  

         

 

Requests for Production

            The Court notes that the separate statement provided by Plaintiff appears to concern a different case. Accordingly, Plaintiff failed to provide a separate statement as required by California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345.

            Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to requests for production set one is DENIED on procedural grounds. Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is DENIED.  


Case Number: 25SMCV03476    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: O

TENTATIVE RULING

July 28, 2026

25SMCV03476 (Shashoua v. State Farm General Insurance Company, et. al.)

 

RELEVANT BACKGROUND

 

            On July 18, 2025, plaintiff Elisha Shshoua (Plaintiff) filed the initial complaint against State Farm General Insurance Company (State Farm) and Does 1-50. On September 15, 2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC) alleging two causes of action: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of duty of good faith and fair dealing.

 

            Plaintiff alleges a theft/burglary occurred on November 20, 2024, causing unspecified damage to his property. (FAC, ¶ 9.) Plaintiff has an insurance policy with State Farm and reported the claim to State Farm. (FAC, ¶¶ 10-11; Exh. A [Policy].) Plaintiff provided documents to State Farm including a police report, itemized inventory, pre-loss photographs, receipts, content collaboration inventory, ESX estimate, and a professional flooring report. (FAC, ¶¶ 14, 17.) Plaintiff alleges State Farm engaged in a prolonged search for a replacement tile to match Plaintiff's existing flooring. (FAC, ¶ 15.) Plaintiff alleges that State Farm failed to approve payment for "the full replacement of the tile flooring" but instead searched for tile to match the flooring. (FAC, ¶¶ 15, 18.)

                       

INSTANT MOTION

 

             On August 1, 2025, State Farm filed the instant demurrer to the second cause of action alleged in the FAC for breach of the duty of good faith and fair dealing, as well as a motion to strike. Plaintiff filed an opposition and State Farm filed a reply.

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

             The Court finds that State Farm has satisfied the meet and confer requirement. (Gargalis Decl., ¶¶ 3-5.)

 

DISCUSSION

 

I.       DEMURRER

 

A.    Second Cause of Action – Breach of Good Faith and Fair Dealing

 

            “A breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing involves something beyond breach of the contractual duty itself and it has been held that bad faith implies unfair dealing rather than mistaken judgment.” (Careau & Co. v. Security Pacific Business Credit, Inc. (1990) 222 Cal.App.3d 1371, 1394.) “If the allegations do not go beyond the statement of a mere contract breach and, relying on the same alleged acts, simply seek the same damages or other relief already claimed in a companion contract cause of action, they may be disregarded as superfluous as no additional claim is actually stated … [T]he only justification for asserting a separate cause of action for breach of the implied covenant is to obtain a tort recovery.” (Id. at pp. 1394-1395.) To recover in tort for breach of the implied covenant, the defendant must “have acted unreasonably or without proper cause.” (Id. at p. 1395 [citations and italics omitted].)

 

            In the insurance context, a plaintiff must establish that the "insurer failed or refused to discharge its contractual duties not because of an honest mistake, bad judgment or negligence, but rather a conscious and deliberate act, which unfairly frustrates the agreed common purposes and disappoints the reasonable expectations of the other party thereby depriving that party of the benefits of the agreement." (Century Surety Co. v. Polisso (2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 922, 948-949; see also Wilson v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2007) 42 Cal.4th 713, 720; and Chateau Chamberay Homeowners Assn. v. Associated Internat. Ins. Co. (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 335, 347.)

 

            Here, State Farm argues that the second cause of action for Breach of Duty of Good Faith and Fair Dealing fails to state facts sufficient to amount to a cause of action because Plaintiff has not alleged facts to show unreasonable conduct by State Farm. State Farm contends that California law is clear than an "insurer's erroneous failure to pay benefits under a policy does not necessarily constitute bad faith entitling the insured to recover tort damage,” citing Opsal v. United Services Auto. Assn. (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 1197, 1205 and Century, 139 Cal.App.4th at pp. 948-949. State Farm correctly asserts that Plaintiff must plead facts demonstrating that State Farm acted unreasonably in denying benefits to him and that the test of bad faith liability is "whether the refusal to pay policy benefits was unreasonable.” (Opsal, 2 Cal.App.4th at p. 1205.)

 

            In opposition, Plaintiff argues that “the FAC alleges a systematic pattern of unreasonable claims handling, including repeated requests for the same documents, dilatory tactics spanning nearly eight months, a prolonged and fruitless search for replacement tile despite knowledge that no matching tile existed, and deliberate refusal to pay benefits the insurer knew were due under the policy.” (Opp at 1:7-12.) In support, Plaintiff points to paragraphs 9, and 14-20 in the FAC.

 

            Plaintiff argues State Farm's conduct in seeking replacement tiles was unreasonable because "it knew it would not be able to do" so and it had "full knowledge that no such tile was available". (Opp., p. 7:2-8, FAC ¶ 18.) But, as State Farm points out in reply, there are no facts alleged to support this conclusion. Additionally, while Plaintiff generally alleges that there were duplicative demands from State Farm (Id ¶ 14, 18), Plaintiff does not identify any such duplicative demands or allege any facts to show that any request was unreasonable. Moreover, Plaintiff argues bad faith lies where the insurer withholds benefits without any reasonable basis, but the allegations in the FAC regarding State Farm’s search for replacement tiles do not establish unreasonableness. Lastly, Plaintiff assertions that State Farm deliberately stalled the process (Id. ¶ 17) is conclusory and lacks supporting facts.

 

            But the Court finds that it is reasonably possible that Plaintiff can cure the defects through amendment.

 

            Accordingly, State Farm’s demurrer to the second cause of action is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

II.    MOTION TO STRIKE

 

            Based on the foregoing, State Farm’s motion to strike is also GRANTED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.

            Plaintiff may have thirty (30) days in which to file and serve an amended complaint.
