Matter: Demurrer

Moving Party: Alexander Habrich and 3761444 Canada, Inc.

Responding Party: Spinning Gold, LLC, Bogart, and Martins 

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Demurrer is overruled.


The Motion to Strike is denied.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is an entertainment dispute. 

The trial date is September 21, 2026.

On June 15, 2026, Alexander Habrich and 3761444 Canada, Inc. filed the operative Third Amended Cross-Complaint (TACC).

On July 22, 2026, Cross-Defendants Spinning Gold, LLC, Timothy Scott Bogart, and Jessica Martins filed their Answer to the TACC.

Habrich and 3761444 Canada, Inc. now demur to the Answer to the TACC.

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, and “treat the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.)  “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”  (Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  It is error “to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if the plaintiff shows there is a reasonable possibility any defect identified by the defendant can be cured by amendment.”  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist.¿(1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

Cross-Complainants contend that all eight challenged defenses are "sham" pleadings that fail as a matter of law based on two core theories: (1) Cross-Defendant Bogart’s December 10, 2020 declaration constitutes binding "judicial admissions," and (2) the Court’s January 29, 2021 preliminary injunction order (stipulated as permanent on September 24, 2025) constitutes the "law of the case" establishing that no foreclosure occurred and that the agreements were invalid. Both arguments fail as a matter of law. 

Under settled California law, a judicial admission is a formal, conclusive concession made in an operative pleading or formal stipulation that entirely removes a fact from issue (Walker v. Dorn (1966) 240 Cal.App.2d 118, 120; Weiss v. Baba (1963) 218 Cal.App.2d 45, 50).

Pretrial witness declarations offered in support of interlocutory motions (such as preliminary injunction applications) constitute evidentiary admissions, not judicial admissions (Walker, supra, 240 Cal.App.2d at pp. 120–121). Statements made in Bogart's 2020 declaration may be used by Cross-Complainants as evidence or for impeachment at trial, but they do not conclusively bind Cross-Defendants as a matter of pleading law or render subsequent affirmative defenses sham on demurrer.

The granting or denial of a preliminary injunction does not amount to an adjudication of the ultimate rights in controversy (Weingand v. Atlantic Savings & Loan Assn. (1970) 1 Cal.3d 806, 820; Continental Baking Co. v. Katz (1968) 68 Cal.2d 512, 528).

 The "law of the case" doctrine applies exclusively to legal determinations made by an appellate court in a prior appeal or writ proceeding governing subsequent trial court proceedings (Kowis v. Howard (1992) 3 Cal.4th 888, 892–895). It does not apply to interlocutory rulings made by a trial court.

The parties’ September 24, 2025 stipulation converting the preliminary injunction to a permanent injunction resolved specific prospective injunctive relief regarding UCC filings and status quo preservation. It did not resolve all underlying factual disputes regarding performance, breach, interference, or monetary offset.

Once the evidentiary assertions and interlocutory rulings are properly evaluated under the standard of review, each challenged defense presents triable issues of historical fact. Therefore, the Demurrer is overruled. The RJN is granted.

For the same reasons, the Motion to Strike is denied.

Moving party to give notice.

On April 16, 206, Plaintiff Alcira De Leon filed the operative Complaint for (1) Government Code Liability, (2) premises liability, and (3) negligence. Plaintiff alleges that “On or about June 19, 2024, Plaintiff, tripped and fell on into an open electrical box located in front of or near the Subject Location. The walking surface was uneven, open, and deformed and as a direct result Plaintiff tripped, fell into, and sustained serious injury to her body.” The “Subject Location” is defined as the “sidewalk, walkway, parkway, and surrounding areas located in front of or near 426 S Alvarado Street, Los Angeles, CA 90057.”

Defendant Dollar Big Bargain, Inc. now demurs to the second and third causes of action for uncertainty and failure to state sufficient facts. Defendant argues that Plaintiff “does not allege that DOLLAR is a public entity. So the claims regarding an uneven sidewalk and open utility box are simply not actionable against DOLLAR. As a matter of law, DOLLAR owed no duty to plaintiff as to any of the alleged dangerous conditions under these circumstances.” 

Because the Demurrer is unopposed, the Complaint is deemed abandoned. (Herzberg v. County of Plumas (2005) 133 Cal.App.4th 1, 20.)

The Demurrer is sustained. The Court is inclined to deny leave to amend.

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 26STCV14072    Hearing Date: September 9, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: September 9, 2026

Case Name: Franceschi, Jr. v. Wedge Office, TRS, LLC, et al.

Case No.: 26STCV14072 

Matter: Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

Moving Party: Defendants Wedge Office, TRS, LLC and Wedge Office, LLC

Responding Party: Plaintiff Ernest Franceschi, Jr.

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is granted, without leave 

to amend.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On May 1, 2026, Plaintiff Ernest Franceschi, Jr., a pro per attorney litigant, filed the operative Complaint for (1) intentional interference with easement in gross, (2) intentional interference with contract, (3) assault and IIED, and (4) civil harassment restraining order and/or injunctive relief.

Plaintiff alleges that security personnel acting on behalf of property management and Allied Universal Security Services harassed and threatened him while he was accessing the LA Fitness facility at the Water's Edge commercial complex. The dispute arose after security guards confronted Plaintiff for feeding wild ducks near a decorative outdoor pond, threatened to call the police for trespassing, and blew away the duck food with a leaf blower.

Defendants Wedge Office, TRS, LLC and Wedge Office, LLC now assert a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings as to the Complaint.

“A defendant may move for judgment on the pleadings on the ground that the complaint does not state facts sufficient to state a cause of action against that defendant. A motion for judgment on the pleadings ‘is equivalent to a demurrer . . . .’ Leave to amend ‘is properly denied if the facts and nature of plaintiffs’ claims are clear and under the substantive law, no liability exists.’ ”  (Templo v. State¿(2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 730, 735.)

Defendants primarily argue that they cannot be liable for the actions of security personnel employed by Allied/GSG Protective Services.

The Complaint indicates that “Defendants WEDGE OFFICE, TRS, LLC and or WEDGE OFFICE, LLC . . . contracted with or otherwise retained GSG to provide security services at the WATER’S EDGE facility.”

A hirer of an independent contractor is generally not vicariously liable for the torts of the contractor committed against third parties (Gipson v. Davis Realty Co. (1963) 215 Cal.App.2d 190, 202; Rest.2d Torts § 409). To hold the hiring property owner directly liable, the plaintiff must plead that the owner retained control over the operational details of the work or specifically commanded/authorized the tortious act (Rest.2d Agency § 212; C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School Dist. (2012) 53 Cal.4th 861, 877).

Here, the Complaint fails to allege facts demonstrating that the Wedge Defendants exercised control over the detailed manner and means of the security guards' work. Furthermore, Plaintiff's theory of ratification fails as a matter of law because a principal cannot ratify the acts of an actor unless the actor purported to act on behalf of that principal (Emery v. Visa Internat. Service Assn. (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 952, 961). Plaintiff expressly alleges that the individual security guards acted in the course and scope of their employment with Allied/GSG, and the cease-and-desist letter was addressed solely to Allied. Because no facts establish direct wrongful acts by the Wedge Defendants or a basis to impute the security guards' conduct to them, all causes of action against the Wedge Defendants fail on this threshold ground.

Additionally, the subject causes of action individually fail for the reasons indicated below.


  1. Easement

Plaintiff alleges that his gym membership agreement with LA Fitness confers upon him a personal "easement in gross" over the common areas of the commercial property (Water's Edge), including the open areas surrounding the decorative pond. Plaintiff further alleges that feeding wildlife/ducks is an activity incident to this easement.

As a matter of law, a commercial gym membership or lease license does not create an easement interest in real property. A gym member possesses at most a revocable license to use the designated facilities, not a property interest in the landlord's real estate. Furthermore, even if an easement for ingress/egress existed, feeding wildlife contrary to property rules is not within the scope of an easement. 

The Motion to the first cause of action is granted.


  1. Interference with Contract

To prevail on a cause of action for intentional interference with contractual relations, a plaintiff must plead and prove (1) the existence of a valid contract between the plaintiff and a third party; (2) the defendant's knowledge of that contract; (3) the defendant's intentional acts designed to induce a breach or disruption of the contractual relationship; (4) actual breach or disruption of the contractual relationship; and (5) resulting damage.  (Reeves v. Hanlon (2004) 33 Cal.4th 1140, 1148.)

The Complaint fails to allege facts demonstrating that Defendants intended to disrupt Plaintiff's contract with LA Fitness, or that Plaintiff was prevented from using the LA Fitness gym facilities. Enforcement of general property rules on private commercial land does not constitute actionable contractual interference absent specific facts showing intentional disruption of the membership contract. 

The Motion to the second cause of action is granted.


  1. Assault 

The essential elements of a cause of action for assault are: (1) defendant acted with intent to cause harmful or offensive contact, or threatened to touch plaintiff in a harmful or offensive manner; (2) plaintiff reasonably believed she was about to be touched in a harmful or offensive manner or it reasonably appeared to plaintiff that defendant was about to carry out the threat; (3) plaintiff did not consent to defendant's conduct; (4) plaintiff was harmed; and (5) defendant's conduct was a substantial factor in causing plaintiff's harm. (CACI No. 1301.) 

Plaintiff’s mere allegation that Defendant Cruz approached him holding a broomstick "so as to suggest that he would strike Plaintiff" while concurrently threatening to report him for trespassing is insufficient. Pleading an actionable assault requires factual allegations of an overt physical gesture demonstrating an imminent threat—such as raising the weapon, brandishing it aggressively, or advancing menacingly toward the plaintiff—rather than passive possession of an object paired with verbal warnings regarding property rules. Because the Complaint alleges ambiguous conduct and verbal threats of calling the police rather than an overt act threatening immediate unlawful force, the pleading is legally insufficient.

The Motion is granted as to the assault claim.


  1. IIED

The elements of the tort of IIED are: “(1) extreme and outrageous conduct by the defendant with the intention of causing, or reckless disregard of the probability of causing, emotional distress; (2) the plaintiff’s suffering severe or extreme emotional distress; and (3) actual and proximate causation of the emotional distress by the defendant’s outrageous conduct. . . . Conduct to be outrageous must be so extreme as to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized community.”  (Christensen v. Superior Court (1991) 54 Cal.3d 868, 903, internal quotations omitted.)  Liability does not extend to mere insults, indignities, threats, annoyances, petty oppressions, or other trivialities.  (Molko v. Holy Spirit Assn. (1986) 46 Cal.3d 1092, 1122.)  

Here, Plaintiff fails to allege “extreme and outrageous” conduct. Therefore, the Motion is granted as to the IIED claim.


  1. Restraining Order & Injunctive Relief

Code of Civil Procedure § 527.6 is a specialized statutory procedure intended to protect individuals from harassment by another natural person. It is not a standalone cause of action to be asserted against a corporate entity or employer in a general civil complaint.

Insofar as injunctive relief is sought as a remedy, an injunction is a remedy, not an independent cause of action. 

The Motion is granted as to the fourth cause of action.

In sum, the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is granted. The Court is inclined to deny leave to amend.

Moving party to give notice.
