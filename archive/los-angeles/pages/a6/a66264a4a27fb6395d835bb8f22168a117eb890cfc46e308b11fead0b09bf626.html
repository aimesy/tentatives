I.        DEMURRER
— OVERRULED

 

Defendants demur to ninth cause of action for
fraud in the SAC pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subd. (e).

 

The Court
finds that Plaintiff’s fraud claim in the SAC is now sufficiently pled. “‘The
elements of fraud ... are: (a) misrepresentation (false representation,
concealment, or nondisclosure); (b) knowledge of falsity (or “scienter”); (c)
intent to defraud, i.e., to induce reliance; (d) justifiable reliance; and (e)
resulting damage.’” (Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024)
17 Cal.5th 1, 32, quoting Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th
631, 638 (Lazar).) Every element of fraud must be pled with
particularity. (Hills Transportation Co. v. Southwest Forest Ind., Inc. (1968)
266 Cal.App.2d 702, 707.) "The specificity requirement means a
plaintiff must allege facts showing how, when, where, to whom, and by what
means the representations were made, and, in the case of a corporate defendant,
the plaintiff must allege the names of the persons who made the
representations, their authority to speak on behalf of the corporation, to whom
they spoke, what they said or wrote, and when the representation was
made." (Lazar, supra, 12 Cal.4th at p.
645; West v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 780,
793.)

 

Here,
Plaintiff alleges that from May 2025 to the present, Defendants (acting through
Silver and other managing agents including William Lopez, Cabrera, and Steffgen)
made various affirmative false misrepresentations related to the Property’s safety
and habitability as well as Defendants’ efforts to remediate various code
violations identified by the City. (SAC, at p. 28 ¶ 163, p. 31 ¶ 160, pp. 33-34
¶¶ 162-165, p. 36 ¶ 175.) In the SAC, Plaintiff has now more clearly identified
the specific instances and circumstances of Defendants’ fraudulent conduct, including
the following false representations and omissions:

 

·        
On
5/19/2025, Silver and Cabrera spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresented
that Defendants intended to resolve the infestations, trash, and other
habitability violations cited by the City and that Defendants would make
necessary repairs. (SAC, at p. 29 ¶ 163(a).)

·        
On
6/2/2025, Silver spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresented that the City
had “cleared all violations,” that the Property was deemed “free from
violations” such that Plaintiff was no longer entitled to rent reductions, and that
if Plaintiff failed to immediately pay overdue rent he would be evicted. (Id.
at pp. 31-32 ¶ 161(a)-(b).)

·        
On
6/30/2025, Silver spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresents that Defendants
had renewed their annual registration with the City and that Defendants had the
right to continue collecting rent from tenants under the renewed registration. (Id.
at p. 32 ¶ 161(c).)

·        
On
7/22/2025, Silver and Cabrera spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresented that
issues related to heat, hot water, and water service were “not occurring” on
the Property and that other tenants were not experiencing similar issues. (Id.
at p. 29 ¶ 163(b).)

·        
On
9/24/2025, Silver and Cabrera spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresented
that Defendants had “completed” repairs to the Property’s heat, hot water, and
water service. (Id. at p. 29 ¶ 163(c).)

·        
On
9/25/2025, Steffgen spoke to Plaintiff via telephone conversation and
subsequent emails and misrepresents that Defendants had obtained their
registration certificate from the City. (Id. at p. 32 ¶ 162.)

·        
On
10/23/2025, Silver and Cabrera misrepresent to Plaintiff Defendants intended to
perform mandated repairs on the Property to cure the Property’s habitability
violations. (Id. at p. 30 ¶ 163(d).)

·        
On
10/27/2025, Steffgen spoke to Plaintiff via telephone conversation and
subsequent emails and misrepresents that Defendants had obtained their
registration certification from the City. (Id. at p. 33 ¶ 163.)

·        
On
11/25/2025, Silver and Cabrera spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresented that
Defendants were making “reasonable efforts” to comply with the City’s orders. (Id.
at p. 30 ¶ 163(e).)

·        
On
1/20/2025, Steffgen spoke to Plaintiff via telephone conversation and
subsequent emails and misrepresents that Defendants had obtained their
registration certificate from the City. (Id. at p. 34 ¶ 164.)

·        
3/19/2026:
Silver spoke to Plaintiff in person and misrepresents that the City had
“cleared” all violations on the Property and that the City had “found no
evidence of mold” in Plaintiff’s Unit. (Id. at p. 33 ¶ 161 (e)-(f).)

·        
On
4/9/2026, Silver communicated with Plaintiff via email and misrepresents that
he had inspected Plaintiff’s Unit and did not detect mold. (Id. at p. 34
¶ 166.)

·        
On
4/16/2026, Steffgen spoke to Plaintiff via telephone conversation and
subsequent emails and misrepresents that Defendants had obtained their
registration certificate from the City. (Id. at p. 34 ¶ 165.)

·        
On
6/5/2026, Silver fails to disclose to Plaintiff that the Property was in danger
of structural collapse due to Defendants’ failure to perform repairs, but
Plaintiff learns from City Inspector Diaz about the status of the Unit’s
imminent ceiling collapse. (Id. at p. 35 ¶ 167.)

 

These amended
allegations provide sufficient factual specificity to provide adequate notice
to Defendants of the nature and source of the alleged fraud and the scope of
Defendants’ potential liability for fraud. For example, Plaintiff now
identifies the specific dates, speakers, and means of communication for each
representation attributed to Defendants. The SAC adequately identifies how,
when, where, and by what means the false representations were made. (See Lazar,
supra, 12 Cal.4th at p. 645.) Plaintiff also differentiates the
specific fraudulent conduct by Silver as an individual compared to the
representatives of West Sunset at large. While Defendants argue that the
above-identified representations are largely representations of future
conditions, rather than past or then-existing facts, most of the alleged
fraudulent statements relate to the Property’s then-existing status (including
whether repairs had been performed, whether the Property had active
habitability violations, whether the Property had complied with the City
orders, and whether Defendants had complied with City registration requirements).

 

Further, while
Defendants argue that Plaintiff has not alleged specific facts showing that he
justifiably relied on any particular representation by Defendants, Plaintiff
alleges that he was “forced to rely” on Defendants’ representations because he
paid Defendants’ excess rent demands in response to Defendants’ threats of
eviction and was delayed in his efforts to recover from Defendants. (SAC, at pp.
32-33 ¶ 161(b), (e); p. 36 ¶ 172.) These allegations specifically identify
which actions Plaintiff took (paying excess rent sums) and refrained from
taking (pursuing litigation earlier) in reliance on Defendants’ representations
about the status and conditions of the Property. Whether Plaintiff’s reliance
on Defendants’ representations was, in fact, reasonable under the circumstances
is a factual question that is not appropriate to resolve on the pleadings.

 

Ultimately,
the Court finds that Plaintiff has now alleged sufficient facts to resolve the prior
uncertainty in the FAC and to state a viable claim for fraud against
Defendants. Therefore, Defendants’ demurrer to Plaintiff’s seventh cause of
action for fraud is OVERRULED.

 

II.       MOTION
TO STRIKE – DENIED

 

Defendants
move to strike Plaintiff’s request for punitive damages from the SAC. (SAC, ¶¶ 1, 88, 125, 140, 158, 174-175;
Prayer, ¶ 4.) The Court finds, however, that the SAC has adequately resolved
the previously identified defects in Plaintiff’s punitive damages request. Not only has Plaintiff now satisfied the
requirements for pleading fraud, but Plaintiff also alleges that both
Defendants “acted with full knowledge” of and in “reckless disregard” for the
consequences of their conduct and the harm to Plaintiff, which together are sufficient
to demonstrate the requisite scienter for punitive damages. (Id. ¶¶ 125, 140.) Moreover, Plaintiff
also identifies that Silver, acting in his capacity as both an individual and
as a managing agent on behalf of West Sunset, knowingly acted with oppression,
fraud, and malice. (Id. ¶ 140.) Plaintiff’s new
allegations resolve the prior group pleading concerns and adequately
differentiate the conduct of Silver and West Sunset. When read in the context
of the SAC as a whole, the Court finds that Plaintiff’s new allegations are sufficient
to state a request for punitive damages at the pleadings stage. (See Perkins
v. Sup. Ct. (1981) 117 Cal.App.3d 1, 6 [“[t]he stricken language must be
read not in isolation, but in the context of the facts alleged in the rest of
petitioner’s complaint.”].)

 

Therefore,
Defendants’ motion to strike the request for punitive damages is DENIED.
