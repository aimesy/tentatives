PLAINTIFF EMELY BRIANA CASILLAS’S MOTION TO COMPEL NONPARTY TRUKSPECT, INC. TO COMPLY WITH DEPOSITION SUBPOENA FOR PRODUCTION OF BUSINESS RECORDS

 

Motion filed on April 17, 2026.
MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Emely Briana Casillas
RESPONDING PARTY: None
NOTICE: Insufficient as to nonparty Trukspect, Inc.
RELIEF REQUESTED: Order compelling nonparty Trukspect, Inc. to produce business records responsive to Plaintiff’s deposition subpoena and monetary sanctions.
RULING: Denied.

 

DISCUSSION

A party seeking discovery from a nonparty may obtain discovery by deposition subpoena for production of business records. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.010.) If a deponent fails to produce documents specified in a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move for an order compelling production. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (a).) The motion “shall be made no later than 60 days after the completion of the record of the deposition.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (b).) For a business-records subpoena, the nonparty must comply or not comply on the date specified for production. The subpoenaing party then has 60 days to meet and confer and, if still unsatisfied, move to compel. (Board of Registered Nursing v. Superior Court (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 1011, 1033-1035.) Meet and confer efforts do not extend the mandatory 60-day deadline. (Id. at p. 1034.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, subdivision (a) permits the Court, on a motion reasonably made, to direct compliance with a subpoena, including a deposition subpoena, on terms the Court declares appropriate. In making an order under section 1987.1, the Court may award reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, if the Court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification, or that one or more requirements of the subpoena were oppressive. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2, subd. (a).)

 

A “written notice and all moving papers supporting a motion to compel an answer to a deposition question or to compel production of a document or tangible thing from a nonparty deponent must be personally served on the nonparty deponent unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail¿or electronic service¿at an address¿or electronic service address¿specified on the deposition record.”¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1346.)¿

 

Here, Plaintiff personally served nonparty Trukspect with a deposition subpoena for production of business records on January 26, 2026. The subpoena required production on February 11, 2026. (Oknyansky Decl. ¶¶ 3-4, Ex. 1.) Plaintiff states that Trukspect did not produce records, serve objections, move to quash, or communicate with Plaintiff by the production date. (Oknyansky Decl. ¶¶ 6-9.) The record was complete no later than February 11, 2026 because Trukspect had either complied with the subpoena or it had not. Plaintiff therefore had until April 13, 2026, after extension for the weekend, to file this motion. Plaintiff filed the motion on April 17, 2026. The motion is therefore untimely.

 

Separately, Plaintiff has not filed a proof of service showing that the notice of motion and moving papers were personally served on Trukspect, or that Trukspect agreed to accept service by mail or electronic service under rule 3.1346. The proof of service shows electronic service on Defendants’ counsel only.

 

Because the motion is denied, Plaintiff is not entitled to sanctions. Even if properly considered, the sanctions request is not properly noticed. Plaintiff’s notice seeks sanctions “against Defendant or its counsel,” not Trukspect, while the memorandum requests sanctions against Trukspect and inconsistently refers to $1,810.00 before requesting $2,860.00.

 

CONCLUSION

Plaintiff Emely Briana Casillas’s motion to compel nonparty Trukspect, Inc. to comply with deposition subpoena for production of business records is DENIED. Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is DENIED.

DEFENDANT MERCEDES-BENZ USA, LLC’S MOTION TO COMPEL BINDING ARBITRATION AND TO STAY ACTION

 

Motion filed on April 13, 2026.
MOVING PARTY: Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC
RESPONDING PARTY: None.
NOTICE: OK.
RELIEF REQUESTED: Defendant seeks an order compelling Plaintiff Salibi Makhsherdzhyan to arbitrate Plaintiff’s claims and staying this action pending arbitration.
RULING: Granted.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On March 11, 2026, Plaintiff Salibi Makhsherdzhyan filed the Complaint against Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC, Mercedes-Benz of Valencia, and Does 1 through 50, asserting two Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act causes of action for breach of express warranty and breach of implied warranty. (Complaint.)

 

Defendant filed this motion on April 13, 2026, supported by the declaration of Sybil Leung and exhibits including the Consumer Lease Agreement and Complaint. (Leung Decl. ¶¶ 2-3, Exhs. 1-2.) No timely opposition was filed.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Existence of Arbitration Agreement

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.2 requires the Court to order arbitration if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists, unless a statutory ground for denial is shown. A party seeking arbitration satisfies its initial burden by attaching the arbitration agreement or setting forth its terms verbatim. (Condee v. Longwood Management Corp. (2001) 88 Cal.App.4th 215, 218-219; see also Iyere v. Wise Auto Group (2023) 87 Cal.App.5th 747, 755.)

 

Here, Defendant has met its initial burden. The Lease Agreement identifies Plaintiff as lessee, Mercedes-Benz of Valencia as lessor, and the subject vehicle as a 2023 Mercedes-Benz EQS580 with VIN W1KCG4EB2PA038057. (Leung Decl., Exh. 1 at p. 1.) The Lease Agreement contains an arbitration provision stating that “[a]ny claim or dispute, whether in contract, tort or otherwise,” including disputes over “the interpretation, scope, or validity of this lease, arbitration section or the arbitrability of any issue,” involving “the vehicle distributor, including Mercedes-Benz USA LLC,” shall, at the election of Plaintiff, the lessor, or a third-party beneficiary, “be resolved by a neutral, binding arbitration and not by a court action.” (Id. at p. 4.) Plaintiff signed the Lease Agreement and acknowledged receipt and agreement to all terms, “INCLUDING THE IMPORTANT ARBITRATION DISCLOSURES ON PAGES 4 AND 5.” (Id. at p. 6.) Plaintiff filed no timely opposition and has not challenged the existence, authenticity, or validity of the agreement. Defendant has shown that an arbitration agreement exists.

 

Third-Party Beneficiary

 

A nonsignatory may enforce an arbitration provision where the contract shows the nonsignatory is an intended third-party beneficiary. The relevant inquiry is whether the third party would in fact benefit from the contract, whether a motivating purpose of the contracting parties was to provide that benefit, and whether enforcement is consistent with the contract’s objectives and the parties’ reasonable expectations. (Goonewardene v. ADP, LLC (2019) 6 Cal.5th 817, 830.)

 

Here, the Lease Agreement expressly identifies “the vehicle distributor, including Mercedes-Benz USA LLC,” as a “Third Party Beneficiary” and expressly permits a “Third Party Beneficiary” to elect arbitration. (Leung Decl., Exh. 1 at p. 4.) That language shows Defendant would benefit from the agreement, that conferring the arbitration right on Defendant was a motivating purpose of the provision, and that enforcement by Defendant is consistent with the parties’ reasonable expectations.

 

Scope and Delegation

 

When an arbitration agreement clearly and unmistakably delegates questions of arbitrability to the arbitrator, those issues are for the arbitrator unless the delegation provision itself is successfully challenged. (Aanderud v. Superior Court (2017) 13 Cal.App.5th 880, 892.)

 

Here, the arbitration provision expressly covers disputes over “the interpretation, scope, or validity of this lease, arbitration section or the arbitrability of any issue.” (Leung Decl., Exh. 1 at p. 4.) Plaintiff has not opposed the motion and has not challenged the delegation provision. Any issue concerning the scope of the arbitration clause or the arbitrability of Plaintiff’s Song-Beverly claims is therefore delegated to the arbitrator.

 

Stay

 

This action is stayed pending completion of arbitration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.4; 9 U.S.C. § 3.)

 

CONCLUSION

 

Defendant Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC’s motion to compel binding arbitration is GRANTED.

 

This action is STAYED pending completion of arbitration.
