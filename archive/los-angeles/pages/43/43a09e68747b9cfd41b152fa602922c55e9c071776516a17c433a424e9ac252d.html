25STCV04873 Nadea M. McPherson v. Volkswagen Group of America, Inc., et al.

Tuesday, July 21, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANTS, VOLKSWAGEN GROUP OF AMERICA, INC. AND AUDI BEVERLY HILLS’, MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF, NADEA M. MCPHERSON’S, ATTENDANCE AT DEPOSITION AND PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS; REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS (0317)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The complaint alleges that Defendants violated a warranty contract relating to a 2020 Audi Q3 Quattro in violation of the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act.

      Defendants move to compel Plaintiff to appear for deposition and produce documents described in the deposition notice. Defendants’ contend that Plaintiff fails to respond to efforts to meet and confer and provide dates for deposition. Defendants also request an order imposing sanction.

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      When a deponent fails to appear for or proceed with a deposition or produce documents without having served a valid objection, the moving party can move to compel the deponent’s appearance and to produce documents. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450 subd. (b)(2).) The moving party must submit a declaration attesting that the parties met and conferred prior to filing the motion. (Id.)

III.    DISCUSSION

      Defendants first served a deposition notice on May 19, 2025, setting the deposition for July 10, 2025. (Cochran decl., ¶ 4.) Defendants contend they made numerous discovery communications, however, Plaintiff failed to confirm dates for deposition. (Id. ¶ 5.)

      Defendants served a second deposition notice on February 17, 2026. (Id. Ex. B.) Plaintiff objected because the deposition had been unilaterally set and Plaintiff was not available. Plaintiff did not provide alternate deposition dates. (Id. ¶ 8.)

      Defendants called on May 13, 2026, and May 14, 2026, to meet and confer regarding the deposition and vehicle inspection, but was not able to reach Plaintiff, nor did Plaintiff return the call. Defendants sent a letter on May 13, 2026, requesting a meet and confer and to confirm dates for the deposition and vehicle inspection. (Id. ¶ 15.)

      The evidence demonstrates that Plaintiff confirmed availability on June 15, 2026. (Abdo decl., ¶ 6, Ex. 2.) Plaintiff twice emailed and offered dates of June 29, 2026, June 30, 2026, and July 1, 2026. As Defendant observes, these attempts were made after Defendants filed this motion on May 14, 2026.  Plaintiff argues that Defendants did not meet and confer prior to filing this motion. The evidence shows that defense counsel twice attempted to meet and confer before filing the motion. Plaintiff’s counsel does not explain the failure to respond until after Defendants filed this motion. Plaintiff sent an email on June 11, 2026 offering dates. (Abdo decl., ¶ 5.)

      Plaintiff has not shown substantial justification for failing to respond to Defendants’ efforts to schedule a deposition. Defendants are entitled to imposition of sanctions against Plaintiff and Plaintiff’s counsel of record in the amount of $1,580 ($395/hour x 4 hours) for reasonable fees incurred and costs of $60.

IV.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ordered to appear for deposition and produce documents within 10 days as Defendants requested. Plaintiff and counsel, Strategic Legal Practices, APC, jointly and severally, are ordered to pay $1,640 to Defendants within 10 days.



Case Number: 25STCV10958    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV10958 Rebecca Robinson v. Joyce Fitzpatrick, et al.

Tuesday, July 21, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT, OR ALTERNATIVELY, SUMMARY ADJUDICATION (9199)

 

I.         BACKGROUND     

      The complaint alleges claims arising from Defendants’ alleged failure to pay Plaintiff for her interest in the licensing or other disposition of a film, “The Color of Medicine” (“the Film”). Plaintiff alleges claims for interference with contract relations, conversion, and inducement of breach of contract.

      Plaintiff moves for summary judgment or adjudication of all claims. Plaintiff timely served the motion on Defendants, who did not file an opposition.

II.        LEGAL STANDARDS

      Summary judgment is proper “if all the papers submitted show that the material facts are undisputed and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c subd. (c).) A party may move for summary adjudication “as to one or more causes of action within an action, one or more affirmative defenses, one or more claims for damages, or one or more issues of duty, if the party contends that the cause of action has no merit, that there is no affirmative defense to the cause of action, that there is no merit to an affirmative defense as to any cause of action, [or] that there is no merit to a claim for [punitive] damages.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c subd. (f)(1).) A motion for summary adjudication shall be granted only if it completely disposes of a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for damages, or an issue of duty. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c. subd.(f)(1).)

      The court applies the three-step analysis to motions for summary judgment or adjudication: (1) identify the issues framed by the pleading, (2) determine whether the moving party established facts which negate the opponents’ claim, (3) if the moving party meets its threshold burden of persuasion and the burden shifts, determine whether the opposing party has controverted those facts with admissible evidence. (Torres v. Reardon (1992) 3 Cal.App.4th 831, 836.)

 

III.       DISCUSSION

      The motion is procedurally defective. As the moving party, Plaintiff’s burden is to demonstrate that the undisputed facts prove each element of the causes of action entitling Plaintiff to judgment. If that threshold burden is established, the burden shifts to Defendants to show a triable issue of one or more material facts. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c(p)(1).) Although Defendants filed no opposition, Plaintiff has not met her initial burden under Code of Civil Procedure section 437c.

      Each cause of action includes an element of damage.

      Plaintiff seeks summary judgment on all causes of action and, alternatively, summary adjudication of each cause of action. Accordingly, Plaintiff must establish, through undisputed material facts supported by admissible evidence, every essential element of each claim. Plaintiff has failed to do so.

      First, Plaintiff has failed to establish the element of damages. Each of Plaintiff's asserted causes of action requires proof of resulting damage. Intentional interference with contract requires proof of resulting damages. (Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. Bear Stearns & Co. (1990) 50 Cal.3d 1118, 1126.) Likewise, conversion requires proof of damages resulting from the alleged wrongful exercise of dominion over property. (Lee v. Hanley (2015) 61 Cal.4th 1225, 1240.)

Although Plaintiff's separate statement establishes the existence of the Withdrawal Agreement and repeatedly asserts that Plaintiff was entitled to receive 33.33 percent of certain proceeds from the Film, the separate statement never establishes that TCM, LLC actually received proceeds subject to distribution, the amount of any such proceeds, the amount allegedly withheld from Plaintiff, or the amount of damages Plaintiff sustained. Instead, the separate statement establishes only an alleged contractual entitlement and an alleged failure to pay. It does not establish the fact or amount of Plaintiff's damages as undisputed material facts.

      Second, Plaintiff fails to establish a specific amount of damages. A plaintiff seeking summary judgment bears the burden of establishing both the existence and amount of damages through undisputed admissible evidence. (Pajaro Valley Water Management Agency v. McGrath (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1093, 1106.) Here, neither the separate statement nor the supporting evidence identifies the amount of licensing revenues received, the amount of profits distributed or distributable, the amount allegedly withheld, or any calculation demonstrating Plaintiff's claimed damages. Without undisputed evidence establishing the amount recoverable, Plaintiff has not established her entitlement to judgment as a matter of law.

      Third, Plaintiff has failed to establish the essential elements of her conversion claim. Conversion requires proof of ownership or a right to possession of specific identifiable property wrongfully exercised by the defendant. Where money is the subject of a conversion claim, the funds must constitute a specific, identifiable sum rather than a mere obligation arising from an alleged contractual debt. Plaintiff's separate statement identifies no segregated fund, identifiable licensing payment, or specific sum allegedly converted. Rather, Plaintiff alleges only that Defendants failed to pay amounts allegedly owed under the Withdrawal Agreement. Such evidence is insufficient to establish, as an undisputed material fact, the essential elements of conversion.

      Finally, Plaintiff has not established every element of each cause of action through separate undisputed material facts. Much of Plaintiff's separate statement consists of repeated background facts regarding the creation of the Film, the parties' relationship, and the Withdrawal Agreement. The material facts are largely duplicated for each cause of action rather than specifically addressing the individual elements of each claim. While the separate statement establishes certain historical facts, it does not establish, through separately identified undisputed material facts, every essential element necessary to entitle Plaintiff to judgment on each cause of action. The Court cannot grant summary judgment or summary adjudication based upon generalized or repetitive factual assertions that fail to establish every required element.

      Because Plaintiff has failed to satisfy her initial burden, the burden never shifts to Defendants, notwithstanding their failure to file opposition. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).)

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

      Plaintiff has failed to establish through undisputed admissible evidence every essential element of the causes of action upon which summary judgment or summary adjudication is sought. Specifically, Plaintiff has failed to establish (1) the existence of damages, (2) the amount of damages, (3) the existence of a specific identifiable sum sufficient to support the conversion claim, and (4) every element of each cause of action through separate undisputed material facts as required by Code of Civil Procedure section 437c. Accordingly, Plaintiff's Motion for Summary Judgment, or in the Alternative, Summary Adjudication, is DENIED.


Case Number: 25STCV17244    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV17244 NHN Global, Inc. v. Paul Sheene Corporation, Paul Sheene, individually

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER TAKING THE DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF’S COMPLAINT OFF CALENDAR (1705)

 

       The complaint alleges that Plaintiff contracted with Defendants to perform office and warehouse improvement. Defendants allegedly breached the contract by overcharging for work performed, performing substandard and unauthorized work, among other things. Plaintiff alleges breach of contract, breach of implied warranty, negligence, and unfair business practices (Business and Professions Code, section 17200).

      Plaintiff did not file an opposition by July 8, 2026 (nine court days before the hearing.)

      The demurrer is procedurally defective. Defendants served the demurrer by email on Ryan DeRose. Proof of electronic service requires, among other things, a statement that the document was served electronically and the name and electronic service address of the person served both of which are absent from the proof of service filed with the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1013b.)

      Accordingly, the demurrer is taken off calendar.


Case Number: 25STCV22581    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV22581 S.T. v. Los Angeles Unified School District

Tuesday, July 21, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING MOTION TO QUASH THE DEPOSITION SUBPOENA ISSUED BY PLAINTIFF, OR IN THE ALTERNATIVE, FOR A PROTECTIVE ORDER (3958)

 

I.         BACKGROUND

      The complaint alleges that Plaintiff was a 14-year-old student at Ernest Lawrence Middle School, which allegedly has a history of racism. Plaintiff alleges they were the victim of an unprovoked and brutal hate crime at the hands of a white parent and student, Defendant John Paul Morreale, while at school for which Morreale was arrested on January 6, 2025. (Complaint, Ex. A.)  Plaintiff alleges nine causes of action arising from this and other racially-charged incidents.

      Defendant Moreale requests an order to quash a deposition subpoena issued by Plaintiff and served on Revive Wellness Counseling Center (“Revive”) for the production of therapy notes, billing and other records relating to psychological counseling therapy services provided by Revive to Moreale concerning or stemming from the alleged incident and its emotional and psychological impact on Moreale. Defendant contends the subpoena seeks confidential information protected by the constitutional right of privacy and violates the psychotherapist/ physician patient privilege including concerns arising from related criminal proceedings stemming from the same incident.

      On May 28, 2026, the Court granted in part, Morreale’s motion to stay the proceedings. The Court did not find that a complete stay of all civil proceedings was warranted. The Court stayed discovery only to the extent it would require Defendant Morreale to provide testimonial communications concerning the facts and circumstances underlying the pending criminal prosecution, including deposition testimony, interrogatory responses, and responses to requests for admission concerning the December 6, 2024, incident. (M.O. 5/28/26.)

II.        LEGAL STANDARDS

       The court can quash a subpoena to protect a party from unreasonable or oppressive demands including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy. The court has discretion to modify the subpoena or direct compliance upon terms or conditions as the court declares, including issuing protective orders. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1.)

      The party claiming a privacy interest bears the burden of proof on the issue.” (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 531. [“The party asserting a privacy right must establish a legally protected privacy interest, an objectively reasonable expectation of privacy in the given circumstances, and a threatened intrusion that is serious.”].)

      The constitutional right to privacy precludes compelled disclosure of medical records pertaining to topics not placed in issue by filing the complaint. (White v. Davis (1975) 13 Cal.3d 757, 761.) Discovery of a party’s confidential medical history is limited to the specific injury at issue. (Britt v. Superior Court (1978) 20 Cal.3d 844, 864 ["The patient thus is not obligated to sacrifice all privacy to seek redress for a specific mental or emotional injury; the scope of the inquiry permitted depends upon the nature of the injuries which the patient-litigant himself has brought before the court.”)

      Additionally, the psychotherapist-patient privilege affords a patient the privilege "to refuse to disclose, and to prevent another from disclosing, a confidential communication between patient and psychotherapist.” (Evid. Code, § 1014.)

      Where privacy rights are implicated, the requesting party must show that the records sought are directly relevant to Plaintiff’s claims and are essential to the fair resolution of the lawsuit. (Davis v. Superior Court (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1008, 1014).  The burden falls on the party asserting privacy to establish the extent and seriousness of the prospective invasion. (Williams v. Superior Court (2017) 3 Cal.5th 531, 557).

      Against that showing, the Court must weigh the countervailing interests the opposing party identifies. (Id.). The Court considers the purpose of the information sought, the effect that disclosure will have on the affected persons and parties, the nature of the objections urged by the party resisting disclosure, and whether there are less intrusive means for obtaining the requested information. (SCC Acquisitions, Inc. v. Superior Court (2015) 243 Cal.App.4th 741, 755).

III.       DISCUSSION

A.     Defendant tendered the issue of his mental condition.

      Defendant argues that his mental or emotional condition was not “tendered.” (Reply 3:2-3.) Defendant relies on People v. Yang (2021) 67 Cal.App.5th 1, 49 which states that a “patient loses the protection of the psychotherapist-patient privilege only if they are first to ‘tender’ the issue in litigation. In that regard, the court looks to see which party was first to raise the issue." Defendant also cites In re Lifschutz (1970) 2 Cal.3d 415 which states that compelling disclosure despite the psychotherapist-patient privilege is limited to "those matters directly relevant to the nature of the specific ‘emotional or mental; condition which the patient has voluntarily disclosed and tendered in his pleadings or in answer to discovery inquiries." to (In re Lifschutz (1970) 2 Cal.3d 415, 431.)

      Form Interrogatory 2.12, propounded by Plaintiff on defendant states:

“At the time of the INCIDENT did you or any other person have any physical, emotional, or mental disability or condition that may have contributed to the occurrence of the INCIDENT? If so, for each person state:

(a) the name, ADDRESS, and telephone number;

(b) the nature of the disability or condition; and

(c) the manner in which the disability or condition contributed to the occurrence of the INCIDENT.” (Gage Decl., Ex. 2, 8:15-21 [italics added].)

      Defendant responded by identifying professionals he has seen with respect to a service-connected disability, including post-traumatic stress disorder. (Id. 9:17-28.)

Additionally, Defendant asserted that his actions were reasonable in light of the circumstances and that he had legitimate reasons for his for his actions. (Id. Ex. 3, Answer, 18th and 22nd Aff. Defs.)

      While Defendant correctly observes that his answer does not specifically raise the “defense of insanity, unconsciousness, diminished or mental incapacity, emotional injury, or PTSD-as-excuse”, Defendant’s discovery responses connect his mental condition to the potential cause of the incident.

      As Defendant voluntarily tendered that his pre-existing condition contributed to the incident, the subpoena seeks documents directly relevant to Defendant’s defense.

B.     The subpoena is narrowly drawn.

      The subpoena was issued on the witnesse identified by Defendant in response to Interrogatory 12.1, namely Revive Wellness Center. (Quadros decl., Ex. B.) The scope of records sought are limited to those records stemming from the “incident” described as the incident at issue in this litigation. (Id. Att. 3.) The documents to be produced are described as:

“1. Therapy notes, progress reports, diagnosis records, prognosis records, patient information sheets, and test results relating to psychological counseling/therapy services provided by Revive Wellness Counseling Center to JOHN PAUL MORREALE concerning or stemming from the INCIDENT and its emotional and psychological impact on JOHN PAUL MORREALE.

 

2. Billing records, including itemized billing statements with diagnostic codes, for

psychological counseling/therapy services provided by Revive Wellness Counseling Center to JOHN PAUL MORREALE concerning or stemming from the INCIDENT and its emotional and psychological impact on JOHN PAUL MORREALE (“Defendant”). (Id. [emphasis added].)

      Defendant contends that therapy notes, diagnoses, prognoses, test results, and patient-information sheets are not directly relevant. (Reply, 8:4) However, Plaintiff does not seek an “unlimited” disclosure as Plaintiff seeks only those records that concern the incident, and its emotional and psychological impact on Defendant.

      Defendant’s Reply argues that the 5th Amendment, while not raised specifically in the moving papers, precludes the disclosure of records  that are testimonial in nature as they impact the pending criminal proceeding arising from the same incident. Plaintiff argues the 5th Amendment Privilege applies “only when the accused is compelled to make a Testimonial Communication that is incriminating.” (Fisher v. U.S. (1976) 425 U.S. 391, 408.) Plaintiff argues that documents in the hands of mental health professionals who have treated or tested defendant and to whom a patient voluntarily gave information without compulsion is not “testimonial.” (Opp.  7:23-26.)

      Plaintiff’s position has merit. Here, Defendant is not personally compelled to give information. In People v. Superior Court (Broderick) (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 584, 591, the court observed that “[t]he United States Supreme Court has long held the federal self- incrimination privilege does not prevent the production of incriminating documents in the hands of third parties”, finding that the “ingredient of personal compulsion [is] missing.” (Id. ["The Ninth Circuit has held the Fifth Amendment does not prevent a doctor or a hospital from being required to produce psychiatric records of a target of a grand jury investigation in response to a subpoena. … After first determining no federal psychotherapist-patient privilege existed, the court found the records to be voluntarily kept business records and beyond the scope of the Fifth Amendment privilege. … The court concluded that ‘[w]here statements are given freely and voluntarily without any compelling influences, the fifth amendment privilege does not apply.’” (People v. Superior Court (Broderick) (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 584, 591–592.)

      Defendant’s reply brief does not address the application of the 5th amendment privilege specifically where records in the hands of a treating physician are sought. The Reply argues that the privilege applies because Plaintiff “candidly states that it seeks these records to prove Defendant’s ‘mental state, intent, and motivation at the time of the incident’ for the assault, battery, Bane Act, Ralph Act, and intentional-infliction claims, and to prove malice … for purposes of punitive damages” and that these are the same issues for which Defendant is being prosecuted. (Reply, 9:9-15.)

      There is no dispute that there are overlapping factual issues between the civil and criminal proceedings, however, the salient issue is whether a physician can be compelled to produce treating records containing information given by the patient/defendant, where the information is freely and voluntarily given to the physician. As noted in the foregoing authority, Defendant’s personal compulsion is not at issue.

IV.       CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion to quash is DENIED.

 


Case Number: 26STCV06855    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 414

26STCV06855 Frank Galindo v. PS2, Inc.

Tuesday, July 21, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING MOTION TO COMPEL ARBITRATION (7055)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

       This is a wage and hour action alleging violations of the Labor Code including retaliation and for violation of the California Private Attorneys General Act. Defendant moves to compel arbitration of all disputes based on an agreement requiring arbitration of the individual claims alleged.

II.     LEGAL STANDARDS

      The court can order the parties to arbitrate the matter on petition of a party to an arbitration agreement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.2). The petitioner’s burden is to establish that a valid arbitration agreement exists. The opposing party’s burden is to establish a defense to enforcement based on a preponderance of evidence.  (Molecular Analytical Systems v. Ciphergen Biosystems, Inc. (2010) 186 Cal.App.4th 696, 705.)

      Courts apply state law to determine who is bound by an arbitration agreement and who may enforce it. (Thomas v. Westlake (2012) 204 Cal.App.4th 605, 614, fn. 7); Rosenthal v. Great Western Fin. Securities Corp. (1996) 14 Cal.4th 394, 410 ["Because the California procedure for deciding motions to compel [arbitration] serves to further, rather than defeat, full and uniform effectuation of the federal law's objectives, the California law, rather than section 4 of the USAA, is to be followed in California courts."].)

      Under Section 2 of the FAA, written arbitration agreements are valid, irrevocable, and enforceable “save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of a contract.” (Arthur Andersen LLP v. Carlisle (2009) 556 U.S. 624, 629–630; 9 U.S.C.A. § 2.) Section 3 of the FAA requires the court to stay the action if it involves issues referable to arbitration. (9 U.S.C.A. § 3.)

III.    DISCUSSION

A.     Defendant has met its burden of showing the existence of an arbitration agreement.

      Defendant need only show the existence of an agreement by a preponderance of evidence. (Espejo v. Southern California Permanente Medical Group (2016) 246 Cal.App.4th 1047, 1058 ["as a preliminary matter the [trial] court is only required to make a finding of the agreement's existence, not an evidentiary determination of its validity.”].)  The moving party need only attach a copy of the agreement to the petition and incorporate it by reference. (Id. at 1058; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1330 [“The provisions must be stated verbatim or a copy must be physically or electronically attached to the petition and incorporated by reference."].)

      Plaintiff signed an arbitration agreement and printed his name. (Schmit Decl., Ex. 1.) Therein, Plaintiff consented to binding arbitration of a broad scope of employment disputes:

“In order to gain the benefits of a speedy, impartial, and cost-effective resolution procedure, PS2 Inc. and I consent to binding arbitration to resolve all claims arising out of my employment and/or termination of my employment or which a court would otherwise be authorized to grant relief,  including but not limited to contract, tort, statutory, and common law claims … . By doing so. the Company and I each knowingly and voluntarily waive rights to a trial before a jury. …

 

“I have carefully read this agreement and have entered into it voluntarily and not in reliance on any promises or representations by the Company other than those contained in this agreement itself.” (Id.)

B.     Plaintiff has not established any defenses to enforcement

      Plaintiff disputes signing the agreement and contends the agreement is not authenticated. (Opp.  2:6.) Plaintiff does not cite any relevant authority; Espejo is contrary to this contention. (Espejo, supra.) Defendant’s reply brief cites Condee v. Longwood Management Corp. (2001) 88 Cal.App.4th 215, 218–219 which held that “for purposes of a petition to compel arbitration, it is not necessary to follow the normal procedures of document authentication. ‘[T]he court shall order the petitioner and the respondent to arbitrate the controversy if it determines that an agreement to arbitrate the controversy exists....’ (View more Negative Treatments§ 1281.2) The statute does not require the petitioner to introduce the agreement into evidence. A plain reading of the statute indicates that as a preliminary matter the court is only required to make a finding of the agreement's existence, not an evidentiary determination of its validity." (Id.)

      Plaintiff submits his own and others’ declarations stating that he did print out his name on the arbitration agreement but is “100% certain” that he did not sign it. (Galindo decl., ¶ 9.) Plaintiff argues the court should not consider any new evidence submitted with the reply. However, Plaintiff’s deposition testimony rebuts Plaintiff’s contention that the signature is not authentic. (Los Angeles Unified School Dist. v. Torres Construction Corp. (2020) 57 Cal.App.5th 480, 499 ["Evidence which is used to fill gaps in the original evidence created by the opposition is particularly appropriate to consider in a reply.") Moreover, the reply evidence consists of Plaintiff’s deposition testimony under penalty of perjury. The reply and evidence in support was served on Plaintiff, giving Plaintiff notice and opportunity to respond further at the hearing. (Jay v. Mahaffey (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1522, 1538.)

      Peter Michael Schmit, Plaintiff’s president, asserts that he personally meets with all applicants and recalls meeting with Plaintiff. He confirms Plaintiff signed the agreement on November 20, 2017. (Schmit decl., ¶ 6). Moreover, Defendant took Plaintiff’s deposition on June 18 and June 24, 2026 (Kowarski reply decl., ¶ 3-5.) Plaintiff admitted printing his name on the agreement and affixing his initials at the bottom right corner of the agreement while also denying that the signature on the agreement was his. (Reply decl., Ex. 42:24-43:7.)

      The meaning and effect of a contract is a matter of interpretation by the court, applying state law principles of interpretation. (Citizens Utilities Co. v. Wheeler (1957) 156 Cal.App.2d 423, 432; Basura v. U.S. Home Corp. (2002) 98 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1215 ["On the threshold issue of whether the parties agreed to arbitrate a matter, courts apply ordinary state law principles that govern the formation of contracts, which principles are not preempted by the FAA."].)

      The burden of authenticating a signature is “not great.” (Ruiz v. Moss Bros. Auto Group, Inc. (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 836, 844.) In Ruiz, Plaintiff disputed the authenticity of the electronic signature attributed to him. Ruiz held that an electronic signature is attributable to a person if it is the act of the person, and this may be shown in any manner” even by circumstantial evidence. (Id.) The manner of authentication is not limited by the Evidence Code. (Id. citing Evidence Code § 1400 [“Authentication of a writing means (a) the introduction of evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that it is the writing that the proponent of the evidence claims it is or (b) the establishment of such facts by any other means provided by law."].)

      Defendant’s case authority that a signature alone is not required for proof of acceptance as “signing” may come in many forms, is persuasive. Defendant cites Donovan v. RRL Corp. (2001) 26 Cal.4th 261, 277 which determined that authentication under the Commercial Code includes any "symbol executed or adopted by a party with present intention to authenticate a writing.” (Id. ["Authentication may be printed, stamped, or written; it may be by initials or by thumbprint. It may be on any part of the document and in appropriate cases may be found in a billhead or letterhead. No catalog of possible authentications can be complete, and the court must use common sense and commercial experience in passing upon these matters. The question always is whether the symbol was executed or adopted by the party with present intention to authenticate the writing.”)

IV.   CONCLUSION

      Defendant has met its burden by a preponderance of evidence that Plaintiff agreed to arbitrate all disputes arising from his employment. Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED.
