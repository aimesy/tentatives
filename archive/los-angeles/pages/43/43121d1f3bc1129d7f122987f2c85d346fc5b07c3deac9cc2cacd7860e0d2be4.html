Case Number: 25NNCV01760    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO COMPEL FORM INTERROGATORIES – SET ONE, SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES – SET ONE, DEMAND FOR INSPECTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE and GRANTING MONETARY SANCTIONS IN PART

I.          INTRODUCTION

            On March 14, 2025, Plaintiff Leelit Minasyan (“Plaintiff’) filed this action against Defendant Jorge Galan (“Defendant”), asserting a single cause of action for negligence arising out of a March 17, 2023, motor vehicle collision.

On June 23, 2026, Defendant filed three motions to compel Plaintiff's initial responses to Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, and Requests for Inspection, and requested monetary sanctions. No opposition has been filed.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290(a), if a party fails to respond to interrogatories, they waive any objections, including those based on privilege or work product protection. When a party does not provide timely responses, the propounding party is entitled to move for an order compelling responses. Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2030.290(b), the Court must order the responding party to provide responses without objections.

If a party fails to serve a timely response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (b).) The failure to timely respond also waives any objections to the demands, including objections based on privilege or work product protection, unless the court relieves the responding party from the waiver upon a showing that the failure resulted from mistake, inadvertence, or excusable neglect and that the responding party has since served responses that are substantially code-compliant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).).

            III.      ANALYSIS

FROGS and SPROGS

Defendant served FROGs and SPROGs, Set One on Plaintiff on July 31, 2025. Responses were due on September 2, 2025. Defendant represents that Plaintiff failed to serve any responses. Thereafter, on September 18, 2025, Defendant sent a meet and confer letter requesting verified responses without objections by September 26, 2025, but no responses were received. Plaintiff has not filed an opposition or otherwise disputed these facts. Based on the foregoing, Defendant is entitled to an order compelling verified responses without objections. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)

Demand for inspection

Defendant served Demand for Inspection, Set One on Plaintiff on July 31, 2025. Responses were due on September 2, 2025. Defendant represents that Plaintiff failed to serve any responses. Defendant thereafter sent a meet and confer letter requesting verified responses without objections by September 26, 2025, but no responses were received. Plaintiff has not filed an opposition or otherwise disputed these facts. Based on the foregoing, Defendant is entitled to an order compelling verified responses without objections. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300.)

IV.       SANCTIONS

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 provides that, “[t]o the extent authorized by the chapter governing any particular discovery method . . . , the court, after notice to any affected party, person, or attorney, and after opportunity for hearing, may impose . . . [monetary, issue, evidence, or terminating] sanctions against anyone engaging in conduct that is a misuse of the discovery process . . . .”  Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.010 provides that misuse of the discovery process includes, but is not limited to, failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery. California Rules of Court, rule 3.1348, subdivision (a) states: “The court may award sanctions under the Discovery Act in favor of a party who files a motion to compel discovery, even though no opposition to the motion was filed, or opposition to the motion was withdrawn, or the requested discovery was provided to the moving party after the motion was filed.”  

Defendant requests monetary sanctions totaling $3,000, representing $1,000 for each of the three motions. Counsel declares an hourly rate of $250 and estimates four hours of attorney time per motion, including reviewing the file, preparing the motion, reviewing any opposition, preparing a reply, and appearing at the hearing. (Lira Decl., ¶ d.)

Because Plaintiff filed no opposition and the three motions involve substantially identical legal and factual issues, the Court finds the requested amount excessive. The Court instead awards attorney's fees for 1.5 hours of work apportioned across the three motions.

The Court GRANTS request for monetary sanctions consisting of $375.00 in attorney's fees (1.5 hours × $250/hour) payable by Plaintiff and Plaintiff's counsel, jointly and severally, to Defendant within 30 days of notice of this order. 

           V.       CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            The Court GRANTS Defendant’s motions to compel SPROGs – Set One, FROGs - Set One and demand for inspection of documents – Set One. Plaintiff is ordered to respond within 30 days of this order. The Court further GRANTS monetary sanctions in the amount of $375.00.

Defendant is to give notice.

Case Number: 26NNCV03109    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PETITONERS’ MOTION TO APPOINT ARBITRATOR

I.          INTRODUCTION

On April 21, 2026, Petitioner Scott Haymaker ("Petitioner") filed the instant petition against Respondent Mobilitas Insurance Company ("Respondent") seeking the assignment of a case number for an uninsured/underinsured motorist arbitration pursuant to Insurance Code section 11580.2, subdivision (f)(1). Petitioner alleges that a case number is necessary to permit the filing of a petition under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 to appoint a neutral arbitrator.

On July 9, 2026, Petitioner filed the instant motion to appoint an arbitrator. On July 20, 2026, Respondent Mobilitas Insurance Company filed an opposition, and on July 27, 2026, Petitioner filed a reply.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure § 1281.6 provides that “If the arbitration agreement provides a method of appointing an arbitrator, that method shall be followed. If the arbitration agreement does not provide a method for appointing an arbitrator, the parties to the agreement who seek arbitration and against whom arbitration is sought may agree on a method of appointing an arbitrator and that method shall be followed. In the absence of an agreed method, or if the agreed method fails or for any reason cannot be followed, or when an arbitrator appointed fails to act and his or her successor has not been appointed, the court, on petition of a party to the arbitration agreement, shall appoint the arbitrator.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1281.6.) Upon such a petition, the court shall nominate five potential arbitrators from lists of people supplied jointly by the parties or obtained from a governmental agency concerned with arbitration or private disinterested association concerned with arbitration. (Ibid.) The parties shall then, within five days of receipt of the nominations, jointly select an arbitrator; if the parties fail to do so, the court shall select an arbitrator from among the nominees. (Ibid.)

III.      ANALYSIS

            Petitioner moves for an order appointing a neutral arbitrator pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 to resolve the parties' underinsured motorist ("UIM") dispute and directing the parties to proceed to arbitration within nine months of the Court's order. Petitioner contends that: (1) the insurance policy contains a valid agreement requiring arbitration of UIM disputes; (2) Petitioner served a written demand for arbitration on October 29, 2025; (3) Respondent acknowledged the demand on November 26, 2025; (4) despite the demand, Respondent has failed to participate in the selection of an arbitrator; and (5) because the parties have been unable to effectuate the contractual arbitration process, the Court must appoint a neutral arbitrator pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 and Insurance Code section 11580.2, subdivision (f). (Motion, pp. 3-9.)

Respondent opposes the motion, arguing that it is premature because: (1) Petitioner has not exhausted all available remedies, specifically the Blue Star Occupational Accident Insurance ("OAI") claim, which Respondent contends is the functional equivalent of workers' compensation benefits and must be resolved before the UIM arbitration proceeds; (2) the parties have not made meaningful efforts to agree upon the selection of an arbitrator as contemplated by Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6; (3) Respondent remains willing to arbitrate and has attempted to meet and confer regarding arbitrator selection; and (4) Petitioner's motion was procedurally defective because it was served one court day late under Code of Civil Procedure sections 1005 and 1010.6. Respondent therefore requests that the Court deny the motion. (Opposition, pp. 2-8.)

As a preliminary matter, the Court declines to deny the motion based on the alleged notice defect. Although Respondent contends the motion was served one court day late under Code of Civil Procedure sections 1005 and 1010.6, a court may consider untimely filed or served motion papers absent a showing that a party's substantial rights were affected. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1300(d); Code Civ. Proc., § 475.) Moreover, a party's appearance at the hearing and opposition to a motion on the merits waives procedural defects or irregularities in the notice of motion. (Carlton v. Quint (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 690, 697.) Here, Respondent received actual notice of the motion, filed a substantive opposition addressing the merits, and identifies no prejudice resulting from the alleged one-court-day notice defect. Accordingly, the Court exercises its discretion to consider the motion on its merits.

CCP 1281.6 - mandatory court appointment

Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 governs the appointment of arbitrators. Where an arbitration agreement specifies a method for appointing an arbitrator, that method must be followed. If the agreement does not provide a method, the parties may agree upon one. However, "[i]n the absence of an agreed method, or if the agreed method fails or for any reason cannot be followed," the court, upon petition by a party to the arbitration agreement, "shall appoint the arbitrator." (Code Civ. Proc., § 1281.6.)

Here, there is no dispute that a valid agreement to arbitrate exists. Likewise, the parties agree that the arbitration provision does not specify a method for selecting an arbitrator. Thus, the dispositive issue is whether the parties agreed upon a method of appointment or whether the agreed method failed such that court intervention is required under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6.

The record reflects that Petitioner demanded arbitration on October 29, 2025, and Respondent acknowledged the demand on November 26, 2025. Despite months of correspondence concerning the arbitration, the parties did not agree upon a method of selecting an arbitrator or identify a mutually acceptable neutral. (Castillo III Decl; Exh 1-2, 5-7.) Petitioner therefore contends the statutory prerequisites for court appointment have been satisfied, while Respondent argues the motion is premature because the parties have not yet engaged in meaningful efforts to agree upon an arbitrator.

Blue Star/OAI dispute

In opposition, Respondent argues that the motion is premature because petitioner has not exauhsted his Blue Star Occupational Accident Insurance ("OAI") claim. Respondent contends that, although rideshare drivers are classified as independent contractors under Proposition 22, Business and Professions Code section 7455, subdivision (e) provides that OAI benefits "shall be considered amounts payable under a worker's compensation law or disability benefit for the purpose of determining amounts payable under any insurance." Given this, Respondent argues OAI benefits are the functional equivalent of workers' compensation benefits and arbitration should not proceed until the OAI claim has been resolved. (Opposition, pp. 4-7.)

The Court is not persuaded. Business and Professions Code section 7455, subdivision (e) does not convert OAI benefits into workers' compensation benefits for all purposes. Rather, by its plain language, the statute provides only that OAI benefits "shall be considered amounts payable under a worker's compensation law or disability benefit for the purpose of determining amounts payable under any insurance." (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 7455, subd. (e), italics added.) Thus, the statute addresses the calculation of benefits or offsets, not whether an arbitrator may be appointed under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6

Likewise, Insurance Code section 11580.2, subdivision (f), governs the conduct of the arbitration where applicable, providing that the arbitrator shall not proceed until the insured's condition is stationary and ratable where workers' compensation rights exist. To the extent any OAI benefits ultimately affect Petitioner's recovery, that issue concerns the amount payable or any applicable offset and does not provide a basis to deny the present motion.

Appointment of arbitrator

Because the Court finds that the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6 have been satisfied, the Court will proceed with the statutory appointment process. Within 10 days of notice of this order, each party shall submit to the Court a list of five proposed neutral arbitrators, together with each proposed arbitrator's qualifications and current contact information. Upon receipt of the parties' submissions, the Court will nominate five individuals pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6. The parties shall then have five days after notice of the Court's nominees to jointly select an arbitrator. If the parties fail to do so, the Court will appoint an arbitrator from the nominees in accordance with Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6.

IV.       CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            The Court GRANTS petitioner’s motion to appoint arbitrator.

            The parties are ordered to submit a list of five proposed neutral arbitrators together with each proposed arbitrator's qualifications and current contact information within 10 days’ notice of this order. The Court will then nominate five individuals pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6. The parties shall then have five days after notice of the Court's nominees to jointly select an arbitrator.

            Petitioner is to give notice.

Case Number: 26NNCV03457    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO SET ASIDE/VACATE DEFAULT

I.          INTRODUCTION

            On May 1, 2026, Plaintiffs Jerome Quemada and Christina Quemada ("Plaintiffs") filed this action against Defendants Louie Fernando Lopez, Jr., EAN Holdings, LLC, KWK Trucking, Inc., and Does 1 through 35, asserting claims arising out of a motor vehicle collision.

On June 24, 2026, the Clerk entered defaults against Defendants KWK Trucking, Inc. and Louie Fernando Lopez (“Defendants”). On July 2, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion to set aside the defaults pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Plaintiffs filed their opposition on July 20, 2026, and Defendants filed their reply on July 27, 202

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) provides for either discretionary or mandatory relief from certain prior actions or proceedings in the trial court. (Luri v. Greenwald¿(2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 1119, 1124.)  

“‘Under the discretionary relief provision, on a showing of “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect,” the court has discretion to allow relief from a “judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against”a party or his or her attorney. Under the mandatory relief provision, on the other hand, upon a showing by attorney declaration of “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect,” the court shall vacate any “resulting default judgment or dismissal entered.”’ [Citation.] Applications seeking relief under the mandatory provision of section 473 must be ‘accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.’ The mandatory provision further adds that ‘whenever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault [the court shall] direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs¿to opposing counsel or parties.’”  (Ibid.; Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

            Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 473, subd. (b).)  

III.      DISCUSSION

Under section 473(b), upon a showing by attorney declaration of “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect,” the Court “shall vacate any resulting default, judgment, or dismissal.”

Here, Defendants KWK Trucking, Inc. and Louie Fernando Lopez seek to vacate the defaults entered on June 24, 2026. Counsel, Stacey L. Walker, declares that although the necessary file materials had been received from the client, internal administrative issues delayed downloading those materials to counsel's server, which in turn delayed counsel's review and preparation of a responsive pleading. Counsel states that the materials became available on June 22, 2026, and that while reviewing the court docket on June 25, 2026, she discovered that defaults had been entered the previous day. Counsel immediately contacted Plaintiffs' counsel to request a stipulation to set aside the defaults, but Plaintiffs declined absent payment of costs. Counsel accepts full responsibility for the defaults, attests they were caused solely by her inadvertence, mistake, and neglect, and attaches Defendants' proposed Answer. (Walker Decl, ¶¶ 3-8; 10-12)

The motion, filed on July 2, 2026, was brought well within six months of the June 24, 2026, entry of default and is therefore timely. The motion includes an attorney affidavit of fault and a proposed Answer. Although Plaintiffs argue the defaults were attributable to delays by Defendants' insurance carrier rather than counsel, counsel unequivocally accepts responsibility for the defaults and states they resulted from an internal administrative issue within counsel's office. Nothing in the record demonstrates that the defaults were caused by anything other than counsel's admitted mistake, inadvertence, or neglect. The statutory prerequisites for mandatory relief under section 473(b) are therefore satisfied.

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Defendants’ motion to vacate the entries.

Sanctions

Plaintiffs request attorney's fees and costs totaling $2,327.84, consisting of $977.84 in costs and $1,350.00 in attorney's fees. (Opposition, p. 4; Konell Decl., ¶ 8.)

"The court may properly order payment of costs or attorney fees to the adverse party as compensation for loss or expense occasioned by the granting of the section 473 motion." (Jade K. v. Viguri (1989) 210 Cal.App.3d 1459, 1474.) Additionally, Code of Civil Procedure section 473 authorizes the Court to impose a penalty of no greater than $1,000 upon the offending attorney or party. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (c)(1)(A).)

In support of the request, Plaintiffs' counsel declares that he expended five hours addressing the defaults and the present motion but seeks reimbursement for only three hours at his hourly rate of $450, together with $977.84 in costs. While Plaintiffs request $2,327.84 in fees and costs, the Court finds that an award of the statutory maximum penalty of $1,000 is just and appropriate under the circumstances. Thus, defense counsel, Stacey L. Walker, is ordered to pay Plaintiffs monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,000 within 30 days of notice of this order.

IV.       CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            The Court GRANTS Defendants’ motion to vacate/set aside defaults. Defendants shall file their Answer within 20 days’ notice of this order.

            Defendants are to give notice.
