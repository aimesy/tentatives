DEPARTMENT 529 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV20950    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: 529

JAMES A KIM, ET AL. VS AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.

 

DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH THE COURT’S FEBRUARY 19, 2026 DISCOVERY ORDER AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

Date of Hearing:       July 2, 2026                                                     Trial Date:       None set

Department:             529                                                                   Case No.:        24STCV20950

 

Moving Party:            Defendant American Honda Motor Co.

Responding Party:     No opposition.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a lemon law action. On August 19, 2024, Plaintiffs James A Kim and James A Kim MP APMC filed a complaint against American Honda Motor Co., Inc. for various violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act. On December 3, 2024, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

Defendant’s Motion to Compel Compliance with the Court’s February 19, 2026 Discovery Order and Request for Sanctions is GRANTED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant Honda moves for an order compelling Plaintiffs James A. Kim and James A. Kim MD APMC’s compliance with the court’s February 19, 2026 Discovery Order granting Defendant’s motion to compel discovery responses.

 

“[I]f a party fails to obey an order compelling further response, the court may make those orders that are just, including the imposition of an issue sanction, an evidence sanction, or a terminating sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010). In lieu of, or in addition to, that sanction, the court may impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010).” (CCP § 2031.310(i).) 

 

Defendant argues as a failure to respond to Defendant’s discovery requests, Defendant filed several motions to compel discovery responses. The court granted Defendant’s motions to compel discovery on February 19, 2026, (Carter Decl. ¶3.) Notably, the court ordered Plaintiff to provide further responses without objection and verifications within 10 days. (Carter Decl. ¶3, Exh. A.) On April 20, 2026, Defendant sent correspondence to Plaintiffs requesting Plaintiffs provide their further responses without objections and verifications on or by April 24, 2025. (Carter Decl. ¶4, Exh. B.) Plaintiff’s Counsel responded, asking for an extension and Defendant agreed. (Carter Decl. ¶4, Exh. B.) Despite this, Plaintiff’s counsel has not provided any further verified responses to Defendant’s requests for discovery. (Carter Decl. ¶5.)

 

As a result of Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the February 19, 2026 Court Order, the court GRANTS Defendant’s motion to compel compliance. The court notes the instant motion is unopposed. Plaintiff is ordered to produce discovery responses without objection and verification within 5 days.

 

The court notes Defendant also requests this court impose monetary sanctions of $500 per day until Plaintiff’s comply with the court’s prior court order. The court denies the request to impose monetary sanctions as the circumstances are not such that the $500 per day requested is warranted.

 


Case Number: 25STCV27612    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: 529

DANIEL J SEELEY vs GENERAL MOTORS, LLC

 

defendant general motors llc’s MOTION FOR COMPLIANCE PURSUANT TO C.C.P. §871.26 AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS  

 

Date of Hearing:       July 2, 2026                                         Trial Date:       None set

Department:             529                                                       Case No.:        25STCV27612

 

Moving Party:            Defendant General Motors LLC

Responding Party:     Plaintiff Daniel J Seeley

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a lemon law action. On September 19, 2025, Plaintiff Daniel J Seeley filed a complaint against General Motors, LLC for various violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as well as Fraudulent Inducement. On February 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Defendant General Motors LLC’s Motion for Compliance Pursuant to C.C.P. §871.26 and Request for Sanctions is MOOT.

 

discussion

 

Defendant General Motors LLC moves this court for an order for compliance requiring Plaintiff Daniel J Sleely to appear and comply with the initial deposition of the Plaintiff under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(c)(1). GM also moves this court for an order imposing monetary sanctions against Plaintiff’s attorneys of record, Strategic Legal Practices, APC, in the sum of $1,500.00.

 

“Within 120 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties have the right to conduct initial depositions, each not to exceed two hours.” (CCP § 871.26(c).) 

 

Defendant GM argues Plaintiff has failed to comply with the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26. On September 19, 2025, Plaintiff filed his suit against GM and on January 2, 2026, GM filed its responsive pleading in the form of its Demurrer to the complaint. Along with its responsive pleading, GM served a meet-and-confer letter to Plaintiff’s noting GM was issuing a notice for Plaintiff to appear for deposition and indicated if Plaintiff was unavailable to appear, to please contact their office. (Stark Decl. ¶¶4, Exh. A.) As part of the responsive pleading e-mail, GM also attached a Notice of Deposition, for Plaintiff to appear for deposition on March 2, 2026. (Stark Decl., ¶ 5, Exh. B.) However, on January 2, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel objected to the Notice of Deposition indicating the deposition was unilaterally noticed for a time and date which Plaintiff and/or Plaintiff’s counsel is unavailable. (Stark Decl., ¶6, Exh. C.) On April 29, 2026, counsel for GM sent a follow-up e-mail to Plaintiff’s counsel requesting dates for Plaintiff’s availability for deposition. (Stark Decl., ¶ 7, Exh. D.) GM has still not received a reply from Plaintiff’s counsel. (Stark Decl., ¶ 8.) The statutory deadline to conduct the deposition of Plaintiff was May 1, 2026.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues the motion is moot and moreover, the motion is premature as the statutory deadline has not yet expired. First, Plaintiff contends Plaintiff has confirmed availability for deposition on July 8, 2026 at 2:00 p.m. and remains ready, willing, and able to appear. Next, Plaintiff contends the 120-day deposition deadline under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(c) runs from the filing of the "answer or other responsive pleading" not from a demurrer. As a result, the 120-days did not begin to run until GM filed its Answer on May 5, 2026.

 

First, the court finds the motion is not premature. As noted by GM, a demurrer is a responsive pleading under the 871.26 given the legislative intent. However, as the parties have now agreed to a deposition time and date, the court finds the motion moot.  

 

The court briefly notes Plaintiff’s contention with service. The parties are to meet and confer to establish mutually agreeable expectations regarding how communication and services will be conducted going forward. The court expects the parties to work out these details without further court involvement.

 

Sanctions

 

Both parties seeks sanctions in the amount of $1,500.00 for having to bring the motion and for having to oppose the motion. The court declines to award sanctions to either party.
