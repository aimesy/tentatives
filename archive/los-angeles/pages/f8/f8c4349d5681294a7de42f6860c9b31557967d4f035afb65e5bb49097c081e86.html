DEPARTMENT S27 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS



Case Number: 22LBCV01066    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.             Facts

Plaintiff, Mary Wilson filed this action against Defendant, Rodger Wilson for damages arising out of a partnership agreement the parties signed during the course of their divorce proceedings.  The operative complaint is the FAC, which was filed on 2/09/23.  It includes causes of action for breach of fiduciary duty, elder abuse, breach of contract, conversion, unjust enrichment, accounting, constructive trust, and violation of Penal Code §496(c).

 

2.             Motion to Enforce Settlement

a.     Settlement Agreement

On 4/08/25, the parties entered into a written settlement agreement for the purpose of resolving their disputes in this action and in a companion action pending between them in Kansas.  The settlement agreement is attached as Exhibit 2 to the moving papers.

b.     §664.6

Plaintiff moves for an order enforcing the settlement per CCP §664.6.  CCP §664.6(a) permits the Court to “enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”  Plaintiff seeks an order “enforcing” the parties’ settlement agreement, but her proposed judgment is a combination of a judgment and an order.  Notably, the proposed judgment/order includes language stating, “The Court shall retain jurisdiction pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 to continue to enforce the Settlement Agreement…”

 

CCP §664.6 does not contemplate entering an order requiring the parties to engage in specified acts once the parties have settled their dispute.  It contemplates only entering a judgment.  It also does not contemplate ongoing jurisdiction to “enforce” a judgment once entered; enforcement of judgments is the parties’ responsibility.

 

c.     Additional Problems

The Court could theoretically enter a judgment to enforce the parties’ settlement agreement.  Plaintiff’s proposed judgment, to the extent it is a judgment, does not, however, seek to enforce the terms of the parties’ agreement.  The proposed judgment asks for a judgment by partition of the parties’ Kansas properties and the parties’ Dawson property, located in California.  The settlement agreement, however, does not include the word “partition” anywhere therein.

 

Nothing in CCP §664.6 authorizes a judge to create the material terms of a settlement, as opposed to deciding what terms the parties themselves have previously agreed upon.  J.B.B. Investment Partners, Ltd. v. Fair (2014) 232 Cal.App.4th 974, 984. 

 

The settlement agreement contemplates the sale of the properties, and provides explicit mechanisms for such sale of the properties, including methods to seek court orders if a party believes the other party is not proceeding properly.  Plaintiff is not seeking any of the orders contemplated by way of the parties’ agreement, but instead is seeking to have the Court create a judgment and companion orders out of whole cloth.  Plaintiff cites no authority permitting the Court to do so, and the Court knows of none.  The motion is summarily denied due to its failure to seek available relief under §664.6.

 

This ruling is without prejudice to Plaintiff’s right to seek any relief contemplated by the parties’ settlement agreement and/or to seek any applicable legal relief with appropriate authority.

 

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24LBCV01986    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     History of Action

Plaintiffs filed this action on 8/13/24.  The case has been set for Final Status Conference on three occasions.  Each time, Plaintiffs’ attorney appeared and reported “not ready.”  On 5/13/26, the Court specially set a hearing on a motion to dismiss for 9/15/26.  The Court also scheduled a trial setting conference for 9/15/26. 

 

2.     Motion to Dismiss

a.     Parties’ Positions

Defendant moves to dismiss.  It moves on two grounds.  First, it contends more than two years have passed and Plaintiffs have not diligently brought the action to trial.  Second, it contends Plaintiffs failed to serve a settlement order as ordered to do by the Court. 

 

Plaintiffs oppose, contending they are prosecuting their action and their attorney’s engagement in other trials does not justify dismissal of the action.  They concede they made a settlement offer after the Court’s deadline to do so, but contend this is not grounds for dismissal of the action. 

 

3.     Dismiss

CCP §583.410 permits dismissal for delay in prosecution “pursuant to this article,” and one such circumstance is when the plaintiffs, through their own fault, have failed to bring the action to trial within two years.  §583.410(a).  Such ground for dismissal is discretionary, and CRC 3.1342(e) details the factors to be considered when determining whether to dismiss the action or not. 

 

This is a difficult issue.  While it is true that the Court continued the FSC numerous times, this is not a situation, like some other Honda cases with SLP, where trial has been continued ten, eleven, or more times.  That said, this cannot go on indefinitely.  The Court is inclined not to impose a harsh dismissal penalty based on Plaintiffs’ attorneys’ past engagement in other matters, but will require Plaintiffs’ attorneys to schedule the trial, at today’s Trial Setting Conference, on a date on which Plaintiffs’ attorneys will be available.  The Court advises Plaintiffs that their failure to have an attorney available for trial on the next scheduled trial date will likely result in dismissal of the action. 

 

The Court finds there is no statutory or other authority permitting dismissal for failure to make a settlement offer, even if the Court orders a party to do so.  The motion on this ground is therefore also denied.  Importantly, Plaintiffs did ultimately make such an offer, though the court-ordered deadline to do so had already passed when they did so.

 

The parties are reminded that there is a Trial Setting Conference on calendar concurrently with the hearing on the above motion.  The Court asks the parties to make arrangements to appear remotely at the FSC and hearing on the motion.

Case Number: 24LBCV02092    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: S27

Plaintiffs filed a request for dismissal of this action on 2/18/26 and served it the same day.  The parties agree that the due date for filing a memorandum of costs was 3/09/26, but Defendant did not file a memorandum of costs until the following day, 3/10/26. 

 

Plaintiffs move to strike the entire tax bill as untimely filed.  Pursuant to Hydratec, Inc. v. Sun Valley 260 Orchard & Vineyard Co. (1990) 223 Cal.App.3d 924, 929, failure to timely file a memorandum of costs may result in waiver of costs. 

 

Defendant argues it should be granted CCP §473(b) relief from its failure to timely file its memorandum.  Defendant does not provide on point authority in this regard.  The Court researched this issue and found Douglas v. Willis (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 287, which held that the discretionary provision of §473(b) applies when a party fails to file a timely memorandum of costs.  The appeal, however, was from a §473(b) motion for relief.  There is nothing in the ruling that suggests this request can be made by way of opposition papers.  Notably, this Court routinely refuses to award Plaintiffs’ attorneys’ office, Strategic Legal Partners, §473(b) relief when the firm seeks such relief in opposition papers, and the Court will not allow Defense Counsel to achieve a different result.

 

The motion to strike costs is granted on the ground that Defendant failed to timely file its memorandum of costs.  The ruling is without prejudice to Defendant’s right to make a §473(b) motion if it has grounds to do so.

 

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV01191    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: S27

 

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff, Harley-Davidson Credit Corp. filed this action against Defendants, Harbor City Enterprises, Inc., Mark Ruffalo, Donna Marie Ruffalo, Trustee of the Ruffalo Family 2003 Trust, and Leslie Ruffalo, Trustee of the Ruffalo Family 1981 Revocable Trust for breach of contract, breach of guarantees, and “on account.”  The crux of the complaint is that Harbor City Enterprises, Inc. entered into a Credit Financing Agreement with Plaintiff pursuant to which Plaintiff would extend credit to HCEI to purchase new and used motorcycles and related accessories and parts.  In 2004, Ronald and Barbara Ruffalo, as trustees of the 1981 trust, executed a continuing guaranty of the obligations.  In 2005, Mark and Donna Ruffalo, as trustees of the 2003 trust, executed a continuing guaranty of the obligations.  In 2024, HCEI defaulted on its obligations, and the default has not been cured. 

 

All defendants are in default with the exception of Leslie Ruffalo, Trustee of the Ruffalo Family 1981 Revocable Trust. 

 

2.     Motion for Summary Judgment

a.     Parties’ Positions

 Plaintiff moves for summary judgment on the complaint in its favor and against Leslie Ruffalo, Trustee of the Ruffalo Family 1981 Revocable Trust, contending undisputed evidence shows the trust guaranteed the debt, the debt is in default, and a sum certain is due on the debt.

 

Defendant opposes the motion.  She argues (a) each element of each cause of action pled in the complaint is not established, (b) the evidence purporting to show the predecessor trustees signed the trust is inadmissible, (c) triable issues of material fact exist as to the amount of damages, (d) triable issues of material fact exist as to the effectiveness of the continuing guaranty. 

 

Plaintiff, in reply, contends evidence of all elements of each cause of action has been presented, the evidence is admissible, and nothing in the opposition raises a triable issue of material fact.

 

b.     Burdens on Summary Judgment

Summary judgment is proper “if all the papers submitted show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”  (Code Civ. Proc. §437c(c).)  Where a defendant seeks summary judgment or adjudication, he must show that either “one or more elements of the cause of action, even if not separately pleaded, cannot be established, or that there is a complete defense to that cause of action.”  (Id. at §437c(o)(2).)  A defendant may satisfy this burden by showing that the claim “cannot be established” because of the lack of evidence on some essential element of the claim.  (Union Bank v. Superior Court (1995) 31 Cal.App.4th 574, 590.)  Once the defendant meets this burden, the burden shifts to the plaintiff to show that a “triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action or defense thereto.”  (Ibid.) 

 

The moving party bears the initial burden of production to make a prima facie showing that there are no triable issues of material fact.  (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850.)  A defendant moving for summary judgment must show either (1) that one or more elements of the cause of action cannot be established or (2) that there is a complete defense to that cause of action.  (Id. at §437c(p).)  A defendant may discharge this burden by furnishing either (1) affirmative evidence of the required facts or (2) discovery responses conceding that the plaintiff lacks evidence to establish an essential element of the plaintiff's case. If a defendant chooses the latter option he or she must present evidence “and not simply point out that plaintiff does not possess and cannot reasonably obtain needed evidence….” Aguilar, supra, 25 Cal.4th at 865-66,

[A] defendant may simply show the plaintiff cannot establish an essential element of the cause of action “by showing that the plaintiff does not possess, and cannot reasonably obtain, needed evidence.” (Id. at p. 854.)  Thus, rather than affirmatively disproving or negating an element (e.g., causation), a defendant moving for summary judgment has the option of presenting evidence reflecting the plaintiff does not possess evidence to prove that element. “The defendant may, but need not, present evidence that conclusively negates an element of the plaintiff's cause of action. The defendant may also present evidence that the plaintiff does not possess, and cannot reasonably obtain, needed evidence—as through admissions by the plaintiff following extensive discovery to the effect that he has discovered nothing” to support an essential element of his case. (Aguilar, supra, at p. 855.) Under the latter approach, a defendant's initial evidentiary showing may “consist of the deposition testimony of the plaintiff's witnesses, the plaintiff's factually devoid discovery responses, or admissions by the plaintiff in deposition or in response to requests for admission that he or she has not discovered anything that supports an essential element of the cause of action.” (Lona v. Citibank, N.A., supra, 202 Cal.App.4th at p. 110.)  In other words, a defendant may show the plaintiff does not possess evidence to support an element of the cause of action by means of presenting the plaintiff's factually devoid discovery responses from which an absence of evidence may be reasonably inferred. (Scheiding v. Dinwiddie Construction Co. (1999) 69 Cal.App.4th 64, 83.)

 

Thus, a moving defendant has two means by which to shift the burden of proof under the summary judgment statute: “The defendant may rely upon factually insufficient discovery responses by the plaintiff to show that the plaintiff cannot establish an essential element of the cause of action sued upon. [Citation.] [Or a]lternatively, the defendant may utilize the tried and true technique of negating (‘disproving’) an essential element of the plaintiff's cause of action.” (Brantly v. Pisaro (1996) 42 Cal.App.4th 1591, 1598.)  Leyva v. Garcia (2018) 20 Cal.App.5th 1095, 1103.

 

Until the moving defendant has discharged its burden of proof, the opposing plaintiff has no burden to come forward with any evidence. Once the moving defendant has discharged its burden as to a particular cause of action, however, the plaintiff may defeat the motion by producing evidence showing that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action.  (Id. at §437c(p)(2).)  On a motion for summary judgment, the moving party's supporting documents are strictly construed and those of his opponent liberally construed, and doubts as to the propriety of summary judgment should be resolved against granting the motion.  (D’Amico v. Board of Medical Examiners (1974) 11 Cal.3d 1, 21.)

 

c.     Evidentiary Objections

Defendant submitted voluminous evidentiary objections with her opposition papers.  The Court will address the material objections below.  The Court declines to rule on the immaterial objections.  CCP §437c(q).

 

d.     Elements of Causes of Action

The complaint includes causes of action against Defendant for breach of guaranty and “on account.”  As Defendant correctly notes in opposition, there is no recognized cause of action in California for “on account.”  It appears Plaintiff intended to plead a common count cause of action for “account stated.”  In any event, the burden on the motion is to show that Defendant has an obligation to pay monies due to Plaintiff and has failed to do so; the failure to brief the elements of the causes of action is not fatal to the motion, as the Court is familiar with the elements of a cause of action for breach of contract and one for common counts.

 

e.     Authentication of Guaranty

Plaintiff purports to authenticate the guaranty via the Declaration of Mark LaMendola.  Defendant, in opposition, contends Plaintiff confusingly submitted four different LaMendola declarations in support of the motion.  Plaintiff submitted various LaMendola declarations in support of its request for default judgment against the other defendants, but only submitted one LaMendola declaration in connection with this motion.  Plaintiff does, however, rely on the other declarations in its separate statement, which is quite confusing. 

 

In the declaration filed with the moving papers, LaMendola declares he is Director of Commercial Operations for Plaintiff and has competent knowledge of the information set forth in the declaration due to his position.  He declares he has access to records, including records relating to loans.  He declares the records are maintained in the ordinary course of business and were made near the time of the occurrence of the events they reflect.  He declares it is Plaintiff’s practice to keep such records in the ordinary course of business.  He declares that, on 3/28/24, Ronald and Barbar Ruffalo executed a continuing guaranty on behalf of the 1981 trust, and indicated “See Exhibit A.” 

 

Defendant correctly notes that there is a failure to authenticate Exhibit A.  Setting aside any other potential problems identified by Defendant, LaMendola did not even state that Exhibit A is a true and correct copy of what it purports to be.  The burden on summary judgment is construed strictly against the party seeking judgment, and absent a statement, under penalty of perjury, that Exhibit A is a true and correct copy of what it purports to be, the foundation objection is sustained and Plaintiff fails to meet its burden to show it is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.

 

c.         Damages Calculations

The Court notes, though not necessary to a resolution of the merits of the motion, that the damages calculations suffer from a similar defect.  LaMendola, in his 12/10/25 declaration filed in support of Plaintiff’s request for judgment against the defaulted co-defendants, indicates, by way of example, that Plaintiff gave Defendants a small credit in the amount of $4000 in early 2025.  ¶10.  He fails, however, to indicate how that credit was calculated, what it was for, or anything else about the credit.  Additionally, on the last page of the list of invoices LaMendola attaches as Exhibit 1 to the 12/10/25 declaration, there are eleven lines showing various amounts of interest purportedly due, but there is no explanation anywhere in the declarations detailing how that interest was calculated. 

 

As Defendant correctly notes in opposition to the motion, pursuant to CDF Firefighters v. Maldonado (2008) 158 Cal.App.4th 1226, 1239, when a plaintiff seeks summary judgment of a contract cause of action, an element of that cause of action is damages, which must be proved up in full in order to obtain a judgment on the cause of action. 

 

The motion is therefore also denied due to Plaintiff’s failure to provide evidence to establish the amount of the damages sought.

 

f.      Additional Issues

The Court declines to rule on Defendant’s arguments relating to the effectiveness of the continuing guaranty, as doing so is not necessary to a resolution of the merits of the motion, which is denied as set forth above.

 

g.     Conclusion

The motion for summary judgment is denied.  Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV02551    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: S27

 

  1. Background Facts

Plaintiffs, Zachary Staebler and Thi X. Staebler filed this action against Defendant, American Honda Motor Co., Inc. for violations of the Song-Beverly Act, breach of implied warranty, and fraudulent inducement – concealment. 

 

  1. Motion for Summary Adjudication

The caption of Defendant’s motion indicates it is a motion for summary adjudication, not for summary judgment.  It was reserved as a motion for summary adjudication. 

 

Defendant’s notice of motion indicates Defendant moves for “judgment in its favor because Plaintiffs do not have any evidence to support the causes of action against AHM or prayer for punitive damages.”  It goes on to state, “AHM is entitled to judgment in its favor as a matter of law because Plaintiffs do not have any evidence to support their fifth cause of action for fraudulent inducement against AHM.”  The notice of motion closes by indicating AHM “bases its motion for summary judgment or, alternatively, summary adjudication” on various documents. 

 

 Defendant’s notice of motion does not specify what issues it seeks to have adjudicated, and its separate statement does not comply with CRC 3.1350(b) and (h), which require the notice of motion to set forth each issue to be adjudicated and the separate statement to repeat the issues verbatim.  The separate statement states two purported grounds for summary adjudication of the concealment cause of action, but does not address any other cause of action, and does not address punitive damages. 

 

It is not clear whether Defendant seeks summary judgment, summary adjudication of the cause of action for fraudulent concealment, and/or summary adjudication of the prayer for punitive damages.  Because the notice is defective, the motion is summarily denied. 

 

Defendant is ordered to give notice.
