DEPARTMENT 517 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22STCV19102    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 517

Following a bench trial in this case, the Court ruled in favor of LA Taxi Cooperative, Inc. (“LATC”) on the claims of Plaintiff Israel Tadesse (“Tadesse”).  On February 6, 2026, LATC filed and served a proposed judgment memorializing the Court's ruling.  The Court entered the judgment that same day.  On February 26, 2026, LATC filed a memorandum of costs that it seeks to recover from Tadesse as the prevailing party in the action.   The amount of the costs is $6,893.54.  Pending before the Court is Tadesse’s motion to strike or tax the costs in LATC’s memorandum of costs. The Court is denying Tadesse’s motion.

Tadesse argues that LATC’s filing of the memorandum of costs was untimely under rule 3.1700 (a) of the California Rules of Court.  The Court disagrees.

Rule 3.1700(a) requires a prevailing party in a civil action to serve and file a memorandum of costs setting forth the costs that the party claims that it is entitled to recover from the other party/parties over which it prevailed.  It specifies the following deadlines to do that: “[W]ithin 15 days after the date of service of the notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5 or the date of service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal, or within 180 days after entry of judgment, whichever is first.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(a)(1).)  Rule 3.1700(a) thus has a 15-day deadline to file and serve the memorandum of costs based on either the clerk’s service of notice of entry of judgment or dismissal under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5 (“section 664.5”) or a party’s service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal; if there is no notice that triggers the 15-day deadline, then the deadline falls back to 180 days after the entry of judgment.

Tadesse argues that the clerk of the Court gave notice of entry of the judgment on February 6, 2026 and thus LATC’s filing of the memorandum of costs on February 26, 2026 was past the 15-day deadline, even factoring into two additional days for the electronic service of the notice of entry of judgment.  Tadesse is mistaken.  The clerk did not give notice of entry of judgment.  The clerk gave LATC notice of the judgment and the minute order stating that the judgment was entered.  But the Court directed LATC’s counsel, not the clerk, to give notice of entry of the judgment.  LATC’s counsel did that on February 13, 2026.  LATC’s filing of the memorandum of costs on February 26, 2026 fell within the 15-day deadline that started to run when the notice of entry of judgment was filed and served by LATC’s counsel.

Tadesse fares no better with his argument for the striking or taxing of the items of costs set forth in the memorandum on the ground that the costs were not reasonably necessary for the conduct of the litigation and were not supported.  The memorandum of costs sets forth three categories of costs that LATC seeks to recover (1) filing and motion fees; (2) deposition costs; and (3) court reporter fees.   Each category reflects costs that a prevailing party is entitled to recover.  Further, the amount of the costs, which LATC documented, was reasonably necessary for the conduct of LATC’s litigation.


Case Number: 25STCV31343    Hearing Date: September 16, 2026    Dept: 517

The Court is granting Defendant's motion to stay the proceedings pending the disposition of the appeal in Variety Media, LLC v. Superior Court, No. B350578.   The Court agrees with Defendant that the disposition of the appeal in the Variety Media case likely will directly affect the core legal issues in Plaintiff's action against the Defendant here in this case. The Court of Appeal heard oral argument in Variety Media on August 25, 2026.  The Court of Appeal's decision must be issued within 90 days of that date.  Under the circumstances, a stay of proceedings in this case is warranted.  In the Court's view, the stay will promote efficiency and conserve the resources of the parties and the Court.  The Court is thus declining at this time to hear the Defendant's demurrer to the Plaintiff's First Amended Complaint, which is on calendar on September 16, 2026.  The Court is continuing the hearing on the demurrer to March 2, 2027 at 8:30.  Defendant shall file and serve 21 days prior to the continued hearing date a supplemental brief in support of the demurrer setting forth the Defendant's position on the implications of the Court of Appeal's decision in the Valley Media case.  Plaintiff shall file and serve 7 days prior to the continued hearing date a supplemental brief in opposition to the demurrer setting forth Plaintiff's position on the implications of the Court of Appeal's decision in the Valley Media case.  Defendant shall not file a reply to the Plaintiff's supplemental brief.

Defendant's stay motion was additionally based on a separate pending appeal in Lucky Opco, LLC v. Superior Court, No. B352848. The Court's decision to grant Defendant's motion is not based on the Lucky Opco case. That is because oral argument in the Court of Appeal in that case has yet to be set.
