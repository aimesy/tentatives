21STCV21246 S.C. an incompetent, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Sergio Cardenas. v. County of Los Angeles, et al.

 

Friday, September 18, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING REQUEST FOR AN ORDER FOR DETERMINATION OF GOOD FAITH SETTLEMENT

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING THE PETITION FOR APPROVAL OF COMPROMISE OF CLAIM FOR A PERSON WITH A DISABILITY (2020)

 

I.          BACKGROUND

      The third amended complaint (“TAC”) alleges that Plaintiff is under the jurisdiction of Los Angeles Juvenile Dependency Court. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants collectively caused Plaintiff to be placed in improper care settings without an adequate case plan and deprived Plaintiff of access to services

      Settling Defendants who signed the stipulation with Plaintiff for good faith determination are ABC Unified School District and William Gee. Non-settling Defendants are Los Angeles Unified School District and College Hospital, Inc., dba College Hospital Cerritos.

II.   DISCUSSION

      The stipulation does not comply with the statute governing good faith determination. Settling parties may proceed by way of a noticed motion in compliance with Code Civ. Proc., § 1005 as joint tortfeasors are entitled to a hearing on the issue of good faith of a settlement entered into by the plaintiff or other claimant and one or more alleged tortfeasors. (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6 subd. (b)(1).)

      Alternatively, the settling parties can serve a notice of settlement with the application for good faith settlement determination by certified mail to all other parties. If a non-settling defendant does not file a motion to contest settlement within 25 days, the court can sign the order. (Id.)

      The statute does not permit a determination of good faith settlement by stipulation and order served electronically on all other parties.

      Accordingly, the Court denies the request to sign the stipulation and order submitted by settling parties on September 15, 2026.

 

 

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING THE PETITION FOR APPROVAL OF COMPROMISE OF CLAIM FOR A PERSON WITH A DISABILITY (2020)

 

      Plaintiff seeks the Court’s approval of Plaintiff’s settlement with Defendants, ABC Unified School District and William Gee. The petition is premature as the Court has not determined that settlement was made in good faith. Additionally, the petition is incomplete as Plaintiff’s counsel proposes to retain $100,000 of the settlement proceeds in counsel’s trust account to be “applied to on-going litigation costs” and further anticipated compromise petitions. (Pet. Att. 3b, p.5.)

      While the Department of Child and Family Services does not assert a lien at this time as Plaintiff represents in the petition, Plaintiff anticipates that DCFS may present liens against future additional settlements. (Id. para. 25). Similarly, the petition is premature in that Plaintiff defers reimbursement of costs at this time “and defer[s] them to future anticipated compromise petition approvals.” (Id. p. 5, 25.) The Court declines to engage in piecemeal approval.

      The petition also anticipates the formation of a special needs trust with $100,000 to be paid to the conservator, Maya Rubin, who is the proposed trustee. (Id. p. 6, para 3.) If funds are distributed to a special needs trust, the Probate Division must review the terms of the trust which must include the provisions required by the California Rules of Court, rule 7.903 and Los Angeles Superior Court Rule 4.116. Los Angeles Super. Ct. Rule A 4.115 subd. (c). 

      Additionally, to facilitate a timely review, a party seeking to establish and fund a trust as part of a petition for approval of a compromise must, within two court days of the filing of the petition for approval of compromise and trust, lodge with the filing window of the Probate Division at the Stanley Mosk Courthouse a physical copy of the face page of the petition to approve compromise and attach a copy of the proposed trust instrument and the proposed order approving the compromise and trust. (Id. subd. (b).)

      Accordingly, the Petition is DENIED.

 

 

 


Case Number: 24STCV04196    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV04196 Samuel Pascual Yacon Ricac, et al. v. Daunita Rene Scott, et al.

Friday, September 18, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER TAKING OFF CALENDAR DEFENDANTS’ MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION OF PERSON MOST KNOWLEDGEABLE OF NATIONAL GENERAL INSURANCE COMPANY (2490)

 

      This action arises from injuries Plaintiffs allegedly sustained in a motor vehicle accident. Defendants move to compel the deposition of the person most knowledgeable at National General Insurance Company (“NGIC”), an entity who is not a party in this action. Defendants personally served a deposition subpoena for personal appearance and production of records on NGIC on March 26, 2026. (Mot.  Ex. A.)

      The motion is defective as the first proof of service attached to the motion reflects that Defendants served the motion on NGIC by mail only. The nonparty deponent must be personally served with the motion “unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail or electronic service at an address or electronic service address specified on the deposition record.” (Cal Rules of Court, Rule 3.1346.).

      Defendants filed another proof of service on September 11, 2026, showing personal service of the motion on NGIC was accomplished on September 2, 2026. Moving parties are required to give 16 days of notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).)

      Accordingly, the court takes the motion OFF CALENDAR.


Case Number: 24STCV09235    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV09235 Tomika Smalls v. Lawrence Title

Friday, September 18, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING DEFENDANT LAWRENCE TITLE’S MOTION TO ENFORCE THE PARTIES’ JANUARY 7, 2026, SETTLEMENT AND FOR AN ORDER ENTERING AND/OR MODIFYING THE JUDGMENT (1619)

 

I.         BACKGROUND

       The first amended complaint alleges that Defendant employed Plaintiff as a resident/ property manager. Plaintiff alleges nine causes of action for wage and hour claims under the Labor Code and a claim for unlawful/unfair business practices.  

      The Court granted Defendant’s first motion to enforce the parties’ settlement and entered judgment pursuant to that agreement was memorialized on the record on January 7, 2026. (M.O. 4/10/26.) The Court also ordered Plaintiff to (1) sign the Stipulation for Judgment of Possession; and (2) sign a dismissal of her cross-complaint in Case No. 25NNCV01902, to be filed along with Title’s dismissal of the complaint. (M.O. 4/20/26.)

      The Court denied Defendants’ application for an OSC Re: Contempt and for imposition of sanctions against Plaintiff for Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the Court’s order. (M.O. 7/2/26.) Instead of complying with the order, Plaintiff filed a notice to relate this matter to the Burbank action. (Id.) That action was dismissed by the Court for lack of prosecution. (Id. p. 2.) Accordingly, the Court denied the motion as moot. (Id.)

 

II.        ARGUMENTS

      Defendant now requests an order to enforce the January 7, 2026, settlement or to amend or modify the judgment to include a mutual and unconditional release of all claims; a provision barring Plaintiff from filing any claims or lawsuits with respect to any issues arising from or relating to conduct that occurred before the January 7, 2026, settlement; as well as a non-disparagement clause.

      Plaintiff’s opposition raises issues of bad faith, a contention that the settlement was fraudulent, and complaints against Plaintiff’s former counsel. Plaintiff argues that Defendant concedes that a long-form agreement was never signed. Plaintiff contends that the Court cannot modify a judgment in “invent new, unagreed-upon terms.” Plaintiff contends that the Court should reject Defendant’s attempt to rely on an unverified, and unredacted transcript.

III.       LEGAL STANDARDS

      A party can move for entry of judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6.) The court’s power is limited to determining the existence of the agreement and to enforce its settlement. (Corkland v. Boscoe (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 989, 994.) The court may receive oral testimony or may determine the motion upon declarations alone. (Id.) The court does not make modifications or force terms not agreed to in the written agreement. (Id.) at 207.

      Interpreting contracts is a judicial function. (Cisneros Alvarez v. Altamed Health Services Corporation (2021) 60 Cal.App.5th 572, 585 [“Ordinarily, the objective intent of the contracting parties is a legal question determined solely by reference to the contract's terms.” (Id.)  The court applies general contract principles when interpreting a settlement agreement.
(Leeman v. Adams Extract & Spice, LLC (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1367, 1374.)The mutual intent of the parties and interpretation of the contract are based on the language of the agreement alone. (Id.)

      In construing contracts, "[t]he words of a contract are to be understood in their ordinary and popular sense, rather than according to their strict legal meaning; unless used by the parties in a technical sense, or unless a special meaning is given to them by usage, in which case the latter must be followed. (Civ. Code, § 1644.) If the contract’s language is clear and unambiguous, the court is "bound to give effect to the plain and ordinary meaning of the language used by the parties." (Coast Plaza Doctors Hosp. v. Blue Cross of California (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 677, 684.)

IV.       DISCUSSION

      Defendant’s first motion to enforce settlement identified the terms of settlement as follows:  Plaintiff would 1) would vacate her apartment within 60 days after the initial payment by Title, (2) would, in the interim, execute a Stipulation for Judgment of Possession of her apartment, and (3) would dismiss her cross complaint in Case No. 25NNCV01902. The Court granted the motion and ordered that judgment be entered consistent with the terms of settlement. The settlement agreement contemplated that the agreement would be formalized in a long-form agreement.

      Defendant admits that the contemplated long-form agreement included mutual general releases and a non-disparagement clause. Defendant seeks amendment of judgment to include those releases although Plaintiff did not sign a long-form agreement that included mutual releases and a non-disparagement clause. Defendant admits Plaintiff will not enter into a long-form agreement with those terms. (Reply 2:2-6.)

      Defendant’s reply states that the parties agreed to mutual general releases on the record and inclusion of a non-disparagement clause, but there is no evidence that Plaintiff agreed to the terms proposed by Defendant’s motion. Defendant does not cite to the portion of the January 7, 2026, transcript that specifically addressed the language of the provisions that Defendant now wants to insert. Additionally, Defendant requests inclusion of a provision barring future claims by Plaintiff, which does not appear on the January 7, 2026 transcript.

      The Court is not authorized "to create the material terms of a settlement, as opposed to deciding what terms the parties themselves have previously agreed upon." (Osumi v. Sutton (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 1355, 1360 [italics in original].) The January 7, 2026 transcript generally referred to an exchange of mutual general releases, a non-disparagement clause with a liquidated damages clause, and confidentiality provision. (D’s Ex. 1, 13:3-6, 14:5-10, 15:13-14.) The proposed language of the anticipated clauses were not read into the record.               

V.        CONCLUSION

      Accordingly, the Court declines to amend judgment to include specific terms that Defendant now proposes. Defendant’s motion is DENIED.

 


Case Number: 24STCV12187    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV12187 Arno Kuigoua v. The Matthew L. Tuck & Associates, PLC, et al.

Thursday, September 18, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT (5199)

 

I.       BACKGROUND

      The complaint alleges claims for legal malpractice for Defendants’ alleged failure to petition the California Supreme Court challenging the Court of Appeal’s denial of Plaintiff’s petition for a writ of mandate. Plaintiff retained Defendants pursuant to an April 16, 2021 contract. The underlying litigation arose from Plaintiff’s administrative challenge of his termination as a registered nurse with the Department of Veteran’s Affairs. Plaintiff alleges claims for professional malpractice, breach of contract, and breach of fiduciary duty.

      The Hon. Jon R. Takasugi sustained Defendants’ demurrer to the complaint without leave to amend and dismissed the action. (M.O. 12/13/24.) The Second District Court of Appeal reversed. (Remittitur filed 3/25/26.)

      Prior to this case being transferred to Department 414, Judge Takasugi related this case to 24STCV33723 Arno Patrick Kuigoua v. Corey Parker, et al. (“the Parker Action”). Plaintiff alleges that the Parker Defendants are appellate lawyers, who Plaintiff retained on August 25, 2020 to prepare and file a writ petition with the California Supreme Court to review an order by the Court of Appeal. (Parker Action Complaint, ¶¶ 10-13.)  

II.     ARGUMENTS

      Plaintiff requests leave to file a first amended complaint to clarify operative facts, conform the pleading to the appellate ruling, and plead facts related to the Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU”) that outlined a deal under which Plaintiff agreed to release all claims against Defendants and forego filing a lawsuit. (Remittitur filed 3/25/26, p. 3.) Plaintiff proposes to allege additional causes of action for fraud, concealment, promissory fraud, negligent misrepresentation, unfair competition, equitable relief and declaratory relief concerning the disputed MOU/release.

      Plaintiff argues the amendments will promote judicial economy by framing all disputed issues in one pleading. Defendants contend the motion is procedurally defective and the amendment will result in prejudice, Plaintiff does not offer any reasons for the delay, Plaintiff has not been diligent and has created overlapping litigation. Defendants argue the new tort claims and claims for punitive damages are defective and amendment will be futile. Defendants  assert that if leave is granted it should be narrowly conditioned.

III.    LEGAL STANDARDS

      The court can permit amendment of any pleading on any terms and “in other particulars” as may be proper. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473(a).) The court’s discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendment of the pleadings. (Nestle v. City of Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 939.) If the motion is timely made, and the granting of the motion will not result in prejudice to the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend. (Morgan v. Superior Court of Cal. In and For Los Angeles County (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.) Amendments are liberally permitted up to the date of trial or during trial where no prejudice is shown. (Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2003) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 761.)

IV.   DISCUSSION

A.     The motion is not procedurally defective.

      The motion complies with the requirements of Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324. Plaintiff provides a red-lined version of the proposed pleading showing additions and deletions. (Mot. Ex. B.) Plaintiff’s declaration explains the amendments and reasons therefor, chiefly that Plaintiff did not allege facts concerning the parties’ MOU, which was the basis for the trial Court’s order sustaining demurrer on grounds Plaintiff had released all claims.

      The Court of Appeal reversed and remanded because the MOU was improperly considered by the trial court, who also resolved questions of fact based on facts represented by counsel and facts that did not appear in the four corners of the pleading. (Remittitur p. 8.)

      Plaintiff declares the facts concerning the proposed new allegations were discovered over time, and Plaintiff desires to conform the pleading after the Court of Appeal’s remand. Plaintiff could not make the amendments earlier because the trial court dismissed the action after sustaining demurrer. (P’s decl.).

B.     Defendants have not shown unreasonable delay.

      The Court of Appeal issued its remittitur on March 25, 2026. After filing an Affidavit of Prejudice against Judge Takasugi, which the court granted, Plaintiff filed this motion for leave to amend on June 1, 2026. Plaintiff did not have an opportunity to seek leave to amend earlier given that the action had been previously dismissed without leave to amend.

      C.   Defendants have not shown substantial prejudice.

      Defendants argue they will be required to pursue additional discovery, prepare new defenses, alter litigation strategy or seek continuance of trial.  However, it does not appear from the Court’s record that the parties have conducted any discovery given the case was at the demurrer stage when it was dismissed.

      Defendants cite Magpali v. Farmers Group, Inc. (1996) 48 Cal.App.4th 471, which is inapposite. There, Plaintiff sought to amend the complaint three days before trial by expanding the claims asserted and opening up entirely new fields of inquiry "without any satisfactory explanation as to why this major change in point of attack had not been made long before trial. (Magpali at 487.)

      Defendants contend they will need to “reopen” discovery. It is not clear what discovery was conducted in the related Parker Action. After filing a demurrer, the Parker Defendants filed a motion to declare Plaintiff a vexatious litigant on April 21, 2025, resulting in an automatic stay of the action until the motion was denied on July 25, 2025. (Code Civ. Proc., § 391.7 subd. (c).) Thereafter, the Parker Defendants demurred to Plaintiff’s complaint and moved to consolidate the related matters. The Hon. Tony L. Richardson declined to rule on the demurrer and other matters given Plaintiff’s representation that Plaintiff would file a notice to relate the cases. (Parker action, M.O. 1/29/26.)  In any event, Plaintiff moves to amend the pleading in this action.

      Defendants argue that this action is “nearly identical” to the Parker action which asserts malpractice in a similar petition-for-review matter. Defendants assert Plaintiff knew Parker’s identity before filing this motion. This is not a basis for denying leave to amend. Defendants raise concern over duplicative discovery and inconsistent rulings which are equally not grounds for denying leave to amend.

      To the extent Defendants argue that the new claims are defective, the court does not consider the validity of the amendments at this stage. The “better course is to allow a plaintiff to amend the complaint and then let the parties test its legal sufficiency in other appropriate proceedings.” (Brown v. Ralphs Grocery Co. (2018) 28 Cal.App.5th 824, 843.) For that reason, the Court denies Defendants’ request to narrowly condition the amended pleading to limit the proposed claims before considering the merits of a demurrer.

V.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file a first amended complaint is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ordered to file the amended pleading within 10 days.


Case Number: 24STCV21891    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV21891 Jenny Song v. Kevin Song, et al.

Friday, September 18, 2026

[TENTATIVE] ORDER TAKING OFF CALENDAR MOTION OF U.S. BANK, NATIONAL ASSOCIATION (1) TO COMPEL THE DEPOSITION OF HEEJAE WON AND THE PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, (2) FOR OSC RE CONTEMPT AGAINST HEEJAE WON, AND (3) FOR SANCTIONS

 

I.         BACKGROUND

      The second amended complaint arises from a dispute over the validity of a deed of trust recorded against real property located at 606-608 N. Fuller Avenue in Los Angeles, alleged to be owned by Plaintiff. Plaintiff alleges that her estranged father, Kevin Song, (“Kevin”) used the property as security for a loan obtained from Velocity Commercial Capital LLC (“Velocity”). Kevin allegedly incorporated a company with the same name as Plaintiff’s previously dissolved company, Lioness Investment, Inc. Kevin allegedly procured the loan by falsely claiming that his company with the same name owned the Fuller Avenue real property, which served as security for the $1,295,000 loan. Velocity subsequently recorded a Deed of Trust (DOT), which Plaintiff alleges is void.

      Defendant, US Bank National Association (“USB”) (transferee of the Velocity note), filed a cross-complaint against Kevin Song, Angela Song, and Lioness Investment, Inc. for breach of contract, fraud, negligent misrepresentation, indemnity, and related claims.

      USB moves to compel the deposition of Heejae Won and to order production of requested documents within three weeks of the Court’s order. USB also asks for an order setting a contempt hearing and to impose monetary sanctions.  

 

II.        DISCUSSION

      The deponent, Hee Jae Won (“Won”) is not a party to this action. Won purportedly received the funds from the loan obtained by Kevin from Velocity.

      A written notice and all moving papers supporting a motion to compel an answer to a deposition question or to compel production of a document or tangible thing from a nonparty deponent "must be personally served on the nonparty deponent unless the nonparty deponent agrees to accept service by mail or electronic service at an address or electronic service address specified on the deposition record." (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1346.)

      The proof of service attached to the motion does not indicate that the deponent was served with the motion in any manner. Accordingly, the motion is TAKEN OFF CALENDAR for defective service of the motion.


Case Number: 24STCV27759    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 414

24STCV27759 Tenor Health Partners, LLC v. Pipeline Health System Holdings, LLC

Friday, September 18, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND AND FILE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT (8384)

 

I.        BACKGROUND

      This action arises from Defendant’s alleged failure to purchase Plaintiff’s assets pursuant to the parties’ letter of intent. Plaintiff alleges contract-related claims, fraud, and declaratory relief.

      Plaintiff requests leave to file a first amended complaint to add a claim for conversion and to clarify allegations regarding the written letter of intent for the purchase and sale of a hospital in Dallas, Texas. The amendment will clarify the failure of required conditions. The conversion claim arises from the same set of alleged facts. After Defendant sold the hospital to a third party, Defendant failed to return Plaintiff’s $250,000 deposit. The amendment clarifies how Defendant did not comply with the requirements of the contract’s exclusivity period.

      Defendant argues the delay is unreasonable and unexplained. Defendant will suffer prejudice. If the Court grants the motion, Defendant asks the Court to continue trial and reopen discovery.

II.   LEGAL STANDARDS

      The court can permit amendment of any pleading on any terms and “in other particulars” as may be proper. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473(a).) The court’s discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendment of the pleadings. (Nestle v. City of Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 939.) If the motion is timely made, and the granting of the motion will not result in prejudice to the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend. (Morgan v. Superior Court of Cal. In and For Los Angeles County (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.) Amendments are liberally permitted up to the date of trial or during trial where no prejudice is shown. (Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2003) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 761.)

III.  DISCUSSION

      Plaintiff did not make this motion earlier because the parties were engaged in discovery from March through August of 2025. (Felton decl., ¶4.) Each party’s deposition was taken in October and November of 2025. (Id.) The parties attempted to resolve the dispute through mediation on March 4, 2026 but were unsuccessful (Id. ¶ 5.) Plaintiff then met and conferred with Defendant regarding the amendment, but on May 12, 2026, Defendant informed that it would not stipulate to an amendment. (Id. ¶ 5.) Plaintiff then filed this motion on June 8, 2026.

      Defendant’s contention that it will suffer prejudice because it will have to engage in discovery and potentially follow-up discovery regarding the new claim is unpersuasive. Notably, the opposition concedes that “[t]his case has always been about one failed hospital acquisition, one non-binding letter of intent, and one $250,000 deposit.” (Opp. 1:2-3.) Given the deposit has always been at issue in the parties’ dispute, there is no basis for continuance of trial at this stage. The trial is currently set for November 2, 2026. However, Defendant is not without remedy as it can move to continue trial and reopen discovery upon making the required showing.

      Defendant’s argument that Plaintiff has reversed position is not a basis for denying the motion. The original complaint alleged the date that the 30-day exclusivity period began. The amended pleading proposes to identify other conditions for when the 30-day period began; the allegations are additional not contradictory.

      Regardless, this argument and other arguments that Plaintiff proposes “reversals” of prior allegations do not affect the liberal policy to permit amendments absent prejudice to the opposing party. To the extent the “reversals” render the proposed pleading defective, the court does not consider the validity of the amendments at this stage. The “better course is to allow a plaintiff to amend the complaint and then let the parties test its legal sufficiency in other appropriate proceedings.” (Brown v. Ralphs Grocery Co. (2018) 28 Cal.App.5th 824, 843.)

V.   CONCLUSION

      Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file an amended complaint is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ordered to file the amended pleading within 10 days.

 


Case Number: 25STCV19390    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: 414

25STCV19390 William McKenna v. FCA US, LLC et al.

Friday, September 18, 2026

 

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL DISCLOSURES AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 871.26 (3357)

 

I.         BACKGROUND

      This action arises from alleged violations of the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act in connection with a warranty contract issued by Defendant manufacturer for a 2022 Jeep Wrangler

      Plaintiff argues Defendant failed to disclose initial documents as required by statute. Plaintiff asks for an order compelling Defendant’s compliance by producing documents and issuing mandatory sanctions of $2,500 against Defendant FCA US, LLC, and its counsel of record.

      Defendant contends it complied with its statutory obligations more than six months before Plaintiff’s motion was filed. (D’s Ex. 2.) Defendant provided over 1300 pages of materials including the warranty claims records, repair orders and reports, sales information, the owner’s manual, warranty information booklet, communications between Plaintiff and FCA US, and Technical Service Bulletins (“TSBs”) and recalls applicable to the Subject Vehicle. (Brown decl., ¶ 4.) After Plaintiff signed a protective order, Defendant made a confidential production on June 23, 2026 and a supplemental confidential production on June 24, 2026. (Brown decl., ¶ 7, Ex. 3-4.)

      The court has considered the motion, opposition, and supporting papers. Plaintiff did not file a reply brief.

II.        DISCUSSION

      The statute at issue requires both parties to disclose documents 60 days after the answer or responsive pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26(b). Defendant has shown that it provided initial disclosures on December 11, 2025. (D’s Ex. 2.) Plaintiff’s counsel does not declare that an initial disclosure was made. (Rucker decl.) Defendant informed that it would make a further production. (Id.) Supplemental disclosures were made on June 23 and 24, 2026 (D’s Ex. 3-5).

III.  CONCLUSION

      As Defendant complied with its statutory obligations, the motion is DENIED.
