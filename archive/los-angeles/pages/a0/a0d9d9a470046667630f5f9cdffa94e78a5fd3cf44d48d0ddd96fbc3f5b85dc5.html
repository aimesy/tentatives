TENTATIVE RULING – NO. 1

 

DEPARTMENT

207

HEARING DATE

September 28, 2026

CASE NUMBER

26SMCV02765

MOTION

Strike Answer

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Alanna Zabel

OPPOSING PARTY

Defendant Co-Opportunity Market (erroneously sued as Co-Opportunity Consumers Cooperative, Inc.)

 

MOTION

 

This case arises from allegations that Plaintiff was injured when she was unknowingly served a cup of scalding hot water. 

 

On May 20, 2026, Plaintiff Alanna Zabel (“Plaintiff”) filed suit against Defendant Co-opportunity Market “Co-opportunity Consumers Cooperative, Inc.” (“Defendant”) alleging four causes of action for (1) negligence; (2) premises liability; (3) negligent hiring, training, supervision, and retention; and (4) negligent infliction of emotional distress.

 

Plaintiff personally served Defendant with a copy of the summons and complaint on June 5, 2026, and Default was entered on July 16, 2026.

 

Notwithstanding, Defendant submitted an Answer on September 2, 2026, that was erroneously accepted and filed by the Clerk of the Court.

 

Plaintiff now moves for an order to strike Defendant’s Answer.  Defendant opposes the motion and Plaintiff replies.

 

ANALYSIS

 

I.                Procedural Requirements

 

Written notice of the motion shall be given at least sixteen (16) court days prior to the noticed hearing date.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b).)  Electronic service extends the time within which the responding party has to respond by an additional two (2) court days.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6.) 

 

Here, Plaintiff electronically served the instant motion on September 2, 2026 and noticed the hearing date for September 28, 2026.  With Court holidays on September 7 (Labor Day) and September 25 (Native American Day), September 2 was only sixteen (16) court days prior to the noticed hearing, instead of the required eighteen (18) to account for electronic service. 

 

Further, a party moving to strike a pleading shall meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference at least five (5) days before filing a motion to strike, and the motion to strike must be accompanied by a declaration describing compliance with the meet and confer requirement.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5.)  Here, Plaintiff’s motion does not include any such meet and confer declaration.

 

Therefore, the Court finds that Plaintiff’s motion is procedurally defective.

 

II.             Substance

 

Notwithstanding the procedural defect, the Court may “at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper” strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 436, subd. (b).

 

Here, the Court entered default against Defendant on July 16, 2026.  Accordingly, “the entry of a default terminates a defendant's rights to take any further affirmative steps in the  litigation until either its default is set aside or a default judgment is entered.”  (Devlin v. Kearny Mesa AMC/Jeep/Renault, Inc. (1984) 155 Cal.App.3d 381, 385.  Further, the entry of default “[c]uts off the defendant's right to file pleadings and motions (other than a motion to set aside default under § 473), and it also cuts off the defendant's right to notices and the service of pleadings or papers.”  (Bailey v. Citibank, N.A. (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 335, 347, emphasis added.) 

 

Therefore, the Court, on its own motion, strikes the Answer erroneously filed after the entry of default, as it was not filed in conformity with the laws of this State.

 

Notwithstanding, “Proceeding to judgment in the absence of a party is an extraordinary and disfavored practice in Anglo–American jurisprudence:  The policy of the law is to have every litigated case tried upon its merits, and it looks with disfavor upon a party, who, regardless of the merits of the case, attempts to take advantage of the mistake, surprise, inadvertence, or neglect of his adversary.”  (Au-Yang v. Barton (1999) 21 Cal.4th 958, 963 [cleaned up].) 

 

Further, “the law strongly favors trial and disposition on the merits,” and as such, any motion to set aside entry of default is to be liberally construed in favor of setting aside the default so that the case may be heard on the merits.  (Minick v. City of Petaluma (2016) 3 Cal.App.5th 15, 24-25.)

 

As such, to prevent further burdening the Court with unnecessary motion practice, and in furtherance of the law’s strong preference for disposition of cases on the merits, the Court strongly encourages the parties to meet and confer forthwith regarding a stipulation to set aside the entry of default against Defendant. 

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

 

For the reasons stated, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion on procedural grounds, but on the Court’s own motion, finding the Answer was erroneously entered after the entry of default, the Court strikes Defendant’s Answer.     

 

The Court notes that the only named Defendant has defaulted, and as such, notice of the Court’s ruling may be unnecessary (see Sporn v. Home Depot USA, Inc. (2005) 126 Cal.App.4th 1294, 1301 [after default has been entered, the defaulted defendant is no longer an active party in the litigation and thus is not entitled to further notices].)  Notwithstanding, to the extent necessary, Plaintiff shall provide notice of the Court’s ruling and file the notice with a proof of service forthwith.

TENTATIVE RULING – NO. 2

 

DEPARTMENT

HEARING DATE

September 28, 2026

CASE NUMBER

26SMCV02765

MOTION

Motion to Seal

MOVING PARTIES

Plaintiff Alanna Zabel

OPPOSING PARTY

none

 

MOTION

 

This case arises from allegations that Plaintiff was injured when she was unknowingly served a cup of scalding hot water. 

 

On May 20, 2026, Plaintiff Alanna Zabel (“Plaintiff”) filed suit against Defendant Co-opportunity Market “Co-opportunity Consumers Cooperative, Inc.” (“Defendant”) alleging four causes of action for (1) negligence; (2) premises liability; (3) negligent hiring, training, supervision, and retention; and (4) negligent infliction of emotional distress.

 

Plaintiff personally served Defendant with a copy of the summons and complaint on June 5, 2026, and Default was entered on July 16, 2026.

 

Plaintiff now moves for an order to file certain exhibits in support of her Application for Default Judgment under seal.  The motion is unopposed.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

            Unless confidentiality is required by law, court records are presumed to be open to the public, pursuant to a potent “open court” policy undergirded by the First Amendment and favoring the public nature of court proceedings.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.550(c); see¿NBC Subsidiary (KNBC-TV), Inc. v. Superior Court¿(1999)¿20 Cal.4th 1178, 1199-10.)¿ Consequently, pleadings, motions, discovery documents, and other papers may not be filed under seal merely by stipulation of the parties; filing under seal requires a court order.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551(a); see¿H.B. Fuller Co. v. Doe¿(2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 879, 888.)¿

 

A sealing order must be sought by means of a motion (or application) and accompanied by a memorandum of points and authorities, as well as evidence and testimony containing facts sufficient to justify the mandatory findings required to support a sealing order.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rules 2.550(d) & 2.551(b).)¿ The proponent of the sealing order must also conditionally lodge the¿unredacted¿matter to be sealed with the court.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551(b)(4).)¿

 

To grant a motion to seal, a trial court must expressly find that: (1) an overriding interest exists that overcomes the right of public access to the record; (2) the overriding interest supports sealing the records; (3) a substantial probability exists that the overriding interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed; (4) the proposed sealing is narrowly tailored; and (5) no¿less restrictive means exist to achieve the overriding interest.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.550 (d).) “If the trial court fails to make the required findings, the order is deficient and cannot support sealing.” (Overstock.com, Inc. v. Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. (2014) 231 Cal.App.4th  471, 487; see also In re Marriage of Tamir (2021) 72 Cal.App.5th 1068, 1087 [“express findings must be made to seal records”].)

 

DISCUSSION

 

            As a procedural matter, “The party requesting that a record be filed under seal must lodge it with the court under (d) when the motion or application is made, unless good cause exists for not lodging it or the record has previously been lodged under (3)(A)(i).  Pending the determination of the motion or application, the lodged record will be conditionally under seal.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.551(b)(4).)

 

Plaintiff requests that the Court seal Exhibits 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13A, 14, 16, 17, 21, and 23 and “As to Exhibits 13, 15, 22, and 24” Plaintiff requests sealing only personal addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses, signatures where appropriate, financial-account information, or other nonpublic identifying information.” 

 

            Yet, Plaintiff has lodged under seal Exhibits 6-8, 9-11, 12, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, and 23.  Exhibits 13, 21, and 24 were lodged as “Unsealed/Public Exhibits.”  Additionally, the Court notes that documents lodged under seal contain further redactions. 

 

            As such, Plaintiff’s motion to seal does not match the lodging, and the Court cannot properly evaluate whether the redactions Plaintiff applied are appropriate.

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

 

Therefore, the Court denies Plaintiff motion to seal on procedural grounds without prejudice to Plaintiff’s filing a renewed motion, if appropriate.  The Court will return all lodged documents to Plaintiff to ensure the confidentiality of the records in the meantime.

 

The Court notes that the only named Defendant has defaulted, and as such, notice of the Court’s ruling may be unnecessary (see Sporn v. Home Depot USA, Inc. (2005) 126 Cal.App.4th 1294, 1301 [after default has been entered, the defaulted defendant is no longer an active party in the litigation and thus is not entitled to further notices].)  Notwithstanding, to the extent necessary, Plaintiff shall provide notice of the Court’s ruling and file the notice with a proof of service forthwith.

Case Number: 26SMCV02785    Hearing Date: September 28, 2026    Dept: 207

TENTATIVE ruling

 

DEPARTMENT

207

HEARING DATE

September 28, 2026

CASE NUMBER

26SMCV02785

MOTION

Enforce Settlement

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Verbena WRC Lincoln, LP

OPPOSING PARTY

none

 

MOTION

 

This case arises from allegations of an unlawful detainer. 

 

On May 20, 2026, Plaintiff Verbena WRC Lincoln, LP (“Plaintiff”) brought suit against Defendant Golden Road Food Services, LLC (“Defendant”) alleging a single cause of action for unlawful detainer.

 

Plaintiff now moves to enforce a settlement agreement between the parties.  The motion is unopposed.

 

ANALYSIS

 

I.                Enforce Settlement

 

“Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 provides a summary procedure to enforce a settlement agreement by entering judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”  (Hines v. Lukes (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 1174, 1182.)  In particular, Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 provides that “[i]f parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court or orally before the court, for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 664.6.) 

 

The issue on a motion to enforce settlement agreement under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 is whether the parties entered into a valid and binding settlement agreement. (See Viejo v. Bancorp. (1989) 217 Cal.App.3d 200, 209, fn. 4 [“a court's power to make factual determinations under section 664.6 is generally limited to whether the parties entered into a valid and binding settlement agreement”]; see also Hines v. Lukes, supra, 167 Cal.App.4th at p. 1182 [“A court ruling on a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6 must determine whether the parties entered into a valid and binding settlement”].)  In other words, the only issue before the court is whether an agreement exists concerning all of the material terms; not whether the agreement has been breached.  (See Hines v. Lukes, supra, 167 Cal.App.4th at p. 1182  [“A settlement is enforceable under section 664.6 only if the parties agreed to all material settlement terms”].)  “If the court finds that the parties failed to agree to all material terms, there is no enforceable settlement so the court must deny the motion for entry of judgment pursuant to a settlement.”  (Id. at p. 1185.)

Moreover, the Hines v. Lukes court stated: “The court ruling on the motion may consider the parties' declarations and other evidence in deciding what terms the parties agreed to, and the court's factual findings in this regard are reviewed under the substantial evidence standard.  If the court determines that the parties entered into an enforceable settlement, it should grant the motion and enter a formal judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.  The statute expressly provides for the court to enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”   (Hines v. Lukes, supra, 167 Cal.App.4th at pp. 1182–1183 [cleaned up].)

 

In support of the motion, Plaintiff advances a written Stipulation for Settlement signed by the parties on July 2, 2026 (by Defendant) and July 6, 2026 (by Plaintiff).  (Ex. 1 to Truong Decl.) 

 

The stipulation provides for Defendant to pay Plaintiff a total of $99,991.43, comprised of back rent in the amount of $97,142.35 plus $2,849.08 in attorneys’ fees.  The first $49,995.71 is due within five (5) days of full execution of the stipulation, and the remainder shall be paid in monthly installments of $4,166.31.  (Ex. 1 to Truong Decl. at ¶ 2-2.2.)  In addition, Defendant is required to continue to make all regular monthly rent payments.  (Ex. 1 to Truong Decl. at ¶ 2.3.)

 

Plaintiff further advances the Declarations of Kelly Weems and of Tiffany Truong.  Weems declares as follows:

 

3. Unfortunately, Plaintiff never received any payments due pursuant to the Settlement. We provided wiring information and the address for payment to Defendant. I have checked on multiple times since the Settlement was signed and we did not receive any payment.

 

(Weems Decl. ¶ 3.)  Similarly, the Truong Declaration provides:

 

3. Unfortunately, Plaintiff never received any payments due pursuant to the Settlement and have failed to comply with the terms of the Settlement. I was in direct contact with Defendant’s General Counsel, John Silvestri, to negotiate and sign the Settlement. Between July 6, 2026, and August 17, 2026, I was in contact with Mr. Silvestri on multiple occasions to coordinate payments due under the Settlement. Defendant’s team represented that payment was made or coming via wire on multiple occasions, but it was never received by Plaintiff. I proposed on multiple occasions that a physical check be submitted if the wire transfer was apparently the problem, but my understanding is that Plaintiff never received one. My last email to Mr. Silvestri was on August 17, 2026, but I never received further information about any submissions of payment.

 

(Truong Decl. ¶ 3.)

 

            Thus, the Court finds that Plaintiff has demonstrated entitlement to a judgment against Defendant in the amount of $99,991.43, and Defendant has not opposed the motion to demonstrate otherwise.

 

II.             Attorneys’ Fees & Costs

 

Paragraph 7.9 of the Settlement Agreement provides, “If any Party to this Stipulation commences a judicial proceeding to enforce any provision of this Stipulation or is required to defend a judicial proceeding commenced by any party to this Stipulation, the unsuccessful Party or parties shall pay to the prevailing Party or Parties all of his or its litigation costs, expenses and reasonable attorney’s fees incurred in the action, including appellate and post-judgment proceeding.”  (Ex. 1 to Truong Decl. at ¶ 7.9.)

 

As such, Plaintiff requests attorneys’ fees and costs in the amount of $885, representing 1.5 hours of attorney time at the hourly rate of $550, totaling $825, plus the $60 filing fee for this motion.  (Truong Decl. ¶ 5.)

 

Therefore, the Cout finds Plaintiff’s request for attorneys’ fees and costs warranted.

 

Conclusion

 

For the foregoing reasons, the Court grants Plaintiff’s unopposed motion to enforce settlement, and will enter the proposed Order lodged on August 31, 2026 in conformity with the ruling.

 

Further, the Court orders Plaintiff to lodge a proposed Judgment in conformity the ruling on or before October 9, 2026. 

 

Further, the Court vacates the Case Management Conference as moot and sets an Order to Show Cause re Entry of Judgment on October 16, 2026.

 

Plaintiff shall also provide notice of the Order and Judgment, and file the notices with proofs of service forthwith.
