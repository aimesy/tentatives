I.            INTRODUCTION

On June 18, 2026, cross-defendant Terry Sheng Quan (“Quan”) filed this special motion to strike pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16. Quan moves to strike the Ninth, Tenth, and Eleventh Causes of Action of cross-complainant Phillip Baltazar’s (“Baltazar”) Second Amended Cross-Complaint (“SACC”). The SACC was filed on September 22, 2025, and includes 11 causes of action. Quan argues that the three causes of action at issue in this motion — Defamation Per Se, Civil Harassment, and Civil Conspiracy to Defame — arise from a single Facebook post Quan allegedly made on November 27, 2024, commenting on Baltazar’s conduct.

Plaintiff/cross-defendant Wai Ngai Fu aka David Fu (“Fu”) filed a joinder to Quan’s motion as Fu is named in Baltazar’s Eleventh Cause of Action for civil conspiracy.

Baltazar filed an opposition to the motion to strike on August 10, 2026.

Quan filed a reply brief on September 16, 2026, which Fu joined.

II.          DISCUSSION

A special motion to strike (“anti-SLAPP motion”) may be filed within 60 days after service of the operative pleading. An anti-SLAPP motion filed after 60 days may be permitted in the court’s discretion. (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.16, subd. (f).) The purpose of the statute’s timing requirements is to facilitate the dismissal of an action subject to a special motion to strike early in the litigation so as to minimize the cost to the defendant. (Chitsazzadeh v. Kramer & Kaslow (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 676, 682 [citing Equilon Enterprises v. Consumer Cause, Inc. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 53, 67].)

Quan requests the Court consider his anti-SLAPP motion even though it was filed more than 60 days after the SACC was served. Quan explains the 9-month delay in filing this motion by claiming that he was financially unable to prepare and file this motion, but did so “once his circumstances allowed.” (Motion, p. 6.) As an initial matter, the Court notes that the claims Quan moves to strike were first raised by Baltazar in December of 2024, meaning that this motion was filed more than a year and a half after Quan was given notice of Baltazar’s defamation and civil harassment claims. Quan additionally claims that Baltazar’s 11 cross-claims were too complex and that it took time to analyze them. However, this motion only concerns three causes of action and allege conduct by Quan of which Quan already has personal knowledge. Moreover, Quan provides no evidence about his financial “circumstances” or how they changed to allow him to file this motion; Quan was able to file a motion for summary judgment a week before filing his anti-SLAPP motion.

The purpose of the anti-SLAPP statute would not be fulfilled if the Court were to hear Quan’s anti-SLAPP motion. Primarily, it would not put the matter to rest early in the litigation to save on costs. Quan admits that extensive discovery has already been taken (and was necessary) in order to disprove Baltazar’s defamation claims and bring this motion. Furthermore, trial is scheduled for November 2, 2026. An immediate appeal of a ruling on Quan’s anti-SLAPP motion would derail the trial date, causing prejudice to all parties, including those who have motions for summary judgment/summary adjudication scheduled throughout October.

III.        CONCLUSION

The Court exercises its discretion to decline to hear Quan’s anti-SLAPP motion. The motion is DENIED.

I.      INTRODUCTION

         On January 30, 2026, plaintiff Alejandro Rios (“Plaintiff”) filed this action asserting causes of action for fraud, fraudulent inducement, negligent misrepresentation, violations of the Consumer Legal Remedies Act, Unfair Competition Law, and False Advertising Law, negligence per se, breach of the implied warranty of merchantability, rescission/restitution, and declaratory relief. Plaintiff alleges defendants Puente Hills Ford, LLC (“Dealer”) and its General Manager, Mike Gilligan (collectively, “Defendants”), misrepresented or failed to inform Plaintiff about a “high-risk safety recall that materially affected [his] decision to purchase a vehicle.” (Compl., ¶ 1.) The vehicle, a 2022 Ford Escape (“Vehicle”) was purchased on September 4, 2023.

          On May 13, 2026, Defendants filed this demurrer to the Complaint arguing that Plaintiff’s claims are barred by a release executed on April 26, 2024.

          Plaintiff filed an opposition brief on June 22, 2026.

          Defendants filed a reply brief on September 16, 2026.

II.     LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the pleadings and will be sustained only where the pleading is defective on its face. (City of Atascadero v. Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 445, 459.) “We treat the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law. We accept the factual allegations of the complaint as true and also consider matters which may be judicially noticed. [Citation.]” (Mitchell v. California Department of Public Health (2016) 1 Cal.App.5th 1000, 1007; Del E. Webb Corp. v. Structural Materials Co. (1981) 123 Cal.App.3d 593, 604 [“the facts alleged in the pleading are deemed to be true, however improbable they may be”].) A

III.    DISCUSSION

          A release agreement is governed by general principles of contract law. (Camacho v. Target Corp.) (2018) 24 Cal.App. 5th 291, 306.) Extrinsic evidence is not admissible to provide a meaning to which an instrument is not reasonably susceptible, and the language of the contract governs its interpretation when it is clear and explicit and does not involve an absurdity. (Shine v. Williams-Sonoma, Inc. (2018) 23 Cal.App.5th 1070, 1082.)

Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s claims are all precluded by a written Release and Settlement Agreement (“RSA”) that was entered into in response to a prior lawsuit involving the Vehicle. The RSA included a waiver of rights under Civil Code section 1542 and the recitals detailed Plaintiff’s purchase of the Vehicle on September 4, 2023, Plaintiff’s claims regarding representations made during the sale, and allegations of predatory lending practices and sales contract terms. (RJN, Ex. B.) Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s claims relate to the same Vehicle and sales transaction which were the subject of the RSA.

          Plaintiff argues that the RSA must be “interpreted in context” and that the recitals only addressed a dispute between the parties regarding oil-services, not a claim regarding the nondisclosure of recalls. (Opp., p. 6.) Plaintiff claims that the scope of the RSA cannot be determined on demurrer, even if the RSA includes a section 1542 waiver. However, the terms of the RSA are clear and apply to Plaintiff’s claims, known and unknown, relating to his purchase of the Vehicle and any predatory lending and sales practices. The scope of the RSA is therefore not limited to the representations regarding the promised oil changes, but also includes claims arising from the purchase transaction that could have been raised but which were unknown by Plaintiff at the time the RSA was executed.

IV.    CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer to Plaintiff’s Complaint is SUSTAINED. Since no amendment to the Complaint can alter the unambiguous language of the Release, Plaintiff fails to show that amendment is possible and no leave to amend is granted. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349- 350 [leave to amend may properly be denied where the plaintiff fails to demonstrate how the complaint can be amended to cure the identified defect].)

United Financial Casualty Company (“UFCC”) is the respondent in an uninsured motorist insurance arbitration initiated by claimants Arsamak Oziev, Iza Ozieva, Amalia Ozieva, Adeliya Ozieva, Salma Ozieva, Aila Ozieva, and Safina Ozieva (collectively, “Claimants”).

The superior court has exclusive jurisdiction over discovery disputes in uninsured motorist arbitrations. (Miranda v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 913, 927.) UFCC moves for an order compelling Claimants’ responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One), without objections. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(b).)

The motion is unopposed and it is undisputed that no responses have been served by any of the Ozieva Claimants. Accordingly, the motion to compel responses is GRANTED and each of the Ozieva Claimants are ordered to serve verified responses, without objections, to UFCC’s Form Interrogatories (Set One) within 20 days of the date of this Order.
