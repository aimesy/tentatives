DEPARTMENT 836 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 26STCV11455    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 836

APPLICATION FOR RIGHT TO ATTACH ORDER

                                   

Date:                           8/4/26 (1:30 PM)                                           

Case Name:                 Shawn R. Carpenter Living Trust v. AmWeCo LLC, et al. (26STCV11455)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Shawn R. Carpenter Living Trust application for right to attach order with respect to defendant AmWeCo LLC is GRANTED.

 

Pursuant to CCP §484.090, the Court finds: (1) the claim is one upon which attachment may be issued; (2) plaintiff has established the probable validity of the claim; (3) attachment is not sought for any purpose other than recovery on the claim; and (4) the amount to be attached is greater than zero. 

 

Plaintiff Shawn R. Carpenter Living Trust’s claims against defendant AmWeCo, LLC are for amounts due under a promissory note. On August 28, 2023, the parties executed a Promissory Note (“Note”), whereby plaintiff agreed to lend defendant $250,000. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 4; Verified Compl. Ex. 1.) In exchange, defendant agreed to repay the amount, plus any interest or any other fees payable under the Note, by August 28, 2024. (Carpenter Decl. ¶¶ 4-5.) By August 28, 2024, defendant failed to tender the entire amount due under the Note, which required plaintiff to make multiple demands of payment from defendant thereafter. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 6.)

 

On May 23, 2025, defendant made a partial payment of $25,000. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 7; Verified Compl. Ex. 2.) On September 29, 2025, defendant made another partial payment of $25,000. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 8; Verified Compl. Ex. 3.) By October 8, 2025, plaintiff demanded payment of the remaining balance of $293,242. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 9; Verified Compl., Ex. 4.) On November 14, 2025, defendant made a third partial payment of $50,000. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 10; Verified Compl. Ex. 5.) To date, defendant has not made any further payments, and the current outstanding balance under the Note is $257,306.60. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 11.)

 

Plaintiff has shown that defendant is in breach of the Note and has failed to cure the defect. (Carpenter Decl. ¶¶ 4-11; Verified Compl. Exs. 1-5.) Defendant has failed to pay the remaining balance of $257,306.60 due under the Note. (Carpenter Decl. ¶ 11.) Thus, the Court finds that plaintiff will likely prevail on its breach of contract claim.

 

In so finding, the Court disregards defendant’s untimely opposition, filed August 3, 2026. (See Cal. Rules of Ct., rule 3.1300(d).)  Even if the Court were to consider defendant’s late opposition, the result would be the same.  Defendant claims plaintiff does not have a probably valid claim for breach of the Note because the parties purportedly entered into a settlement agreement for such claim.  It is undisputed there is no such written settlement agreement.  Nor does the evidence before this Court otherwise establish the existence of any agreement to settle plaintiff’s claim.  To the contrary, up until July 31, 2026, the parties were still attempting to negotiate the terms of an agreement. (See Schuman Decl. ¶ 7 & Ex. E [e-mail thread regarding settlement negotiations].)  Even though defendant may have sent $25,000 to plaintiff on May 14, 2026, which defendant denominated as a settlement payment (Latham Decl. ¶¶ 10-13 & Ex. B), that conduct alone is insufficient to establish any enforceable settlement agreement between the parties, particularly in light of contemporaneous communications between the parties evidencing no meeting of the minds, as well as defendant making no further purported “settlement payments” to plaintiff as further evidence of conduct to establish the existence of a settlement agreement.  (See Schuman Decl. ¶¶ 4, 6 & Exs. A, B.)

 

Accordingly, the Court finds that plaintiff has established the probable validity of its claim upon which attachment may be had.

 

With respect to the property that plaintiff seeks to attach, plaintiff may attach any property of defendant per CCP § 487.010(a) and CCP § 488.300, et seq.  

 

Based on the foregoing, the application is GRANTED. A writ of attachment in the amount of $257,306.60 shall issue upon the posting of a bond in the amount of $10,000 by plaintiff. (CCP § 489.220.) The Court will sign the proposed orders electronically received on May 4, 2026.

 

 


Case Number: 26STCV12956    Hearing Date: August 6, 2026    Dept: 836

APPLICATION FOR WRIT OF POSSESSION

 

 

Date:               8/6/26 (1:30 PM) 

Case:               Financial Pacific Leasing Inc. v. Eduardo A. Pinkus, et al. (26STCV12956)

  

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Application for Writ of Possession by plaintiff Financial Pacific Leasing Inc. as to defendant Eduarado A. Pinkus dba Exclusive Tree Services, is DENIED. The Court finds that plaintiff has not established the probable validity of its claim to possession of the property, namely, a 2014 Chrysler RAM Truck 5500 (VIN: 3C&WRMDL3EG116536).

 

On July 18, 2022, defendant Eduardo A. Pinkus dba Exclusive Tree Services entered into a written Equipment Finance Agreement (the “Agreement”) with non-party TF Group Inc, wherein TF Group Inc. financed the purchase of a 2014 Chrysler RAM Truck 5500 (VIN: 3C7WRMDL3EG116536). (Williams Decl. ¶ 5 & Ex. 1.)  The Agreement grants the Creditor a security interest in the equipment financed by the Agreement, and, under “REMEDIES,” the Agreement provides that, upon default, the Creditor may “take possession” of  such items. (Williams Decl. Ex. 1 at ¶¶ 1, 14.)  

 

Defendant executed a personal guaranty, wherein defendant guaranteed Exclusive Tree Services’ timely payment and performance of all obligations under the Agreement. (Williams Decl. ¶ 6 & Ex. 1 at pg. 3.) Defendant Branda Pinkus also signed the same personal guaranty. (Williams Decl. ¶ 7 & Ex. 1 at pg. 3.)

 

On July 14, 2022, non-party TF Group Inc. entered into an agreement with plaintiff Financial Pacific Leasing Inc., purportedly assigning its rights associated with an Equipment Financing Agreement dated July 11, 2022 between it and defendant. (Williams Decl. ¶ 8 & Ex. 2.)

 

Plaintiff claims that, on or before September 20, 2022, it perfected its security interest in the subject vehicle by recording a lien against the vehicle title with the California Department of Motor Vehicles. (Williams Decl. ¶ 9 & Ex. 3.)

 

On September 1, 2025, defendant defaulted on the terms of the Agreement. (Williams Decl. ¶ 10.) The balance due under the Agreement, exclusive of late charges and other fees, is $39,233.22. (Williams Decl. ¶ 11.) Plaintiff has demanded a return of the subject vehicle from defendant per the Agreement, but defendant has refused to comply, retaining the subject vehicle for his own benefit. (Williams Decl. ¶ 20.)

 

Despite defendant’s default under the Agreement, plaintiff’s evidence does not establish it has a probably valid claim that defendant has improperly retained possession of the subject equipment. In support of its claim against defendant, the assignment of rights upon which plaintiff relies refers to an Equipment Finance Agreement other than the one that plaintiff contends is in default, namely, the Agreement, dated July 18, 2022. (Williams Decl., Exs. 1-2.) Instead, the assignment of rights to plaintiff refers to an agreement dated July 11, 2022.

 

Furthermore, even if the assignment of rights to plaintiff correctly to the July 18, 2022 Agreement, it would nonetheless be unclear how an assignment of rights pursuant to a July 14, 2022 agreement could be made before any such rights would have existed pursuant to the subsequent July 18, 2022 Agreement. Thus, because there is substantial doubt as to whether plaintiff has any rights under the July 18 2022 Agreement, the Court does not find on this record that plaintiff has demonstrated any probable validity of its claim.

 

For the foregoing reasons, the Application is DENIED.
