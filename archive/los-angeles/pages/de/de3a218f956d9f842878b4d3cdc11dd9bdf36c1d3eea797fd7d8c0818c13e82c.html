DEPARTMENT B LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23TRCV04305    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                       Plaintiff Jasmine Farmer

Responding Party:                  Defendant City of Los Angeles

Motion to Compel Depositions of Defendant City’s Employees

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

The motion is GRANTED.  See order below.

BACKGROUND

On December 28, 2023, Jasmine Farmer filed a complaint against defendant City of Los Angeles for dangerous condition of public property.

On July 25, 2024, plaintiff filed an amendment designating Delta Air Lines, Inc. as Doe 1.

On February 25, 2025, plaintiff filed an amendment designating Hensel Phelps Parent 1 Inc. as Doe 2.

On May 19, 2025, Hensel Phelps Construction Co. filed a cross-complaint against Roes 1 through 100 for implied indemnity, contribution, and declaratory relief.

On July 22, 2026, the Court granted plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to request for production of documents, set two by July 31 and awarded sanctions in favor of plaintiff.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

CCP §2025.450(a) provides:  “If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, . . , without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document . . . described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document . . . described in the deposition notice.”

CCP §2025.450(b) provides, “A motion under subdivision (a) shall comply with both of the following:

(1)        The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.

(2)        The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040, or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Jasmine Farmer requests an order compelling the depositions of defendant City of Los Angeles aka City of Los Angeles, Department of Airports, aka Los Angeles World Airports’ employees for dates in July or August 2026.

The complaint alleges that this lawsuit arises from an incident that took place on April 11, 2023 at approximately 4:20 a.m., at the LAX Airport.  Complaint, ¶10.  The City of Los Angeles owned, occupied, leased, used, regulated, controlled, managed, maintained, operated, supervised, repaired, and possessed the roof and ceiling at the LAX Airport.  Id., ¶11.  At the time of the incident, plaintiff was working as a TSA agent.  On that day, she was tasked with checking in employees at the Known Crew Member podium in terminal 3.  Id., ¶12.  While performing this task, plaintiff was at a podium inside the airport.  Id., ¶13.  Above the podium is ceiling tiles.  Id., ¶14.  While plaintiff was checking in airline staff, a wet ceiling tile fell from the ceiling and struck her in the head.  As a result, she suffered severe and permanent injury, including a traumatic brain injury.  Id., ¶15.

Plaintiff explains that on April 2, 2026, plaintiff’s counsel reached out to defense counsel to provide “advance notice” of plaintiff’s intent to notice the deposition of LAWA’s PMQ on eleven areas of inquiry and that the depositions of five LAWA employees would be noticed for dates in May and requested that defense counsel provide witness availability by April 23.  On April 27, having received no availability, plaintiff unilaterally noticed the depositions of five LAWA employees—Christopher Mendoza, Brian Velasquez, Diana Duker for May 28 and Talitha Mitchell and Robert Zamora for May 29.  On May 1, plaintiff served the notice of deposition of LAWA’s PMQ for May 29 along with a request for production of documents.  On May 5, defense counsel requested a two-week extension to respond to plaintiff’s requests for production of documents, set two, but did not provide witness availability.  Plaintiff’s counsel granted a one-week extension to May 12 and expected that defense counsel would also address the outstanding deposition availability.  On May 11, defense counsel requested another week extension and stated that they were also working to obtain deposition dates for the LAWA employee witnesses as well.  Plaintiff’s counsel granted an extension to May 19.  On May 19, defense counsel requested an additional two-week extension, “citing ongoing delays in receiving responsive documents and information from LAWA.”  Defense counsel indicated that they would provide LAWA’s employees’ availability for deposition before the end of May.  Plaintiff’s counsel granted an extension to May 26.  On May 26, defense counsel requested another extension and did not provide any deposition availability.  Plaintiff’s counsel responded, granting one final extension and requiring that both the outstanding discovery responses and deposition availability for all six noticed LAWA witnesses be provided no later than June 2.  Plaintiff’s counsel took the depositions off calendar in reliance on defense counsel’s representations, rather than proceed to certificates of non-appearance.  On June 2, defense counsel contacted plaintiff’s counsel by phone and advised that they were still working to obtain both discovery responses and deposition dates from LAWA and required additional time.  Plaintiff’s counsel declined to grant a further extension.  As of the filing date of the motion on June 10, plaintiff’s counsel had not received LAWA’s employees and PMQ availability.

In opposition, LAWA contends that it has never refused to produce its employee witnesses or its PMQ for deposition.  Rather, defendant asserts, defense counsel has consistently advised plaintiff’s counsel that it was working with its client to obtain the availability of the requested witnesses before confirming deposition dates.  Defendant states that it acknowledges that the process has taken longer than anticipated but the delay is neither willful nor undertaken for the purpose of delay.  Defendant asserts that defense counsel “continues to work with its client to obtain their availability.”

In the reply (filed on July 20), plaintiff notes that defense counsel still has not provided any available deposition dates.  Plaintiff asserts that “an open-ended commitment to produce witnesses at some unspecified future date, unaccompanied by any date, is precisely the conduct that required Plaintiff to seek relief.”

The Court finds that plaintiff properly served deposition notices for May 28 and May 29.  Plaintiff’s counsel reasonably took the depositions off calendar on defense counsel’s numerous representations that they would provide availability, which they have not done.    

The motion is GRANTED.

Sanctions

Plaintiff requests $3272.50 ($595/hr. x 5.5 hrs.) in sanctions against defendant LAWA.  The Court finds that sanctions are warranted against defendant in the amount of $1785 ($595/hr. x 3 hrs.).

ORDER

            The motion is GRANTED.

            Defendant LAWA is ordered to produce for deposition its PMQ and employees as previously noticed by August 21. 

            Defendant LAWA is ordered to pay sanctions to plaintiff in the amount of $1785 within thirty days.

            Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of ruling.


Case Number: 24TRCV02362    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                      Plaintiff Barton Wachs M.D.

Responding Party:                  Defendants Douglas Maupin and Maupin Development Inc.

Motion to Substitute Barton Wachs, M.D. as Successor in Interest to Francesca Wachs

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

            The motion is GRANTED.

BACKGROUND

            On July 15, 2024, plaintiffs Francesca Wachs and Barton Wachs, M.D. filed a complaint against Douglas Maupin and Maupin Development Inc. for (1) breach of promissory notes, (2) open book accounts, and (3) accounts stated.

On December 9, 2025, the Court granted an ex parte application to continue trial from January 5, 2026 to August 31, 2026.

On April 30, 2026, the Court granted plaintiff’s motion to continue trial.  The trial was continued to December 14, 2026.

On June 15, 2026, the Court granted plaintiff Barton Wachs, M.D.’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories and to deem requests for admission admitted.  The Court ordered sanctions against each defendant and their attorney in the amount of $1100.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

Following the death of a party, any legal action by that party may only proceed upon substitution of the decedent’s personal representative or successor in interest in her place.  CCP §§377.31, 367.  CCP §377.31 states that, “[o]n motion after the death of a person who commenced an action or proceeding, the court shall allow a pending action or proceeding that does not abate to be continued by the decedent’s personal representative or, if none, by the decedent’s successor in interest.”

            CCP §377.32(a) states:  “The person who seeks . . . to continue a pending action or proceeding as the decedent’s successor in interest under this article, shall execute and file an affidavit or a declaration under penalty of perjury under the laws of this state stating all of the following:

(1)   The decedent’s name.

(2)   The date and place of decedent’s death.

(3)   ‘No proceeding is now pending in California for administration of the decedent’s estate.’

(4)    If the decedent’s estate was administered, a copy of the final order showing the distribution of the decedent’s cause of action to the successor in interest.

(5)   Either of the following, as appropriate, with facts in support thereof:

(A) ‘The affiant or declarant is the decedent’s successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) and succeeds to the decedent’s interest in the action or proceeding.’

(B)  ‘The affiant or declarant is authorized to act on behalf of the decedent’s successor in interest (as defined in Section 377.11 of the California Code of Civil Procedure) with respect to the decedent’s interest in the action or proceeding.’

(6)   ‘No other person has a superior right to commence the action or proceeding or to be substituted for the decedent in the pending action or proceeding.’

(7)   ‘The affiant or declarant affirms or declares under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct.’

(b) Where more than one person executes the affidavit or declaration under this section, the statements required by subdivision (a) shall be modified as appropriate to reflect that fact.

            (c) A certified copy of the decedent’s death certificate shall be attached to the affidavit or declaration.”

DISCUSSION

            Plaintiff Barton Wachs M.D. requests an order substituting him, as successor in interest, to Francesca Wachs, in place of Francesca Wachs as a plaintiff in this action, pursuant to CCP §§377.31 and 377.32.

            Barton Wachs states in his declaration that Francesca Wachs died on July 29, 2024 in the County of Los Angeles.  No proceeding is now pending in California for administration of Francesca’s estate.  Francesca and Barton held interests in the promissory notes that are the subject of this action as individual receivables, and the notes were never assigned to any trust.  Francesca and Barton were the settlors and original co-trustees of the Wachs Family Living Trust.  Under the terms of the trust, upon Francesca’s death, he became the sole successor trustee and is the sole beneficiary.  Francesca’s interest in the promissory notes that are the subject of the action passed to him under the trust upon her death.  He states that he is the successor in interest of Francesca and that no other person has a superior right to be substituted for Francesa.

            In opposition, defendants object because the supporting papers are “inconclusive, offering only conclusory statements, with no objective evidentiary support,” asserting that plaintiff Barton and plaintiff’s counsel have been litigating this matter for two years before filing the motion for an order permitting continued representation of the decedent’s interest.  Defendant argues that plaintiff and plaintiff’s counsel “impermissibly proceeded” in the matter and amended the complaint by changing the caption without requisite authority.  Defendant seeks a continuance of the motion to conduct discovery.

            In the reply, plaintiff asserts that the opposition does not dispute that Barton Wachs is the successor in interest and does not identify any deficiency in the declaration and offers no evidence that any other person holds an interest in the promissory notes or a right to prosecute the action.  Plaintiff argues that the declaration satisfies each element.  He filed a supplemental declaration correcting the name of the trust to “Wachs Family Trust,” providing as an exhibit, excerpts of the trust restatement, amendment, and execution and notarial pages.  He adds that Francesca and he have two sons, neither of which is a trustee, and that neither of them hold any present interest in the promissory notes that are the subject of the action, and neither has any right to prosecute the action.  He clarifies his prior declaration where he stated that the notes were ever assigned to any trust to “[n]o separate assignment of either promissory note was prepared or executed, and both notes remain in Francesca’s name and mine as the payee” and that on February 23, 2019, Francesca and he executed a General Assignment of Community Property, by which they transferred their community property to themselves as trustee of the trust.  He states that to the extent that instrument encompassed their interests in the notes, he holds those interests as the sole successor trustee of the trust.

The Court finds that Barton Wach’s declaration complies with CCP §377.32.

            The motion is GRANTED.

ORDER

            The motion is GRANTED.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of this ruling.


Case Number: 25TRCV01358    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                      Plaintiff Fabricio L. Volpato

Responding Party:                  Defendant FCA US LLC

Motion for Attorneys’ Fees, Costs, and Expenses

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

            The motion is GRANTED.  See Order below.

BACKGROUND

On April 28, 2025, Fabricio L. Volpato filed a complaint against FCA US LLC for violations of statutory obligations under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as to a 2024 Ram Promaster 2500.

On December 11, 2025, the Court denied plaintiff’s motion for sanctions.

On February 17, 2026, plaintiff filed notice of settlement.

On February 24, 2026, plaintiff filed a memorandum of costs.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

CCP §1021 states: “Except as attorney’s fees are specifically provided for by statute, the measure and mode of compensation of attorneys and counselors at law is left to the agreement, express or implied, of the parties; . . .”  

CCP §1032(b) states: “Except as otherwise expressly provided by statute, a prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs in any action or proceeding.”  

CCP §1033.5(a)(10)(B) states that attorney fees when authorized by statute are allowable as costs and may be awarded upon a noticed motion pursuant to CCP §1033.5(c)(5).    

Civil Code §1794 states, in part: “(a) Any buyer of consumer goods who is damaged by a failure to comply with any obligation under this chapter or under an implied or express warranty or service contract may bring an action for the recovery of damages and other legal and equitable relief. . . . (d) If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney’s fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.” 

“The trial court has ‘broad authority’ to determine the amount of a reasonable attorneys’ fees.  PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095.  “[T]he fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate.”  (Id. [“California courts have consistently held that a computation of time spent on a case and the reasonable value of that time is fundamental to a determination of an appropriate attorneys' fee award.”].)  “Testimony of an attorney as to the number of hours worked on a particular case is sufficient evidence to support an award of attorney fees, even in the absence of detailed time records.”  Martino v. Denevi (1986) 182 Cal. App. 3d 553, 559.  “General arguments that fees claimed are excessive, duplicative, or unrelated do not suffice.”  Premier Medical Management Systems, Inc. v. California Ins. Guarantee Ass’n (2008) 163 Cal. App. 4th 550, 564.

DISCUSSION

Pursuant to Civil Code §1794(d), plaintiff requests an order awarding in plaintiff’s favor $20,115.14, consisting of $15,111.50 in attorney’s fees, a 1.1 multiplier, additional $2150 in attorney’s fees to review opposition, draft reply, and appear at hearing, and $1342.49 in costs and expenses.  

Entitlement

Plaintiff states that on February 17, 2026, the parties reached a settlement via mediation whereby defendant agreed to pay plaintiff $50,000, which includes compensating plaintiff for the subject vehicle, allowing plaintiff to keep the vehicle, paying a civil penalty, and paying attorney fees, costs, and expenses pursuant to a noticed motion.  The parties agreed that plaintiff was the prevailing party for purposes of the motion.  See Stephanie Argent decl.

Defendant does not dispute that plaintiff is the prevailing party.

The Court finds that plaintiff is the prevailing party.

Reasonableness

Plaintiff requests $15,111.50 in attorney’s fees, a 1.1 multiplier, and an additional $2150 in attorney’s fees to review opposition, draft reply, and appear at hearing. 

The Court notes that the “billing records” summary started at page 98 out of 120 pages.  The Court requests that, in the future, plaintiff’s counsel attaches the billing records as Exhibit 1, rather than at the end.  

The summary of billing records identifies eleven timekeepers at the following rates and hours incurred:  Senior partner Carey Wood ($560/hr. x 5.1 hrs.), associate attorney Bobby Walker ($440/hr. x 2.2 hrs.), associate attorney Steph Argent ($430/hr. x 20.4 hrs.), Daisey Hernandez ($215/hr. x .3 hrs.), Maria Flores ($215/hr. x .4 hrs.), Jasmine Ochoa ($215/hr. x 7.3 hrs.), Jonathan Avalos ($215/hr. x .6 hrs.), Brenda Melendez ($215/hr. x 1.1 hrs.), Alejandra Seda ($215/hr. x .4 hrs.), and Maria Chavarin ($215/hr. x .7 hrs.) for a total of 39.4 hrs., of which 10.8 hrs. were incurred by paralegals Hernandez, Flores, Ochoa, Avalos, Melendez, Seda, and Chavarin.

Counsel states in her declaration that the fees were incurred as follows:  prelitigation efforts, initiating litigation, initial disclosures, preparing for PMQ deposition and taking certificate of non-appearance, motion for sanctions (which the Court notes was denied), mediation, CMC, facilitating settlement, and OSC re dismissal. 

In opposition, defendant argues that the hourly rates are excessive and that the Court should apply the rates from the 2023 “Real Rate Report,” capping senior attorney rates at $495/hr., associate rates at $295/hr., and paralegal rates at $100/hr.  Defendant notes that attorney Argent was admitted to the State Bar in December 2024 and has only been practicing “lemon law” since December 2024 and that her billable rate is “shocking” and “not defensible.”  Defendant notes that “the standard lemon law case already involves significant cutting and pasting” and that repetitive lemon law tasks should not take plaintiff’s attorneys such large amount of hours to complete, considering they are self-proclaimed “consumer law experts.”  Defendant asserts also that attorney Argent had been an attorney only four months when the case was filed and that some of the billing entries appear to show that she was “learning on the job.”  Defendant also suggests that the Court subtract “several duplicative and unreasonable entries,” such as $1096 for all the time purportedly billed and associated with CW and SA’s claims of “reviewing” the case with paralegal, “handling attorney,” “supervising attorney,” and each other; $406.50 as to OSC for plaintiff’s failure to appear; $708 for clerical or secretarial tasks; and excessive time for preparation of motion.  Defendant also argues that a “negative multiplier” is warranted rather than a “positive” one.  See Hector E. Zurita-Cruz decl. and highlighted chart.

In reply, plaintiff reiterates their arguments that the hourly rates and hours incurred are reasonable.

The Court rules as follows:  In consideration of the Court’s review of the billing entries and defendant’s objections, the Court finds that a reduction in hours is warranted as many of the tasks are clerical in nature.  The Court also finds merit in defendant’s objections as to Wood and Argent’s hours spent reviewing case or conferring with each other, hours incurred as to the OSC, and hours incurred as to the motion for attorney’s fees.

The Court reduces the hours by three hours as to paralegals and five hours as to attorneys.  The Court notes that applying the rates it finds reasonable, the total amount is slightly less than that proposed by defendant.  Accordingly, the Court will accept the higher figure that defendant finds reasonable.  The Court finds that $8151 is a reasonable amount for attorney’s fees.   A multiplier is not warranted.  Costs are unopposed.

The motion is GRANTED.

ORDER

            The motion is GRANTED.

The Court awards $8151 in attorney’s fees in favor of plaintiff and against defendant FCA US LLC.

The Court awards $1342.49 in costs and expenses. 

            Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of ruling.
