LINDA FOK VS CHICKEN NOW MONTEBELLO, LLC, ET AL.

 

MOTION FOR ENTRY OF DEFAULT AGAINST DEFENDANT CHICKEN NOW MONTEBELLO

 

Date of Hearing:        July 27, 2026                                      Trial Date:       None set

Department:              529                                                      Case No.:        24STCV28848

 

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Linda Fok  

Responding Party:     No opposition

 

 

BACKGROUND

 

On November 4, 2024, Plaintiff Linda Fok filed a complaint against Defendants Chicken Now Montebello, LLC dba Chicken Now, Tammy Lee, and Spinoso Real Estate Group DLS, LLC for intentional infliction of emotional distress, negligence, violation of Unruh Civil Rights Act (Civ. Code §§51, 52), negligent violation of statutory duty, premises liability and false imprisonment.

 

On October 15, 2025, Plaintiff dismissed Defendant Tammy Lee. Plaintiff also dismissed Defendant Spinoso Real Estate Group DLS, LLC on December 22, 2025.

 

Plaintiff named Bridge Group Investments II LLC as Doe 4, Professional Security Consultants as Doe 3, and Centennial Real Estate Management, LLC as Doe 2.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

Plaintiff Linda Fok’s Motion for Entry of Default Against Defendant Chicken Now Montebello is DENIED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff Linda Fok moves this court for an order entering the default of Chicken Now Montebello, LLC, dba Chicken Now (“Chicken Now”), or, in the alternative, directing the Clerk of the Court to enter that default. Plaintiff makes the motion on the grounds Chicken Now has failed to answer or otherwise respond within the time allowed by law. Plaintiff contends it has applied to the Clerk for entry of Chicken Now’s default more than once; however, it keeps getting denied and the most recent rejection rests on a defect Plaintiff cannot cure, because the individual who accepted service on behalf of Chicken Now represented that he was authorized to accept service yet refused to provide his name or any title beyond “Manager.”

 

The court DENIES the motion. The Notice of Rejection provides “*Other - Proof of (Statement of Damage CIV-050) 3/19/26: #1c-Provide “full name” or (5) physical descriptors required (Age; race; sex; height; weight). Correct proof of service. Submit updated Request for Default after rejection.” (emphasis added.) The POS can include physical descriptors if the name was not provided. Plaintiff must seek entry of default through the proper channels.


Case Number: 25STCV09048    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 529

EDWARD ROBINSON VS GENERAL MOTORS, LLC.

 

DEMURRER TO THE THIRD AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Date of Hearing:        July 27, 2026                                      Trial Date:       None set.

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        25STCV09048

 

Moving Party:            Defendant General Motors LLC

Responding Party:     Plaintiff Edward Robinson

Meet and Confer:      Yes. (Allison Decl.)  

 

BACKGROUND

 

On March 27, 2025, Plaintiff Edward Robinson filed a complaint against General Motors, LLC for various violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as well as fraudulent concealment. On August 29, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint. Upon the sustaining of a demurrer, Plaintiff filed a Second Amended Complaint.

 

Plaintiff has now filed a Third Amended Complaint on May 11, 2026.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Defendant General Motors LLC’s Demurrer to the Third Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant GM demurs to the Third Amended Complaint on the grounds Plaintiff’s first, second and third causes of action alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act are time-barred by the statute of limitations.

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732 (internal citations omitted).) 

 

GM argues the statute of limitations bars Plaintiff’s first, second and third causes of action. Specifically, GM contends even assuming the date Plaintiff entered into the express warranty contract was the original delivery date of the Subject Vehicle, Plaintiff filed this action on March 27, 2025, which is after the statute of limitations ran on June 8, 2024. Thus, absent tolling, Plaintiff’s first, second and third causes of action are barred by the statute of limitations. However, Plaintiff does not specifically allege that any of these three tolling circumstances provided for by Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26. GM further contends other tolling doctrines do not apply as the Legislature declined to expressly done so.

 

The court finds the allegations sufficient for the purposes of a demurrer. As the court has previously noted, Plaintiff may allege tolling by delayed discovery, equitable tolling, or other applicable theories but Plaintiff had failed to allege when they discovered the warranty violations and why they could not discover them earlier. (See April 20, 2026 Minute Order.) Section 871.21 uses “shall be tolled as follows,” without expressly foreclosing others. The court finds now the allegations satisfy the standard for pleading late discovery. The TAC now alleges “Plaintiff only discovered (or was on notice inquiry of) GM’s failure to comply with its Song-Beverly obligations in 2023, when the Vehicle exhibited major problems with the drivetrain.” (TAC ¶16.) Moreover, “Plaintiff had no way of uncovering Defendant’s deception with respect to the defects given that Defendant performed various diagnostics and/or undertook repairs and claimed that nothing was wrong with the Subject Vehicle or that the Vehicle had been repaired.” (TAC ¶17.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer to the TAC is OVERRULED.


Case Number: 25STCV19491    Hearing Date: July 27, 2026    Dept: 529

ISRAEL CERVERA VS FCA US LLC, ET AL.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL INITIAL DISCLOSURES PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE §871.26 AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS

 

Date of Hearing:        July 27, 2026                                      Trial Date:       None set

Department:              529                                                      Case No.:        25STCV19491

 

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Israel Cervera

Responding Party:     Defendant FCA US LLC

 

BACKGROUND

 

On June 30, 2025, Plaintiff Israel Cervera filed a complaint against Defendants FCA US LLC and Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram for various violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act as well as Negligent Repair and Fraudulent Inducement Concealment. On November 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

Plaintiff Israel Cervera’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures Pursuant to California Code of Civil Procedure §871.26 and Request for Monetary Sanctions is DENIED.

 

ANALYSIS

 

Plaintiff Israel Cervera moves this court for an order compelling Defendant FCA US LLC to comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 by producing all documents required under subdivisions (h)(6), (h)(7), (h)(8), (h)(9), (h)(10), (h)(12), and (h)(13) and an imposition of mandatory sanctions against FCA US and its counsel of record in the amount of $2,500.00.

 

Effective January 1, 2025, in an action seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle pursuant to Section 871.20 (above), the parties shall: “Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, … without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h) [of Section 871.26].” (CCP § 871.26(b).)¿ 

 

Plaintiff brings this motion to compel FCA to provide complete, verified compliance with the mandatory initial disclosure requirements of California Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26(h). Plaintiff makes the motion on the grounds the 60-day deadline for mandatory initial disclosures under section 871.26(b) was December 1, 2025. FCA, however, did not meet that deadline.

 

In opposition, FCA acknowledges it inadvertently provided the documents late – on June 10, 2026. However, Plaintiff served this motion on June 22, 2026. FCA also argues sanctions should be denied as FCA US did not ignore its obligations; it compiled and produced over 1,000 pages of comprehensive records and FCA US drafted and circulated a narrowly tailored Stipulated Protective Order to protect its proprietary manuals.

 

As the documents have now been produced, the court denies the motion to compel compliance. The court also denies the request for sanctions as the motion was filed after the documents had been produced; it would appear that plaintiff saw no pressing need to require compliance.
