Case Number: 24STCV01381    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 516

GENERAL JURISDICTION DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

 

Case Name:   Circa 1200, LLC v. Marcus

 

Case Number: 24STCV01381

 

Date: September 14, 2026

 

Defendant(s) Against Whom Default Judgment is Sought: Defendant Michael Marcus

 

 

GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff’s requests for damages in the amount of $74,191.82 do not exceed the amount sought in the operative pleading. (Compl., p. 2.) Thus, the requested damages amount is not improper. (Code Civ. Proc., § 580.) Further, Plaintiff submits adequate evidence to substantiate the damages sought. (Garner Decl., ¶¶ 3-4, Ex. A, Ex. B.)

 

Plaintiff also seeks prejudgment interest in the amount of $34.433.14, which accrued at a rate of 10% per annum starting from September 28, 2022, to May 18, 2026. (Scott Decl., ¶¶ 5-6.) The interest amount is proper.

 

Plaintiff’s request for attorney’s fees in the amount of $452.85 is in accordance with LASC Rule 3.214. Plaintiff’s request for costs in the amount of $747.15 is granted. (CIV-100)  

 


Case Number: 25STCV07748    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 516

GENERAL JURISDICTION DEFAULT JUDGMENT

 

 

Case Name:   Huang v. Gen Liu, et al.

 

Case Number: 25STCV07748

 

Date: September 14, 2026

 

Defendant(s) Against Whom Default Judgment is Sought:          Defendant Mojo Group

 

 

DENIED.

 

On June 13, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) asserting causes of action for: (1) wrongful termination in violation of public policy; (2) retaliation in violation of Labor Code sections 1102.5 and 98.6; (3) misclassification of employee as independent contractor; (4) failure to pay minimum wages in violation of Labor Code sections 558, 558.1, 1194, 1194.2, 1197, and 1197.1; (5) failure to pay overtime wages in violation Labor Code sections 510, 558, 558.1, 1194, 1194.2, and 1197.1; (6) failure to provide meal breaks in violation of Labor Code section 226.7; (7) failure to provide rest breaks in violation of Labor Code section 226.7; (8) failure to provide accurate wage statements in violation of Labor Code section 226; (9) waiting time penalties pursuant to Labor Code section 201, and 203; (10) failure to reimburse business expenses; (11) failure to permit inspection of personnel file and payroll records in violation of Labor Code sections 226, subdivisions (c), (f), 432, and 1198.5; (12) unlawful, unfair, fraudulent business practices in violation of Government Code section 17200; (13) intentional interference with contractual relations; (14) intentional interference with prospective economic relations; and (15) negligent interference with prospective economic relations.

 

On April 13, 2026, and June 12, 2026, the Court previously denied Plaintiff’s request for default judgment because the amount in damages exceeded the amount requested in the FAC. (April 13, 2026, Minute Order; June 12, 2026, Minute Order.) The April 13, 2026, Minute Order states:

 

Plaintiff’s FAC is devoid of a damages amount. (See generally, FAC [no specific amounts identified].) The Complaint only states penalty amounts and violation amounts. Plaintiff’s request for default judgment seeks damages in the amount of $150,000. (CIV-100.) Further, the amount requested for a default judgment “‘cannot exceed that demanded in the complaint, in the statement [of damages] required by Section 425.11, or in the statement provided for by Section 425.115.’” (Dhawan v. Biring (2015) 241 Cal.App.4th 963, 968 (Dhawan).) Section 425.11 requires a statement of damages to be separately served on a defendant in “an action to recover damages for personal injury or wrongful death.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 425.11, subd. (b).) Plaintiff’s causes of action are not to vindicate her personal injury, but instead constitute a property right. (Rodriguez v. Cho (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 742, 755.) Strictly construed, serving a statement of damages cannot satisfy section 580 in an action not involving personal injury or wrongful death.” (Dhawan, supra, 241 Cal.App.4th at p. 970.)

 

Thus, the amount sought in the default judgment is improper. (Code Civ. Proc., § 580.)

 

(April 13, 2026, Minute Order.)

 

Plaintiff’s August 25, 2026, default packet  does not cure the deficiencies in the Court’s prior ruling and based thereon is denied.

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             September 14, 2026

Case Name:                 Garsinii v. Growth Management, Inc. et al.

Case No.:                    25STCV17808

Motion:                       Motion to be Relieved as Counsel

Moving Party:             Counsel for Defendants Joy Lewis and John Lewis

Opposing Party:          Plaintiff Namahn Garsinii

 

Tentative Ruling:      The Motions to be Relieved as Counsel are granted.

                                   

 

Albright, Yee & Schmit APC (“Firm”) seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for Defendants Joy Lewis and John Lewis. Plaintiff Namahn Garsinii (“Plaintiff”) filed an opposition to Albright, Yee & Schmit APC’s motion.

 

On August 13, 2026, the Court continued the hearing on the motions. Firm has complied with the statutory and procedural requirements to be relieved as counsel of record.

 

Thus, Albright, Yee & Schmit APC’s motions to be relieved as counsel are granted.
