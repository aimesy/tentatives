Case Number: 26STCV15066    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             September 22, 2026

Case Name:                 Johnson v. Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, et al.

Case No.:                    26STCV15066

Motion:                       Motion to be Relieved as Counsel

Moving Party:             Counsel for Plaintiff

Opposing Party:          None

 

Tentative Ruling:      The Motion to be Relieved as Counsel is denied.     

 

            Plaintiff’s counsel –Valentina A. Mnatsakanyan– seeks to be relieved as counsel of record for Plaintiff Deandre Johnson. No opposition was filed.  

 

            The motion to be relieved as counsel is denied.   

 

Discussion

 

            An attorney is entitled to withdraw upon the consent of the client, or without that consent if approved by the court. (Code Civ. Proc., § 284; Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.) In the latter case, counsel must make a motion to be relieved as attorney of record.  The motion must be made using mandatory forms: Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel – Civil (MC-051), Declaration (MC-052), and Proposed Order (MC-053). (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (a), (b), (e).)

 

            The declaration accompanying the motion to be relieved as counsel must state “in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1).” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (b).)

 

            The notice to the client must be delivered by personal service, electronic means, or mail. If it is done via mail under Code of Civil Procedure section 1013, it must be accompanied by a declaration confirming the service address to be the most current residence or that it is the client’s last known address, and the attorney has been unable to locate a more current address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(1)(A)-(B).) If the notice is served on the client by electronic service under Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6 and rule 2.251, it must be accompanied by a declaration stating that the electronic service address is the client's current electronic service address. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, subd. (d)(2).)

 

            Here, Attorney Valentina A. Mnatsakanyan (“Counsel”) has complied with the Rules of Court by filing forms MC-051,  MC-052, and MC-053.  

 

However, Counsel’s declaration does not adequately explain why she should be relieved as Counsel.

 

Moreover, Counsel has been unable to confirm Plaintiff’s last known address. The Court finds that Counsel’s efforts are insufficient. Counsel must comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 1011(b)(3) to properly serve Plaintiff with the motion and supporting papers if the client’s address is not known.

 

Thus, the Court denies Counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel.


Case Number: 26STCV18904    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             September 22, 2026   

Case Name:                 Lopez v. De La Rosa, et al.

Case No.:                    26STCV18904

Matter:                        Motion for Stay of Proceedings  

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Dori Lopez

Responding Party:       Defendants Rolando De La Rosa and Raymundo De La Rosa

Matter:                        Demurrer

Moving Party:             Defendants Rolando De La Rosa and Raymundo De La Rosa

Responding Party:       Plaintiff Dori Lopez

 

Tentative Ruling:      Plaintiff Dori Lopez’s motion for stay of proceedings or consolidation is denied.

 

                                    Defendants Rolando De La Rosa and Raymundo De La Rosa’s demurrer is sustained in its entirety.

 

 

            On June 15, 2026, Plaintiff Dori Lopez (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants Rolando De La Rosa (“Rolando”) and Raymundo De La Rosa (“Raymundo”) (collectively “Defendants”). The Complaint alleges five causes of action for: (1) quiet title; (2) resulting trust; (3) constructive trust in violation of Civil Code § 2223; (4) partition under Code of Civil Procedure section 872.010 et seq.; and (5) declaratory relief.

 

            On June 23, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s request to deem this present case related with the unlawful detainer proceeding De La Rosa v. Lopez, (Case No. 26VEUD01483). Defendant filed the unlawful detainer action on June 15, 2026. The dispute in both actions involve the Subject Property 8433 Brimfield Avenue, Panorama City, CA 91402 (Subject Property”).

 

            Plaintiff moves to stay the unlawful detainer case between the parties and to consolidate the action for a temporary restraining order (“TRO”). Defendant opposes.

 

           Defendants demur to Plaintiff’s first, third, fourth, and fifth causes of action as uncertain and for failing to allege sufficient facts. Plaintiff opposes.

 

Legal Standard

 

A.     Motion for Stay of Proceedings

 

            “When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending before the court, it may order a joint hearing or trial of any or all the matters in issue in the actions; it may order all the actions consolidated and it may make such orders concerning proceedings therein as may tend to avoid unnecessary costs or delay.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (a).)

 

B.     Demurrer

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading by raising questions of law.¿(Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.)¿“In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)¿The court “‘“treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .”’”¿ (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.)¿In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated.¿(Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) 

 

Analysis

 

A. Motion for Stay of Proceedings

 

            Plaintiff moves for the Court to stay the unlawful detainer case, consolidate the actions, or for injunctive relief in the unlawful detainer case.

 

           As an initial matter, the Court previously denied Plaintiff’s request to deem the cases related pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a). Plaintiff’s request to consolidate the cases is also procedurally improper pursuant to Local Rules of Superior Court, rule 3.3(g).

 

           Thus, the Court denies Plaintiff’s request to consolidate the cases. Additionally, Plaintiff seeks a TRO but provides no legal authority to warrant such a request.

 

            Similarly, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion to stay the proceedings in the unlawful detainer case. ““The constitutionality of these summary procedures is based on their limitation to the single issue of right to possession and incidental damages.” (Martin-Bragg v. Moore (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 367, 388.) “When an unlawful detainer proceeding and an unlimited action concerning title to the property are simultaneously pending, the trial court in which the unlimited action is pending may stay the unlawful detainer action until the issue of title is resolved in the unlimited action, or it may consolidate the actions.” (Id. at p. 385.)

 

            Plaintiff contends that the ongoing dispute in both cases is a “complex issue” that requires the Court to stay the action. However, under Martin-Bragg, a stay remains discretionary. Plaintiff has not provided any evidence warranting a stay of the unlawful detainer proceedings. Plaintiff declares that the unlawful detainer case is ripe but provides no legal authority to support her basis. (Lopez Decl., ¶ 14.)

 

            Thus, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion to stay the unlawful detainer proceedings.

 

             

            B. Demurrer               

                        Meet and Confer

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, “a demurring party is required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or video conference with the party that filed the pleading. . . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

Defendants contend that on July 13, 2026, the parties met and conferred over telephone. (Zadeh Decl., ¶ 3.) Thus, the parties met and conferred.

 

                        Uncertainty

 

Per Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (f), a demurrer must be sustained if a pleading is uncertain. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (f).) The term uncertain means “ambiguous and unintelligible.” (Ibid.) However, demurrers for uncertainty will not be sustained unless “‘the pleading is so incomprehensi[ve] that a defendant could not reasonably respond.’” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695, quoting Mahan v. Charles W. Chan Ins. Agency, Inc. (2017) 14 Cal.App.5th 841, 848, fn.3.)

 

            Taken as a whole, the Complaint is not incomprehensible. The Complaint alleges sufficient facts for Defendants to respond.

 

            Thus, the Court overrules the demurrer on this basis.

 

                        Failure to Allege Sufficient Facts

 

                                    First Cause of Action—Quiet Title

 

            A quiet title action is based on the “[t]he title of the plaintiff as to which a determination under this chapter is sought and the basis of the title.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 761.020, subd. (b).) ““The plaintiff may recover only upon the strength of his or her own title ... and not upon the weakness of the defendant's title. Where the plaintiff relies on a paper title alone he must trace his title (1) to the government; or (2) to grantor in possession at the time of the conveyance to the plaintiff; or (3) to a source common to the chains of title of plaintiff and defendant.”” (Thompson v. Ioane (2017) 11 Cal.App.5th 1180, 1195.)

 

            Defendants contend that Plaintiff has alleged insufficient facts to allege a viable claim for relief. “Where the trust property is real estate, the statute of frauds requires that the declaration of trust must be in writing signed by the trustee.” (Estate of Heggstad (1993) 16 Cal.App.4th 943, 947.) Because this is a property dispute, a written agreement is required. Plaintiff alleges that, pursuant to the terms of an “oral family agreement,,” Defendant Raymundo agreed to hold legal title to the Subject Property for Plaintiff's benefit. (Compl., ¶ 14.) The Subject Property was allegedly transferred to Defendant Ronaldo to hold for Plaintiff's benefit under the same arrangement. (Compl., ¶¶ 15-16.)

           In opposition, Plaintiff does not meaningfully oppose Defendants’ arguments. Plaintiff’s sole argument is the pleading standard for demurrers. However, this argument does not address the Complaint’s failure to allege the dispute raised.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendants’ demurrer for failure to allege sufficient facts.

 

                                    Third Cause of Action—Constructive Trust

 

            “‘A constructive trust is an involuntary equitable trust created by operation of law as a remedy to compel the transfer of property from the person wrongfully holding it to the rightful owner.’” (Communist Party v. 522 Valencia, Inc. (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 980, 990.) “The general principles for imposition of a constructive trust are set forth in Civil Code sections 2223 and 2224. ([Citation.]) Civil Code section 2223 states, ‘One who wrongfully detains a thing is an involuntary trustee thereof, for the benefit of the owner.’ Civil Code section 2224 provides, ‘One who gains a thing by fraud, accident, mistake, undue influence, the violation of a trust, or other wrongful act, is, unless he or she has some other and better right thereto, an involuntary trustee of the thing gained, for the benefit of the person who would otherwise have had it.’” (Higgins v. Higgins (2017) 11 Cal.App.5th 648, 659 (citation omitted).)

 

            Defendants contend that Plaintiff fails to allege any wrongful conduct to assert her claim against them. The Court agrees. Plaintiff alleges that the wrongful conduct was the breach of the family agreement and “other wrongful conduct.” (Compl., ¶ 62.) Plaintiff’s allegation is conclusory.

 

           In opposition, Plaintiff argues that her claim for constructive trust is based on the specific allegations in her second cause of action for a resulting trust. However, the Court is not persuaded. First, Plaintiff does not cite legal authority to support her claim. Second, the Complaint alleges that Plaintiff made all the payments and that Rolando holds legal title to the Subject Property. (Compl., ¶¶ 51-57.) Taking all the allegations as true, the Court finds that Plaintiff has not alleged sufficient facts for a viable cause of action.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendants’ demurrer for failure to allege sufficient facts.

 

                                    Fourth Cause of Action—Partition

                       

            A partition action may be commenced when “[a]n owner of an estate of inheritance, an estate for life, or an estate for years in real property where such property or estate therein is owned by several persons concurrently or in successive estates.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 872.210, subd. (a)(2).) “Section 872.210, subdivision (a)(2) provides the standing requirements to bring a claim to partition real property. Consistent with these general standing principles and with partition's purpose of splitting title amongst current owners ([Citation.]), the statute provides that a partition action “may be commenced and maintained by” “[a]n owner of an estate of inheritance ... in real property where such property or estate therein is owned by several persons concurrently or in successive estates.”” (Amundson v. Catello (2025) 111 Cal.App.5th 817, 824.)

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff contradicts her claims because instead of concurrent ownership, she alleges that she alone paid for the Subject Property. Moreover, Plaintiff’s attempt to plead an alternative theory does not cure the ongoing defect that she contradicts her pleadings. The Court finds that Plaintiff’s allegations are conclusory and do not support her causes of action.

 

            In opposition, Plaintiff provides no legal authority to support her basis.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendants’ demurrer for failure to allege sufficient facts.

 

                                    Fifth Cause of Action—Declaratory Relief

 

            “There is unanimity of authority to the effect that the declaratory procedure operates prospectively, and not merely for the redress of past wrongs. It serves to set controversies at rest before they lead to repudiation of obligations, invasion of rights or commission of wrongs; in short, the remedy is to be used in the interests of preventive justice, to declare rights rather than execute them.” (Travers v. Louden (1967) 254 Cal.App.2d 926, 931.)

 

            Defendant argues that Plaintiff seeks for the Court to make a final determination that she is the rightful owner based on past conduct. Defendant contends that Plaintiff’s claim for declaratory relief duplicates its first, second, and third causes of action.

 

           In opposition, Plaintiff does not make any legal argument to meaningfully oppose Defendants’ arguments.

 

            Thus, the Court sustains Defendants’ demurrer for failure to allege sufficient facts.

 

            Therefore, the Court sustains Defendants’ demurrer in its entirety.

 

            Leave to Amend

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that the pleading can be successfully amended. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

 

Plaintiff will have the opportunity to file an amended pleading in accordance with the Court’s ruling.

 

 

Conclusion

 

            Plaintiff Dori Lopez’s motion to stay of proceedings is denied in its entirety.

 

            Defendants Rolando De La Rosa and Raymundo De La Rosa’s demurrer is sustained in

its entirety with leave to amend. Amended pleading is due by October 12, 2026.

 

 


Case Number: 26STCV20350    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 516

Judge James I. Montgomery

Department 516

Hearing Date:             September 22, 2026   

Case Name:                 Carillo v. BMW of North America, LLC, et al.

Case No.:                    26STCV20350

Matter:                        Demurrer with Motion to Strike

Moving Party:             Plaintiff Armando Carrillo

Responding Party:       Defendant BMW of North America, LLC

 

Tentative Ruling:      The hearing on Plaintiff Armando Carrillo’s demurrer is continued to October 23, 2026.

 

 

            Plaintiff Armando Carrillo (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendants BMW of North America, LLC (“BMW”), a Delaware Limited Liability Company, Bayerische Motoren Werke Aktiengesellschaft, a Multinational Corporation, and DOES 1-20. The Complaint alleges three causes of action for: (1) breach of express warranty under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; (2) breach of implied warranty under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act; and (3) violation of the Song-Beverly Act Section 1793.2, subdivision (b).

 

            Plaintiff demurs to BMW’s 25 affirmative defenses. BMW opposes.

 

Legal Standard

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice.¿(Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿The purpose of a demurrer is to challenge the sufficiency of a pleading by raising questions of law.¿(Postley v. Harvey (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 280, 286.)¿“In the construction of a pleading, for the purpose of determining its effect, its allegations must be liberally construed, with a view to substantial justice between the parties.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 452.)¿The court “ ‘ “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law . . . .” ’ ”¿ (Berkley v. Dowds (2007) 152 Cal.App.4th 518, 525.)¿In applying these standards, the court liberally construes the complaint to determine whether a cause of action has been stated.¿(Picton v. Anderson Union High School Dist. (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 726, 733.) 

 

Analysis

 

 

            Meet and Confer

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, “a demurring party is required to meet and confer in person, by telephone, or video conference with the party that filed the pleading. . . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

            Plaintiff contends that he sent a meet-and-confer email to BMW. (Eissagholian Decl., ¶ 2.) However, email is not a permissible method of meeting and conferring under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41, subdivision (a). The parties must fulfill their statutory obligation to meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or video conference.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).)

 

           Thus, the Court will continue the hearing on Plaintiff’s demurrer to allow the parties to meet and confer in good faith.

 

Conclusion

 

            The hearing on Plaintiff Armando Carrillo’s demurrer is continued to October 23, 2026.

 

            The parties are ordered to meet and confer within 14 calendar days of this hearing. Plaintiff is ordered to file a meet-and-confer declaration in compliance with the respective code sections. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).) Plaintiff’s meet-and-confer declaration is due on October 16, 2026.
