TALI SIGALUS VS MARTIN K. P. CHIANG, ET AL.

 

MOTION TO COMPEL DISCOVERY RESPONSES

 

Date of Hearing:        July 28, 2026                                      Trial Date:       None set  

Department:              529                                                      Case No.:        24STCV26351

 

Moving Party:            Defendant Mason Chiang

Responding Party:     No opposition

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a wage and labor claim. On October 10, 2024, Plaintiff Tali Sigalus filed a complaint against Defendants Martin K. P. Chang and Mason Chang for various Labor Code violations as well as violation of Business and Professions Code and wrongful termination in violation of public policy.

 

On March 7, 2025, a cross-complaint was filed by Martin K.P. Chang against Tali Sigalus for elder financial abuse, intentional interference with contractual relations, intentional interference with prospective economic advantage, intentional misrepresentation, negligent misrepresentation, intentional infliction of emotional distress, negligent infliction of emotional distress, violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200, conversion, breach of employment contract, trespass to land, conversion and breach of fiduciary duty. 

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

I.                    Motion to Compel Responses to Special Interrogatories (Set Two) is GRANTED

II.                  Motion to Deem Admissions Admitted is GRANTED

III.                Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents (Set One) is GRANTED

 

DISCUSSION

 

I.                    MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES (SET TWO)

 

Defendant Mason Chiang moves this court for an order compelling Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Tali Sigalus to serve verified responses, without objection, to Defendant Mason Chiang’s Special Interrogatories, Set Two and for monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,246.50.

 

Where a party fails to serve timely responses to discovery requests, the court may make an order compelling responses. (CCP §§ 2030.290, 2031.300, 2033.280;¿Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants¿(2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 403.) A party that fails to serve a timely response to the discovery request waives any objection to the request, including one based on privilege or the protection of attorney work product. (CCP §§2030.290(a), 2031.300(a), 2033.280(a);¿Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc.,¿supra, 148 Cal.App.4th at 404.) Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.010, the propounding party may also move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction. (CCP § 2033.280(b).) 

 

Defendant Mason Chiang served Special Interrogatories to Tali Sigalus (Set Two) on Plaintiff’s counsel on April 9, 2026. The responses were due by May 10, 2026. As a courtesy, Defendant extended the deadline to respond to May 28, 2026. Despite the extension, Plaintiff failed to serve any responses by the extended deadline and has not served responses to date. No opposition has been filed.

 

Due to Plaintiff’s failure to timely respond the discovery requests, the court GRANTS the motion to compel responses to Special Interrogatories (Set Two). Plaintiff is to produce responses, without objection, within 20 days.

 

The court also GRANTS Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions in the reduced amount of $ $1,718.

 

II.                  MOTION TO DEEM REQUESTS FOR ADMISSIONS ADMITTED

 

Defendant Mason Chiang moves the court for an order deeming the truth of the matters specified in Defendant Mason Chiang’s Request for Admission to Tali Sigalus (Set One) admitted by Plaintiff and for monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,246.50.

 

Where a party fails to serve timely responses to discovery requests, the court may make an order compelling responses. (CCP §§ 2030.290, 2031.300, 2033.280;¿Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants¿(2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 403.) A party that fails to serve a timely response to the discovery request waives any objection to the request, including one based on privilege or the protection of attorney work product. (CCP §§2030.290(a), 2031.300(a), 2033.280(a);¿Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc.,¿supra, 148 Cal.App.4th at 404.) Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.010, the propounding party may also move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted, as well as for a monetary sanction. (CCP § 2033.280(b).) 

 

Defendant Chiang served Requests for Admission to Tali Sigalus (Set One) on Plaintiff's counsel on April 9, 2026. Plaintiff's responses were due on May 10, 2026. As a courtesy, Defendant extended the deadline. However, Plaintiff has not served any responses to date despite Defendant’s attempt to resolve the issue.

 

Due to Plaintiff’s failure to timely respond the discovery requests, the court GRANTS the motion to deem requests for admissions admitted.

 

The court also GRANTS Defendant’s request for monetary sanctions in the reduced amount of $859.

 

III.                MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS (SET ONE)

 

Defendant Mason Chiang moves this court for an order compelling Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Tali Sigalus to serve verified responses, without objection, to Defendant Mason Chiang’s Special Interrogatories, Set Two and for monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,228.50.

Case Number: 25STCV15420    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 529

THE HUMMINGBIRD RANCH, LLC VS JOHN LEONARDO, ET AL.

 

MOTION FOR PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

 

Date of Hearing:        July 28, 2026                                      Trial Date:       None set

Department:              529                                                      Case No.:        25STCV15420

 

Moving Party:            Cross-Complainants John Leonardo and JL Construction and Design, LLC

Responding Party:     Cross-Defendants The Hummingbird Ranch, LLC and Thomas Webb

 

BACKGROUND

 

On May 28, 2025, Plaintiff The Hummingbird Ranch, LLC filed a complaint against Defendants John Leonardo, Nancy Leonardo and JL Construction and Design, LLC for violation of Business and Professions Code section 7031, negligence, money had and received and accounting. On November 10, 2025, the parties stipulated to add Thomas Webb as a Plaintiff to the complaint. On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint, adding American Contractors Indemnity Company and asserting causes of action for violation of Business and Professions Code section 7031, money had and received, breach of contract, an action pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure 1029.8, and claim on contractors license bond.

 

Defendant John Leonardo filed a cross-complaint against The Hummingbird Ranch, LLC and Thomas L. Webb for quantum meruit. On September 15, 2025, Defendant Leonardo filed a First Amended Cross-Complaint, adding a cause of action for fraud and misappropriation; Civil Code §1709 §1710 and violation of Business and Professions Code §7027.3.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

Motion for Preliminary Injunction is DENIED.

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

Cross-Complainants John Leonardo and JL Construction and Design, LLC seek judicial notice of the existence, issuance, and content of County of San Bernardino building permit No. ALTR 2025-00786 for the property at 84889 Amboy Road, Wonder Valley, California, as an official act and record of a public agency — including that the permit lists John Leonardo, License No. 1079154, as the Primary Licensed Professional and General Contractor of record; describes the work as ‘Renew ALTR 2024-00052 for Final Inspection’; communications documents regarding the permit reflect ‘Owner Builder: No’; documents identify Thomas L. Webb as the applicant and representative; and documents reflect inspection activity occurring on the property under permit No. ALTR-2025-00786 in October 2025. (Exh. A.)

 

 

The court grants the request for judicial notice.

 

In opposition, Hummingbird Ranch, LLC and Thomas Webb request this court take judicial notice of the entire filing in this action, Cross-Defendants First Amended Cross-Complaint filed September 15, 2025, and Cross-Defendants Proposed Second Amended Cross-Complaint lodged on or about June 30, 2026.

 

The court grants the request for judicial notice.

 

In reply, Cross-Complaints request this court take judicial notice of the The Minute Order (Ruling on Submitted Matter) entered by this Court on January 13, 2026, ruling on Plaintiff and Cross-Defendants’ demurrer to the First Amended Cross-Complaint (Exh. 1) and The Certified License History for Contractor’s License No. 1079154, certified on June 26, 2026 by the Custodian of Records of the Contractors State License Board (Exh. 2).

 

The court grants the request for judicial notice.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Cross-Complainants John Leonardo and JL Construction and Design, LLC move for a preliminary injunction. Cross-Complainants seek an order restraining Cross-Defendants Thomas L. Webb and The Hummingbird Ranch, LLC, and their agents, from any further use of John Leonardo’s name or License No. 1079154 in connection with the Property or any San Bernadino County permit, including ALTR-2025-00786, and requiring them to take all steps necessary to remove Leonardo as contractor of record on ALTR-2025-00786. Cross-Complainants make the motion on the grounds everyday Cross-Complainants are listed as the contractor of record under License No. 1079154, he is exposed to CSLB discipline and bond liability for work he never performed, supervised, or authorized.

 

For preliminary injunctions, a party must establish both irreparable harm and a likelihood of success on the merits. (Millennium Rock Mortg., Inc. v. T.D. Service Co. (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 804, 812.) “An evaluation of the relative harm to the parties upon the granting or denial of a preliminary injunction requires consideration of: ‘(1) the inadequacy of any other remedy; (2) the degree of irreparable injury the denial of the injunction will cause; (3) the necessity to preserve the status quo; [and] (4) the degree of adverse effect on the public interest or interests of third parties the granting of the injunction will cause.’” (Vo v. City of Garden Grove (2004) 115 Cal.App.4th 425, 435.) “‘[T]he more likely it is that plaintiffs will ultimately prevail, the less severe must be the harm that they allege will occur if the injunction does not issue .... [I]t is the mix of these factors that guides the trial court in its exercise of discretion.’” (Right Site Coalition v. Los Angeles Unified School Dist. (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 336, 342.) “The ultimate questions on a motion for a preliminary injunction are (1) whether the plaintiff is 'likely to suffer greater injury from a denial of the injunction than the defendants are likely to suffer from its grant,’ and (2) whether there is ‘a reasonable probability that the plaintiffs will prevail on the merits’” (Huong Que, Inc. v. Luu (2007) 150 Cal.App.4th 400, 408. Procedurally, an application for a preliminary injunction, must be based upon sufficient evidence.  (CCP §527(a); Bank of America v. Williams (1948) 89 Cal.App.2d 21, 29.) 

 

Cross-Complainants request this court issue a preliminary injunction and restrain Cross-Defendants from use of Leonardo’s license on any San Bernardino permit. Cross-Complainants contend irreparable harm will occur as the permit is active and the County is presently treating Leonardo as the responsible licensee for work he did not perform. Cross-Complainants argue neither Leonardo’s explicit May 5, 2025 disavowal to the County nor this pending litigation stopped the permit’s continued use. Cross-Complainants also argue there is a likelihood of prevailing as the County’s own certified record establishes the operative facts: ALTR-2025-00786 lists Leonardo’s License No. 1079154 as the Primary Licensed Professional, is described as a ‘Renew[al of] ALTR-2024-00052,’ reflects ‘Owner-Builder: No,’ and identifies Webb as the applicant. Leonardo was unaware of this permit renewal, disavowed authorization and did not sign or consent.

 

In opposition, Cross-Defendants argue Leonardo authorized the use of his alleged contractor’s license in conjunction with the 2024 permit application and regardless, relief is moot because Cross-Defendants removed Leonardo’s information from the 786 permit once discovering the County failed to remove his information upon their initial request. Cross-Defendants also submit the declaration of Ricardo Moreno to establish Cross-Defendants did not use Leonardo’s contractor number without his permission.

 

Cross-Complaints argue, in reply, a party cannot defeat an injunction motion by altering the disputed record after being served, and voluntary cessation that begins only after service does not deprive the court of the power to grant effectual relief. Instead, cessation defeats injunctive relief only where there is no reasonable expectation the conduct will recur. (Center for Local Government Accountability v. City of San Diego (2016) 247 Cal.App.4th 1146, 1157.) In this matter, Cross-Complainants contend Plaintiff’s removal of Leonardo was not good-faith abandonment and there is reasonable expectation the conduct will be repeated as Cross-Defendants oppose this injunction, insists the use was authorized, is currently completing work on the property, and has already used the license twice (the second time while this action was pending). Cross-Defendants were also to keep the notice at the project so the claim Mr. Webb never saw the permit is not true. Moreover, the relief sought reaches any future use and is not limited to permit 786 as Cross-Defendants suggest.

 

The court denies the motion for preliminary injunction. As noted by Exhibit 1 of the opposition, Cross-Defendants had requested the contractor information be changed to an owner/builder permit back in August of 2025. Under penalty of perjury, Mr. Webb states when the County issued the 786 Permit, he never viewed a copy of the permit and was unaware that there was any reference to Leonardo. (Webb Decl. ¶10.) Upon discovering this information when he was served the instant motion, Mr. Webb took immediate action and requested to remove any reference to Leonardo. (Webb Decl., Exh. 3.) There is nothing before the court that Cross-Defendants would then again list Leonardo’s name or contractor’s license number as contractor of record on ALTR-2025-00786.

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion for preliminary injunction is DENIED.

 

Cross-Defendants also request the court set an OSC re: sanctions against both Cross-Complainants and their counsel, jointly and severally, to cover the costs to oppose this motion. The court declines to issue an OSC.  This case was a close call and nothing about Cross-complainants’ conduct was inappropriate, in this court’s opinion. 


Case Number: 26STCV09342    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 529

NOUSHIN NOURANI VS BRURIA LEAH RABI HAKAKHA

 

DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

 

Date of Hearing:        July 28, 2026                                      Trial Date:       September 14, 2026  

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        26STCV09342

 

Moving Party:            Defendant Bruria Leah Rabi Hakakha

Responding Party:     Plaintiff Noushin Nourani

Meet and Confer:      Yes. Iloulian Decl.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On March 23, 2026, Plaintiff Noushin Nourani filed a complaint against Defendant Bruria Leah Rabi Hakakha for slander per se, intentional infliction of emotional distress, negligent infliction of emotional distress, and negligence. Plaintiff alleges since November of 2025, Defendant has repeatedly told people that Plaintiff was a whore.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING:

 

Defendant Bruria Leah Rabi Hakakha’s Demurrer to the Complaint is SUSTAINED IN PART.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant demurs to the complaint on the grounds Plaintiff fails to allege facts sufficient to support any cause of action against Defendant. Defendant contends the alleged defamatory statement (“whore”) constitutes non-actionable opinion and rhetorical insult, the negligence based claims are improperly pled and duplicative, and Plaintiff fails to establish essential elements under California law.

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732 (internal citations omitted).) 

 

Defamation

 

Defendant argues to state a claim for defamation, Plaintiff must allege a false statement of fact, not opinion. Defendant maintains California courts have consistently held that name-calling, insults, and epithets—including terms like “whore”—are non-actionable opinion or rhetorical hyperbole, not provable factual assertions. Defendant also argues the claim fails to allege the exact words, who said them, to who and when.

 

Defendant cites no authority to support their proposition that epithets, such as whore, cannot be a claim for defamation. Contento v. Mitchell (1972) 28 Cal.App.3d 356 has expressly found that a defendant, in the presence of third parties, calling the plaintiff a ‘bitch’, ‘thief’, and ‘dirty whore’ involved slander per se. Moreover, “the issue of whether a statement is true or substantially true is normally considered to be a factual one.” (Hughes v. Hughes (2004) 122 Cal.App.4th 931, 937.) Here, Plaintiff has sufficiently alleged Defendant repeatedly told other people Plaintiff was a whore. The complaint alleges Defendant failed to use reasonable care to determine the truth or falsity of the statements and nonetheless, made them. This is sufficient for the purposes of a demurrer.

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer to the first cause of action for defamation is OVERRULED.

 

Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress

 

Defendant demurs to the third cause of action for negligent infliction of emotional distress on the grounds it is not an independent tort and Plaintiff fails to establish any duty owed by Defendant.

 

The court agrees there's no independent duty beyond what's already covered by defamation law. Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS the demurrer to the third cause of action for negligent infliction of emotional distress without leave to amend.

 

Negligence

 

Defendant demurs to the fourth cause of action for negligence on the grounds Plaintiff fails to identify any legally recognized duty of care owed by Defendant, Plaintiff improperly attempts to recast alleged intentional conduct as negligence and is duplicative of other claims.

 

As noted above, Plaintiff has not demonstrated an independent negligence tort for making false statements.

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer to the negligence cause of action is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Uncertainty

 

The court overrules the demurrer for uncertainty. “Demurrers for uncertainty under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e) are disfavored. [Citation.] ‘A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.’ [Citation.] [And a] demurrer for uncertainty should be overruled when the facts as to which the complaint is uncertain are presumptively within the defendant's knowledge.” (Chen v. Berenjian (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 811, 822.)
