DEPARTMENT S27 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS



Case Number: 23LBCV01659    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: S27

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiffs, Michael Caballero and Eunice Angelica Ibarra filed this action against Defendants, Jenny Dang, Tam Pham, and US Land Realty, Inc. for failure to disclose during the course of a real estate transaction.  Plaintiffs were the buyers of the property; Pham and US Land Realty were the sellers of the property; Dang was the dual agent for the purchase of the property.   

 

Plaintiffs filed their complaint on 9/01/23.  On 3/11/24, Defendants filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiffs for breach of contract and negligence.  The crux of the cross-complaint is that the parties, during escrow, learned of problems Defendants had not known when they filed their disclosures, and Defendants thereafter paid cash to have the problems remedied, but Plaintiffs refused to complete the sale despite the good faith efforts to remedy the problems. 

 

2.     Stipulated Judgment

On 10/22/25, the Court signed the parties’ amended stipulated judgment, pursuant to which Defendants were to pay monies to Plaintiffs, sell the subject property to Plaintiffs, cancel a specified escrow account, release funds from the account to Plaintiffs, open a new escrow account, transfer Defendants’ rights to tenant leases in the property, transfer security deposits and related deposits to Plaintiffs, and convey title to the property free and clear of liens, claims, and encumbrances.

 

The stipulated judgment then contains a provision clarifying that, if there is a legal or equitable action, arbitration, or other proceeding brought because of a dispute, breach, default, etc., in connection with the stipulated judgment, the prevailing party can recover attorneys’ fees. 

 

The stipulated judgment then contains a provision clarifying that the Court retains jurisdiction to enforce the judgment pursuant to CCP §664.6.

 

3.     Prior Hearings on Motion to Enforce Stipulated Judgment

On 1/07/26, Plaintiffs filed a motion to enforce stipulated judgment.  On 2/10/26, Plaintiffs filed an amended motion to enforce the stipulated judgment.  Specifically, they sought the following order:

(1) Defendants are to pay $23,785.65 for damages from Defendants breach of the Stipulated Judgment no later than three (3) days from the entry of this Order.  

(2)  Defendants are ordered to pay monetary sanctions in the amount of $24,932.04 for reasonable attorneys fees and costs caused by Defendants breach of the Stipulated Judgment to Plaintiffs no later than three (3) days from the entry of this Order.

(3) Defendants are ordered to produce all contact information for any persons or entities with a lien against real property located at 1520-1522 Bixby Dr., Long Beach, California no later than three (3) days from the entry of this Order.

(4) Defendants are ordered to produce all documents relating to any existing liens against the real property located at 1520-1522 Bixby Dr., Long Beach, California (“Property”) no later than three (3) days from the entry of this Order.

(5) All liens on the Property that were recorded after Plaintiffs’ lis pendens was recorded, on April 4, 2024, are not bone fide liens and Plaintiffs’ lis pendens is superior to those liens. All liens on the Property recorded after April 4, 2024 are hereby discharged.  

(6) The Clerk of the Superior Court shall sign all documents required to transfer title of the Property to the Plaintiffs. 

 

Defendants opposed the motion, and the Court issued a tentative ruling prior to the hearing as follows:

CCP §664.6(a) permits the Court to “enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.”  In this case, the parties did not enter into a stipulation for entry of judgment upon default, nor did they enter into a settlement agreement.  They entered into a stipulated judgment.  There is, therefore, already a judgment.  The Court cannot enforce the parties’ judgment by entering another judgment.  Plaintiff cites no authority permitting the Court to do so, and the Court knows of none. 

 

Additionally, there is no provision in the parties’ stipulated judgment permitting an award of monetary or other damages not contemplated by the settlement itself.  Plaintiff cites no authority permitting the Court to add monetary damages to the stipulated judgment, and again, the Court knows of none.  Indeed, Defendants cite authority expressly precluding the Court from ordering such other and further relief in their opposition papers, and Plaintiffs appear to drop the argument in reply.

 

Plaintiffs correctly note that ¶9 of the stipulated judgment permits them to recover attorneys’ fees in an action or other proceeding to enforce the judgment.  They must, however, prevail on their request in order to recover those fees.  Because Plaintiffs did not provide any authority for the relief they seek, the request is denied.

 

Plaintiffs seek sanctions pursuant to CCP §128.5(f).  Plaintiffs failed to comply with §128.5(f)(1)(A) and (B), which require a motion for sanctions to be made separately from any other request for relief and require a “safe harbor” period before the motion is filed.  Absent compliance with the section, the Court will not award sanctions.

 

At the conclusion of the 3/26/26 hearing on the motion, the Court, at Plaintiffs’ request, continued the hearing on the motion to 4/28/26.  Plaintiffs ultimately took the hearing off calendar, and the Court never issued a final ruling on the motion.

 

4.     Current Motion to Enforce Stipulated Judgment

On 6/05/26, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion to enforce stipulated judgment, setting it for hearing on 9/17/26.  On 6/24/26, Plaintiffs appeared ex parte seeking an order shortening time for hearing on the motion.  The Court granted the application and scheduled the motion for hearing on 7/07/26.  The Court did not clarify whether or when opposition to the motion must be filed in its order granting the application.

 

Despite the lack of opposition to the motion, the motion suffers from the same basic problem detailed above re: the prior motion.  The motion seeks the following order:

(1)  Defendants are ordered to pay monetary sanctions in the amount of $$4,454.70  for reasonable attorney’s fees and costs for the filing of this present Motion to Plaintiffs no later than three (3) days from the entry of this Order.

(2) All encumbrances on the Property that were recorded after Plaintiffs’ lis pendens was recorded, on April 4, 2024, are not bone fide encumbrances and Plaintiffs’ lis pendens is superior to those encumbrances. All liens on the Property recorded after April 4, 2024, are hereby discharged.  

(3) The Deed of Trust recorded on October 11, 2023 with recording number 20230710660, is hereby discharged due to the reconveyance, with recording number 20250770112, recorded on November 5, 2025.  

(4) The Clerk of the Superior Court shall sign all documents required to transfer title of the Property to the Plaintiffs.

(5) A bond of $50,000 is to be paid by Plaintiffs to secure the Deed of Trust with recording number 20230731840 within ninety (90) days of receiving title. As such, the Deed of Trust with recording number 20230731840 is hereby discharged.

(6) Defendant Jenny Dang remains responsible for repayment of the Deed of Trust with recording number 20230731840. 

 

As discussed above, however, there is already a judgment in place in this action.  Thus, to the extent the motion is made pursuant to CCP §664.6, the cited statute does not permit the Court to enter the order sought by Plaintiffs.  §664.6 permits the Court to reduce a settlement to a judgment.  It does not permit the Court to take a judgment that already exists and issue an order “enforcing” the judgment.

 

To the extent Plaintiffs rely on CCP §§128 and 187, Plaintiffs fail to provide authority that stands for the position that the Court can issue the order sought under the circumstances.  Plaintiffs cite Sears v. Rule, 27 Cal.2d (1945) 131, 149 and Redke v. Silvertrust (1971) 6 Cal.3d 94, 108 to support their position that the Court can and should sign title of the property over to Plaintiffs.  The cited cases, however, concern what findings a trial court can make at the conclusion of a trial and what terms can be placed in the judgment; nothing in the cited cases indicates the Court can sign title of a property over from one party to another in order to enforce a judgment. 

 

Finally, Plaintiffs seek imposition of attorneys’ fees as sanctions, but their request suffers from the same defects detailed above in connection with the same request in the prior motion.  To the extent the motion is premised on the attorneys’ fees provision in the parties’ agreement, Plaintiffs have not prevailed on any motion re: enforcement of the judgment, and therefore fees are not appropriate.  The Court has revisited the §128.5 issue and finds the safe harbor period does not apply, as the alleged action or tactic is not the making or opposing of a written motion or the filing and service of a complaint, cross-complaint, answer, or other responsive pleading.  The Court is not, however, familiar with the use of §128.5 in connection with post-judgment enforcement proceedings.  Additionally, §128.5(f)(1)(A) makes clear that a motion for sanctions under §128.5 must be made “separately from other motions or requests.”  Plaintiff tacked the request for relief onto the motion, which primarily seeks relief under §664.6, and this is not permissible.  The request for sanctions is therefore also denied.

 

The Court notes that Plaintiffs filed a motion for an OSC re: contempt on 6/01/26, setting it for hearing on 6/30/26.  For reasons that are not clear, Plaintiffs took the motion for an OSC re: contempt off calendar prior to the hearing, and the Court did not consider the request. 

 

The Court is not, in connection with today’s motion, holding that Plaintiffs have no available relief.  The Court is holding only that Plaintiffs’ cited statutes and authorities do not support the relief sought, and the motion must be denied.  The Court urges the parties to work together to resolve outstanding issues, and makes clear that this ruling is without prejudice to Plaintiffs’ right to renew this or any similar request for relief if Plaintiffs can provide on-point and relevant authority supporting the relief sought.

 

Plaintiffs are ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24LBCV01509    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: S27

1.     Background Facts

Plaintiff, Mooath Saidi filed this action against Defendants, Porsche Cars North America, Inc. (“PCNAI”) and Porsche Long Beach for violations of Song-Beverly and related claims.  On 7/30/25, Plaintiff signed the settlement agreement proposed by PCNAI.  On 8/20/25, with both parties present through their attorneys in the courtroom, the Court dismissed the case in light of the parties’ settlement and retained jurisdiction to enforce the settlement per CCP §664.6. 

 

2.     Motion to Enforce Settlement

On 1/23/26, Plaintiff filed this motion to enforce the settlement by entering judgment in Plaintiff’s favor.  Plaintiff contends Defendant is refusing to respond to his efforts to return his car, and has also made no attempts to make the required payment to him pursuant to the terms of the settlement agreement. 

 

Per CCP §1005(b), opposition to the motion was due nine court days prior to the hearing, which fell on 6/23/24.  Defendant filed opposition papers one day late, on 6/24/26.  The Court has read and considered the opposition papers, but asks Defense Counsel to ensure timely filing in the future.

 

Defendant correctly notes in opposition to the motion that it did not sign the agreement attached as Exhibit A to the moving papers.  CCP §664.6 permits the Court to enforce a settlement agreement “in a writing signed by the parties.”  Plaintiff contends Defendant drafted the settlement agreement, but presents no authority standing for the position that an entity that drafts a settlement agreement is bound by the agreement for purposes of §664.6 even if the entity did not sign the agreement.   Notably, Plaintiff addresses this argument in reply, but cites general contract law, not law interpreting §664.6.  Again, §664.6, by its very terms, requires the writing to be “signed by the parties.”  While Plaintiff may have other enforcement mechanisms, such as an action for breach of contract, the motion under §664.6 cannot be granted unless the writing is “signed by the parties.” 

 

The motion is denied.

Case Number: 25LBCV00438    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: S27

Applicable Law

CCP § 657 provides in part: 

The verdict may be vacated and any other decision may be modified or vacated, in whole or in part, and a new or further trial granted on all or part of the issues, on the application of the party aggrieved, for any of the following causes, materially affecting the substantial rights of such party: 

1. Irregularity in the proceedings of the court, jury or adverse party, or any order of the court or abuse of discretion by which either party was prevented from having a fair trial. 

2. Misconduct of the jury; and whenever any one or more of the jurors have been induced to assent to any general or special verdict, or to a finding on any question submitted to them by the court, by a resort to the determination of chance, such misconduct may be proved by the affidavit of any one of the jurors. 

3. Accident or surprise, which ordinary prudence could not have guarded against. 

4. Newly discovered evidence, material for the party making the application, which he could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered and produced at the trial. 

5. Excessive or inadequate damages. 

6. Insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, or the verdict or other decision is against law. 

7. Error in law, occurring at the trial and excepted to by the party making the application. 

When a new trial is granted, on all or part of the issues, the court shall specify the ground or grounds upon which it is granted and the court’s reason or reasons for granting the new trial upon each ground stated. 

A new trial shall not be granted upon the ground of insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, nor upon the ground of excessive or inadequate damages, unless after weighing the evidence the court is convinced from the entire record, including reasonable inferences therefrom, that the court or jury clearly should have reached a different verdict or decision. 

(CCP § 657.)  

Granting a motion for a new trial requires a re-examination of issues of fact or law after an underlying trial and decision by a judge or jury. (CCP §§ 656, 657.) “The effect of an order granting a new trial is “ ‘as though no trial had ever been had.... The case [is] before the court for trial de novo.’ [Citation.]” [Citation omitted.] The grant of a new trial ‘[leaves] the case at large and the parties [are] placed in the same position as if it had never been tried....’ ” (Saakyan v. Modern Auto, Inc. (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 383, 390.)   

When a motion for a new trial is based upon an “error in law” under CCP § 657(7), “a trial court may grant a new trial if ‘its original ruling, as a matter of law, was erroneous.’” (Collins v. Sutter Mem’l Hosp. (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1, 17–18 [quoting Ramirez v. USAA Cas. Ins. Co. (1991) 234 Cal.App.3d 391, 397].) Error in law is to be contrasted against a verdict that is “against law,” which means that it was “unsupported by any substantial evidence.” (Sanchez-Corea v. Bank of Am. (1985) 38 Cal.3d 892, 906 [quoting Kralyevich v. Magrini (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 784, 789].) Any “error of law,” “irregularity in the proceedings,” or abuse of discretion must also be prejudicial. “[T]he trial court is bound by the rule of California Constitution, article VI, section 13, that prejudicial error is the basis for a new trial, and there is no discretion to grant a new trial for harmless error.” (Sherman v. Kinetic Concepts, Inc. (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1152, 1161 [citation omitted]; see Cal. Const., art. VI, § 13 [“No judgment shall be set aside, or new trial granted, in any cause, on the ground of . . . any error as to any matter of procedure, unless, after an examination of the entire cause, including the evidence, the court shall be of the opinion that the error complained of has resulted in a miscarriage of justice.”].)   

Preliminary Matters

A.    Timeliness of Opposition

            “Within 10 days of filing the notice [of intent to move for new trial], the moving party shall serve upon all other parties and file any brief and accompanying documents, including affidavits in support of the motion. The other parties shall have 10 days after that service within which to serve upon the moving party and file any opposing briefs and accompanying documents, including counter-affidavits. The moving party shall have five days after that service to file any reply brief and accompanying documents. These deadlines may, for good cause shown by affidavit or by written stipulation of the parties, be extended by any judge for an additional period not to exceed 10 days.” (CCP § 659a.)

            Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s opposition should be disregarded as untimely. Plaintiff filed and served the motion on April 29, 2026. Defendant filed its opposition on June 11, 2026, which is untimely under the statute. Defendant’s opposition was due no later than May 13, 2026. Defendant did not file an affidavit or stipulation showing good cause for an extension, and even if good cause was shown, that extension would only give Defendant until May 23, 2026, making the opposition untimely still. Thus, although Plaintiff was able to respond substantively and does not appear prejudiced, the Court must disregard Defendant’s untimely opposition per the statute.

Analysis

B.     Whether the Court Improperly Weighed Evidence

            Plaintiff argues that this Court’s ruling “reflects precisely the type of improper evidentiary weighing prohibited at the summary judgment stage” when the Court accepted Defendant’s evidence regarding weekly inspection practices “while discounting Plaintiff’s evidence and arguments demonstrating that applicable safety regulations required daily inspections and recordkeeping.” Plaintiff cites to their opposition to the MSJ wherein they argued that “California Code of Regulations, title 22, sections 65523 and 65535 impose mandatory duties on pool operators to conduct and document daily inspections of ancillary facilities, including saunas.” (Emphasis added.) Plaintiff puts forth that Defendant’s own evidence established at most a weekly inspection protocol, which directly conflicts with these regulatory requirements.

            Here, Plaintiff’s characterization of their evidence in opposition to the MSJ and the statutory authority for frequency of inspections of the sauna is factually incorrect. Plaintiff’s opposition to the MSJ never credibly put forth that daily inspections of the sauna were statutorily mandated, such that the Court did not err in finding no triable issue of material fact on this issue. Plaintiff’s opposition to the MSJ stated, “Title 22 of the California Code of Regulations, including sections 65501(a), 65502, 65523, and 65535, require operators to maintain facilities in good repair and to conduct and document routine safety inspections. . . 22 CCR § 65523(a) and (d) require operators to maintain written daily records of inspections, maintenance, and corrective actions taken to address safety issues.” CCR§ 65501(a) defines "Ancillary Facility" as “any area used in conjunction with or for the operation of a public pool, including, but not limited to, public dressing rooms, lockers, shower or bathroom areas, drinking fountains, equipment rooms, pool deck areas, pool enclosures, or building spaces, that is intended to be used by pool users.”

            The statutes Plaintiff references make no mention of daily inspections of ancillary facilities such as saunas where Plaintiff’s alleged injury occurred. The only mention of daily inspections in CCR§ 65523 concerns pool operators of public pools being required to “test the disinfectant residual and pH of the public pool water a minimum of once per day. . . The pool operator shall maintain a written daily record of all test results, equipment readings, calibrations, and corrective action taken at the public pool site.” CCR§ 65535 similarly lacks the “daily” language: “The pool operator shall keep all parts of the public pool site well-maintained, including, but not limited to, the public pools, water treatment systems, ancillary facilities, signs, showers, toilets, dressing facilities, drinking fountains, diaper-changing stations, floors, walls, partitions, doors, and lockers.”

            Further, Plaintiff’s cited code section “CCR§ 65502” does not exist.

            Overall, Plaintiff fails to reasonably put forth a basis for their statutory interpretation, and improperly characterizes their interpretation as “evidence” that this Court failed to consider. Plaintiff failed to carry their burden in opposition to summary judgment and this Court was within its bounds to find as such.

            Plaintiff’s next argues that the Court (1) “improperly resolved issues relating to the enforceability of the liability waiver. .  despite evidence [of] statutory noncompliance” and (2) “drew inferences in favor of the moving party while rejecting reasonable competing inferences supported by Plaintiff’s evidence. . . by choosing between those competing interpretations—particularly with respect to inspection adequacy” are similarly unavailing for the reasons already enumerated.

            Thus, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED on this basis.

C.    Irregularity in the Proceedings

            Plaintiff argues that an irregularity in the proceedings occurred when the Court ruled on Defendant’s objections to Plaintiff’s opposition, but failed to rule on Plaintiff’s objections to Defendant’s reply. On April 2, 2026, after briefing concluded and all briefing deadlines passed, Plaintiff objected to the declaration of Madee Rafuson in support of Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment.

            Here, the Court disregarded Plaintiff’s objections to the declaration of Madee Rafuson in support of Defendant’s Motion because they were waived. Defendant relied on the declaration of  Madee Rafuson in its moving papers and separate statement. Plaintiff filed opposition on March 16, 2026, without lodging any objections. Plaintiff puts forth no valid reason for failing to object in opposition, instead waiting to object on April 2, 2026, five (5) days before the hearing and after Defendant filed its reply.

            Plaintiff argues Rafuson’s unavailability for deposition as a basis for the objection to her declaration, which they claim to have learned “shortly before the hearing.” In support of this Motion, Counsel submits his declaration, which largely consists of improper legal argument. Counsel declares, without foundation, that he “is informed and believe that Defendants provided late notice regarding the unavailability of declarant Madee Rafuson, asserting she was no longer employed by Defendants. This late disclosure effectively prejudiced Plaintiff's ability to challenge the veracity of her assertions through a timely deposition.” (Swinton Decl., ¶ 4.) Counsel puts forth no communications with Defendant, or any other facts establishing how or when Plaintiff received “late notice” of Rafuson’s unavailability for deposition. Further, Plaintiff puts forth no reasonable explanation for failing to inquire as to Rafuson’s availability for deposition earlier, rather than seemingly waiting until after they filed opposition. Assuming Rafuson had been available for deposition, which Plaintiff could have only learned after filing opposition, Plaintiff fails to argue how this availability would have changed the Court’s ruling. Plaintiff waived this objection by failing to raise it in opposition. Plaintiff’s lack of diligence is the cause of their own peril.

            Thus, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED on this basis.

D.    Accident or Surprise

            Plaintiff argues for relief on the basis of “surprise or excusable neglect,” again claiming that Plaintiff learned shortly before the hearing that Madee Rafuson was unavailable for deposition. Again, the Court finds that Plaintiff failed to inquire as to the availability of the witness for deposition before filing their opposition and failed to raise the issue in opposition.

Plaintiff declares that “[t]he unavailability of a critical defense declarant, whose statements the Court relied upon in granting Summary Judgment, was a surprise that I could not have reasonably guarded against.” (Hernandez Decl., ¶ 7.) This Court disagrees. Plaintiff’s counsel could have guarded against this “surprise” by timely inquiring as to the declarant’s availability for deposition and raising such objections in opposition. Evan taking Plaintiff’s argument generously, were Plaintiff to have learned after filing opposition that Madee Rafuson was available for deposition, it is unclear how that would have affected the Court’s ruling as Plaintiff could not have used that deposition in its already filed opposition. Plaintiff’s lack of diligence is the root cause of this issue.

            Thus, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED on this basis.

E.     Newly Discovered Evidence

            In Plaintiff Angel Hernandez’s declaration, which largely consists of improper legal argument, Plaintiff declares that “[s]ince the Court's ruling on April 7, 2026, I have

discovered additional evidence regarding the Defendants' failure to comply with specific

safety regulations at the facility. This evidence was not available to me at the time of the

Summary Judgment hearing despite my diligent efforts to investigate the matter.” (Hernandez Decl., ¶ 6.) However, in the body of the Motion, Plaintiff fails to identify what this newly discovered evidence is. Plaintiff essentially makes no argument regarding newly discovered evidence. Legal conclusions are not argument.

            Thus, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED on this basis.

F.     Insufficiency of Evidence or Error in Law

            Plaintiff’s motion makes no argument regarding insufficiency of evidence or error in law.

Thus, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED on this basis.

 

 

CONCLUSION

Plaintiff Angel Hernandez’s Motion for New Trial / Relief from Judgment is DENIED.

Case Number: 25LBCV01397    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: S27

Defendant moves for summary judgment on Plaintiff’s complaint.  Plaintiff failed to timely file opposition papers. 

 

Recently, in Mandell-Brown v. Novo Nordisk Inc. (2025) 109 Cal.App.5th 478, 508, the Court of Appeals considered a situation where a plaintiff failed to file an opposing separate statement prior to the hearing on the defendant’s motion.  The Mandell-Brown Court noted, “Thus, if a plaintiff opposing summary judgment fails to file a separate statement, and the trial court reviews the moving papers and concludes the motion is not deficient on its face, it has discretion under subdivision (b)(3) to grant the motion, without first undertaking a detailed analysis of the supporting evidence to determine if a prima facie showing has been made as to one or more of the elements of each claim."

 

The Court has reviewed the moving papers and finds they are not deficient on their face.  In light of the lack of opposition and an opposing separate statement, the Court grants the motion.

 

Recently, in 24LBCV02791, the Court admonished Plaintiff’s attorney as follows:

The Court notes that there have been numerous motions for summary judgment and/or adjudication filed by Honda in various cases without opposition.  The Court asks Plaintiff’s attorney to dismiss any case or cause of action the plaintiff does not intend to pursue.  Requiring the Court to rule on unopposed motions is not an efficient use of judicial resources.

 

Despite this admonishment, Plaintiff has neither dismissed the action nor opposed the motion.  Again, the motion is granted.  Defendant is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25LBCV01997    Hearing Date: July 7, 2026    Dept: S27

This case is currently set for trial on 9/14/26.  Defendant made a reservation for a motion for summary judgment, setting it on the first available date for hearing, which is 10/29/26.  Defendant seeks an order continuing the trial date until at least thirty days after the MSJ hearing date; specifically, Defendant seeks to continue trial to 11/30/26 or any date convenient for the Court. 

 

To date, no party has filed opposition to the motion.  The Court, in ruling on the motion, is guided by the case of Wells Fargo Bank v. Superior Court.  The Court therein held that a trial court may not refuse to hear a summary judgment motion filed within the time limits of section 437c.  (Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 918. 919.)  Local rules and practices may not be applied so as to prevent the filing and hearing of such a motion. (Id.; Sentry Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 207 Cal.App.3d 526, 529.)  “We are sympathetic to the problems the trial courts experience in calendaring and hearing the many motions for summary judgment.  However, the solution to these problems cannot rest in a refusal to hear timely motions.”  (Id., at p. 530.)

 

In light of the lack of opposition to the motion, the motion is granted.
