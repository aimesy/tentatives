[tentative] Order RE:

 

(1)   DEFENDANT GEORGE W. CARROLL’S MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF PRISCILLA H. WRIGHT’S RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SET ONE, AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

(2)   DEFENDANT GEORGE W. CARROLL’S MOTION TO COMPEL PLAINTIFF PRISCILLA H. WRIGHT’S RESPONSES TO Request for production of documents, SET ONE, AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant George W. Carroll

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright

(1)   Motion to Compel Plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright’s Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One

(2)   Motion to Compel Plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright’s Responses to Request for Production of Documents, Set One

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with these motions.

DISCUSSION

Defendant George W. Carroll (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order (1) compelling plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright (“P. Wright”) to serve responses to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Defendant and against P. Wright and her counsel Rick Augustini and Michael F. Wright in the amount of $2,375.00 as to the Form Interrogatories motion and $2,375.00 as to the Request for Production of Documents motion.  

If a party to whom interrogatories and requests for production of documents are directed¿fails to¿serve¿a timely¿response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses to the interrogatories and the requests for production of documents.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, subd. (b), 2031.300, subd. (b).)¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

Defendant served P. Wright with Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production on March 21, 2023, but P. Wright refused to respond and threatened a motion for protective order.  (Mateescu Decl., ¶ 2; Ex. A.)  The parties entered into a stipulation on May 12, 2023, and Defendant served another first set of Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production on September 16, 2025.  (Mateescu Decl., ¶ 2; Ex. B, C, PDF p. 29 [September 16, 2025 proof of service].)  P. Wright refused to respond again and Defendant served the subject Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production of Documents on September 26, 2025.  (FROGs, Mateescu Decl., Ex. D, PDF p. 39 [September 26, 2025 proof of service]; RFPs, Mateescu Decl., Ex. D, PDF p. 83 [September 26, 2025 proof of service.)  P. Wright did not serve responses to the Form Interrogatories or the Requests for Production.  (Mateescu Decl., ¶ 2.)  

Instead, P. Wright filed, on October 28, 2025, a motion for protective order, in which P. Wright requested an order excusing her from responding to any written discovery from Defendant.  (Oct. 28, 2025 Notice of Mot. for Protective Order, p. 1:4-8 [requesting an order excusing P. Wright from responding “to any written discovery from Defendant[,]” including the “Form Interrogatories . . . that Defendant purported to serve electronically on September 26, 2025 . . . .”].)   

The court finds that Defendant has not shown, as of the date that Defendant filed these motions, that P. Wright failed to timely serve responses to the Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production of Documents because  (1) P. Wright filed her motion for protective order within the time that she was required to serve responses, and (2) P. Wright’s filing of a motion requesting a protective order excusing her from serving responses to the Form Interrogatories and Request for Production stayed P. Wright’s obligation to serve responses thereto until the court ruled on the motion for protective order.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.090, subd. (a) [authorizing a party to file a motion for protective order when interrogatories have been propounded]; Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.060, subd. (a) [authorizing a party to file a motion for protective order when demands for production have been propounded]; Civ. Code, § 3532 [“The law neither does nor requires idle acts”].)  

However, because the court denied P. Wright’s motion for protective order on April 22, 2026, P. Wright was required to serve responses to Defendant’s Form Interrogatories and Request for Production.  P. Wright has shown that she served responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Production on May 22, 2026.  (Augustini Decl., ¶ 13; Ex. M, PDF p. 114, Ex. N, PDF p. 159.)  Thus, the court denies Defendant’s motions to compel responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One and Request for Production of Documents, Set One. 

The court denies Defendant’s and P. Wright’s requests for monetary sanctions because the court finds that the circumstances presented make the imposition of sanctions unjust.

 

ORDER

            The court denies defendant George W. Carroll’s motions to compel plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One and Request for Production of Documents, Set One.

The court denies defendant George W. Carroll’s request for monetary sanctions.

The court denies plaintiff Priscilla H. Wright’s request for monetary sanctions.            

The court orders defendant George W. Carroll to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[tentative] Order RE:

 

plaintiffs/cross-defendants einride us inc., einride inc., and einride ab’s motion to seal portions of demurrer to the cross-complaint and exhibits 1-6 of the supporting smith declaration

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiffs/Cross-Defendants Einride US Inc., Einride Inc., and Einride AB  

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

Motion to Seal Portions of Demurrer to the Cross-Complaint and Exhibits 1-6

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition has been received.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs and cross-defendants Einride US Inc., Einride Inc., and Einride AB (“Cross-Defendants”) move the court for an order sealing portions of Cross-Defendants’ demurrer to cross-complainants Maersk, A/S and Maersk Logistics & Services USA Inc.’s Cross-Complaint.

 

Generally, court records are presumed to be open unless confidentiality is required by law.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.550, subd. (c).)¿ If the presumption of access applies, the court may order that a record be filed under seal “if it expressly finds facts that establish: (1) There exists an overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access to the record; (2) The overriding interest supports sealing the record; (3) A substantial probability exists that the overriding interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed; (4) The proposed sealing is narrowly tailored; and (5) No less restrictive means exist to achieve the overriding interest.”  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.550, subd. (d).)¿ “[T]he party seeking to seal documents, or maintain them under seal, must come forward with a specific enumeration of the facts sought to be withheld and specific reasons for withholding them.”¿ (HB Fuller Co. v. Doe (2007) 151 Cal.App.4th 879, 894.)

The court finds that Cross-Defendants have shown that (1) there exists an overriding interest that overcomes the right of public access to the portions of the cross-complaint set forth above because they (i) describe or relate to confidential contractual terms, and (ii) include similar information that this court has sealed in connection with the complaints, the demurrer, and the opposition to the demurrer in this action, (2) the overriding interest in keeping this information confidential supports sealing the record, (3) a substantial probability exists that the overriding interest will be prejudiced if the record is not sealed, (4) the proposed sealing is narrowly tailored since Defendants request that the court seal only commercially sensitive contents of the demurrer to the cross-complaint, and (5) no less restrictive means exist to achieve the overriding interest.  (Motion, pp. 5:9-7:22; Smith Decl., ¶¶ 2-13.)  (Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Superior Court, supra, 110 Cal.App.4th 1273, 1279.)  Thus, the court grants Cross-Defendants’ motion to seal.

ORDER

The court grants plaintiffs and cross-defendants Einride US Inc., Einride Inc., and Einride AB’s motion to seal portions of the demurrer to the cross-complaint and exhibits 1-6.  

Pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 2.551, subdivision (e), the court directs the clerk to file this order, maintain the records ordered sealed in a secure manner, and clearly identify the records as sealed by this order.  

The court orders cross-defendants Einride US Inc., Einride Inc., and Einride AB to give notice of this ruling.  

IT IS SO ORDERED.

[tentative] Order RE:

 

motions to be relieved as counsel for plaintiff

 

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiffs’ Counsel, Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya of Dolan Law Firm, PC 

 

RESPONDING PARTY:       Unopposed

Motions to be Relieved as Counsel for Plaintiffs

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with these motions.  No opposition papers were filed.

DISCUSSION

Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya on behalf of Dolan Law Firm, PC (“Plaintiffs’ Counsel”) have filed separate motions to be relieved as counsel for plaintiffs Marlen Solano and Ulises Rangel (“Plaintiffs”) in this action.

“The question of granting or denying an application of an attorney to withdraw as counsel (Code Civ. Proc., § 284, subd. 2) is one which lies within the sound discretion of the trial court ‘having in mind whether such withdrawal might work an injustice in the handling of the case.’”¿ (People v. Prince (1968) 268 Cal.App.2d 398, 406 [internal quotations omitted].)¿ The court should also consider whether the attorney’s “withdrawal can be accomplished without undue prejudice to the client’s interests.”¿ (Ramirez v. Sturdevant (1994) 21 Cal.App.4th 904, 915.)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

For a motion to be relieved as counsel under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2), California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362 requires (1) a notice of motion and motion directed to the client (made on the Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-051)); (2) a declaration stating in general terms and without compromising the confidentiality of the attorney-client relationship why a motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (2) is brought instead of filing a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284, subdivision (1) (made on the Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-052)); (3) service of the notice of motion and motion, declaration, and proposed order on the client and on all other parties who have appeared in the case; and (4) the proposed order relieving counsel (prepared on the Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to Be Relieved as Counsel -- Civil form (MC-053)).¿¿¿¿¿ 

The court finds that Plaintiffs’ Counsel’s moving papers are deficient.  First, the two proofs of service filed on July 9, 2026, show that Plaintiffs’ Counsel served Plaintiffs with the moving papers on July 9, 2026.  Thus, Plaintiffs’ Counsel did not timely serve the moving papers.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005, subd. (b) [“all moving and supporting papers shall be served and filed at least 16 court days before the hearing . . . and if the notice is served by facsimile transmission, express mail, or another method of delivery providing for overnight delivery, the required 16-day period of notice before the hearing shall be increased by two calendar days”].)  Second, Plaintiffs Counsel’s declaration also states that “Attorney has personally served the client with copies of the motion papers filed with this declaration.”  (MC-052, ¶ 3a.(1).)  However, the proofs of service show that Plaintiffs’ Counsel served the moving papers on Plaintiffs by overnight delivery, not personal service.  Third, pursuant to California Rules of Court rule 3.1362(d), notice of motion and the moving papers may be given by personal service, electronic service, or mail, not overnight delivery.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(d).)

The court therefore denies Plaintiffs’ Counsel’s motions to be relieved as counsel for Plaintiffs.

ORDER

The court denies Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya of Dolan Law Firm, PC’s motions to be relieved as counsel without prejudice.

The court orders Chirstopher B. Dolan, Maria Pappas-Rajotte, Allison Stone, and Anna Pantsulaya of Dolan Law Firm, PC to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

DATED:  July 16, 2026
