TENTATIVE RULING

 

DION M. PERONNEAU

 

         vs.

 

KEITH JACKSON., et al.

 

 Case No.: 24STCV09259 

Consolidated with Case No.: 24STCV19183

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 14, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion for trial preference is DENIED.

 

            On 4/12/2024, Plaintiff Dion M. Peronneau (Plaintiff) filed suit against Keith Jackson, Mason Shayan, and Ali Shayan, alleging: (1) nuisance; (2) trespass; and (3) negligence.

 

            On 8/20/2026, Plaintiff moved for trial preference.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff moves for an order granting calendar preference pursuant to California Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) section 36(a) and (e).

 

CCP section 36(a) provides for trial preference where: (1) the party is over 70; (2) they have a substantial interest in the action; and (3) their health is such that preference is necessary to avoid prejudice. The standard for granting preference under § 36(a) is a “low threshold” (Fox v. Superior Court (2018) 21 Cal.App.5th 529). The Court in Fox held that a plaintiff need not show they are "likely to die" before trial; rather, they must only show that their health makes preference necessary to protect their interest.

 

CCP section 36(e) provides for discretionary preference where the interests of justice would be served by an expedited trial setting.

 

Here, Plaintiff submitted evidence that she is72 years old, that she is the sole Plaintiff and owner-occupant of the property at issue, and that her house remains partially uninhabitable:

 

The home remains partially uninhabitable as of the filing of this Motion. It does not have working heat. It does not have working air conditioning. Open floor and wall cavities continue to release dust and silt from beneath the structure into the interior living space. Mold has grown on bathroom walls and floors near the shower and drain, and sludge has been observed emerging from the bathroom drain. Plaintiff is unable to open her windows without inviting additional dust intrusion. (JHK Dec., ¶ 11; Peronneau Decl., ¶¶ 6–10.)

 

However, Plaintiff does not submit a declaration from her treating physician (Alpha Opp., p. 4:20-21), “competent medical evidence of a diagnosable injury” (Stewart Opp., p. 3:9), conditions that are “life-threatening” (Alpha Opp., p. 6:10), or a showing that she “may not survive to trial.”

 

As such, the Court finds an insufficient basis to conclude that the health of the party is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudicing the party’s interest in the litigation. (CCP § 36, subd. (a).)

             

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion for trial preference is denied.

 

 

It is so ordered.

TENTATIVE RULING

 

CS GENERAL CONTRACTING, INC.

 

 

         vs.

 

KURT V. JOSE et al.

 

 Case No.: 25STCV02466  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 14, 2026

           

Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant Kurt Jose to produce documents responsive to its Requests for Production is GRANTED.  Responses due within 20 days.  Defendant is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $700.00.

 

On 1/29/2025, Plaintiff CS General Contracting, Inc. (Plaintiff) filed suit against Kurt V. Jose and Edel M. Jose (collectively, Defendants), alleging: (1) breach of contract; (2) foreclosure of mechanic’s lien; (3) quantum meruit; and (4) account stated.

 

            On 6/6/2025, Kurt and Edel Jose (collectively, Cross-Complainants) filed a cross-complaint (XC) against CS General Contracting, Inc., Timonthy Kaipo Aiu, and Hudson Insurance Company, alleging: (1) breach of written contract; (2) breach of express warranty; (3) breach of implied warranty; (4) negligence; (5) disgorgement; (6) unfair business practices; and (7) recovery on a surety bond.

 

On 8/20/2026, Plaintiff moved to compel Defendant Kurt Jose to produce documents responsive to its Requests for Production (RFPs).

 

            The motion is unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

            On 5/13/2026, Plaintiff served an initial set of RFPs on both Defendants. Defendant Kurt Jose responded to the RFPs, essentially stating he would produce bate-stamped documents responsive to the requests. However, to date, no documents have been produced.

 

            Kurt Jose did not oppose this motion, and thus has not offered any substantial justification for this failure.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant Kurt Jose to produce documents responsive to its Requests for Production is granted. Defendant is sanctioned, jointly and severally with counsel, $700.00 ($350/hr x 2 hr).

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 26STCV02543    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 309

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

CHEOL HONG

 

         vs.

 

SHIN JA KIM, et al.

 

 Case No.: 26STCV02543   

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: September 14, 2026

 

Defendants’ demurrer to the first, third, fourth, ninth, and twelfth causes of action is OVERRULED.

 

Defendants’ motion to strike is DENIED.

 

            On 1/26/2026, Plaintiff Cheol Hong (Plaintiff) filed suit against Shin Ja Kim and Michelle Lee (collectively, Defendants), alleging (1) breach of warranty of habitability; (2) breach of implied warranty of habitability; (3) violation of Civil Code section 1942.4; (4) violation of Civil Code section 1942.5; (5) negligence; (6) nuisance; (7) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (8) breach of contract; (9) breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment; (10) breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment; (11) violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200; and (12) violation of LAMC section 45.33.

 

            On 5/12/2026, Defendants demurred to Plaintiff’s first, third, fourth, ninth, and twelfth causes of action. Defendants also move to strike portions of the Complaint.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants argue that Plaintiff’s first, third, fourth, ninth, and twelfth causes of action are insufficiently pled.

 

            After review, the Court disagrees.

 

Plaintiff’s challenged claims are not subject to a heightened pleading standard. All causes of action are pled with sufficient facts to state a claim and to put Defendants on sufficient notice of the nature of the claims. For example, Plaintiff alleges that the habitability conditions at issue begun “in or around 2019” and have continued. This is sufficient at the pleadings stage.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ demurrer to the first, third, fourth, ninth, and twelfth causes of action is overruled.

 

Motion to Strike

 

            Defendants move to strike Plaintiff’s prayer for punitive damages. Given that Plaintiff has operative claims for habitability issues, Plaintiff has necessarily alleged sufficient facts at the pleading stage which could show “…despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.” (Civ. Code § 3294(c)(1).)

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendants’ motion to strike is denied.

 

It is so ordered.
