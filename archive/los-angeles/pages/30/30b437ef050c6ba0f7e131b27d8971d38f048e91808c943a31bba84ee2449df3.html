Case Number: 24STCV10562    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 529

JAIME RODRIGUEZ VS FCA US LLC

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR PAYMENT OF ATTORNEYS’ FEES AND REIMBURSEMENT OF COSTS AND EXPENSES

           

Date of Hearing:        September 14, 2026             Trial Date:       None set.

Department:              529                                         Case No.:        24STCV10562

 

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Jaime Rodriguez

Responding Party:     Defendant FCA US LLC

 

BACKGROUND

 

On April 26, 2024, Plaintiff Jaime Rodriguez filed a complaint against FCA US LLC for violation of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act. Plaintiff purchased a 2022 Dodge Charger vehicle from Chrysler Jeep Dodge Ram in Indio, California. Plaintiff alleges the subject vehicle suffered from defects including engine malfunctions, and electrical defects.

 

On April 6, 2026, the parties filed a Notice of Settlement of Entire Case.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

Plaintiff Jamie Rodriguez’s Motion for Payment of Attorneys’ Fees and Reimbursement of Costs and Expenses is GRANTED, in the reduced amount.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff Jamie Rodriguez moves this court for an award of attorney fees and costs against Defendant in the amount of $55,810.86, including a multiplier of 0.2.

 

Pursuant to the Song-Beverly Act, Civil Code section 1794(d), “[i]f the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.”

 

Plaintiff seeks $47,173.26 in attorneys’ fees and costs pursuant to Civil Code section 1794(d) plus a 0.2x fee enhancement for a total of $55,810.86. Plaintiff seeks this amount based on 74.7 hours of work at the billing rate of $485/hr in 2025 and $515/hr in 2026 for Christopher Danna, $600/hr in 2025 and $625/hr in 2026 for Aaron Fhima, and $430/hr in 2024 and $485/hr in 2025 for Jansher Rasa.

 

In opposition, Defendant argues Plaintiff’s counsel’s billing practices are abusive of the fee shifting provision of the Song-Beverly Act. Defendant first contends the court should apply the rates from the Real Rate Report, which caps the partner rate at $550/hr and the associate rate at $450.00/hr. Defendant also contends the rate history by counsel raises concerns as there has been an increase in rates of approximately 78.6% in the last six years.

 

The court finds the hourly rates of Plaintiff’s counsel reasonable. The range of rates charged in this matter are appropriate given each attorney’s relative experience and qualifications.

 

Defendant next argues the court should reduce the fee award by subtracting several duplicative and unreasonable entries including Plaintiff’s counsel initial flat fee of $3,900.00, block billing of $1,376.00, $1,067.00 for summarizing deposition transcript, $3,492 for drafting the motion to compel, and the like. (See Opp. p. 6-8.)

 

The court finds some of the fees unreasonable. First, the anticipated 9 hours billed for the opposition, attending the hearing and preparing an opposition to a motion to tax costs as well as the 1 hour spent preparing the memorandum of costs is excessive. The court reduces the reply and attending the hearing to two hours, or $1,030.00. The court also reduces the time spent preparing the memorandum of costs to .5 hours or $257.50. Moreover, Plaintiff spent 3 hours preparing nearly identical 6 subpoenas. The court reduces the amount to $970.00. The court also reduces the 7. 2hours billed for reviewing, preparing and drafting the motion to compel PMK and related work to 4 hours or $1,940.00.

 

The court finds the rest of the fees reasonably incurred. For example, Defendant identifies only one entry of the purported block billing. However, the entry has not “prevent[ed] [the court] from discerning which tasks are compensable and which are not.” (Heritage Pacific Financial, LLC v. Monroy (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 972, 1010.)  Similarly, Defendant does not explain sufficiently how the flat fee of $3,900.00 was unreasonably incurred.

 

Based on the foregoing, $2,294.50 will be deducted from the total, totaling $36,258.50. The total anticipated work brings the total to $37,288.50.

 

As for the multiplier, the court declines to award one. This was a standard lemon law case. Counsel’s skill and contingency risk are reflected in the reasonable hourly rates described above.

 

Memorandum of Costs

 

Plaintiff seeks $3,964.60 in costs, including the following: (1) $581.00 in filing fees and motion fees; (2) $150.00 in jury fees; (3) $2,404.31 in deposition costs; (4) $563.08 in service of process fees and (5) $266.21 in fees for electronic filing or service.

 

Defendant does not oppose the costs.

 

Based on the foregoing, the motion for attorney fees and costs is GRANTED, in the reduced amount of $37,288.50 in attorney fees and $3,964.60 in costs, for a total of $41,253.10.


Case Number: 25STCV24839    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 529

ACI INTERNATIONAL VS MY PILLOW, INC.

 

ACI INTERNATIONAL’S MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO (1) SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES AND (2) DEMAND FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS

 

Date of Hearing:        September 14, 2026                         Trial Date:       N/A

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        25STCV24839

 

Moving Party:            Defendant My Pillow, Inc.

Responding Party:     Plaintiff ACI International

 

BACKGROUND

 

On August 25, 2025, Plaintiff ACI International filed a complaint against My Pillow, Inc. for breach of contract, account stated, goods sold and delivered, open book account, and unjust enrichment. Plaintiff alleges Defendant purchased millions of dollars’ worth of goods from Plaintiff but when it came time to pay, Defendant failed to pay for certain of the goods purchased from Plaintiff. Plaintiff alleges as of August 22, 2025, Defendant owes $15,407,573.84.

 

On October 9, 2025, entry of default was entered against Defendant. Default judgment was entered against Defendant on December 23, 2025.

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

ACI International’s Motion to Compel Responses to (1) Special Interrogatories and (2) Demand for Production of Documents is GRANTED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

ACI International (“Creditor”) moves this court for an order compelling Judgment Debtor My Pillow, Inc. (“Debtor”) to respond, without objection, to Special Interrogatories and Demand for Production of Documents served by Creditor on January 16, 2026. ACI also moves this court to impose sanctions against Debtor in the amount of $2,055.00 in connection with this motion.

 

To aid in enforcement of a money judgment, a judgment creditor may demand that the judgment debtor produce documents for inspection and copying that are in the debtor’s possession, custody, or control. (CCP §708.030.) The judgment creditor may move for an order compelling a response to a request for production of documents if the judgment debtor fails to serve a timely response within 30 days. (CCP §§ 2031.300(b); 2031.260(a).) Furthermore, the judgment debtor who fails to serve a timely response “waives any objection to the demand, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product” unless the court grants it relief upon motion. (CCP §2031.300(a).) 

 

A judgment creditor may also propound written interrogatories to the judgment debtor in the manner provided by Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.010 through 2030.410, requesting information to aid in enforcement of a money judgment. (CCP §708.020.) Likewise, a judgment creditor may move for an order compelling a response to interrogatories if the judgment debtor fails to serve a timely response within 30 days. (CCP §§2030.290(b); 2030.260(a).) Furthermore, the judgment debtor who fails to serve a timely response “waives any right to exercise the option to produce writings under Section 2030.230, as well as any objection to the interrogatories, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product” unless the court grants it relief upon motion. (CCP §2030.290(a).) 

 

Creditor argues on January 16, 2026, Creditor served Special Interrogatories on Debtor, which seek to obtain information to aid in enforcement of the Judgment. (Exh. 1.) On that same day, Creditor served Demand for Production of Documents on Debtor, which seeks to obtain information to aid in enforcement of the Judgment. (Exh. 2.) However, Debtor has not served any responses to the Interrogatories or the Demands.

 

In opposition, Debtor argues Counsel had no actual knowledge of the January 16, 2026 discovery until Plaintiff served the Motion to Compel on June 2, 2026. Plaintiff’s own exhibits show that the January 16, 2026 discovery was served directly on Debtor, while the Motion to Compel was later served on attorneys identified as counsel for Debtor. Debtor contends Counsel did not provide courtesy copies of the discovery, confirm whether counsel had received it, or contact counsel before filing the motion to compel seeking waiver and sanctions. (Mirsky Decl. ¶ 9.) As a result, any nonresponse resulted from mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect arising from counsel’s lack of knowledge and the court should preserve Debtor’s objections, set a reasonable deadline to respond and deny sanctions.

 

Creditor, in reply, notes Debtor has since made a belated, partial document production, but to date, has never served a single verified response to any interrogatory or request. Specifically, Debtor has only served copies of (1) a security agreement; (2) payroll records from 2024 only; and (3) bank statements from portions of 2024 and 2025 at one bank. Creditor contends upon continuing the hearing on this motion, Debtor had an additional two and a half months to produce documents and respond to the interrogatories but failed to do so.

 

The court grants the motion to compel. Despite the initial communication issues, it has been two months since the court continued the hearing. Debtor maintains not all documents have not been produced and no discovery responses have been provided. If by the hearing Debtor cannot confirm that the discovery has been fully produced, the court will follow its tentative ruling.

 

As for sanctions, the court will also grant sanctions in the amount of $2,055.00 if Debtor has not produced the documents requested and the discovery responses by the hearing date. As noted by Debtor, it has been over seven months since Debtor first propounded the discovery.


Case Number: 26STCV07159    Hearing Date: September 14, 2026    Dept: 529

ADE KESTER VS PHH MORTGAGE SERVICES, INCS., ET AL.

 

DEFENDANT’S DEMURRER TO THE COMPLAINT

 

Date of Hearing:        September 14, 2026                         Trial Date:       None set.

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        26STCV07159

 

Moving Party:            Defendants Onity Mortgage Corporation, formerly known as PHH Mortgage Corporation, U.S. Bank National Association as Trustee for Lehman XS Trust, Series 2006-GP3, and Western Progressive, LLC

Responding Party:     Plaintiff Ade Kester  

Meet and Confer:      Yes.

 

BACKGROUND

 

On March 5, 2026, Plaintiff Ade Kester filed a complaint against PHH Mortgage Services, Inc., U.S. Bank National Association as Trustee for Lehman XS Trust, Series 2006-GP3, and Western Progressive, LLC for (1) Wrongful Foreclosure; (2) Violation of California Homeowner Bill of Rights; (3) Negligence; (4) Promissory Estoppel; and (5) Unfair Business Practices (Bus. & Prof. Code §17200).

 

[Tentative] Ruling

 

Defendants Onity Mortgage Corporation, formerly known as PHH Mortgage Corporation, U.S. Bank National Association as Trustee for Lehman XS Trust, Series 2006-GP3, and Western Progressive, LLC’s Demurrer’s to the Complaint is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

Defendants Onity Mortgage Corporation, formerly known as PHH Mortgage Corporation, U.S. Bank National Association as Trustee for Lehman XS Trust, Series 2006-GP3, and Western Progressive, LLC request this court take judicial notice of several Quitclaim Deeds, Deeds of Trust, and other documents recorded with the Los Angeles County Recorder’s Office. (RJN, Exhs. 1 – 43.)

 

The court GRANTS the request for judicial notice as to the existence of these documents.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendants Onity Mortgage Corporation, formerly known as PHH Mortgage Corporation, U.S. Bank National Association as Trustee for Lehman XS Trust, Series 2006-GP3, and Western Progressive, LLC demur to the complaint on the grounds the first, second, third, fourth, and fifth causes of action fail to state facts sufficient to constitute a claim against Defendants.

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context. In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. (CCP §§ 430.30, 430.70.) At the pleading stage, a plaintiff need only allege ultimate facts sufficient to apprise the defendant of the factual basis for the claim against him. (Semole v. Sansoucie (1972) 28 Cal. App. 3d 714, 721.) A “demurrer does not, however, admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law alleged in the pleading, or the construction of instruments pleaded, or facts impossible in law.” (S. Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 732 (internal citations omitted).) 

 

Dual Tracking

 

Defendants first demur to the complaint on the grounds Plaintiff fails to state a dual tracking claim against Defendants.

 

Civil Code section 2923.6 prohibits lenders from initiating foreclosure proceedings while simultaneously reviewing the borrower's application for loan modification. 

 

Defendants argue Plaintiff does not state facts establishing a dual-tracking violation. Here, Plaintiff has alleged Plaintiff accepted and violated not only one, but two such modifications. First, Plaintiff obtained the First Modification and defaulted on that. (RJN, Exhs. 8, 9.) Then, Plaintiff obtained a Second Modification – and defaulted on that loan too. (RJN, Exhs. 23, 24, 27, 31.) Moreover, the complaint fails to allege that he submitted documentation indicating a “material change in financial circumstances” in connection with his loan modification request in October 2025. To obtain a third loan modification, Defendants contend Plaintiff would have had to document a material change in financial circumstances and submit such documentation to Defendants.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues the alleged timing falls within the conduct addressed by Section 2923.6 and Morris v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2022) 78 Cal.App.5th 279, which recognizes that Civil Code section 2923.6 section 2923.6 “is all about timing” and held that failure to properly conclude a modification review and provide the required written disposition and appeal opportunity may constitute a material violation. Plaintiff contends that the complaint sufficiently alleges that PHH represented on several occasions that foreclosure activity was on hold while the application was in review, yet the trustee’s sale occurred shortly thereafter. Moreover, subdivision (g) expressly contemplates successive applications where a material change in financial circumstances has been documented and submitted to the servicer. (Civ. Code, § 2923.6(g).)

 

The court finds Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged a claim for violation of Civil Code section 2923.6. As noted by Defendants, Section 2923.6(c)(3) addresses the situation where a borrower has previously accepted a loan modification but subsequently defaults on or breaches their obligations under that modification. Under this section, foreclosure proceedings may resume when the borrower accepts a written first lien loan modification but then defaults on or otherwise breaches the borrower's obligations under the first lien loan modification. The court notes Plaintiff’s reliance on subdivision (g); however, Plaintiff has not alleged a material change in the borrower's financial circumstances since the date of the previous application.

 

Wrongful Foreclosure

 

Defendants demur to the wrongful foreclosure cause of action on the grounds Plaintiff fails to allege that the foreclosure was illegal, fraudulent or willfully oppressive and Plaintiff did not meet the tender requirement.

 

To maintain a wrongful foreclosure claim, a plaintiff must allege that “(1) the defendants caused an illegal, fraudulent, or willfully oppressive sale of the property pursuant to a power of sale in a mortgage or deed of trust; (2) the plaintiff suffered prejudice or harm; and (3) the plaintiff tendered the amount of the secured indebtedness or was excused from tending.” (Chavez v. Indymac Mortgage Services (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 1052, 1062.) “Recognized exceptions to the tender rule include when: (1) the underlying debt is void, (2) the foreclosure sale or trustee’s deed is void on its face, (3) a counterclaim offsets the amount due, (4) specific circumstances make it inequitable to enforce the debt against the party challenging the sale, or (5) the foreclosure sale has not yet occurred.” (Ibid.) Furthermore, “[t]ender is not required where the foreclosure sale is void, rather than voidable, such as when a plaintiff proves that the entity lacked the authority to foreclose on the property.” (Glaski v. Bank of America (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1079, 1100.)

 

Defendants first argue Plaintiff has not alleged any illegal, fraudulent, or willfully oppressive conduct that would suffice to allow a wrongful foreclosure claim. Defendant contends Plaintiff bases his wrongful foreclosure claim upon only two potential grounds: (1) dual tracking under Section 2923.6, and (2) failure to mail notice of trustee’s sale under Civil Code Section 2924b (“Section 2924b”). As noted above, the court has found Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged a claim for dual tracking under Section 2923.6. As for the failure to mail notice, Defendants argue Plaintiff cannot hide behind any alleged violations of Section 2924b, as the recorded deeds and his own allegations show that he was well aware of Defendants’ imminent foreclosure of the Property. (See Lehner v. United States (9th Cir. 1982) 685 F.2d 1187, 1190.)

 

The court disagrees, in part. Lehner addressed only whether oral notice satisfied federal constitutional minimums, while California Civil Code Section 2924b establishes specific statutory requirements. As noted by the court in Knapp v. Doherty (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 76, 88, “[t]he trustor need not receive actual notice of the trustee's sale so long as notice is provided to the trustor that is in compliance with the statute.” Similarly, Sections 2924 – 2924h does not “require actual receipt by a trustor of a notice of default or notice of sale. They simply mandate certain procedural requirements reasonably calculated to inform those who may be affected by a foreclosure sale and who have requested notice in the statutory manner that a default has occurred and a foreclosure sale is imminent.” (Lupertino v. Carbahal (1973) 35 Cal.App.3d 742, 746–747.) However, as further discussed below, Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged he paid or could have paid the debt to stop the foreclosure.  (See Lona v. Citibank, N.A. (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 89, 104.)

 

Next, Defendants argue Plaintiff did not meet the tender requirements. Defendants contend “as a condition precedent to an action by the borrower to set aside the trustee’s sale on the ground that the sale is voidable because of irregularities in the sale notice or procedure, the borrower must offer to pay the full amount of the debt for which the property was security.” (Lona v. Citibank, N.A. (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 89, 112.) Here, Defendants argue nowhere in the complaint does Plaintiff allege that he offered to pay the full outstanding debt owed to Defendants. This automatically dooms Plaintiff’s wrongful foreclosure claim.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues Defendants ignore the recognized exceptions to tender, which include where tender would be inequitable. Here, requiring full tender where the alleged wrongdoing consisted of foreclosing before completion of a modification process would defeat the purpose of that process. Plaintiff also says if additional facts concerning tender or an applicable exception are required, leave to amend should be granted.

 

The court agrees Plaintiff has not alleged facts sufficient to show exception to tender. Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS the demurrer to the first cause of action.

 

Civil Code sections 2924, et seq.

 

Defendants argue this claim fails for three reasons: (1) it is duplicative of the wrongful foreclosure claim; (2) “mere technical violations of the foreclosure process will not give rise to a tort claim[]” (Miles v. Deutsche Bank National Trust Co. (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 394, 409); and (3) Plaintiff fails to allege he paid or could have paid the debt to stop the foreclosure. (See Lona v. Citibank, N.A. (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 89, 104.)

 

First, the court agrees the cause of action is duplicative of the wrongful foreclosure cause of action. Civil Code section 2924b is not its own independent cause of action in the sense of creating a new legal claim for damages. Moreover, Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged that he was prejudiced or harmed or that he paid or could have paid the debt to stop the foreclosure. (See Lona v. Citibank, N.A. (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 89, 104.)  

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the violation of Civil Code sections 2924, et seq. is SUSTAINED WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Negligence

 

Defendants demur to the negligence cause of action on the grounds HBOR imposes no duty upon Defendants for negligence purposes, Plaintiff cannot prevail on a common law negligence claim under the economic loss rule, and Defendants’ alleged conduct did not proximately cause Plaintiff’s damages.  

 

To bring an actionable negligence claim, the defendant owes a duty to the plaintiff proscribed by statute or common law. (Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905, 920.) Lenders do not owe a duty to borrowers by statute and have no special relationship with a borrower to avoid negligently causing monetary harm. (Id., at p. 938.) 

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues Sheen itself distinguishes theories such as negligent misrepresentation and promissory estoppel from the general negligence theory it rejected. Plaintiff further argues the complaint was meant to allege promissory estoppel and Plaintiff requests leave to amend to conform the pleading to the affirmative representations and reliance already alleged and plead promissory estoppel and/or negligent misrepresentation as appropriate.

 

Defendants, in reply, argue both the promissory estoppel and negligent misrepresentation claim would fail because Defendants never made a “clear and unambiguous promise” not to foreclose in the future.

 

The court finds Plaintiff has not sufficiently alleged a claim for negligence or promissory estoppel or negligent misrepresentation. As admitted by Plaintiff, Sheen bars this claim. (Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905, 915.) The complaint at most alleges Defendants made affirmative representations that the files were under review and the foreclosure was on hold. This is not enough under either theory.

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer to the negligence cause of action is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.  As part of that leave, plaintiff may allege alternative theories of negligent misrepresentation or promissory estoppel.

 

UCL

 

Defendants demur to the UCL claim on the grounds Plaintiff lacks standing as he caused his own injury, Plaintiff cannot seek restitution or injunctive relief from Defendant and Plaintiff has not alleged any unlawful, fraudulent, or unfair conduct.

 

To meet the UCL’s standing requirements, a plaintiff must “(1) establish a loss or deprivation of money or property sufficient to qualify as injury in fact, i.e., economic injury, and (2) show that that economic injury was the result of, i.e., caused by, the unfair business practice”. (Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310, 322.)

 

Defendants argue the recorded Notices of Default show that Plaintiff defaulted on the Deed of Trust nearly 20 years ago – many times – and remains in default today. His defaults – not Defendants’ actions – caused the foreclosure. Moreover, Plaintiff cannot obtain any restitution or injunctive relief against Defendants as Defendants do not currently retain any money or property that Plaintiff could reclaim. Finally, none of Defendants’ alleged conduct was unlawful, fraudulent, or unfair.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues he alleges predicate statutory violations and that despite his defaults, causation still exists as proper completion of the modification process could have allowed the borrower to avoid foreclosure. He alleges Defendants undertook a loss-mitigation review, represented the sale was on hold, and nevertheless completed the sale before that represented review was resolved.

 

The court finds absent an alleged statutory violation, Plaintiff lacks facts showing that Defendants have engaged in any business practice which is unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent. The court notes in cases where systematic UCL violations are alleged, injunctive relief may be available to prevent similar conduct affecting other borrowers, even if restitution for the specific foreclosure is not feasible. The court notes the rule in Graham v. Bank of America, N.A. (2014) 226 Cal.App.4th 594 that UCL standing does not apply where a “foreclosure is the result of default, not the alleged conduct of defendants.” However, California courts have recognized exceptions where the defendant's conduct - such as misrepresentations about loan modifications or dual tracking - independently causes or contributes to the foreclosure loss beyond the mere fact of default. The complaint here fails to sufficiently allege an exception.

 

Based on the foregoing, the demurrer is SUSTAINED WITH LEAVE TO AMEND.
