Case Number: 24STLC00282    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Benjamin Amar’s (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Miguel Romero’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Miguel Romero's Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, are denied without prejudice.

 

Defendant Benjamin Amar’s (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Rocio Zavala’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Rocio Zavala’s Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, set for hearing on 08/04/2026 are advanced to today, and denied without prejudice on the same grounds.

 

Defendant Benjamin Amar’s (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Alejandro Bermudez’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Alejandro Bermudez’s Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, set for hearing on 08/05/2026 are advanced to today, and denied without prejudice on the same grounds.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On December 10, 2025, Defendant Benjamin Amar (“Defendant”) served Form Interrogatories, Set One, Special Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, on Plaintiff Miguel Romero (“Plaintiff Romero”). (Motions, Yeremian Decl., Exh. A.) Despite a meet and confer effort, to date, Plaintiff Romero has not served responses to the discovery. (Id. at ¶¶5-6 and Exhs. B-C.) Defendant filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Miguel Romero’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Miguel Romero's Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, on June 24, 2026. The Motions were served on Plaintiff Romero through Attorney Sergei N. Gladkov, Esq. by electronic mail on the same date. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The discovery requests served on Plaintiff Romero do not comply with Code of Civil Procedure section 94, which allows only 35 written discovery requests in the Limited Civil Court unless otherwise authorized by court order or by stipulation. (Code Civ. Proc., § 94, subd. (a); § 95.) Defendant served Plaintiff Romero with more than 35 form interrogatories alone. (Motions, Yeremian Decl., Exhs. A.) The Court additionally notes that the form interrogatories served on Plaintiff Romero include subparts, which also violates subdivision (a)(1) of Code of Civil Procedure section 94.

 

Based on the foregoing, the instant discovery Motions are denied. The Court will not grant motions to compel responses with respect to discovery propounded in violation of the Code of Civil Procedure.

 

Additionally, Defendant filed combined motions to compel responses with respect to two separate sets of discovery: (1) Form Interrogatories, Set One; and (2) Special Interrogatories, Set One. Filing a request to compel responses to two separate sets of discovery as a single motion creates the following problems. First, it negatively impacts the Court’s calendar by placing more motions on the calendar than slots have been provided by the online reservation system. Second, it results in confusion in the motion papers regarding the arguments and evidence with respect to each request. Finally, combining multiple discovery sets into a single motion allows the moving party to avoid paying the requisite filing fees. Statutorily required filing fees are jurisdictional and “it is mandatory for the court clerks to demand and receive statutorily required filing fees.” (See Duran v. St. Luke’s Hospital (2003) 114 Cal.App.4th 457, 460.)

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant Benjamin Amar’s (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Miguel Romero’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Miguel Romero's Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, are denied without prejudice.

 

Defendant Benjamin Amar’s (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Rocio Zavala’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Rocio Zavala’s Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, set for hearing on 08/04/2026 are advanced to today, and denied without prejudice on the same grounds.

 

Defendant Benjamin Amar’s (1) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Alejandro Bermudez’s Responses to Interrogatories - Form and Special, Set One and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Plaintiff Alejandro Bermudez’s Responses to Demand for Inspection and Production of Documents, Set One and Request for Sanctions, set for hearing on 08/05/2026 are advanced to today, and denied without prejudice on the same grounds.

Case Number: 24STLC01293    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Marella Louise Longwell’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is granted.

 

Trial setting conference is scheduled for 08/17/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiff Ismael Samano (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Marella Louise Longwell (“Defendant”) on February 22, 2024. Trial was set for August 21, 2025. On July 31, 2024, the Court granted Plaintiff’s application for service by publication and Plaintiff filed proof of publication on September 5, 2024. Following Defendant’s failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered Defendant’s default on October 14, 2024.

 

Plaintiff’s request for default judgment was rejected on July 31, 2025 and the matter came for trial on August 21, 2025. When Plaintiff’s counsel failed to appear at trial, the case was dismissed without prejudice pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 581, subdivision (b)(3). (Minute Order, 08/21/25.) On December 11, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal. (Minute Order, 12/11/25.) The Court entered default judgment against Defendant on April 14, 2026.

 

Defendant filed the instant Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment on July 9, 2026. Plaintiff filed an opposition on July 21, 2026, and Defendant replied on July 24, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b), or alternatively on equitable grounds. Under Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b), an application for relief must be made no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the moving party’s or counsel’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) The six-month deadline is jurisdictional and not subject to tolling. (Manson, Iver & York v. Black (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 36, 42.) The instant Motion, however, was filed almost two years after entry of default and, therefore, cannot be granted under the moving statute.

 

To qualify for equitable relief based on extrinsic mistake, which exists when circumstances extrinsic to the litigation have unfairly cost a party a hearing on the merits, the defendant must demonstrate: (1) “a meritorious case”; (2) “a satisfactory excuse for not presenting a defense to the original action”; and (3) “diligence in seeking to set aside the default once the fraud [or mistake] had been discovered.” (Mechling v. Asbestos Defendants (2018) 29 Cal.App.5th 1241, 1245-1246 (citing In re Marriage of Stevenot (1984) 154 Cal.App.3d 1051, 1071).)

 

The Motion is supported by the declaration of defense counsel, who is employed as outside counsel for Defendant’s insurance company, Allstate Insurance Company. (Motion, Vogel Decl., ¶1.) Allstate delayed in referring the case to defense counsel until May 28, 2026, resulting in Defendant’s inadvertent non-response. (Motion, Vogel Decl., ¶¶2, 7; Clerkin Decl., ¶5.) The Motion is also accompanied by a copy of Defendant’s proposed Answer to the Complaint, supporting the meritorious defense element. (Motion, Vogel Decl., Exh. D.) Finally, the Motion demonstrates diligence in seeking to set aside the default. As soon as defense counsel was referred the case on or about May 28, 2026, they communicated with Plaintiff’s counsel and were informed that Plaintiff’s counsel would not stipulate to vacate the default and default judgment. (Id. at ¶6.) Six weeks later, Defendant filed the instant Motion for relief.

Plaintiff argues that Defendant’s reliance on Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal. 4th 975 is misplaced and misleading because the facts are not analogous. In Rappleyea, the Court of Appeals determined that two extrinsic circumstances cost the defendants their hearing on the merits. First, they were misinformed by the clerk’s office regarding the fee to file an answer, resulting in their timely answer being rejected. (Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal. 4th 975, 978.) Second, the plaintiff’s attorney misinformed the self-represented defendants that they could not argue that the default entered was taken against them through inadvertence, mistake, or excusable neglect, despite this being allowed under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). (Id. at 979.) Although the facts of Rappleyea are not analogous to those in this action, they demonstrate how the three elements for equitable relief can be met. Defendant’s citation to Rappleyea, therefore, is neither misplaced, nor misleading. Other than this complaint about the facts of Rappleyea, Plaintiff’s opposition does not address which of the three elements for equitable relief are not satisifed here.

 

Therefore, the Court exercises its power in equity to vacate the entry of default and default judgment.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Marella Louise Longwell’s Motion to Vacate Default and Default Judgment is granted.

 

Trial setting conference is scheduled for 08/17/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 24STLC02506    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Mark Tunne’s Petition to Correct and Vacate Arbitration Award and Defendants Brandon Blake and Blake & Wang, P.A.’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award are continued to 09/14/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/17/2026, Defendants are to file proofs of service of the Notice of Ruling, Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award, and Notice of New Hearing Date in accordance with the statutory requirements.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On April 5, 2024, Plaintiff Mark Tunne (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Brandon Blake and Blake & Wang, P.A. (“Defendants”). The Court granted Defendants’ Motion to Compel Arbitration on September 30, 2024. (Minute Order, 09/30/24.) On April 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Petition to Correct and Vacate Arbitration Award. Defendants filed a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award on April 30, 2026.

 

The Petition to Correct or Vacate came for hearing on July 6, 2026, at which time the Court found that it did not meet the statutory service requirements. (Minute Order, 07/06/26.) The Court found the same to be true of the Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award. (Ibid.) The Court continued the hearing on the Petition to Correct or Vacate, and advanced the hearing on the Petition to Confirm, to August 3, 2026. (Ibid.) Plaintiff was ordered to file proof of service of the Petition to Correct or Vacate, and both parties were ordered to file proofs of service of the Notice of New Hearing Date. (Ibid.) The parties were also warned that failure to comply with the order might result in the Petitions being denied. (Ibid.) Finally, Defendants were ordered to give notice of the ruling continuing the hearing date. (Ibid.)

 

On July 14, 2026, Defendants filed a Notice of Ruling and a Notice of Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award. Plaintiff has filed no additional papers since the hearing.

 

Discussion

 

With respect to the documents filed by Defendants on July 14, 2026, the Notice of Ruling and the Notice of Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award show service upon Plaintiff by only electronic mail. (Notice of Ruling, filed 07/14/26, pp. 7-8; Notice of Petition, filed 07/14/26, p. 5.) A review of the Petition to Confirm filed on April 3, 2026, also shows service upon Plaintiff by only electronic mail. (Petition to Confirm, filed 04/30/26, pp. 16-17.) Plaintiff, as a self-represented litigant, must consent to electronic service as set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6. Specifically,

 

Express consent to electronic service may be given by either of the following:

 

(i)             Serving a notice on all parties and filing the notice with the court.

(ii)           Manifesting affirmative consent through electronic means with the court or the court's electronic filing service provider, and concurrently providing the party’s electronic address with that consent for the purpose of receiving electronic service. The act of electronic filing shall not be construed as express consent.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (c)(3).) The Court finds nothing in the record that would demonstrate Plaintiff’s express consent to electronic service.

 

In light of improper service of the Court’s ruling dated July 6, 2026, it does not appear from the record that Plaintiff had proper notice of the new hearing date. Nor from the record can the Court find that Plaintiff was properly served with the Petition to Confirm.  Therefore, the Court continues the hearing on both Petitions to give Defendants an opportunity to correct service of the Notice of Ruling from July 6, 2026 and the Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award upon Plaintiff, and give Plaintiff proper notice of the new hearing date.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Mark Tunne’s Petition to Correct and Vacate Arbitration Award and Defendants Brandon Blake and Blake & Wang, P.A.’s Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award are continued to 09/14/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/17/2026, Defendants are to file proofs of service of the Notice of Ruling, Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award, and Notice of New Hearing Date in accordance with the statutory requirements.

 

 

Defendants to give notice.


Case Number: 24STLC03866    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff The Hartford Underwriters Insurance Company’s Motion for Order to Correct Clerical Error in Judgment and Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc is continued to 09/14/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/17/2026, Plaintiff is to file proof of service of the Motion and Notice of New Hearing Date in accordance with the statutory requirements. If Plaintiff files such proof of service in conformance with this order, the Motion will be granted and the judgment corrected on the date set for the continued hearing.

 

Analysis:

 

On May 29, 2024, Plaintiff The Hartford Underwriters Insurance Company filed the instant action against Defendant AW Collision of Monrovia, a California Corporation (“Defendant”). The Court entered default judgment on December 17, 2024. However, the default judgment does not include the parties’ names at paragraph 5 of the Judicial Council form JUD-100. (Judgment, 12/17/24, ¶5.)  

 

On April 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Order to Correct Clerical Error in Judgment and Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc. The Motion was served on Defendant by electronic mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Initially, the Court addresses the fact that Plaintiff served the Motion on Defendant by electronic mail. (Motion, pp. 5-7.) Not only does the proof of service fail to indicate the electronic service address to which the Motion was sent, but also, unrepresented parties such as Defendant must affirmatively consent to electronic service. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.251(c)(3)(B).) Service of the Motion upon Defendant by electronic mail, therefore, does not meet the statutory requirements for notice. Failure to give notice of the papers and hearing date is a violation of due process and of the statutory notice requirements. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1005; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 757.)

 

On the merits, Plaintiff moves pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) to amend the judgment entered on December 17, 2024 by adding the parties’ names at paragraph 5. The statute provides that “[t]he court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed . . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (d).) The failure to include the parties’ names at paragraph 5 is a clear clerical error that may be corrected under the moving statute. If Plaintiff files a proof of service that demonstrates service of the Motion papers and notice of new hearing date on Defendant in accordance with the statutory requirements, the judgment will be corrected on the date set for the continued hearing, as requested.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff The Hartford Underwriters Insurance Company’s Motion for Order to Correct Clerical Error in Judgment and Amend Judgment Nunc Pro Tunc is continued to 09/14/2026 at 10:00 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse. By 08/17/2026, Plaintiff is to file proof of service of the Motion and Notice of New Hearing Date in accordance with the statutory requirements. If Plaintiff files such proof of service in conformance with this order, the Motion will be granted and the judgment corrected on the date set for the continued hearing.

Case Number: 25STLC02120    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Antonia Flores’ Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions Admitted, or in the Alternative, to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions, Set One, is denied.

 

Defendant Villegas Properties, LLC’s request for sanctions is granted in the amount of $950.00 to be paid by Plaintiff to defense counsel within 20 days’ notice of this order.

 

Plaintiff must appear through counsel until attorney Imperiale’s appearance is terminated.

 

Analysis:

 

On March 18, 2025, Plaintiff Antonia Flores (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Villegas Properties, LLC (“Defendant”). Defendant filed a Demurrer to the Complaint, which the Court overruled on September 8, 2025. (Minute Order, 09/08/25.) Defendant filed its Answer to the Complaint on September 19, 2025.

 

Plaintiff filed a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, which the Court denied on December 17, 2025. (Minute Order, 12/17/25.) On the same day, Plaintiff filed a Notice of Limited Scope Representation indicating that she would be represented by Attorney James T. Imperiale, Esq. at the hearing.

 

Plaintiff filed a Motion to Compel Responses to Plaintiff’s Special Interrogatories, Set One; Requests For Production, Set One; and Requests For Admission, Set One; or in the alternative to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted; Request for Monetary Sanctions, on April 6, 2026. At the hearing on June 4, 2026, the Court heard argument from both parties and then placed the Motion off calendar. (Minute Order, 06/04/26.) The Court ordered Defendant “to respond to Plaintiff's Special Interrogatories, Set One; Requests or Production, Set One; and Requests for Admission, Set One no later than 06/25/2026 without objecting on the basis that the discovery was served on Defendant Villegas Properties, LLC by Plaintiff Antonia Flores in pro per.” (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions Admitted, or in the Alternative, to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions, Set One, on July 2, 2026. Defendant filed an opposition on July 13, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff served Defendant with the Requests for Admission, Set One, on March 10, 2026. (Motion, Flores Decl., ¶2 and Exh. A.) Following the Court’s June 4, 2026 order, Defendant served a “Response to Request for Admissions, or in the Alternative to Deem Requests for Admissions, Admitted, Set One” on Plaintiff on June 13, 2026. (Id. at ¶4 and Exh. B.) Plaintiff received the verification on June 19, 2026, which incorrectly referred to “plaintiff Villegas Properties, L.L.C.” and was signed by Carlos Villegas as “Agent for Plaintiff,” when in fact Villegas Properties LLC is the defendant in this case. (Id. at ¶5 and Exh. C.) Defendant does not dispute that the verification is incorrect and is prepared to serve a corrected verification. (Opp., Ware Decl., Exh. A.)

 

Plaintiff first argues that the responses were untimely, which is incorrect because they were served within the time specified in the Court’s June 4, 2026 minute order. 

 

Plaintiff also argues that the responses are not in substantial compliance with governing statutes because of the reference to Defendant as “plaintiff.”  This is a clerical error that does not, as Plaintiff urges, renders Defendant’s response to the Requests for Admission, Set One, “fatally defective”.  The verification identifies the person who signed it and although it refers to “plaintiff” makes clear that the verification applies to Villegas Properties LLC.  Defendant attaches a corrected verification to its opposition.  (Opp., Ware Decl., ¶6, Exh. A.) 

 

Plaintiff asks in the alternative for the Court to order Defendant to serve further responses.  The request to compel further responses is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.290, which requires an accompanying meet and confer letter. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.290, subd. (b)(1).) Also, a motion to compel further responses must include a separate statement, as required by California Rules of Court Rule 3.1345(a). The Motion includes neither a meet and confer declaration, nor a separate statement. Accordingly, the request for an order compelling Defendant to serve further responses is denied.

 

Defendant moves for an award of sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.280, which mandates sanctions when a party brings an unsuccessful motion without substantial justification. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (d).) Plaintiff has not filed a reply explaining why the request to compel further responses is substantially justified despite the failure to comply with mandatory requirements outlined above. Therefore, Defendant is awarded sanctions of $950.00. (Opp., Ware Decl., ¶¶8-9.)  

 

Finally, the Court addresses Defendant’s argument that Plaintiff is represented by counsel based on the Notice of Limited Scope Representation filed June 4, 2026.  That Notice specified that attorney James Thomas Imperiale would represent Plaintiff only at the hearing on June 4, 2026.  By signing the Notice, Plaintiff agreed to sign a Substitution of Attorney-Civil (form MC-050) at the completion of the specified limited scope representation.  Neither Plaintiff nor attorney Imperiale has filed a form MC-050 reflecting that the limited scope representation is complete, nor has attorney Imperiale filed an Application to Be Relieved as Attorney on Completion of Limited Scope Representation (form CIV-151).  Accordingly, attorney Imperiale remains counsel of record.  Plaintiff must appear through counsel until attorney Imperiale’s appearance is terminated through either of the methods identified above, or through an application pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, § 284(2). 

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Antonia Flores’ Motion to Deem Requests for Admissions Admitted, or in the Alternative, to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions, Set One, is denied.

 

Defendant Villegas Properties, LLC’s request for sanctions is granted in the amount of $950.00 to be paid by Plaintiff to defense counsel within 20 days’ notice of this order.

 

Plaintiff must appear through counsel until attorney Imperiale’s appearance is terminated.

Case Number: 25STLC05604    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff US Alliance Federal Credit Union’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is Granted.

 

The proposed judgment filed on 05/01/2026 is signed and entered this date.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On July 22, 2025, Plaintiff US Alliance Federal Credit Union (“Plaintiff”) filed the Complaint in this action against Defendant Norman Byers (“Defendant”). Defendant filed an Answer to the Complaint, in propria persona, on August 7, 2025. On April 16, 2026, Defendant filed a Substitution of Attorney changing his legal representation to Attorney John Finley, Esq.

 

On May 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings. The Motion was served on Defendant by first-class mail to Attorney John Finley, Esq. on April 29, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for judgment on the pleadings, the standard for which is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer. That is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.  (Bezirdjian v. O’Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322, citing Schabarum v. California Legislature (1998) 60 Cal.App.4th 1205, 1216.) Matters which are subject to mandatory judicial notice may be treated as part of the complaint and may be considered without notice to the parties. Matters which are subject to permissive judicial notice must be specified in the notice of motion, the supporting points and authorities, or as the court otherwise permits. (Id.)  The motion may not be supported by extrinsic evidence. (Barker v. Hull (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 221, 236.)

 

A statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 438, et seq. must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., § 439 [moving party must file declaration demonstrating an attempt to meet and confer in person or by telephone, at least five days before the date a motion for judgment on the pleadings is filed].)

The Motion is supported by a meet and confer declaration. (Motion, D’Anna Decl., Exh. 1.)

 

Substantively, the Motion is brought on the grounds that the Answer fails to allege sufficient facts to raise any defense to the Complaint for breach of contract. The elements of a cause of action for breach of contract are (1) the existence of contract; (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance; (3) defendant’s breach (or anticipatory breach); and (4) resulting damage. (Wall Street Network, Ltd. v. N. Y. Times Co. (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1171, 1178.)

The Complaint alleges that Defendant breached a loan agreement entered into with Plaintiff’s assignor, thereby causing Plaintiff to incur damages of $27,359.02, plus costs to which it has a contractual right under paragraph 5c of the loan agreement. (Compl., ¶¶1, 10-22 and Exhs. A-C.) The Answer admits the material allegations of the Complaint, and only alleges facts regarding Defendant’s financial hardship. (Answer, ¶3b.) The Answer does not deny the material allegations in the Complaint that support the cause of action for breach of contract. Based on Defendant’s admission of fault in the Answer and the lack of any opposition to the instant Motion, the Court finds that Plaintiff is entitled to judgment on the pleadings.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff US Alliance Federal Credit Union’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is Granted.

 

The proposed judgment filed on 05/01/2026 is signed and entered this date.

Case Number: 26STLC00871    Hearing Date: August 3, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Yaro Bezgubenko’s Motion to Set Aside Default is denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On February 9, 2026, Plaintiff Capital Resource International, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action against Defendants Power Net Electric, Inc. (“Defendant Power Net”) and Yaro Bezgubenko (“Defendant Bezgubenko”). Following Defendants’ failure to file a responsive pleading, the Court entered their default on April 22, 2026 and default judgment on June 9, 2026.

 

Defendant Bezgubenko filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Default on June 12, 2026. At the initial hearing on July 7, 2026, the Court noted that the Motion did not include a proof of service showing it was served on Plaintiff, or that Plaintiff was given notice of the hearing date. The Court continued the hearing to give Defendant an opportunity to file proof of service of the papers and notice of hearing date by July 10, 2026. (Minute Order, 07/07/26.)

 

On July 7, 2026, Defendant filed a document that perhaps attempts to show proof of service, but fails to do so. The document is titled “Document name (Proof of delivery”) and attaches a pickup and delivery list from a company called “LA Messenger.” However, it cannot be determined from the list what documents were delivered, how they were delivered, or by whom, and it is not attested to under penalty of perjury. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 1013a, 1013b.) Therefore, the Court cannot find that the Defendant provided Plaintiff with notice of the instant Motion and new hearing date in a manner that complies with the statutory requirements.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Yaro Bezgubenko’s Motion to Set Aside Default is denied.
