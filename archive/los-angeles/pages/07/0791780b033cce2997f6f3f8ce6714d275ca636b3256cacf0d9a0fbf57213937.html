DEPARTMENT N LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 19SMCV00334    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Sand and Sea Inc. (“Plaintiff”) Motion Request to for Undertaking Requiring AquaRecycle, LLC to File an Undertaking is GRANTED.

Appellant AquaRecycle, LLC is ordered to post an undertaking of $153,385.50 if it uses an admitted surety insurer, or $204,514.00 if it does not, as a condition of any stay of an enforcement. 
Plaintiff Sand and Sea Inc. to give notice.

  
REASONING

Plaintiff moves for an order requiring Appellant to file an undertaking in at least the amount of $348,000 as a condition of any stay of enforcement pending its appeal from this Court’s order amending the judgment herein to add AquaRecycle, LLC as a judgment debtor. Plaintiff argues that Appellant should be required to post an undertaking because otherwise, Plaintiff will be prejudiced by continued delay in collection, loss of use of the judgment funds, accrual of interest, and the risk that assets will be placed beyond reach during the appeal.

“Unless an undertaking is given, the perfecting of an appeal shall not stay enforcement of the judgment or order in the trial court if the judgment or order is for . . . [m]oney or the payment of money, whether consisting of a special fund or not, and whether payable by the appellant or another party to the action.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 917.1, subd. (a)(1).) Here, the Order added Appellant as a judgment debtor and thus, one that makes Appellant liable for the payment of money payable by Appellant. 

Furthermore, in People v. American Surety Co. (2019) 31 Cal.App.5th 380 the court explained that “a surety who appeals only from a postjudgment order denying its motion to vacate the summary judgment and to exonerate the bail bond may not obtain a stay of enforcement of the underlying summary judgment by filing an undertaking pursuant to section 917.1. In relevant part, section 917.1 provides: “Unless an undertaking is given, the perfecting of an appeal shall not stay enforcement of the judgment or order in the trial court if the judgment or order is for any of the following: [¶] (1) Money or the payment of money, whether consisting of a special fund or not, and whether payable by the appellant or another party to the action.” (§ 917.1, subd. (a)(1), italics added.) An order denying a motion to vacate a money judgment is a ‘negative order[ ] denying relief to defendant,’ and not an order directing the ‘defendant pay money to plaintiff.’” (Id. at pp. 391–392.) The opposite situation is presented here. The Order was not a negative order that denied relief to Appellant but one that held that Appellant was a judgment debtor. The effect of the Order is that the money judgment has been entered against Appellant as a judgment debtor. (Code Civ. Proc., § 680.250 [“‘Judgment debtor’ means the person against whom a judgment is rendered”]; Code Civ. Proc., § 680.270 [“‘Money judgment’ means that part of a judgment that requires the payment of money”; Highland Springs Conference & Training Center v. City of Banning (2019) 42 Cal.App.5th 416, 426 [“a section 187 motion, if granted, merely allows the judgment creditor to enforce the now-amended judgment against the additional judgment debtor”].)

Even if the Order is not one for “money or the payment of money,” Code of Civil Procedure section 917.9 also supports an undertaking. “The perfecting of an appeal shall not stay enforcement of the judgment or order in cases not provided for in Sections 917.1 to 917.8, inclusive, if the trial court, in its discretion, requires an undertaking and the undertaking is not given, in any of the following cases . . . [a]ppellant is required to perform an act for respondent's benefit pursuant to judgment or order under appeal.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 917.9, (a)(2).) The Order found that Appellant was a judgment debtor and as such is liable for the judgment amount owed to Plaintiff. Appellant is required to pay Plaintiff the judgment amount, which is an act for Plaintiff’s benefit pursuant to the Order. Thus, an undertaking is warranted as a condition for a stay to apply.

Following Code of Civil Procedure section 917.1, “[t]he undertaking shall be for double the amount of the judgment or order unless given by an admitted surety insurer in which event it shall be for one and one-half times the amount of the judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 917.1, subd. (b).) The judgment awarded was for $102,257.00. (October 12, 2021 Judgment.) If Appellant uses an admitted surety insurer, the undertaking is ordered in the amount of. $153,385.50. If Appellant does not use an admitted surety insurer, the undertaking is ordered in the amount of $204,514.00.

Accordingly, plaintiff Sand and Sea Inc.’s Motion for Order Requiring AquaRecycle, LLC to file an undertaking as a condition of any stay of enforcement pending appeal is GRANTED. Appellant AquaRecycle, LLC is ordered to post an undertaking of $153,385.50 if it uses an admitted surety insurer, or $204,514.00 if it does not, as a condition of any stay of an enforcement. 




Case Number: 25SMCV02375    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiffs Divrt, Inc. n/k/a Divert, LLC and Amit Rohatgi’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings is DENIED.

Plaintiffs Divrt, Inc. n/k/a Divert, LLC and Amit Rohatgi to give notice.

REASONING

Plaintiffs move for judgment on the pleadings as to the third and fourth causes of action for declaratory relief in their FAC on the ground that the plain language of the Asset Purchase Agreement (“APA”)  at issue unequivocally mandates that the Court determine and declare that (1) Plaintiffs’ March 18, 2025 Notice of Claim complied with the terms of section 8.4(a) of the APA, and thus Defendants were required under section 8.4(c) to provide Plaintiffs with written notice contesting the validity of the Noice of Claim within 20 days; (2) Defendants’ response to Plaintiffs’ March 18, 2025 Notice of Claim was neither timely nor substantively compliant with the requirements of 8.4(c) and as such Defendants have waived any further right to contest the validity of said Notice of Claim; (3) as of April 9, 2025, Defendants have been obligated to pay Plaintiffs the remaining $2.8 million in Earn-Out finds, plus prejudgment interest at Delaware’s statutory rate; and (4) by obtaining declaratory relief from the Court in Plaintiffs’ favor on the third cause of action for declaratory relief, Plaintiffs shall be entitled to the full modicum of available indemnification under the APA from Defendants for “Losses” as defined in section 8.1 of the APA and owed to Plaintiffs under section 8.2 of the APA including reasonable attorney’s fees incurred in connection with the March 18, 2025 Notice of Claim and this action.

Governing Law
The APA provides that “[t]his Agreement and any dispute arising in connection therewith will be governed by and construed under the law of the State of Delaware without regard to conflicts-of-laws principles that would require the application of any other law.” (FAC, Ex. 1 – APA § 12.11.)

“[I]f the proponent of the clause demonstrates that the chosen state has a substantial relationship to the parties or their transaction, or that a reasonable basis otherwise exists for the choice of law, the parties' choice generally will be enforced unless the other side can establish both that the chosen law is contrary to a fundamental policy of California and that California has a materially greater interest in the determination of the particular issue.” (Maxim Crane Works, L.P. v. Tilbury Constructors (2012) 208 Cal.App.4th 286, 292 [internal quotations omitted].) 

Here, Plaintiffs raise the applicability of Delaware law, but do not put forth any arguments that show Delaware has a substantial relationship to the parties or their transaction or that a reasonable basis otherwise exists for the choice of law. However, Defendants do not dispute, and in fact agree that the Delaware law applies to the interpretation of the substantive provisions of the APA. (Opposition, p. 10:24-25.) Thus, substantive Delaware law applies.

Though Delaware law substantively applies, California law provides procedurally. (World Wide Imports, Inc. v. Bartel (1983) 145 Cal.App.3d 1006, 1012 [“It is well established that while the courts generally enforce the substantive rights created by the laws of other jurisdictions, the procedural matters are governed by the law of the forum”].) 

Defendant’s Verified Answer to the Original Complaint

Both Plaintiffs and Defendants attempt to rely on Defendants’ verified answer to the verified complaint. However, the verified complaint is no longer the pleading before the court and thus, the verified answer to the original complaint is no longer in effect. (Darsie v. Darsie (1942) 49 Cal.App.2d 491, 493–494 [“The law is established in California that an amended complaint supersedes the original complaint and thereafter the original complaint performs no function as a pleading”].) Thus, the controlling answer is the unverified answer to the unverified FAC.

Third Cause of Action – Declaratory Relief - Waiver

Plaintiffs argue that Plaintiffs’ March 18, 2025 Notice of Claim complied with the terms of section 8.4(a) of the APA, and thus Defendants were required under section 8.4(c) to provide Plaintiffs with written notice contesting the validity of the Noice of Claim within 20 days.

Plaintiffs allege that on March 18, 2025, Plaintiffs formally transmitted a Notice of Claim to Defendants pursuant to Sections 8.4(c) and 12.2 of the APA asserting that the fourth and fifth Earn-Out payments were and are due and owing based upon their evidence-based belief that a proper accounting of successfully enabled DIVRT Stack locations consistent with the APA as of October 31, 2024, would have necessarily confirmed satisfaction of both the 2,000 and 2,500-location Milestones, thereby entitling Plaintiffs to immediate payment of the $2.8 million in Earn-Out funds as well as reimbursement for Plaintiffs’ attorneys’ fees incurred as a result of Defendants’ non-compliance with the APA’s reporting and payment provisions, and interest on the $2.8 million principal. (FAC, ¶¶ 102-103; Ex. 4.)

Under Section 8.4, “[u]pon obtaining knowledge of any Loss, a Person seeking to be indemnified under Section 8.1 or Section 8.2 (an “Indemnified Party”) shall promptly give a Notice of Claim to an indemnifying party under Section 8.1 or Section 8.2 (the “Indemnifying Party”) of any Loss in respect of which such Indemnifying Party has a duty to indemnify such Indemnified Party pursuant to Section 8.1 or Section 8.2 (a “Claim”), specifying in reasonable detail, to the extent known, the nature of the Loss for which indemnification is sought and, if practicable, the estimated amount of such Claim, except that any delay or failure to so notify the Indemnifying Party shall only relieve the Indemnifying Party of its obligations hereunder to the extent, if at all, that it is materially prejudiced by reason of such delay or failure.” (FAC, Ex. 1 – APA § 8.4(a) p. 39.) 

Defendants argue that Section 8.4 does not apply because Plaintiffs have not suffered a Loss. “Loss” is defined as “any loss, Liability, Tax, damage, demand, claim, cost, suit, action or cause of action, judgment, award, assessment, interest, penalty or expense (including expense of investigations and fees and disbursements of counsel and other professionals) (each a “Loss” and collectively, the “Losses”) suffered or incurred by any Purchaser Indemnitee arising out of, resulting from or relating to (i) any inaccuracy or breach of any representation or warranty of the Stockholder or Seller contained in this Agreement or the Ancillary Documents, (ii) the breach by the Stockholder or Seller of any covenant, undertaking or other agreement of the Stockholder or Seller contained in this Agreement or the Ancillary Documents, (iii) any inaccuracy in any certificate, instrument or other document delivered by Seller or the Stockholder as required by this Agreement or the Ancillary Documents, (iv) the Excluded Assets or the Excluded Liabilities or (v) the matters set forth on Section 8.1 of the Seller Disclosure Schedule, if any.” (FAC, Ex. 1 – APA Annex “Loss”, § 8.1 [emphasis added].) 

The Court finds that on the face of the FAC, Plaintiffs have not shown that they suffered or incurred a loss because the loss claimed by Plaintiffs is the fourth and fifth Earn-Out payments and as shown by the FAC, whether Plaintiffs are owed the fourth and fifth Earn-Out payments is in dispute. (FAC, ¶¶ 85, 90, 94.) Furthermore, Defendants, in their unverified answer to the unverified FAC, “deny, generally and specifically, each allegation contained in the FAC, the whole thereof, and each and every alleged cause of action contained therein. . . .” (Answer, p. 2:7-11.) The court in Delaware has explained that “[g]enerally, the matter on which the claim for indemnification is premised may be said to have been resolved with certainty only when the underlying investigation or litigation is definitively resolved.” (LaPoint v. AmerisourceBergen Corp. (Del. 2009) 970 A.2d 185, 198.) Thus, because whether Plaintiffs are owed the fourth and fifth Earn-Out payments is in dispute, whether Plaintiffs incurred a loss is in dispute, and as such whether the Notice of Claim under Section 8.4(c) is applicable is in dispute.

Furthermore, even if Section 8.4(c) is applicable, the Court finds that Plaintiffs have failed to show that Defendants waived their right to contest the validity of Plaintiffs’ claim for the remaining $2.8 million in Earn-Out funds because Plaintiffs have not shown that Defendants did not respond to the Notice of Claim. In the April 9, 2025 email, Defendants’ counsel states that “there has been a ‘response’ in multiple respects – I did respond to your March 18 letter, and more fundamentally, your client’s demand for the fourth and fifth earn-out payments is clearly in dispute as it is not warranted by the location count provided to your client in February (which I understand [your] client in turn disputes).” (FAC, Ex. 5.) The APA does not provide that the Indemnifying Party must contest the Claim within 20 days. Rather it only provides that the Indemnifying Party has “twenty (20) days from receipt of the Notice of Claim from the Indemnified Party of such Claim within which to respond thereto.” (FAC, Ex. 1 – APA, § 8.4(c).) Thus, on the face of the FAC, Plaintiffs have not shown that Defendants did not timely respond to the Notice of Claim.

Accordingly, the Court denies the motion for judgment on the pleadings as to the third cause of action.

Fourth Cause of Action – Declaratory Relief – Attorney’s Fees

Because Plaintiffs have failed to show that Defendants did not timely respond to the Notice of Claim and thus Defendants’ waived their right to contest the validity of the Notice, Plaintiffs have not obtained declaratory relief from the Court in Plaintiffs’ favor on the third cause of action for declaratory relief. Thus, Plaintiffs have not shown that they are entitled to the full modicum of available indemnification under the APA from Defendants for “Losses” as defined in section 8.1 of the APA and owed to Plaintiffs under section 8.2 of the APA including reasonable attorney’s fees incurred in connection with the March 18, 2025 Notice of Claim and this action.

Accordingly, the Court denies the motion for judgment on the pleadings as to he fourth cause of action.
