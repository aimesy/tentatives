Case Number: 23NWCV03743    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: P

CRAIG MARTIN v. CITY OF LA HABRA HEIGHTS

CASE NO.: 23NWCV03743 

HEARING: 09/18/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#10

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Plaintiff’s motion for leave to amend is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ORDERED to file the proposed FAC attached to the moving papers within 5 days of this Order.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Craig Martin, as Trustee of the Craig & Vetta Martin Family Trust (Plaintiff) moves for leave to file a first amended complaint (FAC).

 

Background

 

On November 20, 2023, Plaintiff filed this nuisance action against City of La Habra Heights (City) and Does 1 through 20. Plaintiff and his family occupy the residential premises located at 1212 Kashlan Road, in the City of La Habra Heights (Property) and owned by the Craig & Vetta Martin Family Trust (Trust). (Complaint, ¶¶ 1, 7.) This action arises out of a drainage pipe, approximately 36 inches in diameter (Drainage Pipe) which runs beneath the Property and is currently corroding, in need of repair, and causing sink holds in the backyard of the Property. (Complaint, ¶¶ 8-11.) The complaint alleges that Plaintiff has made multiple attempts to contact the City which has disclaimed responsibility.

(Complaint, ¶ 13.) The complaint alleges that a title report shows that the Drainage Pipe is the City’s responsibility and that there is no easement permitting the Drainage Pipe to be located under the Property. (Complaint, ¶¶ 15-18.)

 

The complaint asserts four causes of action: (1) nuisance, (2) trespass, (3) enforcement of easement rights, and (4) declaratory relief.

 

Legal Standard

 

“The court may…in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473 subd. (a).)

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a) provides: “A motion to amend a pleading before trial must:

 

(1) Include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments;

(2) State what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and

(3) State what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.”

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b) requires a separate declaration accompany the motion. The separate declaration must include: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

 

“If the motion to amend is timely made and the granting of the motion will not prejudice the opposing party, it is error to refuse permission to amend and where the refusal also results in a party being deprived of the right to assert a meritorious cause of action or a meritorious defense, it is not only error but an abuse of discretion.” (Morgan v. Superior Court of California (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530.)

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for leave to file a FAC. Plaintiff seeks to update allegations regarding progressive damages, pleads compliance with the Government Claims Act, and adds new legal theories.

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a), Plaintiff explains the allegations to be added and attaches a clean and red-lined copy of the proposed FAC. (Castillo Decl., ¶ 13, Exhs. A, B.) The proposed FAC adds causes of action for dangerous condition of public property and negligence. (Castillo Decl., ¶ 9.)

 

In compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b), Plaintiff explains that “[s]ince the original complaint was filed, the alleged damage has continued and expanded. Plaintiff reports that the sinkhole has destroyed substantial portions of the backyard, damaged the swimming pool, impaired the use and enjoyment of the property, created a hazard for his minor children, and diminished the property’s value.” (Castillo Decl., ¶ 4.) Plaintiff also explains that on June 3, 2026, Plaintiff presented a written claim to the City under Government Code sections 910, et seq. describing the municipal drainage system, sinkhole, and alleged dangerous condition. (Castillo Decl., ¶¶ 6-7, Exh. C.) Plaintiff received a rejection on July 16, 2026. (Castillo Decl., ¶ 8, Exh. D.) Plaintiff explains that “[l]eave to amend was not requested earlier because Plaintiff and counsel continued investigating and documenting the progressive damage, including Defendant City of La Habra Heights expert report on the storm drain obtained on June 25, 2026.” (Castillo Decl., ¶ 11.)

 

In opposition, City argues that Plaintiff’s motion is untimely. City argues that it is prejudiced because Plaintiff seeks to add claims three years after filing the initial complaint which will lead to additional discovery and preparation costs.

 

In reply, Plaintiff argues that Plaintiff does not show actual prejudice since additional discovery and costs of preparation are not sufficient.

 

The Court agrees that the timing does not warrant denial of leave to amend. As shown by Plaintiff, the claims arise out of the same general facts and continuing damage after the initial complaint.

 

City further argues that the proposed claims are futile because the claims are barred under the applicable statute of limitations and Plaintiff failed to present a timely claim under the Government Code.

 

City’s challenges to the claims presentations requirements and Plaintiff’s responsive arguments reflect that the arguments require more extensive analysis and the viability of the new claims should be reserved for a demurrer or motion for judgment on the pleadings. (See Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1048; Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2003) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 760 [“[T]he better course of action would have been to allow [plaintiff] to amend the complaint and then let the parties test its legal sufficiency in other appropriate proceedings.”].)

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to amend is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ORDERED to file the proposed FAC attached to the moving papers within 5 days of this Order.


Case Number: 24NWCV01429    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: P

ANA ACEVEDO, ET AL. v. EFREN LOPEZ FARFAN, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 24NWCV01429 

HEARING:  09/18/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

Petitioner Ana Acevedo’s petitions to approve compromises of claims for minor claimants Dolores Acevedo, Andrew Acevedo, Ezekiel Acevedo, Ana Maria Acevedo, and Abraham Acevedo are GRANTED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

There are five Petitions on calendar: Petitioner Ana Acevedo (Petitioner) petitions to approve compromises of claims for minor claimants (1) Dolores Acevedo, (2) Andrew Acevedo, (3) Ezekiel Acevedo, (4) Ana Maria Acevedo, and (5) Abraham Acevedo (collectively, Claimants).

 

Background

 

On May 6, 2024, Plaintiffs Ana Acevedo; Isaiah Acevedo; Dolores Acevedo, Andrew Acevedo, Ezekiel Acevedo, Ana Maria Acevedo, Abraham Acevedo, and Israel Acevedo, minors by and through their Guardian ad Litem, Ana Acevedo (collectively, Plaintiffs) filed this habitability action against Defendants Efren Lopez Farfan, Suilma Lopez Farfan (Defendants), and Does 1 through 50 regarding their former tenancy at 9108 Bluford Avenue, Whittier CA 90602 (Property). The complaint alleges that the Property was untenantable as a result of failure to repair conditions, including on May 6, 2022, after a fire caused by a defective electric system caused the Property to become completely uninhabitable and forced Plaintiffs to move out of the Property. (Complaint, ¶¶ 10-12.) The complaint asserts four causes of action: (1) failure to provide habitable dwelling, (2) breach of covenant and right to quiet enjoyment and possession of the property, (3) nuisance, and (4) negligence.

 

On August 4, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a Notice of Settlement indicating the parties entered into a conditional settlement agreement.

 

On August 18, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiffs’ ex parte application to specially set the instant Petitions to today’s date to be heard concurrently.

 

Legal Standard

 

A Petition for approval of compromise of claim or action or disposition of proceeds of judgment for minor or person with a disability must conform with Code of Civil Procedure section 372 and Probate Code sections 2504 and 3600 et seq. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Such a petition “must be verified by the petitioner and must contain a full disclosure of all information that has any bearing upon the reasonableness of the compromise.” (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 7.950.) Moving papers should include the Petition of Guardian Ad Litem for Compromise of Disputed Claim of Minor or Person with Disability (Petition) on Form MC-350 and an Order Approving Compromise of Minor’s Claim (Order) Form MC-351.

 

Discussion

 

Petitioner files this Petition on behalf of Claimants Dolores, a minor, age 12; Ana Maria, a minor, age 13; Ezekiel, a minor, age 14; Abraham, a minor, age 14; and Andrew, a minor, age 15. Petitioner provides Petitions on Form MC-350 and Proposed Orders on Form MC-351. These Petitions are unopposed.

 

Based on the similarities between the Petitions, the Court will review them jointly in this ruling and note any relevant differences.

 

          Allocation

 

Defendants will pay $102,000 to Plaintiffs in exchange for release of all claims arising out of the tenancy that Plaintiffs assert against them. (Pet., ¶ 10; Attach.,10c.) Each minor Claimant will be paid $3,333.33. (Pet., ¶ 10.) Of the total settlement proceeds, Defendants are paying $62,000.02 to Petitioner and $20,000 to Isaiah because of their special damages. (Pet., ¶ 11; Attach, 11b(6).) Defendants are paying $3,333.33 to Israel who was a minor at the time of the subject incident and turned 18 in 2025. (Pet., ¶ 11; Attach, 11b(6).)

 

Each claimant will receive $2,000 in net recovery. (Pet., ¶ 16.) While this amount is low, the Court finds the allocation fair and reasonable as Claimants do not have medical expenses and did not receive any care for their emotional distress injuries.

 

          Medical Expenses

 

Petitioner is not claiming any medical expenses for Claimants. (Pet., ¶ 12a.)

 

          Attorney’s Fees & Costs

 

The Petitions each seek $1,333 in attorney’s fees from each Claimant. (Pet., ¶ 13.) The Petition includes a declaration from Plaintiffs’ counsel Chandra Gehri Spencer. (Pet., Attach., 13a.) The requested amount is based on a 40% contingency fee for all Plaintiffs including the minor Claimants. (Spencer Decl., ¶ 10.) Plaintiffs’ counsel provides the Representation Agreement in which Plaintiffs agreed to a 40% contingency fee. (Pet., Attach., 17a.)

 

The Petition indicates that none of the minor Claimants, including Israel, will be paying any costs. (Pet., Attach., 11b(6).)

 

Following deductions for attorney’s fees, each Claimant will receive $2,000. Based on the declaration of counsel and information provided in this Petition, the Court finds this amount reasonable.

 

          Disposition of Settlement Funds

 

The Petition requests each Claimant’s recovery to be deposited in insured accounts in one or more financial institutions in this state, subject to withdrawal only on authorization by the Court. (Pet., ¶ 18b(2).) Petitioner provides the relevant information in Attachment 18b(2).

 

          Additional Orders

 

There are no additional orders.

 

          Deficiencies

 

The hearing date has not been updated in the Proposed Orders.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds the Petitions are fair and reasonable.

 

Petitioner Ana Acevedo’s petitions for approval of minor’s compromise are APPROVED. The Court will sign the Proposed Orders concurrently filed with the Petition. 


Case Number: 26NWCV00668    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: P

AYZ INVESTMENT LLC v. RAYMOND R. & DONNIS H. HOLBROOK TRUST, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 26NWCV00668 

HEARING: 09/18/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#12

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Plaintiff AYZ Investment LLC’s motion to compel nonparty Southern Edison Company’s compliance with its deposition subpoena is GRANTED.

 

II.            Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is DENIED.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff AYZ Investment LLC (Plaintiff) moves to compel nonparty Southern Edison Company (SCE) to comply with its deposition subpoena for production of business records.

 

Background

 

On February 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed this breach of contract action against Defendants Raymond R. & Donnis H. Holbrook Trust (Trust or Seller); Dana Robert Holbrook, individually and as Trustee; Donald La; LH Enterprises, Inc.; and Does 1–50 regarding the commercial real property located at 12631 12637 Los Nietos Road, Santa Fe Springs, California (Property). The complaint alleges that Plaintiff and Trust entered into a written Purchase Sale Agreement (PSA) for the sale of the Property under which Trust agreed to maintain the Property in the same condition through closing and to disclose any material changes in condition. (Complaint, ¶¶ 14-15.) The complaint alleges that Trust concealed damage and prevented Plaintiff from inspecting the Property after the Property experienced repeated criminal activity during escrow. (Complaint, ¶¶ 17-25.)

 

The complaint asserts nine causes of action: (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of Express Warranty; (3) Breach of Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; (4) Fraud – Concealment; (5) Intentional Misrepresentation; (6) Negligent Misrepresentation; (7) Negligence; (8) Violation of Civil Code § 2079; and (9) Declaratory Relief.

 

This is a continuation. On August 21, 2026, the Court continued the hearing on this motion after finding the service of the deposition subpoena to be insufficient. The Court permitted Plaintiff to file a supplemental declaration regarding proof of service.

 

On September 4, 2026, SCE filed a limited response to this motion.

 

Legal Standard

 

A party can obtain discovery from a nonparty via a deposition subpoena. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2020.010, subd. (b); Unzipped Apparel, LLC v. Bader (2007) 156 Cal.App.4th 123, 127.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, subdivision (a) provides: 

 

If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court’s own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands, including unreasonable violations of the right of privacy of the person.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to compel SCE’s compliance with its deposition subpoena.

 

On September 4, 2026, SCE filed a declaration stating that it does not oppose compliance with the deposition subpoena. Counsel for SCE explains “[o]n August 27, 2026, I became aware of a continued hearing on a Motion to Compel Compliance with Deposition Subpoena for Production of Business Records directed to SCE by Plaintiff AYZ Investments, LLC (“Motion”). Upon learning of the Motion and hearing, I immediately began investigating the circumstances surrounding the subpoena and SCE's nonresponse to the subpoena, and I located and reviewed Plaintiff's moving papers.” (Hickerson Decl., ¶ 2.)

 

Plaintiff shows that the deposition subpoena required production of responsive records by April 30, 2026 and that SCE neither produced any documents nor served objections, requested extensions, or made any communications regarding the deposition subpoena. (Fox Decl., ¶¶ 11-12; Exh. A.) Plaintiff also shows that it made informal attempts to compel compliance but SCE had not responded. (Fox Decl., ¶¶ 13-14, 18.)

 

Plaintiff argues that the subpoena seeks service activation records; service interruption records; meter removal and installation records; electrical service applications; work orders; field inspection reports; communications with the City of Santa Fe Springs; service release documentation; records reflecting damage to electrical infrastructure; and records identifying the date electrical service was restored. (Fox Decl., Exh. A.) Plaintiff states that “[t]he records sought are directly relevant to Plaintiff's claims because they concern the timing and condition of electrical service to the property, the work required before service could be restored, and the date upon which electrical service was restored following the theft of the property's electrical infrastructure.” (Fox Decl., ¶ 19.) Plaintiff also attests that the records are uniquely within SCE’s possession and cannot be obtained from another source. (Fox Decl., ¶ 20.)

 

In SCE’s limited response, SCE explains that it attempted to meet and confer with Plaintiff upon learning about this subpoena but has not received any responses. (Hickerson Decl., ¶ 7.)

 

Based on the foregoing, the Courts finds this motion is reasonably made and seeks records that might reasonably lead to admissible evidence. Moreover, SCE does not oppose this motion and is “committed to complying with the subpoena and producing responsive nonprivileged records.” (Hickerson Decl., ¶ 10.) The Court therefore finds an order compelling compliance with the deposition subpoena warranted.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion to compel compliance with its deposition subpoena is GRANTED.

 

Nonparty Southern Edison Company is ORDERED to comply with the deposition subpoena within 30 days of this Order.

 

Sanctions

 

Plaintiff requests monetary sanctions against SCE based on its failure to comply with the deposition subpoena without justification.

 

“[T]he court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.2, subd. (a).)

 

The Court declines to award monetary sanctions against SCE as the Court does not find that the motion was opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification. Plaintiff did not respond to SCE’s communications after learning about this motion. (Hickerson Decl., ¶ 7.) Moreover, Plaintiff does not supply a declaration attesting to the fees incurred in bringing this motion or their reasonableness.


Case Number: 26NWCV01001    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: P

NALLELY CEBALLOS GARCIA v. SEAN T. WASSGREN

CASE NO.: 26NWCV01001 

HEARING:  09/18/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#13

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Plaintiff Nallely Ceballos Garcia’s motions to compel Defendant Sean T. Wassgren to provide responses to form interrogatories, set one and requests for production, set one are GRANTED.

 

II.            Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $1,800 against Defendant only.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Plaintiff Nallely Ceballos Garcia (Plaintiff) moves to compel Defendant Sean T. Wassgren (Defendant) to provide responses to (1) form interrogatories (FROGs), set one and (2) requests for production of documents (RPDs), set one.

 

Background

 

On March 19, 2026, Plaintiff filed this wrongful eviction action against Defendant and Does 1 through 30. The complaint alleges that Plaintiff signed a month-to-month rental agreement with Defendant to rent a private room in the single-family home located at 10609 Bexley Drive, Whittier, CA 90606 (Subject Premises). (Complaint, ¶ 13.) The complaint alleges that during Plaintiff’s tenancy, Defendant repeatedly denied Plaintiff access to the kitchen or shared facilities and entered Plaintiff’s private room while intoxicated and without notice. (Complaint, ¶¶ 16-17.) Despite being told to stop, the complaint alleges Defendant continued to harass and be racist to Plaintiff, ultimately leading to her alleged constructive eviction and service of a 30-day notice to quit. (Complaint, ¶¶ 19-23.)

 

The complaint asserts eight causes of action: (1) violation of the Tom Bane Civil Rights Act (Civ. Code, § 52.1); (2) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (3) constructive eviction; (4) retaliatory eviction; (5) violation of Civil Code § 1940.2—covenant of quiet enjoyment; (6) breach of the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment; (7) violation of Civil Code § 1950.5; and (8) intrusion into private affairs.

 

Legal Standard

 

Interrogatories 

 

A party to whom interrogatories are propounded must respond within 30 days of service of the interrogatories. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.260.) If a party fails to serve a timely response, the party propounding interrogatories may move for an order compelling responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)¿Additionally, “the party to whom the interrogatories are directed waives any right to exercise the option to produce writings under¿Section 2030.230, as well as any objection to the interrogatories, including one based on privilege or on the protection for work product.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290.)¿ 

 

Requests for Production

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.260 subdivision (a), a party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days of service. Failure to do so waives all objections including privilege or on the protection of work product. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (a).) 

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves to compel Defendant to provide responses to sets one of her FROGs and RPDs. These motions are unopposed.

 

Plaintiff served the subject discovery to Defendant on June 26, 2026. (Cohen Decls., ¶ 4.) After not receiving responses by July 29, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel inquired about the responses with defense counsel who stated that he would provide responses within a week. (Cohen Decls., ¶ 5.) Plaintiff further extended the deadline to provide responses to August 21, 2026. (Cohen Decls., ¶ 8.) As of the date Plaintiff filed these motions, Plaintiff had not received responses. (Cohen Decls., ¶ 10.)

 

Here, Plaintiff establishes that she properly served Defendant with the subject discovery and has not received responses despite receiving multiple extensions.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motions to compel discovery responses to FROGs, set one and RPDs, set one are GRANTED.

 

Defendant is ORDERED to provide verified responses, without objections, to form interrogatories, set one and requests for production of documents, set one, within 30 days of this Order. This date may be extended pursuant to agreement between the parties.

 

Sanctions

 

Plaintiff moves for monetary sanctions against Defendant for the failure to provide responses. Plaintiff notes that defense counsel was responsive and unable to obtain responses from Defendant despite diligent efforts. (Cohen Decls., ¶ 8.)

 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290, subdivision (c) and 2031.300, subdivision (c) “[t]he court shall impose a monetary sanction…against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to interrogatories [or demand for production], unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”

 

The Court finds monetary sanctions warranted against Defendant pursuant to the Code. Plaintiff requests $1,560 for the RPDs motion reflecting Plaintiff’s counsel’s hourly rate of $600 and 2.6 hours to prepare the motion. (Cohen Decl., ¶ 11.) Plaintiff requests $1,380 for the FROGs motion reflecting Plaintiff’s 2.3 hours to prepare the motion. (Cohen Decl., ¶ 11.) The Court finds this amount unreasonably excessive given that Plaintiff’s counsel does not support his hourly rate in his declaration and based on the simplicity of the motions. The Court awards a total of three hours for the two motions.

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions is GRANTED in the amount of $1,800 against Defendant.

 

Defendant is ORDERED to pay this amount within 30 days of this Order. This date may be extended pursuant to agreement between the parties.
