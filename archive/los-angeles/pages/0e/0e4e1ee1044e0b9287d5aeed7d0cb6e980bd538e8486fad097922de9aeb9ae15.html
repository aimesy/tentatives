DEPARTMENT 224 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV15393    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 224

Shavi Johnson v. Austin Financial Services, Inc. et al.,

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff's Motion to Strike is GRANTED. Defendant's Memorandum of Costs filed December 9, 2025, is stricken in its entirety.

Introduction

On June 30, 2023, Plaintiff Shavi Johnson filed the Complaint in the instant action against Defendants Austin Financial Services, Inc. and Eric Richardson, alleging causes of action under the Fair Employment and Housing Act for sexual harassment and failure to prevent harassment, together with non-FEHA claims for assault, battery, intentional infliction of emotional distress, negligent hiring and supervision, and violation of the Ralph Civil Rights Act.

On November 25, 2025, following the Court's ruling granting Defendant's Motion for Summary Judgment, judgment was entered in Defendant's favor. The judgment states that Defendant "is the prevailing party and may be entitled to recover costs, in an amount to be determined, as against Plaintiff."

On December 9, 2025, Defendant filed its Memorandum of Costs.

On December 22, 2025, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Strike Defendant Austin Financial Services, Inc.'s Memorandum of Costs.

On September 1, 2026, Defendant filed an Opposition.

On September 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Reply.

Legal Standard

A prevailing party in litigation may recover costs, including but not limited to filing fees. (Code of Civ. Proc. §1033.5, subd. (a)(1).) “Prevailing party” includes the party with a net monetary recovery, a defendant in whose favor a dismissal is entered, a defendant where neither plaintiff nor defendant obtains any relief, and a defendant as against those plaintiffs who do not recover any relief against that defendant. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4).)

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, subdivision (c)(2), allowable costs are only recoverably if they are “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation.” Even mandatory costs, when incurred unnecessarily, are subject to section 1033, subdvision (c)(2). (Perko’s Enterprises, Inc. v. RRNS Enterprises (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 238, 245.)

The memorandum of cost is a verified statement by the party, attorney, or agent that the costs are correct and were necessarily incurred in the case. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(a)(1).) If the items on a verified cost bill appear proper charges they are prima facie evidence that the costs, expenses, and services listed therein were necessarily occurred. (Oak Grove School Dist. v. City Title Ins. Co. (1963) 217 Cal.App.2d 678, 698 (Oak Grove).) “The trial court’s first determination is whether the statute expressly allows the particular item and whether it appears proper on its face; if so, the burden is on the objecting party to show the costs to be unnecessary or unreasonable.” (Foothill-DeAnza Community College Dist. v. Emerich (2007) 158 Cal.App.4th 11, 29 (Foothill-DeAnza).) The burden of showing that an item is not properly chargeable or is unreasonable is upon the party challenging the costs. (Wilson v. Nichols (1942) 55 Cal.App.2d 678, 682-683 (Wilson).)

Where the items are properly objected to, they are put at issue, and the burden of proof is on the party claiming them as costs. (Oak Grove, supra, 217 Cal.App.2d at p. 698.) In other words, the burden is initially on the objecting party to show that the costs are not proper on their face. Then, the burden shifts to the moving party to justify its costs and expenses. If the costs are not facially proper, that is, the costs are not expressly allowed under a statute, then the moving party must justify the requests for the expenses.

Discussion

Defendant Austin Financial Services, Inc. ("Defendant") obtained summary judgment against Plaintiff Shavi Johnson ("Plaintiff"), and judgment was entered in Defendant's favor on November 25, 2025. The judgment recites that Defendant "is the prevailing party and may be entitled to recover costs, in an amount to be determined, as against Plaintiff." On December 9, 2025, Defendant filed a Memorandum of Costs claiming $7,444.05 in costs. Plaintiff then timely moved to strike that memorandum in its entirety on December 22, 2025.

The threshold inquiry is whether Defendant, as the prevailing party in this FEHA action, may recover its costs from Plaintiff by way of a memorandum of costs.

In general, the prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs for suit in any action or proceeding. (Code Civ. Proc., §1032(b); Santisas v. Goodin (1998) 17 Cal.4th 599, 606; Scott Co. of Calif. v. Blount, Inc. (1999) 20 Cal.4th 1103, 1108.)¿

At the same time, the California Fair Employment and Housing Act, enacted at Government Code sections 12900-12996, provides that “[i]n civil actions brought under [FEHA], the court, in its discretion, may award to the prevailing party . . . reasonable attorney's fees and costs . . . except that, notwithstanding [a different Code section not at issue here], a prevailing defendant shall not be awarded fees and costs unless the court finds the action was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless when brought, or the plaintiff continued to litigate after it clearly became so.” (Gov. Code, § 12965, subd. (b).)¿

“Government Code section 12965, subdivision (b), governs cost awards in FEHA actions, allowing trial courts discretion in awards of both attorney fees and costs to prevailing FEHA parties.” (Williams v. Chino Valley Independent Fire District (2015) 61 Cal.4th 97, 99.) Although Code of Civil Procedure section 1032, subdivision (b) broadly mandates that costs be awarded to the prevailing party in civil actions, our Supreme Court in Williams made abundantly clear that Government Code section 12965(b) is an “express exception to Code of Civil Procedure section 1032(b)” in actions governed by FEHA. (Id. at 109.)¿ An "unsuccessful FEHA plaintiff should not be ordered to pay the defendant's fees or costs unless the plaintiff brought or continued litigating the action without an objective basis for believing it had potential merit." (Williams, at pp. 99-100, 115.) Because "even ordinary litigation costs can be substantial," and because the possibility of their assessment "could significantly chill the vindication of employees' civil rights," this asymmetrical standard governs both costs and fees in FEHA actions. (Id. at p. 114.)

It is undisputed that Plaintiff's complaint asserts claims under FEHA — sexual harassment and failure to prevent harassment — such that the section 12965 express exception applies. However, as Plaintiff asserts, under section 12965(b), a successful FEHA defendant is awarded costs only if “the action was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless when brought, or [if] the plaintiff continued to litigate after it clearly became so.” (Gov. Code, § 12965, subd. (b).) If neither of these is the case, Defendant may not be awarded their costs.

As an initial matter, Defendant has not filed a noticed motion seeking recovery of costs. In Neeble-Diamond v. Hotel California By the Sea, LLC (2024) 99 Cal.App.5th 551, the Court of Appeal held that because costs in a FEHA action are not a matter of right, but are discretionary and conditioned on a judicial finding of objective frivolousness, a prevailing defendant may not invoke the ordinary memorandum-of-costs procedure at all. The filing of a cost memorandum is the proper means of securing a cost award only where costs are mandatory; where the defense prevails in a FEHA action, it has no automatic right to recover costs and must instead proceed by noticed motion to establish its entitlement. (Id. at p. 560.) Because the defendant in Neeble-Diamond filed only a cost memorandum, the trial court erred in awarding costs. (Ibid.)

Defendant argues that Neeble-Diamond is distinguishable because it was a "FEHA-only case." (Opp., p. 4.) It was not. The opinion states that the plaintiff's lawsuit alleged "both statutory and nonstatutory causes of action" arising out of her alleged employment status. (Neeble-Diamond, supra, 99 Cal.App.5th at p. 555.) The distinction on which Defendant relies does not exist.

Defendant filed a form MC-010 memorandum and MC-011 worksheet. It did not file a noticed motion, and it has not asked this Court to find that Plaintiff's action was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless. Absent a noticed motion and an express finding, there is no basis on which costs subject to section 12965 may be awarded.

Even had Defendant proceeded by noticed motion, the result would be the same on this record.

In determining whether an action was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless such that an award of costs is appropriate, “it is important that a . . . court resist the understandable temptation to engage in post hoc reasoning by concluding that, because a plaintiff did not ultimately prevail, his action must have beenunreasonable or without foundation. This kind of hindsight logic could discourage all but the most airtight claims, for seldom can a prospective plaintiff be sure of ultimate success.” (Rosenman v. Christensen, Miller, Fink, Jacobs, Glaser, Weil, & Shapiro (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 859, 866 [analyzing identical language applicable to Title VII claims]; see also Williams, supra, 61 Cal.4th at pp. 113 [embracing the “asymmetrical” nature of section 12965(b)’s costs provision in light of the Legislature’s express intent “to encourage persons injured by discrimination to seek judicial relief”].)

Defendant does not argue that Plaintiff's action was frivolous. Its opposition makes no such showing and requests no such finding. The memorandum of costs asserts only that the listed items "are correct and were necessarily incurred," which speaks to the reasonableness of individual charges and not at all to the statutory predicate.

Nor could the finding be made on this record. Defendant prevailed on summary judgment, but summary judgment measures whether a triable issue of material fact exists. It does not measure whether a plaintiff had an objective basis for believing her claims had potential merit. (Williams, supra, 61 Cal.4th at pp. 99-100, 115.) The Court's ruling turned on the employer-side questions of notice and whether Defendant took prompt and appropriate remedial action, not on the absence of any evidentiary basis for the underlying allegation that Plaintiff was sexually assaulted by a coworker in the workplace during her employment. A claim that fails on a contested question of the employer's response is a claim that failed on the merits, not one that was objectively without foundation.

Defendant also does not identify when the claims "clearly became" groundless. The costs claimed span the life of the action, from the August 4, 2023 fee for filing Defendant's answer through the November 10, 2025 dismissal filing, without identifying any point at which the litigation crossed into groundlessness. Neither Plaintiff's October 3, 2023 deposition, nor the depositions of Jason Anish on March 5, 2025, Marilyn Barnes on May 1, 2025, and Kevin Koda on May 7, 2025, nor the July 2025 filing of the summary judgment motion marks such a moment on this record. There is no identifiable date from which costs could be measured, and Defendant has not proposed one.

The presence of non-FEHA causes of action does not change the outcome. In Roman v. BRE Properties, Inc. (2015) 237 Cal.App.4th 1040, 1061, the Court of Appeal held that where non-FEHA claims are "inextricably intertwined" with FEHA claims, the trial court may not award costs unless the action was frivolous. Relying on Williams, the Court of Appeal explained that "it would weaken private enforcement of vital antidiscrimination and disability rights statutes . . . to compel an award of costs . . . simply because the plaintiff, based on the same alleged misconduct, had pleaded other civil rights theories in addition to . . . her FEHA causes of action." (Roman, supra, at pp. 1059-1060.) Every claim pleaded here — the FEHA claims, the common law tort claims, and the Ralph Act claim — arises from the same facts: the alleged sexual assault of Plaintiff by a coworker at work during her employment with Defendant. The depositions, filing fees, and service costs claimed were incurred in a single, undifferentiated defense of that unified set of allegations, and Defendant has not attempted to apportion them. No portion of the claimed costs is recoverable.

The burden of establishing entitlement to costs rests with the party seeking them. Defendant's contention that Plaintiff must disprove each item inverts that burden. The prima facie effect of a verified cost memorandum operates only as to items recoverable as a matter of right, which is the premise Neeble-Diamond holds is absent in a FEHA case. Defendant, not Plaintiff, was obligated to identify which claimed costs were attributable solely to the non-FEHA causes of action and to explain why those costs would not have been incurred in defending the FEHA claims. Defendant has made no such showing.

Because Defendant has not demonstrated, and cannot demonstrate on this record, that Plaintiff's FEHA action was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless when brought or that Plaintiff continued to litigate after it clearly became so, and because Defendant sought its costs by memorandum rather than by the noticed motion Neeble-Diamond requires, Defendant is not entitled to recover costs under Government Code section 12965, subdivision (c)(6). The Court therefore does not reach Plaintiff's objections, if any, to individual line items.

“In amending [FEHA] to authorize discretionary awards of attorney fees and costs, our Legislature, like Congress before it, sought ‘to encourage persons injured by discrimination to seek judicial relief.’ [Citation.]” (Williams, supra, (2015) 61 Cal.4th at p. 112.) In order to vindicate the intent of our Legislature, the striking of costs and fees in this case is appropriate.

Conclusion

Plaintiff's Motion to Strike is GRANTED. Defendant's Memorandum of Costs filed December 9, 2025, is stricken in its entirety.

Case Number: 23STCV30271    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 224

Ocotitla v. Wil Fig LLC

TENTATIVE RULING

The Motion to Set Aside Default is DENIED.

ANALYSIS

Introduction

On December 12, 2023, Plaintiff Ana Ivette Ocotitla filed the Complaint in this action against Defendants Wil Fig LLC dba El Patron Cantina, a limited liability company; Russell Cen aka Russell Con aka Oswaldo Garcia, an individual; and Does 1-100, alleging eight causes of action arising from an alleged sexual assault by a coworker and the alleged reduction of her hours and pay after she reported it.

Defendant Wil Fig LLC appeared and filed an Answer to the Complaint.

On January 21, 2025, the Court granted the motion of Defendant Wil Fig LLC's counsel to be relieved. Defendant Wil Fig LLC thereafter remained unrepresented.

On September 10, 2025, following an Order to Show Cause hearing, the Court struck Defendant Wil Fig LLC's Answer and entered default against it. The Court set a default prove-up hearing for June 2026.

In May 2026, Plaintiff filed her Request for Entry of Default and application for court judgment, and dismissed the individual defendant Russell Cen and the Doe defendants without prejudice.

On June 26, 2026, Defendant Wil Fig LLC filed the instant Motion to Vacate Entry of Default and Default Judgment.

On June 30, 2026, Oakmont Law, PC filed a Notice of Limited Scope Representation on behalf of Defendant Wil Fig LLC, stating that the scope of its representation is limited to setting aside the entry of default and default judgment and to filing a responsive pleading.

On July 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed her Opposition to the Motion.

On September 8, 2026, Defendant filed a Reply.

Legal Standard

Code Civ. Proc., section 473.5 permits the Court to set aside a default and default judgment when the service of a summons has not resulted in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action. Code Civ. Proc., section 473.5 requires the motion to be accompanied by an affidavit showing under oath that the party's lack of actual notice in time to defend the action was not caused by the party's avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5.)¿The notice of motion shall be served and filed within a reasonable time, but in no event exceeding the earlier of the following:¿¿¿

1) two years after entry of a default judgment against him or her; or¿¿¿

2) 180 days after service on him or her of a written notice that the default or default judgment has been entered. ¿¿¿

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5.)

Personal service may be accomplished by personally delivering a copy of the summons and complaint to the person to be served. (Code of Civ. Proc., § 415.10.) If a copy of the summons and complaint cannot with reasonable diligence be personally delivered to the person being served, substitute service may be effected by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint at the person’s “dwelling house, usual place of abode, usual place of business, or usual mailing address . . . in the presence of . . . a person apparently in charge . . . and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and complaint by first-class mail . . . to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left.” (Code of Civ. Proc., § 415.20, subd. (b).)

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) provides that:

The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(d).)

Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default

Relief Pursuant to CCP § 473.5

Defendant moves to set aside the default on the ground that its managing partner, Mark Moss, had no actual notice of this action until May 29, 2026, when he received Plaintiff's Notice of Application for Court Judgment in the mail. Defendant asserts that all pleadings and notices were directed to 815 W. 7th Street, Los Angeles, the mailing address on file with the Secretary of State, which Moss describes as an address associated with a different restaurant. (Moss Decl., ¶¶ 3, 5, 8.)

Section 473.5 applies "[w]hen service of a summons has not resulted in actual notice to a party in time to defend the action." (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (a).) The statute is directed at the party litigant. "Actual notice" under section 473.5 "means genuine knowledge of the party litigant." (Rosenthal v. Garner (1983) 142 Cal.App.3d 891, 895.)

The threshold predicate for relief is absent here. The party litigant is Wil Fig LLC. Wil Fig LLC was served, appeared, filed an Answer through counsel, and participated in this litigation for approximately a year before its counsel moved to be relieved. That general appearance is dispositive of the jurisdictional premise of the motion. (Code Civ. Proc., § 410.50, subd. (a).) Whatever the state of the

Secretary of State's records, service of the summons plainly did result in actual notice to the party, because the party responded to it with a pleading.

Nor did the default arise from any defect in service. It arose from the striking of Defendant's Answer as a sanction following an Order to Show Cause, after Defendant, an entity that cannot appear in propria persona, failed for more than eight months to retain replacement counsel. As Plaintiff correctly observes, a default entered after an answer is stricken is "less of an affirmative 'tactical' choice not to participate in the lawsuit in the first place . . . and more of a sanction" for how the case was litigated. (Sass v. Cohen (2019) 32 Cal.App.5th 1032, 1041.) Section 473.5 supplies no remedy for that.

Defendant's argument is that current management did not personally know of the case. Moss declares that during the pendency of this action his former partner "managed the company's administrative and legal affairs," handled all correspondence and legal notices, and that Moss was not made aware of the litigation "nor did I have any actual knowledge of the case or the answer previously filed on behalf of the company." (Moss Decl., ¶ 6.) That paragraph concedes the point. It acknowledges that an answer was filed on the company's behalf and locates the failure of internal communication inside the company. A corporate defendant does not reset the notice clock each time its management changes hands. The knowledge of the partner who was managing the company's legal affairs during the relevant period is the knowledge of Wil Fig LLC.

Section 473.5 additionally conditions relief on an affidavit showing under oath that the movant's lack of actual notice in time to defend "was not caused by his or her avoidance of service or inexcusable neglect." (Code Civ. Proc., § 473.5, subd. (b).) Moss's declaration attributes the failure to respond to "pure clerical oversight regarding the Secretary of State address." (Moss Decl., ¶ 8.) Maintaining a current address with the Secretary of State is Defendant's statutory obligation. A defect in Defendant's own corporate filings is not a defect in Plaintiff's service, and the failure to correct it over the course of years is not the sort of oversight the statute excuses. More fundamentally, the declaration does not address the operative neglect at all. The Answer was stricken because Defendant did not appear at the Order to Show Cause or obtain counsel, and Defendant made no effort to participate until the prospect of a monetary judgment materialized.

The motion is timely in the abstract, and Defendant has satisfied the procedural requirement of subdivision (b) by attaching a proposed Answer as Exhibit A. Neither cures the failure on the merits.

Relief under CCP § 473.5 is DENIED.

Void Judgment

Defendant next contends that any default judgment will be void because the Complaint states no specific dollar amount of damages, so that a monetary judgment would exceed the demand and the Court's jurisdiction. (Code Civ. Proc., § 580, subd. (a); Greenup v. Rodman (1986) 42 Cal.3d 822, 824.)

Section 473(d) authorizes the Court to "set aside any void judgment or order." No default judgment has been entered in this action. The prove-up hearing was continued to prioritize this motion, and the Court's file reflects no judgment. There is accordingly no judgment, void or otherwise, for the Court to set aside. The ground is not ripe.

At most, Defendant's authority establishes a limit on what may be awarded at the prove-up. In any event, that is a question for the prove-up hearing.

Relief under CCP § 473(d) is DENIED.

Equitable Relief

Defendant argues in the alternative that the Court should exercise its inherent equitable power to vacate the default, invoking the policy that controversies be resolved on their merits. (Fasuyi v. Permatex, Inc. (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 681, 694-703.)

A party seeking equitable relief from a default after the six-month period of section 473(b) has run must show extrinsic fraud or extrinsic mistake, and must satisfy a stringent three-part test: a meritorious defense, a satisfactory excuse for not presenting a defense in the first instance, and diligence in seeking to set aside the default once discovered. (Rappleyea v. Campbell (1994) 8 Cal.4th 975, 981-982.) Relief is unavailable where the moving party's own negligence produced the default; such error is intrinsic. (Kulchar v. Kulchar (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 472-473.)

Defendant cannot make the second showing. It was not "deprived of a fair adversary hearing through no fault of their own." It had counsel, filed an Answer, litigated for a year, then stopped. Its counsel was relieved in January 2025; it did not retain new counsel until June 2026. The Court issued an Order to Show Cause

and struck the Answer only after Defendant failed to respond. The breakdown Moss describes, in which one partner departed and the company's legal affairs went unattended, is an internal failure of the party itself. That is intrinsic neglect, and it is the precise circumstance in which equitable relief is withheld.

The diligence showing fares no better. Defendant acted only when the threat of a monetary judgment became concrete. The Court also notes that discretionary relief under section 473(b) is unavailable, the default having been entered September 10, 2025, more than six months before this motion was filed, and Defendant does not invoke that subdivision. Late requests for relief are jurisdictionally barred. 

Equitable relief is DENIED.

Conclusion

Given the foregoing, the Motion to Set Aside Default is DENIED.

900 Vermont LLC v. Mohammad Khajavi

TENTATIVE RULING

The hearing on Defendant's motion is CONTINUED to November 6, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.. On or before October 9, 2026, Defendant shall file and serve a proposed responsive pleading in this action. If Defendant timely files and serves the proposed pleading, the court will GRANT the motion, vacate and set aside the judgment under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b), and deem the proposed pleading filed as of the date of the order.

The Court also sets a Case Management Conference on November 6, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.

ANALYSIS

Introduction

On July 29, 2025, Plaintiff filed the Complaint for Unlawful Detainer in this action.

On September 30, 2025, Default Judgment was entered against Defendant Mohammad Khajavi.

On November 14, 2025, Defendant filed his first Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgment.

On June 23, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Amended Motion to Set/Aside Vacate Judgment.

No opposition has been filed.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b) provides for two distinct types of relief—commonly differentiated as “discretionary” and “mandatory”—from certain prior actions or proceedings in the trial court. (Luri v. Greenwald (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 1119, 1124 (Luri).)

The discretionary portion of section 473, subdivision (b) provides: “The court may, upon terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

The mandatory portion of section 473 requires the court to, “whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against his or her client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

Motion to Set Aside Default Judgment

Defendant Mohammad Khajavi moves to vacate the default judgment entered on September 30, 2025. Defendant first moved to set aside the judgment on November 14, 2025, within six months of the judgment. The instant Amended

Motion was filed June 23, 2026. Accordingly, the Court treats Defendant’s motion as timely filed.

The substance of Defendant's showing is that although he signed the lease as the named lessee, Taz Dar was the intended and actual occupant, rent payor, and point of contact for Unit 239 from the inception of the tenancy; that Plaintiff's management dealt directly with Mr. Dar on all tenancy and account matters throughout; that Defendant did not reside in the unit or manage its day-to-day affairs; and that, understanding Mr. Dar to be handling the matter, Defendant did not separately respond to the action before default was taken.

Defendant has provided his declaration in support and Defendant acted diligently within a reasonable time.

Section 473(b) requires that a motion for relief be accompanied by the proposed responsive pleading. Defendant's motion does not include one. That omission, standing alone, would preclude relief. Given Defendant's self-represented status, the strong policy favoring merits dispositions, and the otherwise sufficient showing of mistake, the Court CONTINUES the hearing to allow Defendant to cure the defect by filing and serving a proposed responsive pleading.

Conclusion

The hearing on Defendant's motion is CONTINUED to November 6, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.. On or before October 9, 2026, Defendant shall file and serve a proposed responsive pleading in this action. If Defendant timely files and serves the proposed pleading, the court will GRANT the motion, vacate and set aside the judgment under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b), and deem the proposed pleading filed as of the date of the order.

The Court also sets a Case Management Conference on November 6, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.

Case Number: 26STCV10183    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 224

KDIC v. Park et al.,

TENTATIVE RULING

The demurrer to Plaintiff's sole cause of action for Breach of Guarantee Agreement is SUSTAINED, with 20 days’ leave to amend.

ANALYSIS

Introduction

On March 30, 2026, Plaintiff Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation ("KDIC"), a Korean corporation suing as Trustee for the Bankrupt Mirae Savings Bank Co., Ltd., filed this action alleging a single cause of action for breach of a Guarantee.

On May 18, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Demurrer to Plaintiff's Complaint.

On September 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer or a motion to strike, the demurring or moving party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to or the pleading that is subject to the motion to strike for the purposes of determining whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (CCP § 430.41)

Defense Counsel’s Declaration, executed on May 18, 2026 provides that Counsel were unable to meet and confer pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41. Nonetheless, the Court exercises its discretion to proceed on the merits of the demurrer. The Court notes that in the future the parties must comply with CCP 430.41's requirements to confer.

Legal Standard

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿ (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)¿When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.¿ (Wilson v. Transit Authority of City of Sacramento (1962) 199 Cal.App.2d 716, 720-21.)¿In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice.¿(Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)¿“A demurrer tests the pleading alone, and not on the evidence or facts alleged.” (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Servs. (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315.) As such, the court assumes the truth of the complaint’s properly pleaded or implied factual allegations. (Id.) The only issue a demurrer is concerned with is whether the complaint, as it stands, states a cause of action. (Hahn, supra, 147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)

Statute of Limitations

Defendant demurs to the sole cause of action in the Complaint on the ground that it is time-barred by the applicable statute of limitations for a breach of written contract claim, and further that the Complaint fails to allege sufficient facts to invoke the doctrine of equitable tolling.

California Code of Civil Procedure section 337 provides a four-year statute of limitations for "[a]n action upon any contract, obligation or liability founded upon an instrument in writing." (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 337(a).) This statute of limitations governs an action to enforce a written guarantee agreement. A cause of action for breach of contract accrues at the time of breach. (Cochran v. Cochran (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 1115, 1120.)

A statute of limitations defense may be raised by demurrer only where the untimeliness appears clearly and affirmatively on the face of the complaint; it is not sufficient that the complaint shows the action may be barred. (Committee for Green Foothills v. Santa Clara County Bd. of Supervisors (2010) 48 Cal.4th 32, 42.) Where a complaint shows on its face that a claim is untimely, the plaintiff bears the burden of pleading facts establishing a basis for tolling. (E-Fab, Inc. v. Accountants, Inc. Services (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1308, 1315-1316.)

Equitable tolling requires a showing of three elements: (1) timely notice to the defendant; (2) lack of prejudice to the defendant; and (3) reasonable and good faith conduct on the part of the plaintiff. (Saint Francis Memorial Hospital v. State Dept. of Public Health (2020) 9 Cal.5th 710, 724.) Where equitable tolling applies, the limitations period is suspended for the duration of the first proceeding and the tolled interval is then added to the end of the limitations period. (Lantzy v. Centex Homes (2003) 31 Cal.4th 363, 370-371.)

Here, the Complaint alleges that the Loan Agreement was amended by the Supplementary Agreement to set a maturity date of May 26, 2013, that Defendant executed the Guarantee on February 26, 2013, and that One and J defaulted on the Mirae Savings Loan. (Complaint ¶¶ 9-11, 13.) Accordingly, the Complaint adequately alleges that the breach of the Guarantee occurred no later than May 26, 2013. Thus, as alleged, the four-year statute of limitations under Code of Civil Procedure section 337 ran on or about May 26, 2017. Plaintiff's Complaint was filed on March 30, 2026, nearly nine years past the expiration of the limitations period. The claim is therefore untimely on the face of the Complaint, and Plaintiff must plead facts sufficient to invoke tolling.

As to the first element of equitable tolling, the Complaint alleges that Defendant was duly served with process in the Korean Litigation commenced May 10, 2016, appeared through counsel, and litigated vigorously, and that Defendant received notice of and protested Plaintiff's 2019 enforcement efforts. (Complaint ¶¶ 13-14, 17.) Accepting these allegations as true, as the Court must on demurrer, the Complaint adequately alleges timely notice.

Plaintiff concedes, however, that the claim accrued on May 26, 2013, that the limitations period was suspended when the Korean Litigation was filed in May 2016, and that it resumed when the Korean Litigation concluded in November 2017. (Opp. at p. 13.) Taking that concession at face value, the tolled interval is approximately eighteen months, which, added to the end of the four-year period, would extend the deadline to no later than the end of 2018. (Lantzy, supra, 31 Cal.4th at pp. 370-371.) This action was filed on March 30, 2026, more than seven years later. Equitable tolling based on the Korean Litigation therefore cannot render the Complaint timely, and Plaintiff does not argue otherwise; it relies instead on equitable estoppel to account for the remaining period.

Further, the Complaint alleges only that "Plaintiff acted reasonably and in good faith pursuing the Korean Litigation." (Complaint ¶ 22.) This is a conclusion of law, which the Court does not accept as true on demurrer. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) A plaintiff must demonstrate that its late filing was objectively reasonable under the circumstances and subjectively in good faith. (Saint Francis, supra, 9 Cal.5th at p. 729.) The Complaint alleges no facts explaining why Plaintiff did not file this action following the Korean Judgment in 2017, following the partial enforcement in 2019, or concurrently with the Recognition Action in May 2025. Equitable tolling is a narrow form of relief available in unusual circumstances and does not extend to a garden variety claim of excusable neglect. (Id. at p. 730.)

The Court does not reach the second element. Defendant's prejudice arguments rest on extrinsic facts regarding the unavailability of witnesses and documents and the burden of defending three related actions, none of which appear on the face of the Complaint.

Thus, Plaintiff's sole cause of action for Breach of Guarantee Agreement is untimely as pled.

Equitable Estoppel

Plaintiff argues in opposition that Defendant is independently estopped from asserting the statute of limitations. Equitable estoppel is distinct from equitable tolling; it operates after the limitations period has run and addresses the circumstances in which a party will be estopped from asserting the defense because his conduct induced another to forbear suit within the limitations period. (Lantzy, supra, 31 Cal.4th at p. 383.) Its elements are that the party to be estopped was apprised of the facts, intended that his conduct be acted upon or acted such that the party asserting estoppel had a right to believe it was so intended, that the party asserting estoppel was ignorant of the true state of facts, and that it relied upon the conduct to its injury. (Citizens for a Responsible Caltrans Decision v. Dept. of Transportation (2020) 46 Cal.App.5th 1103, 1128.)

The Complaint does not plead estoppel. Paragraph 22 invokes equitable tolling alone, and nowhere does the Complaint allege that Defendant's conduct induced Plaintiff to forbear from filing a direct action on the Guarantee, that Plaintiff was ignorant of the true state of facts, or that Plaintiff relied on Defendant's conduct to its detriment. The historical facts recited in the Complaint — Defendant's appearance in the Korean Litigation, his failure to seek further review of the Korean Judgment, and his protest of the 2019 enforcement efforts — may be consistent with the theory Plaintiff now advances, but the inducement, ignorance, and reliance elements must be pleaded, not inferred from a brief.

Defendant requests that the demurrer be sustained without leave to amend, arguing that paragraph 21 of the Complaint is an allegation destructive of the cause of action because it concedes the Guarantee was enforceable at all relevant times. Leave to amend is properly granted where there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured, and the plaintiff may make that showing for the first time in opposition or at the hearing. (Blank, supra, 39 Cal.3d at p. 318; Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.)

This is the first challenge to the pleading, and Plaintiff has not yet had an opportunity to plead facts bearing on equitable estoppel, or additional facts supporting equitable tolling for the period between November 2017 and March 2026.

Leave to amend is granted to allow Plaintiff to allege further facts supporting the application of equitable tolling and equitable estoppel.

Conclusion

Accordingly, the demurrer to Plaintiff's sole cause of action for Breach of Guarantee Agreement is SUSTAINED, with 20 days’ leave to amend.

Case Number: BC521491    Hearing Date: September 15, 2026    Dept: 224

Mark Zimny v. Henry H. Bahk et al.,

TENTATIVE RULING

The December 14, 2018 judgment is not void. The motion is DENIED.

Introduction

On September 16, 2013, Plaintiff filed the Complaint in this action.

On December 14, 2018, this Court granted judgment in favor of Judgment Creditor A-Ju Tours, Inc., and against Judgment Debtor Henry M. Lee, in the principal amount of $115,000.00.

On June 1, 2026, Henry M. Lee filed the instant Motion to Set Aside Void Judgment and All Orders Derived From the Void Judgment.

On June 23, 2026, Cross-Complainants A-Ju Tours, Inc., and Henry H. Bahk filed an Opposition.

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d) provides that:

The court may, upon motion of the injured party, or its own motion, correct clerical mistakes in its judgment or orders as entered, so as to conform to the judgment or order directed, and may, on motion of either party after notice to the other party, set aside any void judgment or order.

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(d).)

When a court lacks jurisdiction, any ensuing judgment or default is void and subject to direct or collateral attack at any time. (Strathvale Holdings v. E.B.H. (2005) 126 Cal.App.4th 1241, 1249.)

“‘[O]n direct attack, lack of jurisdiction may be shown by extrinsic evidence, i.e., evidence outside the judgment roll.’” (Strathvale Holdings, supra, 126 Cal.App.4th at 1249 (quoting 8 Witkin, Cal. Procedure (4th ed. 1997) Attack on Judgment in Trial Court, § 5, p. 513).) A motion to vacate a void judgment is a direct attack, and the proper procedure of a direct attack on the default is by motion made under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d). (Id.)

Motion to Set Aside Judgment

Lee moves under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d) and the court's inherent authority to vacate void judgments, arguing the judgment is void because the operative FACC neither named Lee nor pleaded intentional interference, so the judgment adjudicated a nonexistent claim against unnamed parties in excess of jurisdiction and in violation of due process.

Improper CCP § 1008 Motion

As an initial matter, Lee’s motion is, in substance, an attempt to relitigate the same “supersession” theory he raised in 2018 before Judge Martin, when he argued the FACC superseded the original cross-complaint. Judge Martin rejected the argument, proceeded to trial on the reinstated intentional interference claim, and entered judgment against Lee. In 2019, the Court again rejected the argument when sanctioning Lee under CCP §¿128.7 for his “mistaken belief that there was no pending claim."

A party cannot evade CCP §¿1008 by rebranding a reconsideration request as a “void judgment” motion. Lee identifies no new law or facts and offers no explanation for failing to raise his arguments earlier. He brings this challenge more than seven years after judgment. A successor judge cannot overturn a prior judge’s ruling under these circumstances.

Therefore, the motion is jurisdictionally barred on this ground alone.

Void Judgment

In November 2015, Judge Rita Miller granted Lee’s anti-SLAPP motion striking the intentional interference claim. On August 7, 2017, however, the Court of Appeal reversed the anti-SLAPP order “insofar as it struck the second cause of action of the cross-complaint” and, through issuance of the remittitur on October 20, 2017, directed the trial court to permit A-Ju Tours to proceed against Lee and his law corporation on that claim. The reinstatement occurred by operation of law, and Lee’s personal jurisdiction is established because he answered the reinstated claim, filed trial-related documents, appeared at the trial call on October 29, 2018, and acknowledged receiving notice of trial before withdrawing his answer for tactical reasons “to avoid any arguments of waiver.” The judgment itself recites that Lee and his corporation did not appear at trial “after having been given due notice.” These facts demonstrate that the court had both subject-matter and personal jurisdiction.

Lee argues that the judgment is void because it imposed liability on him although he was not a named party to the FACC, the only pleading he contends was operative at the time of trial. This contention is without merit because the record establishes that the Court had fundamental jurisdiction over Lee's person independent of the FACC's caption. Jurisdiction over the person, once acquired, is not divested by a party's later decision to absent himself from trial; the judgment itself recites that the Lee Cross-defendants did not appear "after having been given due notice of the time, date and place for trial."

The December 14, 2018 judgment is not void. The motion is DENIED.
