Case Number: 23NWLC13740    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: Y

23NWLC13740: MIDLAND CREDIT MANAGEMENT, INC vs KENNETH SCOTT, AN INDIVIDUAL

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and reviewed Defendant Kenneth Scott’s (“Defendant”) Motion to Vacate Default Judgment (“Motion”) filed on January 8, 2026.  The Court has also reviewed all relevant court records.  Plaintiff Midland Credit Management, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) has not filed an opposition.  

The Court notes that Request for Entry of Default was entered on August 2, 2023 and Default Judgment was entered on August 8, 2023.  

In his Motion, Defendant argues that the judgment is void for lack of personal jurisdiction because Defendant was never properly served with the Summons and Complaint and did not reside at the address where the service was allegedly made.

The Court notes that Proof of Service of Summons by a registered process server by personal service was filed with the Court on June 5, 2023 creating a presumption of service under Evidence Code section 647.  The Proof of Service of Summons indicates personal service on “Kenneth Scott” at 15327 Woodruff Pl, Apt 102, Bellflower, CA 90706.   It describes the individual as “a black-haired black male…35-45 years of age…5’8-5”10, weighing 180-200 lbs.” 

Defendant’s declaration, which was signed under penalty of perjury, states that he did not live at that address on the date of service.  He had no actual notice of this lawsuit until after judgment was entered. Defendant’s declaration, which was signed under penalty of perjury, states that he did not live at that address on the date of service.  He had no actual notice of this lawsuit until after judgment was entered.  Defendant also provides bank statements from June 2023 which identify a different address than the service address.  Here, the Court finds that Defendant has rebutted the presumption of service.   

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d), the Court may set aside any void judgment or order.  A motion pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d) can be brought at any time after judgment.  In California Capital Insurance Company v. Hoehn (2024) 17 Cal.5th 207, the California Supreme Court ruled that a motion to vacate a void judgment due to improper service of process under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(d) is not subject to an arbitrary two-year time limit.

Code of Civil Procedure section 128 also grants jurisdiction to the Court to take appropriate action in the interests of justice.

The Court GRANTS Defendant’s Motion and vacates and sets aside the Default Judgment entered on August 8, 2023. 

The Court grants Defendant 20 days leave to file a responsive pleading.

Case Number: 25NWLC05886    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC05886: LVNV FUNDING LLC vs ROBERTA FLORES MATEO

Tentative Ruling:

On April 8, 2026, the Court set Plaintiff LVNV Funding LLC’s (“Plaintiff”) filed its third Application for Ex Parte Order for Entry of Judgment, which the Court set for hearing on July 10, 2026.

The first application was filed on October 13, 2025 and second application was filed on December 3, 2025.  Both applications, and the related judgments, were rejected because Defendant’s first appearance fee had not been paid.

The Court has read and reviewed Plaintiff’s third Application for Order for Entry of Judgment, including the declaration of counsel in support of entry of judgment pursuant to stipulation.  The Court has also read and reviewed the attached Settlement Agreement and Stipulation for Entry of Judgment Upon Default (“Stipulation”).  The Stipulation is undated and the signature by Defendant is unclear. 

The Court also notes that in the Stipulation Plaintiff agreed to pay $1,406.40, as indicated in Paragraph 2 of the Stipulation and Paragraph 3 of the Application.  In contrast, and without any explanation, Plaintiff seeks judgment in the principal amount of $1,718.15 and costs of $480.50 for a total judgment of $2,198.65.   There is no explanation why the principal amount requested is higher than the amount agreed upon in the Stipulation.  The Court also notes that the costs requested in the judgment different ($279.50) than the the costs requested in the application ($480.50).   

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s third Application for Ex Parte Order for Entry of Judgment is DENIED without prejudice.    

Plaintiff to give notice.

Case Number: 25NWLC57973    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: Y

25NWLC57973: JPMORGAN CHASE BANK, N.A. vs GEORGE PEREZ, JR

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and considered the moving papers filed by Plaintiff JPMorgan Chase Bank. N.A. (“Plaintiff”), including the declaration of counsel and exhibits.  

Plaintiff mailed Requests for Admission (RFA) to counsel for Defendant on February 6, 2026.  The address on the proof of service (Southfield, MI) does not match the address for counsel for Defendant on the Answer (Philadelphia, PA).  Additionally, the address on the proof of service (Whittier, CA) for Plaintiff’s Motion for Order Admitting Trust of the Matters Specified (“Motion”) does not match the address for counsel for Defendant (Philadelphia, PA). Consequently, Plaintiff’s Motion is DENIED.

26NWLC04360: WELLS FARGO BANK, N.A. vs GLORIA E MARQUES, AN INDIVIDUAL

Tentative Ruling:

The Court has read and considered the moving papers filed by Plaintiff Wells Fargo Bank N.A. (“Plaintiff”), including the declaration of counsel.   The Court finds that Plaintiff mailed Requests for Admission (RFA) to counsel for Defendant on March 20, 2026, and provided proof of service by mail.  Plaintiff did not receive any responses from Defendant.  Plaintiff mailed its Motion to Deem Requests for Admission Admitted to counsel for Defendant on May 19, 2026, and provided proof of service by mail.  The address for counsel for Defendant on both proofs of service match the address that is listed on the Answer.
