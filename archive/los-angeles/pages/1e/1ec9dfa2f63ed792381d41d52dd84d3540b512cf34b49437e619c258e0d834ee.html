Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:     September 22, 2026              TRIAL DATE:  Disposed by bench trial

                                                          

CASE:                              Irma Gonzalez v. Martini Associates Development, LLC

 

CASE NO.:                 20STCV17540

                                                

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR STATUTORY ATTORNEY’S FEES

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Irma Gonzalez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      Defendant Frank Martini

 

 

I.         BACKGROUND

 

            On May 8, 2020, Plaintiff, Irma Gonzalez commenced this wage and hour suit against her employer, defendant Martini Associates Development LLC d/b/a Los Angeles Adventurer All-Suite Hotel (“Martini Associates”),[1] alleging various Labor Code violations, and, in relevant part, Private Attorney General Act (PAGA) claims.  Plaintiff latter added Frank Martini (“Martini” or “Defendant”) as a defendant in this action by way of doe amendment.  

 

            This case proceeded to a bench trial against Martini.  On May 1, 2026, the court found in Plaintiff’s favor on six of eight claims and entered judgment in the sum of $33,494.00.

 

            On July 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed this motion for attorney’s fees pursuant to Labor Code sections 218.5(a) and 1194(a).

 

            On September 9, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

 

            Plaintiff filed its reply on September 15, 2026.

 

II.          LEGAL STANDARD

 

“Except as attorney’s fees are specifically provided for by statute, the measure and mode of compensation of attorneys and counselors at law is left to the agreement, express or implied, of the parties; but parties to actions or proceedings are entitled to their costs, as hereinafter provided.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1021.)¿ Attorney’s fees are allowable costs under Code of Civil Procedure section 1032 when authorized by contract, statute, or law.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5(a)(10).)¿¿  

 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

            A.  Judicial Notice

 

            Defendant’s unopposed request for judicial notice is GRANTED.

 

            B.  Analysis

 

Plaintiff seeks an award of attorneys’ fees pursuant to statute.  (See Lab. Code, §§ 1194(a); 218.5(a).)  As the prevailing party, Plaintiff is entitled to a statutory fees award. Plaintiff seeks a fees award of $135,325.00.[2]¿ The request is based upon Plaintiff’s counsel’s timesheets reflecting 319 hours billed, counsel’s hourly billing rates of $1,500, $1,100, $750, and $500, and a 1.5 multiplier.¿ (See Younessi Decl., ¶ 14; Ortiz Decl., ¶ 6; Nakahhiro Decl., ¶ 5; Balderas Decl., ¶ 4; Ex. 1.)  Defendant lodges several challenges to the fees requested, including the reasonableness of the billings rates and specific entries.

 

The determination of reasonable amount of attorney fees is within the sound discretion of trial courts. (PLCM Group v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1095; Akins v. Enterprise Rent-A-Car Co. (2000) 79 Cal.App.4th 1127, 1134.) “The determination of what constitutes a reasonable fee generally ‘begins with the ‘lodestar,’ i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate….’”¿ “[T]he lodestar is the basic fee for comparable legal services in the community; it may be adjusted by the court based on factors including, as relevant herein, (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved, (2) the skill displayed in presenting them, (3) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys, (4) the contingent nature of the fee award….”¿ (Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 154.)¿ In setting the hourly rate for an attorney fees award, courts are entitled to consider the rate of “‘fees customarily charged by that attorney and others in the community for similar work.’”¿ (Bihun v. AT&T Information Systems, Inc. (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th 976, 997 [affirming rate of $450 per hour], overruled on other grounds by Lakin v. Watkins Associated Indus. (1993) 6 Cal.4th 644, 664.) The burden is on the party seeking attorney fees to prove reasonableness of the fees.¿ (Center for Biological Diversity v. County of San Bernardino (2010) 188 Cal.App.4th 603, 615.)¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿ 

A trial court has broad discretion in determining the amount of a reasonable attorney’s fee award which will not be overturned absent a “manifest abuse of discretion, a prejudicial error of law, or necessary findings not supported by substantial evidence.”¿ (Bernardi v. County of Monterey (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 1379, 1393-94.)¿ The court need not explain its calculation of the amount of attorney’s fees awarded in detail; identifying the factors considered in arriving at the amount will suffice.¿ (Ventura v. ABM Industries Inc. (2012) 212 Cal.App.4th 258, 274-75.) “Attorney’s fees need not¿be apportioned when incurred for representation on an issue common to both a cause of action in¿which fees are proper and one in which they are not allowed.” (Reynolds Metals Co. v. Alperson (1979) 25 Cal.3d 124, 129–30; see also Abdallah v. United Sav. Bank (1996) 43 Cal.App.4th 1101, 1111 (noting the impracticability and impossibility to separate compensable and noncompensable time units where it was reasonable for a trial court to find various claims were inextricably intertwined).)¿¿¿ 

¿ 

After reviewing the motion and supporting evidence, the court finds fees in the sum of $80,000.00 is a reasonable award.¿ This amount represents the fees Plaintiff incurred without the addition of any multiplier.  The court declines to award any multiplier because this case did not present complex or novel issues.  ¿¿¿ 

 

IV.       CONCLUSION 

 

The motion is GRANTED IN PART.  Plaintiff is award attorneys’ fees in the sum of $80,000.00. 

 

Plaintiff to give notice, unless waived.

 

 

Dated:   September 22, 2026

  Judge of the Superior Court

Case Number: 25STCV20856    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:       September 21, 2026                                                TRIAL DATE:  Not set

                                                          

CASE:                         Carlos Moz v. Solaris Trading Corporation, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV20856

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO QUASH OVERBROAD SUBPOENA ISSUED BY

DEFENDANT TO J & J TIRES AND WHEELS, INC. AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS IN THE AMOUNT OF $2,800.00

 

MOVING PARTY:               Plaintiff Carlos Moz

 

RESPONDING PARTY:     Defendant Solaris Trading Corporation

 

 

I.         BACKGROUND

 

            On July 15, 2025, Plaintiff, Carlos Moz, commenced this action against defendants Solaris Trading Corporation dba Grand American Tires (“Solaris” or “Defendant”) and Stefan Bostanian for FEHA violations, wage and hour violations, and wrongful termination.

 

On April 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants which removed all but the wage and hour claims for failure to pay wages due (Labor Code §§ 201, 1182.12, 1194, 1194.2), failure to pay minimum wages (Labor Code §§ 1182.12, 1194, 1194.2, 1197), failure to pay overtime compensation (Labor Code §§ 510, 1194), failure to provide rest breaks (Labor Code §§ 226.7, 512), and failure to provide itemized wage and hour statements (Labor Code §§ 226 et seq.). 

 

            On June 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed this motion to quash Solaris’s subpoena issued to Plaintiff’s subsequent employer, J & J Tires and Wheels, Inc. (“J & J”).  Plaintiff seeks sanctions against Solaris and its counsel of record.

 

            On September 3, 2026, Solaris filed an opposition.

 

            On September 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

The motion was heard on September 16, 2026.  In advance of the hearing, the court issued a tentative ruling.  The court exercised its discretion not to consider Solaris’s opposition as untimely and granted the motion.  After oral argument, the court continued the hearing to September 21, 2026 to consider Solaris’s opposition and Plaintiff’s reply.  Having considered the opposition and reply, the court now rules as follows.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

If a subpoena requires the attendance of a witness or the production of books, documents, electronically stored information, or other things before a court, or at the trial of an issue therein, or at the taking of a deposition, the court, upon motion reasonably made by any person described in subdivision (b), or upon the court's own motion after giving counsel notice and an opportunity to be heard, may make an order quashing the subpoena entirely, modifying it, or directing compliance with it upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare, including protective orders. In addition, the court may make any other order as may be appropriate to protect the person from unreasonable or oppressive demands such as unreasonable violations of the right of privacy.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1.)¿¿ 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿ 

In making an order pursuant to motion made under¿subdivision (c) of Section 1987¿or under¿Section 1987.1, the court may in its discretion award the amount of the reasonable expenses incurred in making or opposing the motion, including reasonable attorney’s fees, if the court finds the motion was made or opposed in bad faith or without substantial justification or that one or more of the requirements of the subpoena was oppressive.”¿ (Code of Civ. Proc. § 1987.2.)¿ 

III.      DISCUSSION

 

            Plaintiff moves to quash the subpoena to J & J on the grounds it “(1) constitutes an impermissible fishing expedition, (2) seeks to impinge on Plaintiff’s and third parties’ privacy rights, and (3) seeks documents which are not directly relevant to this action ….”  (Mot., p. 2:22-24.)  The court agrees in considerable part with Plaintiff’s third argument.  The subpoena seeks the following information:

 

PURSUANT TO THE ATTACHED SUBPOENA, PLEASE PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING DOCUMENTS, WRITINGS AND ELECTRONICALLY STORED INFORMATION PERTAINING FROM JANUARY 1, 2022 THROUGH THE PRESENT REQUESTED BELOW:

 

1. Mr. Moz’s resume;

2. Mr. Moz’s curriculum vitae;

3. Mr. Moz’s personnel file;

4. Mr. Moz’s independent contractor file;

5. Mr. Moz’s applications for employment;

6. Mr. Moz’s applications for an independent contractor relationship;

7. Mr. Moz’s earnings records;

8. Background checks of Mr. Moz;

9. All w-2s issues to Mr. Moz;

10. All 1099s issued to Mr. Moz;

11. Mr. Moz’s termination records;

12. All contracts entered into between J & J Tires and Carlos Moz;

13. resumes;

14. payroll records;

15. contracts;

16. job descriptions for all positions worked;

17. timesheets;

18. unemployment claims;

19. response to unemployment claims;

20. termination of employment;

21. complaints of failure to pay wages;

22. complaints of unpaid overtime;

23. complaints of failure to provide meal periods;

24. complaints of failure to provide rest periods;

25. reasonable accommodations;

26. requests for accommodation;

27. work restrictions;

28. medical leaves to seek treatment;

29. doctor’s authorizations to leave work;

30. doctor's orders to be off work;

31. doctor's releases to return to work;

32. disabilities;

33. training;

34. vocational assessments;

35. all communications with Mr. Moz pertaining to job assignments and/or referrals.

 

(Carvajal Decl., ¶ 7, Ex. 1.)  Following the dismissal of Plaintiff’s FEHA and wrongful termination claims, only Plaintiff’s wage and hour claims remain.  On its face, the subpoena does not seek any information relevant to Plaintiff’s for failure to pay wages due, failure to pay minimum wages, failure to pay overtime compensation, failure to provide rest breaks, and failure to provide itemized wage and hour statements, or any defenses thereto.  Instead, the subpoena appears tailored to discover information regarding claims that are no longer at issue (e.g., termination of employment, reasonable accommodations, requests for accommodation).  The subpoena does not seek relevant information and is therefore overbroad.  Having so found, the court will quash the subpoena.  Solaris does not present any argument directing a different result.

           

IV.       CONCLUSION

           

The motion to quash is GRANTED.  Defendant and its counsel of record are ordered to pay sanctions in the sum of $1,260 within 30 days of this order.

 

Moving party to give notice, unless waived.

Case Number: 25STCV28582    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:       September 22, 2026                                 TRIAL DATE:  January 18, 2028

                                                          

CASE:                         Chris Friend v. San Marino Street Properties, LLC, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                 25STCV28582

 

 

PLAINTIFF’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES, SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES, REQUESTS FOR DOCUMENTS, AND REQUESTS FOR ADMISSIONS

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

Plaintiff Chris Friend (“Plaintiff”) brings this premises liability and habitability action against defendants San Marino Street Properties, LLC (“SMSP”), Kamyar Shabani (“Shabani”), and Optimus Properties, LLC (“Optimus”) (collectively, “Defendants”), arising from Plaintiff’s tenancy at 1025 N. Serrano Avenue, Los Angeles, California 90029 (the “Property”).

 

On June 30, July 2, and July 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed ten motions to compel further responses as follows:(1) Shabani’s further responses to Set One of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, Request for Production of Documents, and Requests for Admissions; (2) SMSP’s further responses to Set One of Form Interrogatories, Special Interrogatories, Request for Production of Documents, and Requests for Admissions; and (3) Optimus’s further responses to  Set One of Special Interrogatories and Request for Production of Documents.  Plaintiff requests sanctions against each defendant and their counsel of record.

 

On August 26, September 1, September 4, and September 8, 2026, Defendants filed oppositions.  Defendants request sanctions against Plaintiff.

 

            On September 2 and September 15, 2026, Plaintiff filed replies.

 

            The court considers together the motions.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

           

Under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.300, 2031.310, and 2033.290, parties may move for a further response to interrogatories, requests for production of documents, and requests for admissions where an answer to the discovery requests are evasive or incomplete or where an objection is without merit or too general.¿¿ 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(a)(3) further requires that all motions or responses involving further discovery contain a separate statement with the text of each request, the response, and a statement of factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses. 

 

Monetary Sanctions¿¿ 

¿¿ 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030 is a general statute authorizing the Court to impose discovery sanctions for “misuse of the discovery process,” which includes (without limitation) a variety of conduct such as: making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to discovery; and unsuccessfully and without substantial justification making or opposing a motion to compel or limit discovery.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010.)¿¿¿ 

¿¿ 

If sanctions are sought, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.040 requires that the notice specify the identity of the person against whom sanctions are sought and the type of sanction requested, that the motion be supported in the points and authorities, and the facts be set forth in a declaration supporting the amount of any monetary sanction.¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿ 

Sanctions shall be awarded against any party, person or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further responses, unless the Court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of sanctions unjust.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(d); 2031.310(h); 2033.290(d).)¿ 

 

III.      DISCUSSION

           

            A.  Timeliness

 

Defendants argue the motions are untimely because they served verified responses on March 30, 2026, and there is no written agreement to extend the time for Plaintiff to brings these motions.  Notice of the motions must be given within 45 days of service of the verified response, otherwise, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(c); 2031.310(c); 2033.290(c).)¿ The argument is not well taken.  Defense counsel agreed, in writing, to extend the time for Plaintiff to file motions to compel.  The extension of time was tied to responding to Plaintiff’s May 4, 2026 meet and confer letter.  Defendants never responded.  Accordingly, the deadline to file this motion has not run.  The court finds the motions are timely.[1]

 

B.  Meet and Confer

 

Defendants next argue the motions should be denied because Plaintiff did not meet and confer as to every discovery request at issue.  A motion to compel further responses to discovery must be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.300(b)(1); 2031.310(b)(2); 2033.290(b)(1).)  The court has reviewed Plaintiff’s declarations and is satisfied that he attempted to resolve informally the majority of the issues prior to filing this motion.  Because the meet and confer issue is cabined to select discovery requests, the court will address the challenge where appropriate.  

 

A.  Form Interrogatories (FROG)

 

1.  Shabani

 

FROG No. 2.5:  GRANTED.  Shabani’s objections lack merit.  A further response is warranted.

 

FROG No. 12.6:  GRANTED. Shabani’s objections lack merit.  A further response is warranted.

 

FROG No. 12.7:  GRANTED. Shabani’s objections lack merit.  A further response is warranted.

 

FROG No. 17.1:  GRANTED as to subsections (b), (c) and (d).  Shabani asserted meritless objections and incomplete responses.  Further responses are warranted.

 

2.  SMSP

 

FROG No. 12.4:  GRANTED.  SMSP’s objections lack merit.  A further response is warranted.

 

FROG No. 12.6:  GRANTED. SMSP’s objections lack merit.  A further response is warranted.

 

FROG No. 12.7:  GRANTED. SMSP’s objections lack merit.  A further response is warranted.

 

FROG No. 17.1:  DENIED.  Plaintiff did not accurately state SMSP’s response to this request.

 

B.  Special Interrogatories (SROG)

 

1.  Shabani

 

SROG Nos. 1-8, 10:  GRANTED.  Shabani asserted meritless objections.  Further responses are warranted. 

 

2.  SMSP

 

SROG No. 5:  DENIED.  Plaintiff did not attempt to meet and confer on this request.

 

SROG No. 7:  GRANTED.  SMSP asserted meritless objections.  A further response is warranted.

 

SROG No. 8:  DENIED.  Plaintiff did not accurately state SMSP’s response to this request.

 

3.  Optimus

 

SROG No. 4:  GRANTED.  Shabani asserted meritless objections.  A further response is warranted. 

 

C.  Request for Production of Documents (RFP)

 

1.  Shabani

 

RFP Nos. 1-5:  GRANTED. Shabani asserted meritless objections.  Further responses are warranted.

 

2.  SMSP

 

RFP No. 1:  DENIED.  Plaintiff did not accurately state the correct discovery request.

 

RFP No. 7:  GRANTED. SMSP asserted meritless objections.  A further response is warranted.

 

3.  Optimus

 

RFP Nos. 1, 2, 3, 6, 7: GRANTED.  Optimus asserted meritless objections.  Further responses are warranted.

 

D.  Requests for Admissions (RFA)

 

1.  Shabani

 

RFA Nos. 1-4, 6-13:  GRANTED.  Shabani asserted meritless objections.  Further responses are warranted. 

 

2.  SMSP

 

RFA Nos. 3, 5:  GRANTED.  SMSP asserted meritless objections.  Further responses are warranted. 

 

E.  Monetary Sanctions

 

            Plaintiff requests sanctions against Defendants and their counsel of record.  Given the court’s ruling, the court finds sanctions are warranted.  Because Plaintiff’s motions and supporting papers are poorly drafted, sanctions are imposed in reduced sums as follows: against Shabani and his counsel of record in the sum of $1,000; against SMSP and its counsel of record in the sum of $1,000; against Optimus and its counsel of record in the sum of $500.

 

IV.       CONCLUSION        

 

            The motions to compel further are GRANTED and DENIED as indicated herein.  Defendants are ordered to provide further responses to Plaintiff’s discovery requests within 15 days of this order.

 

            Shabani and his counsel of record are ordered to pay, jointly and severally, sanctions in the sum of $1,000.  SMSP and its counsel of record are ordered to pay, jointly and severally, sanctions in the sum of $1,000; Optimus and its counsel are ordered to pay, jointly and severally, sanctions in the sum of $500.  Sanctions are to be paid within 30 days of this order.

Case Number: 25STCV34888    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 407

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Brock T. Hammond, Department 407

 

 

HEARING DATE:       September 22, 2026                                                TRIAL DATE:  Not set

                                                          

CASE:                         Riehl Construction Consulting, LLC v. 1231 Shadow Hills LLC

 

CASE NO.:                     25STCV34888

 

 

DEFENDANT 1231 SHADOW HILLS LLC aka SHADOW HILL LLC’S MOTION TO EXPUNGE THE NOTICE OF PENDING ACTION UNDER CIVIL CODE AND TO REMOVE MECHANICS LIEN FILED BY PLAINTIFF

 

MOVING PARTY:               Defendant 1231 Shadow Hills LLC aka Shadow Hill LLC

 

RESPONDING PARTY:      Plaintiff Riehl Construction Consulting, LLC

 

I.         INTRODUCTION

 

Plaintiff Riehl Construction Consulting, LLC (“Riehl”) brings this action to recover the balance of $63,384,77 for providing work, labor, services, equipment on the property located at 1231 Shadow Hill Way, Beverly Hills, California 90210 (the “Property”) pursuant to an agreement with defendant 1231 Shadow Hills LLC aka Shadow Hill LLC (“Shadow Hills”).

 

On September 9, 2025, Riehl recorded a mechanics lien on the Property.

 

            O June 25, 2026, Shadow Hills filed this motion to expunge the notice of pending action and for an order removing the mechanics lien filed by Riehl.  Shadow Hills seeks an award of attorney’s fees.

 

On September 9, 2026, Riehl filed an opposition.

 

On September 15, 2026, Shadow Hills filed a reply.

 

II.        EVIDENTIARY OBJECTIONS

 

            Riehl asserts twelve objections to the declaration of Maggie Miracle.  The objections are OVERRULED.

 

            Shadow Hills asserts two objections to the declaration of Brian Friedrich.  The objections are OVERRULED.

III.      LEGAL STANDARD

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) section 405.30, at any time after a notice of pendency of action has been recorded, any party with an interest in the real property may apply to the court to expunge the notice.  A lis pendens may be expunged either under CCP section 405.31 if the pleadings do not contain a real property claim, or under CCP section 405.32 if the court finds that the party claiming the lis pendens has not established by a preponderance of the evidence the probable validity of the real property claim.  Under CCP section 405.30, the party claiming the lis pendens has the burden of proof under sections 405.31 and 405.32. The moving party has the burden of demonstrating that their pleadings contain a real property claim and that the probable validity of their real property claim can be established by a preponderance of the evidence.¿ 

 

IV.       DISCUSSION

 

Shadow Hills seeks to expunge the mechanics lien because (1) Riehl did not comply with Civil Code section 8146(a)(7), and (2) Riehl is not a person entitled to record a mechanics lien under Civil Code section 8400 and 8402.

 

The court finds the first argument is meritorious.  A claim of mechanic’s lien shall be in a verified written statement and shall include, among other things, “[a] proof of service affidavit completed and signed by the person serving a copy of the claim of mechanics lien” that shows “the date, place, and manner of service, and facts showing that the service was made in accordance with this section. The affidavit shall show the name and address of the owner or reputed owner upon whom the copy of the claim of mechanics lien was served pursuant to paragraphs (1) or (2) of subdivision (c), and the title or capacity in which the person or entity was served.”  (Civ. Code, § 8416 (a)(7).)  Civil Code section 8146 further provides, “[a] copy of the claim of mechanics lien, which includes the Notice of Mechanics Lien required by paragraph (8) of subdivision (a), shall be served on the owner or reputed owner. Service shall be made as follows: [¶] (1) For an owner or reputed owner to be notified who resides in or outside this state, by registered mail, certified mail, or first-class mail, evidenced by a certificate of mailing, postage prepaid, addressed to the owner or reputed owner at the owner’s or reputed owner’s residence or place of business address or at the address shown by the building permit on file with the authority issuing a building permit for the work, or as otherwise provided in Section 8174.”  (Civ. Code, § 8416 (c)(1).)  “Failure to serve the copy of the claim of mechanics lien as prescribed by this section, including the Notice of Mechanics Lien required by paragraph (8) of subdivision (a), shall cause the claim of mechanics lien to be unenforceable as a matter of law.” (Civ. Code, § 8416 (e).) 

 

Here, Shadow Hills is the owner of the Property and its place of business is 865 Comstock Ave, 11e, Los Angeles, CA 90024.  However, the proof of service attached to the mechanics lien claim is addressed to 1231 Shadow Hills LLC, c/o Maggie Miracle, 869 Tione Road, Los Angeles, CA 90077.  (See Pierce Decl., Ex C.)  Shadow Hills establishes that the Tione Road address is neither its place of business nor the address shown by the building permit on file.  (Miracle Decl., ¶¶ 9-10.)  The mechanics lien is unenforceable as a matter of law.  Having so concluded, the court does not address Shadow Hills’ second argument.

Attorney’s Fees¿ 

¿ 

Shadow Hills seeks attorney’s fees and costs in the sum of $19,577.75 for bringing this motion.¿ “Section 405.38 changed the law by requiring (as opposed to merely authorizing) a court to award attorney's fees and costs to the party prevailing on a motion to expunge, unless the other party acted with substantial justification or other circumstances would make such an award unjust.”¿ (Shoker v. Superior Ct. of Alameda Cnty. (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 271, 280, italics in original.)¿

 

Here, Shadow Hills is the prevailing party.¿ The court finds an award of attorney’s fees is therefore warranted.  Accordingly, the court awards attorney’s fees in the reduced sum of $1,940.75.

 

V.        CONCLUSION

 

The motion to expunge the mechanics lien is GRANTED.  Defendant 1231 Shadow Hills LLC is awarded attorney’s fees in the sum of $1,940.75. 

 

            Defendant is ordered to give notice, unless waived.
