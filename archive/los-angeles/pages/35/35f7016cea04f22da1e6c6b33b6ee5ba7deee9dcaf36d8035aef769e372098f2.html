Case #25CHCV02316

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE A CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

Motion filed on 6/15/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Defendants PPG Industries, Inc. and Sierracin/Sylmar Corporation

RESPONDING PARTY: all other parties

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED: An order granting Defendants PPG Industries, Inc. and Sierracin/Sylmar Corporation leave to file a cross-complaint.

 

RULING: The motion is granted. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of injuries sustained by Plaintiff Gustavo A. Castro Arana (Plaintiff) on 7/11/23 at or near 12780 San Fernando Road, Sylmar, California 91342 (the Premises).  (Complaint ¶8).  Plaintiff was injured while from an explosion of polytetramethylene ether glycol (PTMEG) from a hose that became disconnected, dislodged, burst, and/or loose while not properly cleared of PTMEG and this caused Plaintiff to become covered in PTMEG (the Incident).  Id.  While covered in the PTMEG at the Premises, there was not an emergency shower or other proper facility or procedure in place to clean the PTMEG off Plaintiff or otherwise render aid to him.  (Id. ¶9).  As a result, Plaintiff suffered serious personal injuries, including but not limited to chemical bums all over his body.  Id.

 

At the time of the Incident, the Premises were owned, leased, occupied, managed, supervised, and/or controlled by Defendants PPG Industries, Inc. (PPG), Eugene Nasch, individually and as Trustee of the Nasch Family Trust dated July 19, 1991 (Mr. Nasch), Sierracin/Sylmar Corporation (Sierra Sylmar) (collectively, the Premises Defendants), and/or DOES 1 to 100.  (Complaint ¶11).  Plaintiff had been hired by Defendant Ventura Transfer Company (Ventura Transfer) and DOES 1 to 10 (the Ventura Defendants) to pick up and transport the PTMEG to the Premises and then participate in its transfer.  (Id. ¶12).  Plaintiff alleges that prior to the Incident, the Ventura Defendants did not provide Plaintiff with proper training, instruction, warning in connection with the aforementioned and Ventura Transfer also failed to provide proper personal protective equipment (PPE) to Plaintiff.  (Id. ¶12). 

 

Plaintiff further alleges that the Premises Defendants undertook the duty of informing Plaintiff as to when it was safe and appropriate for the hose to be disconnected in connection with the Incident and the Premises Defendants negligently and unreasonably made affirmative misrepresentations that the hose was safe for disconnection when it was not.  (Complaint ¶13).  As a result, Plaintiff was injured from an explosion of PTMEG from a hose that became disconnected, dislodged, burst, and/or loose while not properly cleared of PTMEG and this caused Plaintiff to become covered in PTMEG.  Id. 

 

On 7/2/25, Plaintiff filed this action against Ventura Transfer and the Premises Defendants for: (1) General Negligence (against all Defendants), (2) Premises Liability (against the Premises Defendants), (3) Negligent Undertaking (against all Defendants) and (4) Direct Civil Action for California Labor Code Section 3706, et seq. (against Ventura Transfer).  On 11/20/25, PPG and Sierra Sylmar filed a joint answer to the complaint.  On 3/11/26, Ventura Transfer answered the complaint and filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff.  On 4/23/26, Ventura Transfer filed a First Amended Cross-Complaint against Plaintiff.  On 5/26/26, Plaintiff answered Ventura Transfer’s cross-complaint.

 

On 6/15/26, PPG and Sierria Sylmar filed and served the instant motion seeking an order granting Defendants PPG Industries, Inc. and Sierracin/Sylmar Corporation leave to file a cross-complaint.  The motion is unopposed. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

CCP 428.10 provides:

 

“A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint or cross-complaint may file a cross-complaint setting forth either or both of the following:

(a) Any cause of action he has against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him. Nothing in this subdivision authorizes the filing of a cross-complaint against the plaintiff in an action commenced under Title 7 (commencing with Section 1230.010) of Part 3.

(b) Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.”

 

CCP 428.50 provides:

 

“(a) A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint.

(b) Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.

(c) A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint except one filed within the time specified in subdivision (a) or (b). Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.”

 

Here, PPG and Sierra Sylmar seek to file a cross-complaint against Ventura Transfer; Gantrade Corporation and Roes 1-50 for: (1) Express Indemnity; (2) Equitable Indemnity; (3) Contribution; (4) Apportionment of Fault and (5) Declaratory Relief.  The proposed cross-complaint arises out of the same transaction or occurrence as Plaintiff’s complaint.  CCP 428.10(b).  It is in the interests of justice to allow PPG  and Sierra Sylmar to bring their cross-claims in this action.

 

CONCLUSION

 

The  motion is granted.  PPG and Sierra Sylmar are ordered to separately file their cross-complaint which is attached to the Stern declaration as Exhibit A. 

 

In violation of CRC 3.1110(a), counsel for PPG and Sierra Sylmar failed to electronically bookmark the exhibit attached to the motion.  Counsel for the parties are warned that failure to comply with this rule in the future may result in matters being continued so that papers can be resubmitted in compliance with the  rule, papers not being considered and/or the imposition of sanctions.

MINOR’S COMPROMISE

 

Petition filed on 6/18/26.

 

MINOR: Brooklyn Ramirez

GAL: Julie Pomposo-Ramirez

DEFENDANTS: All Site Logistics, LLC and Andres Rosales

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION: This action arises out of a motor vehicle collision that occurred on 10/20/23.  Minor was a passenger in a vehicle with her mother/GAL/co-plaintiff.  As a result of the collision, Minor suffered low back pain.  Minor received care in the emergency room, had x-rays taken and received chiropractic care.  Minor has completely recovered. 

 

SETTLEMENT: Defendants All Site Logistics, LLC and Andres Rosales to pay $15,000 for minor’s claims

 

ATTORNEY FEES: $3,750.00

 

MEDICAL EXPENSES: $7,735.00 (total); $1,357.00 (paid); $3,878.00 (reduction); $2,500.00 (to be paid from settlement)

 

COSTS: $160.51

 

AMOUNT TO BE PAID TO MINOR: $8,589.49 to be placed in a blocked account

 

RULING: 

 

Defendants have not appeared in this action and there is no evidence that they have even been served with the summons and complaint.  It is not clear if this action was filed solely to have this petition approved.  If that is the case, a petition should have been filed in the probate court.  See LASC, Local Rule 4.115(a)(1) citing Probate Code 2505(b) and 3500.  Also, there is no proof of service for the petition.  Even if a proof of service was filed, unless Defendants were personally served, there is no way for the Court to confirm any address where Defendants may have been served.  Further, since Defendants have not appeared in this action, the Court does not have jurisdiction over them to enforce the settlement, if they do not pay it. 

 

There also appears to be a minor procedural defect in the petition.  No.17.c. indicates that the attorney has not received any other attorney fees and No.17.f. indicates that the attorney does not expect to receive any other attorney’s fees.  However, in all likelihood, the attorney will receive a percentage of the co-plaintiff’s settlement of $37,500.00 as compensation.  (See Petition, No.11.b.(5); Attachment 17e).

HEARING ON CLAIMS TO DETERMINE DISBURSEMENT OF SURPLUS FUNDS

 

Petition filed 4/2/26.

 

 

On 4/2/26, Quality Loan Service Corp. (Petitioner) filed a Petition and Declaration Regarding Unresolved Claims and Deposit of Undistributed Surplus Proceeds of Trustee’s Sale. 

 

On 4/10/26, this Court entered an order to deposit surplus funds in the amount of $63,905.38 pursuant to Civil Code 2924j(c) and (d) and discharged Petitioner as Trustee.

 

On 5/15/26, the Court gave notice to all interested parties that a hearing on claims to determine disbursement of surplus funds was set for 7/14/26.

 

The funds are surplus funds after the 10/21/25 non-judicial foreclosure sale of property located at 9555 Woodman Avenue, Unit 11, Los Angeles, CA 91331 (the Property).  The Deed of Trust on the Property was executed by Jo Ann Tovar.  After payment of the foreclosing beneficiary’s unpaid debt, Petitioner identified two junior liens: (1) a deed of trust in favor of Bank of America, N.A. recorded on 5/4/07 and (2) a lien in favor of The Woodman VII Homeowners Association recorded on 3/15/13.  After the foregoing junior liens are satisfied, Jo Ann Tovar, the owner of the Property just prior to the foreclosure sale is entitled to any remaining funds. 

 

After providing notice, Petitioner received no claim from Bank of America,  N.A.  Since Petitioner was unable to determine how to distribute the remaining funds, it filed the instant petition. 

 

On 6/16/26, The Woodman VII Homeowners Association filed a claim to $31,194.29 of the surplus funds on deposit based on delinquent homeowners association dues and fees related to the Property.  No other claims have been filed. 

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that The Woodman VII Homeowners Association is entitled to $31,194.29 of the funds on deposit to satisfy their lien. 

 

The remaining $32,711.09 of the funds on deposit will escheat according to law.

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE A SECOND AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

Motion filed on 6/16/26.

 

MOVING PARTY: Plaintiff Leya Kennedy

RESPONDING PARTY: Defendants Emily Sara Karp and Gilbert Karp

NOTICE: ok

 

RELIEF REQUESTED:  An order granting Plaintiff Leya Kennedy leave to file a Second Amended Complaint. 

 

RULING: The motion is granted. 

 

SUMMARY OF FACTS & PROCEDURAL HISTORY

 

This action arises out of a traffic collision which occurred on 12/6/24.  Plaintiff Leya Kennedy (Plaintiff) contends that on 12/6/24, at approximately 6:45 p.m., Defendant Emily Sarah Karp (Emily) was operating her vehicle southbound on Interstate 5 at speeds of approximately 95 to 100 miles per hour with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.20 percent.  Emily crashed the front of her vehicle into the rear of Plaintiff’s vehicle propelling Plaintiff's vehicle into a concrete barrier.  California Highway Patrol Officer William Leisorek responded to the scene, observed  signs of intoxication, and placed Emily under arrest after she failed field sobriety tests.  Emily  was subsequently charged with felony DUI causing injury under Vehicle Code sections 23153(a) and 23153(b), to which she pleaded nolo contendere and was convicted.  As a result of Defendant's conduct, Plaintiff sustained injuries, including airbag burns and abrasions to her left leg, right leg, right hand, and left wrist, a diffuse traumatic brain injury, acute musculoskeletal injuries to her cervical and lumbar spine requiring interventional pain management, radiculopathy, and post-traumatic stress disorder.  Plaintiff was 20 years old when the crash occurred.

 

On 1/12/26, Plaintiff filed this action against Emily and Defendant Gilbert Karp (Gilbert) (collectively, Defendants) for: (1) Negligence, (2) Negligence Per Se – California Vehicle Code 22350; and (3) Punitive Damages.  On 2/19/26,  Plaintiff filed her First Amended Complaint asserting the same claims.  On 3/12/26, Defendants filed and served a Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint related to punitive damages.  The motion to strike is scheduled for hearing on 7/15/26, the day after the hearing on the instant motion.  Since the filing of the First Amended Complaint, Plaintiff contends that she had discovered new evidence which support her punitive damage allegations, the liability of proposed Defendant Maritza Karp (Maritza) and causes of action for negligence and negligent entrustment.   (See Staskus Decl.).  The proposed Second Amended Complaint alleges causes of action for: (1)  Negligence against all Defendants and Does 1-50 and (2) Negligent Entrustment against Gilbert and Maritza and Does 1-50. 

Despite meet and confer efforts, the parties could not come to an agreement regarding allowing Plaintiff to file the proposed Second Amended Complaint and taking the motion to strike off calendar.  (Staskus Decl.).  Therefore, on 6/16/26, Plaintiff filed (served on 6/15/26) the instant motion seeking an order granting Plaintiff leave to file a Second Amended Complaint.  Defendants have not opposed or otherwise responded to the instant motion. 

 

ANALYSIS

 

Leave to amend pleadings is to be liberally granted so that cases may be decided on their merits.  See CCP 473(a); CCP 576.  Unless there is prejudice to the opposing side, it is an abuse of discretion to deny leave to amend.  See Higgins (1981) 123 CA3d 558, 564-565; Atkinson (2003) 109 CA4th 739.  Here, there is no evidence of undue delay in seeking leave to amend and/or that Defendants will be prejudiced by the granting of the motion. 

 

CONCLUSION

 

The motion is granted.  Plaintiff is ordered to separately file the Second Amended Complaint which is attached to the declaration of John M. Staskus as Exhibit F.

 

Due to the granting of the instant motion and the filing of the Second Amended Complaint, Defendants Emily Sarah Karp and Gilber Karp’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint scheduled for hearing on 7/15/26 is advanced to 7/14/26 and placed off calendar as moot.  The granting of the instant motion does not preclude Defendants’ right to challenge the Second Amended Complaint by way of demurrer, motion to strike, etc., if they so choose.
