TENTATIVE RULING

 

GISEN C SAWAMURA

 

         vs.

 

FCA US, LLC, et al.  

 

 Case No.: 24STCV25369

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 13, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to tax costs is DENIED.

 

On 9/30/2024, Plaintiff Gisen C. Sawamura (Plaintiff) filed suit against FCA US, LLC, and LAX Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram (LAX CDJR), alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

            On 5/10/2026, Plaintiff moved to tax costs.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff moves to tax: Line 11 in the amount of $13,414.00; tax Line 14 in the amount of $1,140.68; and tax Line 15 in the amount of $150.15, for a total reduction of $14,704.83.

 

            As for filing and motion fees, Plaintiff argues:

 

Defendant does not distinguish between costs appropriately categorized under Line 1 (filing and motion fees) and Line 14 (fees for electronic filing or service). However, a review of Defendant’s Memorandum and attached invoices show that these costs were for electronic filing. Here, the Court neither required nor ordered electronic filing. It is also evident from the invoices attached to Defendant’s Memorandum that most, if not all, of these filings were done same-day, needlessly incurring rush charges that Defendant should not be entitled to recover. Vendor service fees, processing surcharges, and convenience fees are not court-imposed and are not recoverable under section 1033.5(a)(14)

 

            (Motion, 2: 17-25.)

 

            If an item of cost is specifically allowable under the Code, the party challenging the cost bears the burden of showing that the cost is not reasonable or necessary. But in the case of discretionary costs which are objected to, the burden of proof lies with the party requesting the item of costs to demonstrate such costs were necessary and reasonable. (Ladas v. Cal. State Automobile Association (1993) 19 Cal. App. 4th 761, 774.)

 

Here, Defendant submitted detailed invoices, supporting documentation, and the accompanying Declaration establishing that the challenged costs were incurred were necessary to defend the case. (Dizon Decl. ¶ 6.)

 

            As for deposition costs, Plaintiff argues:

 

Defendant's Memorandum of Costs is accompanied by no court reporter receipts, no videographer bills, no cancellation fee statements, and no declaration of any kind. Defendant merely attaches an invoice with nothing to indicate on what date these were meant to have occurred. Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5(a)(3) authorizes recovery of deposition costs only for costs actually and reasonably incurred. Without any sufficient documentation, it is impossible to determine what deposition this charge relates to; what specific services were rendered; or whether the amount claimed was actually incurred.

 

            (Motion, 4: 2-8.)

 

Here, Defendant submitted detailed invoices, supporting documentation, and the accompanying Declaration establishing that the challenged costs were incurred were necessary to defend the case. (Dizon Decl. ¶ 6.)

 

As for court reporter fees, Plaintiff argues:

 

Although Defendant attached invoices from reporting vendors, the submitted materials include charges beyond ordinary recoverable transcript costs, including expedited processing, litigation support services, electronic delivery fees, exhibit management charges, and other administrative add-ons. Court reporter fees are recoverable only to the extent they were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation. CCP § 1033.5(a)(11)

           

            (Motion, 4: 12-17.)

 

Here, Defendant submitted detailed invoices, supporting documentation, and the accompanying Declaration establishing that the challenged costs were incurred were necessary to defend the case. (Dizon Decl. ¶ 6.)

 

As for other costs, Plaintiff argues:

 

Parking, mileage reimbursement, and meals are the personal travel and subsistence expenses of defense counsel. These are expressly listed as non-allowable under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5(b)(2), which excludes compensation for the travel expenses of attorneys. Because the nature of these costs has not been disclosed and no supporting documentation has been provided, Defendant has entirely failed to meet its burden.

 

            (Motion, 5: 9-13.)

 

Here, Defendant argues that the travel-related expenses incurred by Defendant’s trial counsel were necessary. The Court agrees. As noted, Defense counsel’s travel to appear for the Final Status Conference (May 7, 2026) and Trial (May 11, 2026) was a necessary expense as Defense counsel appeared in person for such hearings, which is required by the Court. Similarly, the mediation for which Defendant submitted invoices was undertaken in furtherance of resolution efforts and was reasonable and necessary. The Court strongly encourages parties to attempt informal resolution in all instances.

 

            Based on the foregoing, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion to tax costs.

 

It is so ordered.

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

GUILLERMO PLAZA

 

         vs.

 

FCA US, LLC, et al.  

 

 Case No.: 24STCV32313 

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 13, 2026

 

FCA’s motion for summary judgment is GRANTED.

 

On 12/19/2024, Plaintiff Guillermo Plaza (Plaintiff) filed suit against FCA US, LLC, and Bravo Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram of Alhambra (Bravo DCJR), alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

            On 5/13/2026, Defendant FCA US, LLC (Defendant or FCA moved for summary judgment, or in the alternative, summary adjudication of Plaintiff’s Complaint.

 

            The motion is unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendants argue that Plaintiff cannot establish any of his claims because: (1) as to the first cause of action, he cannot show there was a nonconformity in the vehicle that Ford failed to repair under warranty after a reasonable number of repair attempts; (2) as to the second cause of action, Plaintiff cannot establish that any warranty repair took more than 30 days; (3) as to the third cause of action, Plaintiff cannot establish that Ford failed to provide sufficient literature and replacement parts to authorized service and repair facilities; and (4) as to the fourth cause of action, Plaintiff cannot establish that the vehicle was unfit for ordinary purposes during the implied warranty period.

 

As to the first cause of action, to establish a Song-Beverly violation under Civil Code section 1793.2(d), a plaintiff must prove: (1) the Vehicle had a nonconformity covered by the express warranty that substantially impaired its use, value, or safety; (2) the Vehicle was presented to an authorized representative for repair; and (3) the manufacturer or its representative failed to repair the nonconformity after a reasonable number of repair attempts. (Donlen v. Ford Motor Co. (2013) 217 Cal. App. 4th 138, 152, citing Oregel v. Am. Isuzu Motors, Inc. (2001) 90 Cal. App. 4th 1094, 1101.)

 

Here, Defendants submitted evidence that the undisputed repair history establishes that Plaintiff did not complain of or experience any of the Alleged Issues or issues related to an Engine Defect with the Subject Vehicle during the warranty period. (UMF 5-12.) The repair history shows that during the Vehicle’s Basic Limited Warranty, Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle to Defendant Dealership three times, in which only recalls had been performed. (UMF 6-8.) Plaintiff presented the Subject Vehicle to the Defendant Dealership for a check engine light on 3/24/2025, which was caused by an open circuit no longer covered under warranty. (UMF 9-11.)

 

Accordingly, the Court agrees that Plaintiff cannot establish a Violation of California Civil Code § 1793.2(d) as FCA was never afforded a reasonable number of repair attempts for any alleged nonconformity with the Subject Vehicle. Importantly, Plaintiff did not present the Subject Vehicle to Defendant Dealership for the alleged Engine Defect or any of the related Alleged Issues under warranty. (UMF 17.)

 

Accordingly, the Court finds no triable issue of material fact exists as to the first cause of action. The Court takes Plaintiff’s non-opposition to be a concession on the merits.

 

As for the second and third causes of action, Defendants submitted evidence that the repair order records show that during the Vehicle’s Basic Limited Warranty from 2022 to 2024, Plaintiff brought the Subject Vehicle to the Defendant Dealership three times for recall visits. (UMF 5-8.) The repair records do not support that Plaintiff ever brought the Subject Vehicle to an FCA-authorized dealership for the alleged Engine Defect or any of the Alleged Issues in his complaint. (UMF 17.) Likewise, the undisputed evidence establishes that Plaintiff’s Subject Vehicle was never out of service for 30 cumulative days during the Basic Limited Warranty regarding the alleged Engine Defect or any of the Alleged Issues in their complaint. (UMF 18.) Additionally, Plaintiff has not alleged nor identified any prolonged repair period that was related to the issues alleged in her complaint. (UMF 18.)

 

Since the Subject Vehicle was never brought to Defendant Dealership for an attempt to repair any of the issues alleged in their Complaint, the repair facilities and parts requirement under section 1793.2(a)(3) is not applicable. The fact that Plaintiff did not bring the Subject Vehicle in for repairs of the alleged Engine Defect or Alleged Issues cannot support a section 1793.2(b) or section 1793.2(a)(3) claim.

 

Accordingly, the Court finds no triable issue of material fact exists as to the second or third causes of action. The Court takes Plaintiff’s non-opposition to be a concession on the merits.

 

As for the fourth cause of action, an implied warranty arises by operation of law and extends for one year from the date of delivery. (Civ. Code § 1791.1(c).) It is not a guarantee that a vehicle is free from all defects; “it provides for a minimum level of quality.” (Tae Hee Lee v. Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A. Inc. (C.D. Cal. 2014) 992 F. Supp. 3d 962, 979 (quoting Am. Suzuki Motor Corp. v. Superior Ct. (1995) 37 Cal. App. 4th 1291, 1295–96). The fact a plaintiff continues using their vehicle, despite an alleged defect, is evidence that the vehicle remained fit for its ordinary use. (See Id.)

 

Here, Defendant submitted evidence that the Subject Vehicle’s one-year implied warranty from the manufacturer expired on 3/24/2022. (UMF 1-2.)

 

The only period when the facts support that the Plaintiff was not able to use the Subject Vehicle due to issues or repairs was during the 4/18/2022 visit in which the Vehicle was at the dealership for 5 days. (UMF 6.) Recalls Z20, Y76 and RRt1 performed on the 4/18/2022 visit were not a warranty repair arising from an alleged sale defect. (Id.) Plaintiff’s failure to present the Subject Vehicle for repairs related to the alleged Engine Defect or any of the related Alleged Issues in their Complaint supports a reasonable inference that the vehicle was fit for ordinary use. (UMF 17-18.) Plaintiff did not submit evidence in opposition which could support a reasonable inference that it was not. Indeed, the Court takes Plaintiff’s non-opposition to be a concession on the merits.

 

Based on the foregoing, FCA’s motion for summary judgment is granted.

 

It is so ordered.

         vs.

 Case No.: 25STCV04229  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 13, 2026

 

Defendant’s motion to dismiss Plaintiff’s FAC is GRANTED.

 

On 2/13/2025, Plaintiff Liliana Sustar (Plaintiff) filed suit against GM, LLC (Defendant). On 7/28/2025, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint (FAC) alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

On 6/16/2026, Defendant moved to dismiss Plaintiff’s FAC.

 

            The motion is unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

            Defendant moves to dismiss Plaintiff’s FAC for Plaintiff’s repeated noncompliance with the discovery requirements mandated in Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) section 871.26.

 

            In support, Defendant contends that Plaintiff has repeatedly failed to comply with discovery obligations including Plaintiff’s failure to appear for deposition even after this Court’s Order compelling Plaintiff’s appearance, and the failure to produce any of the mandated discovery documents.

 

            This noncompliance, alongside the non-opposition here, indicates that Plaintiff is either unwilling or unable to litigate this action.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion to dismiss Plaintiff’s FAC is granted.

 

It is so ordered.

Plaintiff’s counsel, Strategic Legal Practices, APC’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED, effective upon filing proof of service of the court’s order (MC-053).

 

Legal Standard

 

Motions to withdraw are routinely granted if made in the proper form and presented with proper notice. The court may issue an order allowing an attorney to withdraw from representation, after notice to the client.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) 

 

Discussion

 

Strategic Legal Practices, APC brings the instant motion for relief as counsel as to Plaintiff Myrjamyz Diwa.  

 

            The following documents are properly submitted: Civil Form MC-051 “Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” Civil Form MC-052 “Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” and Civil Form MC-053 “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel.”

 

            The motion is GRANTED.

 

It is so ordered.

On 3/14/2025, Plaintiff Ronald Lew. (Plaintiff) filed suit against American Honda Motor Co. Inc. (Defendant), alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

            On 6/15/2026, Defendant moved to compel inspection of Plaintiff’s vehicle.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff’s opposition makes clear that the substance of the motion is moot, as four inspection dates have since been provided.

 

            However, what remains is the question of sanctions. While Plaintiff has since provided dates for inspection, it does appear this motion was necessary to prompt that. Accordingly, sanctions are still warranted.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Defendant’s motion to compel inspection of Plaintiff’s vehicle is moot.

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

MATTHEW MENDOZA

                          

         vs.

 

CORPORATE BROKERS, LLC dba TENTIAL.

 

                                         

 Case No.: 25STCV09105

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 13, 2026

 

 

Plaintiff’s motion for approval of the PAGA settlement is GRANTED.

 

            On 3/27/2025, Plaintiff Matthew Mendoza (Plaintiff) filed suit against Defendant Corporate Brokers, LLC dba Tential (Defendant), seeking civil penalties pursuant to the Private Attorney General Act (PAGA), and Labor Code section 2698, et seq.

 

            On 6/4/2025, Plaintiff moved for approval of the PAGA settlement.

 

            The motion is unopposed.

 

Discussion

 

The basic terms of the PAGA Settlement Agreement (Settlement Agreement) provide for the following:

 

-          Gross Settlement Amount: $200,000.00

-          Fees Award: $66,666.66

-          Costs Award: $12,021.86

-          Payment to Aggrieved Employees: $28,452.87

-          Payment to LWDA: $85,358.61

-          Settlement Administration Costs: $2,500.00

 

To determine whether a settlement is fair, adequate and reasonable, the court must independently and objectively analyze the evidence and circumstances before it in order to determine whether the settlement is in the best interests of those whose claims will be extinguished. (Kullar v. Foot Locker Retail, Inc.¿(2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 116, 130.) A non-exhaustive list of factors that the trial court should consider in evaluating the reasonableness of a class action settlement agreement includes the strength of plaintiffs' case, the risk, expense, complexity and likely duration of further litigation, the risk of maintaining class action status through trial, the amount offered in settlement, the extent of discovery completed and stage of the proceedings, the experience and views of counsel, the presence of a governmental participant, and the reaction of the class members to the proposed settlement. (Id. at 129.) 

 

            Here, the parties engaged in informal discovery, participated in mediation, and the Settlement Amount reflects the competing risks associated with litigating these claims. Plaintiffs’ motion thoroughly sets forth an analysis of the competing risks here, to show that considerable thought and investigation was put into the decision to settle the claims. (See Korobkin Decl.)

 

            The Court also finds the requested litigation reimbursement fees and costs, as well as the settlement administration costs, to be reasonable.  (See Laffitte v. Robert Half Intern. Inc. (Cal. 2016) 205 Cal.Rptr.3d 555, 573.)

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion for approval of the PAGA settlement is granted.

 

It is so ordered.

TENTATIVE RULING

 

AIGERIM GINAYATOVA

 

         vs.

 

DANNY RODRIGUEZ, et al.

 

 Case No.: 26STCV08554

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: August 13, 2026

 

 

Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file an amended complaint is GRANTED.

 

            On 3/17/2025, Plaintiff Aigerim Ginayatova (Plaintiff) filed suit against Danny Rodriguez, Sandy Rodriguez, and New York Film Academy LTD, alleging: (1) motor vehicle negligence; (2) negligent entrustment; and (4) negligent supervision.

 

            On 7/28/026, Plaintiff moved for leave to amend to file a first amended complaint (FAC).

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff seeks leave to amend to add a proper party and add allegations pertaining to that party.

 

            The policy favoring leave to amend is so strong that it is an abuse of discretion to deny an amendment unless the adverse party can show meaningful prejudice. (Atkinson v. Elk Corp. (2003) 109 Cal.App.4th 739, 761.)

 

            Here, no trial date has been set, no dispositive motions are pending, and the case remains at a stage where amendment will not disrupt case management. Taken together, the Court finds no evidence of meaningful prejudice to justify denying leave to amend.

 

            Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion for leave to file an amended complaint is granted.

 

It is so ordered.
