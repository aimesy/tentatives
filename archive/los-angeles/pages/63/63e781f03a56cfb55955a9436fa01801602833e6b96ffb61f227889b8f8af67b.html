[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE:

 

DEFENDANT YAEL SIMA WINTNER’S MOTION TO COMPEL THE DEPOSITION OF WITNESS DAVID LEVY

           

Background

Plaintiff Meir Levy (“Plaintiff”) filed this action on May 17, 2022 against Defendants Tova Fagan and Yael Sima Wintner.

On July 11, 2023, Plaintiff filed a first amended complaint.

On July 11, 2023, Plaintiff filed an amendment to the complaint naming Pinchas (Paul) Wintner in place of Doe 1.

On March 7, 2024, Plaintiff filed the operative second amended complaint (“SAC”) against Defendants Tova Fagan, Yael Sima Wintner, and Pinchas (Paul) Wintner (collectively, “Defendants”), alleging causes of action for (1) extortion, (2) conspiracy to commit fraud and/or extortion, and (3) intentional infliction of emotional distress.

On February 20, 2026, the parties engaged in an Informal Discovery Conference, and the Court stated that “[t]he parties fulfilled their Informal Discovery requirements re: the issues set forth in the Informal Discovery Conference Statement file[d] by Defendant Wintner on January 29, 2026 as well as to outstanding written discovery propounded by Defendant Wintner to Plaintiff.” (Minute Order 2/20/26.)

Defendant Yael Sima Wintner (“Wintner”) now moves to compel the deposition of party-affiliated witness and Plaintiff’s son, David Levy (“Levy”). No opposition was filed.

Request for Judicial Notice

The Court grants Wintner’s request for judicial notice of Exhibit 1.

Legal Standard

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2017.010, “any party may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, that is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action or to the determination of any motion made in that action, if the matter either is itself admissible in evidence or appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.”

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, or a person designated by an organization that is a party under Section 2025.230, without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent's attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)

“If a deponent fails to answer any question or to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing under the deponent’s control that is specified in the deposition notice or a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer or production.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (a).)

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.4, subdivision (g)(1), “[i]f a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”

Discussion

            Wintner moves to compel the deposition of non-party Levy. Under Code of Civil Procedure section 2020.010, a nonparty may be compelled to provide an oral deposition if subpoenaed. Wintner asserts that deposing Levy is necessary because “Levy is a critical witness in this action due to Plaintiff’s own discovery responses. As set forth in Plaintiff’s responses to Special Interrogatories, Levy was identified as a key witness to Plaintiff’s claims for intentional infliction of emotional distress and civil extortion. (Krueger Decl. ¶ 5, See, Exhibit ‘A’). Thus, Defendant is entitled to depose Levy in an effort to evaluate and defend against the claims made against her and also to examine witnesses that will likely testify at the time of trial.” (Mot., 4:10-15.)

In support of the instant motion, Wintner’s counsel explains that “Defendant first noticed David Levy’s deposition for April 17, 2025.” (Krueger Decl., ¶ 8, Ex. D.) “On April 17, 2025, David Levy and counsel did not appear for the lawfully noticed deposition and Defendant took a Certificate of Non-Appearance.” (Krueger Decl., ¶ 9.) Thereafter, “Levy’s Deposition was lawfully noticed for September 18, 2025. Defendant lawfully served the Deposition Subpoena for Personal Appearance and Production of Documents and Things (‘Subpoena’) through Plaintiff’s counsel of record, who agreed to accept service on behalf of David Levy on September 4, 2025. Levy served no objections. Levy was requested to produce, among other things, documents relating to Levy’s correspondence with Defendants concerning the facts at issue in the relevant suit.” (Krueger Decl., ¶ 6, Ex. B.) “On September 12, 2025, counsel for Plaintiff sent electronic correspondence to Wintner’s counsel, requesting a change in date to the deposition. However, Plaintiff’s counsel’s correspondence did not propose alternative dates and times for deposition, and did not present a formal objection to Defendant Wintner’s notice. Counsel for Defendant responded, requesting [] alternative dates upon which the deposition could be conducted. Counsel for Plaintiff did not respond. Thus, Levy’s deposition remained on calendar for September 18, 2025.” (Krueger Decl. ¶ 7, Ex. C.) However, “both David Levy and counsel did not appear for the lawfully noticed deposition and Defendant took a Certificate of Non-Appearance.” (Krueger Decl. ¶ 10, Ex. E.)

            Wintner’s counsel further explains, “[p]er the Court’s Local Rules, before the filing of this Motion, Defendant noticed an informal discovery conference that was attended by counsel for all parties and counsel for David Levy on February 20, 2026. Still, the witness has not agreed to appear for deposition, and Plaintiff’s counsel has not offered available dates.” (Krueger Decl. ¶ 11). “After the February 20, 2026, informal discovery conference, my office again requested deposition dates. None were provided. Thus, Defendant re-noticed the deposition of David Levy for April 2, 2026.” (Krueger Decl., ¶ 15.) “Counsel for the Plaintiff and for the witness have continued to fail to provide dates on which the witness will be available for deposition.” (Krueger Decl., ¶ 16.) Finally, counsel attests that “[t]his Motion is necessary given this party-affiliated witness’s discovery abuse despite lawfully serving two subpoenas and notices of the deposition, and the quickly approaching trial date. Levy’s failure to appear for deposition has hindered Defendant’s ability to obtain relevant, necessary and discoverable information related to Plaintiff’s alleged case. Defendant’s counsel will have limited time to conduct discovery and have been denied the opportunity to develop their defense to the case.” (Krueger Decl., ¶ 12.)

Wintner’s motion demonstrates that Levy appears to be an important witness in the case because Plaintiff included him multiple times throughout Plaintiff’s answers to Wintner’s special interrogatories. Additionally, Wintner’s counsel’s declaration demonstrates that, on multiple occasions, she attempted to obtain Levy’s deposition, but to no avail. Accordingly, based on Wintner’s explanation, and in light of a lack of opposition, the Court finds it necessary to compel Levy’s deposition.

///

///

///

Conclusion

Based on the foregoing, Wintner’s motion for an order compelling the attendance of non-party David Levy at deposition is GRANTED. The Levy deposition is to take place within 30 days of the date of this order.

Wintner is ordered to give notice of this Order.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: 

 

DEFENDANT SCREAMLINE INVESTMENT CORPORATION’S MOTION TO QUASH SERVICE OF SUMMONS

           

            Background

Plaintiff Albert Ramos Jimenez (“Plaintiff”) filed this action on July 3, 2025 against Defendants Screamline Investment Corporation (“Screamline”) and George Philip Rucke, alleging two causes of action for (1) motor vehicle and (2) general negligence.

Screamline now moves to quash service of summons “pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10, because the proof of service filed in this case indicates that Plaintiff left a copy of the summons and complaint with an employee who was not apparently in charge of the office or usual place of business of Moving Defendant, which is insufficient to effectuate service of the summons and complaint.” (Notice of Mot., 2:10-13.) Plaintiff did not file an opposition.

Request for Judicial Notice

The Court grants Screamline’s request for judicial notice of Exhibit A.

///

///

Legal Standard

Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10 provides in part: “A defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion … (1) To quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a).) “The effect of [a notice of motion to quash service] is to place upon the plaintiff the burden of proving the facts that did give the court jurisdiction, that is the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Coulston v. Cooper (1966) 245 Cal.App.2d 866, 868.)

Discussion

Screamline moves to quash service, asserting that “the purported proof of service on Screamline filed by Plaintiff in this case indicates that Screamline was not served by delivering the summons and complaint on its president, chief executive officer, or other head of the corporation, a vice president, a secretary or assistant secretary, a treasurer or assistant treasurer, a controller or chief financial officer, a general manager, or a person authorized by the corporation to receive service of process as required by statute. Instead, Plaintiff filed a proof of service indicating that the summons and complaint were served by substitute service on a person ‘apparently in charge at the office or usual place of business of the person to be served’ by the name of ‘Chris Pablo.’ Chris Pablo is an employee of Screamline that works in the human resources department only. There is no basis for the statement that he was ‘apparently in charge of the office’ of Screamline’s place of business simply because he may have been present in the office. Service is not proper simply because a process server hands papers to any employee found working at the premises. Therefore, there has been no service of the summons and complaint on Screamline in this case and accordingly, the purported service must be quashed.” (Mot., 4:14-27.)

Screamline cites both Code of Civil Procedure sections 416.10 and 415.20 in support of the instant motion. Section 416.10 applies to what individuals may be served on behalf of a corporation for personal service, whereas section 415.20 applies to substituted service. Specifically, Code of Civil Procedure section 415.20, subdivision (a) states that “[i]n lieu of personal delivery of a copy of the summons and complaint to the person to be served as specified in Section 416.10, 416.20, 416.30, 416.40, or 416.50, a summons may be served by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint during usual office hours in the person’s office or, if no physical address is known, at the person’s usual mailing address, other than a United States Postal Service post office box, with the person who is apparently in charge thereof, and by thereafter mailing a copy of the summons and complaint by first-class mail, postage prepaid to the person to be served at the place where a copy of the summons and complaint were left.”

The procedural rules for a motion to quash service of summons for lack of jurisdiction are well settled: “[a]lthough the defendant is the moving party, the plaintiff must carry the initial burden of demonstrating facts by a preponderance of evidence justifying the exercise of jurisdiction in California.” (In re Automobile Antitrust Cases I & II (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 100, 110.) However, Plaintiff did not file an opposition. Notwithstanding, Evidence Code section 647 creates a rebuttable presumption that proof of service done by a registered process server is adequately effectuated. The proof of service demonstrates service was done by a registered process server.

In support of the instant motion, Screamline includes a declaration from Shoeleh Sapir, which states that “[a]s an officer and director of Screamline, I am aware of the persons employed by Screamline and their positions. I am able to declare that Chris Pablo is an employee in the human resources department of Screamline. He is neither the president, chief executive officer, or other head of the corporation, a vice president, a secretary or assistant secretary, a treasurer or assistant treasurer, a controller or chief financial officer, a general manager, or a person authorized by the corporation to receive service of process. Mr. Pablo also is not a person in charge of Screamline’s office or place of business.” (Sapir Decl., ¶ 2.) Moreover, the declaration attests that “to the best of my knowledge, the summons and complaint in this matter were never received by Screamline in the mail.” (Sapir Decl., ¶ 3.)

Screamline produced evidence that Chris Pablo was not authorized to accept service on behalf of Screamline and was not “apparently in change” because he does not run the office. Screamline also attests that it did not receive service via the mail. Thus, Screamline rebuts the presumption that service was properly effectuated. Because Plaintiff did not file an opposition, he cannot carry his burden demonstrating that service was indeed proper. Accordingly, the motion to quash for improper service is granted.

Conclusion

In light of the foregoing, Screamline’s motion to quash service of summons is GRANTED.

Screamline is ordered to give notice of this Order.
