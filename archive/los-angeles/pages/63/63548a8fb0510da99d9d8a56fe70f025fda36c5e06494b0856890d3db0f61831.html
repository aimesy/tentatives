DEPARTMENT M LAW AND MOTION RULINGS



Case Number: 24SMCV00010    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: M

CASE NAME:           Glowa v. MDR L&M Apartments, LLC, et al.

CASE NO.:                24SMCV00010

MOTION:                  Motion to Compel Independent Medical Examination

HEARING DATE:   7/21/2026

 

Legal Standard

 

Any party may obtain discovery by means of mental or physical examination of a party to the action if the mental or physical condition of that party is in controversy in the action. (CCP § 2032.020.) The demand must specify the date, time, manner, conditions, scope, and nature of the examination, as well as the identity and the specialty, if any, of the physician who will perform the exam. (CCP § 2032.220(c).) Any demand shall be scheduled for a date that is at least 30 days after service of the demand, but the court may shorten this time upon motion.  (CCP § 2032.220(d).) Within 20 days after service of the demand the party to whom the demand is directed shall respond to the demand by a written statement that the party will comply as stated, comply as modified, or refuse. (CCP § 2032.230(a).)   

 

Leave for a party to conduct a mental examination may be granted upon good cause shown. (CCP §§ 2032.310, 2032.320.) The motion must specify the time, place, manner, conditions, scope, and nature of the examination, as well as the identity, and specialty, if any, of the person conducting the examination. (CCP § 2032.310(b).) The motion must be supported by a meet and confer declaration. (Id.) Further, the party’s mental state must be in controversy in the action. (See Acuna v. Regents of University of California (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 639, 653 [trial courts have the discretion to order a mental examination where the plaintiff seeks damages for on-going emotional distress].) “[A] party who chooses to allege that he has mental and emotional difficulties can hardly deny his mental state is in controversy.” (Vinson v. Superior Court (1987) 43 Cal.3d 833, 839-842.)

 

Specific tests must be specified to ensure that “the court has considered any objections to the tests and provides the examiner clear parameters for the examination.” (Carpenter v. Superior Court (2006) 141 Cal.App.4th 249, 263 [invalidating order requiring plaintiff to submit to “standardized written psychological tests” to “test emotional and cognitive functioning”].)

 

If a party stipulates that no claim is being made for mental and emotional distress over and above that usually associated with the physical injuries claimed and that no expert testimony will be presented regarding the mental and emotional distress, then the court shall not order a mental examination of a person unless there is a showing of exceptional circumstances.  (CCP § 2032.320(b) –(c).)  Finally, a separate statement is required to compel a “medical examination over objection.” (CRC Rule 3.1345(a)(6).)

 

ANALYSIS

 

Defendants MDR L&M Apartments, LLC and Common Management Co. move to compel Plaintiff Anna Glowa’s independent mental examination (“IME”) pursuant to CCP section 2031.310. Specifically, Defendant demanded a neuropsychological examination with a licensed clinical neuropsychologist, Dr. Giselle Leal, at 175 SW 7th Street, Suite 2417, Miami, FL, 33130, on August 5, 2026, at 9 a.m. (Ullrich Decl., Ex. 3.) This is less than 20 miles from Plaintiff’s residence at 1133 102nd St #806, Bay Harbor, FL, 33154. (Ullrich Decl., Ex. 1, FROG no. 2.5.)

 

Defendants demonstrate good cause for the noticed mental examination. Plaintiff’s mental condition is at issue. This is a slip-and-fall action. Plaintiff alleges that Defendants own the subject premises where Plaintiff was a resident. Plaintiff alleges she slipped and fell in the stairwell of the premises due to “fluids, liquids, water, urine and/or feces” in the stairwell. (Compl. ¶¶ 6, 14, 28.) Plaintiff’s form interrogatory responses claim damages of “concussion, traumatic brain injury, bruising, blurred vision, nausea, vomiting, onset of migraines . . . brain fog, slowness of thought, emotional distress, difficulty coping with stress/emotional situation . . ..” (Ullrich Decl. Ex. 1, FROG no. 6.2.) Plaintiff alleged ongoing migraines, post traumatic migraines, dizziness, brain fog, irritability, which were either daily or weekly. (Id., FROG no. 6.3.) Her prior treatment included DTI/MRI of the brain and a neurological examination. (Id., FROG no. 6.4.)

 

Defendant met and conferred with Plaintiff. (Ullrich Decl., ¶ 4, Ex. 2.) Plaintiff ultimately agreed to the August 5, 2026, defense medical examination. (Id.) Plaintiff did not return the signed DME notice and stipulation, nor did she reject it, despite emails reminding her counsel of it several times. (Id.)

 

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED. Plaintiff is ordered to appear at the examination as noticed.


Case Number: 25SMUD02510    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: M

CASE NAME:           7254 Hollywood, LLC, v. Davis, et al.

CASE NO.:                25SMUD02510

MOTION:                  Motion for New Trial

HEARING DATE:   7/21/2026

 

Legal Standard

 

A motion for new trial is a creature of statute. (Neal v. Montgomery Elevator Co.¿(1992) 7 Cal. App. 4th 1194, 1198.) A movant must satisfy CCP sections 657 and 659. Under Code of Civil Procedure section 657, a motion for new trial may be granted if there is any:

 

[¶] 1. Irregularity in the proceedings of the court, jury, or adverse party, or any order of the court or abuse of discretion by which either party was prevented from having a fair trial. [¶] 2. Misconduct of the jury; and whenever any one or more of the jurors have been induced to assent to any general or special verdict, or to a finding on any question submitted to them by the court, by a resort to the determination of chance, such misconduct may be proved by the affidavit of any one of the jurors. [¶] 3. Accident or surprise, which ordinary prudence could not have guarded against. [¶] 4. Newly discovered evidence, material for the party making the application, which he could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered and produced at the trial. [¶] 5. Excessive or inadequate damages. [¶] 6. Insufficiency of the evidence to justify the verdict or other decision, or the verdict or other decision is against law. [¶] 7. Error in law, occurring at the trial and excepted to by the party making the application.  

 

When ruling on an application for a new trial, the court sits as an independent trier of fact. (Lane v. Hughes Aircraft Co. (2000) 22 Cal.4th 405, 412.) The court, therefore, has broad discretion to order new trials, limited only by the obligation to state its reasons for granting a new trial and the existence of substantial evidence in the record to support those reasons. (Ibid.) In assessing the need for a new trial, the court must rely on its view of the overall record, taking into account such factors, among others, as the nature and seriousness of the alleged misconduct, the general atmosphere, including the judge’s control, of the trial, the likelihood of prejudicing the jury, and the efficacy of objection or admonition under all the circumstances. (Dominguez v. Pantalone (1989) 212 Cal.App.3d 201, 211.)

 

The party intending to move for a new trial must file with the clerk and serve upon each adverse party a notice of his intention to move for a new trial, designating the grounds upon which the motion will be made and whether the same will be made upon affidavits or the minutes of the court or both, either (1) “after a decision is rendered and before the entry of judgment”; (2) “within 15 days of the date of mailing notice of entry of judgment by the clerk of the court . . . , or service upon him or her by any party of written notice of entry of judgment, or within 180 days after the entry of judgment, whichever is earliest”; or (3) if another party files the first motion for new trial, “each other party shall have 15 days after the service of that notice upon him or her to file and serve a notice of intention to move for a new trial.” (CCP § 659.) These time limits are jurisdictional and cannot extended or waived by stipulation nor court order. (Marriage of Herr (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1463, 1469 [while trial judge characterized order as one “granting reconsideration,” ruling effectively granted new trial and was untimely].)

 

 

Analysis

 

Defendant Elijah Von Davis moves for a new trial, asserting (1) an irregularity in the proceedings of the court, jury, or adverse party, or any order of the court or abuse of discretion by which either party was prevented from having a fair trial (CCP § 657(1); and (2) newly discovered evidence, material for the party making the application, which he could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered and produced at the trial (CPP § 657(4)).

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

Defendant’s request for judicial notice is GRANTED as to the records maintained by the LA County Department of Health, specifically Exhibit A, pp. 7-15. (Evid. Code § 452(d).) The remainder of the request is DENIED, as the email correspondence between Defendant and the County (Ex. A pp. 1-6) and the transcript for a restraining order hearing (Ex. B) are not subject to judicial notice.

 

Irregularity

 

            Defendant argues that the Court improperly excluded the jury from considering his evidence binder, including certain certified public records, due to admittedly severe formatting omissions. (Davis Decl., ¶ 4.) Defendant argues the certified, dispositive public agency documentation would have established that Plaintiff relied on manufactured safety clearances lacking mandatory calibration and equipment logs, and lacked certain habitability characteristics in violation of Civil Code section 1942.4.

 

            Defendant cites no cognizable irregularity or abuse of discretion. Defendant does not present the specific evidence excluded, or explain how the Court abused its discretion by excluding such evidence. Defendant also did not object to the exclusion of said evidence at the time of trial. As the parties may recall, despite numerous pre-trial discussions, Defendant failed to comply with the standing trial order by individually identifying exhibits, marking them for identification, and providing copies for the court and opposing counsel.  Prior to trial and even during trial, the Court gave Defendant multiple opportunities to put together his evidence into individually marked exhibits, including being dark from trial so that his exhibits could be assembled. Despite these extensions, Defendant never assembled individual exhibits in any form.

 

New Evidence

 

            Defendant contends that he has secured certified trial transcripts and direct public health monitoring data which demonstrate that the subject premises’ utility clearances were systemically fabricated. Defendant does not explain why he could not have obtained this evidence with reasonable diligence. The cover letter of the County’s response to Defendant’s public records request is dated March 31, 2026, which should have given Defendant enough time to present those records at the trial on June 2-8, 2026. Additionally, the hearing transcript is not relevant or admissible in this action. Defendant presents this transcript to show that Plaintiff’s management testified that the unit was clear of habitability violations, despite allegedly knowing that the property's utility infrastructure was non-calibrated and that his unit’s stove and oven remain non-functional. (Davis Decl., ¶ 6.) However, this transcript does not establish any contradiction which might impeach Plaintiff’s position that the unit was clear of habitability violations. Plaintiff consistently held the position that the unit was clear of habitability issues. In any event, any potentially relevant information discussed at that hearing concerning habitability violations could have been presented at trial during Davis’s testimony, without reliance on this transcript.

 

Accordingly, the motion is DENIED.
