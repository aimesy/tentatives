Case Number: 19STLC10911    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Wed., September 23, 2026                             JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           Cal Interpreting & Translations v. Fit Vascular                                

COMP. FILED:         11-26-19

CASE NUMBER:      19STLC10911                       

NOTICE:                  OK                                                                 

                                                                                                           

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION FOR CHARGING ORDER UNDER CALIFORNIA CORPORATIONS CODE SECTION 17705.03

 

MOVING PARTY:   Plaintiff Cal Interpreting & Translations

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

MOTION FOR CHARGING ORDER

(Corp. Code, § 17705.03) 

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Court DENIES Cal Interpreting & Translations’s motion for a charging order.

 

 

SERVICE:

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          None filed as of September 17, 2026             [   ] Late                      [X] None

REPLY:                     None filed as of September 17, 2026             [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Cal Interpreting & Translations obtained a default judgment against Fit Vascular in October 2020.  It successfully moved to amend the judgment to run against “FIT VASCULAR DBA TLK MEDICAL GROUP AKA TLK MEDICAL GROUP, LLC.”  The judgment remains unpaid.  Cal Interpreting now moves for a charging order against TLK Medical Group, LLC’s transferable interest in the unsatisfied judgment.

 

            A lien on a judgment debtor’s interest in a partnership or LLC is created by service of a notice of motion for a charging order on the judgment debtor and on either:  (1) all partners or the partnership, or (2) all members or the LLC.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 708.320.)  The Court previously continued this hearing to allow Cal Interpreting to comply with this section.  Cal Interpreting has since filed proper proof of service of this motion and accompanying documents on judgment debtor Fit Vascular/TLK Medical and its managing members Rio Investments and Keith Reilly. 

 

            The problem now is with the substance of Cal Interpreting’s request.  Corporations Code section 17705.03 charges the transferable interest that a judgment debtor holds in an LLC—the member’s right to distributions.  (Corp. Code, § 17705.03, subd. (a); Rice v. Downs (2021) 73 Cal.App.5th 213, 226.)  In other words, the charging order runs against a judgment debtor that is a member of the LLC, to get that member’s distributions from the LLC.  Fit Vascular/TLK Medical is the judgment debtor here, not a member of the judgment debtor, and Cal Interpreting asks the Court to charge the transferable interest of TLK Medical.  An LLC does not hold a transferable interest in itself.  There is no “transferable interest of the judgment debtor” against which a charging order can issue.

 

            Corporations Code section 17705.03 is not the proper mechanism for collecting on a judgment entered against the LLC itself as judgment debtor.  (See id., § 17705.03, subd. (f) [this section is the exclusive remedy for satisfying a judgment “against a member or transferee” out of that debtor’s transferable interest].)

 

            The Court DENIES Cal Interpreting’s motion.

ANALYSIS:

 

            Jose R. Rubalcaba, Jr. petitions the Court to release property located at 540 San Gabriel Boulevard, Rosemead, California 91770 from a mechanics lien recorded in March 2021 by Adrian Navarro.

 

After a mechanics lien has been recorded, “[t]he owner of property or the owner of any interest in property subject to a claim of lien may petition the court for an order to release the property from the claim of lien if the claimant has not commenced an action to enforce the lien within the time provided in Section 8460.”  (Civ. Code, § 8480, subd. (a).)  A claimant must commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days of recording it, otherwise the claim of the lien expires and is unenforceable. (Civ. Code, § 8460, subd. (a).) 

 

The Court previously continued this hearing to allow Rubalcaba to file proof of service of this petition, and Rubalcaba has since filed proof of substitute service.  Rubalcaba has now properly complied with the service requirements, petition allegation requirements, and demand requirements in filing this petition.  (Civ. Code, §§ 8108, 8482, 8484, 8486.)  Navarro did not file an opposition indicating otherwise.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Rubalcaba’s petition to release the mechanics lien.  Rubalcaba is to give notice, and to file a proposed order within 10 days of this ruling.

 


Case Number: 26STCP02641    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 25

RESP. PARTY:         None

 

PETITION TO RELEASE MECHANICS LIEN

(Civ. Code § 8460, et seq.)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

            The Court GRANTS Empire Entourage, LLC’s petition to release the mechanics lien.  Empire is awarded $4,500.00 in attorney fees.

 

            Empire is to give notice.

 

SERVICE:

 

            [X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

            [X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

            [X] At least 15 Days Lapsed (Civ. Code § 8486(b))                          OK

            [X] Correct Manner of Service (Civ. Code § 8486(b))                      OK

 

OPPOSITION:          None filed as of September 21, 2026             [   ] Late                      [X] None

REPLY:                     None filed as of September 21, 2026             [   ] Late                      [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Empire Entourage, LLC petitions the Court to release property located at 235 Orchid Court, Montebello, California 90640 from a mechanics lien recorded in December 2024 by We Design, LLC.

 

After a mechanics lien has been recorded, “[t]he owner of property or the owner of any interest in property subject to a claim of lien may petition the court for an order to release the property from the claim of lien if the claimant has not commenced an action to enforce the lien within the time provided in Section 8460.”  (Civ. Code, § 8480, subd. (a).)  A claimant must commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days of recording it, otherwise the claim of the lien expires and is unenforceable. (Civ. Code, § 8460, subd. (a).) 

 

The Court previously continued this hearing to allow Empire to file proof of service of its petition, and Empire has since filed proof of personal service.  Empire has now properly complied with the service requirements, petition allegation requirements, and demand requirements in filing this petition.  (Civ. Code, §§ 8108, 8482, 8484, 8486.)  We Design did not file an opposition indicating otherwise.

 

Accordingly, the Court GRANTS Empire’s petition to release the mechanics lien.

 

Empire also requests attorney fees pursuant to Civil Code section 8488, subdivision (c), which provides for reasonable attorney fees to the prevailing party on a petition to release a mechanics lien.  Given the Court’s ruling to grant the petition, Empire is the prevailing party and is entitled to reasonable attorney fees.  Empire requests $6,500.00 in attorney fees, reflecting 13 hours of its counsel’s work at the rate of $500.00 per hour for obtaining factual background and documents, reviewing and analyzing the documents, conducting research, preparing and revising the petition, preparing the petition, preparing and overseeing service of the documents, reviewing an opposition, preparing a reply, preparing and attending this hearing, and preparing and recording the documentation needed to release the mechanics lien.

 

Empire doesn’t break down its attorney’s time in performing each of those tasks, some of which are not fairly filled at attorney rates.  Moreover, no opposition was filed and so there was no reason for Empire to review one—or to prepare a reply.  Given those two facts, the Court deducts four hours of attorney time. 

 

Accordingly, the Court awards attorney fees in the reduced amount of $4,500.00, reflecting nine hours of counsel’s work at the rate of $500.00 per hour.

 

            Empire is to give notice and to provide a proposed order within 10 days of this ruling.


Case Number: 26STLC03170    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 25

HEARING DATE:    Wed., September 23, 2026                             JUDGE /DEPT:            Eisenman/25

CASE NAME:           Omar v. South Goodyear Investments Corporation              

COMP. FILED:         05-11-26

CASE NUMBER:      26STLC03170

NOTICE:                   OK                                                                 

                                               

 

PROCEEDINGS:      MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT           

 

MOVING PARTY:   Defendant South Goodyear Investments Corporation

RESP. PARTY:         Plaintiff Shalaby Omar

 

MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT

(Code Civ. Proc., § 473(b))

 

TENTATIVE RULING:                                                                       

 

The Court GRANTS South Goodyear Investments Corporation’s motion to set aside the default entered against it.  The July 22, 2026 default is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.

 

The Court DENIES Shalaby Omar’s request for fees and costs.

 

South Goodyear is to give notice.

 

SERVICE: 

 

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

[X] 16/21 Court Days Lapsed (CCP §§ 12c, 1005(b))                       OK

 

OPPOSITION:          Filed on August 24, 2026                               [  ] Late                       [  ] None

REPLY:                     Filed on August 28, 2026                              [  ] Late                       [  ] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Shalaby Omar sued South Goodyear Investments Corporation for violation of the Unruh Civil Rights Act.  Omar obtained a default against South Goodyear in July 2026.  South Goodyear now moves to have the default set aside under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) because it was entered due to South Goodyear’s mistake or excusable neglect.

 

            A motion pursuant to the discretionary relief provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), must (1) be timely, (2) be accompanied by a proposed pleading, and (3) demonstrate that the default was taken through the party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); Austin v. Los Angeles Unified School District (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 918, 928.)  Neglect is excusable if a reasonably prudent person under similar circumstances might have made the same error. (Austin, at p. 929.)

 

            South Goodyear’s motion, filed August 18, 2026, satisfies each of those requirements.  South Goodyear attaches a proposed answer to its motion.  South Goodyear’s general counsel, Gregson M. Perry, declares that he attempted to calendar the responsive-pleading deadline, made a calendaring error in calculating the deadline, and as a result no responsive pleading was timely filed.  Perry further attests that he discovered the error six days after default was entered and immediately retained counsel to seek relief.  Omar does not oppose setting aside the default but rather argues that Perry should be required to pay fees, which is discussed further below. 

 

Given these facts, the Court GRANTS South Goodyear’s request for relief under the discretionary provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).  (See Kramer v. Traditional Escrow, Inc. (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 13, 28 [Code of Civil Procedure section 473 is applied liberally where the party in default moves promptly and the party opposing the motion will not suffer prejudice].)  The July 22, 2026 default is hereby SET ASIDE AND VACATED.  South Goodyear must file the answer attached to its motion within 10 days.

 

            Omar contends that because Perry is an attorney, he should pay legal fees and costs pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).  Omar misinterprets that section and South Goodyear’s motion.  Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) contains two distinct mechanisms:  one for discretionary relief based on a party’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect, and one for mandatory relief based on an attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.  Only the mandatory provision requires fee-shifting.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

 

            But South Goodyear’s motion is based on the discretionary provision premised on its own calendaring error, not the error of its counsel of record in this matter, Erik E. Woodbury.  Default was entered before any attorney of record appeared, outside counsel was not retained until after default was entered, and the motion attributes the calendaring error to South Goodyear as the client rather than to Woodbury as counsel of record.  The Court is granting relief based on section 473 (b)’s discretionary provision—as requested in South Goodyear’s motion—and not on the statute’s mandatory provision.  The Court therefore DENIES Omar’s request for fees and costs against Perry.

 

            South Goodyear is to give notice.


Case Number: 26STLC03256    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: 25

MOTION TO CONSOLIDATE

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1048; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350) 

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

The Court DENIES Mercury Insurance Company’s motion to consolidate.

 

Lee is to give notice.

[X] Proof of Service Timely Filed (CRC, rule 3.1300)                     OK

[X] Correct Address (CCP §§ 1013, 1013a)                                      OK

ANALYSIS:

 

            Mercury Insurance Company petitioned the Court to assign a case number for purposes of consolidation with Los Angeles County Superior Court Case No. 25STLC06553.  Mercury then filed this motion to consolidate this action with Case No. 25STLC06553 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1048 and California Rules of Court rule 3.350.

 

            Mercury’s motion fails to comply with the mandatory procedural requirements of California Rules of Court rule 3.350.  The notice of the motion does not list all named parties in each case, the names of those who have appeared, and the names of their respective attorneys of record; does not contain the captions of the cases sought to be consolidated; and was not also filed in Case No. 25STLC06553.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.350(a)(1).)  Additionally, the motion was not filed in the lowest numbered case only, which would be Case No. 25STLC06553, and was not served on all attorneys of record in both cases.   (Id., rule 3.350(a)(2).) 

 

            Accordingly, the Court DENIES Mercury’s motion.

 

            Lee is to give notice.
