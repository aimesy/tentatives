LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Meet and Confer 

 

Prior to filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to satisfy their meet and confer obligations pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41, and demonstrate that they so satisfied their meet and confer obligation by submitting a declaration pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41(a)(2) & (3). ¿The meet and confer requirement also applies to motions to strike. (CCP § 435.5.)¿

 

Here, the parties met and conferred via telephone on May 1, 2026. (Levine Decl. ¶ 4.) This requirement is therefore met.

 

Demurrer – Sufficiency of Facts 

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿(Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.¿In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice.¿(Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)¿“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. …. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”¿(Hahn¿147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)¿¿ 

¿ 

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true. (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.) Courts also consider exhibits attached to the complaint and incorporated by reference. (See Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94 (Frantz).)¿ 

 

Motion to Strike 

 

The court may, upon a motion, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper, strike any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading. (CCP § 436(a).) The court may also strike all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Id., § 436(b).) The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleading or by way of judicial notice. (Id.¿§¿437.)¿“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.” (Vaccaro v. Kaiman¿(1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768.)¿¿¿¿

Entire SAC – Time-Barred

 

Defendants contend the claims are all time-barred for the adult Plaintiffs. Those Plaintiffs are: Gabriela Aju Valenzuela, Lilian Gutierrez, Hazel Climaco-Zepeda, Kevin Climaco, Luis Cisneros, Kevin Cardoso, and Hazam Muhammed (collectively, the Adult Plaintiffs). Defendants contend the facts on the face of the SAC show the claims accrued by December 2017 so the Adult Plaintiffs’ case, filed February 25, 2025, is untimely on all claims. They add that the allegation these claims somehow relate back to a filing in a separate case is unsupported by law.

 

Plaintiffs argue that equitable tolling applies to these claims.

 

The limitations periods for each cause of action, in order of appearance, are as follows:

 

The statute of limitations for negligence is two years. (CCP § 335.1.)

 

The statute of limitations for breach of implied warranty of habitability is four years. (CCP §§ 337 and 337.2 [breach of written lease and abandonment of property]; See Green v. Superior Court (1974) 10 Cal.3d 616, 624 [“Our holding in this case reflects our belief that the application of contract principles, including the mutual dependency of covenants, is particularly appropriate in dealing with residential leases of urban dwelling units.”])

 

The statute of limitations for breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment is four years. (CCP §§ 337 and 337.2.)

 

The statute of limitations for nuisance is three years. (CCP § 338(b).) The statute of limitations for premises liability is two years. (CCP § 335.1.)

 

The statute of limitations for intentional infliction of emotional distress is two years. (CCP § 335.1; Wassmann v. South Orange County Community College District (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 825, 852-853.)

 

The statute of limitations for violation of Civ. Code § 1942.4 is three years. (CCP § 338.)

 

The statute of limitations for constructive eviction and willful interruption of services is four years. (CCP §§ 337 and 337.2.)

 

The statute of limitations for retaliation (Civ. Code § 1942.5) is three years. (CCP § 338.)

 

The statute of limitations for violation of unfair competition law is four years. (Cortez v. Purolator Air Filtration Products Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 163, 178-179.)

 

The statute of limitations for breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing is four years. (CCP §§ 337 and 337.2.)

 

i.                    Equitable tolling

“Equitable tolling is a judicially created doctrine that, where applicable, will suspend or extend a statute of limitations as necessary to ensure fundamental practicality and fairness.” (Long v. Forty Niners Football Co., LLC (2019) 33 Cal.App.5th 550, 554-555 [internal quotations omitted].) It applies when an injured person has several legal remedies and, reasonably and in good faith, pursues one. (Id. at p. 555.) “Thus, it may apply where one action stands to lessen the harm that is the subject of a potential second action; where administrative remedies must be exhausted before a second can proceed; or where a first action, embarked upon in good faith, is found to be defective for some reason.” (Ibid.)

 

A plaintiff seeking the benefit of equitable tolling must show: “timely notice, and lack of prejudice, to the defendant, and reasonable and good faith conduct on the part of the plaintiff.” (Long, supra, 33 Cal.App.5th at p. 555.) Timely notice means the first claim was filed within the statutory period and must alert the defendant in the second claim of the need to begin investigating the facts which form the basis for the second claim. (Hopkins v. Kedzierski (2014) 225 Cal.App.4th 736, 747.) “The second prerequisite essentially translates to a requirement that the facts of the two claims be identical or at least so similar that the defendant's investigation of the first claim will put him in a position to fairly defend the second.” (Ibid.) While the third element is not clearly defined, there is a timing component where quick refiling satisfies it. (Ibid.)

 

“Where a claim is time-barred on its face, the plaintiff must specifically plead facts that would support equitable tolling.” (Long, supra, 33 Cal.App.5th at p. 555.)

 

Here, the requirements for equitable tolling are met. First, Plaintiffs allege a prior claim filed within the statutory period where Plaintiffs asserted the same claims as in this action. (SAC ¶ 40.) This satisfies the first element. Second, Plaintiffs allege the claims were the same, which satisfies the second element. (SAC ¶ 40.) Third, Plaintiffs allege that after receiving an adverse ruling on their demurrer due to misjoinder of the Crawford Defendants, they dismissed those claims. (SAC ¶¶ 42, 43, 44.) It appears that the tolling therefore applied between the initial filing on April 28, 2023 and the ruling on the demurrer on December 12, 2024. Two months later, Plaintiffs refiled this second action. In short, it does not appear untimely on its face.

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES the Crawford Defendants’ demurrer to the entire SAC on this ground.

 

Entire Complaint – Gabriela Aju Valenzuela Misjoinder

 

The Crawford Defendants contend Plaintiff Gabriela Aju Valenzuela is misjoined because she resides at a separate property and none of the facts alleged pertained to that property.

 

Plaintiffs argue that Plaintiff Gabriela Aju Valenzuela is properly included under the permissive joinder statute because there are common questions of law and fact between the neighboring apartment buildings with the same owners and same habitability issues.

 

CCP § 378(a) provides that all persons may join as plaintiffs if: “(1) They assert any right to relief jointly, severally, or in the alternative, in respect of or arising out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences and if any question of law or fact common to all these persons will arise in the action; or [¶] They have a claim, right, or interest adverse to the defendant in the property or controversy which is the subject of the action.”

 

There is a presumption in favor of joinder. (Smith v. Superior Court of Alameda County (2026) --- Cal.Rptr.3d ---- at *5.) A “right to relief arises from the same transaction or occurrence under section 378 so long as there is any factual relationship between the claims joined.” (Ibid.)

 

After reviewing the SAC, the court agrees with the Crawford Defendants that Plaintiff Gabriela Aju Valenzuela is misjoined. Critically, Plaintiff Valenzuela resides at a separate property – 4024 Gelber Place. (SAC ¶ 9.) The remaining Plaintiffs reside at a different property – 4029 Gelber Place. (SAC ¶¶ 10-25.) There are no allegations of a common scheme, series of transactions or occurrences, or common questions of law or fact. (Smith, supra at *6.)

 

Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS the demurrer on this ground.

 

Sixth Cause of Action – Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress (IIED)

 

The Crawford Defendants contend this claim fails to allege that Plaintiffs suffered severe emotional distress or that the Crawford Defendants acted with actual malice.

 

Plaintiffs argue that these arguments are irrelevant to whether they sufficiently plead their claim, which they did.

 

The necessary elements for intentional infliction of emotional distress (IIED) are: (1) outrageous conduct by the defendant; (2) which was intentional or reckless in nature; (3) severe emotional distress; and (4) causation. (Huntingdon Life Sciences, Inc. v. Stop Huntingdon Animal Cruelty USA, Inc.¿(2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 1228, 1259 (Huntingdon).)¿For purposes of pleading IIED,¿ 

conduct is outrageous if it is “so extreme as to exceed all bounds of that usually tolerated in a¿civilized community.” (Davidson v. City of Westminster¿(1982) 32 Cal.3d 197, 209.)¿¿ 

¿ 

Although emotional distress may consist of any highly unpleasant mental reaction such as fright, grief, shame, humiliation, embarrassment, anger, chagrin, disappointment or worry [citation], to make out a claim, the plaintiff must prove that emotional distress was severe and not trivial or transient.” (Wong v. Tai Jing¿(2010) 189 Cal. App. 4th 1354, 1376.) Such distress must be “of such substantial quality or enduring quality that no reasonable person in civilized society should be expected to endure it.”¿(Potter v. Firestone Tire & Rubber Co.¿(1993) 6 Cal. 4th 965, 1004.)¿ 

¿ 

Here, Plaintiffs sufficiently alleged their claim for IIED. The element at issue is whether Plaintiffs alleged emotional distress. They did. (SAC ¶¶ 77, 144.) This allegation is bolstered by prior allegations of being children (or minors) at the time of living in extremely dilapidated conditions. (SAC ¶ 142.) This is sufficient for the pleadings stage.

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES the demurrer to the sixth cause of action.

 

Eighth Cause of Action – Willful Interruption of Services

 

The Crawford Defendants contend this claim fails because Plaintiffs remain in possession.

 

Plaintiffs argue this is a requirement for constructive eviction, not willful interruption of services.

 

“A landlord shall not with intent to terminate the occupancy under any lease or other tenancy or estate at will, however created, of property used by a tenant as his residence willfully cause, directly or indirectly, the interruption or termination of any utility service furnished the tenant, including, but not limited to, water, heat, light, electricity, gas, telephone, elevator, or refrigeration, whether or not the utility service is under the control of the landlord.”

(Civ. Code § 789.3.)

 

After reviewing the SAC, Plaintiffs sufficiently stated this claim. First, it does not matter that they remain in possession as that is not a statutory element. Second, Plaintiffs alleged they held a leasehold interest in the subject properties, the Crawford Defendant willfully interrupted utility services including those for heat and electricity, with the intent to terminate the occupancy/leasehold. (SAC ¶¶162, 163, 164.) This is sufficient for the pleadings stage.

 

Accordingly, the court OVERRULES the demurrer to the eighth cause of action.

 

Tenth Cause of Action – Violation of Unfair Competition Law

 

The Crawford Defendants contend this claim is separately time-barred because no tolling applies.

 

Plaintiffs argue that tolling applies because the UCL is not explicitly exempted from CCP § 352.

 

For similar reasons articulated as to the entire SAC, the court OVERRULES the demurrer on this ground.

Punitive Damages

 

The Crawford Defendants contend Plaintiffs insufficiently alleged facts supporting punitive damages and did not allege acts by corporate leaders.

 

Plaintiffs argue they sufficiently alleged facts supporting punitive damages via the Crawford Defendants’ endangering Plaintiffs’ health and safety through the dilapidated conditions of the subject properties. They argue further that they allege liability of the corporate entities via their agents Mark Crawford and Mary Crawford a/k/a Joanna Crawford.[2]

 

To obtain punitive damages, a plaintiff must plead sufficient facts in support of punitive damages.¿ (See¿Hilliard v. A.H. Robins Co.¿(1983) 148 Cal.App.3d 374, 391-92.)¿ In addition,¿punitive damages are allowed only where “it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.”¿ (Civ. Code, § 3294(a).)¿ Courts have viewed despicable conduct as conduct “so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people. (Scott v. Phoenix Schools, Inc., (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 702, 715.) Further, Civil Code § 3294(c) provides the definition of malice, oppression, and fraud. Malice is “conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others.” (Ibid.) Oppression is “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person's rights.” (Ibid.) Fraud is “an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury.”¿(Ibid.)¿¿ 

 

In Stoiber v. Honeychuck, the premises was so dilapidated that it needed to be torn down. ((1980) 101 Cal.App.3d 903, 912 [describing the following defects identified by the Kern County health Department: “heavy cockroach infestation, broken interior walls broken deteriorated flooring on front porch, falling ceiling, deteriorated, overfused electrical wiring, lack of proper plumbing connection to sewage system in bathroom, sewage under bathroom floor, leaking roof, broken, windows, and fire hazard.”]) The Court there found that the plaintiff pled sufficient facts to support her prayer for punitive damages because the defendant had actual knowledge of defective conditions in the premises, acted with full knowledge of the consequences thereof and the damage being caused to plaintiff, and their conduct was willful, oppressive, and malicious. (Id. at p. 920.)

 

Here, as in Stoiber, Plaintiffs alleged that the Crawford Defendants had actual knowledge of the defects, that the defects were causing Plaintiff damages, and that Defendants’ conduct was willful, oppressive, and malicious. (SAC ¶ 6; see also SAC ¶¶ 55-72 [multiple reports from governmental inspections].) This is sufficient for the pleadings stage.

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES the motion to strike punitive damages.

 

Attorneys’ Fees

 

There is no argument supporting this request. The court considers it abandoned. [See, e.g., Atchley v. City of Fresno (1984) 151 Cal. App. 3d 635, 647 [“Where a point is merely asserted by appellant's counsel without any argument of or authority for the proposition, it is deemed to be without foundation and requires no discussion by the reviewing court.”])

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES the motion to strike as to attorneys’ fees request.
