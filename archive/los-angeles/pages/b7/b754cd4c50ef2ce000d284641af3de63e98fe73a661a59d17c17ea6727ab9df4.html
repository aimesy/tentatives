Case Number: 24STLC09540    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 26

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiffs Melvin Anthony Hanible, Jr. and Jeremy Jamar White’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal is granted. The dismissal entered on 06/30/2026 is hereby vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service of Summons and Complaint is scheduled for 08/27/2026 at 09:30 AM in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Plaintiffs Melvin Anthony Hanible, Jr. and Jeremy Jamar White (“Plaintiffs”) filed the instant action for motor vehicle negligence against Defendant Edgardo Melgard (“Defendant”) on December 31, 2024. Following Plaintiffs’ failure to appear for trial on June 30, 2026, the Court dismissed the action without prejudice. (Minute Order, 06/30/26.)

 

Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion to Vacate Dismissal on July 1, 2026. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

The Motion is brought pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure, section 473, subdivision (b). Under this statute, an application for relief must be made no more than six months after entry of the order from which relief is sought and must be accompanied by an affidavit of fault attesting to the party or attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise or neglect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b); English v. IKON Business Solutions (2001) 94 Cal.App.4th 130, 143.) A timely request for relief from an entry of default, default judgment, or involuntary dismissal, based on attorney fault, must be granted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).)

 

Plaintiffs timely filed the Motion one day after dismissal of the action and included an attorney affidavit of fault explaining that Plaintiff’s counsel failed to appear due to a calendaring error. (Motion, Fu Decl., ¶¶5-6.) Based on the timely request for relief and supporting attorney affidavit of fault, the case must be reinstated pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b).

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiffs Melvin Anthony Hanible, Jr. and Jeremy Jamar White’s Motion to Vacate Dismissal is granted. The dismissal entered on 06/30/2026 is hereby vacated.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Failure to File Proof of Service of Summons and Complaint is scheduled for 08/27/2026 at 09:30 AM in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

Case Number: 25NWLC51459    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion for Order for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default; Request for Monetary Sanctions is granted. The Court strikes Defendant Norberto Ochoa’s Answer filed on 11/12/2025 but does not enter default or award Defendant monetary sanctions.

 

Order to Show Cause Regarding Entry of Default and Default Judgment is set for 08/27/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On September 8, 2025, Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc. (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Norberto Ochoa (“Defendant”). Defendant, in propria persona, filed an Answer on November 12, 2025.

 

On March 12, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Special Interrogatories; Request for Monetary Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to First Set of Requests for Production of Documents; Request for Monetary Sanctions, against Defendants with respect to service of responses. (Minute Order, 03/12/26.) The Court ordered Defendant to serve verified discovery responses within 20 days’ service of the order. (Ibid.) Notice of the ruling was mailed to Defendant on March 16, 2026. (Notice of Ruling, filed 03/16/26.)

 

On May 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Order for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default; Request for Monetary Sanctions. Plaintiff served the Motion on Defendant by electronic and first-class mail on the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff seeks an order striking Defendant’s Answer under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, pursuant to which the Court may impose a terminating sanction by one of the following orders:

 

(1)   An order striking out the pleadings or parts of the pleadings of any party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process.

(2)   An order staying further proceedings by that party until an order for discovery is obeyed.

(3)   An order dismissing the action, or any part of the action, of that party.

(4)   An order rendering a judgment by default against that party.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (d).) Where a party willfully disobeys a discovery order, courts have the discretion to impose terminating, issue, evidence or monetary sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2023.010, subds. (d), (g); R.S. Creative, Inc. v. Creative Cotton, Ltd. (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 486, 495.) The court should look at the totality of the circumstances in determining whether terminating sanctions are appropriate. (Lang v. Hochman (2000) 77 Cal.App.4th 1225, 1246.) Ultimate discovery sanctions are justified where there is a willful discovery order violation, a history of abuse, and evidence showing that less severe sanctions would not produce compliance with discovery rules. (Van Sickle v. Gilbert (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 1495, 1516.) “[A] penalty as severe as dismissal or default is not authorized where noncompliance with discovery is caused by an inability to comply rather than willfulness or bad faith.” (Brown v. Sup. Ct. (1986) 180 Cal.App.3d 701, 707.)

 

As set forth above, on March 12, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s discovery motions, and ordered Defendant to serve responses within 20 days’ service of the order. (Motions, Omrani Decl., ¶6 and Exh. 2.) Notice of the ruling was served on Defendant on March 16, 2026. (Ibid.) As of the filing of the instant Motion, however, Defendant has not served responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶7.) 

 

Under the above authority, terminating sanctions are warranted for Plaintiff’s non-compliance with the discovery orders. Despite notice of the discovery motions, service of the Court’s orders, and service of the instant Motion for sanctions, Plaintiff has not served responses as ordered nor filed any opposition to explain its disregard of its discovery obligations and the Court’s order.  Defendant’s repeated refusal to engage in the discovery process demonstrates that their compliance with the Court’s orders cannot be achieved through lesser sanctions. Although terminating sanctions are a harsh penalty, “[t]he court [is] not required to allow a pattern of abuse to continue ad infinitum.” (Mileikowsky v. Tenet Healthsystem (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 262, 280.)  When a party fails to respond to discovery, fails to comply with orders to respond to discovery, and then does not respond to a motion for terminating sanctions, it establishes a pattern of abuse that the Court has no basis to conclude will cease.  Accordingly, the Court strikes Defendant’s Answer. However, it does not enter Defendant’s default, which is not an authorized action under the moving statute.

 

Plaintiff also moves for monetary sanctions under subdivision (a) of the moving statute, on account of Defendant’s misuse of the discovery process. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.030, subd. (a).) Failing to respond to an authorized method of discovery, especially in light of the Court’s orders, is a misuse under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.010. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subd. (d).) Plaintiff moves for an award of $1,873.82, which the Court finds excessive. (See Motion, Omrani Decl., ¶8.) Moreover, the terminating sanctions sufficiently address the abuse of the discovery process.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff Creditors Adjustment Bureau, Inc.’s Motion for Order for Terminating Sanctions, Striking Defendant’s Answer and Entering Default; Request for Monetary Sanctions is granted. The Court strikes Defendant Norberto Ochoa’s Answer filed on 11/12/2025 but does not enter default or award Defendant monetary sanctions.


Order to Show Cause Regarding Entry of Default and Default Judgment is set for 08/27/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.

 

 

Moving party to give notice.


Case Number: 25STLC0523    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Plaintiff International Fidelity Insurance Company’s Motion for Summary Judgment is granted as to Defendant Dallas Hijazeen.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Entry of Default and Default Judgment Against Defendant Planet Cars Inc. dba Cash Or Finance Auto is scheduled for 010/08/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.  Plaintiff to give notice to both Defendants and file proof of notice within five days.  Plaintiff may seek to have Defendant Planet Car’s default entered no sooner 30 days after giving notice.

 

                                                                                                                               

Analysis:

 

On July 9, 2025, Plaintiff International Fidelity Insurance Company (“Plaintiff”) filed the instant action for breach of contract against Defendants Planet Cars Inc. dba Cash Or Finance Auto (“Defendant Planet Cars”) and Dallas Hijazeen (“Defendant Hijazeen”) (together, “Defendants”). On September 22, 2025, an Answer was filed to the Complaint by Defendant Hijazeen, purporting to be filed on behalf of “Defendants Planet Cars Inc. dba Cash Or Finance Auto.” (Answer, filed 09/22/25, p. 1.)  Only Defendant Hijazeen has paid a first appearance fee.

 

On April 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant Motion for Summary Judgment, seeking judgment in its favor as to both Defendants, which it served on Defendant Hijazeen by first-class and electronic mail on the same day. Defendant Hijazeen is also Defendant Planet Car’s agent for service of process, but the proof of service does not list Defendant Hijazeen in that separate capacity.  No opposition has been filed to date.

 

Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for summary judgment on the Complaint pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 437c. On a motion for summary judgment or adjudication of a particular cause of action, a moving plaintiff must show that there is no defense by proving each element of the cause of action entitling the party to judgment on that cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).) Then the burden shifts to the defendant to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action or a defense. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).) Additionally, in ruling on the Motion, the Court must view the “evidence [citations] and such inferences [citations], in the light most favorable to the opposing party.” (Intrieri v. Superior Court (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 72, 81 [citing Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 843].)

 

Allegations in the Complaint

 

The Complaint alleges a single cause of action for breach of indemnity agreement based on the following facts. The parties entered into an indemnity agreement on or about September 3, 2015, pursuant to which Plaintiff issued a Motor Vehicle Dealer License Bond in the amount of $50,000.00. (Compl., Attachment BC-2 and Exh. B.) After Plaintiff issued the Bond, it received claims and civil actions from consumers who purchased vehicles from Defendants. (Id. at Attachment BC-2.) Plaintiff demanded that Defendants defend the claims and hold Plaintiff harmless, but Defendants refused. (Ibid.) Defendants thereby breached the agreement on or about February 14, 2023. (Compl., ¶¶BC-1, BC-2.) Plaintiff was forced to retain counsel and defend the claims and civil actions, which it later settled in good faith. (Id. at Attachment BC-2.) Plaintiff paid $20,200.00 to the claimants and to counsel for fees to defend the civil actions. (Ibid.) Plaintiff made demand on Defendants for indemnification, but they failed to indemnify Plaintiff. (Ibid.) As a result, Plaintiff has been damaged in the amount of $21,206.50, plus interest according to proof. (Id. at ¶BC-4.)

 

Cause of Action for Breach of Equipment Finance Agreement

 

The elements of a cause of action for breach of contract are (1) the existence of contract; (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance; (3) defendant’s breach (or anticipatory breach); and (4) resulting damage. (Wall Street Network, Ltd. v. N. Y. Times Co. (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 1171, 1178.)

 

In support of the motion, Plaintiff presents the following facts as undisputed. On September 3, 2015, Defendants executed a personal indemnity agreement in favor of Plaintiff to obtain a $50,000 motor vehicle dealer license bond (“the Bond”). The indemnity agreement required Defendants to indemnify and hold Plaintiff harmless against any claim brought against Plaintiff by an alleged victim of misconduct committed by Defendants. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 1; Sordillo Decl., ¶¶2-3 and Exh. 1 at ¶2.) The indemnity agreement covers all losses incurred by Plaintiff. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 1; Sordillo Decl., ¶¶2-3 and Exh. 1 at ¶2.) When two civil actions were filed against Plaintiff under the Bond, Plaintiff gave notice to Defendants and demanded to be held harmless. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 2; Sordillo Decl., ¶¶5-6 and Exhs. 3-4.) Defendants did not hold Plaintiff harmless or indemnify Plaintiff. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 3; Sordillo Decl., ¶7.) On counsel’s advice, Plaintiff settled the civil actions for $10,000.00 and $6,500.00, respectively. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 4; Sordillo Decl., ¶8 and Exh. 5.) Plaintiff incurred legal expenses of $4,706.50 to defend the civil actions. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 1; Sordillo Decl., ¶¶9-10 and Exh. 5.) Defendants refused to indemnify Plaintiff after demand was sent; Plaintiff has incurred damages of $21,206.50, plus interest at ten percent per annum from February 14, 2023 until entry of judgment. (Motion, Separate Statement, Fact No. 6; Sordillo Decl., ¶11.)

 

This evidence carries Plaintiff’s initial burden of proof with respect to the cause of action for breach of contract. The burden now shifts to Defendants to demonstrate the existence of a triable issue of material fact. However, no opposition has been filed to date.

 

The Court grants the motion as to Defendant Hijazeen only.  A motion for summary judgment may be filed no sooner than 60 days after a party’s appearance in the action.  (Code Civ. Proc., 437c(a)(1).)  Defendant Planet Cars has not yet made a first appearance in this action so any motion for summary judgment is premature.  The Answer filed on 9/22/2025 purports to be filed on its behalf, however it was filed and signed only by Defendant Hijazeen in pro per, and corporations must appear through counsel. (CLD Constr., Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal. App. 4th 1141, 1145 (A corporation “must be represented by licensed counsel in proceedings before courts of record”).) Further Defendant Planet Cars has not paid an appearance fee, and therefore cannot file documents in this case.  (See Government Code, § 70612; Hu v. Silgan Containers Corp. (1999) 70 Cal. App. 4th 1261, 1268 (“A clerk may properly refuse to file a document until the filing fee is paid, and the document is not deemed filed until payment.”)  The Answer is not effective as to Defendant Planet Cars for that additional reason.   These defects are curable, however they have not been cured to date.

 

In light of Defendant Planet Cars’ failure to Answer or otherwise respond to the Complaint following service, the Court sets an Order to Show Cause re Entry of Default and Default Judgment on the date indicated below.  Plaintiff must give notice of this order to both Defendants.  Plaintiff may seek to have Defendant Planet Car’s default entered no sooner 30 days after giving notice.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff International Fidelity Insurance Company’s Motion for Summary Judgment is granted as to Defendant Dallas Hijazeen.

 

Order to Show Cause Re: Entry of Default and Default Judgment Against Defendant Planet Cars Inc. dba Cash Or Finance Auto is scheduled for 010/08/2026 at 9:30 am in Department 26 at the Spring Street Courthouse.  Plaintiff to give notice to both Defendants and file proof of notice within five days.  Plaintiff may seek to have Defendant Planet Car’s default entered no sooner 30 days after giving notice.

Case Number: 26STCP01237    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 26

 

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Petitioner John Panek’s Petition to Vacate Contractual Arbitration Award is denied.

 

Petition of Respondent Kaise Corporation to Confirm Arbitration Award is advanced from 09/14/2026 and granted. The arbitration award is confirmed in favor of Respondent Kaise Corporation in the amount of $14,589.00 and with the directive that “Respondent shall remove Mechanic’s Lien No. 20250291047 filed against Complainant’s property within fourteen (14) calendar days after receipt of Award payment from Complainant.”

 

Respondent is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days of this ruling.

 

 

Analysis:

 

On March 26, 2026, Petitioner John Panek (“Petitioner”) filed the instant Petition to Vacate Contractual Arbitration Award against Respondent Ramirez / Kaise Corporation (“Respondent”). On May 11, 2026, Petitioner filed an Amended Petition to Vacate Contractual Arbitration Award.

 

Respondent filed a Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award on June 10, 2026, to which Petitioner filed a combined answer and opposition on July 1, 2026. The Petition to Confirm Arbitration Award is set for hearing on September 14, 2026. Respondent also filed an opposition to the Amended Petition to Vacate Arbitration Award on July 9, 2026.

 

Discussion

 

The Petition to Vacate is not a model of clarity. It purports to vacate an arbitration award issued on February 18, 2026, but which is not attached to the Petition. (See Pet., Exh. G.) The request to vacate is brought on the basis that the award was obtained by corruption, fraud, or other unfair means, the arbitrator was corrupt, misconduct of a neutral arbitrator substantially prejudiced Petitioner's rights, the arbitrator exceeded his or her authority, and the award cannot be fairly corrected, the arbitrator unfairly refused to postpone the hearing or to hear evidence useful to settle the dispute, and the arbitrator should have disqualified himself or herself after Petitioner made a demand to do so. (Pet., ¶10c.)

 

Filing Requirements of a Petition to Confirm, Correct, or Vacate Arbitration Award (CCP § 1285.4)

 

“A petition under this chapter shall:

 

(a)   Set forth the substance of or have attached a copy of the agreement to arbitrate unless the petitioner denies the existence of such an agreement.

 

(b)   Set forth the names of the arbitrators.

 

(c) Set forth or have attached a copy of the award and the written opinion of the arbitrators, if any.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1285.4, subds. (a)-(c).)

 

The Petition does not meet these requirements because it neither names the arbitrator nor attaches a copy of the arbitration award.

 

 

Service of Petition and Notice of Hearing (CCP § 1290.4)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4 states in pertinent part:

 

“(a) A copy of the petition and a written notice of the time and place of the hearing thereof and any other papers upon which the petition is based shall be served in the manner provided in the arbitration agreement for the service of such petition and notice.

 

(b) If the arbitration agreement does not provide the manner in which such service shall be made and the person upon whom service is to be made has not previously appeared in the proceeding and has not previously been served in accordance with this subdivision: ¶ (1) Service within this State shall be made in the manner provided by law for the service of summons in an action.”

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1290.4, subds. (a)-(b).) No proof of service of the Petition to Vacate, Amended Petition to Vacate, or Notice of Hearing is on file with the Court. Respondent raises the lack of proper service in its opposition to the Petition to Vacate, and in doing so, objects to the Court’s exercise of jurisdiction without proper service.

 

Respondent received the original Petition by certified mail, but did not sign or return the acknowledgement of receipt. (Opp., Franklin Jr. Decl., ¶11 and Exh. 3.) Service by mail is only complete “on the date a written acknowledgment of receipt of summons is executed, if such acknowledgment thereafter is returned to the sender.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 415.30, subd. (c).) The requirements for service by mail, therefore, are not met. Petitioner also emailed Respondent the original Petition, which is not an authorized method to serve a summons under Code of Civil Procedure section 415.10, et seq. (Opp., Franklin Jr. Decl., ¶12 and Exh. 5.) With respect to the Amended Petition, no record of service exists and Respondent denies that service was even attempted. (Id. at ¶13.)

 

The service requirements under Code of Civil Procedure section 1290.4, therefore, are not met and the Court lacks jurisdiction over Respondent. (See Abers v. Rohrs (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 1199, 1206.)

 

Deadline for Service of Petition (CCP § 1288)

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 1288, “[a] petition to vacate an award or to correct an award shall be served and filed not later than 100 days after the date of the service of a signed copy of the award on the petitioner. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1288.) The arbitration award was served on both parties on February 18, 2026. (Opp., Franklin Jr. Decl., Exh. 2, p. 3.) The 100-day deadline, therefore, expired on May 29, 2026. As Petitioner has not demonstrated service of the Petition by the deadline, the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 1288 are also not satisfied.

 

These jurisdictional defects require the Court to deny the Petition to Vacate Contractual Arbitration Award.

 

Grounds to Vacate the Arbitration Award

 

Even if the Court were to consider the substance of the Petition despite the service defects discussed above, the Petition does not refer to any specific legal authority to vacate the award other than the broad reference to Code of Civil Procedure section 1285, et seq. in the standard language of the Judicial Council form. The bases to vacate the award raised in the Petition correspond to those grounds provided for in Code of Civil Procedure section 1286.2, subdivision (a)(1) to (a)(6). The Petition, however, does not provide coherent evidence to support these claims. Petitioner provides no supporting declaration and attaches an assortment of exhibits without clearly explaining their relation to the grounds raised to vacate the award. (See Pet., pp. 5, 8-19.) Therefore, Petitioner has also not demonstrated a substantive basis to vacate the arbitration award.

 

Conclusion

 

Petitioner John Panek’s Petition to Vacate Contractual Arbitration Award is denied.

 

Petition of Respondent Kaise Corporation to Confirm Arbitration Award is advanced from 09/14/2026 and granted. The arbitration award is confirmed in favor of Respondent Kaise Corporation in the amount of $14,589.00 and with the directive that “Respondent shall remove Mechanic’s Lien No. 20250291047 filed against Complainant’s property within fourteen (14) calendar days after receipt of Award payment from Complainant.”

 

Respondent Kaise Corporation is to file and serve a proposed judgment in accordance with this order within 20 days of this ruling.

 

 

Respondent Kaise Corporation to give notice.

Case Number: 26STLC00094    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: 26

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

Defendant Diamond Environmental Services, LP’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

Plaintiff Xavier Dunn is ordered to serve responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Defendant’s requests for sanctions are denied.

 

 

Analysis:

 

Defendant Diamond Environmental Services, LP (“Defendant”) served Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Requests for Production of Documents, Set One, on Plaintiff Xavier Dunn on April 23, 2026. (Motions, Ghomi Decl., Exh. A.) Despite a meet and confer effort, to date, Plaintiff Xavier Dunn has not served verified responses to the discovery requests. (Id. at ¶¶6-8 and Exh. 2) Defendant filed the instant (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions on June 25, 2026. The Motions were served on Plaintiff Xavier Dunn through Attorney Sergei N. Gladkov, Esq. by electronic mail the same day. No opposition has been filed to date.

 

There is no requirement for a prior meet and confer effort before a motion to compel initial responses can be filed. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Further, the motions can be brought at any time after the responding party fails to provide the responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290, 2031.300.) Based on Plaintiff Xavier Dunn’s failure to timely respond to the propounded discovery, Defendant is entitled to an order compelling service of verified responses to the discovery requests without objections.

 

Defendant moves for sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030. In City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, the Supreme Court observed:

 

It is already well-established that a court may not rely on [Code of Civil Procedure] section 2023.030 to override the limitations prescribed by any other applicable sanctions provision in the [Civil Discovery] Act. A court may invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 only when confronted with an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision. And where it invokes that authority, it is constrained by the long-settled rules generally governing the imposition of discovery sanctions under the Act.

 

(City of Los Angeles v. Pricewaterhousecoopers, LLP (2024) 17 Cal.5th 46, 74-75.) The moving statutes, Code of Civil Procedure sections 2030.290 and 2031.300, only make sanctions available when a party unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c); § 2031.300, subd. (c).) Plaintiff Xavier Dunn filed no opposition to the instant Motions and cannot be sanctioned under sections 2030.290 or 2031.310. Nor has Defendant shown that Plaintiff Xavier Dunn’s failure to serve discovery responses is “an unusual form of discovery abuse, or a pattern of abuse, not already addressed by a relevant sanctions provision.” Therefore, the Court declines to invoke its independent authority to impose sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030.

 

 

 

Conclusion

 

Defendant Diamond Environmental Services, LP’s (1) Motion to Compel Responses to Form Interrogatories and Request for Sanctions; and (2) Motion to Compel Responses to Requests for Production of Documents and Request for Monetary Sanctions, are granted in part and denied in part.

Plaintiff Xavier Dunn is ordered to serve responses to the Form Interrogatories, Set One, and Request for Production of Documents, Set One, within 20 days’ service of this order. Defendant’s requests for sanctions are denied.
