DEPARTMENT 514 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV02925    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 514

Anderson v. Williams
23STCV02925
Defendant’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings or, in the Alternative, to Strike

Tentative Ruling

The Court will call this matter.

The Court’s tentative ruling is that the request for judgment on the pleadings is denied and the requests to strike are denied in part and granted in part.

Background

On February 10, 2023, Durand Anderson (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against Thelma Williams and Does 1 through 10 for (1) breach of warranty of habitability, (2) negligent maintenance of premises, (3) nuisance, (4) breach of quiet enjoyment, and (5) intentional infliction of emotional distress, all arising out of alleged habitability issues with a residence located at 1555 West 80th Street in Los Angeles.

On June 28, 2023, Plaintiff’s counsel filed a declaration with the Court stating that Defendant Williams was deceased.

July 3, 2023, Plaintiff amended the complaint to name Vickie Buie, successor trustee of the Thelma E. Williams Revocable Trust (“Buie”), as Doe 1.

On September 27, 2023, Defendant Buie filed an answer.

On November 17, 2023, Buie filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff.  Plaintiff filed an answer to the cross-complaint on December 5, 2023.

On June 18, 2026, Defendant Buie filed this motion for judgment on the pleadings or, in the alternative, to strike.  Plaintiff filed an opposition July 21, and Buie filed a reply on August 4.

Trial is set for August 17, 2026.

Legal Standard

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

California practice recognizes two types of motions for judgment on the pleadings.  

First, there is a statutory motion.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 438.)  A defendant may bring a statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings when “the complaint does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against that defendant.”  (Id., subd. (c)(1)(B)(ii).)  A statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings must be filed within certain time limits, and it is subject to a statutory meet-and-confer requirement.  (Id., subd. (e); Code Civ. Proc., § 439.)  The grounds for a statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings must “appear on the face of the challenged pleading or from any matter of which the court is required to take judicial notice.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 438, subd. (d).)

Second, there is a non-statutory motion for judgment on the pleadings, which “may be made at any time either prior to the trial or at the trial itself.”  (Ion Equipment Corp. v. Nelson (1980) 110 Cal.App.3d 868, 877; see also Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota) (1995) 11 Cal.4th 138, 145 fn. 2; Tarin v. Lind (2020) 47 Cal.App.5th 395, 402; Stoops v. Abbassi (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 644, 650.)    

A motion for judgment on the pleadings is the functional equivalent of a demurrer.  (People ex rel. Harris v. Pac Anchor Transp. (2014) 59 Cal.4th 772, 77; City of Rancho Palos Verdes v. State (2025) 114 Cal.App.5th 13, 23.)  “The standard for granting a motion for judgment on the pleadings is essentially the same as that applicable to a general demurrer, that is, under the state of the pleadings, together with matters that may be judicially noticed, it appears that a party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”  (Bezirdjian v. O'Reilly (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 316, 321-322; accord Los Angeles Unified School Dist. v. Torres Construction Corp. (2020) 57 Cal.App.5th 480, 494; Templo v. State (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 730, 735; Southern California Edison Co. v. City of Victorville (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 218, 227; see also 1 Weil & Brown, California Practice Guide: Civil Procedure Before Trial [2025] ¶ 7:275.)

Motion to Strike

Under Code of Civil Procedure section 435, “Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1).)

Code of Civil Procedure section 436 provides:

“The court may, upon a motion made pursuant to Section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems proper:

(a) Strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.

(b) Strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) In ruling on a motion to strike, the court must assume the truth of the properly pleaded facts in the complaint or other pleading. (Turman v. Turning Point of Central California, Inc. (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 53, 63.)

Before a motion to strike is filed, the parties must “meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 435.5, subd. (a).) 

Requests for Judicial Notice

Defendant Buie asks the Court to take judicial notice of (1) a County of Los Angeles Certificate of Death stating that Thelma Elrean Williams died on November 27, 2022; and (2) Plaintiff’s Complaint.

Plaintiff asks the Court to take judicial notice of (1) Defendant’s Cross-Complaint and (2) the Doe Amendment naming Defendant Buie as Doe 1.

The requests to take judicial notice of documents in the Court’s file are granted, but Plaintiff had previously filed a declaration with the Court attesting to the death of Defendant Williams.  This fact is not reasonably in dispute.  The request for judicial notice of the death certificate, an official government document, is granted.

Discussion

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

Defendant Buie brings this non-statutory motion and moves for judgment on the pleadings as follows: (1) on every cause of action against Defendant Williams on the ground that she died before this action was filed; and (2) on the Fifth Cause of Action (for intentional infliction of emotional distress) on the ground that the complaint fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

Causes of Action Against Williams

Defendant Buie seeks an order granting her motion for judgment on the pleadings on all causes of action against Defendant Williams.  (Mem., at pp. 6-7.)  Defendant Buie does not assert that she represents an estate of Defendant Williams or otherwise has any authority to act on behalf of Defendant Williams.  Accordingly, the motion of Defendant Buie to obtain relief on behalf of Defendant Williams is denied.

The Court recognizes that Defendant Williams was never served and in fact died before this action was filed.  The Court expects that, at or before the time of trial, Plaintiff will dismiss Defendant Williams. 

Fifth Cause of Action (Intentional Infliction of Emotional Distress)

Defendant Buie also seeks an order granting her motion for judgment on the pleadings on the Fifth Cause of Action (for intentional infliction of emotional distress) asserted against her in the complaint. 

The elements of a cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress (“IIED”) are: (1) that the defendant engaged in “extreme and outrageous conduct … with the intention of causing, or reckless disregard of the probability of causing, emotional distress”; (2) that the plaintiff suffered “severe or extreme emotional distress”; and (3) that the plaintiff's injuries were actually and proximately caused by the defendant's outrageous conduct. (Hughes v. Pair (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1035, 1050-1051; see also Christensen v. Super. Ct. (1991) 54 Cal.3d 868, 905-906; Colonial Van & Storage, Inc. v. Super. Ct. (2022) 76 Cal.App.5th 487, 506; Wilson v. Hynek (2012) 207 Cal.App.4th 999, 1009; CACI No. 1600.) 

Conduct is “outrageous” if it “exceed[s] all bounds of that usually tolerated in a civilized community.”  (Hughes, supra, 46 Cal.4th at p. 1051.)  “[M]ere insults” and “other trivialities” are not sufficient.  (Ibid.; see also, e.g., Crouch v. Trinity Christian Center (2019) 39 Cal.App.5th 995, 1007.)  The conduct must be directed at the plaintiff or, alternatively, defendant must act with reckless disregard of plaintiff and the probability that the conduct will cause severe emotional distress to plaintiff.  (Christensen, supra, 54 Cal.3d at pp. 905-906; So v. Shin (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 652, 671.)  Conduct may be considered outrageous, depending on all of the circumstances, if a defendant “(1) abuses a relation or position which gives [the defendant] power to damage the plaintiff's interest; (2) knows the plaintiff is susceptible to injuries through mental distress; or (3) acts intentionally or unreasonably with the recognition that the acts are likely to result in illness through mental distress.”  (Molko v. Holy Spirit Ass’n (1988) 46 Cal.3d 1092, 1122; CACI No. 1602.)  Outrageous behavior can also occur when a defendant knows of a dangerous condition, fails to warn about the danger, and the plaintiff suffers physical harm as a result.  (Wilson v. Southern California Edison Co. (2015) 234 Cal.App.4th 123, 152.)

In the complaint, Plaintiff alleges that he rented a residential property on West 80th Street in Los Angeles (the “Property”); that Defendants (including Defendant Buie as Doe 1) owned and operated the Property; and that the Property was “generally unsafe, unsanitary, unhealthy, uninhabitable, untenantable, in a serious state of disrepair, and in gross violation of building, health and safety laws.”  (Compl., ¶¶ 1, 4, 19.)  Plaintiff alleges that he complained of the conditions to Defendants, who fraudulently told him repairs would be made; no repairs were made. (Compl., ¶ 21.) Plaintiff alleges that Defendant knew these representations were false and were made for the sole purpose of exploiting Plaintiff. (Compl., ¶ 22.) Plaintiff alleges that the Property contained roach infestation, bed bugs, plumbing issues, mold, deteriorated kitchen floor, faulty electrical, inoperable windows and doors, no cold water, and no functional heater. (Compl., ¶ 23.) Plaintiff alleges that he and members of his family became ill with rashes and breathing issues arising from these conditions of the Property. (Compl., ¶ 23.)  Plaintiff states that Defendant knew of these conditions prior to Plaintiff’s tenancy and failed to disclose them. (Compl., ¶¶ 24-25.) Defendant failed and refused to make any repairs after Plaintiff’s request and report to the Housing Authority of the County of San Bernardino. (Compl., ¶ 38.)  And any repairs that were made after the Housing Authority issued orders for repairs were delayed and inadequate/ineffective. (Compl., ¶ 39.)  After Defendants were ordered to correct the conditions of Property, Defendants harassed Plaintiff with the threat of eviction. (Compl., ¶ 40.)  Moreover, Plaintiff alleges, Defendants’ conduct, including the retaliation and harassment, was based in part on Plaintiff’s race.  (Compl., ¶¶ 122-126.)

In ruling on a motion for judgment on the pleadings, the Court must accept the allegations in the complaint as true.  Taking the allegations in the complaint as true, Plaintiff has alleged sufficient facts to support his Fifth Cause of Action.  Defendants’ conduct, as alleged, went far beyond a few unkind words or unkept promises.

The motion for judgment on the pleadings on the Fifth Cause of Action is denied.

Motion to Strike

Defendant Buie moves to strike: (1) the allegations of discrimination in paragraphs 5, 122-126, and 123 of the complaint; and (2) the prayer for punitive damages under Civil Code section 1942.5, subdivision (f).

As a threshold matter, the Court notes that a motion to strike is statutory in nature, and Defendant Buie’s motion is not timely.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 435, subd. (b)(1).)  Under Code of Civil Procedure section 436, however, the Court may make an order striking allegations from a pleading “at any time in its discretion.”  Given that trial is scheduled to commence soon, and given that Plaintiff has had ample notice and an opportunity to be heard, the Court, in its discretion, will consider Defendant Buie’s late motion to strike.

Discrimination Allegations (Paragraphs 5, 122-126, and 133)

Defendant Buie seeks to strike the allegations of discrimination appearing in paragraphs 5, 122-126, and 133 of the complaint.  She argues that these allegations are “immaterial,” but that is not accurate.  Plaintiff brings a cause of action for intentional infliction of emotional distress; Plaintiff alleges (among other things) that he was subject to harassment and retaliation motivated, in part, by his race.  The Court must accept these allegations as true in ruling on a motion to strike.  And if these allegations are true, the discrimination issue is material to liability and damages on the intentional infliction of emotional distress cause of action.

Prayer for Punitive Damages Under Civil Code Section 1942.5, Subdivision (f)

In his complaint, Plaintiff seeks to recover punitive damages under Civil Code sections 1942.5 and 3294.  Plaintiff seeks to strike the reference to section 1942.5.  Plaintiff does not, however, seek to strike the request for punitive damages under Civil Code section 3294.  (Mem., at p. 10.)

The Court begins by noting that there is no provision for punitive damages in subdivision (f) of Civil Code section 1942.5.  There is a provision for punitive damages in subdivision (h)(2) of that section, but Plaintiff in his complaint does not plead a cause of action for retaliatory eviction or any other violation of Civil Code section 1942.5.

Accordingly, the motion to strike the reference to section 1942.5, subdivision (f) in the sixth item of the prayer for relief (on page 28, line 13 of the complaint) is granted.

In ordinary circumstances, the Court would grant Plaintiff leave to amend.  As trial is scheduled to commence shortly, however, the Court will call this matter to discuss whether Plaintiff seeks leave to amend, which will necessitate some further delay in the trial.

Conclusion

The Court DENIES the motion of Defendant Vickie Buie, successor trustee to the Thelma E. Williams Revocable Trust, for judgment on the pleadings.

The Court DENIES IN PART and GRANTS IN PART the motion of Defendant Vickie Buie, successor trustee to the Thelma E. Williams Revocable Trust, to strike portions of the complaint.

The Court STRIKES the reference to Civil Code section 1942.5, subdivision (f), on page 28, line 13 of the complaint.

The Court otherwise DENIES the motion to strike.

The Court WILL CALL this matter regarding whether Plaintiff seeks leave to amend.

Moving party is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 23STCV06505    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 514

Williams v. Shim
23STCV06505 
Order to Show Cause Re: Default Judgment

Tentative Ruling

Plaintiff Mahogany Williams submitted a request for entry of default judgment on June 10, 2026.  That request is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.

Plaintiff seeks entry of a judgment consisting of $39,448.10 in special damages, $70,000 in general damages, $3,429.95 in interest, and $2,292.56 in costs, for a total of $115,170.61. 

The Court notes the following issues. 

First, Plaintiff has submitted evidence of special damages in the amount of $9,448.10, not the requested $39,448.10.  

Second, in light of the evidence regarding special damages, Plaintiff has not presented evidence sufficient to support her claim for $70,000 in general damages.  

Third, Plaintiff seeks costs for mailing and investigations, which are not recoverable under Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, subdivision (b).

The Order to Show Cause is CONTINUED for approximately 60 days.  Plaintiff may submit a new request for entry of judgment supported by any additional evidence and/or a revised proposed judgment.

Plaintiff to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCP04820    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 514

The court will call this matter.

Case Number: 25STCV000340    Hearing Date: August 11, 2026    Dept: 514

Taghiof v. Levi
25STCV000340
Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Defendant Daniel S. Levi to Provide Further Responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One)
Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Defendant Daniel S. Levi to Provide Further Responses to Special Interrogatories (Set One)
Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Defendant Daniel S. Levi to Provide Further Responses to Requests for Production (Set One)

Tentative Ruling

The motions are denied.  The requests for sanctions are denied.

Background

This case arises out of a motor vehicle accident on October 23, 2023, on the 405 Freeway just south of National Boulevard in Los Angeles.

On January 7, 2025, Claire Jaleh Taghiof (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action.

On March 15, 2025, Daniel S. Levi and Jennifer Levi filed an answer.

On December 23, 2025, the Court denied, as moot, Plaintiff’s motion to compel further responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), and Request for Production of Documents (Set One), as further responses had been served while the motions were pending.

On May 19, 2026, Plaintiff filed three discovery motions: (1) Motion to Compel Defendant Daniel S. Levi (“Defendant”) to Provide Further Responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One); (2) Motion to Compel Defendant to Provide Further Responses to Special Interrogatories (Set One); and (3) Motion to Compel Defendant to Provide Further Responses to Requests for Production (Set One).  The motions were noticed for hearing on June 18.

On June 5, Defendant filed oppositions to the motions relating to the special interrogatories and requests for production. 

On June 11, Plaintiff filed a reply to those two oppositions and a notice that Defendant had not opposed the motion relating to the form interrogatories. 

On June 18, the Court continued the hearing on these motions. 

On July 1, Defendant filed an opposition to the motion relating to the form interrogatories. 

On July 7, Plaintiff filed a reply to that opposition.

Trial is set for April 19, 2027.

Legal Standard

“On receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete. (2) An exercise of the option to produce documents under Section 2030.230 is unwarranted or the required specification of those documents is inadequate. (3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (a).)

Notice of a motion to compel further responses must be given “within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing.” (Id., subd. (c).)

A motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a meet-and-confer declaration and a separate statement or, in the discretion of the Court, a “concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute.” (Id., subd. (b)(1) & (b)(2); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345.)

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a further response to interrogatories, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (d).)

“On receipt of a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, the demanding party may move for an order compelling further response to the demand if the demanding party deems that any of the following apply: (1) A statement of compliance with the demand is incomplete.  (2) A representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive.  (3) An objection in the response is without merit or too general.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a).)

Notice of a motion to compel further responses must be given “within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing.” (Id., subd. (c).)

A motion to compel further responses must set forth specific facts showing good cause for the discovery and must be accompanied by a meet-and-confer declaration and a separate statement or, in the discretion of the Court, a “concise outline of the discovery request and each response in dispute.” (Id., subd. (b)(1)-(3); Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345.)

“[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel further response to a demand, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (h).)

In Chapter 7 of the Civil Discovery Act, Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (a) provides, in pertinent part, that the court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that any person “engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct.” A “misuse of the discovery process” includes (among other things) failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery; making, without substantial justification, an unmeritorious objection to discovery; making an evasive response to a discovery request; disobeying a court order to provide discovery; and making or opposing, unsuccessfully, a motion to compel without substantial justification. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subds. (d)-(h).)

Discussion

A party moving to compel further responses to interrogatories or requests for production must file, with the motion, a declaration establishing that the moving party has satisfied the statutory requirement to meet and confer as set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.300, subd. (b)(1) & § 2031.3010, subd. (b)(2).) 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2016.040, as amended by the Legislature effective January 1, 2026, provides:

“A meet and confer declaration in support of a motion shall state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion.”

(Code Civ. Proc., § 2016.040, subd. (a).)

Plaintiff has not satisfied this requirement.  To the contrary, Plaintiff’s only attempt to meet and confer following service of the supplemental responses at issue in March 2026 was a single email.  (Mooshekh Decls., ¶¶ 16-18 & Exhs. 1.8, 2.8, 3.8.)

On that basis, the Court must deny all three motions.  The Court need not reach, and does not reach, the other arguments presented by the parties.

The motions to compel are denied.

As Plaintiff’s motion is denied, so is her request for sanctions.

The Court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions.  Although Plaintiff has brought an unsuccessful motion to compel, the Court finds that under all of the circumstances, the imposition of sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel would be unjust.

Conclusion

The Court DENIES the motion of Plaintiff Claire Jaleh Taghiof to compel Defendant Daniel Levi to provide further responses to Form Interrogatories (Set One).

The Court DENIES the motion of Plaintiff Claire Jaleh Taghiof to compel Defendant Daniel Levi to provide further responses to Special Interrogatories (Set One).

The Court DENIES the motion of Plaintiff Claire Jaleh Taghiof to compel Defendant Daniel Levi to provide further responses to Requests for Productions (Set One).

The Court DENIES both parties’ requests for sanctions.

Moving party is ordered to give notice.
