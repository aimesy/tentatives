Case Number: 24STCV16465    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 10 – Swenson He, LLC v. Lothian Road WRX, Inc., et al.

Defendant Grover Henry Colin Nix, IV’s Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

Defendant Grover Henry Colin Nix, IV moves for judgment on the pleadings as to Plaintiff’s second cause of action for implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

Request for Judicial Notice

          Defendant requests the Court take judicial notice of its tentative ruling issued for the June 9, 2026 hearing on Defendant’s demurrer.

          Plaintiff requests the Court take judicial notice of its two rulings on Defendant’s demurrer and Plaintiff’s motion for summary judgment.

The Court will take judicial notice of the rulings on Defendant’s demurrer and Plaintiff’s motion for summary judgment, but not the tentative ruling, which is not a court record. The other two items are court records subject to judicial notice under Evidence Code section 452(d). Plaintiff’s request for judicial notice is granted. Defendant’s request for judicial notice is denied.

Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings

          In his motion, Defendant asks this Court to “address the legal consequence of its contract ruling.” (Motion, at p. 5.) Specifically, Defendant argues that there is a tension between the Court’s decision to sustain his demurrer as to Plaintiff’s cause of action for breach of contract, but overrule the demurrer as to Plaintiff’s implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.

The Court declines to revisit this issue for two reasons.

First, Defendant recognizes that he may not bring a motion for judgment on the pleadings as to an issue raised in a prior demurrer. (See CCP § 438.) He concedes that his prior demurrer sought to defeat Plaintiff’s claim on “substantially similar grounds” (Motion, at p. 7), but invites the Court to view this motion as a common law motion for judgment on the pleadings. Defendant cites several cases that supposedly confirm that consideration of a “common law” motion for judgment on the pleadings may still be available to trial courts; however, none of the cases cited support the conclusion that the Court may consider a common law motion for judgment on the pleadings made on the same ground as raised in a prior demurrer.

Second, Defendant invites the Court to exercise its inherent authority to reconsider its prior order on its own motion. The Court has inherent constitutional power sua sponte to reconsider, correct, and change its own interim decisions. (Le Francois v. Goel (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1107.) The Court declines to reconsider its prior order here. Notably, this Court’s prior order explained that the agreement bound both Defendant Lothian Road WRX, Inc. and Grover Henry Colin Nix, IV to perform under the contract. This Court held:

The agreement binds both Lothian Road WRX, Inc. and Grover Henry Colin Nix, IV to perform various obligations. For example, the settlement agreement states that it is “between Swenson He, LLC and SH Fund I, L.P. (hereinafter, connectively [sic] “Claimants”) and Grover Henry Colin Nix, IV and Lothian Road WRX, Inc. (hereinafter, “Respondents”).” (Id., at p. 1.) In addition, paragraph 17 of the agreement states: “A Party breaching any acknowledgement, warranty, payment obligation, and/or representation in this Settlement Agreement and Release shall hold the other Parties harmless from any such breach.” (Id., at p. 8.)

(6/9/26 Minute Order.)

While Defendant Grover Nix may not have owed a specific payment obligation, this does not mean he did not have other duties under the contract that could imply unfair dealing. The FAC alleges that Defendant Nix was the one who acted on behalf of Defendant Lothian Road WRX, Inc. as president, and made various representations that were untrue, including a representation of an intent to pay. (See FAC, ¶¶ 10, 15, 19-20.) At the pleading stage, these allegations support a separate claim for implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. There is nothing for the Court to reconcile in its prior order.

Disposition

Defendant Grover Henry Colin Nix, IV’s motion for judgment on the pleadings as to Plaintiff Swenson He, LLC’s second cause of action in its first amended complaint is denied.


Case Number: 24STCV18663    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 8 - Caroline Lipe v. J. Frank Associates, LLC, et al.

Plaintiff Caroline Lipe’s Motion to Compel Defendant to Produce Person(s) Most Qualified and Documents in Deposition Notice

Plaintiff Caroline Lipe moves to compel Defendant J. Frank Associates, LLC to produce person(s) most qualified (“PMQ”) and documents in deposition notice.

As an initial matter, the Court notes that this singular motion was brought to seek relief for multiple issues. Plaintiff seemingly seeks (1) an order compelling further deposition testimony from Defendant’s PMQ Anne Hoffnung (or otherwise compel a different PMQ to appear) as to deposition topic nos. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 18; (2) an order compelling Defendant to produce a PMQ to testify to topic nos. 1, 3, 7, and 9; and (3) an order compelling Defendant to produce all documents requested in the PMQ notice of deposition. Plaintiff also requests monetary sanctions. The Court will address each in turn.

PMQ as to Topic Nos. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 18

“If a deponent fails to answer any question …, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480(a).)

Plaintiff argues that the PMQ that was designated to testify on topic nos. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 18, Anne Hoffnung, was not properly prepared for her June 1, 2026 deposition because she did not know the answers to certain questions. For support, Plaintiff includes a separate statement which includes the topic Hoffnung was supposed to testify to, followed by generalized arguments that Hoffnung did not know the answers to several questions on the topic and some citations to deposition testimony. For example, for topic no. 6, the PMQ was designated to testify as to “All communications between any partner of Defendant J. Frank Associates, LLC and anyone else regarding Plaintiff’s disability between 2019 and 2023.” (Pl. Sep. Statement, at p. 6.) Instead of providing the Court with the text of the actual question(s) posed at deposition and Hoffnung’s allegedly deficient responses, Plaintiff states:

Anne Hoffnung could not recall or responded “I don’t know” to several questions on this topic, even though she was supposed to be the person most qualified to testify on the company’s behalf on this subject matter. Maldonado v. Superior Court (2002) 94 Cal.App.4th 1390, 1395-1398. See excerpts from Hoffnung Depo, attached to Laba Declaration, e.g. Hoffnung Depo. pg. 212 line 6 – pg. 217 line 7. See also Hoffnung Depo. pg. 240 line 2-5. Hoffnung Depo. pg. 241 line 2-pg. 242 line 4.

(Ibid.)

Under California Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(c), “The separate statement must include—for each discovery request … to which a further response, answer, or production is requested—the following: (1) The text of the request, interrogatory, question, or inspection demand; (2) The text of each response, answer, or objection, and any further responses or answers;  (3) A statement of the factual and legal reasons for compelling further responses, answers, or production as to each matter in dispute.”

This separate statement does not include the text of each deposition question and the text of each response or objection. Without this information, the Court cannot properly evaluate whether further deposition testimony as to certain questions should be compelled, or whether Hoffnung indeed did not sufficiently prepare for the deposition.

Moreover, under the Code, “the deponent shall designate and produce at the deposition those of its officers, directors, managing agents, employees, or agents who are most qualified to testify on its behalf as to those matters to the extent of any information known or reasonably available to the deponent.” (CCP § 2025.230.) Here, Defendant’s PMQ is its Chief Human Resources Officer who has worked for the company for more than sixteen years. (Davis Decl., ¶ 4.) Defense counsel attests that Hoffnung prepared for the deposition: she “reviewed discovery documents produced in this litigation, the employee handbook, Plaintiff’s performance reviews, emails concerning Plaintiff’s performance, and a compiled timeline of events.” (Id., ¶ 7.) Hoffnung testified at her PMQ deposition for approximately seven hours. (Id., ¶ 6.) To the extent Plaintiff is dissatisfied that Hoffnung did not have the answers to some deposition questions posed, without knowing the questions or the responses provided, there is little relief that the Court can afford. Based on the nature of the PMQ deposition process, Defendant may be limited to what evidence it can offer at trial if its PMQ could not provide that evidence during the deposition.  To the extent Plaintiff argues that Defense counsel instructed Hoffnung not to answer certain questions, Plaintiff should have outlined the specific questions, and specific objections raised by counsel, in her separate statement.

It is also unclear to the Court if Plaintiff seeks to depose Hoffnung a second time or desires to depose a different PMQ designated for the topics identified. In either case, the Court lacks sufficient information to compel the relief sought.

PMQ as to Topic Nos. 1, 3, 7, and 9

          As to topics nos. 1, 3, 7, and 9 on the deposition notice, Plaintiff argues that a PMQ was not produced to appear for a deposition. This argument is without merit. The record reflects that counsel shared a mutual understanding that Jed Repko would be Defendant’s PMQ as to topic nos. 1, 3, 7, and 9. (Davis Decl., Ex. E.) However, the parties agreed to proceed with Repko’s deposition in his individual capacity first to evaluate whether a second deposition of Repko in his capacity as PMQ would be necessary. (Ibid.) Here, Repko’s deposition in his personal capacity occurred on July 28, 2026. (Davis Decl., ¶ 15.) The record shows that counsel disagreed as to whether all PMQ topics were covered by the single deposition and Plaintiff’s counsel agreed to meet and confer about it off the record at Repko’s deposition. (Davis Decl., Ex. H.) However, instead of meeting and conferring further about the issue, Plaintiff filed this motion three days later, claiming that Repko’s deposition as a percipient witness was not a substitute for his PMQ deposition and that “defense counsel refused to provide any dates before the end of July for the second PMQ witness to appear for questioning.” (Reply, at p. 7.) However, there is no evidence that Defense counsel refused to produce Repko for a PMQ deposition. It appears that Defense counsel was awaiting further meet and confer on the issue. Because Defendant did not fail to produce Repko for a PMQ deposition, the Court will not compel this relief.

Document Production

          Plaintiff seeks further document production for request nos. 1, 3, 6, 14, and 16 listed in the PMQ deposition notice.

          For request no. 1, Plaintiff seeks an unredacted version of an email produced by Defendant. However, neither party submitted a copy of the redacted documents, nor the accompanying privilege log. Plaintiff only provides isolated quotes and general details about the redacted documents. Without sufficient evidence, the Court cannot determine whether an unredacted version of this email should be compelled.

For request no. 3, Plaintiff seeks an unredacted version of Plaintiff’s self-evaluation. In opposition, Defendant agreed to produce this. This is moot.

          For request no. 6, Defendant properly objected to the request based on overbreadth and privacy. The request seeks “Eric Brielmann’s personnel records, including all performance evaluations, reviews, peer evaluations, complaints against Eric Brielmann, investigations into Eric Brielmann, and any requests to be reassigned from Eric Brielmann.” In Defendant’s opposition, Defendant agreed to produce Brielmann’s personnel records, “excluding benefit-enrollment forms and tax or financial documents.” (Davis Decl., ¶ 30.) The Court finds that this narrowing is appropriate and will order Defendant to produce it.

          For request no. 14, Defendant made several objections. In opposition, Defendant claims that no responsive documents exist. The Court cannot compel production of documents that do not exist.

          For request no. 16, Plaintiff seeks “All communications regarding the creation of, comments upon, revisions to, or other discussions of Plaintiff's self-evaluation.” Defendant made several objections to the request. However, in opposition, Defendant claims that it will “produce any located communications regarding Plaintiff’s self evaluation.” The Court will compel production in accordance with Defendant’s representations.

Sanctions

          Both parties move for sanctions against one another. “The court shall impose a monetary sanction . . . against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel an answer or production, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480(j).)

          The Court declines to award sanctions here. Both parties were partially successful. Plaintiff obtained an order compelling production as to some document requests. Meanwhile, Defendant successfully opposed Plaintiff’s request to compel PMQ deposition testimony. Both parties acted with substantial justification.

Disposition

Plaintiff Caroline Lipe’s motion to compel Defendant J. Frank Associates, LLC to produce person(s) most qualified and documents in deposition notice is granted in part.

Defendant is ordered to produce responsive documents to request nos. 6 and 16 within 10 court days.


Case Number: 25STCV15935    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 7 - Joshua Steven Bernia v. Qyou USA Inc., et al.

Plaintiff Joshua Steven Bernia's Motion for Leave to File First Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Joshua Steven Bernia moves for leave to file his first amended complaint to add a cause of action for failure to pay wages due upon separation. Defendants did not file an opposition.

Code of Civil Procedure section 473(a)(1) provides, “The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.” Courts exercise their discretion “liberally to permit amendment,” and “[t]he policy favoring amendment is so strong that it is a rare case in which denial of leave to amend can be justified.” (Howard v. County of San Diego (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428.) A motion for leave to amend will normally be granted unless (a) the party seeking to amend has delayed bringing the proposed amendment; and (b) the delay in seeking leave to amend will cause prejudice to an opposing party. (Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.)

While Plaintiff arguably could have amended his complaint sooner—he resigned from employment in May 2026—that does not mean his delay caused any undue prejudice to Defendants. Trial is still several months away. Defendants did not file an opposition to explain any prejudice they would suffer as a result of the amendment. Thus, the Court will therefore permit Plaintiff to file the first amended complaint. 

Disposition

Plaintiff Joshua Steven Bernia’s motion for leave to file a first amended complaint is granted. Plaintiff is ordered to file forthwith the proposed first amended complaint attached as Exhibit 3 to the Declaration of Boris Dalis.


Case Number: 26STCV01168    Hearing Date: September 22, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 - Kai Kyungdong Lee v. KTL Mart, Inc., et al.

          Defendants’ Demurrer to Plaintiff’s Complaint

          Defendants KTL Mart, Inc. and Krissy Lee demur to Plaintiff Kai Kyungdong Lee’s complaint for breach of contract.

Request for Judicial Notice

          Defendants request the Court take judicial notice of five court records from a separate superior court case. However, there are no documents attached to the notice. The Court exercises its discretion to take judicial notice of the court records. The Court can access them through the court system. Defendants are cautioned to comply with all court rules in the future.

Defendants’ request for judicial notice is granted under Evidence Code section 452(d).

Defendant KTL Mart

          Plaintiff argues Defendant KTL Mart, Inc. cannot bring this demurrer because default has been entered against it. The Court agrees. Because Defendant KTL Mart, Inc. is in default, it is not entitled to file this demurrer. “The entry of a default terminates a defendant’s rights to take any further affirmative steps in the litigation until either its default is set aside or a default judgment is entered.” (Devlin v. Kearny Mesa AMC/Jeep/Renault, Inc. (1984) 155 Cal.App.3d 381, 385.) As such, the demurrer as brought by Defendant KTL Mart, Inc. is hereby stricken. The Court will only consider the demurrer as brought by Defendant Krissy Lee, who is not in default.

Uncertainty

Defendant Lee contends the complaint is uncertain because it does not provide specific facts about Plaintiff’s performance of contract obligations. “A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly’s of Cal., Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.) “ ‘ “Demurrers for uncertainty are disfavored, and are granted only if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” ’ ” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695, alterations omitted.) The complaint must sufficiently apprise defendants of the claims against them. (Ibid.) Here, this failure to provide additional details does not prevent Defendant from answering the complaint.

Sufficiency of Allegations

To state a cause of action for breach of contract, Plaintiff must allege “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) the resulting damages to the plaintiff.” (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.)

Defendant Lee challenges the second element: “plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance.” She argues that Plaintiff did not comply with the requirement to timely file a dismissal with prejudice. Specifically, the Settlement Agreement, attached as Exhibit A to Plaintiff’s complaint, provides:

The remaining balance of Sixty Thousand Dollars ($60,000.00) shall become payable only upon Plaintiff’s compliance with all conditions precedent outlined herein, including filing a dismissal with prejudice as stipulated in Section 2 . . . . Failure by Plaintiff to comply with these conditions precedent or any breach of confidentiality or non-disparagement obligations shall nullify the requirement for Defendants to make any additional payments

[¶¶]

[Section 2] Dismissal. Within seven (7) days after the receipt of the Initial Payment, Plaintiff shall file a Request for Dismissal with prejudice and with the court retaining jurisdiction pursuant to the Code of Civil Procedure § 664.6.

(Compl., Ex. A, Settlement Agreement, at p. 2.)

Defendant points the Court to judicially noticeable court records showing that Plaintiff filed a request for dismissal on May 5, 2025, that was “not entered” by the clerk. (See RJN, Item 1.) Defendant contends that this court record, together with the complaint’s allegation that “[o]n or about April 22, 2025, Defendants paid the initial installment of $40,000 . . . .” (Compl., ¶ 19) compels the conclusion that Plaintiff was required to file the request for dismissal by April 29 and failed to do so. Therefore, Defendant argues, Plaintiff cannot sufficiently plead “plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance.”

The Court rejects Defendant’s argument. The face of the complaint, combined with the judicially noticed documents, does not reveal the date Plaintiff received the $40,000 initial payment, which would start the clock to file any dismissal. General demurrers can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable; evidence or extrinsic matters are not considered. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 430.30, 430.70; Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) The complaint’s allegations only confirm when payment was made, not when payment was received. (See Compl., ¶ 19.) Given the material before the Court, it is possible that Plaintiff was not in “receipt” of the initial payment until well after April 22, and timely filed the request for dismissal on May 5. Given the allegations in the complaint and judicially noticed documents, Plaintiff states a viable claim for breach of contract at the pleading stage.

Disposition

          Defendant KTL Mart, Inc.’s demurrer is hereby stricken.

          Defendant Krissy Lee’s demurrer as to Plaintiff’s complaint is overruled. Defendant Lee should file any responsive pleading within 21 days.
