MOTION TO TAX COSTS

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Martin Ramirez

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  Defendant InterSolutions, LLC

Motion to Tax Costs

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Martin Ramirez (“Plaintiffs”) moves the court for an order striking or taxing defendant InterSolutions LLC’s (“Defendant”) Memorandum of Costs.

“A ‘verified memorandum of costs is prima facie evidence of the propriety’ of the items listed on it, and the burden is on the party challenging these costs to demonstrate that they were not reasonable or necessary.”  (Adams v. Ford Motor Co. (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 1475, 1486 [internal citation omitted].) 

First, the court denies Plaintiff’s request to strike the Memorandum of Costs in its entirety. Plaintiff cannot claim inadequate notice of the Memorandum of Costs based on service by mail because service by mail is expressly authorized by statute. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1013(a).) Moreover, Plaintiff has shown no prejudice from any claimed defect in service because he timely filed and served this motion 14 days after the March 20, 2026 Memorandum of Costs was filed. (Proof of Service of Motion filed April 3, 2026.) (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1700(b)(1) [motion to strike or to tax costs must be served and filed within 15 days of service of cost memorandum].) The court also rejects Plaintiff’s collateral attack on the issue of Defendant’s forfeiture and revival by the Franchise Tax Board. The court comprehensively addressed and ruled on that issue in its March 5, 2026 order on Defendant’s Motion for Summary Judgment. (March 5, 2026 Minute Order.)

Second, the court grants Plaintiff’s motion to tax Defendant’s request for $1,695.00 in “Other” costs based on an award of sanctions as an inappropriate item for a memorandum of costs. An award of monetary sanctions may be enforced as a money judgment. “[M]onetary sanction orders are enforceable through the execution of judgment laws.  [Citation.]  These orders have the force and effect of a money judgment, and are immediately enforceable through execution, except to the extent the trial court may order a stay of the sanction.”  (Newland v. Superior Court (1995) 40 Cal.App.4th 608, 615 [internal citation omitted]; Jones v. Otero (1984) 156 Cal.App.3d 754, 759 [“An order imposing monetary sanctions may be enforced under the Enforcement of Judgments Law”].)

Third, the court denies Plaintiff’s motion to tax Defendant’s claimed costs relating to filing and motion fees, service of process fees, court reporter fees, and copy costs. These costs are expressly recoverable, and Plaintiff has not met his burden to show that these costs were not reasonable or necessary. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(1), (4), (11), (13); Adams, supra, 199 Cal.App.4th at p. 1486.)

 

ORDER

The court grants in part plaintiff Martin Ramirez’s motion to strike or tax costs as follows:

The court orders that the following costs are taxed from defendant InterSolutions, LLC’s Memorandum of costs: $1,695.00 in “Other” costs (Item 15).

The court orders that defendant InterSolutions, LLC shall recover from plaintiff Martin Ramirez costs and expenses in the amount of $8,564.75

The court orders defendant InterSolutions, LLC. to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

(1)  MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION APPEARANCE OF GREGORY FISHER AND FOR SANCTIONS

(1) Motion to Compel Deposition Appearance of Gregory Fisher and for Sanctions.

(2) Motion to Compel Deposition Appearance of Tracy Hill and for Sanctions.

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with these motions. No opposition or reply was filed.

DISCUSSION

Motion to Compel Deposition Appearance of Gregory Fisher

Defendant Delmore J. Morsette (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order (1) compelling plaintiff Gregory Fisher to attend deposition and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Defendant and against plaintiff Gregory Fisher in the amount of $495.00.

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party . . . , without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it . . . , the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).) A motion under this subdivision “shall be accompanied by, … when the deponent fails to attend the deposition …, a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).)

The court denies Defendant’s motion to compel plaintiff Gregory Fisher to appear for deposition because Defendant has not filed a declaration stating that his counsel contacted plaintiff Gregory Fisher to inquire about the nonappearance. (Taouk Decl. ¶¶ 5-6.) For the same reason, the court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions. 

Motion to Compel Deposition Appearance of Tracy Hill

Defendant moves the court for an order (1) compelling plaintiff Tracy Hill to attend deposition and (2) awarding monetary sanctions in favor of Defendant and against plaintiff Tracy Hill in the amount of $495.00.

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party . . . , without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it . . . , the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).) A motion under this subdivision “shall be accompanied by, … when the deponent fails to attend the deposition …, a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(2).)

The court denies Defendant’s motion to compel Plaintiff to appear for deposition because Defendant has not filed a declaration stating that his counsel contacted plaintiff Tracy Hill to inquire about the nonappearance. (Taouk Decl. ¶¶ 5-6.) For the same reason, the court denies Defendant’s request for sanctions.   

ORDER

The court DENIES defendant Delmore J. Morsette’s motion to compel the deposition appearance of plaintiff Gregory Fisher and for sanctions.

The court DENIES Delmore J. Morsette’s motion to compel the deposition appearance of plaintiff Tracy Hill and for sanctions.

The court orders defendant Delmore J. Morsette to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

DEMURRER TO FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendants Wan Zhu “Kathryn” Li, Hong Li, and Rena International Corporation.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response

Demurrer to First Amended Complaint.

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition or reply was filed. The court has also considered the Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint filed by plaintiffs Ting Moore, Howard Moore, Fanzi Meng, Peter Zhen Zhang, Lianying Mao, Sufen Zheng, and Kewen Zhang (“Plaintiffs”) on April 21, 2026.

DISCUSSION

Defendants Wan Zhu “Kathryn” Li, Hong Li, and Rena International Corporation (“Defendants”) demur to the First Amended Complaint in its entirety. Defendants state that Plaintiffs, through their counsel, agreed to file a Second Amended Complaint during a teleconference with Defendants’ counsel on March 16, 2026. (Shioda Decl. ¶ 8.) No amendment was filed before this demurrer was brought on April 8, 2026, and Defendants cite Plaintiffs’ failure to amend as a reason for the demurrer. (Demurrer p. 4:17-19.) However, on April 21, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a “Motion for Leave to File a Second Amended Complaint” which seeks to amend the pleadings “to address issues raised in Defendants’ demurrer to the First Amended Complaint.” (Motion for Leave p. 2:8-9.) Plaintiffs therefore concede the objections to the First Amended Complaint, and the court therefore sustains the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.10 subd. (e).)

However, based on the filing of the Motion for Leave, the court also finds that Plaintiffs have met their burden “to articulate how [they] could amend [their] pleading to render it sufficient.” (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Assn., Inc. v. Parth¿(2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290.)¿The court therefore grants Plaintiffs leave to amend their complaint.

ORDER

The court SUSTAINS defendants Wan Zhu “Kathryn” Li, Hong Li, and Rena International Corporation’s demurrer to plaintiffs Ting Moore, Howard Moore, Fanzi Meng, Peter Zhen Zhang, Lianying Mao, Sufen Zheng, and Kewen Zhang’s First Amended Complaint.

The court grants the plaintiffs 20 days leave to file a Second Amended Complaint that cures the defects in the First Amended Complaint identified by the defendants’ demurrer.

The plaintiffs’ Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint scheduled to be heard November 20, 2026, at 10:00 A.M., is advanced to this date and vacated because it is moot. 

 

 

The court orders defendants Wan Zhu “Kathryn” Li, Hong Li, and Rena International Corporation to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Plaintiff Theodore Kyriazis

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response.

Motion for Leave to File First Amended Complaint

The court considered the moving papers filed in connection with this motion. No opposition or reply was filed.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff Theodore Kyriazis (“Plaintiff”) moves the court for an order granting him leave to file a First Amended Complaint to add the City of Los Angeles as a defendant to both causes of action.

Upon consideration of the arguments and evidence presented by Plaintiff in support of their motion, the court finds that (1) it is in furtherance of justice to allow Plaintiff to file the First Amended Complaint in order to make the amendments described above, and (2) the defendants in this action have not shown that they will be unduly prejudiced by this amendment because they did not file opposition papers or other evidence with the court to establish prejudice.  (Code Civ. Proc., §¿473, subd. (a); Mac v. Minassian (2022) 76 Cal.App.5th 510, 519 [“‘ “California courts ‘have a policy of great liberality in allowing amendments at any stage of the proceeding so as to dispose of cases upon their substantial merits where the authorization does not prejudice the substantial rights of others’ ” ’”]; Kyriazis Decl. ¶¶ 2-4, Ex. 1.)  

ORDER

The court GRANTS plaintiff Theodore Kyriazis’s motion for leave to file a First Amended Complaint.

The court orders plaintiff Theodore Kyriazis to file the First Amended Complaint in the form attached as Exhibit 1 to the declaration of Theodore Kyriazis within five days of the date of this order.

The court orders plaintiff Theodore Kyriazis to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
