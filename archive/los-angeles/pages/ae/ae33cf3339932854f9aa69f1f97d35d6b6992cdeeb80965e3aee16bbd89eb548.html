Case Name: Vasile v. West Valley Hospitality, Inc., et al.

Case No.: 23STCV25164

Matter: Motion to Strike Answer

Moving Party: Plaintiff Lillian Vasile

Responding Party: Unopposed

Ruling: The Motion is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


Plaintiff Lillian Vasile seeks to strike the Answer of MST Hospitality West Valley, Inc. because it does not have counsel and because it is a suspended corporation.

Because a corporate entity cannot represent itself (see, e.g., Caressa Camille, Inc. v. Alcoholic Beverage Control Appeals Bd. (2002) 99 Cal.App.4th 1094, 1101), and “[a] corporation which has its powers suspended . . . lacks the legal capacity to prosecute or defend a civil action during its suspension” (Sade Shoe Co. v. Oschin & Snyder (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 1509, 1512-1513), the Motion to Strike is granted. MST’s default is entered.

Moving party to give notice.



Case Number: 25STCP03909    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 10, 2026

Case Name: Phibonacchii v. Northlight Specialty Insurance Company

Case No.: 25STCP03909

Matter: Motion to Declare Plaintiff to be a Vexatious Litigant, etc.

Moving Party: Respondent Northlight Specialty Insurance Company

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted in part.

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On April 29, 2026, Respondent Northlight Specialty Insurance Company filed a motion to find Petitioner Lord Phibonacchii to be a vexatious litigant and to restrain concerning communications with parties in this lawsuit. 

Under Code Civ. Proc. § 391(b), a vexatious litigant is a person who does any of the following:

(1) In the immediately preceding seven-year period has commenced, prosecuted, or maintained in propria persona at least five litigations other than in a small claims court that have been (i) finally determined adversely to the person or (ii) unjustifiably permitted to remain pending at least two years without having been brought to trial or hearing.

(2) After a litigation has been finally determined against the person, repeatedly relitigates or attempts to relitigate, in propria persona, either (i) the validity of the determination against the same defendant or defendants as to whom the litigation was finally determined or (ii) the cause of action, claim, controversy, or any of the issues of fact or law, determined or concluded by the final determination against the same defendant or defendants as to whom the litigation was finally determined.

(3) In any litigation while acting in propria persona, repeatedly files unmeritorious motions, pleadings, or other papers, conducts unnecessary discovery, or engages in other tactics that are frivolous or solely intended to cause unnecessary delay.

(4) Has previously been declared to be a vexatious litigant by any state or federal court of record in any action or proceeding based upon the same or substantially similar facts, transaction, or occurrence.

Previously, Petitioner dismissed this action but then moved to set aside that dismissal specifically to oppose the instant Motion. No opposition, however, has been filed.

Respondent’s evidence, including the Declaration of Suzanne Badawi and records of federal and state proceedings, demonstrates that Petitioner has engaged in a pattern of meritless litigation, repeated unsuccessful motions, and has been designated a vexatious complainant by the California State Bar. Petitioner has shown a clear pattern of filing, dismissing, and refiling actions to avoid adverse rulings, forum shopping, and failing to cooperate in discovery, satisfying the statutory criteria under § 391(b)(1) and § 391(b)(3). Petitioner is declared to be a vexatious litigant.

The Court will also enter a prefiling order against Petitioner.

Respondent also seeks a protective order restraining Petitioner’s communications to various counsel and unrelated third-parties, which Respondent contends is harassing and intimidating.

While some of Petitioner’s behavior is concerning, due to free speech implications, the Court, in the exercise of its discretion, will not enter a protective order.

In sum, Petitioner is declared to be a vexatious litigant subject to a prefiling order. However, no protective order will be entered at this time. 

The Motion is granted in part as set forth herein.

Moving party to give notice.

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 10, 2026

Case Name: Gray v. L.K.H. Capital Holdings, LLC, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV05514 

Matter: Motion to Compel Further Responses

Moving Party: Defendant Capital Investment Realty Group Inc. 

Responding Party: Plaintiff Veronica Grey

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted in part.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is a personal injury lawsuit relating to a slip and fall on stairs.

Defendant Capital Investment Realty Group Inc. seeks to compel further responses to its requests for production and special and form interrogatories from Plaintiff Veronica Grey. Defendant also seeks to deem its RFAs admitted.

The Motion is denied as to RFP no. 15, which seeks “all” documents relating to Plaintiff’s employment in the last 3 years, RFP no. 18, which seeks all federal and state tax returns, and RFP no. 24, which seeks all social media posts showing Plaintiff's physical activities. These requests are overbroad or implicate the taxpayer's privilege. 

The Motion is granted as to the remainder of the RFPs and form and special interrogatories, which seek basic and relevant information about the subject incident. Plaintiff’s objections lack merit, and the responses are evasive.

Also, the Court will compel further responses as to all RFAs at issue, rather than deeming the RFAs admitted. Plaintiff provided evasive responses that she does not have the necessary knowledge when the RFAs seek straightforward information—for example, “Admit that YOU were carrying items in YOUR hands at the time of the INCIDENT.”

The Motion is granted in part. Further responses are to be provided within 30 days as to all discovery at issue, except RFP nos. 15, 18, and 24. 

The Court will not award sanctions because Defendant improperly consolidated four discovery motions into one motion and provided a non-compliant separate statement.

Moving party to give notice.

Case Number: 26STCP01719    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 10, 2026

Case Name: Center Street Lending, VIII SPE, LLC v. FEI Enterprises, Inc.

Case No.: 26STCP01719

Matter: Petition to Expunge Mechanic’s Lien

Moving Party: Center Street Lending, VIII SPE

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Petition is granted.


Petitioner to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On May 4, 2026, Center Street Lending, VIII SPE filed a Petition to expunge the mechanic’s lien recorded by Respondent FEI Enterprises, Inc. against 2347 Ocean View Ave., Los Angeles, CA 90057.

Civ. Code § 8480(a) states, “The owner of property or the owner of any interest in property subject to a claim of lien may petition the court for an order to release the property from the claim of lien if the claimant has not commenced an action to enforce the lien within the time provided in Section 8460.”

Civ. Code § 8460(a) states, “The claimant shall commence an action to enforce a lien within 90 days after recordation of the claim of lien. If the claimant does not commence an action to enforce the lien within that time, the claim of lien expires and is unenforceable.”

Here, the subject lien was recorded on October 14, 2025, but no lawsuit has apparently been filed to enforce the lien.  Because an opposition was not submitted, Respondent failed to establish the validity of the subject lien.  (See Civ. Code § 8488(a) [“The claimant has the burden of proof as to the validity of the lien.”].)  Therefore, the Petition to Expunge Mechanic’s lien is granted.

Petitioner to give notice.
