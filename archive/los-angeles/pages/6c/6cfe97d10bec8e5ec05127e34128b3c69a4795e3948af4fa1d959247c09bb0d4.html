Tentative Ruling

Shakibian v. AJR Investment, Case no. 22STCV17299

Hearing date September 18, 2026

Defendant Shakibian’s Motions to Compel Discovery and Deem RFAs Admitted & Sanctions - UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff Shakibian sues defendants AJR Investment Partnership, LP, AJR Investment Partnership, LLC (collectively “AJR”) and others for premises liability. Defendant moves to compel discovery as to supplemental RFPs, set one, supplemental interrogatories, set one, and to deem RFAs, set one, admitted. The motions are unopposed.

“If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the following rules apply... (b) The party propounding the interrogatories may move for an order compelling response to the interrogatories.” Code Civ. Proc. §2030.290. If a party to whom requests for admission are directed fails to serve a timely response, propounding party may move to deem the matters admitted. Code Civ. Proc. §2033.280(2)(b).

Defendant served supplemental RFPs and interrogatories on 4/6/26 and received no responses by the deadline. Decl. Meyering, exh. A, para. 6. Defense counsel Meyering attempted to meet and confer with plaintiff’s counsel Janfaza multiple times via email prior to filing these motions; plaintiff’s counsel failed to respond. Id. at paras. 4-5, exhs. B-C. The motions are unopposed. An order compelling code-compliant discovery responses, without objection, is warranted. The meet and confer efforts are sufficient.

Defendant served RFAs, set one, on 5/5/26. Decl. Meyering, para. 5, exh. A. The parties agreed to multiple deadline extensions, with responses due 7/15/26. Id. at para. 6. Plaintiff failed to serve verified responses by the deadline. Following meet and confer efforts, plaintiff’s counsel represented verified responses without objection would be served by 8/3/26 but failed to served verified responses. Id. at para. 7. The motion is unopposed. An order deeming RFAs admitted is granted.

Sanctions are mandatory under Code Civ. Proc. §2033.280(c). Defendant requests joint and several sanctions against plaintiff and counsel Janfaza. As counsel failed to respond to multiple meet and confer efforts and failed to oppose these motions or offer explanation for the failures, joint and several sanctions are appropriate.

Defendant requests sanctions of $420 on the motion to compel supplemental RFPs. Decl. Meyering, para. 7. Defendant requests a reasonable hourly rate of $120/hour. Id. Defendant requests 1 hour for motion drafting and 2 hours for hearing, plus $60 in filing fees. Id. As all motions will be heard together, the court awards 1 hour for motion drafting and 1 hour for hearing, plus the requested $60 in filing fees.

Defendant requests sanctions of $420 on the motion to compel supplemental interrogatories. Decl. Meyering, para. 7. Defendant requests 1 hour for motion drafting and 2 hours for hearing, plus $60 in filing fees. Id. As all motions will be heard together, the court will not award additional hours for hearing. The court awards 1 hour at $120/hour for motion drafting and the requested $60 in filing fees.

Defendant requests sanctions of $585 on the motion to deem RFAs admitted. Decl. Meyering, para. 12. Defendant requests a rate of $185/hour. Id. No explanation is offered for the different rate sought; the court awards the rate of $120/hour. Defendant requests 1 hour for motion drafting and 2 hours for hearing, plus $60 in filing fees. Id. As all motions will be heard together, the court will not award additional hours for hearing. The court awards 1 hour for motion drafting at $120/hour and the requested $60 in filing fees.

In total, the court awards 3 hours for motion drafting and 1 hour for hearing, all at $120/hour, totaling $480, plus $180 in filing fees. GRANTED; code-compliant responses without objections within 30 days. Defendant’s RFAs, set one, are deemed admitted. Joint and several sanctions of $660 payable by plaintiff and counsel within 30 days.


Case Number: 23VECV05702    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Barrett v. Jackson, Case no. 23VECV05702

Hearing date September 18, 2026

Plaintiff Barrett’s Motion to Compel Defendant Jacksun to File Responsive Pleading

Plaintiff Barrett sues defendants Jacksun, erroneously sued as “Jackson,” Jermaine L. Jackson, Inc. and Work Records, Inc., alleging sexual assault. Default was entered on 4/23/26, and default judgment was entered on 5/14/26. Defendant Jacksun moved to set aside the default and default judgment, quash service and request leave to file a proposed demurrer. The court granted the motion to set aside, denied the request to quash and ordered Jacksun to file the proposed demurrer within 10 days. See 6/20/26 Min. Order; 7/7/26 Min. Order.

Defendant Jacksun did not file the demurrer as ordered. Defendant Jacksun did file a notice of intent to file a writ in the Court of Appeal on 7/7/26. On 9/9/26 the Court of Appeal ordered plaintiff to file a response to Jacksun’s petition on or before 10/9/26, and Jacksun to file a reply on or before 11/9/26. See 9/9/26 Not. of Ruling, Jacksun v. Superior Court (Barrett), case no. B355686.

Plaintiff now moves to compel Jacksun to file a responsive pleading pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. §128(a)(4). Jacksun opposes.

Code Civ. Proc. §128(a)(4) permits the court to compel obedience to its judgments, orders and processes. Plaintiff argues the court should compel Jacksun to file a responsive pleading in accordance with the court’s prior order.

Defendant Jackson argues Code Civ. Proc. §418.10 precludes such an order. Code Civ. Proc. §418.10(c) states:

“If the motion is denied by the trial court, the defendant, within 10 days after service upon him or her of a written notice of entry of an order of the court denying his or her motion, or within any further time not exceeding 20 days that the trial court may for good cause allow, and before pleading, may petition an appropriate reviewing court for a writ of mandate to require the trial court to enter its order quashing the service of summons or staying or dismissing the action. The defendant shall file or enter his or her responsive pleading in the trial court within the time prescribed by subdivision (b) unless, on or before the last day of the defendant's time to plead, he or she serves upon the adverse party and files with the trial court a notice that he or she has petitioned for a writ of mandate. The service and filing of the notice shall extend the defendant's time to plead until 10 days after service upon him or her of a written notice of the final judgment in the mandate proceeding. The time to plead may for good cause shown be extended by the trial court for an additional period not exceeding 20 days (emphasis added).”

As the writ process is ongoing in the appellate court, this court has no authority to order Jacksun to file a responsive pleading. The parties are to contact the court within 2 court days of any appellate court rulings to re-set this motion for hearing, or defendant Jacksun may elect to file a responsive pleading within 5 court days of an appellate court ruling. A case management regarding status of the appeal will be set in 90 days.

Tentative Ruling

Karout v. Ball N Bounce 2, Case no. 24VECV04943

Hearing date September 18, 2026

Plaintiff Karout’s Petition to Approve Minor’s Compromise

Minor plaintiff Karout, by and through his father/guardian ad litem, sues defendant Ball N Bounce 2 for a leg fracture sustained when plaintiff jumped from an elevated platform in defendant’s indoor playground. Plaintiff petitions for approval of minor’s compromise with defendant. The petition is unopposed.

Plaintiff suffered a right tibial fracture and has fully recovered. Minor’s Comp., paras. 5-9. Plaintiff is to receive $150,000. Id. at para. 10. The entirety of the compromise amount will go to plaintiff. Id. at para. 11. Plaintiff incurred medical expenses of $143,370.70. Minor’s Comp., para. 12(a). Plaintiff paid $8,155.32 via an ERISA self-funded plan. Id. at para. 12(b)(2)(b). The plan is to be reimbursed. Id.

Attorney’s fees of $40,000 are requested. Minor’s Comp., para. 13(a). Costs of $11,210.54 are requested. Id. at para. 13(b). The net balance of $90,634.14 is to be deposited into a single-premium deferred annuity, subject to withdrawal only with court authorization. Id. at para. 18(b)(3). The terms of the minor’s compromise are reasonable. The petition is unopposed. GRANTED.


Case Number: 25VECV07364    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Bedrosian v. Mercury Insurance Co., Case no. 25VECV07364

Hearing date September 18, 2026

Defendant Mercury’s Demurrer to the Second Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Bedrosian sues defendant Mercury Insurance Co. over defendant’s handling of an underinsured-motorist (“UIM”) claim. Defendant demurred to five of the six causes of action in the original complaint. The first amended complaint abandoned three of those claims and pled causes of action for breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing and violation of Bus. & Prof. Code §17200, et seq. Defendant’s demurrer to the first and third causes of action and motion to strike the prayer for equitable relief under the third cause of action was sustained with leave to amend. See 6/30/26 Min. Order.

The second amended complaint pleads causes of action for breach of contract and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. Defendant demurs to the first cause of action. Plaintiff opposes. The opposition was untimely by 6 court days. This is prejudicial to defendant, which filed a substantive reply by the statutory deadline, only 3 court days later. The court will consider the substance of the opposition but will take its untimeliness into consideration.

Though the SAC purports to attach the subject insurance policy, it is not attached. As the policy was attached to the FAC, exh. A, the court takes judicial notice of the policy sua sponte pursuant to Evid. Code §452(d).

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Defendant demurs to the breach of contract claim, which requires allegations of breach of an express contractual provision. Archdale v. American Specialty Lines Ins. Co. (2007) 154 Cal.App.4th 449, 466–467; Frances T. v. Village Green Owners Ass’n. (1986) 42 Cal.3d 490, 512–513. The policy (FAC, exh. A) states what defendant owes plaintiff and under what circumstances payment is due, but no express term as to when payment is owed. Id. The policy provides for an arbitration process prior to payment on a claim. Id.

Plaintiff alleges defendant breached the terms provided by coverage schedule H, conditions 8 and 9. Coverage H promises to pay all sums the insured is legally entitled to recover. FAC, exh. A. Condition 8 states the amount payable: UIM limits, less amounts paid by those legally liable. Id. Condition 9 makes any amount due payable to the insured. Id.

Plaintiff alleges Civ. Code §1657 supplies payment timing, arguing coverage H does not provide specific payment timing, so that term is to be determined by statute. See California Drilling & Mach. Co. v. Crowder (1922) 58 Cal.App. 529. Plaintiff alleges defendant breached by failing to make immediate payments under the statutory timeline. SAC, para. 74. Plaintiff alleges payment was due 5/16/22, with 7/12/23 and 12/5/24 as residual dates by which payment should have been made. Id. at paras. 75(a)-(c).

Defendant argues Civ. Code §1657 is inapplicable, as Ins. Code §11580.2(f) controls the timing language permitted in insurance policies. The policy states entitlement to UIM benefits and the amount thereof “shall be made by agreement” between the insured and Mercury “or, if they fail to agree, by arbitration.” SAC, para. 70; FAC, exh. A. This language must be included by law per sec. 11580.2(f). See Freeman v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. (1975) 14 Cal.3d 473, 480. The language of the policy is clear; where the parties dispute the amount owed, payment is not due until arbitration has been completed. Section 1657’s immediate payment language does not control. Whether defendant breached requires an allegation that defendant failed to pay after an arbitration award was issued.

As the parties disputed the claim, the dispute proceeded to arbitration, and the claim was settled by defendant paying the full remaining policy limit prior to the arbitration hearing. SAC, paras. 34, 49, 60. Under the language of the policy, as per statute, no provision required defendant to pay by a certain date, upon receipt of a unilateral demand, or upon a concession by a retained expert. Id. at paras. 75(a)-(c). Defendant’s obligation was to pay after arbitration concluded. As payment was rendered prior to the conclusion of arbitration, it was timely.

The only basis for the breach of contract claim is the alleged untimeliness of the payment. Plaintiff failed to allege the required element of breach. As plaintiff has had two prior attempts to plead the cause of action, further leave to amend would be futile. SUSTAINED without leave to amend as to the first cause of action for breach of contract.
