MATTER

Request for Default Judgment

 

This case arises from a dispute concerning an outstanding debt owed for medical services rendered in connection with an underlying slip and fall lawsuit.  The crux of the dispute is that the medical providers argue that defendants patient and law firm breached their respective contracts by not paying all outstanding sums owed, and that the law firm also tortiously breached its fiduciary duties by collecting the sums owed, but refusing to disburse it in full to the medical providers.

 

On July 2, 2025, Plaintiffs DOCS Spine + Orthopedics, successor to Perri + Siddique Spine Surgery (“DOCS”); Albert P. Wong, M.D. (“Wong”); and Parham Yashar, M.D. dba PY26 Neurosurgery P.C. (“Yashar”) (together, “Plaintiffs”) brought suit against Defendants Michael Ryan Dufour (“Dufour”); Dufour Law, P.C.[1] (“Dufour Law”); Valena Mitchell (“Mitchell”) (together, “Defendants”) and Does 1-20, alleging nine causes of action as follows:

 

By DOCS:

 

(1)   Breach of Contract (against Dufour and Mitchell)

(2)   Common Counts (against Dufour and Mitchell)

(3)   Intentional Tort – Breach of Fiduciary Duty (against Dufour and Dufour Law)

 

By Yashar:

 

(4)   Breach of Contract (against Dufour and Mitchell)

(5)   Common Counts (against Dufour and Mitchell)

(6)   Intentional Tort – Breach of Fiduciary Duty (against Dufour and Dufour Law)

 

By Wong:

 

(7)   Breach of Contract (against Dufour and Mitchell)

(8)   Common counts (against Dufour and Mitchell)

(9)   Intentional Tort – Breach of fiduciary duty (against Dufour and Dufour Law)

 

Mitchell and Dufour were served by publication on March 30, April 6, April 13, and April 20, as ordered by the Court on March 12, 2026. 

 

Dufour Law, P.C. was served via the Secretary of State on April 6, 2026, as ordered by the Court on March 26, 2026. 

 

Default was entered against Defendants on June 10, 2026 and the Doe Defendants were dismissed on August 31, 2026.  Plaintiffs now request default judgment as follows:

 

            By DOCS: $511,364.70, representing $415,237.50 special damages as demanded in the Complaint and $96,127.20 in prejudgment interest.

 

            By Yashar: $42,176.10, representing $34,250 special damages as demanded in the Complaint and $7,926.10 in prejudgment interest.

 

            By Wong: $68,852.51, representing $39,652 special damages as demanded in the Complaint and $9,176.70 in prejudgment interest.

 

            In addition, the parties seek $2,958.17 in costs and $17,854 in attorneys’ fees, jointly and severally.

 

A.    Damages

 

            In the Complaint, DOCS seeks damages in the amount of $415,237.50; Yashar seeks damages in the amount of $34,250; and Wong seeks damages in the amount of $39,652. 

 

            Therefore, Plaintiffs do not seek damages in excess of what is pled in the Complaint. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 580, subd. (a) [“The relief granted to the plaintiff, if there is no answer, cannot exceed that demanded in the complaint”]; Levine v. Smith (2006) 145 Cal.App.4th 1131, 1136-1137 [“when recovering damages in a default judgment, the plaintiff is limited to the damages specified in the complaint”].) 

 

a.      DOCS

 

            In support of the request, DOCS advances the Declaration of Brian Perri, which provides:

 

2. I am a Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine, and practice in the Los Angeles area. DOCS Spine + Orthopedics is the successor to Perri + Siddique Spine Surgery, and is the medical group where I work.

 

3. I performed three surgeries on defendant Valena Mitchell at various times between August 17, 2021 and March 28, 2023.

 

4. As a result of the surgeries and various visits, Ms. Mitchell incurred a total bill for the services of DOCS Spine + Orthopedics in the amount of $415,237.50. A copy of Ms. Mitchell’s billing summary reflecting that sum (which has been redacted to exclude confidential information) is attached as Exhibit “A.”

 

5. The billing was issued pursuant to a June 7, 2021 lien agreement signed by the defendants with DOCS Spine + Orthopedics, which provided for payment to be made from the settlement of her personal injury claim.

 

6. It is my understanding that Ms. Mitchell’s personal injury claim was resolved on or about May, 2024. I have not been paid any portion of my bill since that time, despite filing and serving this lawsuit.

 

Perri Decl. ¶¶ 2-6.)   However, Exhibit A itemizes outstanding balances totaling only $413,137.50, not $415,237.50, as requested.  Therefore, DOCS has substantiated damages only in the amount of $413,137.50.

 

b.     Yashar

 

            In support of the request, Yashar advances the Declaration of Parham Yashar, M.D., which provides as follows:

 

2. I am a neurosurgeon, and I do business using the name PY26 Neurosurgery P.C.

 

3. Mitchell. On February 21, 2023, I assisted with a surgery performed on defendant Valena

 

4. As a result of the surgery and related visits, Ms. Mitchell incurred a bill for my services in the amount of $34,250. A copy of the patient ledger reflecting that sum (which has been redacted to exclude confidential information) is attached as Exhibit "A."

 

5. The billing was issued pursuant to a lien agreement signed by the defendants in this action on January 23, 2023, which provided for payment to be made from the proceeds from her personal injury claim.

 

6. It is my understanding that the personal injury claim was resolved on or about May, 2024. I have not been paid any portion of my bill since that time, despite filing and serving this lawsuit.

 

(Yashar Decl. ¶¶ 2-6.)  Further, the invoices attached as Exhibit A to the Yashar Declaration itemize an outstanding balance of $34,250 as requested.

 

c.      Wong

 

            In support of the request, Wong advances the Declaration of Albert P. Wong, M.D., which provides:

 

2. I am a neurosurgeon, and practice in the Los Angeles area with DOCS Spine+ Orthopedics.

 

3. On August 17, 2021, I assisted with a surgery on defendant Valena Mitchell.

 

4. As a result of the surgery, Ms. Mitchell incurred a bill for my services in the amount of $39,625. A copy of a billing summary reflecting that sum (which has been redacted to exclude confidential information) is attached as Exhibit "¿."

 

5. The billing was issued pursuant to a June 7, 2021 lien agreement signed by the defendants with my medical group, DOCS Spine + Orthopedics, which provided for payment to be made from the settlement of her personal injury claim.

 

6. It is my understanding that Ms. Mitchell's personal injury claim was resolved on or about May, 2024. I have not been paid any portion of my bill since that time, despite filing and serving this lawsuit.

 

(Wong Decl. ¶¶ 2-6.)   Yet, the invoices attached as Exhibit A to the Wong Declaration itemize an outstanding balance of only $39,625, not $39,652, as requested.

 

B.    Prejudgment Interest

 

            Plaintiffs seek prejudgment interest at the legal rate of 10% annually for the 845 days from June 1, 2024 to September 24, 2026.  The Court generally finds that the requested interest rate and days are appropriate.

 

            However, in light of the adjustments to the principal balances, as outlined above, the Court finds prejudgment interest should be awarded as follows:

 

·       DOCS: $413,137.50 principal balance x 10% ÷ 365 days/yr = $113.19 daily interest x 845 days = $95,645.55.

 

·       Yashar: $34,250 principal balance x 10% ÷ 365 days/yr = $9.38 daily interest x 845 days = $7,926.10, as requested.

 

·       Wong: $39,625 principal balance x 10% ÷ 365 days/yr = $10.86 daily interest x 845 days = $9,176.70.

 

C.    Attorneys’ Fees and Costs

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, which outlines recoverable costs to a prevailing party under Code of Civil Procedure section 1032, permits the recovery of attorneys’ fees when authorized by contract, statute, or law.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (a)(10).)  Code of Civil Procedure section 1021 provides “[e]xcept as attorney’s fees are specifically provided for by statute, the measure and mode of compensation of attorneys and counselors at law is left to the agreement, express or implied, of the parties [….]”  Similarly, Civil Code section 1717 provides “[i]n any action on a contract, where the contract specifically provides that attorney’s fees and costs, which are incurred to enforce that contract, shall be awarded either to one of the parties or to the prevailing party, then the party who is determined to be the party prevailing on the contract, whether he or she is the party specified in the contract or not, shall be entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees in addition to other costs.”  (Civ. Code, § 1717, subd. (a).)

 

The Code of Civil Procedure defines the “prevailing party” as follows:

 

[T]he party with a net monetary recovery, a defendant in whose favor a dismissal is entered, a defendant where neither plaintiff nor defendant obtains any relief, and a defendant as against those plaintiffs who do not recover any relief against that defendant. If any party recovers other than monetary relief and in situations other than as specified, the “prevailing party” shall be as determined by the court, and under those circumstances, the court, in its discretion, may allow costs or not and, if allowed, may apportion costs between the parties on the same or adverse sides pursuant to rules adopted under Section 1034.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4).)

 

            Attached as Exhibits A and C to the Complaint are Medical Lien Agreements between Mitchell and Dufour on the one hand and DOCS Spine/Dr. Wong on the other hand, which provides, “Attorney further agrees that in the event this lien is litigated that the prevailing party will be awarded attorney fees and costs.”  That provision is signed only by attorney Dufour.

           

            Further, attached as Exhibit B to the Complaint is the Lien Agreement as between Yashar, Mitchell, and Dufour.  Paragraph 9 of that agreement provides as follows:

 

9. ENFORCEMENT OF AGREEMENT. Should suit be filed, or any other action taken to enforce this agreement, outside of interpleader, the prevailing party shall be entitled to reasonable attorney's fees and costs. Jurisdiction and Venue is in Los Angeles, California where this agreement is entered into and to be carried out by the parties.

 

(Ex. B to Complaint at ¶ 9.)  

 

            Thus, DOCS and Wong are entitled to recoup attorneys’ fees as against the Dufour Defendants only, whereas Yashar is entitled to recoup attorneys’ fees as against both Mitchell and Dufour.        

 

            As for the amount of attorneys’ fees, Plaintiffs advance the Supplemental Declaration of J. Patrick Fleming, Jr., which provides:

 

5. To assist the Court, I have reviewed the billing summary attached hereto as Exhibit “D.” It shows that through May 30, 2026, my clients incurred $6,664 billed at $595/hour for 8.7 hours of my work, and $10,912 billed at $620/hour for 17.6 hours of my work. In addition, it shows an additional $288 incurred for paralegal and legal assistant time. All totaled, this adds up to $17,854

 

(Supp. Fleming Decl. at ¶ 5.)

 

            Therefore, Plaintiffs have adequately substantiated the requested attorneys’ fees.  Because Yashar’s pro rata share of the principal balance is about 7%, Yashar shall be entitled to recoup about 7% of the requested attorneys’ fees, or $1,255.61 against Mitchell. 

 

            Plaintiffs also request costs, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,958.17, representing $497.20 in filing fees, $2,082.22 in process server fees, and $378.75 for efiling.  (See CIV-100.)  Plaintiffs’ request for costs is granted as Plaintiffs are the prevailing parties in this action.   (Code Civ. Proc., § 1032, subd. (a)(4).) 

 

CONCLUSION

 

            Plaintiffs’ requests for default judgment is granted in part as follows:

 

·       DOCS: special damages in the amount of $413,137.50 and prejudgment interest in the amount of $95,645.55

 

·       Yashar: special damages in the amount of $34,250 and prejudgment interest in the amount of $7,926.10. 

 

·       Wong: special damages in the amount of $39,625 and prejudgment interest in the amount of $9,176.70

 

            In addition, Plaintiffs are entitled to their costs, jointly and severally, in the amount of $2,958.17. 

 

            Further, Plaintiffs are entitled to recoup attorneys’ fees, jointly and severally, against Defendants Dufour and Dufour Law in the amount of $17,854.  Of that amount, Defendant Mitchell is jointly and severally responsible for $1,255.61 as to Yashar only.

 

            Plaintiffs shall lodge a revised proposed Judgment in conformance with the ruling on or before October 9, 2026.  As such, the Court continues the Order to Show Cause re Entry of Default Judgment to October 30, 2026 at 8:30 A.M. in Department 207.  No appearance will be necessary at the Order to Show Cause hearing if the judgment is entered beforehand. 

 

            Unless Plaintiffs waive notice, the Clerk of the Court shall provide notice of the Court’s ruling.

MOTIONS

Motions to be Relieved as Counsel

MOVING PARTY

Bobby Benjy of Benjy Law Corporation

OPPOSING PARTY

(none)

 

MOTION

 

            Bobby Benjy of Beny Law Corporation, counsel for Plaintiffs Saraly Anavim (aka Linda Anavim) and Dr. Elliot Anavim (“Counsel”) moves to be relieved as counsel, citing an irreconcilable breakdown of the attorney-client relationship.  The motions are unopposed.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 284 provides “[t]he attorney in an action or special proceeding may be changed at any time before or after judgment or final determination as follows: 1. Upon the consent of both client and attorney, filed with the clerk, or entered in the minutes; 2. Upon the order of the court, upon the application of either client or attorney, after notice from one to the other.”

 

Procedural Requirements

 

California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362, requires:

 

(1) the motion must be made on form MC-051; (subd. (a));

 

(2) it must be accompanied by a declaration on form MC-052 stating why the motion is brought under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(2) instead of a consent brought under section 284(1); (subd. (c));

 

(3) a proposed order on form MC-053 must be lodged with the court, specifying all hearing dates scheduled in the action or proceeding, including the date of trial, if known; (subd. (e)); and

 

(4) The documents must be served on the client and on all parties that have appeared in the case. (subd. (d).)

 

If the notice is served by mail or electronic service, it must be accompanied by a declaration indicating that the address served is the current address, or in the case of service by mail, that it was served on the last known address and a more current address could not be located after reasonable efforts within 30 days before filing the motion.  (Ibid.)  The court may delay the effective date of the order relieving counsel until proof of service of a copy of the signed order on the client has been filed with the court.”  (Ibid.) 

 

Substantive Requirements

 

Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16(a) outlines the reasons a lawyer must withdraw from representation of a client:

 

(1)   the client is bringing an action, conducting a defense, asserting a position in litigation, or taking an appeal, without probable cause and for the purpose of harassing or maliciously injuring any person;

 

(2)   the representation will result in violation of the Rules of Professional Conduct or the State Bar Act;

 

(3)   the lawyer’s mental or physical condition renders it unreasonably difficult to carry out the representation effectively; or

 

(4)   the client discharges the lawyer.

 

Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16(b) outlines the reasons a lawyer may withdraw from representation of a client:

 

(1)   the client insists upon presenting a claim or defense in litigation, or asserting a position or making a demand in a non-litigation matter, that is not warranted under existing law and cannot be supported by good faith argument for an extension, modification, or reversal of existing law;

 

(2)   the client either seeks to pursue a criminal or fraudulent course of conduct or has used the lawyer’s services to advance a course of conduct that the lawyer reasonably believes was a crime or fraud;

 

(3)   the client insists that the lawyer pursue a course of conduct that is criminal or fraudulent;

 

(4)   the client by other conduct renders it unreasonably difficult for the lawyer to carry out the representation effectively;

 

(5)   the client breaches a material term of an agreement with, or obligation, to the lawyer relating to the representation, and the lawyer has given the client a reasonable warning after the breach that the lawyer will withdraw unless the client fulfills the agreement or performs the obligation;

 

(6)   the client knowingly and freely assents to termination of the representation;

 

(7)   the inability to work with co-counsel indicates that the best interests of the client likely will be served by withdrawal;

 

(8)   the lawyer’s mental or physical condition renders it difficult for the lawyer to carry out the representation effectively;

 

(9)   a continuation of the representation is likely to result in a violation of these rules or the State Bar Act; or

 

(10)         the lawyer believes in good faith in a proceeding pending before a tribunal that the tribunal will find the existence of other good cause for withdrawal.

 

DISCUSSION

 

            Counsel has filed forms MC-051, MC-052, and MC-053 for each client.  The attorney declarations (MC-052) indicate that the motions were filed instead of filing consents because:

 

An irreconcilable breakdown in the attorney-client relationship has occurred, and the client has, by conduct, rendered it unreasonably difficult for counsel to carry out the representation effectively. (Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4).) Counsel gave the client advance written notice of counsel's intention to seek relief and an opportunity to avoid the necessity of this motion by executing a substitution of attorney; the client has not done so, and a consent under Code of Civil Procedure section 284(1) is therefore unavailable. Counsel states the grounds in general terms, as permitted by California Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(c), in order to preserve the client's confidences and secrets. (Bus. & Prof. Code, section 6068, subd. (e)(1); Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct, rule 1.6.) Should the Court require a further showing, counsel respectfully requests leave to make that showing in camera.

(MC-052 at ¶ 2.)  As such, the Court finds that the motions comply with the Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 1.16(b)(4).) 

 

            The proofs of service indicates each of the requisite forms were served electronically on counsel for Defendant and on the clients by mail. 

 

However, the attorney declarations indicate that the clients’ addresses have been confirmed current via a public records search.  This is insufficient to demonstrate that the attorney has confirmed with the client that the addresses are current.  Rather, that is indicative that counsel has been unable to locate a more current address for the client after making various efforts, pursuant to paragraph 3(b)(2) of form MC-052, yet Counsel provided the information under paragraph 3(b)(1).  Despite the technical, procedural issue, the Court finds that Counsel has substantially complied with the Rules of Court. 

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

 

Having found the Motions both procedurally and substantively proper, the Court Grants Counsel’s Motions to be Relieved as Counsel. 

 

Counsel must serve the signed orders (forms MC-053), which shall include information about all future hearings and proceedings noticed by any party, or ordered by the Court, on the clients and all other parties who have appeared in the action, within 10 days of the date of this Order, and file the proofs of service of such.  Counsel will remain the attorney of record for Plaintiffs Saraly Anavim (aka Linda Anavim) and Dr. Elliot Anavim until Counsel files the requisite proofs of service.  (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1362(e).) 

 

Further, to ensure that the Court’s records are updated following the filing of the proofs of service, Counsel shall contact the Court to advise that the proofs of service has been filed.

MOTIONS

Motion for Leave to File a Cross-Complaint

MOVING PARTY

Defendant City of Santa Monica

OPPOSING PARTY

none

 

MOTION

 

On October 21, 2025, Plaintiff Javohir Abdusattarov (“Plaintiff”) brought suit against Defendants City of Santa Monica (“City”); County of Los Angeles (“County”); and Damon White (“White”) alleging two causes of action for (1) motor vehicle negligence and (2) general negligence.

 

City and County answered the Complaint on March 24, 2026.  City now moves for leave to file a cross-complaint.  The motion is unopposed.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A party against whom a cause of action has been asserted in a complaint or cross-complaint may file a cross-complaint setting forth either or both of the following:

 

(a)   Any cause of action he has against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him. Nothing in this subdivision authorizes the filing of a cross-complaint against the plaintiff in an action commenced under Title 7 (commencing with Section 1230.010) of Part 3.

 

(b)   Any cause of action he has against a person alleged to be liable thereon, whether or not such person is already a party to the action, if the cause of action asserted in his cross-complaint (1) arises out of the same transaction, occurrence, or series of transactions or occurrences as the cause brought against him or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the property or controversy which is the subject of the cause brought against him.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 428.10, subds. (a)-(b).) 

 

(a)   A party shall file a cross-complaint against any of the parties who filed the complaint or cross-complaint against him or her before or at the same time as the answer to the complaint or cross-complaint.

 

(b)   Any other cross-complaint may be filed at any time before the court has set a date for trial.

 

(c)   A party shall obtain leave of court to file any cross-complaint except one filed within the time specified in subdivision (a) or (b). Leave may be granted in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.

 

(Code Civ. Proc., § 428.50, subds. (a)-(c).) 

 

“Cross-complaints for comparative equitable indemnity would appear virtually always¿transactionally¿related to the main action.”¿¿(Time for Living, Inc. v. Guy Hatfield Homes¿(1991) 230 Cal.App.3d 30, 38; see also Countrywide Home Loans, Inc. v. Superior Court (1999) 69 Cal.App.4th 785, 799 [“Undoubtedly, a claim for contribution or indemnity ‘arises out of’ the same transaction or occurrence as the plaintiff's claim”].)

 

Indeed, where a cause of action would otherwise be lost, leave to amend is appropriate even if the party was negligent in not moving for leave to amend earlier.  “The legislative mandate is clear.  A policy of liberal construction of section 426.50 to avoid forfeiture of causes of action is imposed on the trial court.  A motion to file a cross-complaint at any time during the course of the action must be granted unless bad faith of the moving party is demonstrated where forfeiture would otherwise result.”  (Silver Organizations, Ltd. v. Frank (1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 98-99.)   

 

DISCUSSION

 

            City seeks leave to file a cross-complaint against Plaintiff to assert a compulsory negligence claim arising from the same motor vehicle collision alleged in Plaintiff’s Complaint.  Thus, because City’s claims are against Plaintiff, the cross-complaint is generally proper.  Further, because the cross-claim is compulsory, it would be lost if leave is denied.        

 

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

 

            Adhering to the liberal policy of granting leave to avoid forfeiture of claims, the Court grants City’s unopposed motion for leave to file the cross-complaint against Plaintiff. 

 

            Further, the Court orders City to file and serve the Cross-Complaint attached to the Motion on or before October 9, 2026. 

 

Further, the Court will enter the proposed Order lodged on August 28, 2026 in conformity with the ruling. 

 

City shall provide notice of the Court’s ruling and file the notice with a proof of service forthwith.
