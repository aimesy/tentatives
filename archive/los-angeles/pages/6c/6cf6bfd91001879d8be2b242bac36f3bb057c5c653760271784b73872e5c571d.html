DEPARTMENT F43 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25CHCV00039    Hearing Date: July 20, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-20-26

Case # 25CHCV00039, Fabian, et al. v. Joers, et al.

Trial Date: 08-07-28

 

MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian

RESPONDING PARTY:  No opposition was filed.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order compelling defendant John Carl Joers to serve verified responses to plaintiff Fabian’s first set of form interrogatories and to impose monetary sanctions of $2,335 against defendant Joers and his counsel of record.

 

RULING:  Motion is granted.  The court awards plaintiff Fabian $1,310 in monetary sanctions.

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

The personal injury arises from a motor vehicle collision in which defendant John Carl Joers rearended plaintiffs Estela Yadhira Fabian and Omar Morales.  Plaintiffs allege defendant Joers was driving a vehicle owned by defendant McLogan Supply Co., Inc. and acting within the scope of his employment with McLogan Supply.  On January 6, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a complaint against both defendants, asserting negligence, vicarious liability, and negligent entrustment.  McLogan Supply Co. answered on March 24, 2025, and Joers answered on November 6, 2025.

 

On April 7, 2026, plaintiff Fabian electronically served defendant Joers with her first set of form interrogatories.  (Declaration of Taylor Scott Kruse ¶ 1, Exh. 1.)  Responses were due May 11, 2026.  On May 12, 2026, plaintiff Fabian extended the deadline for defendant Joers to serve objection-free responses to May 15, 2026.  (Id. ¶ 3, Exh. 2.)  No responses were ever served.

 

On June 10, 2026, plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian filed the instant motion to compel defendant John Carl Joers’s objection-free, verified responses to her first set of requests for production.  Plaintiff Fabian also seeks to impose $2,335 in monetary sanctions against defendant Joers and his counsel of record, Shelby Clark and Hinshaw & Culbertson, LLP.  No opposition was filed.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Compelling Responses to Form Interrogatories

 

A propounding party may move to compel responses to form interrogatories where the responding party fails to provide any responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (b).)  The propounding party must show the interrogatories were properly served, that the time to respond expired, and no response has been served.  (Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)  Unless excused by a protective order, the responding party must serve responses (an answer, objection, or election to allow inspecting or copy records) within 30 days after the interrogatories are served or according to an agreed upon deadline extension.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.210, subd. (a), 2030.270.)  Failing to respond within these time limits waives objections.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).)

 

The court has reviewed the moving papers and finds that plaintiff Fabian properly served her requests for production and granted defendant Joers a deadline extension.  However, defendant Joers has failed to serve any responses.  Moreover, defendant Joers does not oppose plaintiff Fabian’s motion and provide a substantial justification for failing to provide responses.

 

Therefore, the court grants plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian’s motion to compel defendant John Carl Joers’s responses to her first set of form interrogatories.

 

Defendant Coers is ordered to serve objection-free, verified responses within 30 days of the issuing of this order.

 

            B.  Sanctions

 

Plaintiff Fabian asks the court to impose monetary sanctions in the amount of $2,335 against defendant Joers and his counsel of record, Shelby Clark with Hinshaw & Culbertson, LLP, jointly and severally.  (Kruse Decl. ¶ 12.)

 

The court must impose sanctions against any party who “unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel responses to interrogatories unless, it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (c).)

 

Plaintiff Fabian is entitled to sanctions for the seasons stated above in Section A.

 

Plaintiff’s counsel charges $650 per hour.  (Kruse Decl. ¶ 11(a).)  The request includes:  1.5 hours preparing the motion and supporting papers—$975; 1.0 hour reviewing and replying to an opposition—$650; 1.0 hour preparing for and attending the motion hearing remotely—$650; and a $60 filing fee.

 

The court finds the requested hourly rate is excessive, considering counsel provides no information concerning his experience in order to support the rate.  The court lowers attorney Kruse’s hourly rate to $500 per hour.  Finally, the court reduces the time requested by 1.0 hour because no opposition was filed.

 

Therefore, the court awards plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian monetary sanctions in the reduced amount of $1,310:  1.5 hours preparing the motion and supporting papers—$750; 1.0 hour preparing for and attending the motion hearing remotely—$500; and a $60 filing fee.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

The court grants plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian’s motion to compel defendant John Carl Joers’s responses to her first set of form interrogatories.

 

The court orders defendant Joers to serve code-compliant, objection-free, and verified responses within thirty (30) days of the issuing of this order.

 

The court awards plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian $1,310 in monetary sanctions.  Defendant Joers and his attorney of record are ordered to pay plaintiff’s counsel within thirty (30) days of the issuing of order.

 

Plaintiff Estela Yadhira Fabian to give notice.


Case Number: 25CHCV02505    Hearing Date: July 20, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-20-26

Case # 25CHCV02505, Quintero, et al. v. Geragosian

Trial Date: 10-30-28

 

MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Saro Geragosian

RESPONDING PARTY:  No response was filed.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order compelling plaintiff Lilia Quintero to serve objection-free, verified responses to defendant’s first set of requests for production and imposing $435 in monetary sanctions against plaintiff Quintero and her counsel Igor Fradkin and Downtown LA Law Group, jointly and severally.

 

RULING:  Motion is granted.  The court awards defendant Geragosian $435 in monetary sanctions.

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

The personal injury action arises out of a vehicle collision in which plaintiffs Lilia Quintero and Daniela Haro were injured when defendant Saro Geragosian (Defendant) collided with their vehicle.  On July 16, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a complaint against Defendant, asserting four causes of action:  (1) motor vehicle; (2) negligence; (3) misdemeanor hit and run in violation of Vehicle Code section 20001, subdivision (a); and (4) intentional infliction of emotional distress.

 

On October 8, 2025, Defendant electronically served plaintiff Lilia Quintero with his first set of requests for production.  (Declaration of Nageen C. Jalali ¶ 2, Exh. A.)  Responses were due November 10, 2025.  Despite being granted six deadline extensions, plaintiff Quintero has yet to serve responses.  (Jalali Decl. ¶¶ 3-5, Exh. B.)

 

On May 27, 2026, Defendant filed the subject motion to compel plaintiff Quintero’s objection-free, verified responses to his first set of requests for production and to impose $435 against Quintero and her counsel for record, jointly and severally.  No opposition was filed.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Compelling Responses to Requests for Production

 

A demanding party may move to compel responses to requests for production where the responding party fails to provide any responses.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2031.300, subd. (b), 2030.290, subd. (b).)  The demanding party must show the requests were properly served, that the time to respond expired, and no response has been served.  (Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 403-404.)  The responding party must respond separately to each demand by agreeing to comply, stating an inability to comply, or objecting to all or part of the demand.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.210, subd. (a).)  The responding party must serve responses within 30 days after the requests for production are served or according to an agreed upon deadline extension.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.260, subd. (a)(1)-(3).)  Failing to respond within these time limits waives objections.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290, subd. (a).)

 

The court finds that Defendant properly served plaintiff Quintero with his first set of requests for production.  However, plaintiff Quintero failed to serve responses by the original deadline or the subsequent deadline extensions and has failed to provide a substantial justification for the delay in serving responses.  Nor does plaintiff Quintero oppose the instant motion.

 

Therefore, the court grants defendant Saro Geragosian’s motion to compel responses to his first set of requests for production.  Plaintiff Lilia Quintero is ordered to serve objection-free, verified responses and responsive documents within 30 days of the issuing of this order.

 

            B.  Sanctions

 

Defendant asks the court to impose $435 in monetary sanctions against plaintiff Quintero and her counsel of record, jointly and severally.  (Jalali Decl. ¶ 6.)

 

The court must impose sanctions against the party or attorney, or both, who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to requests for production, unless the court finds that the sanctioned party “acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300, subd. (c).)

 

For the reasons stated in Section A above, Defendant is entitled to monetary sanctions.

 

Defendant’s counsel charges $250 per hour.  (Jalali Decl. ¶ 6.)  The request includes:  1.5 hours preparing the motion and a $60 filing fee.

 

The court finds the requested hourly rate and time billed is reasonable and grants Defendant’s request for $435 in monetary sanctions.

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

The court grants defendant Saro Geragosian’s motion to compel plaintiff Lilia Quintero’s responses to his first set of requests for production.

 

The court orders plaintiff Lilia Quintero to serve code-compliant, objection-free, and verified responses within thirty (30) days of the issuing of this order.

 

The court awards defendant Saro Geragosian $435 in monetary sanctions.  Plaintiff Quintero and her attorney of record are ordered to pay defense counsel within thirty (30) days of this order.

 

Defendant Saro Geragosian to give notice.


Case Number: 26CHCV01065    Hearing Date: July 20, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-20-26

Case # 26CHCV01065, Gee, et al. v. Rhodes

Trial Date: N/A

 

DEMURRER AND MOTION TO STRIKE AN ANSWER

 

MOVING PARTIES:  Plaintiffs Steven Gee and Sonia Gee

RESPONDING PARTY:  Defendant Stephen Rhodes, in pro per

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order sustaining plaintiffs’ demurrer to the defendant’s Answer or striking the eleven affirmative defenses in defendant’s Answer.

 

RULING:  Demurrer to the Answer’s eleven affirmative defenses is sustained with leave to amend.

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On March 16, 2026, plaintiffs Steven Gee and Sonia Gee (Plaintiffs) filed this action, seeking declaratory relief as to their legal rights with respect to ownership of real property.  Plaintiffs allege they own real property (Parcel 48) which is adjacent to real property (Parcel 51) owned by defendant Stephen Rhodes (Defendant).  In order to access Parcel 48, Plaintiffs, their predecessor, and the owners of several other parcels (Parcels 49 and 50) have utilized a small shortcut over the southern corner of Defendant’s Parcel 51 since 1986.  Plaintiffs claim their predecessor’s use of the shortcut has been actual, open and notorious, continuous, and hostile, without Defendant’s or his predecessors’ consent.  As such, Plaintiffs assert the shortcut is a valid, binding and enforceable prescriptive easement, appurtenant to Parcel 48, and for the benefit of Parcel 48 as the dominant estate, and thereby imposing a burden and servitude upon Parcel 51, as the servient estate.  According to Plaintiffs, Defendant will suffer no harm or damages if the Prescriptive Easement is validated by the court, because the Prescriptive Easement does not impose any meaningful burden on Defendant or Parcel 51, as it represents less than one percent of Defendant’s 289,789 square foot estate.  In contrast, Plaintiffs will suffer enormous harm and damages if their Prescriptive Easement rights are refuted.

 

On May 5, 2026, Defendant filed and served an Answer.  Defendant is self-represented.

 

On June 11, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant demurrer to the Answer and motion to strike the Answer’s eleven affirmative defenses, asserting the Answer fails to state sufficient facts.

 

On July 7, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition, asserting the defenses are based on factual disputes concerning the roadway, Plaintiffs claimed easement rights, permission, historical use, City planning records, and other matters directly related to Plaintiffs’ claims.  Defendant’s supporting declaration and exhibits provide additional factual detail to support the defenses.

 

No reply was filed.

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

Demurrers and motions to strike must be accompanied by a “meet and confer” declaration stating that the parties met and conferred “in person, by telephone, or by video conference” and stating that (1) the parties failed to reach an agreement resolving the issues raised in the demurrer and motion to strike;  or (2) that the party who filed the pleading subject to the demurrer or motion to strike failed to respond to the meet and confer request or failed to meet and confer in good faith.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.41, subd. (a)(3), 435.5, subd. (a)(3).)  In Department F43, meet and confer means in person or via phone, not by letter or email.  (Department F43 Courtroom Information, p. 2.)

 

The “meet and confer” requirement was met because Defendant met with Plaintiffs’ counsel by  telephone on May 12, 2026.  (Declaration of David Leventhal ¶ 2.)  The parties stipulated to allowing Defendant to file an amended answer by June 5, 2026 and allowing Plaintiffs until June 12, 2026, to file a demurrer to the original answer.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Demurrer to an Answer

 

 “A party against whom an answer has been filed may object, by demurrer as provided in Section 430.30, to the answer upon any one or more of the following grounds:  (a) The answer does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense. (b) The answer is uncertain, [meaning] ambiguous and unintelligible. and (c) Where the answer pleads a contract, it cannot be ascertained from the answer whether the contract is written or oral.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.20, subds. (a)-(c).)  “When any ground for objection to a complaint, cross-complaint, or answer appears on the face thereof, or from any matter of which the court is required to or may take judicial notice, the objection on that ground may be taken by a demurrer to the pleading.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30, subd. (a).)  “A demurrer to an answer may be taken to the whole answer or to any one or more of the several defenses set up in the answer.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.50, subd. (b).)  “A demurrer shall distinctly specify the grounds upon which any of the objections to the complaint, cross-complaint, or answer are taken.  Unless it does so, it may be disregarded.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.60.)

 

The inquiry is “whether the answer raises a defense to the plaintiff’s stated cause of action.” (Timberidge Enterprises, Inc. v. City of Santa Rosa (1978) 86 Cal.App.3d 873, 879-880.)  A demurrer to an answer “admits all facts well pleaded in the answer, including denials.”  (Warren v. Harootunian (1961) 189 Cal.App.2d 546, 548; South Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 733 [noting an answer’s sufficiency depends on the complaint].)  “[T]erse legal conclusions” are insufficient and the answer must aver to facts “as carefully and with as much detail as the facts which constitute the cause of action and are alleged in the complaint.” (FPI Development, Inc. v. Nakashima (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367, 384.)  Moreover, “if one of the defenses or counterclaims is free from the objections urged by demurrer, then a demurrer to the entire answer must be overruled.”  (South Shore Land Co. v. Petersen (1964) 226 Cal.App.2d 725, 734.)  When “the answer sets up a good defense and denies material allegations of the complaint,” “it is sufficient as against a general demurrer.”  (Barasch v. Epstein (1957) 147 Cal.App.2d 439, 443.)

 

The answer to the complaint must contain (1) the general or specific denial of the material allegations of the complaint controverted by the defendant. and (2) a statement of any new matter constituting a defense.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 431.430, subds. (b)(1)-(2).)  “New matter” refers to “something relied on by a defendant which is not put in issue by the plaintiff.  Thus, where matters are not responsive to essential allegations of the complaint, they must be raised in the answer as ‘new matter.’” (Walsh v. West Valley Mission Community College Dist. (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1532, 1546, citation omitted [defining “new matter” as “any ground urged in avoidance of the complaint, i.e., some independent reason why plaintiff should be barred from recovery, even if everything alleged in the complaint was true.”].)  Any issue on which defendant bears the burden of proof at trial is a “new matter” and must be specially pled in the answer.  (Harris v. City of Santa Monica (2013) 56 Cal.4th 203, 239)

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) 

 

Here, Defendant’s Answer includes a general denial of each allegation in the Complaint, each cause of action, and Plaintiffs’ alleged damages.  (Answer at p. 1.)

 

Defendant’s Affirmative Defenses consists solely of legal conclusions without alleging any supporting facts or incorporating the documents attached to his supporting declaration.

 

After reviewing the Answer and the demurrer, it appears Defendant attempted to get by with pleading the bare minimum.  Defendant pleads conclusory legal conclusion devoid of facts that dispute Plaintiffs’ allegations or demonstrate Plaintiffs fail to allege a declaratory relief claim.  Instead, Defendant presents evidence from outside the four corners of the Answer to demonstrate factual disputes:  text messages from April, May, and June of 2025;  a proposed easement correspondence from April of 2025 through June of 2025; a June 18, 2025, letter to the City of Santa Clarita concerning the easement and access; a City Correspondence Regarding Easement Verification and Access Requirements; Planning Commission Agenda Report, Resolution, and Conditions of Approval; Recorded Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions - Parcel Map No. 8645; and Recorded Parcel Map No. 8645.  (Declaration of Stephen Rhodes, Exhs. A-I.)

 

Defendant does not ask the court to take judicial notice of these documents and provides no explanation for failing to incorporate the exhibits into the Answer or to include allegations concerning the content of the exhibits.  (See Evid. Code, § 452.)  “The trial court shall take judicial notice of any matter specified in Section 452 if a party requests it and:  (a) Gives each adverse party sufficient notice of the request, through the pleadings or otherwise, to enable such adverse party to prepare to meet the request; and (b) Furnishes the court with sufficient information to enable it to take judicial notice of the matter.”  (Evid. Code, § 453, subds. (a)-(b).)

 

Defendant must plead his answer with more facts and a greater specificity. 

 

Finally, Defendant fails to cite any legal authority to support the contentions in his Opposition.  Absent a good faith basis for modifying or extending an existing law, litigants are generally prohibited from asserting a position in litigation without authority.  (See, e.g., In re Estate of Randall (1924) 194 Cal. 725, 728-729 [“Contentions supported neither by argument nor by citation of authority are deemed to be without foundation, and to have been abandoned.”];  see also Lafferty v. Wells Fargo Bank (2013) 213 Cal. App. 4th 545, 571-572 [“When a point is asserted without argument and authority for the proposition, ‘it is deemed to be without foundation and requires no discussion by the reviewing court.’”];  Atchley v. City of Fresno (1984) 151 Cal. App. 3d 635, 647 [“Where a point is merely asserted by appellant’s counsel without any argument of or authority for the proposition, it is deemed to be without foundation and requires no discussion by the reviewing court.”].)

 

Because Defendant has failed to plead sufficient facts to support his affirmative defenses, the court sustains Plaintiffs’ demurrer to the Answer with leave to amend. 

 

 

            B.  Motion to Strike

 

In the alternative, Plaintiffs’ asks the court to strike each affirmative defense for failing to adequately state an affirmative defense to the Complaint.

 

Because the court has sustained the demurrer to the entire Answer, this portion of Plaintiffs’ moving papers is moot.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Demurrer to the Answer’s eleven affirmative defenses is sustained with leave to amend.

 

Defendant Stephen Rhodes may file and serve and amended Answer within twenty (20) days of the issuing of this order.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.
