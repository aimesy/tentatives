[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF MICHAEL BRIAN SMITH’S MOTION TO SET ASIDE/VACATE JUDGMENT [CRS #8855] Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Michael Brian Smith

Opposing Party: Defendant, Michael Kors Inc.

Notice: OK

Plaintiff Michael B. Smith brought this suit against his former employer, Defendant Michael Kors Inc., alleging race discrimination in his employment and a subsequent unlawful layoff.

On March 26, 2026, the Court granted a motion for judgment on the pleadings filed by Defendant, which disposed of the then-operative March 19, 2024, Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”). Plaintiff filed a motion for reconsideration on March 30, 2026, which was opposed by Defendant, and was ultimately denied by the Court on May 20, 2026. The Court entered judgment in Defendant’s favor that same day.

Now before the Court is Plaintiff’s motion to set aside/vacate the May 20, 2026, judgment on the grounds that another action affecting the outcome of the proceedings before this Court is purportedly presently pending before the Compton, California branch of the Los Angeles Superior Court as 22CMCV00753, Smith v. Michael Kors Inc. (the “Santa Monica action”). (See Motion, p. 2, ¶ 4.)

The motion is DENIED, with prejudice.

Plaintiff’s motion is noticed as being premised on Code of Civil Procedure sections 473, subdivision (d), and 1008, subdivisions (a), (b), and (e). (Motion, pp. 1-2.) Both premises fail.

As argued in Defendant’s opposition, there is no applicable provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), here. (See Opposition pp. 11-12.) Plaintiff is asking that the Court vacate a final judgment based on the purported pendency of the Santa Monica action. This is not a valid ground for relief under section 473, subdivision (d), which is limited to clerical errors and void judgments and orders, including those voided by extrinsic fraud or mistake, neither of which Plaintiff’s paper show. (See Kramer v. Traditional Escrow, Inc. (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 13, 29 [discussing § 473, subd. (d)].)

Similarly, none of the grounds for party-requested reconsideration—new or different facts, circumstances, or law (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (a))—appear here, as the requested relief is based on the purported pendency of the Santa Monica action rather than some fact, circumstance, or new law relating to this action.

Last, the Court takes judicial notice of Exhibit A attached to Defendant’s opposition, which shows a June 18, 2026, minute order for the Santa Monica action, in which the court in that case denied a similar motion by Plaintiff in this case to vacate or set aside the judgment in that case, which was entered on November 6, 2025. (Opposition, Request for Judicial Notice, Ex. A, pp. 6-7.)

Plaintiff’s motion is accordingly DENIED. No further motions seeking identical relief shall be entertained absent a proper statutory basis.

Case Number: 22STCV12323    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 731

ALLA KUTZ, an individual, IGOR KUTZ, an individual, Plaintiff(s), vs. MIKHAIL SIRETSKIY, an individual; BIG BLOCK REALTY, INC., a California corporation; CANGAS RE, LLC, a limited liability company; CAL WEST ESCROW, a California corporation; and DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, Defendant(s).

CASE NO: 22STCV12323 [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION OF DEFENDANT MIKHAIL SIRETSKIY TO WITHDRAW AND/OR AMEND DEEMED ADMISSIONS TO PLAINTIFFS ALLA KUTZ AND IGOR KUTZ’S REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION PURSUANT TO CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE § 2033.300 Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 31, 2026

Moving Party: Defendant, Mikhail Siretskiy [in pro per]

Opposing Party: None

Notice: OK

Before the Court is Defendant Mikhail Sirestkiy’s (“Defendant”) unopposed motion for an “order withdrawing and/or amending the matters deemed admitted in response to Plaintiffs Alla Kutz and Igor Kutz’s Requests for Admission and permitting Defendant to serve verified responses.” (Mot., p. 2.)

After review, and though unopposed, the Court DENIES Defendant’s motion.

When a responding party fails to respond to requests for admissions within the statutory or agreed-upon timeframe, the substance of the requests for admission is not automatically deemed admitted; instead, the party who served the requests for admission must make a motion to deem as

admitted the truth of the matters in the requests for admission. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (b).) Here, the Court’s record fails to reflect that Plaintiffs filed a motion to deem as admitted the truth of their admission requests directed to Defendant Mikhail Siretskiy. Consequently, Defendant is requesting that deemed admissions be withdrawn where no such admissions were ever admitted. No relief, as framed in the notice of motion, can follow. (Cf. Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (a) [waiver of objections to requests for admission and motion mechanism to lift waiver].)

Defendant’s motion is accordingly DENIED.

Case Number: 23STCV16399    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 731

JALEH ELGHANIAN, an individual, Plaintiff(s), vs. ABNER PARTIYELI, an individual, et al., Defendant(s).

CASE NO: 23STCV16399 [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF JALEH ELGHANIAN’S MOTION TO: (1) CONTINUE TRIAL AND ALL RELATED DEADLINES; (2) REOPEN AND EXTEND DISCOVERY; (3) COMPEL DEPOSITIONS OF DEFENDANTS ABNER PARTIYELI AND FARSHID PARTIYELI; AND (4) IMPOSE MONETARY SANCTIONS AGAINST DEFENDANTS AND THEIR COUNSEL Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Jaleh Elghanian

Opposing Party: Defendants Abner Partiyeli, Farshid Partiyeli, Robertson Regency Center, LLC, 914 Holding Group LLC, Clara Industrial Holding, LLC

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

Before the Court in a single filing are Plaintiff Jaleh Elghanian’s opposed motions to continue trial, reopen and extend discovery, compel the depositions of two defendants, and corresponding requests for sanctions.

After review, the Court notes and rules as follows.

2. Preliminary Observation

The Court declines to deny the relief requested by Plaintiff based on Plaintiff combining five separate requests for relief into a single filing. (See Opposition, pp. 4, 8.) No statute or rule of court, local or state, requires that motions for the relief here requested by Plaintiff must appear in separate filings.

3. Motion to Continue Trial

Plaintiff’s motion to continue trial is MOOT.

The motion was filed on May 22, 2026, and requested continuance of trial from June 15, 2026, to a future date. However, trial in this action was continued at the June 5, 2026, Final Status Conference to December 14, 2026.

Consequently, the requested relief is MOOT.

4. Motion to Reopen and Extend Discovery

Plaintiff’s motion to reopen and extend discovery is DENIED.

Here, as based on a trial date of June 15, 2026, discovery in this action was cut off on May 15, 2026. (See 5/22/24 Minutes [setting trial for “11/17/25”]; 6/26/25 Minutes [by party agreement, continuing trial to 6/15/26, with cutoffs to run from new trial date]; 6/5/26 Minutes [trial continued to 12/14/26, with “[a]ll discovery cutoff dates remain[ing] with the original trial date”].)

In assessing whether to reopen discovery, courts consider “any matter relevant to the leave requested,” including: (1) the necessity and the reasons for the discovery; (2) the diligence or lack of diligence of the party seeking the discovery or the hearing of a discovery motion, and the reasons that the discovery was not completed or that the discovery motion was not heard earlier; (3) any likelihood that permitting the discovery or hearing the discovery motion will prevent the case from going to trial on the date set, or otherwise interfere with the trial calendar, or result in prejudice to any other party; and (4) the

length of time that has elapsed between any date previously set, and the date presently set, for the trial of the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.050, subd. (b).)

Here, Plaintiff seeks to reopen discovery to take the depositions of Defendant Abner Partiyeli and Defendant Farshid Partiyeli, for which Plaintiff first noticed depositions on April 15, 2024, more than two years before this motion was filed in May 2026. (Motion, Farivar Decl., ¶ 5, Exs. 1, 2.)

While the moving papers profess diligence in seeking these depositions (Motion, pp. 11-13), the Court is not persuaded. Simply put, Plaintiff’s explanations fall short of a satisfactory answer as to why Plaintiff did not file motions for orders compelling the individual Defendants’ depositions prior to the discovery cutoff.

Moreover, given that trial is currently scheduled for December 2026, it is possible that reopening discovery to take the depositions of the individual Defendants could require pushing back trial to a future date.

Under these circumstances, reopening discovery is not merited, and Plaintiff’s motion for that relief is DENIED.

5. Motions to Compel Deposition

Plaintiff’s motions to compel the depositions of Defendant Abner Partiyeli and Defendant Farshid Partiyeli are DENIED.

As explained in the preceding subsection, discovery was cut off on May 15, 2026. No Court-ordered depositions can move forward under these circumstances.

6. Requests for Sanctions

Plaintiff’s requests for sanctions, tied to the motions to compel depositions, are DENIED as derivative of the motions to compel depositions.

Plaintiff Jaleh Elghanian is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV00460    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 731

SEVA VENTURES, LLC, a California Limited Liability Company; Plaintiff, vs. THE HANDSEL GROUP, LLC, a California Limited Liability Company separately and doing business as The Grove Post-Acute Care Center; and DOES 1 through 50 inclusive; Defendants.

CASE NO: 24STCV00460 [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF SEVA VENTURES, LLC’S MOTION TO REOPEN DISCOVERY BASED ON NEW TRIAL DATE Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Seva Ventures, LLC

Opposing Party: Defendant The Handsel Group, LLC

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

Plaintiff Seva Ventures, LLC, sues Defendants The Handsel Group, LLC, Doe 1 Henry Kim, Doe 2 George Vergara, and Does 3 through 50 pursuant to a January 12, 2024, First Amended Complaint alleging various claims arising from Defendants’ alleged refusal to comply with the terms of a contractual agreement through which Plaintiff acquired a 70% ownership interest in The Handsel Group, LLC, in lieu of unpaid monies owed by Defendants to Plaintiff.

On June 12, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to reopen discovery, which The Handsel Group opposed on July 8, 2026. Plaintiff’s motion is now before the Court.

2. Motion to Reopen and Extend Discovery

a. Legal Standard on Motion to Reopen and Extend Discovery

In assessing whether to reopen discovery, courts consider “any matter relevant to the leave requested,” including: (1) the necessity and the reasons for the discovery; (2) the diligence or lack of diligence of the party seeking the discovery or the hearing of a discovery motion, and the reasons that the discovery was not completed or that the discovery motion was not heard earlier; (3) any likelihood that permitting the discovery or hearing the discovery motion will prevent the case from going to trial on the date set, or otherwise interfere with the trial calendar, or result in prejudice to any other party; and (4) the length of time that has elapsed between any date previously set, and the date presently set, for the trial of the action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2024.050, subd. (b).)

b. Discussion

After review, the Court finds in favor of Plaintiff.

A brief procedural history will assist in illustrating the basis for the Court’s determination:

(1) On January 8, 2024, Plaintiff filed this action;

(2) On May 25, 2024, the Court set trial for November 17, 2025;

(3) On June 26, 2025, the Court continued trial to June 15, 2026;

(4) On August 4, 2025, Plaintiff filed a notice noting a settlement “in principle” between the parties and requesting that the trial and final status conference dates be taken off calendar and set for a case management conference;

(5) On August 7, 2025, the Court interlineated and thereafter executed the proposed order filed by Plaintiff with the notice of settlement, therethrough taking the trial and final status conference then set in this action off calendar;

(6) The Court also set an OSC re: Dismissal (Settlement) for October 1, 2025;

(7) On October 1, 2025, the Court held the OSC and, after conferring with counsel, continued the OSC to December 8, 2025;

(8) On November 24, 2025, Plaintiff filed a joint response by the parties in relation to the then-upcoming December 8th OSC, in which the parties noted again that they had “agreed, in principle, to a settlement of this case”, specifically “by way of Plaintiff’s purchase of Defendant”;

(9) On December 8, 2025, the Court held the OSC and, after conferring with counsel, continued the OSC to March 9, 2026;

(10) On March 6, 2026, Plaintiff filed a substitution of attorney replacing counsel in the action; and

(11) Just three days later, on March 9, 2026, the Court held the OSC and, after conferring with counsel, set trial in this action for November 15, 2027.

Plaintiff’s moving papers add to the record by noting that the parties’ settlement agreement broke down sometime after November 24, 2025, and that shortly after substituting in around March 2026, Plaintiff’s current counsel discovered the need for additional discovery based on the facts that:

(1) Previous counsel’s discovery efforts were incomplete, but Defendant will have necessarily generated additional discoverable documents in the year since they responded to Plaintiff’s first set of Requests for Production (Motion, Kaufman Decl., ¶ 12);

(2) New Plaintiff’s counsel attempted to resume discovery around May 19, 2026, and when rebuffed on the ground that discovery was closed, immediately initiated meet and confer efforts to reopen discovery, to which The Handsel Group via counsel refused to stipulate (Motion, Kaufman Decl., ¶¶ 13-15);

(3) Plaintiff’s newly served discovery was not duplicative of the first set of discovery (see Motion, Kaufman Decl., ¶¶ 16-18);

(4) Beyond written discovery, Plaintiff intends to take depositions, where prior counsel for Plaintiff only served production requests and failed to serve special interrogatories and to take depositions (Motion, Kaufman Decl., ¶¶ 4, 19); and

(5) This motion was filed immediately after meet and confer efforts failed (Motion, Kaufman Decl., ¶ 20).

The above facts, in juxtaposition to the controlling authority cited above, show a clear basis for relief. There is a necessity for discovery where only a single set of production requests have been served, with no interrogatories or deposition having followed. Plaintiff diligently sought relief via current counsel after negotiations between the parties broke down and Defendant made it clear that it would not stipulate to reopening discovery. Permitting discovery to go forward at this time is unlikely to affect the date of trial, which is set remotely away for November 2027. And given the more than a year between now and trial, and the context of failed negotiations taking nearly a year of the parties’ time, the time considerations here at issue do not disfavor relief.

In opposition, Defendant argues that (1) Plaintiff fails to clarify what discovery will in fact be sought if discovery is reopened, (2) Plaintiff failed to act diligently in propounding discovery before the cutoff, (3) Plaintiff has lacked diligence in seeking relief, (4) trial will likely be impacted by heavy discovery to follow the reopening of discovery, (5) Plaintiff will surely seek leave to amend the pleadings to name Doe Defendants, (6) a significant amount of time has elapsed between previously set trial dates and the current trial date, and (7) Plaintiff has acted in bad faith.

The Court is unconvinced by Defendant’s arguments. Plaintiff’s moving papers are sufficiently clear in specifying that no interrogatories and no depositions were previously served or taken, thus clearly identifying some of the discovery that will be at issue. The Court reads the same facts as Defendant to determine that Plaintiff acted with diligence when considering the parties’ failed settlement negotiations. The Court is also not convinced that the November 2027 trial date will be affected by reopening discovery, nor that too much time has elapsed between trial dates or that Plaintiff has acted in bad faith. As for Doe amendments, the place to challenge Doe amendments via LASC form SCLAC CIV 105 is not in papers related to this motion.

Under these circumstances, the Court finds valid grounds to reopen discovery.

c. Conclusion

Plaintiff’s motion is accordingly GRANTED.

Trial and trial-related deadlines SHALL run in accordance with the current November 2027 trial date.

Plaintiff Seva Ventures, LLC, is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV18438    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 731

INTEGRATIVE SURGICAL ASSOCIATES GROUP LLC, a California limited liability company; and FARNAD MEDICAL CORPORATION, a California corporation, Plaintiff(s), vs. BOBBY BAKHTIAR MOUSSAZADEH, an individual; B MOUSSAZADEH, A MEDICAL CORPORATION, a California corporation; and DOES 1 through 1,000, inclusive, Defendant(s).

CASE NO: 24STCV18438 [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: INTEGRATIVE SURGICAL ASSOCIATES GROUP LLC AND FARNAD MEDICAL CORPORATION’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH SUBPOENA AGAINST ABGIN LAW, AND REQUEST FOR MONETARY SANCTIONS AGAINST ABGIN LAW AND ITS COUNSEL Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiffs, Integrative Surgical Associates and Farnad Medical Corporation

Opposing Party: None

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

This action involves disputes arising from a business relationship between the parties whereby Defendants would use Plaintiffs’ facilities to provide medical treatment to patients, with the parties’ separately billing for their corresponding services. Plaintiffs allege that Defendants, who serviced clients on a lien basis, were falsifying records for services billed but never rendered.

Before the Court is Plaintiffs’ unopposed March 2, 2026, motion to compel compliance with a deposition subpoena for records served on non-party Abgin Law, one of the personal injury law firms

allegedly connected with Defendants’ alleged lien-based services. The motion was originally set for hearing on April 30, 2026, and was continued to June 11, 2026, on April 21, 2026, and again to July 21, 2026, on June 11, 2026.

2. Motion to Enforce Compliance with Deposition Subpoena; Request for Sanctions

After review, and though unopposed, the Court DENIES Plaintiffs’ motion as untimely.

At the onset, a general timeline is helpful to the Court’s discussion:

(1) Around November 1, 2024, Plaintiffs serve 49 subpoenas for records, including a subpoena for records served by Plaintiff on Abgin Law, which included requests for documents and communications relating to professional services provided by Defendants to clients of the 49 law firms on which subpoenas were served. The date for production for the Abgin Law subpoena was November 25, 2024. (Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶ 3, Ex. A.)

(2) Around November 19, 2024, Defendants filed a motion to quash the subpoenas served by Plaintiffs, including the Abgin Law subpoena. (Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶ 5, Ex. B.)

(3) On June 24, 2025, the Court granted the motion except as to 10 law firms, to be designated in a list to Defendants within one week from the Order, and Defendants to communicate with those law firms in the first instance within a week of receipt of Plaintiffs’ list. (See Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶ 7, Ex. E.)

(4) On July 1, 2025, Plaintiffs filed their list of ten firms, including Abgin Law. (Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶ 10, Ex. G.)

(5) In July 2025, counsel for Plaintiffs and Abgin Law communicated regarding the subpoena. (Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶¶ 12-13, Ex. I.)

(6) On July 28, 2025, Abgin Law served statements of compliance qualified by objections to the requests in the deposition subpoena for records directed to Abgin Law. (Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶ 14, Ex. J.)

(7) Between July 2025 and January 2026, counsel for the parties met and conferred regarding production, with Abgin Law promising to produce documents but continually and to date failing to do so. (Motion, Takaloo Decl., ¶¶ 15-16.g.)

(8) On March 2, 2026, Plaintiffs filed this motion. (See Motion, p. 1.)

Under this framing, we have a non-party witness (Abgin Law), consumer records (records of Abgin Law’s clients relating to Defendant’s alleged medical services and Abgin Law communications with Defendants), and non-production.

It follows that the controlling authority is as follows: When a nonparty witness fails to produce records in response to a deposition subpoena, the subpoenaing party's remedy is a motion to compel under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.480, subdivision (a). That motion must be filed no later than 60 days after the completion of the record of the deposition. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (a).) For a business records subpoena that has been validly served, the record of the deposition is complete — and the 60-day clock begins to run — once the entire inspection at issue is finished, including the raising of objections during that process. (Unzipped Apparel, LLC v. Bader (2007) 156 Cal.App.4th 123, 134 [Objections served in response to a subpoena for business records constitute a record of a deposition, and such a record is complete as of the date of production wherein objections were served, triggering the 60-day, jurisdictional timeframe to file a motion to compel].) The 60-day deadline is jurisdictional and cannot be extended by agreement or conduct of the parties. (See Board of Registered Nursing v. Superior Court (2021) 59 Cal.App.5th 1011, 1034 (Board of Registered Nursing).) A nonparty must comply (or not) with the subpoena on the date specified for production. (Ibid.)

Applying the above case law to the facts at hand, discussed above, because no production occurred as of the date of inspection—November 25, 2025 (Motion, Takaloo Decl., Ex. A, § 1)—a completion of the record occurred here 60 days after the date of inspection, i.e., Friday, January 24, 2025. This motion was filed nearly 14 months later in March of 2026, well beyond the jurisdictional limit of 60 days discussed in Board of Registered Nursing. Consequently, Plaintiff’s motion must fail.

While Plaintiffs’ motion argues that the motion is timely because “it is filed by the parties’ agreed deadline of March 2, 2026, following a 30-day extension of the previously agreed deadline of January 31, 2026, and because Third Party has still failed to comply with the Subpoena[] (Takaloo Decl., ¶ 16, Ex. K[]),” Board of Registered Nursing was clear that the 60-day deadline was not extended by meet and confer efforts. (Board of Registered Nursing, supra, 59 Cal.App.5th at p. 1034.) Similarly here, agreements between Plaintiffs and Abgin Law to ‘continue’ the applicable motion to compel deadline are ineffective where the trigger for Plaintiffs’ motion was the non-production as of the date for inspection in November 2024.

To the extent that Plaintiffs argue that the proceedings related to Defendants’ motions to quash 49 subpoenas should equitably toll or otherwise extend the 60-day deadline set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.480, the Court would not find that position availing. The Court cannot locate any authority, nor have Plaintiffs provided any authority to the effect that the filing of a motion to quash automatically stays, tolls, or otherwise affects the 60-day section 2025.480 deadline. (See Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (a), et seq.) This contrasts with other statutory schemes, which, for example, provide that a party may file motion to stay and quash a deposition notice, the filing of which automatically stays the taking of the deposition. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.410, subd (c).) The Court finds the difference significant as established authority holds that when the Legislature uses materially different language in statutory provisions addressing the same subject or related subjects, the normal inference is that the Legislature intended a difference in meaning. (People v. Trevino (2001) 26 Cal.4th 237, 242.) Here, the Court concludes that the absence of stay language in section 2025.480 signifies the Legislatures intent not to extend, stay, toll, or otherwise affect section 2025.480’s 60-day deadline, as based on the filing of a motion to quash relating to the deposition notice or subpoena at hand.

Plaintiffs motion is accordingly deemed untimely following January 24, 2025, and thus DENIED.

Plaintiffs Integrative Surgical Associates and Farnad Medical Corporation are ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 24STCV25389    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 731

CENTERSTONE SBA LENDING, INC., a California corporation, Plaintiff, v. CROWNCO INC., a California corporation; CHARLES E. MORRISON, an individual; DOES 1-100, inclusive, Defendants.

CASE NO: 24STCV25389 [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF’S MOTION FOR SUMMARY ADJUDICATION AS TO DEFENDANTS Dept. 731 9:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Centerstone SBA Lending, Inc.

Opposing Party: None

Notice: Defective

Plaintiff Centerstone SBA Lending, Inc. sues Defendants Crownco Inc. and Charles E. Morrison pursuant to a March 30, 2024, Complaint alleging four causes of action arising from allegations that Defendants breached a Small Business Administration (“SBA”) loan agreement entered by the parties by failing to repay, as of the time the Complaint was filed, $1,647,808.09 to Plaintiff, plus interest.

Defendant Morrison via counsel answered the Complaint on November 18, 2024, and filed a substitution of attorney on May 18, 2026. Crownco Inc. filed a notice of bankruptcy stay on November 18, 2024, on which the record fails to reflect an update beyond the Court’s minutes on May 27, 2025.

Before the Court is Plaintiff’s unopposed motion for summary adjudication of the first cause of action alleged in the Complaint, as against Defendant Charles E. Morrison. Although unopposed, the motion suffers from procedural defects requiring denial without prejudice.

The Court identifies two procedural defects requiring denial.

First, Plaintiff failed to file a valid proof of service establishing timely service of the motion. Without competent evidence demonstrating compliance with the notice requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 437c(a)(2), the Court cannot determine whether the statutory notice requirements have been satisfied.

Second, the motion does not comply with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350(b). Rule 3.1350(b) requires that the specific cause of action, affirmative defense, claim for damages, or issue of duty be stated in the notice of motion and repeated verbatim in the separate statement. Plaintiff’s separate statement does not repeat the issue identified in the notice of motion as required by the rule.

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s motion is DENIED, without prejudice.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV18027    Hearing Date: July 21, 2026    Dept: 731

JOSE GALAVIZ, Plaintiff, vs. FCA US LLC; LOS ANGELES CHRYSLER DODGE JEEP RAM; and DOES 1 through 10, inclusive, Defendants.

CASE NO: 25STCV18027 [TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT’S DEMURRER; AND DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO STRIKE Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Defendants, FCA US LLC and Los Angeles Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram

Opposing Party: Plaintiff, Jose Galaviz

Notice: OK

1. Background Facts

Plaintiff Jose Galaviz filed this action against Defendants FCA US LLC (“FCA”) and Los Angeles Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram (“LA Jeep”) (collectively, “Defendants”) for damages arising out of fraudulent concealment and negligent repair. Before the Court is Defendants’ opposed demurrer against the operative First Amended Complaint’s (“FAC”) fraudulent concealment and negligent repair claims, and Defendants’ opposed motion to strike the FAC’s prayer for punitive damages.

2. Demurrer

a. Legal Standard on Demurrer

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747; see Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).)

To sufficiently allege a cause of action, a complaint must allege all the ultimate facts—that is, the facts needed to establish each element of the cause of action pleaded. (Committee on Children’s Television, Inc. v. General Foods Corp. (1983) 35 Cal.3d 197, 212, superseded by statute as stated in Branick v. Downey Savings & Loan Assn. (2006) 39 Cal.4th 235, 242.) “[E]ach evidentiary fact that might eventually form part of the plaintiff’s proof need not be alleged.” (C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School Dist. (2012) 53 Cal.4th 861, 872.)

In testing the sufficiency of the cause of action, the demurrer admits the truth of all material facts properly pleaded. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-67.) Courts read the allegations liberally and in context. (Taylor v. City of Los Angeles Dept. of Water and Power (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 1216, 1228, disapproved on other grounds, Jones v. Lodge at Torrey Pines Partnership (2008) 42 Cal.4th 1158, 1162.) A demurrer, however, “does not admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1967) 67 Cal.2d 695, 713.)

A demurrer may only be used to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

b. Meet and Confer

Here, Defendants make a sufficient showing of meet and confer required for demurrer. (Demurrer, Lopez Decl., ¶¶ 2-4, Ex. A; see Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(3)(A).)

c. Discussion

i. First Cause of Action, Negligent Repair against L.A. Jeep: SUSTAINED, with leave to amend.

The Court finds in favor of Defendants as to the Complaint’s first cause of action of negligent repair based on the economic loss rule barring relief.

Under the economic loss rule, a plaintiff may not recover in tort for negligently inflicted purely economic losses, unless the defendant's injury-causing conduct violated a duty independent of the

contract and the defendant's conduct caused physical damage or personal injury. (Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 20, 26, 44 (Rattagan); see Robinson Helicopter Co., Inc. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979, 988 (Robinson Helicopter).) In other words, there is no tort duty to guard against purely economic losses. (Southern California Gas Leak Cases (2019) 7 Cal.5th 391, 398.)

Economic loss consists of damages for inadequate value, costs of repair and replacement of the defective product or consequent loss of profits—without any claim of personal injury or damages to other property. (Robinson Helicopter, supra, at p. 988; see Jimenez v. Superior Court (2002) 29 Cal.4th 473, 483; Northern American Chemical Co. v. Superior Court (1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 764, 777, fn. 8.)

Here, the FAC’s first cause of action does not allege any injury to any person arising from the negligence-based cause of action, instead alleging negligent repair based on L.A. Jeep’s service work on the subject vehicle in this litigation. (Complaint, ¶¶ 44-48.)

In opposition to demurrer, Plaintiff argues that the economic loss rule does not apply here because it is not clear on the face of the pleadings that only economic losses are at issue—noting that L.A. Jeep “may have” (emphasis added) caused damage to other portions of the Subject Vehicle”—and arguing that negligent repair may proceed where damage to one vehicle component caused damage to other components, comparing to one California case—Jimenez v. Superior Court (2002) 29 Cal.4th 473 (“Jimenez”)—and otherwise citing federal law. (Opposition to Demurrer, pp. 10-11.) Neither argument is persuasive based on the documents before the Court and in the record.

The face of the pleadings cuts against Plaintiff. The paragraphs preceding and incorporated into the first cause of action fail to specifically allege facts against L.A. Jeep, and the first cause of action itself merely alleges that L.A. Jeep “breached its duty to Plaintiffs to use ordinary care and skill by failing to properly store, prepare and repair the Subject Vehicle in accordance with industry standards,” explicitly noting only a single (“at least one”) repair visit to L.A. Jeep. (FAC, ¶¶ 1-43, 44-48.)

As for Plaintiff’s argument regarding damage to other components, no clear damage-to-other-components allegations appear on the face of the FAC, nor does the Opposition point to any specific

paragraph of the FAC making such an allegation, for which reason the Court disregards Plaintiff’s argument as to the ‘components exception’ under Jimenez. (See Opposition p. 11; cf. Motion, pp. 6-7.)

Defendants’ demurrer is accordingly SUSTAINED as relates to the first cause of action.

The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend based on the parties’ papers showing a dispute as to whether the pleadings can be amended to properly state an exception to the economic loss rule. (Brenner v. City of El Cajon (2003) 113 Cal.App.4th 434, 444 [burden on pleading party to show how complaint can be amended and legal effect on pleadings].)

ii. Second Cause of Action, Concealment against FCA: SUSTAINED, with leave to amend.

Plaintiff’s second cause of action similarly fails on demurrer based on the economic loss rule.

In Dhital v. Nissan N. Am., Inc., the Court of Appeal initially addressed the economic loss rule in the specific context of fraudulent inducement by concealment. (Dhital v. Nissan N. Am., Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 840.) The court held concealment-based claims for fraudulent inducement are not barred by the economic loss rule “because a defendant’s conduct in fraudulently inducing someone to enter a contract is separate from the defendant's later breach of the contract or warranty provisions that were agreed to.” (Id. at 841.)

But more recently, the California Supreme Court offered further guidance on the economic loss rule. In Rattagan v. Uber Techs., Inc., the issue was whether a Plaintiff could “assert an independent claim of fraudulent concealment in the performance of a contract?” (Rattagan, supra, 17 Cal.5th at p. 38.) The Court held that a plaintiff may only assert a separate claim of fraudulent concealment based on conduct occurring in the course of a contractual relationship “if the elements of the cause of action can be established independently of the parties’ contractual rights and obligations and the tortious conduct exposes the plaintiff to a risk of harm beyond the reasonable contemplation of the parties when they entered into the contract.” (Id. at p. 45, emphasis added.)

Of particular importance to this Court, the California Supreme Court initially agreed to hear Dhital on appeal but stayed the case pending considering of a “related issue” in Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc. Then, on December 18, 2024, approximately three months after issuing its decision in Rattagan, the Court dismissed the appeal in Dhital without hearing the case. This might reasonably suggest that the Court viewed Rattagan as having resolved the issue raised by Dhital.

Moving back to the allegations here, the FAC’s second cause of action alleges fraudulent concealment of a defective engine in the subject vehicle. (See FAC, ¶¶ 48-56 [engine at ¶ 50].) Such allegations on their face fall squarely into Rattagan, foreclosing relief as a matter of law because, on the face of the pleadings, to the extent any tort occurred here, it occurred during the “course of the contract” for the subject vehicle. (See, e.g., FAC, ¶¶ 7-25.)

Plaintiff’s opposition argues that the manufacturer’s alleged failure to disclose the defects falls under Dhital and that Rattagan actually supports Plaintiff’s position. The Court is not persuaded by Plaintiff’s arguments. The alleged “failure to disclose” defects occurred exactly at the time of the sale. Under Plaintiffs’ theory, Defendant had an affirmative duty to inform Plaintiffs at the time of the sale (or immediately prior thereto). However, as discussed above, any tort here occurred during the course of contract, and thus, the Supreme Court’s opinion in Rattagan controls for purposes of this analysis, the economic loss rule applies to the second cause of action, and the concealment claim thus fails.

Defendants’ demurrer is accordingly SUSTAINED as relates to the first cause of action.

The demurrer is sustained with leave to amend based on the parties’ papers showing a dispute as to whether the pleadings can be amended to properly state an exception to the economic loss rule. (Brenner v. City of El Cajon (2003) 113 Cal.App.4th 434, 444 [burden on pleading party to show how complaint can be amended and legal effect on pleadings].)

d. Conclusion

Defendants’ demurrer is SUSTAINED with leave to amend as to both the first and second causes of action alleged in the First Amended Complaint.

3. Motion to Strike

The court may, upon a motion or at any time in its discretion and upon terms it deems proper: (a) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (b) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Code Civ. Proc. § 436, subds. (a), (b); Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782 [“Matter in a pleading which is not essential to the claim is surplusage; probative facts are surplusage and may be stricken out or disregarded”].)

Here, as the Court has sustained Defendants’ demurrer to the first and second causes of action, which are the only causes of action alleged in this lawsuit, Plaintiff’s prayer for punitive damages is unsupported surplusage, for which reason Defendants’ motion to strike is GRANTED.

Defendants FCA US LLC and Los Angeles Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram are ordered to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: PLAINTIFF MICHAEL BRIAN SMITH’S MOTION TO SET ASIDE/VACATE JUDGMENT [CRS #8855] Dept. 731 8:30 a.m. July 21, 2026

Moving Party: Plaintiff, Michael Brian Smith

Opposing Party: Defendant, Michael Kors Inc.

Notice: OK

Plaintiff Michael B. Smith brought this suit against his former employer, Defendant Michael Kors Inc., alleging race discrimination in his employment and a subsequent unlawful layoff.

On March 26, 2026, the Court granted a motion for judgment on the pleadings filed by Defendant, which disposed of the then-operative March 19, 2024, Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”). Plaintiff filed a motion for reconsideration on March 30, 2026, which was opposed by Defendant, and was ultimately denied by the Court on May 20, 2026. The Court entered judgment in Defendant’s favor that same day.

Now before the Court is Plaintiff’s motion to set aside/vacate the May 20, 2026, judgment on the grounds that another action affecting the outcome of the proceedings before this Court is purportedly presently pending before the Compton, California branch of the Los Angeles Superior Court as 22CMCV00753, Smith v. Michael Kors Inc. (the “Santa Monica action”). (See Motion, p. 2, ¶ 4.)

The motion is DENIED, with prejudice.

Plaintiff’s motion is noticed as being premised on Code of Civil Procedure sections 473, subdivision (d), and 1008, subdivisions (a), (b), and (e). (Motion, pp. 1-2.) Both premises fail.

As argued in Defendant’s opposition, there is no applicable provision of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (d), here. (See Opposition pp. 11-12.) Plaintiff is asking that the Court vacate a final judgment based on the purported pendency of the Santa Monica action. This is not a valid ground for relief under section 473, subdivision (d), which is limited to clerical errors and void judgments and orders, including those voided by extrinsic fraud or mistake, neither of which Plaintiff’s paper show. (See Kramer v. Traditional Escrow, Inc. (2020) 56 Cal.App.5th 13, 29 [discussing § 473, subd. (d)].)

Similarly, none of the grounds for party-requested reconsideration—new or different facts, circumstances, or law (Code Civ. Proc., § 1008, subd. (a))—appear here, as the requested relief is based on the purported pendency of the Santa Monica action rather than some fact, circumstance, or new law relating to this action.

Last, the Court takes judicial notice of Exhibit A attached to Defendant’s opposition, which shows a June 18, 2026, minute order for the Santa Monica action, in which the court in that case denied a similar motion by Plaintiff in this case to vacate or set aside the judgment in that case, which was entered on November 6, 2025. (Opposition, Request for Judicial Notice, Ex. A, pp. 6-7.)

Plaintiff’s motion is accordingly DENIED. No further motions seeking identical relief shall be entertained absent a proper statutory basis.
