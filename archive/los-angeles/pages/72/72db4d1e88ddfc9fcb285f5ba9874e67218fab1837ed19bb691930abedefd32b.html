Tentative Ruling:

No. 8 – Felicity Trevino v. 600 S Spring Owner LLC, et al.

Defendant SBDTLA 1, LLC’s Motions to Compel Plaintiff’s Responses to Form Interrogatories and Requests for Production

Defendant SBDTLA 1, LLC moves for orders compelling Plaintiff Felicity Trevino to respond to form interrogatories and requests for production. Plaintiff did not file an opposition.

A party must respond to written discovery “[w]ithin 30 days after service.” (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.260(a) [interrogatories], 2031.260(a) [requests for production].) When the responding party fails to serve a timely response to interrogatories or requests for production, the requesting party may move for an order compelling responses. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(b) [interrogatories], 2031.300(b) [requests for production].) Failing to serve a timely response results in waiving any objections. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.290(a) [interrogatories], 2031.300(a) [requests for production].)

Plaintiff failed to serve timely responses to Defendant’s discovery requests. Defendant served form interrogatories and requests for production on Plaintiff on October 17, 2025. (Liston Decls., ¶ 2.) As of the date these motions were filed, Plaintiff had still not responded. (Id., ¶ 6.) Defendant is therefore entitled to an order compelling Plaintiff to respond to written discovery.

Sanctions

Defendant also requests monetary sanctions against Plaintiff and her counsel. Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.300(c) provides, “[T]he court shall impose a monetary sanction … against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (See also Code Civ. Proc., § 2030.290(c) [interrogatories].) Plaintiff failed to provide any responses by the deadline. There is no evidence that Plaintiff acted with substantial justification.

The Court finds sanctions are appropriate. Defendant did not, however, reasonably incur $4,340 in expenses in preparing these two motions. (Liston Decls., ¶ 7.) These motions are almost identical to each other. The Court exercises its discretion to award counsel fees incurred for drafting one motion (7 hours at a $310 rate.) The Court will also reduce the hours incurred by counsel by two hours, as it accounts for review of an opposition brief and preparation of a reply brief. Because no opposition brief was filed, no reply brief was necessary. 5 hours at a $310 rate is $1,550. Thus, Defendant reasonably incurred a total of $1,550 in attorney fees.

Disposition

Defendant SBDTLA 1, LLC’s motions to compel responses to form interrogatories and request for production are granted. Plaintiff Felicity Trevino is ordered to serve verified responses without objections within 21 days.

Plaintiff Felicity Trevino and her counsel of record are ordered to pay Defendant SBDTLA 1, LLC $1,550 in sanctions within 21 days.


Case Number: 25STCV35176    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 9 - Leo Rank v. HR Renovations Inc. dba Hybrid Renovations, et al.

    Cross-Defendant Leo Rank's Demurrer to Cross-Complainant's First Amended Cross Complaint

Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Leo Rank (“Cross-Defendant”) demurs to Defendant/Cross-Complainant HR Renovations Inc.’s (“Cross-Complainant”) first cause of action for breach of contract and second cause of action for fraud as alleged in the first amended cross-complaint (“FAXC”).

Uncertainty

Cross-Defendant contends that the FAXC is uncertain because it fails to attach the operative agreement underlying the breach of contract claim. “A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly’s of Cal., Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.) “ ‘ “Demurrers for uncertainty are disfavored, and are granted only if the pleading is so incomprehensible that a defendant cannot reasonably respond.” ’ ” (A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc. (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695, alterations omitted.) The complaint must sufficiently apprise defendants of the claims against them. (Ibid.) Here, while the Court acknowledges that Cross-Complainant failed to attach the contract, this omission does not prevent Cross-Defendant from answering the claims against him.

Sufficiency of Allegations

A.   Breach of Contract

Cross-Complainant fails to allege sufficient facts for breach of contract. The elements of this cause of action are: “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) the resulting damages to the plaintiff.” (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.)  “A written contract may be pleaded either by its terms—set out verbatim in the complaint or a copy of the contract attached to the complaint and incorporated therein by reference—or by its legal effect. [Citation.] In order to plead a contract by its legal effect, plaintiff must ‘allege the substance of its relevant terms. This is more difficult, for it requires a careful analysis of the instrument, comprehensiveness in statement, and avoidance of legal conclusions.’ ” (McKell v. Washington Mutual, Inc. (2006) 142 Cal.App.4th 1457, 1489.)

Here, Cross-Complainant does not adequately allege the existence of a contract. He alleges the independent contractor agreement is attached to the FAXC, but it is not. (See FAXC, ¶ 11.) Cross-Complainant concedes this fact in opposition. (See Opp., at p. 4.) The Court will grant Cross-Complainant leave to amend to properly attach the contract to the pleading.

B.    Fraud

“The elements of fraud are (a) a misrepresentation (false representation, concealment, or nondisclosure); (b) scienter or knowledge of its falsity; (c) intent to induce reliance; (d) justifiable reliance; and (e) resulting damage.” (Hinesley v. Oakshade Town Ctr. (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 289, 294.) The facts constituting the alleged fraud must be alleged factually and specifically as to every element of fraud, as the policy of “liberal construction” of the pleadings will not ordinarily be invoked. (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 645.)

The Cross-Complainant sufficiently alleges all elements of fraud. The FAXC states that Rank made a misrepresentation on or about September 2, 2025 when he represented he was “uniquely qualified and available to generate business leads for Cross-Complainant for a minimum period of one year.” (FAXC, Second Cause of Action, ¶ 2.) It alleges that Rank knew the representations to be false, because he intended to obtain the $20,000 advance and then quit. (Id., ¶ 3.) Further, the FAXC alleges that he made these misrepresentations to induce Cross-Complainant’s reliance, that Cross-Complainant relied on the representations, and the resulting damages. (Id., ¶¶ 5-7.) This is sufficient at the pleading stage to state a claim for fraud.

Cross-Complainant's Request for Punitive Damages for Breach of Contract

    Cross-Complainant requests punitive damages under its breach of contract cause of action. (Id., ¶ 19.) No such relief can be afforded for breach of contract. (Civ. Code § 3294.) Cross-Defendant did not raise this issue in its demurrer or by motion to strike. If Cross-Complainant includes a request for punitive damages as part of its amended cross-complaint, and if Cross-Defendant does not raise the issue on its own, the Court notifies the parties that it may, pursuant to its authority under Code of Civil Procedure section 436, strike the request for punitive damages from the amended cross-complaint's cause of action for breach of contract.   

Disposition

Plaintiff/Cross-Defendant Leo Rank’s demurrer to Defendant/Cross-Complainant HR Renovations Inc.’s first cause of action for breach of contract in the FAXC is sustained with 21 days’ leave to amend. It is overruled as to the second cause of action for fraud.
