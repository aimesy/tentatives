Demurrer to Second Amended Complaint

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant American Honda Motor Company, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  Plaintiffs Claudia Calderon and Fausto Mendoza.

Demurrer to Second Amended Complaint

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this demurrer.

DISCUSSION

Defendant American Honda Motor Company, Inc. (“Defendant”) demurs to the Second Amended Complaint filed by plaintiffs Claudia Calderon and Fausto Mendoza (“Plaintiffs”) with respect to the first three causes of action for violation of Civil Code section 1793.2 subdivisions (d), (b), and (a)(3).

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the first cause of action for violation of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (d) because Defendant has not shown that it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).)  Plaintiffs have alleged that Defendant, as distributor, issued a warranty on the used vehicle that is the subject of this action at the point of sale, which subjects Defendant to the same obligations as a manufacturer issuing a warranty with a new motor vehicle pursuant to Civil Code section 1795.5.

Under section 1793.2, subdivision (d)(2), the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act provides that a manufacturer shall promptly replace a new motor vehicle, or make restitution to the buyer, if the manufacturer is unable to service or repair a new motor vehicle to conform to the applicable express warranties.  (Civ. Code, § 1793.2, subd. (d)(2).)  As noted by Defendant, the California Supreme Court recently held that “a motor vehicle purchased with an unexpired manufacturer’s new car warranty does not qualify as a ‘motor vehicle sold with a manufacturer’s new car warranty’ under section 1793.22, subdivision (e)(2)’s definition of ‘new motor vehicle’ unless the new car warranty was issued with the sale.”  (Rodriguez v. FCA US LLC (2024) 17 Cal.5th 189, 196 [emphasis added].) However, a distributor of used consumer goods “in a sale in which an express warranty is given” has the same obligations under the Song-Beverly Act as a manufacturer of new goods. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1795.5.)

          Here, Plaintiffs have alleged that Defendant Honda is a distributor of motor vehicles who issued a new Certified Pre-Owned warranty with Plaintiffs’ purchase of the vehicle. (SAC ¶ 9-12.) Plaintiffs have thus pled that Defendant, as a distributor, issued an express warranty in a sale of a used consumer good. Civil Code section 1795.5 therefore applies to impose the same express warranty obligations as a manufacturer of new consumer goods, i.e., the obligations imposed by section 1793.2.

          The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the second cause of action for violation of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (b) because Defendant has not shown that it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have alleged, for the reasons stated above, facts establishing that the vehicle is subject to the express warranty provisions of the Song-Beverly Act pursuant to Civil Code section 1795.5. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) 

The court overrules Defendant’s demurrer to the third cause of action for violation of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (a)(3) because Defendant has not shown that it does not state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action since Plaintiffs have alleged, for the reasons stated above, facts establishing that the vehicle is subject to the express warranty provisions of the Song-Beverly Act pursuant to Civil Code section 1795.5. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) 

ORDER

The court OVERRULES defendant American Honda Motor Company, Inc.’s demurrer to plaintiffs Claudia Calderon and Fausto Mendoza’s Second Amended Complaint.

The court orders plaintiffs Claudia Calderon and Fausto Mendoza to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Motion for Sanctions

 

 

MOVING PARTY:            Defendant Uber Technologies, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY:  Plaintiff Danielle Penberthy

Motion for Sanctions

The court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers filed in connection with this motion.

DISCUSSION

Defendant Uber Technologies, Inc. (“Defendant”) moves the court for an order (1) awarding monetary sanctions for Defendant and against plaintiff Danielle Penberthy (“Plaintiff”) and (2) dismissing the action with prejudice pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 128.5 and 128.7.

The court finds that Defendant has not complied with the safe harbor provisions of Code of Civil Procedure sections 128.5 and 128.7.

Notice of a motion for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7

“shall be served as provided in section 1010 [of the Code of Civil Procedure] but shall not be filed with or presented to the court unless, within 21 days after service of the motion . . . the challenged paper, claim, defense, contention, allegation, or denial is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.7, subd. (c)(1) [emphasis added].) A motion for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.5 pertaining to the filing of a pleading is subject to an identical safe-harbor requirement. (Code Civ. Proc., § 128.5, subd. (f)(1)(B).)

          Section 1010 of the Code of Civil Procedure provides that notices may be served on parties “in the manner prescribed in this chapter,” that is, Chapter 5 of Title 14 of Part 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure. Section 1010.6, which is within Chapter 5, provides for electronic service of documents, and states that, for any electronically served document, “[a]ny period of notice, or any right or duty to do any act or make any response within any period or on a date certain after the service of the document, which time period or date is prescribed by statute or rule of court, shall be extended after service by electronic means by two court days” subject to certain enumerated exceptions not applicable here. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1010.6, subd. (a)(3)(B) [emphasis added].) By the plain language of this provision, the right or duty of a party to withdraw or correct a pleading challenged under sections 128.5 or 128.7 is extended by two court days for electronic service.

Defendant presents evidence that it presented Plaintiff with its Motion for Sanctions by email—that is, electronic service—on April 21, 2026. (Lozaya Decl. Ex. B [email service of motion].) Applying the extension for electronic service, Plaintiff’s last day to correct or withdraw the Complaint was 21 days plus two court days from April 21, 2026. The last day of Plaintiff’s safe harbor period was therefore not, as Defendant claims, May 12, 2026 (21 days from April 21), but May 14, 2026 (two court days later). Defendant’s motion was filed May 13, 2026, which is before the safe harbor period ended.

          Thus, because Defendant has not complied with the safe harbor provision, the court denies Defendant’s motion. (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 128.5, subd. (f)(1)(B); 128.7, subd. (c)(1), Transcon Financial, Inc. v. Reid & Hellyer, APC (2022) 81 Cal.App.5th 547, 551 [“the law requires strict compliance with the safe harbor provisions.  [Citation.]  Failure to comply with the safe harbor provision ‘precludes an award of sanctions’”] [internal citations omitted].)

 

ORDER

The court DENIES defendant Uber Technologies, Inc.’s motion for sanctions.

The court orders plaintiff Danielle Penberthy to give notice of this ruling.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
