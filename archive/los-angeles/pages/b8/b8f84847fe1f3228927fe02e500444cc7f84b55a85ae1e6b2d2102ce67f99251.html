Case Number: 23SMCV02370    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Cross-Defendant David Laporte’s Demurrer to Cross-Complainants, Reynaldo Adalin and Donna M. Adalin’s Cross-Complaint for Equitable Indemnity, Equitable Contribution, and Declaratory Relief is OVERRULED.

Cross-Defendant David Laporte shall file and serve an answer to Defendants/Cross-Complainants Reynaldo Adalin and Donna M. Adalin’s Cross-Complaint within ten (10) days of entry of this order. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1320(j).)

Cross-Defendant David Laporte to give notice. 

First Cause of Action: Equitable Indemnity and Second Cause of Action: Equitable Contribution
“Equitable indemnity, which requires no contractual relationship, is premised on a joint legal obligation to another for damages; it is subject to allocation of fault principles and comparative equitable apportionment of loss.” (C.W. Howe Partners Inc. v. Mooradian (2019) 43 Cal.App.5th 688, 700, quotation marks omitted.) “The elements of a cause of action for equitable indemnity are (1) a showing of fault on the part of the indemnitor and (2) resulting damages to the indemnitee for which the indemnitor is equitably responsible.” (Ibid., quotation marks, brackets, and ellipses omitted.) Equitable contribution “is the right to recover, not from the party primarily liable for the loss, but from a co-obligor who shares such liability with the party seeking contribution.” (Hartford Casualty Insurance Co. v. Mt. Hawley Insurance Co. (2004) 123 Cal.App.4th 278, 287, emphasis omitted.)

In the cross-complaint, Cross-Complainants allege that Plaintiff Susana Maribel Samoya filed a complaint against them, and Cross-Complainants retained an arborist who recently inspected the area where the incident occurred had observed surface roots from a neighboring tree near the sidewalk uplift, and the tree is believed to be on Cross-Defendant’s property, such that if Cross-Complainants are found negligent, then they are entitled to indemnification and contribution from Cross-Defendant. (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 7-11, 15.) Cross-Complainants have sufficiently alleged these claims based on a theory that if they are liable, Cross-Defendant is fully or partially responsible for such damages, and Cross-Complainants need not plead anything additional at this juncture. Moreover, Cross-Complainants may advance a position that may differ from a position taken in regard to a different pleading, i.e., an alternative theory of liability. Accordingly, Cross-Defendant’s demurrer to the first and second causes of action is OVERRULED.

Third Cause of Action: Declaratory Relief
“To qualify for declaratory relief, a party would have to demonstrate its action presented two essential elements: (1) a proper subject of declaratory relief, and (2) an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to the party’s rights or obligations.” (Jolley v. Chase Home Finance, LLC (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 872, 909, quotation marks and brackets omitted.)

Cross-Complainants allege that there is a controversy between them and Cross-Defendant as to who is liable for what damages. (Cross-Compl. ¶¶ 20-21.) The Court finds this to be a sufficient theory of a basis for declaratory relief. Thus, Cross-Defendant’s demurrer to the third cause of action is OVERRULED.

Case Number: 24SMCV05044    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Joseph Ariel Hazani’s Motion to Amend Judgment or Motion for Leave to File Fourth Amended Complaint is DENIED.

Clerk to give notice. 

REASONING

Plaintiff Joseph Ariel Hazani (“Plaintiff”) moves the Court for leave to file a Fourth Amended Complaint in this action. Plaintiff’s motion is entitled as both a Motion to Amend Judgment and a Motion for Leave to File Fourth Amended Complaint. Plaintiff argues that the proposed amended pleading “is a clinical restructuring that strips away all narrative grievances and focuses exclusively on a triable commercial property dispute” (Mot., p. 3), he contends that he had a learning curve in representing himself that can now be resolved through use of artificial intelligence, and the dismissal of the action was merely because of formatting errors with his earlier pleading.

In short, Plaintiff is asking for 4th opportunity to properly plead his action after it was dismissed. It follows that this is more properly characterized as a motion for reconsideration. Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (a), allows a Court to reconsider a prior order as follows:

When an application for an order has been made to a judge, or to a court, and refused in whole or in part, or granted, or granted conditionally, or on terms, any party affected by the order may, within 10 days after service upon the party of written notice of entry of the order and based upon new or different facts, circumstances, or law, make application to the same judge or court that made the order, to reconsider the matter and modify, amend, or revoke the prior order. The party making the application shall state by affidavit what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order or decisions were made, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown.

Plaintiff provides no new facts, circumstances, or law with this motion, and it is well established that “[the burden under [Code of Civil Procedure] section 1008 is comparable to that of a party seeking a new trial on the ground of newly discovered evidence: the information must be such that the moving party could not, with reasonable diligence, have discovered or produced it at the trial.” (New York Times Co. v. Superior Court (2005) 135 Cal.App.4th 206, 212-213.) Second, it is well established that “[w]hen a litigant is appearing in propria persona, he is entitled to the same, but no greater, consideration than other litigants and attorneys.” (Nelson v. Gaunt (1981) 125 Cal.App.3d 623, 638.) In other words, a misunderstanding of legal requirements is not itself a basis to allow a party to amend a pleading. Third, Plaintiff represents to the Court that he intends to litigate this action through use of artificial intelligence tools, i.e., Plaintiff will not do the work himself and instead will rely on tools that are prone to hallucination and, quite simply, not equipped to litigate a case. Fourth, and perhaps most importantly, the proposed pleading is, again, incomprehensible, perhaps because of the reliance on artificial intelligence to “write” a pleading, i.e., Plaintiff mentions a commercial and legal agency and pause of this business due to certain conduct, but he does not describe the agency, making any damages unclear. For these reasons, Plaintiff Joseph Ariel Hazani’s Motion to Amend Judgment or Motion for Leave to File Fourth Amended Complaint is DENIED.

Case Number: 25SMCP00456    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Petitioner Stephen Copen's Petition for Exemption and Judicial Review of Inspection by the City of Los Angeles is DENIED.  

LAMC § 161.301 sets forth the kinds of real property subject to inspection under SCEP as
well as certain exemptions. (Defendant RJN, Ex. 1) Property with “two or more
dwelling units, efficiency dwelling units, light housekeeping rooms, guest rooms, and suites” is
subject to SCEP inspection unless an exception applies. The Petitioner's Property has 22 guest rooms and is
classified as a dormitory according to the Certificate of Occupancy. (Kasumyan Decl., ¶ 3; Ex. A)
The Property is rented out for student housing. The approved use is residential. (Kasumyan Decl.,
¶ 3, Defendant Ex. A) Accordingly, the Property is subject to SCEP unless an exemption applies.  

SCEP has seven (7) exemptions, as listed below:
(1) Owner occupied units.
(2) Housing accommodations in hotels, motels, inns, or tourist homes. This exception
does not apply to residential hotels as defined in California Health and Safety Code
Section 50519(b)(1).
(3) A dwelling unit in a condominium or a nonprofit stock cooperative.
(4) Housing accommodations in any hospital; state licensed community care facility;
convent, monastery, or other facility occupied exclusively by members of a religious
order; extended medical care facility; asylum; on-campus fraternity or sorority houses;
or on-campus housing accommodations owned, operated or managed by an institution
of higher education, a high school, or an elementary school for occupancy by its
students.
(5) Housing accommodations which a government unit, agency or authority owns,
operates, or manages, or which are specifically exempted from municipal regulation
by state or federal law or administrative regulation. This exception shall not apply
once the governmental ownership, operation, or management regulation is
discontinued.
(6) Mobile homes, or mobile home parks, or recreational vehicles as defined in
California Civil Code Section 799.29, or recreational vehicle parks.
(7) Vacant properties. (Defendant RJN, Ex. 1)

Based on the foregoing, the Petition is DENIED.

Case Number: 25SMCV06193    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants Royal West, LLC; Joel K. Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020; and Mitzi March Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020’s Motion to Consolidate Case No. 25SMCV06193 and 25SMUD01364 for All Purposes is GRANTED. Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC) is hereby consolidated with Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMUD01364 (Royal West, LLC v. Jaradat), with Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC) serving as the lead case. All future filings shall be made in Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC).

Defendants Royal West, LLC; Joel K. Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020; and Mitzi March Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020 to give notice. 

REASONING

The trial court has discretion to consolidate actions involving common questions of law or fact. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1048, subd. (a).) The purpose of consolidation is “to promote trial convenience and economy by avoiding duplication of procedure, particularly in the proof of issues common to both actions.” (Estate of Baker (1982) 131 Cal.App.3d 471, 485.) 

Defendants Royal West, LLC; Joel K. Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020; and Mitzi March Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020 (“Defendants”) move to consolidate this case with Case No. 25SMUD01364 (Royal West, LLC v. Jaradat) on the grounds that the two cases involve the same parties, common questions of law and fact, and arise out of the same commercial lease, such that both disputes require interpreting clauses of the same lease agreement and turn on the rights and obligations of the parties during the tenancy. Plaintiffs Moen Jaradat and Fusion Looks LLC (“Plaintiffs”) oppose the motion on the ground that the cases are no longer related because Defendant Royal West, LLC now seeks equitable remedies in the other action that are unrelated to this case that arises out of an incident of lockout that happened in the past.

The Court has reviewed the operative complaints filed in both actions and finds that these actions should be consolidated for all purposes. The First Amended Complaint in Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC) asserts claims for breach of contract, wrongful eviction, trespass, conversion, restitution, unfair business practices, negligence, forcible entry, forcible detainer, and nuisance against Defendants and Defendant Amanda Hughes, with the breach of contract alleged only against Defendants, based on a theory that Defendants breached the lease by failing to give notice before instituting an unlawful detainer action against them and unlawfully locking them out of the premises. The First Amended Complaint in Case No. 25SMUD01364 (Royal West, LLC v. Jaradat) involves only Royal West, LLC, a defendant in the present action, and Moen Jaradat, a plaintiff in the present action, based on a theory that the premises had been abandoned, causing theories of lockout, such that Royal West, LLC brings claims for breach of contract, quiet title, ejectment, and declaratory relief based on a theory that Royal West, LLC is entitled to possession of the premises. While the present action involves four additional parties than Case No. 25SMUD01364, both parties concern competing positions as to possession under the lease agreement, such that the trier of fact could be expected to understand both cases without confusion, and litigating these actions separately is likely to involve a duplication of efforts.

Accordingly, Defendants Royal West, LLC; Joel K. Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020; and Mitzi March Mogul, individually and as Trustee of the Joel K. Mogul Living Trust dated October 29, 2020’s Motion to Consolidate Case No. 25SMCV06193 and 25SMUD01364 for All Purposes is GRANTED. Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC) is hereby consolidated with Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMUD01364 (Royal West, LLC v. Jaradat), with Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC) serving as the lead case. All future filings shall be made in Los Angeles Superior Court Case No. 25SMCV06193 (Jaradat v. Royal West, LLC).
