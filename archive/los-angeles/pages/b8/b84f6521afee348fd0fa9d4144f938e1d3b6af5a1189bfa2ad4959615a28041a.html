            This is a Lemon Law action based on alleged defects in Plaintiffs’ vehicle. 

            Defendant FCA US, LLC moves for sanctions against Plaintiffs pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(j). 

TENTATIVE RULING

            Defendant FCA US, LLC’s request for sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel is GRANTED in the mandatory amount of $1,500. (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(j)(1).) Sanctions are to be paid to Defendant’s counsel within 15 business days. (Id.)

ANALYSIS

Motion For Sanctions

Discussion

            The Court has reviewed the moving, opposing and reply briefs filed by the parties, but only addresses the points which the Court deems to be material to the disposition of this motion.

            Defendant FCA US, LLC moves to for sanctions against Plaintiff pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26 (j) due to Plaintiff’s failure to timely serve the mandatory initial disclosures within 60 days after the filing of Defendant’s responsive pleading, as required by section 871.26, subdivision (b) and failed to provide complete information or the required documents.

(b) Within 60 days after the filing of the answer or other responsive pleading, all parties shall, without awaiting a discovery request, provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).

(Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(b).)  The Court notes that the instant motion shows that rather than reducing the proliferation of discovery motions in Song-Beverly litigation, the 2024 amendments to the Song-Beverly Act simply created new fields to fight old battles.  The legislative history for the bill amending the Song-Beverly Act ambitiously stated:

The expedited discovery provisions of this measure will reduce protracted discovery disputes. Discovery disputes, most notably arguments over what must be turned over to opposing parties in litigation, [are] one of the largest drivers in delays in almost every type of civil litigation. Lemon law litigation is no different. Presently, parties squabble over what maintenance records are to be disclosed, what warranty documents must be disclosed, and how depositions can be conducted. Every time one of these disputes arises, the court is forced to calendar a motion hearing and delay a potential trial. Given that lemon law cases are typically very document-heavy matters and involve a limited universe of documents, many of these discovery disputes represent little more than unnecessary litigation tactics that only serve to delay justice.

(Assem. Com. on Judiciary, analysis on A.B. 1755 (2023-2024 Reg. Sess.) as amended August 26, 2024, p. 14.)  Yet here we sit, with this being the fourth motion pursuant to section 871.26 calendared in the past five weeks.

            On August 6, 2025, Defendant filed a demurrer and motion to strike to the Complaint. As such, Plaintiff had until Monday October 5, 2025 to serve initial disclosures and documents. On August 15, 2025, Plaintiff served initial disclosures, with no document production. (Declaration of Armando Lopez, ¶ 4; Exh. A.)

            Although Plaintiff’s initial disclosure was timely, the failure to provide documents did not comply with Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(b), (f). In the Reply, Defendant indicates that Plaintiff still has not produced all of the required documents or provided all information mandated by § 871.26(f) & (g), including information regarding collisions, aftermarket parts, or the identify of any servicing dealerships and dates when the visits occurred. Plaintiff has not demonstrated good cause for failure to provide documents specified in Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(f). These are documents which the client should have provided to counsel as part of the intake process.

            The Court agrees with Defendant’s argument in the Reply:

Section 871.26 places the disclosure obligation on the disclosing party. Nothing in the statute excuses compliance because the opposing party may independently possess similar information. If Plaintiffs’ position were accepted, any disclosure obligation could be avoided simply by asserting that the opposing party already has access to the requested information. Because that is not what the Legislature enacted, the argument should be rejected. 

(Reply, Page 2:18 – 22.)

            Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(j)(1) provides:

(j) Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows:

 

(1) A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff’s attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).

(Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(j)(1).) 

            Defendant’s request for sanctions against Plaintiff’s counsel is GRANTED in the mandatory amount of $1,500. (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(j)(1).) Sanctions are to be paid to Defendant’s counsel within 15 business days. (Id.) 

Case Number: 26STCP03055    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 734

THE FOLLOWING CONSTITUTES THE COURT'S RULING ON PETITIONER'S EX PARTE APPLICATION.  THE MATTER WILL NOT BE CALLED FOR HEARING.

The Court has reviewed the ex parte application of Petitioner LA Settlement Funding, and finds the matter suitable for decision in chambers without argument.

In the ex parte application, Petitioner contends that relief is appropriate because “the Court’s reservation system is not built for eleven filings arriving at once.”  The Court will point out that this design is deliberate:  there must be limits on the number of reservation slots for motion hearings available through the CRS system.  The limits in the CRS system account for the anticipated amount of work that particular types of motions will require.  Without this system, the Court cannot allocate resources correctly, anticipate the need to review moving and opposition papers, and ensure time for hearings.  By combining eleven payment transfer requests into a single petition, and setting a single hearing for all eleven, Petitioner’s counsel unilaterally attempted to pack the work of eleven petitions into a single hearing slot.  This is no different from reserving a table for one at a restaurant, then showing up with a party of eleven, demanding to be served.

Nevertheless, the application is GRANTED, on conditions.  The Court will authorize the special setting of hearings for all proposed transfers on November 13, 2026 at 8:30 a.m., on the following conditions:

  1. Petitioner shall pay eleven separate motion filing fees of $60 to the Clerk of the Court.
  2. At least 10 days before the hearing, Petitioner must file a supplemental brief or table that clearly indicates, in a separate section for each of the 11 proposed transferors, the following:  (1) all information required by Insurance Code section 10139.5(c), including complete information regarding dependents, income, and any financial resources beyond income; and (2) any documents required under the Insurance Code, or an explanation of why such documents cannot be provided or are not required. 

Failure to pay the required motion fees, or failure to comply with the Court’s order regarding the filing of supplemental briefs, may result in a continuance of the hearings or the imposition of sanctions pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 177.5.
