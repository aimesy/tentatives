DEPARTMENT C LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22NWCV01287    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: C

MARTINEZ vs AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.

CASE NO.:  22NWCV01287

HEARING:   8/5/26 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Defendant AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.’s Motion to Strike or in the Alternative to Tax Costs is GRANTED in part and DENIED in part. Plaintiff’s demand for costs in the amount of $35,848.79 is reduced by $7,528.50 for a total of $28,320.29. 

Moving Party to give NOTICE.

 

 

Defendant AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC. (Defendant) moves to strike, or in the alternative, tax costs pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. (CCP), §§ 1032, 1033.5, subds. (a), (c)(2)–(3).

 

Background

 

This is a lemon law case. On February 3, 2026, Plaintiff Yolisma Martinez (Plaintiff) obtained a Jury Verdict in her favor in the amount of $31,236.51 against Defendant.

 

Legal Standard

 

Except as otherwise expressly provided by statute, a prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs in any action or proceeding.  (CCP § 1032(b).)

 

The losing party may dispute any or all of the items in the prevailing party’s memorandum of costs by a motion to strike or tax costs.  (CRC 3.1700(b).)  If the items appearing in a cost bill appear to be proper charges, the burden is on the party seeking to tax costs to show that they were not reasonable or necessary.  (Ladas v. California State Automotive Assoc. (1993) 19 Cal.App.4th 761, 773-774.)  On the other hand, if the items are properly objected to, they are put in issue and the burden of proof is on the party claiming them as costs.  (Ibid.) 

 

Discussion

 

WITHDRAWN COSTS:

 

Plaintiff concedes two of the six challenged charges were included in error: the dupicate court-reporter charge in the amount of $2,312.20 and the $578.20 Jon Minami nonappearance charge, were included in error.

 

Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED as to Item 12 for court reporter fees in the amount of $2,312.20, and as to Item 4 for the $578.20 Jon Minami nonappearance charge.

 

ITEM 4:

 

Under section 1033.5, a prevailing party is entitled to the reasonable cost of taking the other party's depositions, unless it appears that taking thereof was unnecessary. (Ceranski v. Muensch (1943) 60 Cal.App.2d 751._

 

Defendant seeks to tax $631.00 for the non-appearance of Defendant’s custodian of records. Defendant does not dispute that this cost is recoverable under C.C.P. section 1033.5 (or more broadly as an expense under the Song-Beverly Act) but disputes that it was reasonable. While it is undisputed that Defendant objected to Plaintiff’s notice of deposition and informed Plaintiff that they would not attend the deposition, Plaintiff took a formal notice of non-appearance because the custodian of records deposition was important to the case and trial was imminent. Defendant did not provide alternative deposition dates. Therefore, Plaintiff is entitled to reasonable costs for the non-appearance.

 

The motion to tax $631.00 as to Item 4 is DENIED. 

 

ITEM 13:

 

Section 1033.5 allows recovery only for items authorized by statute and “reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation.”

 

Defendant seeks to tax $4,352.60 in travel fees, $282.50 private-investigator report, and the $3.00 “Court Sanctions” charge.

 

Travel costs, unless for an authorized witness, are not recoverable per Ladas v. California State Automotive Assoc. (1993) 19 Cal.App.4th 761, 775-76 ["Routine expenses for local travel by attorneys or other firm employees are not reasonably necessary to the conduct of litigation."].) Therefore, Plaintiff’s unitemized $4,352.60 “Travel” entry in her Memorandum of Costs which identifies no traveler or purpose is not recoverable. The remaining fees could be recoverable in the Court’s discretion under CCP § 1033.5(c)(4) if they were reasonably necessary to the conduct of litigation. The Court finds that they are not recoverable here.  Therefore, the Court shall strike costs associated with the $282.50 private-investigator report and the $3.00 “Court Sanctions” charge as non-statutory overhead fees.

 

The motion to tax $4,638.10 as to Item 13 is GRANTED. 

 

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s demand for costs in the amount of $35,848.79 is reduced by $7,528.50 for a total of $28,320.29.
