Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Case Name: Dechter v. Massman, et al.

Case No.: 23STCV11406 

Matter: Motion for Relief

Moving Party: Plaintiff Bradley J. Dechter

Responding Party: Defendants Ronald Massman and DHX–Dependable Hawaiian Express, 

Inc. 

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On May 19, 2023, Plaintiff Bradley J. Dechter filed a Complaint.  On October 9, 2023, Plaintiff filed the operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants Ronald Massman and DHX–Dependable Hawaiian Express, Inc. (“DHX”) for (1) breach of oral contract, (2) breach of oral contract, (3) fraud, and (4) unjust enrichment.  Plaintiff alleges that he is entitled to an interest in DHX due to his contributions, but that his ownership interest has been refused.  

On March 13, 2026, the Court denied Plaintiff’s motions to compel further responses because they were filed more than 45 days after Defendants’ responses.

Plaintiff now seeks relief from the loss of its motions to compel, arguing that the electronic filing service had an error when Plaintiff attempted an 11:45-11:50 pm filing, causing the actual filings to take place after midnight, at 12:03 am and 12:11 am. Plaintiff seeks relief under Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 2.259(c) and CCP § 473(b).

Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 2.259(c) states: “If a technical problem with a court's electronic filing system prevents the court from accepting an electronic filing on a particular court day, and the electronic filer demonstrates that he or she attempted to electronically file the document on that day, the court must deem the document as filed on that day. This subdivision does not apply to the filing of a complaint or any other initial pleading in an action or proceeding.”

The evidence shows that Plaintiff attempted the filings on June 25, 2025, at around 11:45 pm. If the filings were successful at that time, the filings would have been timely. Because apparently there was some sort of system error that delayed the actual filings past midnight and into June 26 and because there is a policy of hearing disputes on their merits, the Court will revive the motions to compel under Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 2.259(c).

The Motion is granted.

Moving party to give notice.



Case Number: 24STCV03983    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 2, 2026

Case Name: Deang v. Glendora Congregate Living, Inc., et al.

Case No.: 24STCV03983

Matter: Motion to Expunge Lis Pendens

Moving Party: Defendants Suzanna Sinapyan, Hovaness Chamichyan, and Glendora 

Congregate Living, Inc. 

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


Plaintiff, a pro per litigant, apparently filed a lis pendens as to 549 E Ada Ave, Glendora, CA 91745.

Defendants Suzanna Sinapyan, Hovaness Chamichyan, and Glendora Congregate Living, Inc. now seek to expunge the lis pendens.  

Code Civ. Proc. § 405.30 provides, “[a]t any time after notice of pendency of action has been recorded, any party, or any nonparty with an interest in the real property affected thereby, may apply to the court in which the action is pending to expunge the notice. . . . Evidence or declarations may be filed with the motion to expunge the notice. The court may permit evidence to be received in the form of oral testimony, and may make any orders it deems just to provide for discovery by any party affected by a motion to expunge the notice. The claimant shall have the burden of proof under Sections 405.31 and 405.32.”

Moreover, Code Civ. Proc. § 405.31 provides, “[i]n proceedings under this chapter, the court shall order the notice expunged if the court finds that the pleading on which the notice is based does not contain a real property claim. The court shall not order an undertaking to be given as a condition of expunging the notice where the court finds the pleading does not contain a real property claim.”

Code Civ. Proc. § 405.32 states, “[i]n proceedings under this chapter, the court shall order that the notice be expunged if the court finds that the claimant has not established by a preponderance of the evidence the probable validity of the real property claim. The court shall not order an undertaking to be given as a condition of expunging the notice if the court finds the claimant has not established the probable validity of the real property claim.”

Here, Plaintiff failed to file any opposition, and, therefore, failed to carry her burden.

The Motion is granted.

Case Number: 25STCV24668    Hearing Date: July 2, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 2, 2026

Case Name: Rosamond Ranch, LP v. Robison, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV24668

Matter: Motion for Trial Preference

Ruling: The Motion is denied.

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On August 21, 2025, Plaintiffs Rosamond Ranch, LP, Calitex, LLC, Elias Shokrian, and Derek Boisse filed a Complaint for (1) legal malpractice, (2) breach of contract, and (3) breach of fiduciary duty. 

On June 30, 2026, the Court allowed the filing of a first amended complaint in order to (a) remove previously dismissed defendants; (b) clarify specific factual allegations regarding the entry of the default judgment in the underlying action; and (c) plead damages with greater specificity by identifying a claim for lost rent in the amount of $2,226,794.44, and aligning the prayer for relief among the remaining defendants.

The trial date is November 6, 2028.

Plaintiff now seeks a trial preference because he “is over the age of 70 and currently 81 years old; 2. Plaintiff suffers from multiple ongoing medical conditions, including hypertension, hyperlipidemia, elevated blood sugar, gout, and stress-related health complications exacerbated by the ongoing litigation; . . . Absent trial preference, Plaintiff faces a substantial risk that his advanced age and fragile and deteriorating state of his health will impair his ability to meaningfully participate in trial proceedings currently scheduled for November 6, 2028, thereby prejudicing his substantial interests in this litigation.”

Code Civ. Proc. § 36(a) states, “A party to a civil action who is over 70 years of age may petition the court for a preference, which the court shall grant if the court makes both of the following findings: (1) The party has a substantial interest in the action as a whole. (2) The health of the party is such that a preference is necessary to prevent prejudicing the party’s interest in the litigation.”

While the Plaintiff, Elias Shokrian, is 82 years old and has provided a physician’s note concerning hypertension and anxiety, the Plaintiff has failed to show that these chronic, controlled conditions require an expedited trial to avoid prejudice. Unlike the plaintiff in Fox v. Superior Court (2018) 21 Cal.App.5th 529, who faced immediate incapacity and severe, debilitating side effects from cancer treatment, Mr. Shokrian has not provided evidence that his current health prevents him from meaningfully participating in trial proceedings on the standard schedule. 

The physician’s recommendation explicitly advises Mr. Shokrian to "minimize his litigation activity" to avoid stress that could trigger serious cardiovascular events. Advancing this trial date to occur within 120 days would have the opposite effect, forcing an intensive, compressed discovery and trial schedule.

Lastly, granting a preference at this stage, when a FAC is about to be filed, would unfairly truncate discovery and prejudice the Defendants' due process rights. Defendants have not yet had sufficient opportunity to complete discovery or file potential dispositive motions, such as for summary judgment, which is critical in a complex legal malpractice action.

Therefore, the Motion is denied. 

Moving party to give notice.
