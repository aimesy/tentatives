DEPARTMENT 517 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23STCV05240    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 517


In preparing for the final status conference this morning, the Court observed the following:

1. Mr. Cole still has not filed a substitution of attorney to show that he is counsel of record for the Pedram Defendants in Blumenfeld v. Mason.  Mr. Cole has substituted in only as to the related case, 732 N.Vista LLC v. Blumenfeld.

2.  At the last hearing, Mr. Cole stated that the Pedram Defendants may dismiss their Cross-Complaint in Blumenfeld v. Mason and that 732 N. Vista LLC may dismiss the related case.   No dismissals have been filed.   Will there be?

3.  The exhibit list that the parties submitted last October in Blumenfeld v. Mason shows many disputes over admissibility.   The Court will need to work with the parties to resolve the disputes as best we can prior to the trial. This disputes are in addition to the disputes over exhibits that are the subject of Pedram's request for an Evidence Code section 402 hearing about certain documents that he would like to have admitted at trial.

4.  The Pedram Defendants have not filed proposed jury instructions.  Only Plaintiff has done so.

5.  Neither side has filed a joint statement of the case.  

6.  The joint witness list that the parties submitted last October has a time estimate of 59 hours, which is roughly 13 days (the Court generally is available for trial 4.5 hours per day) not including jury selection.  It should not take 3 weeks for the trial in this case. 

The Court will inquire af the FSC whether a mandatory settlement conference with a bench officer is still something that the parties are considering.

Case Number: 24STCV27348    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 517

STATUS OF EFFORTS TO SETTLE THE CASE

The Court will begin the hearing today by asking counsel whether settlement is still a possibility.  To that end, the Court will inquire how many of the Miramontes cases actually have gone to trial and how many have settled.

PLAINTIFFS' MOTIONS IN LIMINE # 1

# 1 Denied.   Defendant must, of course, comply with Evidence Code sections 783 and 1106 if it seeks to introduce evidence of the sort addressed by Plaintiffs' motion.

# 2.  Granted.  The motion was unopposed.

# 3.   Denied.  If the evidence at trial supports a comparative fault argument and instruction, then Defendant may request it.  The motion prematurely seeks to bar this.

# 4  Granted

# 5  Denied.  The Court will ask Plaintiffs' counsel at the hearing whether there is any caselaw that supports this motion.  The Court is unaware of any and hence the tentative decision to deny the motion.

# 6  Granted in part.  The use of electronic tools in connection with the opening statement is permissible.  But the Court cannot up front approve everything and anything that is part of the electronic presentation.  Counsel must meet and confer about the evidence that will be presented.

DEFENDANT'S MOTIONS IN LIMINE 

# 1  Denied.  Overbroad and premature, as Plaintiffs correctly argue in opposition to the motion.

# 2   Granted, but only along the lines urged by the Plaintiffs in their opposition to the motion.

#3    This is the MIL about the aviary.   Do Plaintiffs oppose this motion?

# 4   Denied.  The jury should hear the evidence that the Defendant seeks to exclude in this motion.

#5  Denied.

VOIR DIRE

Is there a written questionnaire that counsel have used in the other Miramonte cases that went to trial?


PROPOSED STATEMENT OF THE CASE

The three Plaintiffs allege that when they were children, they attended Miramonte Elementary School, which is part of the Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District ("LAUSD"), and that as students there they were sexually by a Miramonte teacher, Mark Berndt.   The Plaintiffs further allege that Berndt's misconduct has caused them to suffer significant harm.   The Plaintiffs contend that LAUSD personnel were negligent in supervising Berndt and that therefore LAUSD must pay money to the Plaintiffs to compensate them for the harm they have suffered.  LAUSD denies that its administrators were negligent and disputes the nature and extent of the harm and damages that the Plaintiffs are claiming.

JURY INSTRUCTIONS, VERDICT FORM, AND EXHIBITS 

At the hearing, the Court will ask counsel to offer information regarding what jury instructions were given, what verdict form was used, and what exhibits on the joint exhibit list in this case were admitted/excluded in the Miramonte cases that have gone to trial.


THE COURT'S ENGAGEMENT IN TRIAL IN ANOTHER CASE ON JULY 20

It is highly likely that the Court will be engaged in trial in another case on July 20.   That is because several cases that are on calendar for trial the week of July 13 will be going to trial.  All are short. But as many as 3 of them could go, more or less back to back to back beginning next week. The Court will discuss with counsel what this means for the timing of the trial in Jane Doe v. LAUSD.

Case Number: 26STCV12202    Hearing Date: July 10, 2026    Dept: 517

The Court is granting Plaintiff's motion for leave to file a Second Amended Complaint.  Whether, as Defendant contends, Plaintiff failed to exhaust administrative remedies on the claims in the Second Amended Complaint should be tested through a demurrer or motion for summary judgment.  Defendant's position that allowing Plaintiff to amend would be futile due to a failure to exhaust administrative rests on a declaration of a claims investigator for Defendant attesting to purported facts and a request for judicial notice regarding the record of Plaintiff's filing of administrative claims that are inappropriate at this stage. Plaintiff shall serve and file his proposed Second Amended Complaint by July 20, 2026.
