The Court’s tentative ruling is to GRANT final approval, contingent on counsel addressing the following concerns.  Class Members’ Response Deadline is August 28, 2026 and the Extended Response Deadline for Class Members who received a remailed notice is September 14, 2026. BACKGROUND

This is a wage and hour class action. Defendant is an airline company which offers daily passenger flights to/from the county of Los Angeles, where Plaintiff worked.

On September 28, 2023, Plaintiff initiated this action by serving Defendant and the California Labor and Workforce Development Agency (“LWDA”) with a Notice of Alleged Labor Code Violations (“PAGA Notice”).

On December 5, 2023, Plaintiff filed a class action complaint action against Defendant for: (1) Failure to Pay Overtime Wages in Violation Of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 510 and 1198; (2) Failure to Provide Required Meal Periods in Violation Of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 226.7 & 512(a) and the Applicable IWC Wage Order; (3) Failure to Provide Required Rest Periods in Violation Of Cal. Lab Code § 226.7 and the Applicable IWC Wage Order; (4) Failure to Pay Minimum Wages in Violation Of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 1194, 1197 & 1197.1; (5) Failure to Provide Final Wages When Due in Violation Of Cal. Labor Code §§ 201 and 202; (6) Failure to Provide Wages During Employment When Due in Violation Of Cal. Labor Code § 204; (7) Failure to Provide Accurate Itemized Statements in Violation Of Cal. Lab. Code § 226(a); (8) Failure to Maintain Requisite Payroll Records in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code § 1174(d); (9) Failure to Reimburse Employees for Required Expenses in Violation of Cal. Lab. Code §§ 2800 and 2802; and (10) Unfair Competition in Violation of Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 17200, et seq.

On December 5, 2023, Plaintiff filed a separate representative PAGA complaint in the Los Angeles Superior Court, Case No. 23TRCV04045, alleging a single cause of action for violations of the California Labor Code § 2698, et seq. (“PAGA Action”).

On April 7, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Operative First Amended Complaint alleging the following causes of action: (1) Violation of California Labor Code §§ 510 and 1198 (Unpaid Overtime); (2) Violation of California Labor Code §§ 226.7 and 512(a) (Unpaid Meal Period Premiums); (3) Violation of California Labor Code § 226.7 (Unpaid Rest Period Premiums); (4) Violation of California Labor Code §§ 1194, 1197, and 1197.1 (Unpaid Minimum Wages); (5) Violation of California Labor Code §§ 201 and 202 (Final Wages Not Timely Paid); (6) Violation of California Labor Code § 204 (Wages Not Timely Paid During Employment); (7) Violation of California Labor Code Statements); (8) Violation of California Labor Code § 1174(d) (Failure To Keep Requisite Payroll Records); (9) Violation of California Labor Code §§ 2800 and 2802 (Unreimbursed Business Expenses); (10) Violation of California Business & Professions Code §§ 17200, et seq; (11) Violation of California Labor Code § 2698, et seq. (California Labor Code Private Attorneys General Act of 2004).

On April 9, 2026, the PAGA Action was dismissed without prejudice.

Counsel represents that prior to mediation the parties engaged in investigation and discovery wherein: 1) Class Counsel requested Plaintiff’s employee file from Defendant; 2) Defendant produced various documents, including but not limited to, copies of Plaintiff’s payroll records and applicable company policies during the Class Period; 3) Defendant produced time and payroll records, along with copies of applicable handbooks, policies, and agreements; and 4) Lawyers for Justice, PC., created several damage models predicated on the analysis of the data provided by Defendant.

On September 24, 2024, the Parties participated in a full day mediation presided over by Steven Rottman, Esq. Although the Parties did not settle at mediation, after continued negotiations and bargaining, the Parties reached an agreement to settle the matter and signed a Memorandum of Understanding on March 31, 2025. A partially executed long form Settlement Agreement was filed with the Court on January 16, 2026 attached to the Declaration of Shani O. Zakay (“Zakay Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval as Exhibit 1. 

On May 1, 2026, the Court continued preliminary approval for further briefing and revisions. In response, on June 21, 2026, counsel filed a fully executed Amended Settlement Agreement attached to the Declaration of Nicole Noursamadi (“Noursamadi Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval as Exhibit 1.

On June 1, 2026, the Court continued preliminary approval for further briefing and revisions. In response, on June 18, 2026, counsel filed a fully executed Second Amended Settlement Agreement attached to the Supplemental Declaration of Nicole Noursamadi (“Noursamadi Supp. Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval as Exhibit 1.

Preliminary Approval was granted on June 24, 2026.  Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered. (See Declaration of Norma Ayala (“Ayala Decl.”).)

C.      Attorney Fees and Costs

            Class Counsel request an award of $227,500 (35%)  in fees and $22,612.10 in costs. (Motion ISO Fees, 13:19-21, 15:2-6.)  

 The Settlement Agreement provides for fees up to $227,500 (35%)  in fees and $30,000 in costs (Settlement Agreement ¶3.2.2); class members were provided notice of the requested awards and none objected. (Ayala Decl., ¶12 and Exhibit A thereto.)

The following fee split has been agreed to herein: 85% to Lawyers for Justice, PC; 75% to JCL Law Firm; and 7.5% to Zakay Law Group, APLC. (Declaration of Maria Halwadjian (“Halwadjian Decl.”) ISO Preliminary Approval, ¶19.)

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 254.)  Here, class counsel request attorney fees using the percentage method and via loadstar crosscheck. (Motion ISO Fees, pgs. 2-13.)

In common fund cases, the Court may employ a percentage of the benefit method, as cross-checked against the lodestar. (Laffitte v. Robert Half Int’l, Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.5th 480, 503.) The fee request represents 35% of the gross settlement amount, which is above the average generally awarded in class actions.  (See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 [“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”].)

        Therefore, counsel have spent a total of 304.1 hours for a total lodestar of $236,040, which would require a small negative multiplier to yield the requested fee amount. (Ibid.) Notice of the fee request was provided to class members in the notice packet and no one objected.

        Fees are set at 33 1/3% of the GSA or $216,666.66, which represents a reasonable percentage of the total funds paid by Defendant and rewards counsel for any risk they took in this case.  The Court finds there are no special circumstances warranting a higher percentage recovery than the one-third fee award that is typically provided.

        As for costs, Class Counsel is requesting $22,612.10. (Matavosian Decl. ISO Final, ¶46 and Exhibit B thereto; Lapuyade Decl. ISO Final, ¶11 and Exhibit 1 thereto; Zakay Decl ISO Final, ¶13 and Exhibit 2 thereto.)

The costs include, but are not limited to, filing costs ($1,435), Legal Document Server, Inc. costs, OneLegal costs, CPAnlaysis Data Analytics ($3,025), and mediation costs ($15,000). (Ibid.) The costs appear to be reasonable in amount and reasonably necessary to this litigation.  

         Based on the above, the Court awards $216,666.66 (33.33%) in fees and $22,612.10 in costs.

D.     Incentive Award

The Settlement provides for up to $7,500 for a service payment to the Named Plaintiff. (Settlement Agreement, ¶3.2.1.)

 Plaintiff Woldemariam represents that his contributions to this litigation include, but were not limited to spending 15 hours on the following: obtaining counsel, having numerous conversations with counsel, searching for and providing documents, preparing for mediation, and reviewing the settlement.  (Woldemariam Decl., ¶¶5, 10-12.)

Based on the above, as well as the benefits obtained on behalf of the class, the Court grants an enhancement payment in the amount of $7,500.

 

E.      Claims Administration Costs

The claims administrator is requesting $5,990 for the costs of settlement administration. (Ayala Decl., ¶19.) This is less than the estimated cost of $6,000 provided for in the Settlement Agreement (¶3.2.3), and disclosed to class members in the Notice, to which there were no objections. (Ayala Decl., ¶12 and Exhibit A thereto.)

Based on the above, the Court awards administration costs in the requested amount of $5,990.
