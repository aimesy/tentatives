DEPARTMENT 514 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 25STCP04406    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 514

Zehnaly v. Zehnaly
25STCP04406
Plaintiff’s Demurrer to Answer of Defendant

Tentative Ruling

The demurrer is overruled.

Background 

On November 14, 2025, Armen Zehnaly (“Plaintiff”) filed the complaint in this action against Ghazar Zehnaly, individually and as trustee of the 2011 Zehnaly Family Trust dated January 21, 2011, (“Defendant”) and J.P. Morgan Chase Bank, N.A., and Does 1 through 100, asserting causes of action for (1) breach of fiduciary duty, (2) conversion, (3) violation of Penal Code section 496, subdivision (a), (4) partition, and (5) dissolution of partnership.

After the Court overruled Defendant’s demurrer to the complaint, Defendant filed an answer on April 6, 2026.  On the same day, Defendant filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff and Roes 1 through 100, asserting causes of action for (1) elder abuse, (2) conversion, (3) breach of fiduciary duty, (4) violation of Penal Code section 496, (5) inducing breach of contract, (6) interference with contractual relations, (7) interference with prospective business advantage, and (8) negligence.

Plaintiff filed an answer to the Cross-Complaint on May 8, 2026.

Currently before the Court and set for hearing on June 24 is a demurrer that Plaintiff filed to Defendant’s answer to the complaint.  On June 2, Defendant filed an opposition, and Plaintiff filed a reply on June 8.

The hearing was initially set for June 15 and was continued to June 24, 2026.

No trial date has been set.

Legal Standard

A demurrer to an answer, although rare, is authorized by Code of Civil Procedure section 430.20.  The demurrer may be brought on the ground that the answer “does not state facts sufficient to constitute a defense,” that the answer is “uncertain,” or that when an answer pleads a contract “it cannot be ascertained from the answer whether the contract is written or oral.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.20, subds. (a)-(c).)

Meet and Confer

Before filing a demurrer, “the demurring party shall meet and confer in person or by telephone with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41, subd. (a).)  The Court has considered the evidence from each side and determines that the statutory requirement is satisfied. 

Discussion               

Plaintiff demurs to each and every one of the 54 affirmative defenses in Defendant’s answer.  Plaintiff argues that none of the 54 defenses is adequately supported by factual allegations (Mem., at p. 3); that none of the 54 defenses identifies which affirmative defense is asserted against which cause of action in the complaint (Mem., at pp. 3-4); and that the Fourth through Twenty-Second Affirmative Defenses sounds in contract but it cannot be ascertained whether the contract is in writing or oral (Mem., at p. 4.)

The Court has reviewed the answer and determines that each of the affirmative defenses is adequately pleaded.  To the extent that Plaintiff seeks additional information regarding the evidentiary support for the affirmative defenses, Plaintiff may propound appropriate discovery, including (at an appropriate time), but not limited to, Form Interrogatory 15.1.

The demurrer is overruled.

Conclusion 

The Court OVERRULES the demurrer of Plaintiff Armen Zehnaly to Defendant’s demurrer.

Moving party is ordered to give notice.
