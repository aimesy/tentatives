DEPARTMENT B LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24TRCV01547    Hearing Date: August 12, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                      Defendants Stadco LA, LLC, et al.

Responding Party:                  Defendant Quality Commercial Cleaning

Motion for Protective Order Re Discovery

 

            The Court considered the papers filed in support of and in opposition to the motion.

RULING

            The motion is GRANTED.

BACKGROUND

            On May 6, 2024, plaintiff Tokiwa Henry filed a complaint against Stadco LA, LLC, Stadco LA Manager, LLC, Hollywood Park Management Company, LLC, Hollywood Park Management Company II, LLC, Hollywood Park Land Company, LLC, Brightview Landscapes, LLC, ABM Industries, Incorporated, ABM Industry Groups, LLC, Quality Commercial Cleaning, Inc. dba Spectrum Facility Maintenance for negligence and premises liability based on a slip and fall that occurred on July 23, 2022, at SoFi Stadium near a pedestrian pathway at the intersection of Touchdown Drive and Kareem Court.

            On March 10, 2025, plaintiff filed an amendment designating Pincay RE, LLC as Doe 1.

            Trial is scheduled for October 12, 2026.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

            Under CCP §2030.090, “When interrogatories have been propounded, the responding party, and any other party or affected natural person or organization may promptly move for a protective order.  This motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.  (b) The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party or other natural person or organization from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense.  This protective order may include, but is not limited to, one or more of the following directions:  (1) That the set of interrogatories, or particular interrogatories in the set, need not be answered. . . .”

            Under CCP §2031.060, “(a) When an inspection, copying, testing, or sampling of documents, tangible things, places, or electronically stored information has been demanded, the party to whom the demand has been directed, and any other party or affected person, may promptly move for a protective order. This motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.  (b) The court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any party or other person from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense. This protective order may include, but is not limited to, one or more of the following directions:  (1) That all or some of the items or categories of items in the demand need not be produced or made available at all.”

            See also CCP §2025.420(a) for depositions and CCP §2033.080(a) for requests for admission.

            “[T]he burden is on the party seeking the protective order to show good cause for whatever order is sought.”  Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255 (citation omitted).

DISCUSSION

            Defendants Stadco LA, LLC, Stadco LA Manager, LLC, Hollywood Park Management Company, LLC, Hollywood Park Management Company II, LLC, Hollywood Park Land Company, LLC, and Pincay RE, LLC requests that the Court enter a protective order in conformity with the “Los Angeles Model Stipulation and Protective Order – Confidential Designation Only” form adopted by the Los Angeles Superior Court.

            Defendants assert that defendant Quality Commercial Cleaning, Inc. refused to sign the Stipulated Protective Order that defendants circulated among the parties to facilitate discovery, including document production.  Defendants argue that good cause exists because moving defendants have been requested to produce materials in discovery that must be designated as confidential, and they further expect to be requested to produce additional confidential materials in the future.  Defendants explain that the other defendants, ABM Industry Groups, LLC, Brightview Landscapes, LLC, and Quality Commercial Cleaning Inc., contract with moving defendant HPMC to provide services in areas surrounding SoFi Stadium, and that the contracts are, by their terms, “confidential” and contain sensitive and proprietary information.  Defendants note that deposition testimony that concerns such contracts and the relationships between HPMC and the other defendants is potentially confidential.

            In opposition, defendant Quality Commercial Cleaning argues that the motion is “procedurally barred” as there was no reasonable good faith attempt at informal resolution.  Defendant also argues that moving defendants have not carried their burden of showing good cause as to any document.  Defendant further asserts that entering a “stipulation” is unavailable because the parties have not stipulated and that the proposed order is overbroad.

            The Court finds that moving defendants have shown good cause for entry of a protective order based on the “Los Angeles Model Stipulation and Protective Order – Confidential Designation Only” form adopted by the Los Angeles Superior Court.  Defendant Quality Commercial Cleaning is not prohibited from obtaining any documents that another party “in good faith believes” is entitled to confidential treatment and there is a procedure to challenge the “designation.”  The proposed protective order is standard practice and a court-approved model and frequently used to permit production but limit disclosure.  The Court further finds that moving defendants engaged in a good faith attempt to resolve this issue short of motion practice.

ORDER

            The motion is GRANTED.

            Defendant Quality Commercial Cleaning’s request for sanctions is DENIED.

            Moving defendants to give notice of ruling.
