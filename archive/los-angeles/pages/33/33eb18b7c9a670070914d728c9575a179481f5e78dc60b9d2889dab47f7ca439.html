Case Number: 24STCV30033    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 14 - John Salib v. Keith Ellington Frazer, et al.

          Defendant Daniel Deleon’s Motion for Determination of Good Faith Settlement

Defendant Daniel Deleon moves for an order determining the good faith of his settlement with Plaintiff John Salib under Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6, subdivision (a).

When only some of multiple defendants alleged to be jointly liable settle a case, the settling parties may apply to the court for a determination of good faith settlement.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6(a).) A determination of good faith “shall bar any other joint tortfeasor or co-obligor from any further claims against the settling tortfeasor or co-obligor for equitable comparative contribution, or partial or comparative indemnity, based on comparative negligence or comparative fault.” (Id., subd. (c).)        

Any opposing party bears “the burden below to show the settlement amount was ‘so far “out of the ballpark” in relation to’ the [various] factors that the settlement was inconsistent with the equitable objectives of section 877.6.” (Cahill v. San Diego Gas & Electric Co. (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 939, 967; accord Code Civ. Proc., § 877.6(d).)

After considering all relevant factors, the Court finds the settlement between Defendant Deleon and Plaintiff was made in good faith. In making such a determination, the Court must consider various factors stated in Tech-Bilt, Inc. v. Woodward-Clyde & Associates (1985) 38 Cal.3d 488, 499-500. “ ‘Good faith may be found only if there has been no collusion between the settling parties and where the settlement amount appears to be within the “reasonable range” of the settling party’s proportionate share of comparative liability for a plaintiff’s injuries.’ ” (Long Beach Memorial Medical Center v. Superior Court (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 865, 872.) 

Defendant Deleon settled with Plaintiff for $25,000. (Elkerton Decl., ¶ 3.) Defendant states that the settlement was made in good faith, and was the result of arm's-length negotiations. (Motion, at p. 8.) After considering all relevant factors, the Court finds Defendant Deleon presents sufficient evidence showing this settlement is within the reasonable range of his potential share of liability.    

No other party has asserted a lack of good faith or presented any evidence on that issue. “The party asserting the lack of good faith shall have the burden of proof on that issue.” (CCP § 877.6(d).)

Disposition

Defendant Daniel Deleon’s motion for determination of good faith settlement is granted. Under Code of Civil Procedure section 877.6, the Court hereby finds the settlement between Defendant Daniel Deleon and Plaintiff John Salib was made in good faith.


Case Number: 25STCV10798    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 16 - Vahigh Sarhadrahimbad v. Mobilitas Insurance Company

Petitioner Vahigh Sarhadrahimbad’s Motion to Compel Consolidated Arbitration

Petitioner Vahigh Sarhadrahimbad moves to consolidate the pending arbitration against Respondent Mobilitas Insurance Company’s (“Mobilitas”) with a separate arbitration against Respondent United States Fire Insurance (“US Fire”). US Fire filed an opposition.

Request for Judicial Notice

          Petitioner requests the Court take judicial notice of three different rulings issued by various trial court departments concerning similar motions. These materials are not relevant to this Court’s ruling of the issues and provide no binding authority. The request for judicial notice is denied.

Factual Background

Petitioner previously filed a motion to consolidate two arbitration proceedings between Petitioner and Mobilitas, and Petitioner and nonparty Blue Star Insurance Company. In opposition to the motion, nonparty Blue Star argued that it was the incorrect party to arbitrate the matter. The Court denied the motion on April 27, 2026, reasoning in part that Blue Star was not a party to the case and there was no evidence that it was even compelled to arbitrate under any agreement. In response to the Court’s ruling, Petitioner identified the correct party to arbitrate the matter, US Fire, substituted it as a Defendant in this action on April 29, 2026, and filed the instant motion.

Consolidate Arbitration Proceedings

Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.3 provides, in relevant part:

A party to an arbitration agreement may petition the court to consolidate separate arbitration proceedings, and the court may order consolidation of separate arbitration proceedings when:

(1) Separate arbitration agreements or proceedings exist between the same parties; or one party is a party to a separate arbitration agreement or proceeding with a third party; and

(2) The disputes arise from the same transactions or series of related transactions; and

(3) There is common issue or issues of law or fact creating the possibility of conflicting rulings by more than one arbitrator or panel of arbitrators.

Here, the first two elements are not disputed, as US Fire acknowledges that it will arbitrate the matter with Petitioner and that two arbitration agreements exist. However, US Fire argues that Petitioner cannot show there is a common issue of law or fact between the separate arbitration proceedings to meet the third element. US Fire contends that Mobilitas’ arbitration concerns uninsured/underinsured motorist (“UM/UIM”) coverage, which is distinct from US Fire’s benefits under its Occupational Accident Certificate (“OAC”). Specifically, the UM/UIM arbitration addresses the amount of tort-based damages Petitioner is entitled to recover from the uninsured/ underinsured motorist policy. The proceeding is conducted under Insurance Code section 11580.2. In contrast, the OAC arbitration addresses third-party contractual rights, analyzes contract-based benefit eligibility, limits, and offsets, and is conducted based on the arbitrator’s scheduling order. Thus, US Fire argues, there are no overlapping issues that would risk the possibility of conflicting rulings.

The Court disagrees. Even though Respondents’ obligations arising under different contracts, these coverage disputes arise from the same motor vehicle accident and will necessarily involve an evaluation of Petitioner’s injuries and treatment, which are relevant under both policies. US Fire admits that the OAC arbitration will involve a determination of covered expenses for reasonable charges ordered by a physician for medically necessary treatment. This inquiry will require evaluation of the same injury and treatment evidence at issue in the UM/UIM proceedings. Further, Mobilitas is entitled to any offset amount paid by US Fire. Thus, litigating these common issues in a consolidated arbitration would be most efficient. The arbitrator can determine how the arbitration will proceed, such that each carrier may need to participate only in those portions that affect their policies, thereby minimizing any burden. The Court exercises its discretion under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.3 to order consolidated proceedings.

Disposition

Petitioner Vahigh Sarhadrahimbad’s motion to compel consolidated arbitration is granted. Respondent United States Fire Insurance is hereby ordered to arbitrate this matter in a consolidated proceeding alongside Respondent Mobilitas Insurance Company and Petitioner.

The parties should proceed under a single neutral arbitrator as set forth in Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.3. If the parties cannot agree on an arbitrator, the Court will appoint a neutral arbitrator consistent with the procedures under Code of Civil Procedure section 1281.6.

The case remains stayed.


Case Number: 25STCV18418    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 13 - Itria Ventures LLC v. MK Hotel Palmdale LLC, et al.

          Plaintiff Itria Ventures LLC’s Motion to Enforce Settlement Agreement

Plaintiff Itria Ventures LLC moves to enforce their settlement agreement and enter judgment against Defendants MK Hotels Palmdale LLC; MK Sky Harbor LLC; MK Hotels Brea LLC; MK Scottsdale South LLC; MDVJ, LLC; MK Mesa Hotels LLC; Mehulkumar Kantilal Ahir; and Kantilal Karsanbhai Ahir.

          Defendants did not file an opposition to the instant motion.

Entry of Judgment

Plaintiff shows sufficient grounds to enter judgment of $550,184.58 against Defendants. Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6, subdivision (a) provides in relevant part, “If parties to pending litigation stipulate, in a writing signed by the parties outside the presence of the court... for settlement of the case, or part thereof, the court, upon motion, may enter judgment pursuant to the terms of the settlement.” 

The parties entered a written agreement to settle the action. (Smalbach Decl., ¶ 4, Ex. 1 (“Settlement Agreement”).) The agreement requires Defendants to make weekly payments of $9,807.69 from September 29, 2025 to December 29, 2025, and weekly payments of $13,423.07 from January 5, 2026 until the full amount was paid. (Settlement Agreement, ¶ 1(d), (e).) Here, Defendants breached the agreement when they failed to timely cure a remaining deficit of a payment installment after it was rejected by the bank for insufficient funds. (Smalbach Decl., ¶ 8.) Since then, approximately 20 additional payment installments have been rejected by the bank, causing an arrearage to accumulate as reflected in Plaintiff’s merchant accounting statement attached as Exhibit 3. (Ibid.) Defendants have not made a settlement payment since April 14, 2026. (Ibid.)

The Settlement Agreement provides that if any party breached the agreement, the Court retained jurisdiction to enforce its terms under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6. (Settlement Agreement, ¶ 8.) Defendants defaulted under the agreement. The Court will enter judgment against them.

Attorney Fees

Plaintiff requests $8,442.50 in attorney fees and $60 in costs for bringing this motion to enforce settlement. (Richards Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) The settlement agreement provides, “If any Party brings an action or proceeding to enforce the terms hereof, or to declare rights hereunder, the Prevailing Party (as hereinafter defined) in any such proceeding, action, or appeal thereon, shall be entitled to all reasonable attorneys’ fees (in addition to all other costs and expenses incurred).” (Settlement Agreement, ¶ 9.) The Court has reviewed the time records submitted by counsel (attached as exhibit A to the Declaration of Richards) and determines that these attorney fees were reasonably incurred. The Court will award fees and costs in full.

Pre-Judgment Interest

Plaintiff requests $39,795.36 in prejudgment interest. (Richards Decl., ¶ 5.) This interest is calculated at a ten percent rate starting from the date of default. (Ibid.) The Court will award this amount. 

The Court will therefore enter judgment for $550,184.58 in damages, $39,795.36 in pre-judgment interest, and $8,442.50 in attorney fees, and $60 in costs, for a total of $598,482.44.  

Disposition

          Plaintiff Itria Ventures LLC’s motion to enforce settlement agreement is granted. The Court will modify and enter the proposed judgment Plaintiff submitted on May 8, 2026.


Case Number: 26STCV08156    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 510

No. 15 - Xanthe Petrossi v. Skyview Apartments LLC

          Defendant Skyview Apartment LLC’s Motion to Strike Portions of Plaintiff’s Complaint

Defendant Skyview Apartment LLC moves to strike 13 portions of Plaintiff’s complaint. Each challenged portion pertains to Plaintiff’s claim for punitive damages.

As an initial matter, the Court notes that Plaintiff’s opposition brief was untimely served on Defendant. Under Code of Civil Procedure section 1005(b), all papers opposing a motion must be served at least 9 court days before the hearing. Here, the opposition papers were served on Plaintiff on June 30, 2026, only 8 court days before the hearing. There is no explanation for this delay in Plaintiff’s papers. However, the Court will exercise its discretion to consider Plaintiff’s untimely opposition when ruling on this motion.

Courts may strike allegations that are “not essential to the statement of a claim or defense,” “neither pertinent to nor supported by an otherwise sufficient claim or defense,” or “[a] demand for judgment requesting relief not supported by the allegations.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 431.10(b).) Defendant moves to strike portions of the complaint regarding punitive damages. Courts may strike allegations related to punitive damages where the facts alleged “do not rise to the level of malice, oppression or fraud necessary” to recover punitive damages under Civil Code section 3294. (Turman v. Turning Point of Central California, Inc. (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 53, 64.) 

Here, the Court finds that Plaintiff alleges sufficient facts for punitive damages. The complaint alleges that Defendant knew of the safety requirements governing operation of the public pool in its apartment complex. (Compl., ¶ 42(a)-(b).) Despite having six months to inspect and remedy several safety defects, such as lack of signage and inaccessible rescue equipment, Defendant continued to operate the pool. (Id., ¶ 22(b).) Plaintiff alleges that Defendant made the decision “in the interest of maximizing profits while knowingly putting lives of tenants, residents, and persons at the SUBJECT PROPERTY in danger of severe injury or death.” (Id., ¶ 42(c).) It alleges “Defendants, in knowingly or recklessly creating said substantial risk and high probability of injury or death to tenants, residents, and other persons at the SUBJECT PROPERTY, was oppressive, despicable, highly reprehensible, and done in the conscious disregard for the rights and safety of tenants, residents, and other persons at the SUBJECT PROPERTY, including Decedent EDWIN PETROSSI and Plaintiff.” (Id., ¶ 42(f).)

The Court cannot conclude that, as a matter of law, these allegations do not suffice to constitute malice as “despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others” (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (c)(1)) or oppression as “despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person’s rights” (id., subd. (c)(2)). 

Defendant also argues Plaintiff does not allege sufficient facts to hold a corporate entity liable for punitive damages. Civil Code section 3294(b) provides that for a corporation to be liable for punitive damages, “the advance knowledge and conscious disregard [of an employee’s unfitness], authorization, ratification or act of oppression, fraud, or malice must be on the part of an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporation.” Under this provision, “punitive damages can properly be awarded against a corporate entity as a principal, because of an act by its agents, if the corporate employer authorized or ratified wrongful conduct.” (Pulte Home Corp. v. American Safety Indemnity Co. (2017) 14 Cal.App.5th 1086, 1124.)

Plaintiff alleges sufficient facts for ratification. Plaintiff alleges that the underlying conduct was ratified by “managerial employees of said Defendants, and were carried out with the consent of their officers, directors, and/or managing agents.” (Compl., ¶ 42(f).) Though lacking detail, this text constitutes a sufficient allegation of ultimate fact and suffices for liability under Civil Code section 3294, subdivision (b) at the pleading stage.

However, punitive damages are only recoverable under Plaintiff’s fourth cause of action for “survival action.” (See CCP § 377.34(a) [“In an action or proceeding by a decedent's personal representative or successor in interest on the decedent's cause of action, the damages recoverable are limited to the loss or damage that the decedent sustained or incurred before death, including any penalties or punitive or exemplary damages that the decedent would have been entitled to recover had the decedent lived . . . .”].) Punitive damages are generally unavailable for a wrongful death negligence claim. (See CCP § 377.61 [“In an action [for wrongful death], damages may be awarded that, under all the circumstances of the case, may be just, but may not include damages recoverable under Section 377.34.”].) Therefore, the Court will strike references to the recovery of punitive damages that appear under Plaintiff’s wrongful death, negligence-based claims.

Disposition

Defendant Skyview Apartment LLC’s motion to strike portions of Plaintiff’s complaint is granted in part.

The Court strikes the following allegations: the phrase “As such, the imposition of punitive damages against Defendants SKYVIEW and DOES 1 through 50, inclusive, and each of them, is appropriate” located in paragraphs 22(f) and 37(d) of the complaint. Defendant is to file its answer to the complaint within 21 days.
