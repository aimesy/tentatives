Tentative Ruling

Carlos v. Carter, Jr., Case no. 23VECV05371

Hearing date July 30, 2026

Defendant Carter, Jr.’s Motion for Court to Enter Costs Against Plaintiff – UNOPPOSED

Plaintiff security guard sues for assault, battery, and intentional infliction of emotional distress arising from an incident at defendant Carter’s home. The court imposed terminating sanctions on plaintiff on 4/29/26. See 4/29/26 Min. Order. Defendant moves for costs of $14,511. See 6/24/26 Memo. The motion is unopposed.

Per the court’s 6/10/26 judgment in defendant’s favor: (1) judgment is entered in favor of defendant and against plaintiff; (2) plaintiff shall take nothing from defendant on plaintiff’s claims; (3) defendant shall recover $29,225 in sanctions against plaintiff and counsel of record, jointly and severally; and (4) defendant shall recover his costs from plaintiff “by noticed motion.” See 6/10/26 Judgment.

Defendant filed and served a memorandum of costs as required by California Rules of Court rule 3.1700(a)(1). Decl. Difuntorum, para. 3, exh. B. The costs sought were reasonably incurred throughout the course of this 2.5 year-old case, which saw multiple discovery and sanctions motions. Id. The costs sought are unopposed. GRANTED. Defendant is awarded $14,511 in costs.


Case Number: 25VECV05964    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Sahagian v. Hyundai Motor America, Case no. 25VECV05964

Hearing date July 30, 2026

Defendant Hyundai’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Sahagian sues defendant Hyundai Motor America for violations of the Song-Beverly Act. Defendant demurs to the second, third and fifth causes of action in the first amended complaint. Plaintiff opposes.

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Defendant demurs to the second cause of action for violation of Civ. Code §1793.2(b). “Where those service and repair facilities are maintained in this state and service or repair of the goods is necessary because they do not conform with the applicable express warranties, service and repair shall be commenced within a reasonable time by the manufacturer or its representative in this state. Unless the buyer agrees in writing to the contrary, the goods shall be serviced or repaired so as to conform to the applicable warranties within 30 days. Delay caused by conditions beyond the control of the manufacturer or its representatives shall serve to extend this 30-day requirement. Where delay arises, conforming goods shall be tendered as soon as possible following termination of the condition giving rise to the delay.” Civ. Code §1793.2(b).

Defendant argues plaintiff failed to plead necessary material facts with sufficient specificity to sustain a section 1793.2(b) claim. “[S]tatutory causes of action must be pleaded with particularity.” Covenant Care, Inc. v. Superior Court (2004) 32 Cal.4th 771, 790. Plaintiff argues the broad allegations of the FAC sufficiently acquaint defendant with the nature of the claims.

The FAC alleges plaintiff “presented the Vehicle to [defendant]’s authorized repair facilities for repairs on multiple occasions during the warranty period, but continued to experience symptoms of the defects and non-conformities.” FAC, para. 13. The FAC alleges defendant “failed to commence the service or repairs within a reasonable time and failed to service or repair the Vehicle so as to conform to the applicable warranties within 30 days, in violation of Civil Code section 1793.2, subdivision (b).” Id. at para. 40. The FAC fails to allege when or to which location(s) plaintiff presented the vehicle for repair, the complained-of issues or the number of times plaintiff presented the vehicle. The FAC is insufficient and lacks the particularity required to allege a 1793.2(b) claim. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendant demurs to the cause of action for violation of Civ. Code §1793.2(a)(3). “Every manufacturer of consumer goods sold in this state and for which the manufacturer has made an express warranty shall:… (3) Make available to authorized service and repair facilities sufficient service literature and replacement parts to effect repairs during the express warranty period.” Civ. Code §1793.2(a)(3). Defendant argues plaintiff failed to plead the necessary material facts with sufficient specificity to sustain a section 1793.2(a)(3) claim. Plaintiff argues the complaint’s allegations are “enough to allow [defendant] to review its records and prepare its defense.” Opp., 1:10-11. This is not the standard for statutory claims.

The FAC alleges “[defendant] failed to make available to its authorized service and repair facilities sufficient service literature and replacement parts to effect repairs during the express warranty period.” FAC, para. 45. The FAC contains no allegations as to what facility or facilities plaintiff is referring, nor allegations specifying what service literature or replacement parts ought to have been supplied. Generalized allegations do not satisfy the particularity requirements for statutory causes of action. Covenant Care, supra. Plaintiff must plead, at a minimum, dates of attempted service, service location(s) and what literature or parts were improperly withheld to sustain a Civ. Code §1793.2 claim. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendant argues the breach of warranty claims are untimely and asserts allegations of equitable tolling are improper. See FAC, paras. 30-31. The court sustained demurrers to the second and third causes of action with leave to amend, and the remaining demurrer is not against a warranty cause of action. Thus, the court declines to consider general equitable tolling arguments at this time.

Defendant demurs to the claim for fraudulent inducement – concealment. Fraud, including negligent misrepresentation, must be pled with specificity. Small v. Fritz Companies, Inc. (2003) 30 Cal.4th 167, 184. Plaintiff must allege facts showing how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were made, and, in the case of a corporate defendant, the names of the person(s) who made the representation(s), their authority to speak on behalf of the corporation, to whom they spoke, what they said or wrote and when the representation was made. West v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 780, 793, citing Tarmann v. State Farm Mut. Auto Ins. Co. (1991) 2 Cal.App.4th 153, 157.

The FAC alleges defendant knew the lithium-ion battery was defective and intentionally failed to disclose this fact to plaintiff. FAC, para. 55. However, the FAC fails to allege with whom plaintiff spoke with at the dealership, their alleged authority to speak on defendant’s behalf, when, where and the substance of any such communications. This is insufficient.

Unlike most causes of action where there is a “policy of liberal construction of the pleadings,” claims involving fraud require particularity, that is, “pleading facts which show how, when, where, to whom, and by what means the representations were tendered.” Stansfield v. Starkey (1990) 220 Cal.App.3d 59, 73. The particularity requirement applies to every individual element of a claim sounding in fraud. See Cooper v. Equity General Ins. (1990) 219 Cal.App.3d 1252, 1262. Failing to allege the substance, when, where, by whom or by what authority are fatal to the fraud claim. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendant separately argues the fraud claim is barred by the economic loss rule. As the court sustained the demurrer to the fifth cause of action, the economic loss rule argument will not be addressed at this time.


Case Number: 26VECV01494    Hearing Date: July 30, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Merge Portfolio Owner v. Wilmington Trust National Ass’n., Case no. 26VECV01494

Hearing date July 30, 2026

Defendants’ Demurrer with Motion to Strike the First Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Merge Portfolio Owner, LLC sues defendants Wilmington Trust National Association and K-Star Asset Management LLC, alleging contractual fraud. Plaintiff obtained a loan from defendants, secured by commercial real estate; plaintiff defaulted. Plaintiff alleges defendants engaged in bad faith conduct following the default while the parties were working to resolve the default. Defendants demur to the first amended complaint and move to strike. Plaintiff opposes.

Defendants request judicial notice of: (1) underlying loan agreement; (2) pre-negotiation letter agreement; and (3) cash management agreement. GRANTED per to Evid. Code §452; Scott v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 743, 754.

Defendants’ Demurrer to the FAC

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Defendants demur to the cause of action for breach of contract – loan agreement. Defendants argue plaintiff failed to allege an actual breach of the contract. The FAC alleges defendants failed to respond within 10 days to inquiries about a new master lease agreement, despite being required to do so by section 4.14 of the loan agreement. FAC, paras. 22-24. The loan agreement states: “(g) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained herein, to the extent Lender’s prior approval is required for any leasing matters set forth in this Section 4.14, Lender shall have ten (10) Business Days from receipt of written request and all required information and documentation relating thereto in which to approve or disapprove such matter, provided that such request to Lender is marked in bold lettering with the following language: “LENDER’S RESPONSE IS REQUIRED WITHIN TEN (10) BUSINESS DAYS OF RECEIPT OF THIS NOTICE PURSUANT TO THE TERMS OF A LOAN AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE UNDERSIGNED AND LENDER” and the envelope containing the request must be marked “PRIORITY.” In the event that Lender fails to respond to the leasing matter in question within such time, Lender’s approval shall be deemed given for all purposes.” RJN 1, §4.14(g).

The FAC alleges plaintiff submitted the master lease to defendants for approval. FAC, para. 22. The FAC alleges plaintiff “substantially performed by repeatedly alerting Lender Defendants of the extreme urgency of the requested approval.” Id. at para. 23. The FAC contains no allegations that plaintiff complied with procedural notice requirements set forth in the loan agreement, i.e., proper capitalization or marking the envelope containing the request as priority mail. It is “proper to sustain a general demurrer because the petitioners [have] not alleged the happening of the condition precedent.” Daum v. Superior Court (1964) 228 Cal.App.2d 283. General allegations of substantial compliance via repeated notice are insufficient; plaintiff must allege specific performance in compliance with the terms of the contract to sustain a claim for breach of contract. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendants demur to the cause of action for breach of contract – pre-negotiation agreement. Defendants argue plaintiff’s allegations do not constitute an actual breach of the pre-negotiation agreement. The FAC alleges: “[d]efendants breached the terms of the PNA . . . by publicly filing [p]laintiff’s operating account bank statements and other confidential materials without filing them under seal in a January 2025 complaint.” FAC, para. 69. The FAC alleges this breach arises from a confidentiality clause in paragraph 1 of the pre-negotiation agreement. Id. at para. 65. The pre-negotiation agreement states: “…the Parties expressly stipulate and agree that both the content and the existence of statements and communications both written and oral among the Parties created during such Negotiations shall remain strictly confidential and protected.” RJN 2, para. 1. The pre-negotiation agreement further states: “…this paragraph shall not pertain to information which, in the absence of Agreement, Noteholder would be entitled to obtain, inspect or review pursuant to the Loan Documents.” Id.

Per the terms of the loan agreement, defendants are entitled to obtain, inspect and review financial and management information. RJN 1, §4.12(c). Per the language of the pre-negotiation agreement, plaintiff’s bank statements are not protected. The FAC fails to specify what, if any, other allegedly confidential information was disclosed. This is insufficient in the face of the judicially noticeable terms of the agreement to sustain a cause of action for breach of contract. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendants demur to the third and fourth causes of action for derivative claims for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. Defendants argue the allegations “do not go beyond the statement of a mere contract breach and, relying on the same alleged acts, simply seek the same damages or other relief already claimed in a companion contract cause of action, they may be disregarded as superfluous as no additional claim is actually stated.” Careau & Co. v. Security Pac. Bus. Credit, Inc. (1990) 222 Cal.App.3d 1371, 1395.

The FAC alleges defendants “did not act fairly and in good faith” (FAC, paras. 73-91) and acted in bad faith by “intentionally delaying responses to Plaintiff’s proposals, refusing to meaningfully engage with revenue-enhancing leasing opportunities, restricting funds needed for operation of the Property, maintaining accusations of misconduct after receiving contrary information, and using their contractual powers for the purpose of frustrating Plaintiff’s ability to realize the benefits reasonably contemplated by the Loan Agreement.” Id., para. 79. These are the same allegations of wrongdoing underpinning the breach of contract claims.

Plaintiff argues its allegations of breach of the implied covenant supplement, but do not duplicate, the breach of contract claims. See Carma Devs. (Cal.), Inc. v. Marathon Dev. Cal., Inc. (1992) 2 Cal. 4th 342, 372 citing Racine & Laramie, Ltd. v. Department of Parks & Recreation (1992) 11 Cal.App.4th 1026, 1031-1032.

The implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing “cannot impose substantive duties or limits on the contracting parties beyond those incorporated in the specific terms of their agreement.” New Hampshire Ins. Co. v. Ridout Roofing Co. (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 495, 504. The exercise of contractually granted authority cannot, as a matter of law, constitute bad faith. Id.

Per California law, defendants had no duty to agree to plaintiff’s requests or authorize plaintiff’s initiatives. See Sheen v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2022) 12 Cal.5th 905. Plaintiff fails to allege claims for breach of contract and fails to allege additional facts sufficient to show separate breaches of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. The allegations as pled are duplicative of the failed breach of contract claims. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendants demur to the fifth cause of action for intentional interference with contractual relations, sixth cause of action for intentional interference with prospective economic relations, and seventh cause of action for negligent interference with prospective economic relations. Defendants argue these tort causes of action are barred by Sheen, supra, as a matter of law and are insufficiently pled. “When a borrower requests a loan modification, a lender owes no tort duty sounding in general negligence principles to ‘process, review and respond carefully and completely to the borrower’s application.” Sheen, supra, at 922.

The FAC alleges defendants interfered with the “contract between plaintiff and plaintiff’s broker.” FAC, paras. 93-99, 101-109, 111-120. The allegations underpinning the fifth, sixth and seventh causes of action are that defendant K-Star employee, asset manager Morden, “made contact with one or more brokers affiliated with the Property and informed them that the Property was in special servicing.” Id. at para. 45. The FAC alleges the communication implied financial distress to plaintiff’s broker(s) which “immediately rippled through Plaintiff’s network.” Id.

The FAC contains no allegations that Morden’s communications were intended to interfere, nor allegations detailing how plaintiff’s contract or economic prospectives were harmed. Allegations that the alleged interference was intentional or caused an actual breach in a contractual relationship are necessary to sustain causes of action for negligent and intentional interference. See Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title Guaranty Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26, 55; Roy Allan Slurry Seal, Inc. v. American Asphalt South, Inc. (2017) 2 Cal.5th 505, 512. The FAC fails to plead necessary facts with sufficient particularity. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendants demur to the eighth cause of action for negligent supervision/hiring. The FAC alleges Morden was “unfit and incompetent to perform the work for which he was hired.” FAC, para. 123. The FAC alleges no facts showing: (1) defendants’ actual negligence in hiring, supervising or training Morden; (2) defendants knew or should have known that hiring Morden created a particular risk or hazard and that particular harm materialized; or (3) a causal relationship between defendants’ supervision and plaintiff’s unspecified alleged harm. See Delfino v. Agilent Techs., Inc. (2006) 145 Cal. App. 4th 790, 815. This is insufficient. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendants demur to the ninth cause of action for unfair business practices. Bus. & Prof. Code §17200 et seq. While a plaintiff may bring a claim under the UCL if a practice violates some other law, if the underlying or “borrowed” claim fails, so too does the UCL claim. Angels v. Westwood One Broadcasting Services, Inc. (2005) 129 Cal. App. 4th 1050, 1060. The court sustained defendants’ demurrers to each of the claims. Further, plaintiff fails to allege with specificity what “unfair, unlawful, and/or fraudulent acts” defendants committed beyond those already alleged in the prior causes of action. See Cansino v. Bank of Am. (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1462, 1475 (dismissing UCL claim against bank for lack of specific allegations). SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendants’ Motion to Strike

The court sustained defendants’ demurrer in its entirety; the motion to strike is MOOT.
