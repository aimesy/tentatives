Case Name: The Access Fund I, LP, et al. v. Dash Radio, Inc., et al. 

Case No.: 24STCV02743

Matter: Demurrer

Moving Party: Defendants Kevin Tsujihara and Scott P. Keeney 

Responding Party: Plaintiffs The Access Fund I, LP; The Norton Family Living Trust; PG 

Mountain Ventures LLC; Buh Dop a Dee Ventures, LLC; The 2001 Arthur 

Family Trust; and AKH Global, LLC

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Demurrer is overruled.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA 

CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.

 


On April 6, 2026, Plaintiffs The Access Fund I, LP; The Norton Family Living Trust; PG  Mountain Ventures LLC; Buh Dop a Dee Ventures, LLC; The 2001 Arthur Family Trust; and AKH Global, LLC filed the operative Fourth Amended Complaint (“4AC”) for (1) fraudulent misrepresentation, (2) breach of contract, (3)-(4) breach of fiduciary duty, (5) breach of duty of loyalty, (6) intentional interference with prospective economic advantage, (7) successor liability, (8) fraudulent transfer, (9) civil theft, and (10) violations of the UCL.

Plaintiffs allege, “In April 2022, Plaintiffs collectively invested $650,000 in convertible promissory notes in Dash Radio, Inc. [ ] pursuant to a Note Purchase Agreement. Plaintiffs’ investment was premised, in large part, on representations made that Dash Radio would have a robust business that included an online radio and a memorabilia authentication service. Instead, defendants drove Dash Radio into insolvency and misrepresented Dash Radio’s financial state thereby depriving Plaintiffs of their rights under the Note Purchase Agreement. In a clear breach of these representations, defendants, at the behest of defendant Scott P. Keeney [ ], took Dash Radio’s business and concepts and diverted those opportunities to other entities where Mr. Keeney had a financial interest and deprived Dash Radio’s investors and creditors of their investment. For example, defendants diverted Dash Radio’s online radio business to another entity for below market value, which substantially contributed to Dash Radio’s inability to repay Plaintiffs.” (4AC ¶ 1.)

Defendants Kevin Tsujihara and Scott P. Keeney now demur to the third, fourth, and fifth causes of action for failure to state sufficient facts.

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, and “treat the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.)  “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”  (Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  It is error “to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if the plaintiff shows there is a reasonable possibility any defect identified by the defendant can be cured by amendment.”  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist.¿(1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

Defendants contend that the 3rd, 4th, and 5th causes of action remain direct in substance because Plaintiffs continue to allege creditor-specific injuries and seek individual monetary damages.

Under Delaware law, determining whether a claim is direct or derivative depends on two questions: (1) Who suffered the alleged harm (the corporation or the suing stockholders/creditors individually)? and (2) Who would receive the benefit of any recovery or remedy? (Tooley v. Donaldson, Lufkin & Jenrette, Inc. (Del. 2004) 845 A.2d 1031, 1033).

The 4AC cures the prior pleading defect. Paragraphs 92, 104, and 117 explicitly allege harm to Dash Radio itself resulting from the Board’s decision to sell the corporation’s assets for less than 2% of their previous valuation earlier that year. Furthermore, the 4AC adds a dedicated prayer for derivative relief on behalf of Dash Radio, ensuring that recovery on these specific causes of action flows to the entity. The co-existence of individual claims or contextual background regarding creditor harm elsewhere in the pleading does not destroy the derivative character of these specific corporate injury claims at the demurrer stage.

Because Plaintiffs did not make a pre-suit demand on Dash Radio's board, they must plead particularized facts showing demand would have been futile.

Under United Food & Commercial Workers Union v. Zuckerberg (Del. 2021) 262 A.3d 1034, demand is excused as futile if at least half of the board members: (1) Received a material personal benefit from the alleged misconduct; (2) Face a substantial likelihood of liability on the claims; or (3) Lack independence from someone who received a material personal benefit or faces a substantial likelihood of liability. “If the answer to any of the questions is ‘yes’ for at least half of the members of the demand board, then demand is excused as futile.” (United Food & Com. Workers Union & Participating Food Indus. Emps. Tri-State Pension Fund v. Zuckerberg  (Del. 2021) 262 A.3d 1034, 1059.)

The 4AC identifies the 4-member Board at the time of the transaction as Keeney, Tsujihara, Burbank, and Zeisser, all of whom signed the unanimous board consent approving the asset sale. The 4AC specifically alleges that as part of the asset sale, each of the four directors was offered an exclusive opportunity to receive equity in the acquiring LITT Entities—a non-ratable benefit withheld from all other creditors and shareholders of Dash Radio (4AC ¶¶ 52, 86, 99, 111). Under Delaware law, an exclusive, non-pro-rata investment opportunity extended to approving directors in connection with an end-stage transaction selling corporate assets supports a reasonable inference of a conflict of interest sufficient to excuse demand at the pleading stage (Orman v. Cullman (Del. Ch. 2002) 794 A.2d 5, 23). Because the 4AC satisfies the first prong of Zuckerberg for a majority of the board, demand futility is adequately pleaded. 

For all these reasons, the Demurrer is overruled. An answer is to be filed within 10 days.

Moving party to give notice.

Case Number: 25STCV01127    Hearing Date: July 29, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 29, 2026

Case Name: Hartley, et al. v. Living Dead Media, et al.

Case No.: 25STCV01127

Matter: (1) Motion for Preliminary Injunction

(2) Demurrer

Ruling: The Motion for Preliminary Injunction is denied.


The Demurrer is sustained. Leave to amend is to be argued.


Plaintiffs to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


Cross-Complainant Living Dead Media, LLC (“LDM”) filed its Cross-Complaint on August 1, 2025, asserting ten causes of action against Cross-Defendants: (1) Declaratory Relief, (2) Malicious Prosecution, (3) Unfair Business Practices Under Cal. Civ. Code 17200, (4) Unfair Competition, (5) Intentional Interference With Prospective Economic Advantage, (6) Intentional Interference With Contractual Relations, (7) Violation of §43 of the Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. §1125 (a)(1)(A) – False Association, (8) Violation of §32 of the Lanham Act, 15 U.S.C. §1114 – Infringement, (9) California Common Law Trademark Infringement, and (10) DMCA Misrepresentation.

In its Cross-Complaint, LDM alleges that it is the exclusive owner of the intellectual property, trademark, and film development rights surrounding The Return of the Living Dead franchise, asserting that Cross-Defendants (including original cast members) breached underlying license agreements and engaged in an unlawful scheme to undermine LDM's commercial expansion. LDM claims that after it invested substantial resources to develop consumer merchandise, secure trademarks, and greenlight a new sequel/reboot motion picture, Cross-Defendants tortiously interfered with LDM's business operations by asserting unauthorized ownership over the franchise, contacting LDM's commercial partners and event organizers, demanding unpaid royalties, and improperly attempting to license their individual names and likenesses in connection with unauthorized movie projects and merchandise.


  1. Preliminary Injunction

LDM seeks a preliminary injunction to restrain and enjoin the Cross-Defendants (including original cast members) from producing, developing, or licensing any motion pictures or spin-offs, exploiting the title or elements of The Return of the Living Dead, and interfering with LDM's commercial licensees and planned movie projects.

“In deciding whether to issue a preliminary injunction, a court must weigh two ‘interrelated’ factors: (1) the likelihood that the moving party will ultimately prevail on the merits and (2) the relative interim harm to the parties from issuance or nonissuance of the injunction. . . . The trial court's determination must be guided by a ‘mix’ of the potential-merit and interim-harm factors; the greater the plaintiff’s showing on one, the less must be shown on the other to support an injunction.”  (Butt v. State of California (1992) 4 Cal.4th 668, 677-78 (citations omitted).)  “Plaintiff [or Cross-Complainant] carries the burden of proof and persuasion on these issues.”  (Drakes Bay Oyster Co. v. California Coastal Com. (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 1165, 1172.)

First, LDM fails to demonstrate a probability of success on the merits regarding its asserted film rights and exclusive franchise control. Public copyright records establish that the 1983 shooting script for The Return of the Living Dead was registered by Hemdale Film Corporation, and the 1985 motion picture copyright was registered to Cinema '84. In 2015, Cinema '84 assigned the motion picture copyright to Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Studios, Inc. ("MGM"). LDM does not allege in its Cross-Complaint that it owns the copyright to the 1985 film or the 1983 script. While LDM asserts rights to control movie production or sequels, the official chain of title resides with MGM.

Furthermore, LDM lacks trademark protection for movie production or distribution services. Records from the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office ("USPTO") confirm that LDM’s 2017 International Class 41 application for motion picture production services was refused and ultimately abandoned in 2018. While LDM holds registered marks for ancillary merchandise (e.g., cosmetics, beverages, costumes), it holds no active mark for film or entertainment services. LDM therefore lacks the legal standing to enjoin Cross-Defendants from utilizing film-related rights.

Second, an unreasonable delay in seeking emergency relief refutes the assertion of urgent injury. The record demonstrates that LDM was aware of the complained-of conduct as early as July 2024, yet waited approximately 16 months—until November 2025—to serve its motion for preliminary injunction. The balance of equities does not weigh in LDM’s favor. 

Accordingly, the Motion for Preliminary Injunction is denied.


  1. Demurrer

Plaintiffs and Cross-Defendants Beverly Hartley, Linnea Quigley, Thom Matthews, Miguel Nunez, Jr., John Philben & Alba Francesca as representative of James Karen now demur to the entirety of the Cross-Complaint for uncertainty and failure to state sufficient facts.

No opposition was filed.

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, and “treat the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.)  “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”  (Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  It is error “to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if the plaintiff shows there is a reasonable possibility any defect identified by the defendant can be cured by amendment.”  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist.¿(1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

LDM’s state law tort and unfair competition claims are preempted by Section 301 of the federal Copyright Act. Under the two-part preemption test, state law claims are preempted if: (1) the work at issue falls within the subject matter of copyright as defined in 17 U.S.C. §§ 102 and 104; and (2) the rights asserted under state law are equivalent to any of the exclusive rights within the general scope of copyright as specified by 17 U.S.C. § 106.

Here, the underlying subject matter—the 1985 motion picture, screenplays, and potential sequels or derivative works—falls directly within the statutory subject matter of copyright. Moreover, LDM’s tort claims allege that Cross-Defendants improperly interfered with LDM’s ability to exploit, license, or produce movie projects. Because these claims allege nothing more than unauthorized exercise of distribution, reproduction, or derivative creation rights without an "extra element" that qualitatively alters the nature of the action, they are preempted by federal copyright law (17 U.S.C. § 301). LDM cannot evade federal copyright requirements or its lack of chain of title by recharacterizing copyright claims as state law torts.

Even if not preempted, the claims fail on the merits as pleaded. Judicially noticed public records establish that MGM, not LDM, holds record title to the 1985 motion picture copyright, and LDM’s Class 41 USPTO application for film services was abandoned. LDM cannot establish the requisite business expectancy or enforceable legal right required for tortious interference claims regarding movie production. Additionally, LDM’s merchandise trademarks do not give LDM ownership over the individual actors' statutory right of publicity (Civ. Code § 3344). 

To the extent LDM bases its tort claims on cease-and-desist letters or communications sent to third-party event organizers and partners, those statements are absolutely privileged. Civil Code § 47(b) protects pre-litigation communications made in good faith to achieve the objects of litigation. (Rubin v. Green (1993) 4 Cal.4th 1187, 1194.)

For all these reasons, the Demurrer is sustained. Leave to amend is to be argued. The RJN are granted.

Matter: Demurrer

Moving Party: Defendants Beverly Hills Properties, LLC and Rochelle Sterling

Responding Party: Plaintiffs Eun Sung Koo and Brian Sung Jeong

Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


This is a landlord-tenant dispute.

Defendants Beverly Hills Properties, LLC and Rochelle Sterling now demur to the Complaint’s fourth, sixth, seventh, eighth, ninth, and tenth causes of action for uncertainty and failure to state sufficient facts.

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, and “treat the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.)  “A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. Therefore, it lies only where the defects appear on the face of the pleading or are judicially noticed. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”  (Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  It is error “to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if the plaintiff shows there is a reasonable possibility any defect identified by the defendant can be cured by amendment.”  (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist.¿(1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.)

The Demurrer is sustained as to the 6th and 8th causes of action, as Plaintiff concedes. Leave to amend is denied.

The Demurrer is sustained, without leave to amend, as to the tenth cause of action for declaratory relief as this claim impermissibly pertains to a past controversy. (See Osseous Techs. of Am., Inc. v. DiscoveryOrtho Partners LLC (2010) 191 Cal.App.4th 357, 366.) 

The Demurrer is sustained, with 20 days leave to amend, as to the claim for negligent misrepresentation for lack of specificity. There is no one allegation in which Plaintiff properly sets forth all of the “who, what, where, when, why, and how” of the representation.

The Demurrer is overruled as to the fourth cause of action for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, which is properly based on Defendants frustrating the benefits of the lease agreement by concealing known water/mold defects and withholding inspection reports. 

The Demurrer is overruled as to the derivative 9th cause of action pursuant to the UCL.

In sum, the Demurrer is sustained as to the 6th, 8th, and 10th causes of action, without leave to amend, sustained as to the 7th cause of action, with 20 days leave to amend, but otherwise overruled.

Moving party to give notice.

Case Name: Castrillo v. Cartpanda, Inc.

Case No.: 26STCV18168

Matter: Notice of Related Case

Ruling: The cases will not be related.


There is a notice of related cases as to 26STCV18168 and 26STCV19991, which are lawsuits by different individuals who claim they received spam from the same Defendant. 

The inquiry on a notice of related cases is whether the cases at issue (1) involve the same parties and are based on the same or similar claims; (2) arise from the same or substantially identical transactions, incidents, or events requiring the determination of the same or substantially identical questions of law or fact; (3) involve claims against, title to, possession of, or damages to the same property; or (4) are likely for other reasons to require substantial duplication of judicial resources if heard by different judges.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.300(a).)

The benefits of relation are minimal for the instant matter such that the cases will not be related.
