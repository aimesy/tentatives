CASE NAME:                        DIRECT COUNSEL, LLC, vs FARAHI LAW FIRM, et al.

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi

OPPOSING PARTY:             Plaintiff Direct Counsel, LLC

PROCEEDING:                      Demurrer with motion to strike

RULING SUMMARY:          Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi’s Demurrer to the First Cause of Action (Breach of Contract), Second Cause of Action (Breach of Implied Contract in the Alternative), Third Cause of Action (Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing), and Fifth Cause of Action (Quantum Meruit/Unjust Enrichment in the Alternative) is overruled.

 

Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi’s Demurrer to the Fifth Cause of Action (Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage) and Sixth Cause of Action (Fraudulent Concealment/Deceit) is sustained.  Plaintiff must seek leave to amend by motion if Plaintiff wishes to file a further amended complaint adding claims. 

 

Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi’s Motion to Strike is denied as to the Fifth and Sixth Causes of Action; granted as to Plaintiff’s prayer (paragraph 6 of the Prayer) for punitive damages without leave to amend; granted as to Plaintiff’s allegation (in paragraph 78) and prayer (in paragraph 5) for attorneys’ fees, without leave to amend; and denied as to alter ego allegations.

                                                                                                                                               

Background

 

On November 27, 2024, plaintiff Direct Counsel, LLC (“Plaintiff”) filed its Complaint against defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC (“Farahi Law”) and Justin Farahi (“Farahi” and, with Farahi Law, “Defendants”), alleging eight causes of action for (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; (3) Breach of Implied Contract; (4) Fraud; (5) Conversion; (6) Violation of Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200 et seq.; (7) Violation of Pen. Code § 496; and (8) Declaratory Relief.

 

On April 18, 2025, the Court sustained Defendants’ demurrer to the Complaint with leave to amend.

 

On April 28, 2025, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleging seven causes of action for (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of Implied Contract — in the alternative; (3) Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; (4) Promissory Fraud; (5) Quantum Meruit/Unjust Enrichment in the alternative; (6) Violation of Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200 et seq.; and (7) Declaratory Relief.

 

On July 16, 2025, the Court sustained Defendants’ demurrer to the First through Fifth Causes of Action with leave to amend, and the Sixth and Seventh Causes of Action without leave to amend.

 

On July 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed the Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”) alleging five causes of action for (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of Implied Contract — in the alternative; (3) Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; (4) Promissory Fraud; and (5) Quantum Meruit/Unjust Enrichment in the alternative.

 

On February 26, 2026, the Court overruled Defendants’ demurrer to the First, Second Third, and Fifth Causes of Action, and sustained as to the Fourth Cause of Action. The Court granted leave to amend as to the alter-ego allegations only. The Court also granted Defendants’ Motion to Strike regarding punitive damages.

 

On March 9, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Third Amended Complaint (“TAC”), alleging six causes of action for (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Breach of Implied Contract in the Alternative; (3) Breach of the Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; (4) Quantum Meruit/Unjust Enrichment in the Alternative; (5) Intentional Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage; and (6) Fraudulent Concealment/Deceit. The TAC, itself, states that the First through Fourth Causes of Action are those approved by the Court and the Fifth and Sixth Causes of Action are new. (See TAC at 1: 25-27.)

 

On April 10, 2026, Defendants filed this Demurrer with Motion to Strike.  No Opposition has been filed.

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

Before filing a demurrer or motion to strike, the moving party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to, in person or telephonically, to determine whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 430.41, 435.5.)¿¿¿ 

 

The meet and confer requirement¿has¿been met. (Decl. Farahi at ¶ 5.)

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A.    Demurrer

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are¿apparent¿from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.30(a); Blank v. Kirwan¿(1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿“To survive a demurrer, the complaint need only allege facts sufficient to state a cause of action; each evidentiary fact that might eventually form part of the plaintiff’s proof need¿not be¿alleged.”¿(C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School Dist.¿(2012) 53 Cal.4th 861, 872.)¿For the purpose of¿testing the sufficiency of the cause of action, the demurrer admits the truth of all material facts properly pleaded.¿ (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist.¿(1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-967.)¿A demurrer “does not admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.”¿(Daar v. Yellow Cab Co.¿(1967) 67 Cal.2d 695, 713.)

 

B.    Motion to Strike

 

Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof. (Code Civ. Proc. § 435(b)(1); Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1322(b).) The court may, upon a motion or at any time in its discretion and upon terms it deems proper: (1) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Code Civ. Proc. § 436(a)-(b); Stafford v.¿Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782 [“Matter in a pleading which is not essential to the claim is surplusage; probative facts are surplusage and may be stricken out or disregarded”].)

 

C.    Leave to Amend

 

“Where the defect raised by a motion to strike or by demurrer is reasonably capable of cure, leave to amend is routinely and liberally granted to give the plaintiff a chance to cure the defect in question.” (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon¿(2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1146.) “A trial court does not abuse its discretion when it sustains a demurrer without¿leave to amend¿if either (a) the facts and the nature of the claims are clear and no liability exists, or (b) it is probable from the nature of the defects and previous unsuccessful attempts to plead that the plaintiff cannot state a claim.” (Cantu v. Resolution Trust Corp.¿(1992)¿4 Cal.App.4th 857, 889.)¿¿ The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Goodman v. Kennedy¿(1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.)¿¿

 

DISCUSSION

 

A.    Demurrer

 

Defendants demur to all causes of action. The Court previously ruled that the demurrers to the First Cause of Action (Breach of Contract), Second Cause of Action (Breach of Implied Contract in the Alternative), Third Cause of Action (Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing), and Fifth Cause of Action (Quantum Meruit/Unjust Enrichment in the Alternative) were overruled. Plaintiff has now pled these causes of action as the First through Fourth Causes of Action in TAC. The Court has already ruled upon these causes of action and Plaintiff has pled them in reliance on the Court’s ruling. Therefore, the Court overrules the demurrer as to these causes of action.

 

1.     New Causes of Action – Fifth Cause of Action (Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage) and Sixth Cause of Action (Fraudulent Concealment/Deceit)

 

“Following an order sustaining a demurrer or a motion for judgment on the pleadings with leave to amend, the plaintiff may amend his or her complaint only as authorized by the court's order. [Citation.] The plaintiff may not amend the complaint to add a new cause of action without having obtained permission to do so, unless the new cause of action is within the scope of the order granting leave to amend. [Citation.]” (See Harris v. Wachovia Mortgage, FSB (2010) 185 Cal.App.4th 1018, 1023 [internal citations omitted].)

 

The law respects form less than substance. (Civ. Code § 3528.)  In Datig v. Dove Books (1999) 73 Cal.App.4th 964, 983 fn. 19, the Court allowed a party to plead a cause of action for recission of contract after having pleaded a cause of action for breach of contract as, under election of remedies doctrine, it is an alternative theory.  In Patrick v. Alacer Corp (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 995, 1015, the Court acknowledged the general rule for leave to amend, but found it inapplicable where new cause of action “directly responds” to trial court’s reason for sustaining a demurrer.

 

The Court has reviewed the new causes of action. They are based upon the previous Fourth Cause of Action in the TAC, though the name of the claim is no longer “promissory fraud.” However, the Court gave leave to amend only regarding the alter-ego allegations. These two new causes of action do not address the alter-ego allegations and, as such, exceed the scope of the Court’s leave to amend.

 

Accordingly, the demurrer to the Fifth and Sixth Causes of Action on the basis that they exceed the scope of the Court’s leave to amend is sustained.

 

B.    Motion to Strike

 

For the reasons explained above, the Court denies Defendants’ motion to strike the Fifth and Sixth Causes of Action.  Defendants’ request that Plaintiff’s prayer (paragraph 6 of the Prayer) for punitive damages be stricken is granted without leave to amend, because, as with the prior amended complaint, Plaintiff has failed to allege facts that would support a request for punitive damages.  Defendants’ request that Plaintiff’s allegation (in paragraph 78) and prayer (in paragraph 5) for attorneys’ fees be stricken is granted without leave to amend, because plaintiff has not alleged any contractual or statutory provision authorizing a request for fees.  Defendants’ request that alter ego allegations be stricken is denied, as Plaintiff has alleged sufficient additional facts to support the allegations regarding alter ego.

 

Conclusion

 

Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi’s Demurrer to the First Cause of Action (Breach of Contract), Second Cause of Action (Breach of Implied Contract in the Alternative), Third Cause of Action (Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing), and Fifth Cause of Action (Quantum Meruit/Unjust Enrichment in the Alternative) is overruled.

 

Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi’s Demurrer to the Fifth Cause of Action (Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage) and Sixth Cause of Action (Fraudulent Concealment/Deceit) is sustained.  Plaintiff must seek leave to amend by motion if Plaintiff wishes to file a further amended complaint adding claims. 

 

Defendants Farahi Law Firm, APC and Justin Farahi’s Motion to Strike is denied as to the Fifth and Sixth Causes of Action; granted as to Plaintiff’s prayer (paragraph 6 of the Prayer) for punitive damages without leave to amend; granted as to Plaintiff’s allegation (in paragraph 78) and prayer (in paragraph 5) for attorneys’ fees, without leave to amend; and denied as to alter ego allegations.

Case Number: 25STCV08158    Hearing Date: August 13, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 08/13/2026

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV08158

CASE NAME:                        KRISTINA I FRISCH, et al. vs FCA US, LLC., et al.

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiffs Kristina I. Frisch and Stephen B. Falk

OPPOSING PARTY:             Defendant FCA US, LLC

PROCEEDING:                      Motion to Compel initial disclosures prusuant to CCP 871.26

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiffs Kristina I. Frisch and Stephen B. Faulk’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures is granted in part as to the service manual related to the alleged engine defect, and otherwise denied.

 

Defendant FCA US, LLC is to provide the service manual within 10 days of this Court Ruling.

 

Sanctions of $2,500.00 are imposed on Defendant FCA US, LLC’s counsel of record, Lauren K. VanDenburg.  Sanction are to be paid to Plaintiffs’ counsel within 10 days of this Court Ruling.

                                                                                                                                               

Background

 

On March 20, 2026, plaintiffs Kristina I. Frisch (“Frisch”) and Stephen B. Faulk (“Faulk” and, collectively, “Plaintiffs”) filed a Complaint against defendants FCA US, LLC (“FCA”) and Glendale Chrysler Jeep Dodge Ram (“Glendale Dealer” and, with FCA, “Defendants”), alleging six causes of action for (1) Violation of Subdivision (d) of Civ. Code § 1793.2 against FCA; (2) Violation of Subdivision (b) of Civ. Code § 1793.2 against FCA; (3) Violation of Subdivision (a)(3) of Civ. Code § 1793.2 against FCA; (4) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability (Civ. Code §§ 1791.1, 1794, 1795.5) against FCA; (5) Negligent Repair against Glendale Dealer; and (6) Fraudulent Inducement – Concealment against FCA.

 

Plaintiffs allege that on or about April 13, 2023, they entered into a warranty contract with FCA regarding a 2023 Chrysler Pacifica with vehicle identification number 2C4RC1S70PR554976 (the “Subject Vehicle”), manufactured and/or distributed by FCA; the warranty contract included various warranties; defects and nonconformities o warranty manifested themselves within the applicable express warranty period, including but not limited to an engine defect; FCA had a duty to promptly offer to repurchase or replace the Subject Vehicle at the time it failed to conform the Subject Vehicle to the terms of the express warranty after a reasonable number of repair attempts; and FCA did not do so. Regarding Glendale Dealer, Plaintiffs allege that it negligently repaired the Subject Vehicle.

 

On June 3, 2025, Defendants filed a Demurrer with Motion to Strike. The Demurrer was sustained with leave to amend as to the Fifth Cause of Action (Negligent Repair) and overruled otherwise. The Motion to Strike was denied as moot.

 

On October 13, 2026, Hon. Nicholas F. Daum recused himself.  On December 05, 2025, the case was reassigned to Dept. 37, not Dept. 413.

 

On February 13, 2026, FCA filed its Answer.

 

On May 7, 2026, Plaintiffs filed this Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures Pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26 (“Motion to Compel”). FCA filed its Opposition on July 31, 2026.  On August 06, 2026, Plaintiffs filed their Reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

Claims against vehicle manufacturers under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (“Song-Beverly Act”) are subject to special discovery provisions when those manufacturers elect to be governed by Code Civ. Proc. § 871.20 et seq., implemented by the Legislature to address the growing number of cases related to Song-Beverly restitution or replacement of motor vehicles. (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.20(a).) The operative statute requires an initial disclosure and certain specified documents within 60 days of the filing of the defendant’s responsive pleading. (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26, subd. (b) [60-day requirement], subds. (f)-(h) [defining documents and information to be produced].) The statute further requires the parties to make available the plaintiff and the defendant, or if the defendant is not a natural person, the person who is most qualified to testify on the defendant’s behalf, for limited depositions within 120 days. (Id., subd.(c).)

 

The failure to produce as required under these provisions warrants the following sanctions, on an escalating basis:

 

(j) Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: 

 

(1) A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff's attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b). 

 

(2) A one-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($1,500) sanction against the plaintiff's attorney or two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney respectively, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the provisions relating to depositions as prescribed in subdivision (c). 

 

(3) For a plaintiff's repeated noncompliance with subdivision (b), (c), or (d), a court shall order the case dismissed without prejudice and the plaintiff's attorney to be responsible for costs awarded to the manufacturer. 

 

(4) For a manufacturer's or defendant's repeated noncompliance with subdivision (b), (c), or (d), a court shall order that evidentiary sanctions attach precluding the manufacturer or defendant from introducing evidence at trial regarding whether the motor vehicle had a nonconformity that substantially impaired the use, value, or safety of the motor vehicle, or whether the motor vehicle was repaired to match the written warranty after a reasonable number of opportunities to do so. 

 

(Id., subd. (j)(1)-(4).)¿

 

This section does not apply to a party who is not represented by counsel. (See Id., subd. (k).) 

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiffs’ argument that FCA has failed to comply with section 871.26 disclosures is based upon the following contentions: (1) that FCA has not provided verifications with its disclosures pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h); (2) that a simple VIN search returns the majority of mandatory disclosures; and (3) that FCA has failed to produce (a) complete DealerCONNECT records; (b) autopay claims; (c) trouble not found/unverified complaint claims; (d) LOP 85-41-XX-XX AND LOP 08-19-XX-40 records; (e) all applicable service manuals; and (f) complete records of Technical Service Bulletins (“TSBs”), Informational Service Bulletins (“ISBs”), Customer Satisfaction Notifications (“CSNs”), and Rapid Service Updates (“RSUs”) applicable to the Subject Vehicle.

 

FCA argues that the motion is moot because it has produced the required documents and, Plaintiff does not show that FCA failed to produce a required statutory category. FCA asserts that initial disclosures under section 871.26 are not tantamount to discovery through the Civil Discovery Act, and Plaintiff cannot use the statute to obtain broader discovery than the statute provides. As such, FCA argues that Plaintiffs’ reliance on Higginson v. Kia Motors

America, Inc. (2026) 118 Cal.App.5th 316 (“Higginson”) is misplaced, because Higginson concerns discovery under the Civil Discovery Act. FCA further argues that Plaintiffs’ demand for all records is overbroad and not required by section 871.26, and sanctions are unwarranted.

 

In reply, Plaintiffs identify the following categories as completely unanswered;

 

·       Category (1) Category 1 — Owner's Manual (§ 871.26(h)(1));

·       Category 9 Technical Service Bulletins (§ 871.26(h)(9));

·       Category 10 – Information Service Bulletins (§ 871.26(h)(10))

·       Category 12 — Warranty Policies and Procedure Manuals (§ 871.26(h)(12));

·       Category 13 — Service Manuals (§ 871.26(h)(13)); and

·       Category 17 — Warranty Extensions (§ 871.26(h)(17)).

 

Plaintiff contends the following categories are incomplete:

 

·       Category 6 — Motor Vehicle Information Reports / PDI (§ 871.26(h)(6));

·       Category 7 — Entire Warranty Transaction History (§ 871.26(h)(7)); and

·       Category 8 — Field Actions (§ 871.26(h)(8)).

 

Plaintiffs note that FCA has not objected based upon the need for a protective order.

 

FCA correctly points out that Higginson is not applicable to the issues Plaintiffs raise regarding FCA’s 871.26 disclosures.

 

FCA is also correct that section 871.26 does not include a requirement that disclosures be accompanied by a verification. Had the Legislature wanted to include a verification requirement in connection with the disclosures, it would have done so, as it did in connection with the provisions of the Civil Discovery Act.  Plaintiffs cite no applicable authority imposing a verification requirement for initial disclosures under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26.  And Plaintiffs’ citation to section 2016.090(a)(5) is misleading, in that the quotation Plaintiffs included in their Points and Authorities, that the disclosures ““shall be verified either in a written declaration by the party or the party’s authorized representative, or signed by the party’s counsel” omits the first part of subdivision (a)(5), which states:  “A party's disclosures under this section shall be verified . . . .”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 2016.090(a)(5) (emphasis added).)  Plaintiffs’ counsel needs to be more careful in the citations they provide to the Court to make sure that they are not misleading by omission. 

 

In light of the foregoing, the arguments surrounding TSBs, ISBs, CSNs, and RSUs based on lack of verification are not supported by authority. (See Mtn. at G.)  Further, Plaintiffs concede that all TSBs applicable to the Subject Vehicle have been produced.

 

Plaintiffs have not explained what disclosure categories listed in section 871.26(h) would require FCA to produce DealerConnect Report, AutoPay Claims, Complaint Claims as well as LOP records.  Therefore, their request for an order compelling disclosure of those documents is denied.

 

Plaintiffs also have not stated a basis for their request that the Court compel FCA to produce service manuals for any alleged defects other than the engine defect.  Plaintiffs alleged that the subject vehicle suffered from an engine defect. (Compl. ¶¶ 16-17.)  In their motion, Plaintiffs assert: “For a 2023 Chrysler Pacifica with alleged defects in the engine, transmission, and electrical systems, FCA must produce service manuals for each of these systems. FCA’s failure to produce service manuals for all three systems is a material omission.” (Mtn. at 12:21-23.)

 

Section 871.26 requires a defendant to produce “service manuals reasonably related to the nonconformities pertaining to the motor vehicle.”  (Code Civ. Proc. § 871.26(h)(13).)  FCA offered no evidence that it produced service manuals reasonably related to the alleged engine defect.  Its statement of compliance in its Opposition does not address service manuals, even those relating to the alleged engine defect. The Court grants the motion to compel for this category.

 

Plaintiffs seek $2,500.00 in sanctions.  Section 871.26(j)(1) of the Code of Civil Procedure provides: “Unless the party failing to comply with this section shows good cause, notwithstanding any other law and in addition to any other sanctions imposed pursuant to this chapter, a court shall impose sanctions as follows: [¶] (1) A … two-thousand-five-hundred-dollar ($2,500) sanction against the defense attorney …, paid within 15 business days for failure to comply with the document production requirements as prescribed in subdivision (b).”

 

In light of the noncompliance with regard to production of the service manual, as noted above, the Court imposes a $2,500.00 sanction on defense counsel of record, Lauren K. VanDenburg.

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiffs Kristina I. Frisch and Stephen B. Faulk’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures is granted in part as to the service manual related to the alleged engine defect, and otherwise denied.

 

Defendant FCA US, LLC is to provide the service manual within 10 days of this Ruling.

 

The Court orders sanctions of $2,500.00 against defendant FCA US, LLC’s counsel of record, Lauren K. VanDenburg. Sanctions are to be paid to Plaintiffs’ counsel within 10 days of this Ruling.
