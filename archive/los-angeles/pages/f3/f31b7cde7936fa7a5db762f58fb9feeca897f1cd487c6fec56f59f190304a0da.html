MOTION

Motion for Reconsideration

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Tiffany McBride

OPPOSING PARTY

Defendants Ernesto Soltero and Gabriela Robles

 

MOTION

Plaintiff Tiffany McBride (“Plaintiff”) moves for reconsideration of this Court’s order of March 23, 2026, in which the Court denied Plaintiff’s motion to enforce a settlement agreement.  Defendants Ernesto Soltero and Gabriela Robles (“Defendants”) oppose the motion.

ANALYSIS

Code of Civil Procedure section 1008, subdivision (b) requires a party seeking reconsideration to “state by affidavit what application was made before, when and to what judge, what order or decisions were made, and what new or different facts, circumstances, or law are claimed to be shown” in the current motion.  The moving party on a motion for reconsideration “must provide not only new evidence but also a satisfactory explanation for the failure to produce that evidence at an earlier time.”  (Mink v. Superior Court (1992) 2 Cal.App.4th 1338, 1342, internal quotations and citations omitted.)

Plaintiff argues, in essence, the Court’s ruling, as reflected in the March 23, 2026 order, is incorrect.  That is not a basis for reconsideration. 

Plaintiff maintains the Court did not address various authorities and evidence which were before it.  These authorities and evidence cannot serve as the basis for a motion for reconsideration, as they are not new or different evidence and authorities from those before the Court when the Court denied Plaintiff’s motion.  Moreover, Plaintiff has presented no authority for the proposition the Court is obligated to remark on everything the parties present to it in support and in defense of a motion, especially when the Court provides the legal and factual bases for its ruling.

Plaintiff also argues the Court did not permit her to present all her authorities at oral argument.  However, “Plaintiff expressly disavows any reliance on the hearing limitation as an independent ground for reconsideration.”  (Motion for Reconsideration, p. 16.)  Moreover, to the extent Plaintiff desired to introduce additional authorities in response to any argument made by Defendants, she was obligated to do so through her reply brief, otherwise she would be precluded for due process and fairness reasons to present them for the first time at the hearing of the matter.  

For these reasons, including Plaintiff not satisfying the requirements for bringing a motion for reconsideration in the first instance, the motion is denied.

Plaintiff is to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same. 

 

 


Case Number: 24STCV08210    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 415

   TENTATIVE RULING

 

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

July 28, 2026

CASE NUMBER

24STCV08210

MOTION

Motion for Summary Judgment, or, in the Alternative, Summary Adjudication

MOVING PARTY

Defendant Cummins Inc.

OPPOSING PARTY

Plaintiff Robert Reed

 

 

BACKGROUND

Defendant Cummins Inc. (“Defendant”) moves for summary judgment on Plaintiff Robert Reed’s (“Plaintiff”) operative first amended complaint, or, in the alternative, summary adjudication of the causes of action therein. 

However, Defendant has not “repeated, verbatim, in the separate statement of undisputed material facts” each issue on which Defendant seeks summary adjudication and identified “[e]ach supporting material fact claimed to be without dispute with respect to” each issue.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1350, subds. (b), (d)(1)(B).)

Accordingly, the Court denies the motion for summary adjudication as procedurally improper.  “There is little flexibility in the procedural imperatives of [Code of Civil Procedure section 437c], and the issues raised by a motion for summary judgment (or summary adjudication) are pure questions of law. As a result, section 437c is unforgiving; a failure to comply with any one of its myriad requirements is likely to be fatal to the offending party.”  (Brantley v. Pisaro (1996) 42 Cal.App.4th 1591, 1607.) 

Therefore, the Court considers the motion as a motion for summary judgment.  Plaintiff opposes the motion. 

LEGAL STANDARD – MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

“[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears the burden of persuasion that there is no triable issue of material fact and that he is entitled to judgment as a matter of law[.] There is a triable issue of material fact if, and only if, the evidence would allow a reasonable trier of fact to find the underlying fact in favor of the party opposing the motion in accordance with the applicable standard of proof.”  (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850.)  “[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears an initial burden of production to make a prima facie showing of the nonexistence of any triable issue of material fact; if he carries his burden of production, he causes a shift, and the opposing party is then subjected to a burden of production of his own to make a prima facie showing of the existence of a triable issue of material fact.”  (Ibid.)  

EVIDENCE

            Plaintiff’s objections to Defendant’s evidence are overruled.

DISCUSSION

The Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (Song-Beverly) applies to “any new product or part thereof that is used, bought, or leased for use primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, except for clothing and consumables.”  (Civ. Code, § 1791, subd. (a).) 

In the instant case, Plaintiff contends he purchased a 2021 Tiffin Allegro Bus with an engine which Defendant manufactured.  (See Declaration of Mikle S. Jew, Exhibit 5, p. 33.)  In response to Defendant’s written discovery, Plaintiff admitted Defendant repaired the engine and the vehicle “has not experienced any repeat occurrences” of the matters necessitating repairs between October 2022 and December 2022, and he has not sought additional repairs to the engine since the completion of repairs in December 2022.  (Id., Exhibit 1, pp. 8-10.)  Plaintiff also admitted he has used the vehicle for transportation purposes since it was repaired in December 2022.  (Id., Exhibit 2, pp. 7-8.)

With respect to Plaintiff’s breach of implied warranty of merchantability claim, Defendant’s evidence in this regard satisfies its burden to establish the engine is “fit for the ordinary purposes for which” Plaintiff uses it.  (Civ. Code § 191.1, subd. (a)(2); see also Declaration of Mikle S. Jew, Exhibit 3, p. 5.)  Accordingly, Defendant has shifted the burden to Plaintiff to raise triable issues of material fact as to whether the engine is not fit for ordinary use such that Plaintiff may maintain a claim for breach of implied warranty under Song-Beverly. 

Likewise, Defendant’s evidence meets its burden to show Plaintiff cannot prevail on the claim for breach of express warranty, because Defendant repaired any defects in the engine.  To prevail on the claim for breach of express warranty, Plaintiff must show “the manufacturer or his representative did not repair the nonconformity after a reasonable number of repair attempts.”  (Donlen v. Ford Motor Co. (2013) 217 Cal.App.4th 138, 152.)  Defendant’s evidence shows it successfully repaired the engine.  As such, Defendant has shifted the burden to Plaintiff to raise triable issues of material fact as to whether Plaintiff may prevail on the claim for breach of express warranty.

In opposition, Plaintiff advances only the declaration of Plaintiff’s counsel, David N. Barry (“Counsel”).  Counsel does not explain how he has personal knowledge of issues concerning the vehicle and the repairs thereto such that Counsel is competent to testify and authenticate documents.  As such, his testimony is inadmissible.  (Evid. Code, § 702.)  As Plaintiff fails to advance any admissible evidence, Plaintiff has concomitantly failed to raise triable issues of material fact as to whether Plaintiff can prevail on the claims in the operative first amended complaint.  Thus, the motion for summary judgment is granted.

Defendant is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.


Case Number: 24STCV15768    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 415

MOTION

Motion to Compel Further Discovery Responses

MOVING PARTY

Plaintiff Michael Hope

OPPOSING PARTY

Warren Lotus, LLC

 

MOTION

Plaintiff Michael Hope (“Plaintiff”) moves to compel further responses from Defendant Warren Lotas, LLC (“Defendant”) to Requests for Production of Documents, set one (“RPD”).  Defendant opposes the motion.

ANALYSIS

If the propounding party deems responses to requests for production of documents unsatisfactory, the propounding party may move to compel further responses. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310.)

In response to the RPD at issue, Defendant provided responses after objecting to the RPD individually and as a set, “leaving nothing to address in a motion to compel.”  (American Federation of State, County & Municipal Employees v. Metropolitan Water Dist. (2005) 126 Cal.App.4th 247, 269.)  Because Defendant responded to the RPD, the Court cannot compel a further response on the basis Defendant also asserted objections.  However, if Defendant withheld any responsive documents based on any applicable privilege, Plaintiff is entitled to a privilege log.  The Court, therefore, grants the motion to compel further responses to the RPD as to Defendant providing a privilege log.  (Hernandez v. Superior Court (2003) 112 Cal.App.4th 285, 291, fn. 6.)

Plaintiff also contends Defendant’s responses to the RPD are incomplete.  Ordinarily, Plaintiff cannot move to compel Defendant to further respond on the basis Plaintiff believes Defendant’s responses are such.  (See, e.g., Holguin v. Superior Court (1972) 22 Cal.App.3d 812, 820 [Court of Appeal notes with respect to responses to requests for admissions, a trial court cannot force a party to respond in a particular way if the party is willing to risk perjury prosecution or financial sanctions].)  Then, Plaintiff’s remedy is to offer Defendant’s allegedly incomplete responses as admissions or as prior inconsistent statements to impeach Defendant at trial, if Defendant seeks to introduce evidence it did not disclose in discovery. (See Evid. Code, §§ 1220; 1235.)

In the instant case, however, Plaintiff has shown, to the extent Defendant has not produced certain financial information such as income statements, balance sheets, tax returns, bank statements, general ledgers, and brokerage statements, the Court deems these are responsive documents to the RPD at issue, and they are reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence in the instant action based on Plaintiff’s claim of sustaining damages resulting from his purported 5% ownership interest in Defendant.  Any privacy concerns Defendant may have can be addressed by way of adherence to the Stipulated Protective Order into which the parties entered on October 28, 2025.  For any claim of privilege, as noted above, Defendant must provide a privilege log which identifies each document being withheld based on the asserted privilege, and the log must have “sufficient factual information for [Plaintiff] to evaluate the merits” of such privilege claims. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.140, subd. (c)(1).)  

In addition, if Plaintiff contends Defendant has not produced those documents it agreed to produce, Plaintiff must file a motion to compel Defendant’s compliance with its agreement to comply with the RPD under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031. (See Code Civ Proc., § 2031.320, subd. (a) [if party responds to a demand for production of documents and thereafter fails to produce documents in accordance with that party’s statement of compliance, the demanding party may move for an order compelling compliance].) For example, Defendant indicated in response to RPD No. 36, it would “produce the responsive, non-privileged documents in its custody, control or possession in the form of profit and loss statements and balance sheets from 2019 through 2023.”  (See e.g., Declaration of David H. Boren, Exhibit B, pp. 24-25.)  Defendant provides a substantially similar response to RPD Nos. 34 and 37.   Thus, if such documents have not been produced to date, Plaintiff’s recourse is to move to compel Defendant’s compliance with its stated commitment.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

            To the extent Defendant has not yet produced responsive documents, as discussed above, Defendant is to do so within 30 days of notice of this order, unless the parties agree to a different time frame.  If Defendant claims privileges regarding such production, defendant must also provide a privilege log with sufficient detail to allow Plaintiff to evaluate whether such assertions of privilege are meritorious.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.

 


Case Number: 24STCV25655    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 415

 

                  TENTATIVE RULING

 

DEPARTMENT

415

HEARING DATE

July 28, 2026

CASE NUMBER

24STCV25655

MOTION 

Motion for Protective Order

MOVING PARTIES

Defendants City of Los Angeles and Michael Pounds

OPPOSING PARTIES

Plaintiffs Ricardo Ramirez, Sr., Renee Villalobos, and the Estate of Ricardo Ramirez, Jr., by and through Ricardo Ramirez, Sr.

 

MOTION

            Plaintiffs Ricardo Ramirez, Sr., Renee Villalobos, and the Estate of Ricardo Ramirez, Jr., by and through Ricardo Ramirez, Sr. (“Plaintiffs”) brought the instant action against Defendants City of Los Angeles and Michael Pounds (“Defendants”) based on the death of Decedent Ricardo Ramirez, Jr. (“Decedent”).  Defendants move for a protective order regarding the deposition of Plaintiff Renee Villalobos (“Deponent”).

ANALYSIS

Plaintiffs argue Defendants’ motion is procedurally improper because Defendants did not file a separate statement.  No separate statement is required in connection with a motion for a protective order.  (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345, subd. (a) (Motion for protective order not listed among proceedings for which a separate statement is required].)  Accordingly, Defendants’ motion is proper.

Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.420 provides, “[t]he court, for good cause shown, may make any order that justice requires to protect any . . . deponent . . . from unwarranted annoyance, embarrassment, or oppression, or undue burden and expense . . . .” 

Defendants seek a protective order, requiring Plaintiffs’ counsel to “limit personal insults, attempts to annoy, harass, and general incivility towards defense counsel to the breaks during the deposition of Plaintiff Renee Villalobos, or any time the parties are ‘off the record’ so that the deposition can be completed.”  (Notice of Motion for Protective Order, p. ii.)  The Court cannot grant a protective order which imposes such vague limitations in advance of a deposition.  Likewise, the Court cannot impose a protective order which directs Plaintiffs’ counsel to “[r]efrain from speaking objections and coaching of the [sic] Renee Villalobos during testimony.”  (Ibid.) 

Defendants also seek a protective order to the effect “[i]nstructions ‘not to answer’ be limited to questions implicating privilege, privacy, or a legal contention.”  (Notice of Motion for Protective Order, p. ii.)  Counsel cannot instruct a deponent not to answer any question unless the question implicates a privilege.  (Stewart v. Colonial Western Agency, Inc. (2001) 87 Cal.App.4th 1006, 1014.)  The Court need not impose a protective order which simply restates the law applicable to depositions.  Accordingly, the motion for protective order is denied.

If Defendants seek to compel Deponent to appear and complete her deposition, Defendants must file a motion to compel under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.450, subdivision (a), which provides, in relevant part, if a party “fails to . . . proceed with [a deposition], . . .  the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony . . .  described in the deposition notice.”  The Court advises the parties the Court would grant such a motion, as the colloquy between the attorneys at the deposition was such the deposition could not continue..  (Declaration of Irving Estrada, Exhibit A, pp. 65-71.)  Under the circumstances, the Court would be inclined to impose sanctions against Plaintiffs’ counsel were Plaintiffs to oppose a motion to compel Deponent to appear and complete her deposition. 

Likewise, if Defendants seek to compel Deponent to answer any questions at deposition, Defendants must move to compel Deponent to answer deposition questions as outlined in Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.480, subdivision(a).  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.480, subd. (a).)

Nonetheless, the Court admonishes both parties and makes clear hereby its expectation they will resolve any and all issues concerning Deponent’s continued deposition testimony without any need for Court intervention. 

The Court declines to award sanctions to Plaintiffs for costs incurred in opposing the motion, while noting Plaintiffs’ counsel was combative and uncooperative in completing the deposition.  (Declaration of Irving Estrada, Exhibit A, pp. 59-60, 62-63, 65, 67-69.)  As such, the Court considers an award of sanctions to Plaintiffs would be unjust under the circumstances.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.420, subd. (h).)

Defendants are to give notice of the Court’s ruling and to file proof of service of same.
