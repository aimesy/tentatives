Eric Bonholtzer’s motion to be relieved as counsel is GRANTED, effective upon filing proof of service of the court’s order (MC-053).

 

Legal Standard

 

Motions to withdraw are routinely granted if made in the proper form and presented with proper notice. The court may issue an order allowing an attorney to withdraw from representation, after notice to the client.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 284(2).) 

 

Discussion

 

Eric Bonholtzer brings a motion for relief as counsel as to Plaintiff Elizabeth Sanchez. 

 

            The following documents are properly submitted: Civil Form MC-051 “Notice of Motion and Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” Civil Form MC-052 “Declaration in Support of Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel,” and Civil Form MC-053 “Order Granting Attorney’s Motion to be Relieved as Counsel.”

 

            The motion is GRANTED.

 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV17764    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

YAMATO CAPITAL FUND, LLC

                          

         vs.

 

ADOLFO CALLERES, et al.

                                         

 Case No.: 25STCV17764

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 27, 2026

 

 

Defendant Elevation Wellness Center’s demurrer to the third cause of action is SUSTAINED, WITHOUT LEAVE TO AMEND. 

 

On 6/18/2025, Plaintiff Yamato Capital Fund, LLC (Plaintiff) filed suit against Adolfo Calleres, Alejandro Calleres, and Elevation Wellness Center. On 3/10/2026, Plaintiff filed a second amended complaint (SAC), alleging: (1) breach of contract; (2) breach of implied warranty of good faith and fair dealing; (3) promissory fraud; and (4) declaratory relief.

 

            On 4/13/2026, Defendant Elevation Wellness Center (Defendant or EWC) demurred to the third cause of action.

 

Discussion

 

            EWC argues that Plaintiff cannot state a claim for promissory fraud, “as the fraud claim merely attempts to convert express contract terms and alleged breaches of a written Lease agreement into a separate fraud claim and further fails to allege each element of a fraud claim against EWC.” (Motion, 3: 6-9.)

 

            After review, the Court agrees.

 

            The Court previously sustained EWC’s demurrer to the third cause of action, finding that Plaintiff’s promissory fraud cause of action attempts to convert contractual terms and the alleged non-performance into fraud.

 

            Now, the SAC’s third cause of action alleges that Defendants Adolfo and Alejandro “orally represented to Plaintiff that they were officers and owners of EWC, and in that capacity, further orally represented on their own behalf and on behalf of EWC” that: (1) they and their company were experienced in the cannabis business; (2) they and their company had the financial means to enter into the Lease; and (3) they and their company had knowledge of all laws, codes, and regulations regarding the cannabis business. (SAC ¶ 24.) Plaintiff alleges these representations were false and that it would not have entered into the Lease had it known of the alleged falsity. (SAC ¶¶ 25–26.) Plaintiff further alleges that it “justifiably relied on the promises and/or representations of Defendants” based on the above representations. (SAC ¶ 27.)

 

“A person may not ordinarily recover in tort for the breach of duties that merely restate contractual obligations.” (Stop Loss Ins. Brokers, Inc. v. Brown & Toland Med. Grp. (2006) 143 Cal. App. 4th 1036, 1041 citing Aas v. Superior Court (2000) 24 Cal. 4th 627, 643.)

 

            Taken as true for purposes of a demurrer, each of these representations is fundamentally based on the same conduct underlying the breach of contract claim. The SAC’s three categories of alleged misrepresentations—concerning experience in the cannabis business (SAC ¶ 24(1)), financial means to enter into the Lease (SAC ¶ 24(2)), and knowledge of applicable laws and regulations (SAC ¶ 24(3))—are all covered by the express contractual provisions and requirements with respect to maintaining a valid lease and for payment under the Lease.

 

First, the alleged misrepresentation concerning experience in the cannabis business is covered by Lease provisions requiring Lessee to use the Premises only for the Agreed Use and to comply with all Applicable Requirements related to that use. (SAC, Ex. 1, ¶¶ 6.1, 6.3.)

 

Second, the alleged misrepresentation concerning financial means to enter into the Lease is covered by the Lease’s payment obligations. (SAC, Ex. 1, ¶¶ 4, 5.)

 

Third, the alleged misrepresentation concerning knowledge of all laws, codes, and regulations is covered by multiple Lease provisions. (SAC, Ex. 1, ¶¶ 2.3, 2.4, 6.3, 7.1(a).)

 

Given that this was Plaintiff’s second opportunity to allege facts which could show fraud separate and apart from breach of contract, the Court finds additional leave to amend would be futile.

 

Based on the foregoing, EWC’s demurrer to the third cause of action is sustained, without leave to amend. 

 

It is so ordered.

Case Number: 25STCV19352    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: 309

Superior Court of California

County of Los Angeles

 

DEPARTMENT 17

 

TENTATIVE RULING

 

JANIE ZAMORA

 

         vs.

 

NISSAN NORTH AMERICA, INC.

 

 Case No.: 25STCV19352  

 

 

 

 Hearing Date: July 27, 2026

 

Plaintiff’s motion to compel Defendant Nissan’s compliance pursuant to CCP section 871.26 is DENIED.

 

On 6/30/2025, Plaintiff Zanie Zamora (Plaintiff) filed suit against Nissan North America, Inc. and HGREG Nissan Buena Park alleging violations of the Song-Beverly Warranty Act.

 

On 4/8/2026, Plaintiff moved to compel Defendant Nissan North America, Inc.’s (Nissan or Defendant) compliance pursuant to CCP section 871.26.

 

Discussion

 

            Plaintiff moves for an order for compliance requiring Defendant to provide the complete production of documents and written disclosure of information required under Code of Civil Procedure subsections 871.26, subdivisions (b) and (h).

 

The enactment of Assembly Bill 1755 was codified in Chapter 12 of Title 10 to Part 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) (herein, Chapter 12), which went into effect 1/1/2025. (See CCP § 871.20, et seq.) In passing Assembly Bill 1755, the legislature enacted provisions that identified a series of written disclosures that a plaintiff must provide to all other parties within sixty days of the filing of the responsive pleading. (Code. Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (g).) Furthermore, the Legislature identified a series of seven discrete categories of documents that a plaintiff must also produce within sixty days of the filing the responsive pleading: (1) Sales or lease agreement. (2) Copy of current registration. (3) Any finance information, account information, including payment history and estimated payoff amount, and any loan modification agreements. (4) Any repair orders, including to third-party repair facilities or the location of where information relating to repair orders may be found. (5) Documents detailing all underlying claimed incidental damages. (6) Information pertaining to the market value of the motor vehicle that is currently in the consumer’s possession. (7) Any written, pre-suit communications with the manufacturer, including, but not limited to, any restitution or replacement request. (Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (f).)

 

In opposition, Defendant contends that there is nothing to compel as Nissan has made all code-compliant disclosure, including:

 

-         No. A – 2020 Nissan Sentra Owner’s Manual and Maintenance Information Booklet & Supplement (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000001-000561);

 

-         No. B – 2020 Nissan Warranty Information Booklet & Supplement (NNA_Zamora, J_AB1755_000562-000803);

 

-         No. C – Warranty Information Printout (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000804);

 

-         No. D – Product Brochure for 2020 Nissan Sentra vehicles (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000805-000812);

 

-         No. E – Factory Invoice (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000813-000815);

 

-         No. F – Monroney Label (Window Sticker) (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000816);

 

-         No. G – Vehicle Details & Service History (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000817-000820);

 

-         No. H – Repair Orders (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000821-000867);

 

-         No. I – DBS National Service History (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000868-000907);

 

-         No. J – Warranty Case Information (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000908-000915);

 

-         No. K – Consumer Affairs File (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000916-000933 – produced pursuant to protective order);

 

-         No. L – Technical Service Bulletins and/or Recalls for 2020 Nissan Sentra vehicles (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_000934-001507);

 

-         No. M – Service Manual for 2020 Nissan Sentra vehicles (NNA Zamora, J_AB1755_001508); and

 

-         No. AA – Nissan Assurance Products Resource Manual (APRMs) (NNA_CA_000001- 000397; NNA_CA_000698-001362; NNA_CA_001384-002051; NNA_CA_002672- 003346 – produced pursuant to protective order)

 

CCP § 871.26(h) specifies seventeen discrete categories of documents that must be disclosed automatically. The statute is categorical and content-based – not system-based. The Court agrees that Nissan’s production is sufficient, and there is nothing additional to compel.

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s motion for an order for compliance requiring Nissan’s compliance is denied. 

 

 

 

It is so ordered.
