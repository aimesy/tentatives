Plaintiffs Ky Jung Nam and NKJ Investment LLC (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) seek $1,453.48 in costs as the prevailing party pursuant to Civil Code section 1794(d). Under California Code of Civil Procedure § 1794(d), “If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action”

On June 10, 2026, Defendant filed this motion to tax $617.30 in costs. These costs include $113.25 in service of process fees related to Camino Real Chevrolet and $504.05 in electronic filing fees. Defendant argues that the cost to serve Camino Real Chevrolet is unreasonable because it is nearly three times the cost of that to serve Defendant. The fact that substituted service on Camino Real Chevrolet cost $113.25 while personal service of the summons and complaint on Defendant only cost $41.18 is not, by itself, evidence that the expense was not reasonably incurred. Rather, the expense was reasonably incurred because Camino Real Chevrolet was a named defendant and had to be served. Furthermore, Defendant provides no evidence of comparable costs to conduct substituted service on an entity and the increased cost is reasonable given the additional requirements for effecting substituted service. Therefore, the motion to tax costs is DENIED with respect to the cost to serve Camino Real Chevrolet.

Defendant also argues that Plaintiffs must identify the filings for which electronic filing fees are requested because there is no other way to tell whether those costs were reasonably incurred. On July 9, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition brief with a table itemizing the documents that were filed and the costs incurred to electronically file and serve them. The documents consist of proofs of service, case management statements and status reports, trial documents, and oppositions to Defendant’s motions in limine. While this list of documents seems, on its face, proper, Plaintiffs produce no declaration or any actual evidence. Accordingly, the motion to tax costs is GRANTED with respect to the electronic filing fees of $504.05.

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiffs’ costs are reduced from $1453.48 to $949.43.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND COMPLAINT

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

          Plaintiff Calvin Salmon seeks leave to file a Third Amended Complaint (“TAC”) that corrects the name of the actual defendant, conforms the pleading to evidence obtained in discovery, and adds causes of action arising from the facts of the same incident pleaded in the initial complaint. The motion is unopposed.  

The court may, in its discretion and after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading, including adding or striking out the name of any party, or correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect. (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).) A motion to amend a pleading must include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments and must state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted or added, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the allegations are located.  (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a).) The motion shall also be accompanied by a declaration attesting to the effect of the amendment, why the amendment is necessary and proper, when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered, and why the request for amendment was not made earlier. (Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 1.324(b).)

          Plaintiff argues that the TAC is necessary in order to amend the name of the defendant from CVS Pharmacy, Inc. to Garfield Beach CVS, LLC. The TAC also conforms the factual allegations to the evidence produced in discovery and obtained by subpoena (including surveillance footage produced by Defendants and records from the Los Angeles Police department) and adds punitive damages allegations. Plaintiff argues that the proposed amendments are timely because they are based on information obtained between January and April 2026, and this motion could not have been brought until the federal court remanded the action in June of 2026 and jurisdiction was returned to this Court.

          The motion is unopposed and it does not appear that allowing the filing of the TAC will cause any prejudice. Trial is scheduled for February 22, 2027, no new parties have been named, fact discovery remains open, and it is undisputed that the amendments are based on facts already within Defendant’s possession.

          Accordingly, the motion is GRANTED and Plaintiff is ordered to file the proposed TAC within 5 court days.

          Plaintiff to give notice.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER RE: DEFENDANT JIE PHILLIPS’ DEMURRER TO PLAINTIFF CHONG LIU’S COMPLAINT

 

Dept. 3

8:30 a.m.

I.      INTRODUCTION

         Defendant Jie Phillips (“Defendant”) demurs to the Third and Fourth Causes of Action asserted in plaintiff Chong Liu’s operative First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) filed on January 2, 2026. The Third Cause of Action is for fraud and the Fourth Cause of Action is for “fraudulent and unauthorized use of contractor’s license and permit system.”

II.     LEGAL STANDARD

A demurrer may be brought if insufficient facts are stated to support the cause of action asserted. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (e).) A demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the pleadings and will be sustained only where the pleading is defective on its face. (City of Atascadero v. Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 445, 459.)

III.    DISCUSSION

          Plaintiff’s Third and Fourth Causes of Action are both rooted in fraud. The elements of fraud are misrepresentation, knowledge of falsity, intent to induce reliance on the misrepresentation, justifiable reliance on the misrepresentation, and resulting damages. (Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, 638 (Lazar).)

A.   Third Cause of Action

Plaintiff alleges that on March 22, 2025, Defendant informed him that he was not permitted to enter the jobsite in Lancaster, California. (FAC, ¶ C(11).) Plaintiff alleges that despite excluding him from the job site, Defendant demanded he continue to perform under the existing contract for purposes of passing the city’s inspection on March 27, 2025, of the drywall, ceiling tiles, T-bar ceiling components, HVAC, and related work. (FAC, ¶ C(12).) Defendant allegedly promised that if the inspection passed, she would pay him the money owed on the construction contract. (Id., ¶ C(14).) Plaintiff alleges he arranged and attended the inspection in reliance on Defendant’s promise of payment, but was damaged when Defendant did not pay Plaintiff. (Id., ¶¶ C(14-15).

Defendant argues that Plaintiff cannot state a fraud claim because Plaintiff was already contractually obligated to attend the inspection and cannot prove that any damages he sustained were caused by his attendance. (Demurrer, pp. 6-8.) In opposition, Plaintiff argues that his damages extend beyond the payments owed under the contract and include “contractor-of-record exposure”, “permit and inspection-related risks”, “potential attribution of later construction work or corrective work to Plaintiff”, and “CSLB complaints and regulatory exposure.” (Opp., p. 6.) These additional damages are speculative and Plaintiff has not alleged specific facts showing that any of those alleged risks resulted in any actual damages. Plaintiff’s damages arising from the contract would exist even in the absence of Defendant’s fraudulent inducement and Plaintiff cannot, as a matter of law, assert a fraud claim based on this theory. (Rutherford Holdings, LLC v. Plaza Del Rey (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 221, 235; Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [burden on plaintiff to show manner of amendment].) Accordingly, the demurrer to the Third Cause of Action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

B.   Fourth Cause of Action

As part of his Fourth Cause of Action for fraudulent and unauthorized use of contractor’s license and permit system, Plaintiff alleges that Defendant “exploit[ed] [his] contractor license, permit status, and permit chain to advance construction and inspection.” (FAC, ¶ A(3).) Plaintiff alleges he was placed into “factual and legal exposure for permit responsibility, regulatory compliance, and governmental oversight for work [he] did not authorize, did not control, and could not supervise.” (FAC, ¶ A(4).)

Defendant demurs to this cause of action as legally incognizable. The Court agrees. First, any fraudulent misrepresentation by Defendant regarding the permits was not made to Plaintiff, therefore he did not rely on any misrepresentation and could not have sustained damages based on those misrepresentations. Also, Plaintiff does not allege that he actually sustained damages from Defendant’s conduct.

In opposition, Plaintiff concedes that the label of the claim might need to be amended, but insists that even if the demurrer is sustained, the Court should keep the factual allegations underlying the Fourth Cause of Action so that it can support his other claims. However, Plaintiff does not show how these factual allegations are relevant to pleading the elements of his other causes of action. To the extent Plaintiff argues that he needs to incorporate the allegations into Plaintiff’s Fifth Cause of Action against JLF Construction LLC for tortious interference, that claim is is already four pages long. Plaintiff also requests leave to amend but provides no facts or legal authority showing how amendment is possible. (Goodman, supra, 18 Cal.3d at p. 349.)

Accordingly, the demurrer to the Fourth Cause of Action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

IV.    CONCLUSION

Defendant’s demurrer to the Third and Fourth Causes of Action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend. If Plaintiff wishes to add to his Fifth Cause of Action, he may seek leave to file a Second Amended Complaint or stipulate with the other parties for leave to do so.

Moving party to give notice.
