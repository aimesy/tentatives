DEPARTMENT N LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 22SMCV02738    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

 

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgement is DENIED. Alternatively, the Motion for Partial Judgment Notwithstanding the Verdict is also DENIED.

 

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. to give notice.

 

REASONING

 

Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc. (“Defendant”) moves for an order setting aside the second amended judgment (the “Judgment”) entered on May 4, 2026 and entering judgment that provides for the return of the Subject Vehicle to Defendant with clear title. Alternatively, Defendant moves for an order granting partial judgment notwithstanding the verdict and amending the Judgment to require the return of the Subject Vehicle with clear title.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff Teresa Zarate Gomez (“Gomez”) contends that the Court lacks jurisdiction to rule on the present motion because she filed an appeal of the Judgment on May 18, 2026. The Court agrees.

 

Code of Civil Procedure Section 916, subdivision (a), which states: “Except as provided in Sections 917.1 to 917.9, inclusive, and in Section 116.810, the perfecting of an appeal stays proceedings in the trial court upon the judgment or order appealed from or upon the matters embraced therein or affected thereby, including enforcement of the judgment or order, but the trial court may proceed upon any other matter embraced in the action and not affected by the judgment or order.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 916, subd. (a).) “The purpose of the automatic stay rule is ‘to protect the appellate court’s jurisdiction by preserving the status quo until the appeal is decided. The rule prevents the trial court from rendering an appeal futile by altering the appealed judgment or order by conducting other proceedings that may affect it. [Citation.]’ ” (Dowling v. Zimmerman (2001) 85 Cal.App.4th 1400, 1428.)

 

“To accomplish this purpose, section 916, subdivision (a) stays all further trial court proceedings ‘upon the matters embraced’ in or ‘affected’ by the appeal. In determining whether a proceeding is embraced in or affected by the appeal, we must consider the appeal and its possible outcomes in relation to the proceeding and its possible results. [W]hether a matter is ‘embraced’ in or ‘affected’ by a judgment [or order] within the meaning of [section 916] depends on whether postjudgment [or postorder] proceedings on the matter would have any effect on the ‘effectiveness' of the appeal.” (Varian Medical Systems, Inc. v. Delfino (2005) 35 Cal.4th 180, 189.) “If so, the proceedings are stayed; if not, the proceedings are permitted.” (Id.) “The fact that the postjudgment or postorder proceeding may render the appeal moot is not, by itself, enough to establish that the proceeding affects the effectiveness of the appeal and should be stayed under section 916. Rather, something more is needed. For example, the trial court proceeding must directly or indirectly seek to ‘enforce, vacate or modify [the] appealed judgment or order.’ ” (Id.) “Or the proceeding must substantially interfere with the appellate court’s ability to conduct the appeal.” (Id. at 190.)

 

“By contrast, an appeal does not stay proceedings on ‘ancillary or collateral matters which do not affect the judgment [or order] on appeal’ even though the proceedings may render the appeal moot.” (Varian Medical Systems, Inc., supra, 35 Cal.4th 191.) “A postjudgment or postorder proceeding is also ancillary or collateral to the appeal despite its potential effect on the appeal, if the proceeding could or would have occurred regardless of the outcome of the appeal.” (Id.) However, “[t]he automatic stay rule is subject to certain exceptions.” (Id.)

 

With respect to a motion for judgment notwithstanding the verdict, an appeal does not stay the proceedings. (See Foggy v. Ralph F. Clark & Associates, Inc. (1987) 192 Cal.App.3d 1204, 1213 [“[A] motion for judgment notwithstanding the verdict is, just as a motion for new trial, a matter collateral to the judgment. This ruling, just as the Legislature intended, enables the trial court to consider both a motion for judgment notwithstanding the verdict and a motion for new trial at the same time and avoids what would otherwise be the absurd result of depriving a party of a post-judgment remedy authorized by the Legislature.”].) However, “[t]he trial court’s power to grant a motion for JNOV [judgment notwithstanding the verdict] is the same as its power to grant a directed verdict.” (Kephart v. Genuity, Inc. (2006) 136 Cal.App.4th 280, 289.) “The court must accept as true the evidence supporting the jury’s verdict, disregarding all conflicting evidence and indulging in every legitimate inference that may be drawn in support of the judgment. The court may grant the motion only if there is no substantial evidence to support the verdict.” (Id.; see also Stubblefield Construction Co. v. City of San Bernardino (1995) 32 Cal.App.4th 687, 703.) Defendant raises no argument that evidence presented at trial does not support the jury’s verdict. Instead, Defendant seeks to amend the judgment to affirmatively order Plaintiff to return the Subject Vehicle with clear title, arguing that Plaintiff is required to return the Subject Vehicle to avail herself to the restitution remedy under the Song-Beverly Act (“SBA”).

 

Likewise, there is no indication that the holding in Foggy applies to motions made under Code of Civil Procedure Section 663 and/or 663a. In fact, the motion fails to cite to any authority to support such assertion. As such, the relief sought by the present motion constitute ‘matters embraced’ in or ‘affected’ by the pending appeal. Even assuming arguendo that a motion per Code of Civil Procedure Section 663 and/or 663a were deemed ancillary or collateral matters not affected by appeal, the present motion would still fail. “A judgment or decree, when based upon a decision by the court, or the special verdict of a jury, may, upon motion of the party aggrieved, be set aside and vacated by the same court, and another and different judgment entered, for either of the following causes, materially affecting the substantial rights of the party and entitling the party to a different judgment:

1. Incorrect or erroneous legal basis for the decision, not consistent with or not supported by the facts; and in such case when the judgment is set aside, the statement of decision shall be amended and corrected.

2. A judgment or decree not consistent with or not supported by the special verdict.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 663.) Here, the jury never determined whether or not Plaintiff was required to return the Subject Vehicle with clear title to Defendant. (See Special Verdict, 11/3/25; Plf. Second Amended Judgment, 5/4/26.) Although this Court found that the return of the Subject Vehicle is consistent with the general policy of restoring Plaintiff to her position prior to the purchase of the Subject Vehicle, it never made an explicit finding that Plaintiff was required to return the Subject Vehicle in connection with obtaining a restitution remedy. (Min. Order, 3/3/26.) Contrarily, Plaintiff was merely ordered to prepare a new proposed judgment in accordance with the Court’s finding. (Min. Order, 3/3/26.) The Judgment, which expressly states: “Return of vehicle with clear title and free of lien shall be determined by post judgment motion” was the result of the March 3, 2026 ruling.

 

Based on the foregoing, Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.’s Motion to Set Aside/Vacate Judgement is DENIED. Alternatively, the Motion for Partial Judgment Notwithstanding the Verdict is also DENIED.

 


Case Number: 25SMCV06574    Hearing Date: July 16, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC’s Demurrer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC shall prepare, serve, and submit a proposed judgment as per statute.

Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC to give notice.

REASONING

Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC (“Defendant”) demurs to each claim stated in Plaintiff Anthony Jones (“Plaintiff”)’s First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) on the grounds that they fail to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action against Defendant.

Legal Standard
“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967), but the Court does not “assume the truth of contentions, deductions, or conclusions of fact or law” (Moore v. Regents of University of California (1990) 51 Cal.3d 120, 125).

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (See Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349 [court shall not “sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment”]; Kong v. City of Hawaiian Gardens Redevelopment Agency (2002) 108 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1037 [“A demurrer should not be sustained without leave to amend if the complaint, liberally construed, can state a cause of action under any theory or if there is a reasonable possibility the defect can be cured by amendment.”]; Vaccaro v. Kaiman (1998) 63 Cal.App.4th 761, 768 [“When the defect which justifies striking a complaint is capable of cure, the court should allow leave to amend.”].) The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)

First Cause of Action: Equitable Accounting
“A cause of action for an accounting requires a showing that a relationship exists between the plaintiff and defendant that requires an accounting, and that some balance is due the plaintiff that can only be ascertained by an accounting. An action for accounting is not available where the plaintiff alleges the right to recover a sum certain or a sum that can be made certain by calculation.” (Teselle v. McLoughlin (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 156, 179, citations and paragraph break omitted.)

In the first cause of action, Plaintiff alleges that Defendant received and controls the records relating to Plaintiff’s payments on a specified account and the application of those payments, and Plaintiff cannot determine how his payments were applied without a court-ordered accounting, such that an accounting is necessary to determine how payments were applied and whether Defendant has obtained or seeks a double recovery. (FAC ¶¶ 20-23.) It follows that Plaintiff’s accounting claim is simply one seeking information about the application of his payments, not that he is owed any money by Defendant or that they wrongfully applied his payments, i.e., Plaintiff fails to allege that some balance is due to him. Plaintiff’s opposition sheds no light on why this claim should proceed. Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the first cause of action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Second Cause of Action: Restitution / Unjust Enrichment
“The elements for a claim of unjust enrichment are receipt of a benefit and unjust retention of the benefit at the expense of another. The theory of unjust enrichment requires one who acquires a benefit which may not justly be retained, to return either the thing or its equivalent to the aggrieved party so as not to be unjustly enriched.” (Lyles v. Sangadeo-Patel (2014) 225 Cal.App.4th 759, 769, quotation marks and citations omitted.)

In the second cause of action, Plaintiff alleges that Defendant knowingly accepted payments from Plaintiff, a non-lessee and nonobligor, and Defendant is now asserting the right to retain both the vehicle and Plaintiff’s funds without providing credit, set-off, or accounting, such that retention of both the collateral and Plaintiff’s payments is inequitable and unjust. (FAC ¶¶ 25-26.) Notably, “[u]njust enrichment is not a cause of action”; it is simply “a restitution claim.” (Hill v. Roll International Corp. (2011) 195 Cal.App.4th 1295, 1307; see also Melchior v. New Line Productions, Inc. (2003) 106 Cal.App.4th 779, 793 [“there is no cause of action in California for unjust enrichment”].) Moreover, Plaintiff alleges that he voluntarily made the payments to Defendant on a third party’s lease for a vehicle (FAC ¶ 9), and it is unclear why Defendant’s retention of those payments would be unjust, particularly where Plaintiff appears to assert that the account was terminated for nonpayment (FAC ¶ 13). Further, given that Plaintiff alleges he was not a party to the lease agreement, there is no basis to conclude that he has standing to assert an unjust enrichment claim as it relates to the vehicle. Thus, Defendant’s demurrer to the second cause of action is also SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Third Cause of Action: Declaratory Relief
“To qualify for declaratory relief, a party would have to demonstrate its action presented two essential elements: (1) a proper subject of declaratory relief, and (2) an actual controversy involving justiciable questions relating to the party’s rights or obligations.” (Jolley v. Chase Home Finance, LLC (2013) 213 Cal.App.4th 872, 909, quotation marks and brackets omitted.)

A cause of action for declaratory relief should not be used as a second cause of action for the determination of identical issues raised in another cause of action. (General of America Insurance Co. v. Lilly (1968) 258 Cal.App.2d 465, 470.) “The availability of another form of relief that is adequate will usually justify refusal to grant declaratory relief” (California Insurance Guarantee Association v. Superior Court (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 1617, 1624), and a duplicative cause of action is subject to demurrer (Palm Springs Villas II Homeowners Association, Inc. v. Parth (2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 268, 290).

In the third cause of action, Plaintiff seeks a judicial declaration as to whether Defendant may retain Plaintiff’s payments after termination when Plaintiff is not the lessee or obligor, whether Defendant must provide a complete accounting, and whether Defendant must credit Plaintiff’s payments against the vehicle’s present value and avoid double recovery. (FAC ¶ 29.) For the reasons stated above, the nature of Plaintiff’s claim remains unclear, i.e., Plaintiff fails to allege any facts showing wrongful retention of the lease payments made by Plaintiff or that he has standing to challenge Defendant’s retention of the vehicle due to nonpayment. Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the third cause of action is also SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Fourth Cause of Action: Conversion – Money – Alternative
“Conversion is the wrongful exercise of dominion over the property of another. The elements of a conversion claim are: (1) the plaintiff’s ownership or right to possession of the property; (2) the defendant’s conversion by a wrongful act or disposition of property rights; and (3) damages.” (Lee v. Hanley (2015) 61 Cal.4th 1225, 1240.)

In the fourth cause of action, Plaintiff alleges that he paid money directly to Defendant for the vehicle and the account, Defendant exercised dominion and control over Plaintiff’s funds by retaining them under a forfeiture and termination theory lacking lawful justification as applied to a non-lessee, and Defendant’s conduct substantially interfered with Plaintiff’s property rights in his funds. (FAC ¶¶ 31-33.) Again, though, Plaintiff alleges that he made payments on a third party’s account voluntarily (FAC ¶ 9), and there are no facts showing that Defendant wrongfully retained those funds when they were said to be for payments owed on the account. It would seem that Plaintiff takes issue with paying more than the market value for the vehicle in these payments (FAC ¶¶ 15-16), but this is not a basis for a conversion claim. Thus, Defendant’s demurrer to the fourth cause of action is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Fifth Cause of Action: Unfair Business Practices – Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200
To set forth a claim for a violation of Business and Professions Code section 17200, Plaintiff must establish Defendant was engaged in an “unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business act or practice and unfair, deceptive, untrue or misleading advertising” and certain specific acts. (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 17200.) A cause of action for unfair competition “is not an all-purpose substitute for a tort or contract action.” (Cortez v. Purolator Air Filtration Products Co. (2000) 23 Cal.4th 163, 173.)

In the fifth cause of action, Plaintiff alleges that Defendant engaged in unlawful and unfair business acts by accepting substantial third-party payments from non-obligors, refusing transparent accounting of payment application and credits, and asserting retention of both collateral and third-party funds without lawful crediting practices. (FAC ¶ 35.) The nature of any unlawful business practice is unclear because Plaintiff alleges that he voluntarily paid on a third party’s account, there is no legal authority stating that an entity like Defendant, or any other recipient, cannot accept, retain, and credit a payment made on an account by a third party, and there is no basis to conclude that Defendant wrongfully retained the payments or the vehicle, for the reasons stated above. Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the fifth cause of action is also SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

For the reasons stated herein, Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC’s Demurrer to Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend. Defendant Mercedes-Benz Financial Services USA LLC shall prepare, serve, and submit a proposed judgment as per statute.
