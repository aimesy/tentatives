DEPARTMENT 735 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV22136    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 735

Beverly Hills View, Inc. v. Visiontech Group, Inc.

Plaintiff/Cross Defendant Beverly Hills View, Inc.’s Motion to Compel Deposition of Cross-Complainant Visiontech Group, Inc.’s Person Most Knowledgeable

BACKGROUND 

On August 29, 2024, Plaintiff Beverly Hills View, Inc. filed a complaint against Defendant Visiontech Group, Inc., asserting causes of action for (1) breach of contract; (2) fraud; and (3) breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. Plaintiff alleges it purchased LED growing lights for marijuana cultivation from Defendant that did not function.

On November 4, 2024, Visiontech filed a cross-complaint against Beverly Hills View, Inc., asserting causes of action for (1) breach of contract; and (2) goods rendered.

DISCUSSION

Beverly Hills View (“BHV”) moves to compel the deposition of Visiontech’s person most knowledgeable. BHV served a deposition notice on January 2, 2025, with a deposition date of January 31, 2025. BHV asserts that Visiontech’s person most knowledgeable did not appear for the January 31, 2024 deposition. BHV then noticed a second deposition on March 5, 2025, for April 1, 2025, and a third deposition on May 7, 2025, for June 5, 2025. Visiontech’s PMK did not appear for either deposition. The categories for deposition include the contract, the defective lights, Visiontech’s failure to replace the defective lights, and Vistiontech’s efforts to source the replacements elsewhere. Visiontech has not filed an opposition to this motion. BHV is entitled to take the deposition of Visiontech’s PMK.

BHV requests $4,382.00 in sanctions. As Visiontech has not opposed this motion, the Court will not allow recovery of fees for reviewing an opposition and drafting a reply. The request for sanctions is granted in the reduced amount of $3,093.00 (6.7 hrs x $450.00 per hour + $78.00 in costs.)

CONCLUSION

            The Court grants BHV's motion to compel the deposition of Visiontech’s person most knowledgeable. The Court grants the request for sanctions in the reduced amount of $3,093.00.

Visiontech’s PMK is ordered to appear for deposition within 30 days of notice of ruling.

BHV to give notice.


Case Number: 25STCV02161    Hearing Date: August 5, 2026    Dept: 735

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories from Defendant

 

BACKGROUND 

On January 27, 2025, Plaintiff Nussbaum APC filed a complaint against Defendant Brendan Bakir, asserting causes of action for (1) breach of contract; (2) account stated; (3) services rendered; and (4) quantum meruit. Plaintiff alleges Defendant failed to pay attorney’s fees incurred for representation. Defendant filed a cross-complaint against Plaintiff on May 18, 2026.

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves to compel further responses to Special Interrogatories, set one, from Defendant. Plaintiff served the subject discovery on March 12, 2026. Defendant provided responses in the form of objections on May 6, 2026.

Interrogatory Nos. 1 through 18 concern Defendant’s contentions regarding the retainer agreement, whether Plaintiff performed, and any claimed breach. Interrogatory Nos. 19 through 25 concern legal services and communications relating to the Bae and PCH action, as well as Plaintiff’s representation of Defendant. Interrogatory Nos. 26 through 41 concern payments, invoice objections, unpaid invoices, and claimed offsets. Interrogatory Nos. 42 through 50 seek the witnesses, documents, facts, and contentions supporting Defendant’s defenses and his challenges to Plaintiff’s invoices, charges, rates, costs, and damages. Defendant objected to each interrogatory on the grounds that they are vague, incomplete, unduly burdensome and seek confidential and privileged information.

In opposition, Defendant argues that Plaintiff failed to properly meet and confer. Defendant represents that Plaintiff did not email the meet and confer correspondence and other documents relating to this motion to Defendant’s staff as requested and included the documents in hyperlinks that expired after 5 days. Defendant also requests the Court deny sanctions because Plaintiff is a law firm representing itself and cannot recover attorney’s fees as sanctions.

The Court will grant the motion as the interrogatories seek relevant and discoverable information. The Court declines to award sanctions as law firms representing themselves may not recover attorney’s fees as sanctions. Plaintiff argues that it is still entitled to fees as a corporation being represented by in-house counsel under PLCM Group, Inc. v. Drexler (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084, 1092–1094. The case cited by Plaintiff in support of this argument, however, does not involve a law firm and Plaintiff does not present evidence showing the counsel working on this action are special “in-house” counsel with an agency relationship to represent the corporate law firm’s interests.

CONCLUSION

The Court grants Plaintiff's motion to compel further responses to special interrogatories from Defendant. The Court denies Plaintiff’s request for sanctions.

Defendant is ordered to provide further responses to the subject discovery within 30 days of notice of ruling.

Plaintiff to give notice.
