ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO SPECIAL INTERROGATORIES, FORM INTERROGATORIES, REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION, AND MOTION TO DEEM RFAS ADMITTED FROM DEFENDANT SUNSHINE RESIDENTIAL HOME

 

I.               BACKGROUND 

Plaintiff Zachary Vann (Plaintiff), through his conservator, filed this action for elder abuse and negligence against Defendants Sunshine Residential Home, LLC (“Sunshine”), Oyewolfe Joseph Jose (“Joseph Jose”), Toluwalope Jose (“Jose”), Zainab Aderounmu, FNP-C (“Aderounmu”), Samuel Smith, MD (“Smith”) (collectively, “Defendants”), and Angela Ofoegbu, DNP (“Ofoegbu”) on July 3, 2025.

The clerk entered the defaults of Sunshine, Joseph Jose, and Aderounmu on November 3, 2025. The clerk entered the default of Jose on November 4, 2025. The clerk entered the default of Smith on December 1, 2025.

Plaintiff dismissed his claims against Ofoegbu on January 23, 2026. On March 9, 2026, the Court granted Defendants’ motions to set aside default.

Plaintiff now moves to compel Sunshine’s responses to his Requests for Production (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), and Form Interrogatories (Set One). Plaintiff moves to deem his Requests for Admission admitted as to Sunshine.

II.            PROCEDURAL HISTORY

On July 3, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint alleging (1) Elder Abuse and Neglect and (2) Negligence.

On January 23, 2026, Plaintiff dismissed Ofoegbu.

On March 9, 2026, the Court granted Defendants’ motions to set aside default and Defendants filed their Answers.

On July 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant discovery motions.

As of September 22, 2026, no opposition had been filed.

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

A.             Requests for Production

A party upon whom a demand for the production of documents has been made has 30 days after service of the demand to respond. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.260(a).)[1] The propounding and responding parties may agree to extend the time for response. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.270(a).) When a party fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party’s remedy is to seek a motion to compel response. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.300(b).) A responding party that does not provide responses waives the right to object to the demand, including objections based on privilege or work product. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2031.300(a).)¿

B.             Interrogatories

If a party to whom interrogatories are directed fails to serve a timely response, the propounding party may move for an order compelling responses and for a monetary sanction. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.290(b).) The statute contains no time limit for a motion to compel where no responses have been served. All that need be shown in the moving papers is that a set of interrogatories was properly served on the opposing party, that the time to respond has expired, and that no response of any kind has been served. (See Leach v. Superior Court (1980) 111 Cal.App.3d 902, 905-906.)

C.             Requests for Admission

If a party to whom request for admissions are served fails to provide a timely response, the party to whom the request was directed waives any objections, including based on privilege or the work product doctrine. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.280(a).) The requesting party can move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and the truth of any matters specified in the request be deemed admitted, as well as for monetary sanctions. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.280(b).) The court shall issue this order unless the party to whom the request was made serves a response in substantial compliance prior to the hearing on the motion. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.280(c).)   

IV.          ANALYSIS

Plaintiff moves to compel Sunshine’s responses to his Requests for Production (Set One), Special Interrogatories (Set One), and Form Interrogatories (Set One). Plaintiff also moves to deem admitted as to Sunshine his Requests for Admission (Set One). Because the Motions rely on substantially similar facts, the Court will address them together.

On May 12, 2026, Plaintiff served discovery on Sunshine including his SROGs, FROGs, RFPs, and RFAs. (See e.g. FROGs Declaration of Anthony W. Willoughby II filed July 30, 2026 (“FROGs Willoughby Decl.”, ¶ 2, Ex. A.) Sunshine has not provided any discovery responses to date. (See id., ¶ 5.)

The Court finds Plaintiff is entitled to an order compelling Sunshine’s responses to Plaintiff’s RFPs, FROGs, and SROGs. The Court finds Plaintiff is entitled to an order deeming the truth of matters admitted in his RFAs. Thus, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motions to Compel SROGs, FROGs, and RFPs. The Court also GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem RFAs Admitted.

Sunshine is ordered to provide code compliant responses without objection to Plaintiff’s RFPs, FROGs, and SROGs on or before October 16, 2026.

V.             DISCOVERY SANCTIONS

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction¿. . .¿against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel¿. . .” responses to interrogatories or production demands, absent substantial justification or other circumstances that make imposing the sanctions unjust.¿(Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.290(c), § 2031.300(c).) The court shall also impose a “monetary sanction…on the party or attorney, or both, whose failure to serve a timely response to requests for admission necessitated this motion.” (Id., § 2033.280(c).)

Plaintiff requests discovery sanctions in the amount of $960.00 in connection with his Motion FROGs, SROGs, and RFPs. This total comprises 1 hours to prepare each Motion and 1 hour to prepare a Reply and attend the hearing at counsel’s hourly rate of $450.00. (See FROGs Willoughby Decl., ¶¶ 7-10.) Plaintiff’s counsel also incurred three $60.00 filing fees. (See id.)

The Court awards Plaintiff’s counsel $1,080.00. The Court finds 1.5 hours to prepare the substantially similar, routine discovery motions and 0.5 hours to remotely attend the hearing at counsel’s requested rate is reasonable. The court awards the total filing fee amount.

Sunshine’s counsel is ordered to pay Plaintiff’s counsel discovery sanctions in the amount of $1,080.00 on or before October 29, 2026.

VI.          CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motions to Compel SROGs, FROGs, and RFPs. The Court GRANTS Plaintiff’s Motion to Deem RFAs Admitted.

Sunshine is ordered to provide code compliant responses without objection to Plaintiff’s RFPs, FROGs, and SROGs without objection on or before October 16, 2026.

Sunshine’s counsel is ordered to pay Plaintiff’s counsel discovery sanctions in the amount of $1,080.00 on or before October 29, 2026

          IT IS SO ORDERED.

I.               BACKGROUND 

Plaintiffs Israel Alcantara (“Alcantara”), Susana Parocua (“Parocua”), Danny Sebastian Alcantara, Lopez Emmanuel Alexander, Rocha Diaz Jorge Armando, and Antonio Zoquitecatl Ortiz (collectively, “Employee Plaintiffs”) (collectively, “Plaintiffs”) allege Defendants CNC Properties, LLC (“CNC”), Atlantic Star, LLC (“Atlantic Star”), and Kyung Mi Choi (“Choi”) (collectively, “Defendants”) created and maintained a hazardous and unsafe working environment at 15826 Ventura. Bl., Suite 110, Encino, CA 91436 (the “Property”). Plaintiffs own or are employed at a restaurant that operates from a unit at the Property. As a result of these violations, Plaintiffs allege they suffered serious health problems arising from issues, including but not limited to, massive water leakage, water damage, flooding, and mold exposure.

Atlantic Star and CNC have filed a Cross-Complaint alleging breach of contract related claims against Alcantara and Parocua.

Alcantara and Parocua (jointly, “Cross-Defendants”) move to quash service of summons of the Cross-Complaint.

(11) Premises Liability; and (12) Injunctive Relief.

On June 17, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleging (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Negligence; (3) Violation of California Civil Code section 1927 (Contractual and Tortious Breach of Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment); (4) Intentional Misrepresentation (Fraud); (5) Negligent Misrepresentation; (6) Negligence Per Se (Violation of Building Codes and Health and Safety Codes); (7) Negligent Infliction of Emotional Distress; (8) Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; (9) Nuisance; (10) Fraudulent Concealment; (11) Violation of Business and Professions Code sections 17200, et seq.: (12) Premises Liability; and (13) Injunctive Relief.

On July 17, 2026, Cross-Complainants Atlantic Star and CNC filed a Cross-Complaint alleging (1) Breach of Contract; (2) Contractual Indemnity; (3) Implied Indemnity; (4) Negligence; (5) Contribution and Apportionment; and (6) Declaratory Relief against Alcantara and Parocua.

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

“Code of Civil Procedure § 418.10(a)(1)[1] provides, “[a] defendant, on or before the last day of his or her time to plead or within any further time that the court may for good cause allow, may serve and file a notice of motion… [t]o quash service of summons on the ground of lack of jurisdiction of the court over him or her.” A motion to quash must be granted if the court finds that either (1) there is no basis for exercising personal jurisdiction over the defendant or (2) service on the defendant was improper. (See Ziller Elecs. Lab GmbH v. Superior Court (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 1222, 1229.)  When a defendant moves to quash service of the summons and complaint, the plaintiff has “the burden of proving the facts that did give the court jurisdiction, that is the facts requisite to an effective service.” (Coulston v. Cooper (1966) 245 Cal.App.2d 866, 868.)

V.             ANALYSIS

The Court will address the briefs together as they are based on substantially similar arguments and facts. Cross-Defendants move to quash service of summons on grounds that the Cross-Complaint was electronically served on their counsel but was not personally served on Cross-Defendants. (See Alcantara Mot. at 4:3-13; Parocua Mot. at 4:3-13.)

Cross-Complainants argue service was proper as Code of Civil Procedure § 428.60(2) requires service of the Cross-Complaint on Cross-Defendants’ attorney rather than personal service on the individual Cross-Defendants. (See Alcantara Opp. at 2:11-16; Parocua Opp. at 2:9-21.) Cross-Complainants argue they complied by electronically serving Plaintiffs’ counsel, Katherine R. Cohan, on July 17, 2026 at kcohanesq@gmail.com, the same address she identified in Plaintiffs’ FAC and which has been consistently used for electronic service in this action since May 29, 2026. (See id.) Cross-Complainants also contend service of this Motion was procedurally defective as they were not served with the Motions and learned of them upon review of the docket. (See e.g. Alcantara Declaration of Jieun Park filed September 11, 2026, ¶ 4.) Additionally, Cross-Complainants contend the proof of service is defective. (See Alcantara Opp., at 5:1-5; Parocua Opp. at 5:1-5.)

As an initial matter, the Court finds the Motions were not served on Cross-Complainants. The proofs of service do not show that service was effected as they are not signed by Cross-Defendants’ counsel even though they indicate they were prepared by Ms. Cohan. (See e.g. Alcantara Opp., Proof of Service.) However, because Cross-Complainants ultimately learned of the Motions and filed their Oppositions, the Court finds no basis to deny for lack of service of the Motions.

Next, the Court will deny the Motion on grounds that service was properly executed. Under Code of Civil Procedure § 428.60(2), “If a party has appeared in the action, the cross-complaint shall be served upon his attorney, or upon the party if he has appeared without an attorney, in the manner provided for service of summons or in the manner provided by Chapter 5 (commencing with Section 1010) of Title 14 of Part 2 of this code.” Cross-Defendants have appeared in this action by commencing this action as plaintiffs under the representation of Ms. Cohan. The Cross-Complaint was served by electronic service on Ms. Cohan at the email provided for electronic service as recorded in this action’s docket. (See Cross-Complaint, Proof of Service; Code Civ. Proc. § 1010.6; Rules of Court, rule 2.251.) Accordingly, the Cross-Complaint was correctly served on Cross-Defendants’ counsel pursuant to § 428.60(2). Thus, the Court DENIES Cross-Defendants’ Motions to Quash Service.

VI.          CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Cross-Defendants’ Motions to Quash Service.

Cross-Defendants have until October 5, 2026, to file a responsive pleading.
