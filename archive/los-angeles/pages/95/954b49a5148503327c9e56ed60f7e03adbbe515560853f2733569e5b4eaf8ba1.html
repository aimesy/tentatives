Case Number: 23STCV26267    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: 730

TENTATIVE 

 

 

The request for default judgment submitted on May 14, 2026, is DENIED for the following reasons: 

 

Cross-Complainant JSAMJ, Inc. requests the Court impose judgment of $100,000.00 in damages, based upon “fees, costs, and expenses in excess of $100,000.00 to investigate and defend claims arising from work attributable to [Cross-Defendant] JNN’s scope.” (Jones Decl. ¶ 22.) However, no evidence was submitted in support of this amount. The only attached exhibit outlines the costs associated with the underlying renovation and construction services relevant to the underlying complaint. There are no exhibits or statements made under penalty of perjury pertaining to costs incurred due to investigate and defending claims arising from JNN’s scope. The Court cannot confirm damages of $100,000.00 are warranted by the facts.  

 

The OSC re entry of default judgment is continued to the court’s next available date in approximately 60 days. No later than 15 days before the new hearing date, Plaintiff is to submit a new default judgment package correcting these defects. Failure to do so may result in the imposition of sanctions, including monetary sanctions and/or dismissal. 

Case Number: 25STCV27061    Hearing Date: July 17, 2026    Dept: 730

TENTATIVE 

 

 

The request for default judgment submitted on June 4, 2026, is DENIED for the following reasons: 

 

In Section 8 of the CIV-100 form, Plaintiff Nonie Barclay (“Barclay”) asserts Defendant Alla Volk (“Volk”) was not in the military because “Plaintiff knows Defendant personally and knows she was not in the military,” and Volk’s “pre-lit counsel” “never mentioned any issues with the military.” This is not an appropriate basis for the court to determine Volk is not in the military at this time. Barclay has failed to satisfy this requirement. 

 

Barclay did not submit a declaration from a percipient witness or other evidence in support of the $500,000.00 in damages sought. Barclay’s declaration attests Barclay’s paychecks bounced and that Barclay was subjected to numerous wage/hour violations. Beyond an assertion that Barclay was given a $950.00 paycheck and asked not to check it, there are no numbers or figures to support any particular amount in damages. (Barclay Decl. ¶ 2.) The “supplemental application for default judgment by the court,” which is not a declaration submitted under penalty of perjury, asserts Barclay suffered $44,793 in wage/hour damages and $420,000 in termination related damages without any evidence to supplement these figures. Barclay does not provide proof of previous salary or any admissible evidence in support of the alleged emotional damages allegedly totaling $350,000.00. Barclay has failed to provide any admissible evidence in support of the requested primary damages.  

 

Although Barclay later served a statement of damages requesting $2,000,000.00 in punitive damages, Barclay’s underlying complaint does not make a request for punitive damages in the prayer for relief. In order to obtain punitive damages, Barclay must put Volk on notice as to the request for punitive damages via request in a complaint’s prayer for relief. This cannot be later supplemented with a statement of damages; it must be properly pled. Barclay should either amend the underlying complaint to add in a request for punitive damages in the prayer for relief, vacating default, or remove the request for punitive damages.  

 

The OSC re entry of default judgment is continued to the court’s next available date in approximately 60 days. No later than 15 days before the new hearing date, Plaintiff is to submit a new default judgment package correcting these defects. Failure to do so may result in the imposition of sanctions, including monetary sanctions and/or dismissal.

Superior Court of California

AHMED ASHOUR

Plaintiff,

vs.

PARASYS, INC., et al.

Defendants

Case No.:

25STCV32573

Hearing Date:

July 17, 2026

ORDER GRANTING MOTION TO VACATE DEFAULT

 

I.         BACKGROUND

Defendant Robert Ebe (“Ebe”), a security guard acting within the scope of his employment by Defendant Parasys, Inc. (“Parasys”), allegedly demeaned, assaulted, and battered Plaintiff Ahmed Ashour (“Ashour”), unprovoked. Ashour sues Defendants for five causes of action, including civil assault and battery.

On March 10, 2026, the Court entered default against Ebe.

On April 16, 2026, Ebe filed the instant motion to vacate entry of default. Ashour filed an opposition.

 

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b)[1], allows the Court to vacate dismissal, default or any other order taken against a party via the party's own mistake, inadvertence, surprise or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein. 

“Section 473(b) provides for both discretionary and mandatory relief.  [Citation.]”  (Pagnini v. Union Bank, N.A. (2018) 28 Cal.App.5th 298, 302.) The discretionary provision grants relief based upon a party or legal representative’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. The discretionary provision states in pertinent part: 

“The court may, upon any terms as may be just, relieve a party or his or her legal representative from a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against him or her through his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect. Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” 

The mandatory provision states in pertinent part:  

“Notwithstanding any other requirements of this section, the court shall, whenever an application for relief is made no more than six months after entry of judgment, is in proper form, and is accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, vacate any (1) resulting default entered by the clerk against his or her client, and which will result in entry of a default judgment, or (2) resulting default judgment or dismissal entered against his or her client, unless the court finds that the default or dismissal was not in fact caused by the attorney’s mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. The court shall, whenever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault, direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties.”

“The purpose of this mandatory relief provision is to alleviate the hardship on parties who lose their day in court due to an inexcusable failure to act by their attorneys. [Citation.]”  (Rodriguez v. Brill (2015) 234 Cal.App.4th 715, 723, emphasis added.)

 

III.      DISCUSSION

Ebe requests the Court vacate default pursuant to section 473, subdivision (b), as default was entered due to attorney’s mistake. In order for a court to grant relief pursuant to the mandatory provision of section 473, subdivision (b), a request for relief must be made within 6 months of entry of dismissal. It must contain an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to their mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect, resulting in entry of default, along with a copy of a proposed pleading.

Ashour served Ebe with the complaint via mail, pursuant to section 415.30. Service was not effectuated until January 6, 2026, when it was acknowledged by Ebe’s counsel. (Mot., Reccius Decl. ¶ 2.) After failing to timely meet and confer, on February 6, 2026, Ebe filed an untimely declaration for automatic extension for demurrer, extending the deadline to March 9, 2026. (See 2/6/26 Declaration of Demurring Party in Support of Automatic Extension.) Ebe filed a demurrer on March 9, 2026; although initially received, the demurrer was subsequently denied due to failure to provide hearing information and comply with the pleading format. (Mot., Reccius Decl. ¶¶ 6-9.) Ebe refiled the demurrer on March 11, 2026. However, Ashour had requested and been granted entry of default against Ebe on March 10, 2026.

Ebe requests the Court vacate entry of default as default was only entered due to Ebe’s counsel’s mistake. Counsel timely filed a demurrer that was subsequently rejected for defects in form, not in content or timing. Had counsel submitted a properly formatted demurrer, default would not have been entered against Ebe. This is supported by declaration from Benjamin S. Reccius, Ebe’s counsel, who attested the error was his own. (Id. at ¶ 24.)

Ashour argues this does not constitute Reccius’s mistake or excusable neglect because the actual formatting mistake was committed by Reccius’s paralegal, Jeanine Giardina. Ashour argues that, because Giardina did not provide a declaration attesting to her mistake, Ebe has failed to satisfy the requirement of demonstrating an attorney’s mistake via declaration of the attorney. However, the actual individual who incorrectly formatted the motion, or submitted the improperly formatted motion, is irrelevant for purposes of the analysis. Reccius, as the attorney assigned to this case, was responsible for ensuring properly formatted pleading papers were timely submitted in this case. The work of counsel’s staff is attributable to Reccius, including the staff’s mistakes. Therefore, Ebe has properly demonstrated default was entered due to counsel’s mistake.

This motion was filed on April 16, 2026, less than six months after entry of default. Therefore, it is timely for purposes of granting relief under section 473, subdivision (b). The motion also contains a copy of Ebe’s proposed demurrer, satisfying the pleading requirements. Relief is mandatory. Therefore, the Court grants the motion.

 

IV.       CONCLUSION

Defendant Robert Ebe’s motion to vacate default is GRANTED.

Ebe is ordered to give notice.
