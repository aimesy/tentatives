Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 21, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           L.M. v. DOE 1

 

CASE NO.:                22STCV38409

 

MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT, OR ALTERNATIVELY, SUMMARY ADJUDICATION

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant Los Angeles Unified School District

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff L.M.

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order granting summary judgment of the first and third causes of action in LAUSD’s favor; or

2.      Orders in LAUSD’s favor granting summary adjudication of the following issues:

a.       There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law on Plaintiff’s first cause of action for negligence;

b.      There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law on Plaintiff’s third cause of action for negligent hiring, retention, and supervision;

c.       There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law because there is no evidence that LAUSD negligently hired the school psychologist;

d.      There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law because there is no evidence that LAUSD negligently supervised the school psychologist;

e.       There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law because there is no evidence that LAUSD negligently retained the school psychologist;

f.        There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law because there is no evidence that LAUSD negligently supervised Plaintiff; and

g.      There is no triable issue as to any material fact, and Defendant LAUSD is entitled to judgment as a matter of law because any claim for damages as a result of the school psychologist’s physical assault was not revived under CCP § 340.1 and is barred by the statute of limitations.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for summary judgment, or summary adjudication, is DENIED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On December 9, 2022, Plaintiff L.M. (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against DOE 1, DOE 2 (Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) negligence [school]; (2) negligence [Doe 2]; (3) negligent hiring, retention, and supervision [school]; (4) negligent hiring, retention, and supervision [Doe 2].

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff brings this revival action pursuant to CCP § 340.1 for damages arising from childhood sexual abuse while under Defendants’ custody and care. She alleges she was sexually abused and assaulted by Defendants’ employee and/or agent while she was a student.

 

On January 4, 2023, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC) with the same causes of action. It amended Doe 1 to be Los Angeles Unified School District (LAUSD).

 

On March 7, 2023, LAUSD filed a stipulation and proposed order to strike treble damages from the FAC which the court GRANTED.

 

On March 8, 2023, LAUSD filed an answer.

 

On January 10, 2024, Plaintiff filed a stipulated protective order which the court GRANTED.

 

On April 16, 2024, LAUSD filed a stipulation and order to continue trial and all related dates which the court GRANTED.

 

On April 11, 2025, LAUSD filed a stipulation and order to continue trial and all related dates which the court GRANTED.

 

On June 16, 2026, LAUSD filed the instant motion for summary judgment, or alternatively summary adjudication (MSJ). On September 1, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On September 10, 2026, LAUSD filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

“[T]he party moving for summary judgment bears the burden of persuasion that there is no triable issue of material fact and that he is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co. (2001) 25 Cal.4th 826, 850, fn. omitted (Aguilar).) “Once the [movant] has met that burden, the burden shifts to the [other party] to show that a triable issue of one or more material facts exists as to that cause of action . . . .” (Code Civ. Proc., (CCP) § 437c, subd. (p)(2); see Aguilar, supra, at p. 850.) The party opposing summary judgment “may not rely upon the mere allegations or denials of its pleadings,” but rather “shall set forth the specific facts showing that a triable issue of material fact exists . . . .” (CCP § 437c, subd. (p)(2).) A triable issue of material fact exists where “the evidence would allow a reasonable trier of fact to find the underlying fact in favor of the party opposing the motion in accordance with the applicable standard of proof.” (Aguilar, supra, at p. 850.)

 

The opposing party on a motion for summary judgment is under no evidentiary burden to produce rebuttal evidence until the moving party meets his or her initial movant’s burden.¿ (Binder v. Aetna Life Insurance Company (1999) 75 Cal.App.4th 832, 840.)¿ Once the initial movant’s burden is met, then the burden shifts to the opposing party to show, with admissible evidence, that there is a triable issue requiring the weighing procedures of trial.¿ (CCP § 437c(p).)¿ The opposing party may not simply rely on his/her allegations to show a triable issue but must present evidentiary facts that are substantial in nature and rise beyond mere speculation.¿ (Sangster v. Paetkau (1998) 68¿Cal.App.4th 151, 162.)¿ Summary judgment must be granted “if all the evidence submitted, and ‘all inferences reasonably deducible from the evidence’ and uncontradicted by other inferences or evidence, show that there is no triable issue as to any material fact and that the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”¿ (Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp. (1992) 7¿Cal.App.4th¿1110, 1119.)¿¿¿ 

 

The pleadings dictate the scope of a motion for summary judgment. “The materiality of a disputed fact is measured by the pleadings [citations], which ‘set the boundaries of the issues to be resolved at summary judgment.’ [Citations.]” (Hutton v. Fid. Nat'l Title Co. (2013) 213 Cal. App. 4th 486, 493.) The pleadings here are challenging to decipher.

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Evidentiary Objections

 

The court need not rule on the two objections lodged by Defendant as they are not material to the court’s ruling.

 

Summary Judgment/Adjudication

 

First Cause of Action: Negligence

 

LAUSD contends summary judgment is appropriate because they cannot be liable for general negligence, there is no evidence that LAUSD negligently supervised Mr. Newton, there is no evidence LAUSD negligently supervised Plaintiff, and the evidence fails to support vicarious liability.

 

Plaintiff argues there is ongoing critical discovery needed to defeat the motion, that LAUSD is liable for both negligently supervising her and Mr. Newton, and her silence is not dispositive of her claims.

 

LAUSD replies that Plaintiff lacks authority for most of her positions, her argument on failure to report lacks merit, and then reiterates their original arguments.

 

According to the FAC, LAUSD was negligent in the placement and supervision of Plaintiff while at school and in the failure to keep her safe. (FAC ¶ 70.) Put differently, this is a negligent supervision of the student claim.

 

The necessary elements for negligence are: (1) that the defendant owed a legal duty to the plaintiff to use due care; (2) a breach of that duty; (3) causation; and (4) damages to the plaintiff.¿¿(County of Santa Clara v. Atlantic Richfield Co.¿(2006) 137 Cal.App.4th 292, 318.)

 

“School districts and their employees have never been considered insurers of the physical safety of their students, but rather are placed under a general duty to supervise the conduct of children on school grounds during school sessions, school activities, recesses, and lunch periods.” (Bartell v. Palos Verdes Peninsula Sch. Dist. (1978) 83 Cal.App.3d 492, 498, citing Dailey v. Los Angeles Unified School District (1970) 2 Cal.3d 741, 747, 748.)

 

“‘It is the duty of the school authorities to supervise at all times the conduct of the children on the school grounds and to enforce those rules and regulations necessary to their protection. The school district is liable for injuries which result from a failure of its officers and employees to use ordinary care in this respect…’ ‘[w]hat is ordinary care depends upon the circumstances of each particular case and is to be determined as a fact with reference to the situation and knowledge of the parties.’” (J.H. v. Los Angeles Unifed School Dist. (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 123, 141 citing Taylor v. Oakland Scavenger Co. (1941) 17 Cal.2d 594, 600; Bellman v. San Francisco H.S. Dist. (1938) 11 Cal.2d 576, 582.) The law is well established that “a public school district may be vicariously liable under [Government Code] section 815.2 for the negligence of administrators or supervisors in hiring, supervising and retaining a school employee who sexually harasses and abuses a student.” (C.A. v. William S. Hart Union School Dist. (“C.A. v. William S. Hart”) (2012) 53 Cal.4th 861, 879.) 

 

A claim for negligent supervision of students requires “facts demonstrating that school personnel failed to exercise ordinary prudence.” (Roe v. Hesperia Unified School District (2022) 85 Cal.App.5th 13, 29.) “Either a total lack of supervision or ineffective supervision may constitute a lack of ordinary care on the part of those responsible for student supervision.” (Ibid. [internal brackets and quotations omitted.])

 

Here, LAUSD met their initial burden as to lack of knowledge to defeat the foreseeability element. First, they provided evidence that no district employee or supervisor witnesses any of the alleged abuse or acts by Mr. Newton. (Separate Statement of Undisputed Material Facts (SSUMF) SSUMF No. 37.) Second, they provide evidence via Plaintiff’s testimony that she did not report the abuse to LAUSD. (SSUMF Nos. 21, 25, 35.) The burden shifts.

 

Plaintiff, in turn, met her burden to demonstrate a foreseeability issue. First, she provides evidence via her testimony that she repeatedly told her teacher she did not want to go with Mr. Newton and she acted out to let the teacher know she didn’t want to go. (SSUMF No. 24.) She also provided evidence, via her testimony, that she returned to class from these sessions withdrawn. (SSUMF No. 25 (Depo Transcript at 112:10-24.) Finally, she provides evidence that these sessions occurred at the back of the school, in a locked room, with the window shades drawn. (SSUMF No. 23 (Depo Transcript at 63:24-65:19.) Taken together, this is sufficient evidence of constructive knowledge sufficient to reach the jury.

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES summary judgment on the first cause of action.

 

Third Cause of Action: Negligent Hiring, Retention, and Supervision

 

LAUSD’s arguments here are similar to the first cause of action. So, too, are Plaintiff’s.

 

According to the FAC, LAUSD knew or should have known of Mr. Newton’s unfitness to perform the work for which he was hired and this caused Plaintiff’s injury. (FAC ¶¶ 90-95.) Put differently, this is a negligent supervision of the employee claim.

 

The necessary elements for negligent supervision and retention are: (1) that the employer hired¿the¿employee;¿(2)¿who¿was¿incompetent or unfit;¿(3) that the¿employer had reason to believe undue risk of harm would exist because of the employment; and¿(4) that¿harm¿occurred.  (Federico v. Superior Court¿(1997) 59 Cal.App.4th 1207, 1213-1214.) 

 

“A school district is liable for the negligence or supervisory employees who knew or should have known of a school employee’s dangerous propensities, but nevertheless hired, retained and failed to properly supervise the employee.” (Rancho Cucamonga School Dist. v. Superior Court. (2025) 116 Cal.App.5th 718, 721 [internal quotations and brackets omitted.]) A school district “may be liable for hiring, supervising, or retaining an employee if the district’s supervisory personnel had actual or constructive knowledge of the employee’s dangerous propensities.” (Ibid.) “Constructive knowledge is knowledge that may be shown by circumstantial evidence which is nothing more than one or more inferences which may be said to arise reasonably from a series of proven facts.” (Ibid. [quotations omitted])

 

Here, LAUSD met their initial burden that they lacked actual or constructive knowledge. First, they provided evidence that no district employee or supervisor witnesses any of the alleged abuse or acts by Mr. Newton. (SSUMF No. 37.) Second, they provide evidence via Plaintiff’s testimony that she did not report the abuse to LAUSD. (SSUMF Nos. 21, 25, 35.) Consistent with Rancho Cucamonga, this is sufficient to shift the burden.

 

Plaintiff, in turn, met her burden to demonstrate a triable issue of fact as to constructive knowledge. Critically, she provides evidence via her testimony that she repeatedly told her teacher she did not want to go with Mr. Newton and she acted out to let the teacher know she didn’t want to go. (SSUMF No. 24.) She also provided evidence, via her testimony, that she returned to class from these sessions withdrawn. (SSUMF No. 25 (Depo Transcript at 112:10-24.) This activity raises two inferences: on the one hand, the Plaintiff may have been demonstrating difficult behavior, but on the other hand she may have been exhibiting this behavior as a result of abuse by Mr. Newton. This is unlike Rancho Cucamongo, where there was “no evidence any district employee observed anything that reasonably should have triggered suspicion of abuse to justify an inference of constructive knowledge.” (Rancho Cucamonga, supra, 116 Cal.App.5th at p. 722.) This is therefore appropriate for the jury.

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES summary judgment on the third cause of action.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

1.      Motion for summary judgment, or summary adjudication, is DENIED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 21, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Modvans, Inc. v. Atlantic Casualty Insurance Company, Inc.

 

CASE NO.:                24STCV25111

 

MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSE TO DEPOSITION SUBPOENA FOR PRODUCTION OF BUSINESS RECORDS

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant/Cross-Complainant Atlantic Casualty Insurance Company, Inc.

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): None

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order to compel SetApart Accountancy Corp. to comply with a deposition subpoena for production of business records.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion to compel response to deposition subpoena for production of business records is DENIED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On September 30, 2024, Plaintiff¿ModVans, Inc. (Plaintiff or Modvans) filed a Complaint against Defendants Atlantic Casualty Insurance Company, Nigel Green Insurance Agency, Inc., and¿Amwins¿Access Insurance Services, LLC (Defendants) with three causes of action for: (1) Breach of the Duty to Pay a Covered Insurance Claim; (2) Breach of the Duty of Good Faith and Fair Dealing; and (3) Negligence.¿ 

¿ 

According to the Complaint, Plaintiff¿had an insurance policy issued by Defendant Atlantic¿Casualty Insurance Company (Atlantic)¿(the Policy) that Defendant¿Nigel Green Insurance Agency, Inc. (Green) obtained for it by working with Defendant Atlantic’s managing agent,¿Amwins¿Access Insurance Services, LLC (Amwins).¿Plaintiff alleges that on December 11, 2023,¿a fire destroyed its production facility which caused Plaintiff to sustain approximately¿$5,800,500.00. Plaintiff further alleges that¿Defendants improperly bound the Policy at¿old coverage limits despite Plaintiff’s negotiation for an increase and that Defendants¿are not performing under the Policy.¿¿ 

¿ 

On November 8, 2024,¿Amwins¿filed an Answer.¿ 

¿ 

On November 19, 2024, Green filed an Answer.¿ 

¿ 

On December 26, 2024, Atlantic filed a demurrer which the court OVERRULED.¿ 

 

On April 24, 2025, Atlantic filed an Answer and a cross-complaint against Modvans for declaratory relief. 

 

On May 9, 2025, Modvans filed an Answer to Atlantic’s cross-complaint. 

 

On April 28, 2026, Amwins filed the instant motion for summary judgment, or alternatively, summary adjudication (MSJ). 

 

On April 29, 2026, Green filed an application for determination of good faith settlement which the court GRANTED. 

 

On May 26, 2026, Amwins filed a motion to contest Green’s good faith settlement which the court DENIED.

 

On June 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to Amwins’ MSJ. On July 9, 2026, Amwins filed a reply.

 

On July 20, 2026, the MSJ came on for hearing. After oral argument, the court ordered supplemental briefing. On August 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed a supplemental brief. On August 7, 2026, Amwins filed a supplemental brief.

 

On August 4, 2026, Atlantic filed the instant motion to compel compliance with third party subpoena. Oppositions were due on or before September 8, 2026. As of September 15, 2026, the court has not received any opposition.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

“If a deponent fails to answer any question or to produce any document¿or tangible thing under the deponent's control that is specified in the deposition notice or a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer or production.” (Code Civ. Proc. (CCP) § 2025.480(a).) 

 

“This motion shall be made no later than 60 days after the completion of the record of the deposition, and shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under¿Section 2016.040.” (CCP § 2025.480(b).) 

 

“The court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel an answer or production, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.” (CCP § 2025.480(j).) 

 

“The court may impose a monetary sanction ordering that one engaging in the misuse of the discovery process, or any attorney advising that conduct, or both pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct. The court may also impose this sanction on one unsuccessfully asserting that another has engaged in the misuse of the discovery process, or on any attorney who advised that assertion, or on both. If a monetary sanction is authorized by any provision of this title, the court shall impose that sanction unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  

 

(CCP § 2023.030(a).) 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Atlantic contends relief is proper because they issued a subpoena to SetApart, the subpoena commanded production on July 20, 2026, they personally served it on SetApart, and SetApart failed to comply. The court has no oppositions.

 

Here, relief is premature. Notably, the subpoena is defective. First, the subpoena was issued on June 18, 2026 and personally served on SetApart’s agent on July 6, 2026. (Declaration of Catherine A. Valloso ¶¶ 4, 5, 7, Exhibit A [proof of service], Exhibit B [deposition subpoena].) SetApart’s response was due by July 20, 2026. (Id. at ¶ 6, Exhibit B.) Atlantic indicates that while this is only 14 days, now more than 15 days have elapsed, and they did not seek to enforce compliance on that date. (Ibid.) The code states that the deposition subpoena “shall command compliance . . . no earlier than 20 days after the issuance, or 15 days after the service, of the deposition subpoena, whichever date is later.” (CCP § 2020.410(c).) On its face, then, the deposition subpoena is defective.[1] Relief is therefore premature.

 

Accordingly, the court DENIES Atlantic’s motion to compel compliance.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion to compel response to deposition subpoena for production of business records is DENIED.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Case Number: 25STCV22993    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 21, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Tomas Camilo Micheo v. Los Angeles Unified School District, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                25STCV22993

 

DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendants Los Angeles Unified School District and Bennett Quevado

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiff Tomas Camilo Micheo

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      Demurrer to the entire complaint as time-barred for failing to comply with the Government Tort Claims Act;

2.      Demurrer to the first, fourth, fifth, and sixth causes of action for failing to state sufficient facts to constitute a cause of action.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Demurrer to the entire complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On August 4, 2025, Plaintiff Tomas Camilo Micheo, by and through his Guardian ad Litem Tomas Micheo (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendants Los Angeles Unified School District (LAUSD) and Bennett Quevado (Quevado) (sometimes collectively Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) battery; (2) negligence; (3) negligent supervision; (4) violation of Civ. Code § 51; (5) intentional infliction of emotional distress; and (6) abuse of process.

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff was subjected to physical restraint, intimidation, harassment, and unwarranted disciplinary actions by Defendants when Plaintiff attempted to use the restroom. Plaintiff alleges that Quevado restrained and battered him but Plaintiff was suspended and charged with battery instead. Plaintiff also alleges a series of continued harassment.

 

On July 24, 2026, Defendants filed the instant demurrer. On September 8, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. On September 14, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Meet and Confer 

 

Prior to filing a demurrer, the demurring party is required to satisfy their meet and confer obligations pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41, and demonstrate that they so satisfied their meet and confer obligation by submitting a declaration pursuant to Code of Civ. Proc. §430.41(a)(2) & (3). ¿The meet and confer requirement also applies to motions to strike. (CCP § 435.5.)¿

 

Here, the parties met and conferred telephonically on June 26, 2026 and July 21, 2026. (Declaration of George Tourkow ¶¶ 6, 8.) This requirement is therefore met.

 

Demurrer – Sufficiency of Facts 

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.¿(Hahn v. Mirda¿(2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context.¿In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice.¿(Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.)¿“A demurrer tests the pleadings alone and not the evidence or other extrinsic matters. …. The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.”¿(Hahn¿147 Cal.App.4th at 747.)¿¿ 

¿ 

When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true. (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.) Courts also consider exhibits attached to the complaint and incorporated by reference. (See Frantz v. Blackwell (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 91, 94.)

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The court GRANTS Defendants’ request for judicial notice of Exhibit A (Plaintiff’s government tort claim) and Exhibit B (LAUSD’s written notice of rejection). (Evid. Code § 452(c), (h).)

 

Demurrer

 

Entire Complaint – Time-Barred & Compliance with Tort Claims Act

 

Defendants contend the complaint is time-barred pursuant to Gov. Code § 945.6(a)(1) because Plaintiff filed suit more than six months after LAUSD sent a valid written claim rejection. They also contend Plaintiff failed to allege compliance with the Tort Claims Act.

 

Plaintiff argues this is a fact-heavy question since Plaintiff’s counsel and LAUSD adjusters exchanged emails whereby LAUSD knew Plaintiff’s counsel’s address was changing and nonetheless sent the rejection to the prior address.

 

Defendants reply that the judicially noticeable documents establish receipt and rejection of Plaintiff’s claim, Plaintiff’s arguments are outside the four corners of the complaint,  and there are no due process violations.

 

Gov. Code § 910 requires, among other things, the name and post office address of the claimant and the post office address to which the person presenting the claim desires notices to be sent. (Gov. Code § 910(a)-(b).)

 

As a threshold matter, neither party addressed Gov. Code § 912.4. That section required LAUSD to act on the claim “within 45 days after the claim has been presented.” (Gov. Code § 912.4(a); Cavey v. Tualla (2021) 69 Cal.App.5th 310, 324, 329 (Cavey).) “If the board fails or refuses to act on a claim within the time prescribed by this section, the claim shall be deemed to have been rejected by the board on the last day of the period within which the board was required to act upon the claim.” (Gov. Code § 912.4(c).)

 

Here, the claim was signed on August 21, 2023, stamped by LAUSD-Board Secretariat on August 24, 2023, and marked Received by RMIS on August 28, 2023. (RJN, Exhibit A.) 45 days from these dates were October 5, 2023, October 8, 2023, and October 12, 2023. The claim was therefore deemed rejected by no later than October 12, 2023. (Gov. Code § 912.4(c).) However, LAUSD provided written notice pursuant to Gov. Code § 913 on January 5, 2024. (RJN, Exhibit B.)

 

Gov. Code § 913 requires written notice of rejection of presented claims. (Gov. Code § 913(a).) The rejection must include a warning that includes reference to the six month limitations period. (Id. at subd. (b).) Additionally, Gov. Code § 915.4 requires personal delivery or mailing the rejection notice to the person presenting the claim or making the application. (Gov. Code § 915.4(a)(1)-(2).) If the rejection is mailed, it can be sent to an address “to which the person presenting the claim or making the application desires notices to be sent . . . .” (Ibid.) In fact, LAUSD did not have discretion to mail it to an address aside from the one articulated in the claim form. (See Cavey, supra, 60 Cal.App.5th at p. 345-346.)

 

In Cavey, the Court of Appeal concluded that the notice of claim rejection was defective. There, the entity mailed the rejection to the plaintiff’s counsel’s mailing address and not to the plaintiff’s post office box included on the claim form. (Cavey, supra, 69 Cal.App.5th at p. 345.) The Court reasoned that this did not comply with “the unambiguous statutory requirement” because it “was not mailed to the address specified” in Gov. Code § 915.4(a)(2). (Ibid.)

 

So, too, here. It is undisputed that Plaintiff included counsel’s Beverly Hills address on the claim form. (RJN, Exhibit A; Opp. 8:12-23.[1]) This is the same address included on the claim form rejection. (RJN, Exhibit B; Opp. 8:12-23.) Thus, LAUSD complied with the “unambiguous statutory requirement” to mail the rejection to the address included on the claim form. (Cavey, supra, 69 Cal.App.5th at p. 345.)

 

What is more, “a claimant is required to comply with the six-month statute of limitations associated with government tort claims upon proof that the notice of rejection was served even if it was not actually received by the claimant.” (Him v. City and County of San Francisco (2005) 113 Cal.App.4th 437, 445.) Because of the statutory requirement for the entity to act on the claim within 45 days, “following a reasonable time after the expiration of the 45 days, a claimant should be aware that the claim has been denied and the statutory notice of that denial has not been provided.” (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff filed this action on August 4, 2025 – well after six months from the October 2023 deemed rejected dates or the January 5, 2024 written notice of rejection.

 

Accordingly, the court SUSTAINS the demurrer to the entire complaint as time-barred.

 

Leave to Amend

 

Leave to amend should be liberally granted if there is a reasonable possibility an amendment could cure the defect.¿ (County of Santa Clara v. Superior Court (2022) 77 Cal.App.5th 1018,1035.)¿ The Plaintiff has the burden of demonstrating that leave to amend should be granted, and that the defects can be cured by amendment. (“Plaintiff must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.” (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349).

 

Plaintiff requests leave to amend to add facts concerning LAUSD’s adjuster and promises not to send mailings until receiving counsel’s new address. (Opp. 18:4-8.) However, these communications would not save the complaint because the communications occurred after the 45 day period for LAUSD to act on the claim already expired. The court does not see how this can be reasonably cured.[2]

 

Accordingly, leave to amend is DENIED.  

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Demurrer to the entire complaint is SUSTAINED without leave to amend.

Pursuant to CCP § 581d, this written order of dismissal constitutes a judgment and shall be effective for all purposes. The Clerk shall note this judgment in the register of actions in this case.¿

 

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Notably, Plaintiff does not dispute these facts. (Opp. 8:12-23.) Instead, Plaintiff wishes to add additional facts, absent from the complaint or these documents, for the court to consider.

[2] The court’s denial of leave to amend is further supported by Plaintiff’s own alternative theory under Government Code section 945.6(a)(2). As discussed above, the Complaint alleges that the asserted continuing conduct ended with Plaintiff’s middle-school graduation in 2023. Although the Complaint does not allege the precise date of graduation, the allegations permit no reasonable inference that the asserted conduct continued until August 4, 2023. Plaintiff has identified no additional conduct occurring within two years of filing. Amendment would therefore be futile even under Plaintiff’s proposed alternative limitations period.


Case Number: 25STCV35256    Hearing Date: September 21, 2026    Dept: 511

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 21, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Katarina Removic, et al. v. Essex Cochran, LP, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                25STCV35256

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE CROSS-COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendants Essex Cochran, LP; Essex Property Trust, Inc.; and Essex Management Corporation

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Plaintiffs Katarina Removic, Slobodan Kovacevic, and Katya Kovacevic

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order granting leave to file a cross-complaint against Plaintiff Removic and Fenn Termite and Pest Control, Inc.

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for leave to file cross-complaint is GRANTED;

2.      Defendants to file their cross-complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On December 3, 2025, Plaintiffs Katarina Removic, Slobodan Kovacevic, and Katya Kovacevic by and through her Guardian ad Litem Katarina Removic (Plaintiffs) filed a complaint against Defendants Essex Cochran, LP, Essex Property Trust, Inc., and Essex Management Corporation (Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) negligence; (2) tortious breach of implied warranty of habitability; (3) breach of the implied warranty of habitability; (4) private nuisance; (5) breach of covenant of quiet enjoyment; (6) violation of Civ. Code § 1942.4; (7) intentional infliction of emotional distress; (8) violation of the UCL; (9) unlawful retaliatory eviction; and (10) violation of the Los Angeles Municipal Tenant Anti-Harassment Ordinance (TAHO).

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiffs were tenants pursuant to a written lease at property owned and/or managed by Defendants. Plaintiffs allege various habitability issues with the property that caused injuries and of which they formally complained to the Los Angeles Housing Department and Defendants.  Plaintiffs allege that as a result of these complaints, Defendants harassed Plaintiffs.

 

On March 3, 2026, Defendants filed an answer.

 

On July 9, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion for leave to file a cross-complaint. On September 8, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an opposition. On September 11, 2026, Defendants filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

A cross-complaint may be either permissive or compulsory. Compulsory cross-complaints are based on allegations arising out of the same transactions or occurrences as the causes of action which the plaintiff alleges in his or her complaint. (Code Civ. Proc., (CCP) § 426.10, subd. (c).)¿Compulsory cross-complaint causes of action must exist at the time an answer to the original cross-complaint is¿required.¿(CCP § 426.30, subd. (a).)¿ All other cross-complaints are permissive.¿(CCP § 428.10.) If a party¿fails to¿file any cross-complaint before or when they file an answer, they must move the court for leave to file a cross-complaint.¿ (CCP § 428.50.) The court “shall” grant leave to file a compulsory cross-complaint,¿as long as¿the party¿demonstrates¿that,¿notwithstanding¿being late, the motion for leave is brought in good faith.¿ (CCP § 426.50.)¿The court “may” grant leave to file a permissive cross-complaint “in the interest of justice at any time during the course of the action.”¿ (CCP § 428.50, subd. (c).)¿¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿ 

A policy of liberal construction of section 426.50 to avoid forfeiture of causes of action is imposed on the trial court.¿ (Silver Orgs. v. Frank¿(1990) 217 Cal. App. 3d 94, 98-99.) ¿A motion to file a cross-complaint at any time¿during the course of¿the action must be granted unless substantial evidence of bad faith is¿demonstrated. ¿(Ibid.)¿

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Defendants contend leave to file a cross-complaint is warranted because it is a compulsory cross-complaint, there is no bad faith, there will be no prejudice in allowing the cross-complaint to be filed, and judicial economy is served by allowing the filing.

 

Plaintiffs argue Defendants blew their deadline without good cause, their unexplained delay coupled with demonstrably false excuse is bad faith, seeks to enforce a void provision in the lease, and the claims against Fenn Termite are not compulsory (a standard Defendants did not address at all).

 

Defendants reply that there is no bad faith in Defendants’ delay due to counsel’s oversight and lack of investigation of all claims at the time Defendants filed their answer. They reiterate their other arguments.

 

Compulsory vs. Permissive¿¿¿ 

¿ 

Cross-complaints against parties other than plaintiff, i.e., co-defendant or a third person not yet party to the action, are permissive only. (See CCP § 428.10.)¿ A defendant can cross-complain against such non-plaintiff only if the cause of action asserted in the cross-complaint “(1) arises out of the¿same transaction, occurrence, or¿series¿of transactions or occurrences [set forth in the complaint] … or (2) asserts a claim, right, or interest in the¿property¿or¿controversy¿which is the subject of the cause [of action] brought against him.” (CCP § 428.10(b).)¿¿ 

¿

Here, the proposed cross-complaint is compulsory. Critically, it contains claims against Katarina Removic (a plaintiff) that arise out of the same transaction and occurrence as alleged in the underlying complaint (the written lease agreement). There are also claims for indemnity against a third-party, Fenn. However, the court is unaware of authority that provides for a mixed cross-complaint.

 

Thus, this is a compulsory cross-complaint.

 

Bad Faith¿¿ 

¿ 

‘‘Bad faith,’ is defined as ‘[t]he opposite of ‘good faith,’ generally implying or involving actual or constructive fraud, or a design to mislead or deceive another, or a neglect or refusal to fulfill some duty or some contractual obligation, not prompted by an honest mistake . . ., but by some interested or sinister motive[,] . . . not simply bad judgment or negligence, but rather . . . the conscious doing of a wrong because of dishonest purpose or moral obliquity; . . . it contemplates a state of mind affirmatively operating with furtive design or ill will.¿ [Citation.]’¿ [Citations.]’¿ [Citation.]”¿ (Silver Organizations Ltd. v. Frank¿(1990) 217 Cal.App.3d 94, 100.)¿¿ 

 

Here, there is no evidence of bad faith. Notably, Defendants’ counsel admits to their own oversight or delay in analyzing the contracts at issue in the proposed cross-complaint. (Declaration of Richard A. Muench ¶¶ 4, 5, 6.) There is no evidence Defendants consciously did wrong or were dishonest. Plaintiffs’ other challenges concern validity and are more appropriately determined at demurrer or summary judgment.

 

Thus, there is no bad faith.

 

Undue Prejudice¿¿ 

¿ 

Here, there is no undue prejudice. First, Defendants filed this motion four months after filing their answer. The court is not convinced this is a serious delay. Second, trial is not set until 2028 which provides plenty of time for the parties to appear and conduct discovery.

 

Thus, there is no undue prejudice.

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Defendants’ motion for leave to file a cross-complaint.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for leave to file cross-complaint is GRANTED;

2.      Defendants to file their cross-complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

Tentative Ruling

 

Judge Upinder S. Kalra, Department 511

 

HEARING DATE:   September 21, 2026                                       

 

CASE NAME:           Fannie Nancy Moore v. Good Shepherd Health Care Center of Santa Monica, et al.

 

CASE NO.:                26STCV04975

 

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND THE COMPLAINT

 

MOVING PARTY:  Plaintiff Fannie Nancy Moore by and through her successor in interest Taequeenia Ellis

 

RESPONDING PARTY(S): Defendant J P H Consulting, Inc.

 

REQUESTED RELIEF:

 

1.      An order permitting Plaintiff leave to file a second amended complaint which adds a cause of action for violation of Health & Safety Code § 1430(b).

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

1.      Motion for leave to file a second amended complaint is GRANTED;

2.      Plaintiff shall file their second amended complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

 

On February 11, 2026, Plaintiff Fannie Nancy Moore, by and through her Guardian ad Litem Tarqueenia Ellis (Plaintiff) filed a complaint against Defendants Good Shepherd Health Care Center of Santa Monica (Good Shepherd) and J P H Consulting, Inc. (JPH) (sometimes collectively Defendants) with causes of action for: (1) elder abuse; and (2) negligence.

 

According to the complaint, Plaintiff suffered severe injury from Defendants’ neglect.

 

On April 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed the operative first amended complaint (FAC). It changed the Plaintiff to Fannie Nancy Moore by and through her successor in interest Tarqueenia Ellis.

 

On July 1, 2026, Defendants filed an answer.

 

On July 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed the instant motion for leave to amend the complaint. On September 8, 2026, JPH filed an opposition. On September 14, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD:

 

Code Civ. Proc. § 187 provides: 

When jurisdiction is, by the Constitution or this Code, or by any other statute, conferred on a Court or judicial officer, all the means necessary to carry it into effect are also given; and in the exercise of this jurisdiction, if the course of proceeding be not specifically pointed out by this Code or the statute, any suitable process or mode of proceeding may be adopted which may appear most conformable to the spirit of this Code.

 

Code Civ. Proc. § 473(a)(1) provides:¿¿ 

The court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading or proceeding by adding or striking out the name of any party, or by correcting a mistake in the name of a party, or a mistake in any other respect; and may, upon like terms, enlarge the time for answer or demurrer.¿ The court may likewise, in its discretion, after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code.¿ 

¿ 

“This discretion should be exercised liberally in favor of amendments, for judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters in the same lawsuit.”¿ (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court¿(1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047.)¿ Ordinarily, the court will not consider the validity of the proposed amended pleading in ruling on a motion for leave since grounds for a demurrer or motion to strike are premature.¿ The court, however, does have discretion to deny leave to amend where a proposed amendment¿fails to¿state¿a valid cause of action as a matter of law and the defect cannot be cured by further amendment.¿ (See¿California Casualty General Ins. Co. v. Superior Court¿(1985) 173 Cal.App.3d 274, 281 (overruled on other grounds by¿Kransco¿v. American Empire Surplus Lines Ins. Co.¿(2000) 23 Cal.4th 390).)¿¿ 

¿ 

Under California Rules of Court Rule (CRC), rule 3.1324(a), a motion to amend a pleading shall (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) state what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located.¿¿¿¿¿¿ 

¿¿¿¿¿ 

Under¿CRC, rule 3.1324(b), a separate declaration must accompany the motion and must specify (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reasons why the request for amendment was not made earlier. 

 

ANALYSIS:

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

The court DENIES JPH’s request for judicial notice as immaterial to this ruling.

 

Leave to Amend

 

Plaintiff alleges leave to add one legal theory based on nearly identical facts is warranted, that Plaintiff recently discovered this theory based on Defendants’ document production, and Defendants will not suffer prejudice by the amendment since there is no trial date scheduled.

 

JPH argues leave to amend is improper as to the new claim against it because the violation can only be brought against the licensee and JPH is not the licensee (both as alleged by Plaintiff and via exhibits subject to judicial notice).

 

Plaintiff replies that JPH’s arguments exceed the scope of what is considered in granting leave to amend and that Plaintiff also plead alter ego liability.

 

Here, leave to amend is warranted. First, Plaintiff included a copy of the amended complaint and stated the proposed changes, thereby satisfying CRC, rule 3.1324(a). Second, Plaintiff satisfied CRC, rule 3.1324(b) via her counsel’s separate declaration. The procedural requirements for this motion are therefore met. Third, there is no indication Defendants will suffer prejudice since trial is not yet set. Indeed, JPH did not raise this in their opposition. Finally, JPH’s arguments as to validity are more appropriately decided at the demurrer stage.

 

Accordingly, the court GRANTS Plaintiff’s motion for leave to amend.

 

CONCLUSION:

 

            For the foregoing reasons, the Court decides the pending motion as follows:

 

1.      Motion for leave to file a second amended complaint is GRANTED;

2.      Plaintiff shall file their second amended complaint within 7 days of this ruling.

Moving party is to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.
