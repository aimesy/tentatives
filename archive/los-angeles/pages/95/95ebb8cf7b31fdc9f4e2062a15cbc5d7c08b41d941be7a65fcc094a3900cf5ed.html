DEPARTMENT L LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24NWCV03111    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: L

Plaintiff Yeva Harutyunyan’s Motion to Compel the Deposition of Defendant BMW of North America, LLC’s Person Most Knowledgeable and Production of Documents is GRANTED.

 

Plaintiff to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is a lemon law action regarding 2022 BMW X6. On September 10, 2024, Plaintiff Yeva Harutyunyan (“Plaintiff”) filed action against Defendants BMW of North America, LLC, Mckenna BMW, and Does 1 to 50. The Complaint alleges the following: 1) Violation of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act Breach of Express Warranty, 2) Violation of the Song Beverly Consumer Warranty Act Breach of Implied Warranty, and 3) Violation of Business and Professions Code § 17200.

 

Plaintiff now moves for an order 1) compelling the deposition of Defendant BMW of North America, LLC’s (“Defendant”) Person Most Knowledgeable (“PMK”) and production of documents, and 2) striking Defendant’s objections to the Matters for Examination and Requests for Production of Documents.

 

No opposition has been filed as of July 8, 2026.


Legal Standard

 

Under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a), a party may move to compel a deponent’s attendance and production for inspection of documents if after serving the deposition notice the responding party fails to serve valid objections and fails to appear for the deposition or produce any document described in the deposition notice. A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section¿2025.450, subdivision (a) must (1) provide specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document and (2) be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration. (Code Civ. Proc., §¿2025.450, subd. (b).)

 

Meet and Confer

 

“[W]hen the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, [the

motion shall be accompanied] by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.450 subd. (b)(2).)

 

Plaintiff’s meet and confer efforts are detailed in the next section. The Court finds the meet and confer requirement has been met.

 

Discussion

 

On March 4, 2025, Plaintiff served a Notice of Deposition of Defendant’s PMK, setting the deposition for March 25, 2025. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 15, Exh. 3.)

 

On March 20, 2025, Defendant served its objections to the Notice of Deposition, stating that it would not produce its PMK on the noticed deposition date. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 16, Exh. 4.)

 

On March 23, 2025 and April 4, 2025, Plaintiff requested alternative dates for the deposition. (Terzian Decl., ¶¶ 17-18, Exhs. 5-6.) Defendant responded that its PMK was on medical leave until April 14, 2025 and that it would provide alternative dates upon the PMK’s return. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 19, Exh. 7.)

 

On September 23, 2025, after not hearing from Defendant for five months, Plaintiff served a First Amended Notice of Deposition of Defendant’s PMK, setting the deposition for December 19, 2025. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 20, Exh. 8.)

 

On December 18, 2025, Defendant served its objections to the Notice of Deposition, stating that it would not produce its PMK on the noticed deposition date. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 21, Exh. 9.) The parties further met and conferred regarding a deposition date, and Defendant indicated that its PMK had no availability until June 9, 2026. (Terzian Decl., ¶¶ 22-26, Exhs. 10-14.)

 

On January 26, 2026, the parties held a telephonic meet and confer, where Plaintiff accepted June 9, 2026 deposition date. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 27, Exh. 15.) Plaintiff served a Second Amended Notice of Deposition. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 28, Exh. 16.)

 

On June 1, 2026, Defendant served its objections to the Notice of Deposition, stating that it would not produce its PMK on the noticed deposition date. (Terzian Decl., ¶ 29, Exh. 17.)

 

The parties further met and conferred regarding alternative dates, and Plaintiff accepted a June 22, 2026 deposition date. (Terzian Decl., ¶¶ 30-34, Exhs. 18-22.) It was later determined by Defendant that such date was offered in error, and Defendant’s PMK would not be available under July 14, 2026, a date past the discovery cutoff date at the time. (Terzian Decl., ¶¶ 35-38, Exhs. 23-26.) Plaintiff filed this Motion on June 16, 2026.

 

No opposition has been filed.

 

The Court notes that, since this Motion was filed, trial has been continued to October 28, 2026. (See 6/18/26 Minute Order.) Thus, the new discovery cutoff date is September 28, 2026.

 

The Court finds that the parties have been unable to determine a mutually agreeable deposition date for Defendant’s PMK despite Plaintiff’s efforts. The Court thus grants Plaintiff’s Motion.

 

          Matters for Examination and Requests for Documents

 

Plaintiff’s Notices of Deposition identify thirty-four (34) Matters for Examination and sixteen (16) Requests for Production of Documents.

 

Defendant objects to each Matter for Examination on grounds that they are vague, ambiguous, overly broad, irrelevant, not reasonably particularized, protected by privileges, confidential, and/or calls for expert testimony or the premature disclosure of expert information.

 

Defendant objects to each Request for Production of Documents on grounds that they are vague, ambiguous, overly broad, not reasonably particularized, protected by privileged, and/or confidential.

 

The Court notes that, under Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.230, Plaintiff need only provide matters for examination to guide Defendant in selecting a PMK for deposition. Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.230 provides, in relevant part, that “[i]f the deponent named is not a natural person, the deposition notice shall describe with reasonable particularity the matters on which examination is requested. In that event, the deponent shall designate and produce at the deposition those of its officers, directors, managing agents, employees, or agents who are most qualified to testify on its behalf as to those matters to the extent of any information known or reasonably available to the deponent.”

 

A request for documents in conjunction with a deposition notice is not the equivalent of a request for production of documents under Code of Civil Procedure section 2031.010 et seq. A request for production of documents in a deposition notice need only specify with reasonable particularity the materials or category of materials to be produced by the deponent. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.220, subd. (a)(4).) A motion to compel production of documents related to a deposition only requires a showing of “good cause justifying production.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (b)(1).) “Objections to the competency of the deponent, or to the relevancy, materiality, or admissibility at trial of the testimony or of the materials produced are unnecessary and are not waived by failure to make them before or during the deposition.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.460 subd., (c).)

 

The Court finds that Plaintiff’s Matters for Examination and Requests for Production of Documents are reasonably particularized. Defendant offers no opposition suggesting otherwise.

 

Conclusion

 

Based on the foregoing, Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel the Deposition of Defendant’s Person Most Knowledgeable and Production of Documents is granted. Defendant’s PMK is ordered to appear for a deposition and produce the requested documents. The parties shall meet and confer to determine the date, time, and location of the depositions, which shall take place no later than 30 days from the date of the Court’s issuance of this order. The date may be extended by agreement of the parties.


Case Number: 25NWCV03188    Hearing Date: July 9, 2026    Dept: L

Defendant Haydee Del Carmen Valdivia’s Demurrer to the Second Amended Complaint is OVERRULED.

 

Defendant is ordered to file an answer within 10 days of this order.

 

Defendant to give notice.

 

Background

 

This is an action for breach of contract. On September 9, 2025, Plaintiff Mario Romero, in pro per, (“Plaintiff”) filed this action against Defendant Haydee Del Carmen Valdivia (“Defendant”). On September 25, 2025, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (FAC).

 

On February 24, 2026, the Court sustained Defendant’s Demurrer to the FAC with 30 days leave to amend. (2/24/26 Minute Order.)

 

On March 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Second Amended Complaint (SAC). The SAC alleges one cause of action for breach of contract.

 

Defendant now demurs to the SAC on grounds that it fails to state a claim.

 

On June 26, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition. No reply has been filed as of July 8, 2026.


Legal Standard

 

“[A] demurrer tests the legal sufficiency of the allegations in a complaint.” (Lewis v. Safeway, Inc. (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 385, 388.) A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (See Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (“Donabedian”) (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994 [in ruling on a demurrer, a court may not consider declarations, matters not subject to judicial notice, or documents not accepted for the truth of their contents].) For purposes of ruling on a demurrer, all facts pleaded in a complaint are assumed to be true, but the reviewing court does not assume the truth of conclusions of law. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hosp. Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.) 

 

Where a demurrer is sustained, leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.) The burden is on the plaintiff to show the court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Id.; Lewis v. YouTube, LLC (2015) 244 Cal.App.4th 118, 226.) However, “[i]f there is any reasonable possibility that the plaintiff can state a good cause of action, it is error to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend.” (Youngman v. Nevada Irrigation Dist. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 240, 245.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that the parties meet and confer “in person, by telephone, or by video conference.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) “The demurring party shall file and serve with the demurrer a declaration stating either of the following: (A) The means by which the demurring party met and conferred with the party who filed the pleading subject to demurrer, and that the parties did not reach an agreement resolving the objections raised in the demurrer. (B) That the party who filed the pleading subject to demurrer failed to respond to the meet and confer request of the demurring party or otherwise failed to meet and confer in good faith.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (b)(A).)

 

The Notice of Motion states that, “[p]ursuant to Cal Rules of Court 3.724 & Cal code of Civil Procedure 430.42[,] Defense Counsel reached out to Plaintiff on 6 Apr. 2026 and our meet & confer met [sic] with no results.” (Notice., 1:25-27.)

 

There is no declaration from Defense counsel describing the “means” by which counsel attempted to meet and confer. Although the Court finds the meet and confer requirement has not been met, the Court will proceed with the merits of the Demurrer in interests of judicial efficiency.

 

Discussion

 

As a preliminary matter, the Court notes that Defendant’s “BRIEF STATEMENT OF THE FACTS AND CASE” section inappropriately outlines facts not contained in the SAC. The Court may not consider such extrinsic facts in ruling on the Demurrer. (See Donabedian, supra, 116 Cal.App.4th at p. 994.)

 

          First Cause of Action – Breach of Contract

 

To state a cause of action for breach of contract, Plaintiff must be able to establish “(1) the existence of the contract, (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for nonperformance, (3) defendant’s breach, and (4) the resulting damages to the plaintiff.” (Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 821.) 

 

The plaintiff must attach the contract at issue to the complaint or plead the material terms to assert a breach of contract claim. (Construction Protective Servs., Inc. v. TIG Specialty Ins. Co. (2002) 29 Cal.4th 189, 198–99; San Diego Housing Comm’n v. Indust. Indem. Co. (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 526, 536.) 

 

Defendant argues that the SAC fails to allege any agreement between the parties. Defendant notes that the contract alleged to have been breached is attached as Exhibit A to the FAC. Exhibit A is a lease agreement between non-party Jennifer Yi and the parties. Defendant asserts that Exhibit A does not demonstrate that a contract was created between the parties.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the SAC adequately alleges a cause of action for breach of contract. The SAC alleges Plaintiff and Defendant orally agreed to each pay half of the monthly rent due under the lease agreement and extension. Defendant breached the contract by failing to pay her portion of rent, forcing Plaintiff to cover her portion.

 

No reply has been filed.

 

The breach of contract cause of action alleges the following:

 

“BC-1. Plaintiff alleges that on 05/20/2022 a[n] oral agreement was made between MARIO ROMERO AND HAYDEE DEL CARMEN VALDIVIA. The essential terms of the agreement are as follows: Plaintiff and Defendant entered into an oral agreement to rent the subject property located at 10422 Western Ave., Downey, CA 90241. Under the terms of the oral agreement between Plaintiff and Defendant, Defendant agreed to pay one-half of the monthly rent for, two years, in the amount of $3,250.00. Under the terms of the oral agreement between the Plaintiff and Defendant, Defendant agreed to pay one-half of the monthly rent, for one year, in the amount of $3,450.00 for the extended time on the lease.

 

BC-2. On or about 06/01/2022 defendant breached the agreement by the following acts: Defendant agreed to rent the subject property located at 10422 Western Ave., Downey, CA 90241. Defendant failed and refused to pay her share of the rent as agreed except for one payment made on or around June 2024. As a result, Plaintiff was required to pay Defendant's portion of the rent to avoid eviction and other damages.

 

BC-3. Plaintiff has performed all obligations to defendant except those obligations plaintiff was prevented or excused from performing.

 

BC-4. Plaintiff suffered damages legally (proximately) caused by defendant's breach of the agreement as follows: Defendant currently owes Plaintiff $56,250.00 in unpaid rent.”

 

(SAC, p. 3.)

 

The Court finds that Exhibit 1 attached to the SAC is a residential lease agreement (“Lease”) between the parties and non-party Jennifer Yi for rent of property located at 10422 Western Ave, Downey, CA 90241. (SAC, Exh. 1.) According to the Lease, the parties agreed to pay a monthly rent of $3,250.00 from May 31, 2022 to May 31, 2024. Exhibit 2 attached to the SAC is an “Extension of Lease” which extends the parties’ Lease for one year (until May 31, 2025) with a monthly rent of $3,450.00. (SAC, Exh. 2.)

 

Exhibit 3 attached to the SAC contains a text message (purportedly from Defendant) appearing to show Defendant’s single payment made to Plaintiff in June 2024 referred to in section BC-2. Exhibit 3 also contains a text message indicating a $1,200.00 payment made by Defendant to Plaintiff on July 26, 2026. Exhibit 3 further contains undated text messages from Defendant that state the following: “I sent you the money for rent” and “50/50 right. So for now on I’ll do it this way. […].” (See SAC, Exh. 3.)

 

Reading the allegations liberally, the Court finds that the SAC adequately states a cause of action for breach of oral contract. Specifically, the SAC alleges that the parties orally agreed they would split rent for their 2-year Lease (monthly rent of $3,250.00) and for the 1-year Lease extension (monthly rent of $3,450.00). The material terms of the oral contract are sufficiently alleged—that is all that is required. The SAC further alleges that Defendant failed to pay her share of rent, notwithstanding one payment made in June 2024. Plaintiff was damaged in the amount of $56,250.00 in unpaid rent. The Court thus finds that all elements of breach of oral contract have been alleged. To the extent that there are any ambiguities, such issues can be addressed via discovery. (See Khoury v. Maly's of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616 [“A demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.”].)

 

To the extent that Defendant argues that no such oral contract existed, the merits of such arguments are not appropriate at the demurrer stage.

 

Accordingly, Defendant’s Demurrer to the SAC is OVERRULED.
