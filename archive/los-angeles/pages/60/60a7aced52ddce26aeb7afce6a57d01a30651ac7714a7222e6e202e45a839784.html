  1. PLAINTIFFS MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 1 FOR AN ORDER EXCLUDING ANY EVIDENCE, ARGUMENT OR COMMENT REGARDING INSURANCE COVERAGE MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES; AND DECLARATION OF MARK J. GEYLIKMAN IN SUPPORT OF MOTION filed 4/28/2025

 

GRANT. The insurance evidence has limited probative value and presents a substantial risk of confusion and improper reduction of damages and it is therefore excluded under the collateral-source rule and Evidence Code section 352. As far as alleged subrogation, Defendant EDW conflates a possible subrogation or reimbursement interest held by an insurer with Plaintiff’s standing to pursue Plaintiff’s own damages. The payments between the insurance companies = does not establish that Plaintiff assigned or released the claim, that the payment represented the full amount of the loss, or that Plaintiff is barred from seeking additional damages. The jury will determine the total loss caused by Defendants. As will be discussed in the other MIL’s, any credit or reimbursement issue may be addressed by the Court after the verdict.

 

  1. PLAINTIFFS NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 2 TO EXCLUDE PAUL RUSSELLS TESTIMONY AT TRIAL MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES; SUPPORTING DECLARATION OF STEPHEN E. FOSTER filed 3/17/2026

 

GRANT. First, the court finds that Plaintiffs made a complete and timely exchange under section 2034.260 and have objected to the challenged testimony. Plaintiffs therefore may seek exclusion under section 2034.300. Second, the defense declarations assign both experts the same subjects. Defendant EDW had already retained Peter Nils Van Dijk on those subjects before supplementally designating Paul Russell. Paul Russell's designation therefore did not satisfy section 2034.280, and Defendant obtained no order authorizing augmentation. The withdrawal of Peter Nils Van Dijk's does not validate the designation. Third, Defendant EDW knew Plaintiffs sought exclusion no later than its receipt of the March 2026 motion, which Defendant acknowledged by email. Approximately six months passed without a motion to augment. Defendant EDW could have sought augmentation in the alternative while maintaining that its original supplementation was valid. Thus, Defendant EDW's failure to comply was unreasonable. There is no explanation for failing to seek augmentation after receiving the exclusion motion. Defendant EDW has known for six months that Plaintiffs were actively seeking exclusion, even if Defendant EDW considered their position legally incorrect.

The Court has considered the early disclosure of Paul Russell, Plaintiffs' requests for deposition dates, the contemplated opportunity to depose him, and the substantial effect of excluding Defendant EDW's sole remaining expert. The September 25, 2025 correspondence does not establish any agreement. The overlap was identified early and the designation was challenged, and given those facts the Defendant' EDWs prolonged failure to seek the statutory relief available to it was not reasonable.

As far as the alternative request that the court “treat this opposition as a motion to augment/replace its expert witness”, the request is denied. There is no noticed augmentation motion before the Court. The Court cannot grant augmentation merely by relabeling the opposition as a motion, because, as the plaintiff points out, the opposition does not provide the procedural and evidentiary showing required. Lastly, the expert-discovery deadline has expired. The Court declines to convert the opposition into an augmentation motion and denies the request to defer resolution so Defendant EDW may pursue relief it could have sought during the preceding six months. Plaintiffs' motion in limine is GRANTED. Pursuant to section 2034.300, Defendant is precluded from offering Paul Russell's expert opinion testimony on the subjects identified above.

  1. PLAINTIFFS NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 3 TO EXCLUDE DOMINICK CAPELOUTO AND TOM BLATCHLEYS TESTIMONY AT TRIAL MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES; SUPPORTING DECLARATION OF STEPHEN E. FOSTER filed 3/17/2026

 

GRANT. The operative date here is the initial trial date. The demand, exchange, and expert-discovery deadlines are calculated from that date. The code does not require a new demand every time trial is continued. Therefore, Defendants’ contention that the trial continuance eliminated their obligation to comply with the expert-disclosure statutes is not persuasive. So while the continuance order reset applicable deadlines, it did not vacate the prior expert-exchange demand or authorize Defendants to identify experts informally on the eve of trial. Therefore, the Defendants needed to have properly designated the proposed experts, provided the required declarations and writings, and made them available for deposition, or seek leave to submit tardy information or augment their designation. They failed to do so. Moreover, Plaintiffs’ motion in limine #3 was filed more than six months ago, giving Defendants ample opportunity to seek appropriate relief, move to augment their designation, or otherwise cure the alleged defect. Their failure to take any action during that time further demonstrates that their nondisclosure was unreasonable. Given Plaintiffs’ timely compliance and the resulting prejudice, exclusion is required under Code of Civil Procedure section 2034.300.

 

  1. PLAINTIFFS' MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 4 FOR AN ORDER EXCLUDING ANY EVIDENCE, ARGUMENT OR COMMENT AS TO THE LAFD STATISTICAL REPORT MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES; AND DECLARATION OF MARK J. GEYLIKMAN IN SUPPORT OF MOTION filed 8/24/2026

Grant. The Court sustains Plaintiffs’ objections to the fire-department report on hearsay and foundation grounds. The report is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matters stated, and Defendants have not established an applicable hearsay exception or adequate foundation. In some circumstances, similar to police reports, the official-records exception may permit admission of parts of a report documenting observations made by a percipient public employee. That exception, however, does not automatically make admissible unsupported opinions, estimates, or valuations. The report’s information concerning the amount of loss lacks a foundation based on personal knowledge and would most certainly rely on additional hearsay from insurers, contractors, or other third parties. A firefighter with personal knowledge may testify to what the firefighter personally observed, provided the witness was properly disclosed and noticed. The report itself, and the estimates or valuations contained in it, are inadmissible.

 

  1. PLAINTIFFS MOTION IN LIMINE NO. 5 FOR AN ORDER: (A) EXCLUDING ANY EVIDENCE, ARGUMENT OR COMMENT AS TO A JULY 15, 2026, LAFD LETTER SURREPTITIOUSLY SOLICITED BY WPES COUNSEL AND ITS PRIVATE INVESTIGATOR; AND (B) EXCLUDING ANY EVIDENCE, ARGUMENT, etc. Filed 9/3/2026

The motion is granted in part and denied in part. The letter itself is hearsay, and Defendants have not established any applicable hearsay exception; it is therefore excluded. To the extent Defendants seek to present testimony from an arson investigator concerning independently observed or otherwise admissible facts relating to the fire, the Court will determine the admissibility of that testimony at a hearing outside the presence of the jury, pursuant to Evidence Code section 402. Plaintiffs’ motion for sanctions is denied.

I.                BACKGROUND

 

On or about July 4, 2023, Plaintiff Mario Aquino (“Plaintiff”) purchased a 2023 Cadillac Escalade (the “Vehicle”). (Complaint ¶¶ 8.) The Vehicle was defective. (Complaint ¶ 9.)

 

On December 5, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendant General Motors LLC (“Defendant”) and Does 1 through 10, alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of express warranty; (2) violation of Song-Beverly Act – breach of implied warranty; and (3) violation of Song-Beverly Act – section 1793.2, subdivision (b).

 

On May 21, 2026, Plaintiff filed a notice of settlement of entire case.

 

On August 5, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion for attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses (the “Motion”). On September 4, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition. On September 11, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply.

 

II.             LEGAL STANDARD

California Civil Code section 1794, subdivision (d) states:

 

If the buyer prevails in an action under this section, the buyer shall be allowed by the court to recover as part of the judgment a sum equal to the aggregate amount of costs and expenses, including attorney's fees based on actual time expended, determined by the court to have been reasonably incurred by the buyer in connection with the commencement and prosecution of such action.

 

“The plain wording of the statute requires the trial court to base the fee award upon actual time expended on the case, as long as such fees are reasonably incurred – both from the standpoint of time spent and the amount charged.” (Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers of California, Inc. (“Robertson”) (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 785, 817, original italics.) The “prevailing party has the burden of showing that the fees incurred were reasonably necessary to the conduct of the litigation, and were reasonable in amount.” (Id. at pp. 817-818.) “The amount of an attorney fee to be awarded is a matter within the sound discretion of the trial court.” (Id. at p. 822.)

 

“[T]he lodestar adjustment method is the prevailing rule for statutory attorney fee awards to be applied in the absence of clear legislative intent to the contrary[.]” (Robertson, supra, 144 Cal.App.4th at p. 818.) The lodestar adjustment method “requires the trial court to first determine a touchstone or lodestar figure based on a careful compilation of the actual time spent and reasonable hourly compensation for each attorney. [Citation] The touchstone figure may then be augmented or diminished by taking various relevant factors into account, including (1) the novelty and difficulty of the questions involved and the skill displayed in presenting them; (2) the extent to which the nature of the litigation precluded other employment by the attorneys; and (3) the contingent nature of the fee award, based on the uncertainty of prevailing on the merits and of establishing eligibility for the award.” (Id. at p. 819.) However, a trial court is “not required to apply a multiplier.” (Id. at p. 822, original italics.)

 

III.           DISCUSSION

Plaintiff moves for an order awarding Plaintiff attorneys’ fees and costs in the sum of $43,737.00, reflecting $42,948.75 in attorneys’ fees (including a multiplier of 0.5, or $14,316.25). (Memorandum p. 1.) Plaintiff also moves for costs in the sum of $788.25. (Id.)

Hourly Rates:

Defendant argues Plaintiff’s counsels’ hourly rates are excessive and unsupported. (Opposition pp. 10-13.)

Plaintiff’s counsels billed at rates ranging from $525.00 per hour to $700.00 per hour. (Daghighian Decl., ¶¶ 7-11.) Plaintiff’s counsel Sepehr Daghighian has “20+ years of litigation experience” and billed at $625.00 per hour. (Id., ¶¶ 5, 7.) Plaintiff’s counsel Michael H. Rosenstein has “30+ years of litigation experience” and billed at $700.00 per hour. (Id., ¶ 8.) Plaintiff’s counsel Brian T. Shippen-Murray, who played a supervisory of counsel role, has been an attorney since 2012 and billed at $550.00 per hour. (Id., ¶ 9.) Plaintiff’s counsel Alastair Frederick Hamblin has been practicing lemon law since 2015 and billed at $550.00 per hour as an associate. (Id., ¶ 10.) Plaintiff’s counsel Miguel A. Ortiz has practiced law since 2006 and billed at $525.00 per hour as an associate. (Id., ¶¶ 11.)

The court recognizes that Plaintiff’s counsel possesses experience in this area of law. However, based on the court’s own observations in this matter and its review of counsel’s submissions, the court finds the hourly rates requested, are in part, above what is reasonably warranted. 

The court finds $600.00 per hour is reasonable for Sepehr Daghighian and Michael H. Rosenstein, $500.00 per hour is reasonable for Brian T. Shippen-Murray, Alastair Frederick Hamblin, and Miguel A. Ortiz.  (See Goglin v. BMW of North America, LLC (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 462, 464, 473-474 [concluding a trial court did not abuse its discretion in finding a $575 hourly rate was appropriate in a Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act action.])

Time Billed:

Plaintiff’s counsels billed a total of 49.40 hours on this matter. (Daghighian Decl., Ex. A pdf p. 4.)

In opposition, Defendant argues Plaintiff’s counsels’ hours billed are excessive. (Opposition pp. 3-8.)

The court agrees with Defendant that some of the tasks billed are excessive. It is appropriate to reduce a fee award because of “a lack of efficiency in litigating the case and a lack of clarity in tasks performed.” (See Mikhaeilpoor v. BMW of North America, LLC (2020) 48 Cal.App.5th 240 256 [“Further, counsel’s billing entries demonstrate a lack of efficiency in litigating the case and a lack of clarity in tasks performed. This evidence supports the trial court’s finding that plaintiff’s counsel failed to act efficiently.”]) Plaintiff’s counsels are experienced in this area of the law. Thus, the court will adjust the reflected time to reflect the court’s understanding that the time billed should reflect Plaintiff’s counsels’ expertise in this area of the law.

Lodestar Multiplier:

Plaintiff requests a 0.5 lodestar multiplier. (Memorandum pp. 11-13.)

The court understands that litigating Song-Beverly actions requires technical expertise.  But “a trial court should award a multiplier for exceptional representation only when the quality of representation far exceeds the quality of representation that would have been provided by an attorney of comparable skill and experience billing at the hourly rate used in the lodestar calculation. Otherwise, the fee award will result in unfair double counting and be unreasonable.” (Ketchum v. Moses (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122, 1139.)  Here, Plaintiff’s counsels displayed the skill, proficiency, and technical expertise commensurate with counsels’ experience and specialty. Nothing before the court indicates Plaintiff’s counsels’ representation exceeded that which would have been provided by other attorneys of comparable skill and experience billing at counsels’ rates.

 

Accordingly, the court denies Plaintiff’s request for an enhancement.

 

Costs:

 

Plaintiff requests $788.25 in costs. (Memorandum pp. 13-14.)

 

In opposition, Defendant argues the request should be denied in its entirety because Plaintiff has not filed a Memorandum of Costs and has only provided a table generated by Counsels’ billing system. (Opp., at p. 13.) Thus, Defendant argues that the reasonableness or necessity of the costs are uncertain. (Id.)

 

The court agrees with Defendant that Plaintiff was required to file a Memorandum of Costs to recover non-fee litigation costs, like filing and service of process fees, even in a Song-Beverly action. Pursuant to California Rules of Court, rule 3.1700, subdivision (a)(1): “A prevailing party who claims costs must serve and file a memorandum of costs within 15 days after the date of service of the notice of entry of judgment or dismissal by the clerk under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.5 or the date of service of written notice of entry of judgment or dismissal, or within 180 days after entry of judgment, whichever is first. The memorandum of costs must be verified by a statement of the party, attorney, or agent that to the best of his or her knowledge the items of cost are correct and were necessarily incurred in the case.”

 

Accordingly, the court denies Plaintiff’s request for costs.

 

IV.           CONCLUSION

 

For the foregoing reasons, the request for attorneys’ fees is granted in the reduced sum of $11,470.00, to be paid by Defendant to Plaintiff’s counsel within thirty (30) days of this order. The request for costs is denied
