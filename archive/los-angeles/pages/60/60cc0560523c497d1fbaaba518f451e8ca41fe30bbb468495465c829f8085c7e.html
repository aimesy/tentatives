ANALYSIS:

 

            State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company filed this subrogation action against Maritza Barragan and Yuritzi Betancio Perez.  Barragan defaulted and Perez answered.  State Farm then propounded eight requests for admission on Perez.  Perez failed to respond, so State Farm now moves to have the requests deemed admitted.

 

A party must respond to requests for admissions within 30 days after service of such requests.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.250, subd. (a).)  If a party fails to do so, it waives the right to object, and the propounding party may move for an order that the genuineness of any documents and truth of any matters specified in the requests be deemed admitted.  (Id., § 2033.280, subd. (a), (b).) 

 

State Farm served Perez with the requests for admission in January 2026.  To date, no responses have been served.  Perez has not filed an opposition indicating otherwise.  Accordingly, the Court GRANTS State Farm’s motion and the contents of State Farm’s first set of requests for admission are deemed admitted. 

 

            Perez must also pay sanctions for causing State Farm to move to deem the requests for admissions admitted.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.280, subd. (c).)  State Farm requests $460.00—for two hours of its counsel’s work at $200.00 per hour plus $60.00 in filing fees—from Perez.  The Court finds this request reasonable.  Perez must pay State Farm $460.00 within 30 days of this order.

 

            State Farm is to give notice.

DEMURRER TO COMPLAINT

(Code Civ. Proc., §§ 430.10, et seq.)

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

 

            The Court SUSTAINS Krystal Dry’s demurrer WITH 20 DAYS’ LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

            Dry is to give notice.

REPLY:                     None filed as of August 14, 2026                   [  ] Late                       [X] None

 

ANALYSIS:

 

            Fred Martin Wimberley filed a document styled as a “Petition” in which he alleged that Krystal Dry is “guilty of a Building and Safety Code by not having the security gate . . . fixed properly,” and “the black trash can . . . hasn’t been emptied for several days.”  Wimberley requests a “court order . . . directing Dry” to fix both of those issues.  To the extent Wimberley’s filing is a complaint, Dry now demurs to the complaint pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (a) for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, and subdivision (e) for failure to state sufficient facts to constitute a cause of action. 

 

 

While Dry is correct that a court in a limited civil action cannot issue a permanent injunction unless permitted under Code of Civil Procedure section 85, 86, or 580, that does not deprive this Court of subject matter jurisdiction.  Dry’s argument pertains to whether this case is properly classified as a limited or unlimited action.

 

As for whether Wimberley’s pleading states a cause of action:  The complaint fails to comply with California Rules of Court, rules 2.100, 2.108, 2.111 and 2.112 to such a degree that the Court is unable to determine what cause of action Wimberley is attempting to allege.

 

And as for any new allegations in Wimberley’s opposition, they do not appear in a pleading.  The Court does not consider them in ruling on a demurrer.  Wimberley may add allegations to an amended pleading.

 

The Court SUSTAINS Dry’s demurrer.  Wimberley may file a first amended complaint that complies with the California Rules of Court within 20 days of notice of this ruling.

 

            Dry is to give notice.
