I.                Background

 

On April 26, 2023, Plaintiff Serge Svetnoy (“Plaintiff”) filed a First Amended Complaint against Defendants Rust Movie Productions, LLC; Hannah Gutierrez Reed; Sarah Zachry; Seth Kenny; Dave Halls; El Dorado Pictures, Inc.; Cavalry Media, Inc.; Thomasville Pictures, LLC; Brittany House Pictures; Short Porch Pictures, LLC; 3rd Shift Media, LLC; Alexander R. On July 17, 2026, Moving Defendants Baldwin and El Dorado Pictures (“Moving Defendants”) filed the instant Application of Matthew D. Kelly to Appear as Counsel Pro Hac Vice (the “Application”), which requests the Court permit Matthew D. Kelly (“Applicant”) to appear as counsel pro hac vice in the instant matter.

 

On July 29, 2026, Plaintiff filed a Non-Opposition, stating Plaintiff does not oppose the instant Application.

 

To date, no other response or opposition has been filed to the Application.

 

II.              Legal Standard

 

For out-of-state counsel to appear before a California court as counsel pro hac vice, out-of-state counsel must be (1) a member in good standing of another state bar, (2) eligible to practice in any United State court or the highest court in another jurisdiction, and (3) associated with an active member of the California Bar as attorney of record. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).)

 

Further, the application must state:¿ 

¿ 

(1) The applicant's residence and office address;¿ ¿ 

(2) The courts to which the applicant has been admitted to practice and the dates of admission;¿ 

(3) That the applicant is a licensee in good standing in those courts;¿ 

(4) That the applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court;¿ 

(5) The title of each court and cause in which the applicant has filed an application to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this state in the preceding two years, the date of each application, and whether or not it was granted; and¿ 

(6) The name, address, and telephone number of the active licensee of the State Bar of California who is attorney of record.¿ 

¿ 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(d).)¿ 

 

Out-of-state counsel must file a verified application and a proof of service, which provides that a copy of the application and a notice of hearing have been served on all parties and on the California Bar at its San Francisco office. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(c)(1).)

 

No person is eligible to appear as counsel pro hac vice if the person is: (1) A resident of the State of California; (2) Regularly employed in the State of California; or (3) Regularly engaged in substantial business, professional, or other activities in the State of California. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a).)

 

Out-of-state counsel is also subject to the California Bar’s Rules of Professional Conduct. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(f).)

 

Finally, any application fee set by the State Bar of California must be paid by counsel pro hac vice. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(e).)

 

III.            Discussion

 

Moving Defendants submit a verified Application for the Applicant to appear pro hac vice.

 

            Here, the verified Application provides the following information: (1) the Applicant’s residential address is located in Milford, Michigan and office address is 233 South Wacker Drive, Suite 5500, Chicago, Illinois, 60606; (2) the Applicant was admitted to the State Bar of Illinois in 2009; the State Bar of Michigan in 2022; and various federal courts in 2012, 2015-2016, 2021, and 2023-2024; (3) the Applicant is in good standing in each of the courts the Applicant has been admitted; (4) the Applicant has applied to appear pro hac vice in California in Case No. 30 2025-01522348-CU-FR-CJC in the preceding two years; and (5) Arthur J. Chapman, California State Bar No. 79866, and Segal McCambridge Singer Mahoney are the attorneys of record with the following address and telephone numbers: Segal McCambridge Singer Mahoney, 10866 Wilshire Blvd., Suite 400, Los Angeles, California, 90024; phone number (310) 207-7722. (Verified App., Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40.)

 

            Further, the Application states that the Applicant paid the pro hac vice fee to the State Bar of California and served the Application on the State Bar of California Office of Admissions. (Verified App., p. 3; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40.)

 

Finally, Moving Defendants file proof of service demonstrating the Application and notice of hearing were served on Plaintiffs, all responding parties, and the State Bar of California. (See Verified App., p. 3; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(c)(1).)

 

Thus, the unopposed Application is GRANTED.

 

IV.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Defendants Alexander R. Baldwin III and El Dorado Pictures, Inc.’s Application of Matthew D. Kelly to Appear as Counsel Pro Hac Vice and Application is GRANTED.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice.

II.              Procedural Issues

 

The Court notes the parties submitted defective separate statements in support of the moving and opposing papers. The Court admonishes the parties that a separate statement is meant to be “a concise list of the material facts and the evidence that supports them.” (Beltran v. Hard Rock Hotel Licensing, Inc. (2023) 97 Cal.App.5th 865, 875, superseded by statute on other grounds.) It should “provide[ ] a convenient and expeditious vehicle” to resolve matters in a summary fashion. (Collins v. Hertz Corp. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 64, 74, italics added.) However, the Separate Statements here fail to note which element of which specific cause of action each recitation in the Separate Statement refers. The Separate Statement here fails to comply with the guidelines set forth in California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350.

 

The Court is neither required nor inclined to scour the briefing and evidence to overcome the burden of a defective Separate Statement. Further, the Court finds the Notice of Motion is confusing as to whether the Motion is being brought as a motion for summary judgment or a motion for summary adjudication. Indeed, the caption states the motion is for summary adjudication but the body of the notice of motion states the motion is for summary judgment. Defendants are cautioned to ensure any notices of motion comport with the body of the motion and the relief requested to the Court.

 

Based thereon, the Moving party is ordered to submit a new Separate Statement in Support of Defendants’ Motion for Summary Adjudication/Judgment strictly in compliance with the California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350. Non-compliance may result in denial of the Motion.

 

The responding party is required to submit their Separate Statement in Opposition, also strictly in compliance with California Rules of Court, rule 3.1350.

 

Moving Parties’ Separate Statement is due by August 14, 2026. The Opposing Party’ Statement is due by August 28, 2026.

ANALYSIS:

 

I.                Background

 

On June 30, 2025, Plaintiffs Katey M. Miro and Lucinda N. Miro (“Plaintiffs”) filed a Complaint for Violation of Statutory Damages against Defendants FCA US, LLC and Bravo Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram of Alhambra (“Defendants”) and Does 1-10.

 

On December 4, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) for Violation of Statutory Damages against Defendants.

 

On January 13, 2026, Defendant Bravo Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram of Alhambra filed an Answer to the FAC.

 

On April 2, 2026, FCA US LLC filed an Answer to the FAC.

 

On June 16, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures Pursuant to California Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Monetary Sanctions (the “Motion”) against FCA US.

 

On July 30, 2026, FCA US filed an Opposition.

 

No Reply has been filed.

 

II.              Legal Standard and Discussion

 

Plaintiffs move to compel FCA US’s further disclosures under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and requests the Court impose sanctions against FCA US under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 as well. The Motion states that FCA US has provided initial disclosures under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 but contends the disclosures are deficient as they are unverified and do not account for records Plaintiffs believe are in FCA US’s possession. (See Motion, pp. 6-14.)

 

In Opposition, FCA US states the Motion should be denied because Plaintiffs’ counsel failed to meet and confer in good faith regarding the initial disclosures; the Code of Civil Procedure does not require verified initial disclosures, and Plaintiffs improperly rely on the a code provision from the Discovery Act to state verifications are required in this instance; and FCA US provided initial disclosures and supplemental disclosures prior to the instant hearing. (See Opp., pp. 1-11.) Further, FCA US states sanctions are unwarranted as FCA US has complied and acted in good faith regarding the initial disclosures. (See Opp., pp. 11-12.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 “only applies to a civil action seeking restitution or replacement of a motor vehicle pursuant to [Code of Civil Procedure section 871.20]” and requires that within 60 days after filing a responsive pleading, all parties shall provide to all other parties an initial disclosure and documents pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivisions (f), (g), and (h).

 

Here, the parties do not dispute that Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 applies to the instant action.

 

The Motion is brought as a motion to compel initial disclosures under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26; however, the Court finds the Motion is, in substance, a motion to compel further responses to FCA US’s mandatory disclosures provided to Plaintiffs. Indeed, the parties do not dispute that FCA US provided initial disclosures under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 to Plaintiffs (see Motion; Opp., Cronin Decl., pp. 1-4, Exhs. 1, 3-4); rather, Plaintiffs’ Motion centers on the assertion that FCA US provided disclosures were insufficient for various reasons. (See Motion.)

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 does not provide a legal mechanism for compelling further responses to mandatory disclosures provided under Section 871.26, and Plaintiff’s Motion is neither brought pursuant to the Discovery Act nor complies with the Discovery Act’s requirements for a motion to compel further responses.

 

Further, the Motion attempts to add on Discovery Act requirements, such as verifications, into Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 without any supporting legal authority.

 

The Court may not grant relief that is not contemplated or permitted by the moving statute and referenced legal authority, and Plaintiff fails to provide any legal authority authorizing or mandating the Court to proceed otherwise.

 

            For these reasons, the Motion is DENIED.

 

III.            Conclusion & Order

 

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiffs Katey M. Miro and Lucinda N Miro’s Motion to Compel Initial Disclosures Pursuant to California Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26 and Request for Monetary Sanctions is DENIED.

 

Moving parties are ordered to give notice.
