Case Name: Prioette v. Abbvie Inc., et al.

Case No.: 23STCV30404

Matter: Application for Pro Hac Vice Admission 

Moving Party: Plaintiff Perry Proiette

Responding Party: Unopposed

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Application is granted.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


Plaintiff Perry Proiette alleges that he is an optician and that Defendants Abbvie Inc. and Allergan, Inc. came into Plaintiff's workplace to unlawfully distribute samples of Vuity eye drops that caused Plaintiff to experience vitreous detachment within his eye. On January 27, 2026, Plaintiff filed the First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) for (1) negligence, (2) products liability (design), (3) products liability (warning), and (4) negligence per se.

Plaintiff now seeks pro hac vice admission for Kyle A. Dingman.

The Application is granted because it meets all requirements of Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 9.40. 

Moving party to give notice.



Case Number: 24STCV10426    Hearing Date: July 13, 2026    Dept: 310

Tentative Ruling

Judge Kevin C. Brazile

Department 310

Hearing Date: July 13, 2026

Case Name: Vardan v. Shadow Mountain View Condominium Association, Inc., et al.

Case No.: 24STCV10426

Matter: Motion to Compel Further Responses

Moving Party: Plaintiff Antonyan Vardan

Responding Party: Defendant Shadow Mountain View Condominium Association

Notice: OK

Ruling: The Motion is granted in part.


Moving party to give notice.


The Court encourages all parties to appear remotely via LA CourtConnect.  If submitting on the Court's tentative ruling, please follow the instructions provided above.


On May 28, 2025, Plaintiff Antonyan Vardan filed the operative Second Amended Complaint (“SAC”) asserting 24 causes of action.  Plaintiff alleges that after he objected to an unpermitted gate, Defendant “Kazamel orchestrated a retaliatory and procedurally invalid vote to remove him [from the HOA board]. That marked the beginning of a targeted effort to silence and eliminate Plaintiff. Over the following years, he faced illegal vehicle tows, wage garnishment, perjured testimony in court, denial of HOA election rights, and mass defamatory communications—all designed to isolate him socially and professionally. Coordinated actions by HOA members, attorneys, and vendors—under Kazamel’s leadership— culminated in Plaintiff’s job loss, failed businesses, and personal bankruptcy in 2025.”

Plaintiff now seeks to compel further responses to his RFPs, set four, from Defendant Shadow Mountain View Condominium Association, Inc.

The Motion is granted as to RFP no. 1 (decision to record lien). The objections lack merit, and a privilege log should be provided, although it need not contain any documents generated by counsel for this lawsuit. 

The Motion is denied as to RFP nos. 2-4 (pre-lien letter, accounting, communications) because a sufficient response was provided and if Plaintiff seeks additional documents he can propound broader discovery requests.

The Motion is granted as to RFP nos. 5-6 (communications between Defendants about the lien; internal review) because a privilege log must be provided.

The Motion is granted as to RFP no. 7 (internal dispute resolution) because the objection lacks merit.

The Motion is denied as to RFP nos. 8-23 because Plaintiff’s separate statement is deficient on this discovery.

The Motion is granted in part as set forth herein. Further responses/production within 30 days. No sanctions.

Plaintiff also seeks to compel further responses to his RFPs, set three, from Defendant Tarek Kazamel. Kazamel did not file an opposition, such that the Motion is granted against Kazamel. Further responses/production within 30 days.

Moving party to give notice.
