Case Number: 25STCP00038    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 June 24, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCP00038 [LEAD] [Related Case 25VECV02593]

CASE NAME:                        Karen Shainsky v. Farmers Insurance Exchange; and related cross-actions

MOVING PARTY:                Cross-Defendant Farmers Insurance Exchange

OPPOSING PARTY:             Cross-Complainant Karen Shainsky

PROCEEDING:                      MOTION FOR STAY OF PROCEEDINGS

RULING SUMMARY:          Farmers Insurance Exchange’s Motion for Stay of Proceedings is denied.  The Court sets the case for a Case Management Conference on ____________, 2026, at 8:30AM, in Dept. 413. 

 

Background

 

On January 3, 2025, Karen Shainsky (“Shainsky”) filed a petition to confirm arbitration award against Farmers Insurance Exchange (“Farmers”) under lead case number 25STCP00038, and on March 18, 2025, the Court confirmed the award acknowledging that the award did not address coverage issues and that any issues could be resolved in a subsequent lawsuit.

 

On May 9, 2025, Farmers filed a Complaint under case number 25VECV02593 against Shainsky for declaratory relief seeking a judicial declaration of rights, duties and obligations under the Policy (“Declaratory Relief Action”).  Shainsky filed a Cross-Complaint for breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and unfair business practices (“Breach Cross-Complaint”) against Farmers.

 

On January 28, 2026, the cases were ordered related.

 

On February 26, 2026, Farmers filed a motion seeking an order staying the proceedings of Shainsky’s Cross-Complaint pending the outcome of the Declaratory Relief Action.

 

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

Shainsky asks the Court to take judicial notice of (1) Farmer’s Complaint for Declaratory Relief filed on May 9, 2025, in case no. 25VECV02593; (2) Shainsky’s Cross-Complaint filed on October 6, 2025, in case no. 25VECV02593; and (3) Court Order dated January 28, 2026 consolidating cases numbered 25STCP00038 and 25VECV02593 and making 25STCP00038 the lead case.

 

The request is granted. (Evid. Code § 452(d), (h); see¿Kalnoki v. First American Trustee Servicing Solutions, LLC (2017) 8 Cal.App.5th 23,37.) The Court takes judicial notice of these documents only as to “the existence, content and authenticity of public records and other specified documents”; it does not take judicial notice of the truth of any factual matters asserted in those documents. (Dominguez v. Bonta (2022) 87 Cal. App. 5th 389, 400.)¿

 

I.          Legal Standard

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 128 empowers the Court “to enforce order in the proceedings before it” and “to own and control its process and orders so as to make them conform to law and justice.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 128(a)). “[A] court ordinarily has inherent power, in its discretion, to stay proceedings when such a stay will accommodate the ends of justice . . . . [T]he power to stay proceedings is incidental to the power inherent in every court to control the disposition of the causes on its docket with economy of time and effort for itself, for counsel, and for litigants.” (OTO, L.L.C. v. Kho (2019) 8 Cal. 5th 111, 141) (citing People v. Bell (1984) 159 Cal.App.3d 323, 329; Landis v. North American Co. (1936) 299 U.S. 248, 254).)  

 

“Where the rights of the parties to the second action cannot be properly determined until the questions raised in the first action are settled the second action should be stayed.” (See, e.g., Thriftimart, Inc. v. Superior Court of L.A. Cty. (1962) 202 Cal. App. 2d 421, 425)(citing Simmons v. Superior Court (1950) 96 Cal.App.2d 119, 130.) 

 

II.        Discussion

 

As Farmers argues, the Court has discretion to stay a case until another case is adjudicated, where the outcome of the case that a party seeks to stay depends on the outcome of the other case.  But that is not the situation here.  Farmers filed its action for declaratory relief, and, in the same action, Shainsky filed her cross-complaint alleging, among other claims, breach of contract and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.  Both the main action and the cross-complaint involve issues regarding the parties’ respective rights and obligations under the applicable insurance policy.  Therefore, it would not be appropriate to require Shainsky to wait to pursue the cross-complaint until the Declaratory Relief Action is adjudicated. 

 

The Court acknowledges Farmers’ concerns about litigating both at the same time, but, as Shainsky acknowledges, the Court can address those concerns through case management, including staging of discovery if appropriate.  Farmers (as well as Shainsky) have the right to seek such orders from the Court.  And it would behoove counsel for the parties to meet and confer regarding discovery matters before a Case Management Conference is held, so that the case may be litigated as efficiently as possible for their clients. 

 

Conclusion

 

For the reasons explained above, Farmers Insurance Exchange’s Motion for Stay of Proceedings is denied.  The Court sets the case for a Case Management Conference on ____________, 2026, at 8:30AM, in Dept. 413.

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 June 24, 2026

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV06025

CASE NAME:                        Raymundo Perez Hernandez v. General Motors, LLC                       

MOVING PARTY:                 Plaintiff, Raymundo Perez Hernandez

OPPOSING PARTY:             Defendant, General Motors, LLC

PROCEEDING:                      MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES AND DOCUMENTS TO PLAINTIFF’S AMENDED REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION OF DOCUMENTS, SET ONE

RULING SUMMARY:          Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production, Set One is GRANTED.  Defendant is ordered to serve verified further responses that comply with the mandatory language for responses in section 2031.310, 2031.320, and 2031.330 of the Code of Civil Procedure and produce all responsive documents within 30 days of the date of this order.             

Background

 

This is a lemon law action arising from Raymundo Perez Hernandez’s (“Plaintiff”) purchase of a 2023 Chevrolet Silverado 1500 (“Subject Vehicle”) from Chevrolet of Montebello on October 6, 2023.

 

On March 4, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint against General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) alleging causes of action for: (1) violation of Civil Code Section 1793.2(d);¿(2) violation of 1793.2(b);¿(3) violation of 1793.2(a)(3);¿(4) breach of express warranty; and (5) breach¿of the implied warranty of merchantability.

 

On May 19, 2026, Plaintiff filed a motion to compel further responses to requests for production of documents, set one (“RPDs”).  On June 12, 2026, Defendant filed an opposition.

 

I.         Legal Standard

 

On receipt of responses to requests for production, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that a statement of compliance is incomplete, a representation of inability to comply is inadequate, incomplete, or evasive, or an objection in the response is without merit. (See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2030.310, subd. (a)(1)-(3).)  The moving party must also include reasons why further answers should be ordered: legal or factual arguments why the answers given were incomplete or nonresponsive, or the objections invalid.  (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(c).)  The responding party has the burden to justify objections in response to a motion filed to compel further responses.  (Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255.)   

 

II. Meet and Confer

 

A¿motion to compel further responses “shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration.”¿¿(Code Civ. Proc., §;¿2031.310, subd. (b)(2).)¿¿The¿declaration must state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented in the motion.¿¿(Code Civ. Proc.,¿§ 2016.040.)¿¿“[A]¿reasonable and good faith attempt at informal resolution entails something more than bickering with [opposing] counsel….¿ Rather, the law requires that counsel attempt to talk the matter over, compare their views, consult, and deliberate.”¿¿(Clement v. Alegre¿(2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1277, 1294.)¿¿ 

 

On December 1, 2025, the parties engaged in a telephonic meet and confer regarding the allegedly inadequate responses. (Jiang Decl., ¶ 23.) The parties engaged in a second telephonic meet and confer regarding the alleged inadequate amended responses. (Jiang Decl., ¶ 28.)

 

Therefore, the parties have satisfied the meet and confer requirement.

 

III. Discussion

 

Plaintiff moves for an order compelling a further response to RPDs Nos. 16 and 19-21.

 

No. 16: All DOCUMENTS, including but not limited to electronically stored information and electronic mails, concerning or relating to any internal analysis or investigation by YOU or on YOUR behalf regarding the AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT in vehicles of the same year, make, and model as the SUBJECT VEHICLE. [This request shall be interpreted to include, but not be limited to, any such investigation to determine the root cause of such AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT, any such investigation to design a permanent repair procedure for such AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT, any such investigation into the failure rates of parts associated with such AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT, any cost analysis for implementing a proposed repair procedures, any savings analysis not implementing a proposed repair procedures, etc.]

 

No. 19: All DOCUMENTS, including but not limited to electronically stored information and electronic mails, concerning customer complaints, claims, reported failures, and warranty claims related to AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT, in vehicles of the same year, make, and model as the SUBJECT VEHICLE, including but not limited to any databases in YOUR possession with information from dealers, service departments, parts departments, or warranty departments, and all documents concerning YOUR response to each complaint, claim or reported failure.

 

No. 20: All DOCUMENTS, including but not limited to electronically stored information and electronic mails, concerning failure rates of vehicles of the same year, make, and model as the SUBJECT VEHICLE as a result of AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT.

 

No. 21: All DOCUMENTS, including but not limited to electronically stored information and electronic mails, concerning or relating to any fixes for AUDIO SYSTEM DEFECT in vehicles of the same year, make, and model as the SUBJECT VEHICLE.

 

Defendant objects to all requests on the grounds that the relevant information to this request was provided previously. Defendant further objects based on attorney-client privilege, work product, and the request is overbroad. Defendant later amended their response to provide the BATES-stamped documents that were responsive to this request.

 

Plaintiff argues Defendant’s amended responses are not Code compliant, and the documents listed in the BATES-stamped lists are not responsive to the requests. More specifically, Plaintiff argues the amended responses do not contain a requested root cause investigation document, failure rate document, internal investigation, or analysis document, or any documents relating to the creation and issuance of important service campaigns, Recalls or TSBs. (Mot., p. 7:4-7; Jiang Decl., ¶ 31.)

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff that Defendant’s objections are largely without merit and fail to conform to the requirements of the Discovery Act. Namely, Defendant does not state whether its responses comply in whole or in part or the reason it is unable to comply in whole. The RPDs are not overly broad, unduly burdensome, vague, and ambiguous. Given that the RFP seeks, among other things, Defendant’s internal analysis or investigations regarding similar vehicles. The requests are reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.

 

Therefore, the Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production, Set One is GRANTED.  

 

Conclusion

 

Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Production, Set One is GRANTED.  Defendant is ordered to serve verified further responses that comply with the mandatory language for responses in section 2031.310, 2031.320, and 2031.330 of the Code of Civil Procedure and produce all responsive documents within 30 days of the date of this order.

Case Number: 25STCV19432    Hearing Date: June 24, 2026    Dept: 413

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 June 24, 2026

 

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV19432

CASE NAME:                        Agripina Gil v. General Motors, LLC

 

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant, General Motors, LLC

OPPOSING PARTY:             Plaintiff, Agripina Gil

PROCEEDING:                      DEMURRER

RULING SUMMARY:          The Demurrer to the Fifth Cause of Action is overruled.  Defendant shall file its Answer within 10 days.  The Court continues the Case Management Conference to, and sets an Order to Show Cause re Filing of Answer for, ___________, 2026, at 8:30AM. 

                                                                                                                                                           

 

Background

 

This is a lemon law case. On July 19, 2024, Agripina Gil (“Plaintiff”) entered into a warranty contract with General Motors, LLC (“Defendant”) regarding a 2024 GMC Sierra 1500 (Vin No. 3GTPHCEK2RG330590) (“Subject Vehicle”).

 

On June 27, 2025, Plaintiff filed a complaint against Defendant alleging: (1) violation of Civil Code Section 1793.2(d); (2) violation of 1793.2(b); (3) violation of 1793.2(a)(3); (4) breach of the implied warranty of merchantability and (5) fraudulent inducement-concealment. 

 

On October 9, 2025, Defendant filed the demurrer.  On December 11, 2025, Plaintiff filed an opposition.  On December 18, 2025, Defendant filed a reply.

 

On February 24, 2026, Plaintiff filed a reply. The Court will disregard this reply as it is improper.

 

Request for Judicial Notice 

 

The court may take judicial notice of records of any court of record of the United States. (Evid. Code, § 452(d)(2).) However, the court may only judicially notice the existence of the record, not that its contents are the truth. (Sosinsky v. Grant (1992) 6 cal.app.4th 1548, 1565.)¿ 

¿  

The court may take judicial notice of “official acts of the legislative, executive, and judicial departments of the united states and of any state of the united states,” “[r]ecords of (1) any court of this state or (2) any court of record of the united states or of any state of the united states,” and “[f]acts and propositions that are not reasonably subject to dispute and are capable of immediate and accurate determination by resort to sources of reasonably indisputable accuracy.” (Evid. Code, § 452, subds. (c), (d), and (h).)¿“taking judicial notice of a document is not the same as accepting the truth of its contents or accepting a particular interpretation of its meaning.” (joslin v. H.a.s. ins. Brokerage¿(1986) 184 cal.app.3d 369, 374.) 

Defendant requests the Court take judicial notice of the Second Amended Complaint in Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828. (Reply, Allison Decl., ¶ 2, Ex. A.)

The Court grants Defendant’s request for judicial notice, only as to the existence of the Second Amended Complaint and not for the truth of its contents.

Demurrer[1]

 

I.         Legal Standard

 

Where pleadings are defective, a party may raise the defect by way of a demurrer. (Coyne v. Krempels (1950) 36 Cal.2d 257, 262.) A demurrer tests the sufficiency of a pleading, and the grounds for a demurrer must appear on the face of the pleading or from judicially noticeable matters.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.30(a); Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) In evaluating a demurrer, the court accepts the complainant’s properly pled facts as true and ignores contentions, deductions, and conclusory statements. (Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1976) 67 Cal.2d 695, 713; Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.) Moreover, the court does not consider whether a plaintiff will be able to prove the allegations or the possible difficulty in making such proof. (Fisher v. San Pedro Peninsula Hospital (1989) 214 Cal.App.3d 590, 604.) 

 

Leave to amend must be allowed where there is a reasonable possibility of successful amendment. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.)¿The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Ibid.)

 

II.        Discussion

 

a.     Fifth Cause of Action: Fraudulent Inducement – Concealment

 

Defendant demurs to Plaintiff’s fifth cause of action for fraudulent inducement – concealment arguing Plaintiff has not stated facts sufficient to plead the cause of action and the cause of action is barred by the economic loss rule and independent tort principle.

 

i.                 Failure to State a Cause of Action

 

To state a claim for fraudulent inducement-concealment, Plaintiffs must allege: (1) the defendant “concealed or suppressed a material fact,” (2) the defendant was “under a duty to disclose the fact to the plaintiff,” (3) the defendant “intentionally concealed or suppressed the fact with the intent to defraud the plaintiff,” (4) the plaintiff was “unaware of the fact and would not have acted as he did if he had known of the concealed or suppressed fact,” and (5) “as a result of the concealment or suppression of the fact, the plaintiff must have sustained damage.” (BiglerEngler¿v. Breg, Inc.¿(2017) 7 Cal.App.5th 276, 310-311;¿Rattagan¿v. Uber Technologies, Inc.¿(2024) 17 Cal.5th 1, 40.)¿ 

 

Absent a fiduciary relationship between the parties, a duty to disclose can arise in only three circumstances: (1) the defendant had exclusive knowledge of the material fact; (2) the defendant actively concealed the material fact; or (3) the defendant made partial representations while also suppressing the material fact. (BiglerEngler, supra, 7 Cal.App.5th at p. 311;¿LiMandri v. Judkins¿(1997) 52 Cal.App.4th 326, 336.)¿However, the duty to¿disclose¿only arises under any of these circumstances if there¿is¿“the necessary relationship giving rise to a duty to¿disclose¿as a ‘transaction’ between the plaintiff and defendant ….” (Bigler-Engler, supra, 7 Cal.App.5th at p. 311;¿Rattagan¿v. Uber Technologies, Inc., supra,¿17 Cal.5th¿at¿40¿[requiring¿a “preexisting relationship between the parties, such as ‘between seller and buyer ... or parties entering into any kind of contractual agreement.’ ”]Warner Construction Corp. v. City of Los Angeles¿(1970) 2 Cal.3d 285, 294 [“In transactions which do not involve fiduciary or confidential relations”];¿Hoffman v. 162 North Wolfe LLC¿(2014) 228 Cal.App.4th 1178, 1187–89 [rejecting concealment claim where plaintiffs “were not¿involved in a transaction with the parties they claim defrauded them”];¿LiMandri, supra,¿52 Cal.App.4th at p. 337 [“such a relationship can only come into being as a result of some sort of transaction between the parties”].)¿ 

 

A concealment cause of action has a more relaxed specificity requirement as compared to a claim based on affirmative misrepresentations; the rule of specifically pleading how, when, where, to whom and by what means, misrepresentations were communicated is intended to apply to affirmative misrepresentations and not to concealment. (Alfaro v. Community Housing Improvement System & Planning Assn, Inc. (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 1356, 1384.) The relaxed standard relies on the natural logic that a plaintiff cannot specifically plead a fraud committed by omission, because there is no “how, when”, etc., for a plaintiff identify. (Id.) 

 

Plaintiff argues that the warranty contract with Defendant creates a sufficient transactional relationship, citing Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc., (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 844.)  The¿Dhital¿court stated that the plaintiffs’ allegations were sufficient to overcome the defendant's argument that there was no buyer-seller relationship giving rise to a duty to¿disclose. (Id.) Moreover, the plaintiffs sufficiently alleged that the requisite buyer-seller relationship existed because the plaintiffs had alleged that they bought the car from a Nissan dealership, Nissan backed the purchase with an express warranty, and Nissan's authorized dealerships are its agents for purposes of the sale of Nissan vehicles to customers. (Id.)¿ 

 

First, Plaintiff does allege the existence of a material fact concealed. Plaintiff¿alleges that Defendant¿failed to¿disclose¿the transmission defect to them at¿the¿time they¿purchased¿the Subject Vehicle.¿(Compl., ¶ 46.)¿The¿court in¿Dhital¿found that plaintiffs who allege the effects of a defect on a class of a certain part sufficiently allege a defect in a particular vehicle’s part. (Dhital,¿supra, 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 844 [“plaintiffs alleged the CVT transmissions were defective in that they caused such problems as hesitation, shaking, jerking, and failure to function…We decline to hold… that plaintiffs were required to include in the SAC more detailed allegations about the alleged defects in the CVT transmissions.”].) Thus, Plaintiff’s allegation as pled is sufficient as to the material defect element.

 

Second, the Complaint alleges a contractual relationship between the parties. Plaintiff alleges she entered into a warranty agreement with Defendant regarding the Subject Vehicle. (Compl., ¶ 6.)  This warranty contract can form the basis of¿a¿transactional relationship.¿(See LiMandri v. Judkins (1997) 52 Cal.App.4th at p. 337.) Thus,¿Plaintiffs alleged¿a¿direct transactional relationship with Defendants which imposed¿a duty¿on Defendants¿to¿disclose¿any¿known¿vehicle defects.

 

Third, Plaintiff sufficiently pleads that Defendant had exclusive knowledge of the transmission defect through non-public pre-release testing data, consumer complaints, and other internal sources. (Compl., ¶ 49.)¿¿Defendant cites¿Roe v. Ford Motor Co., Case No. 2:18-cv-12528-LJMAPP, 2019 WL 3564589, at *6 (E.D. Mich. Aug. 6, 2019), to argue¿that¿Plaintiff must state what “pre-releasing testing data” revealed and/or tie said data to the alleged defects. (Demurrer p. 13.)  However, this case comes from the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan.¿Therefore, this case and its rulings do not constitute precedent in this matter. The Court will not require that level of detail¿at the pleading stage, and¿instead¿applies the principles¿established¿in¿Dhital,¿supra,¿84 Cal.App.5th at p.844.¿¿

 

Plaintiff has also sufficiently¿pled¿that Defendant actively concealed the transmission defects. (Compl., ¶¶ 51-54.)¿¿ 

 

Last, Plaintiff sufficiently¿pled¿actual and justifiable reliance. (Compl., ¶ 66.) The alleges that, “[h]ad Plaintiff known that the Vehicle, and its 10-speed transmission, were defective prior to and at the time of sale, Plaintiff would not have purchased the Vehicle.” (Compl., ¶ 55.) This line¿demonstrates¿that Plaintiff¿relied upon Defendant’s omissions and/or¿concealment, not the warranty contract, and that Plaintiff would not have¿purchased¿the vehicle if¿he¿had been aware of existing, known defects. 

 

Therefore, Plaintiff has sufficiently stated a cause of action for fraudulent inducement – concealment.  

 

ii.               Economic Loss Rule

 

The economic loss rule provides: ‘[W]here a purchaser's expectations in a sale are frustrated because the product he bought is not working properly, his remedy is said to be in contract alone, for he has suffered only ‘economic’ losses.”' (Robinson Helicopter Co. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979, 988.) The rule “requires a purchaser to recover in contract for purely economic loss due to disappointed expectations, unless he can demonstrate harm above and beyond a broken contractual promise.” (Id.)

 

However, the Court in Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc., expressly held that fraudulent inducement by concealment claims are not barred by the economic loss rule. (Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc., (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828, 843-45.)  

Here, based upon the facts alleged, the economic loss rule does not apply to Plaintiff’s claims.

Accordingly, Defendant’s demurrer to the fifth cause of action is OVERRULED.

Conclusion

 

The Demurrer to the Fifth Cause of Action is overruled.  Defendant shall file its Answer within 10 days.  The Court continues the Case Management Conference to, and sets an Order to Show Cause re Filing of Answer for, ___________, 2026, at 8:30AM.
