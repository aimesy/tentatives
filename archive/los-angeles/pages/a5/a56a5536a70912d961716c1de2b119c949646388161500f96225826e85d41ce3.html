DEPARTMENT 529 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24STCV20950    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 529

JAMES A KIM, ET AL. VS AMERICAN HONDA MOTOR CO., INC.

 

DEFENDANT JAGUAR LAND ROVER NORTH AMERICA, LLC’S MOTION TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE WITH THE COURT’S FEBRUARY 19, 2026 DISCOVERY ORDER AND REQUEST FOR SANCTIONS

 

Date of Hearing:       July 1, 2026                                                     Trial Date:       None set

Department:             529                                                                   Case No.:        24STCV20950

 

Moving Party:            Defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC

Responding Party:     No opposition.

 

BACKGROUND

 

This is a lemon law action. On August 19, 2024, Plaintiffs James A Kim and James A Kim MP APMC filed a complaint against American Honda Motor Co., Inc. for various violations of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act. On December 3, 2024, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING

 

Defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC’s Motion to Compel Compliance with the Court’s February 19, 2026 Discovery Order and Request for Sanctions is GRANTED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Defendant Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC moves for an order compelling Plaintiffs James A. Kim and James A. Kim MD APMC’s compliance with the court’s February 19, 2026 Discovery Order granting Defendant’s motion to compel discovery responses.

 

“[I]f a party fails to obey an order compelling further response, the court may make those orders that are just, including the imposition of an issue sanction, an evidence sanction, or a terminating sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010). In lieu of, or in addition to, that sanction, the court may impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010).” (CCP § 2031.310(i).) 

 

Defendant argues as a failure to respond to Defendant’s discovery requests, Defendant filed several motions to compel discovery responses. The court granted Defendant’s motions to compel discovery on February 19, 2026, (Carter Decl. ¶3.) Notably, the court ordered Plaintiff to provide further responses without objection and verifications within 10 days. (Carter Decl. ¶3, Exh. A.) On April 20, 2026, Defendant sent correspondence to Plaintiffs requesting Plaintiffs provide their further responses without objections and verifications on or by April 24, 2025. (Carter Decl. ¶4, Exh. B.) Plaintiff’s Counsel responded, asking for an extension and Defendant agreed. (Carter Decl. ¶4, Exh. B.) Despite this, Plaintiff’s counsel has not provided any further verified responses to Defendant’s requests for discovery. (Carter Decl. ¶5.)

 

As a result of Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the February 19, 2026 Court Order, the court GRANTS Defendant’s motion to compel compliance. The court notes the instant motion is unopposed. Plaintiff is ordered to produce discovery responses without objection and verification within 5 days.

 

The court notes Defendant also requests this court impose monetary sanctions of $500 per day until Plaintiff’s comply with the court’s prior court order. The court denies the request to impose monetary sanctions as the circumstances are not such that the $500 per day requested is warranted.

 


Case Number: 25STCV17547    Hearing Date: July 1, 2026    Dept: 529

MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND PLAINTIFFS’ COMPLAINT

 

Date of Hearing:        July 1, 2026                                        Trial Date:       None set. 

Department:              529                                                     Case No.:        25STCV17547

 

Moving Party:            Plaintiff Aaron Hall

Responding Party:     No opposition

 

BACKGROUND

 

On June 17, 2025, Plaintiff Aaron Hall filed a complaint against Defendant Gary Winthorpe Productions, Inc. and Daniel John Duncan for breach of contract, fraud and accounting. Plaintiff alleges in 2019, the parties entered into an exclusive contract under which Plaintiff agreed to perform and create content solely for Defendants, in exchange for 80% of all revenue generated from the use and exploitation of his work. Despite earning at least $2,000,000 from Plaintiff’s work through advertising revenue, sponsorships, and other commercial use, Defendants have paid absolutely nothing to Plaintiff.

 

[TENTATIVE] RULING:

 

Plaintiff Aaron Hall’s Motion for Leave to Amend Plaintiff’s Complaint is GRANTED.

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff Aaron Hall moves this court for an order permitting leave to amend and to file a First Amended Complaint.

 

“A court may, in furtherance of justice, and on any terms as may be proper, allow a party to amend any pleading.” (CCP § 473(a)(1).) The court’s discretion will usually be exercised liberally to permit amendments of the pleadings. (Nestle v. City of Santa Monica (1972) 6 Cal.3d 920, 939.)¿A complainant may obtain leave from the trial court to amend their pleading beyond the number of amendments allowed under Code of Civil Procedure section 472 (a) by filing a noticed motion. (CRC, Rule 3.1324.)¿ 

 

The motion must: (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) state what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located. (CRC, Rule 3.1324(a).)¿¿ 

 

The motion must be accompanied by a declaration stating: (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) why the request was not made earlier.¿(CRC, Rule 3.1324 (b).)¿ 

 

Plaintiff seeks to add three new Labor Code violations and a claim for violation of Business & Professions Code 17200 on the grounds new counsel for Plaintiff realized Mr. Hall’s complaint should include Labor Code violations. Plaintiff contends the new causes of action do not change the underlying factual basis of the complaint and merely adds additional legal theories for recovery. Plaintiff attaches a copy of the proposed First Amended Complaint and a redline of the proposed First Amended Complaint and the original complaint.

 

The court GRANTS the motion for leave to amend. Plaintiff has substantially complied with the requirements and the motion is unopposed.
