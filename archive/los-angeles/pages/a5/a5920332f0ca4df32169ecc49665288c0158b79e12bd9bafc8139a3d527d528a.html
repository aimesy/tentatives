#13 - NANCY MENDOZA vs FORD MOTOR COMPANY, et al.

Plaintiff’s motion for attorneys’ fees, costs, and expenses is DENIED as untimely.
Plaintiff’s motion for relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b) is DENIED.

Under California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1702, a motion claiming attorney’s fees before trial court judgment must be served and filed within the time for filing a notice of appeal.  Under California Rules of Court, Rule 8.104, the ordinary time to appeal is 60 days after service of a notice of entry or a filed-endorsed copy of the judgment, or 180 days after entry of judgment if no such notice is served.

The clock starts to run on the time to move for attorney fees from either the service of notice of entry of judgment or dismissal (starting a 60-day clock), or if no such notice is given, the entry of judgment or dismissal (starting a 180-day clock).  (Hatlevig v. General Motors LLC (2026) 118 Cal.App.5th 644, 649.)

Discretionary relief is available under Code of Civil Procedure section 473 for a judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against a party through mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.

In determining whether the attorneys mistake or inadvertence was excusable, the court inquires whether a reasonably prudent person under the same or similar circumstances might have made the same error.  (Zamora v. Clayborn Contracting Group, Inc. (2002) 28 Cal.4th 249, 258.)  In other words, the discretionary relief provision of section 473 only permits relief from attorney error fairly imputable to the client, i.e., mistakes anyone could have made.  (Ibid.)  Conduct falling below the professional standard of care, such as failure to timely object or to properly advance an argument, is not therefore excusable. (Ibid.)

The fee motion was untimely.

The Court need not decide whether Plaintiff’s November 12, 2025 notice triggered a 60-day deadline, because the fee motion was untimely even under the more generous 180-day outside limit.  The operative dismissal occurred on October 1, 2025, when the Court ordered the complaint dismissed without prejudice under Code of Civil Procedure section 664.6.  Plaintiff was required, at the latest, to serve and file the fee motion within 180 days of that dismissal. Plaintiff did not file the fee motion until June 30, 2026, which was more than 180 days after October 1, 2025.

Mandatory relief is unavailable.

Plaintiff filed the fee motion and the section 473(b) motion at the same time on June 30, 2026. By its terms, Code of Civil Procedure section 473(b) only offers mandatory relief for defaults and resulting default judgments.  Thus, mandatory relief is unavailable.

Plaintiff has not shown excusable neglect warranting discretionary relief.

Plaintiff’s papers show that counsel’s office intended to calendar a 180-day deadline, but instead calendared October 1, 2026 and did not discover the mistake until June 26, 2026. (Jacobson Decl., ¶ 7.)  Plaintiff also asserts that the office discovered the error during an internal audit and then filed the instant motion four days later.  (Jacobson Decl., ¶ 7.)

That showing establishes prompt action after discovery, but it does not establish excusable neglect.  The motion does not explain how a deadline counsel says was understood to be 180 days after dismissal became a date one full year after dismissal.  Nor do the papers explain what office procedure produced that error or why the error remained undetected for months after the dismissal.  The inconsistency between the notice of motion’s description of a “three-day delay” and counsel’s description of a one-year miscalendaring further weakens the showing.  (Motion, p. 1; Jacobson Decl., Jacobson Decl., ¶ 7.)

The Court must determine whether a reasonably prudent person under similar circumstances might have made the same mistake.  On this record, the Court cannot make that finding.  Plaintiff has not established entitlement to mandatory relief, and the present record does not establish excusable neglect sufficient to warrant discretionary relief.

Unless waived by the parties, Plaintiff is to give notice.

#14 - GUOJIE ZHAO, et al. vs ROBERT CHIEN

The procedural requirements for a motion to amend have been satisfied here because the motion includes a proposed amended pleading and redline, and Yeh’s declaration addresses the effect of the amendments, their asserted necessity, when the supporting facts were discovered, and why the request was not made earlier. (Yeh Decl., ¶¶ 3-9; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1324.)

The Court does not minimize the timing of the request. The operative First Amended Cross-Complaint was filed on February 26, 2025, the motion was filed on July 9, 2026, and trial is set for September 15, 2026.

Even so, the record does not show the kind of prejudice that justifies denial. Fact discovery remains active, the PMQ/PMK deposition is still ongoing, and expert discovery is not yet complete.

The proposed new claims are represented to arise from the same construction-defect, abandonment, contract, and misrepresentation facts already at issue in the action. The proposed amended complaint adds two causes of action, adds Zhao to the disgorgement claim, and supplies a more detailed catalog of alleged defects.

Here, the Court finds it appropriate to permit the amendment and allow the parties to test the amended pleading through ordinary responsive procedures rather than to deny leave outright.

The Court agrees with the opposition that no prior answer should be deemed the operative response to the Second Amended Cross-Complaint. (Opp., p. 7.) Chien does not oppose preserving Cross-Defendants’ right to respond by demurrer, motion to strike, or other pleading challenge. (Reply, p. 7.)

The Court declines to include a prospective ruling that the amendment may not be used in support of any future continuance request. (Opp., p. 2.) That issue is not properly decided on this motion.

The Court also declines to impose costs upon Cross-Complainant Chien. The opposition requested Chien to bear the reasonable costs if leave were granted, but the opposition does not set out any quantified or otherwise specific showing of such costs.

The motion is GRANTED.  The amended Cross-Complaint shall be filed within 10 days.

Moving Party is to give notice.

#12 - ASTGHIK GEVORGYAN, et al. vs LOS ANGELES UNIFIED SCHOOL DISTRICT, et al.

The Petition for Approval of Compromise of Claim or Action or Disposition of Proceeds of Judgment for Minor or Person With a Disability is GRANTED.

The Court has reviewed the petition and the information disclosed concerning the accident, the minor’s injuries, the treatment received, the complete recovery, the proposed settlement amount, the claimed medical expenses, the requested attorney’s fees, the requested costs, and the proposed disposition of the net proceeds.

The gross settlement for the minor is $5,000.00.  Requested deductions consist of $250.00 in medical expenses, $1,250.00 in attorney’s fees, and $58.18 in litigation expenses, leaving net proceeds of $3,441.82 for the minor.  The net balance of $3,441.82 is approved for distribution as requested in item 18b(5) of the petition to Arman Khachatryan, at 10137 Arleta Ave. Unit 1, Arleta, CA 91331. 

The medical-expense portion of the petition states that Medi-Cal paid $150.00, seeks no reimbursement from the proceeds, and that a medical provider lien for Back In Motion Chiropractic is to be satisfied in the amount of $250.00.

The requested attorney’s fee equals 25 percent of the minor’s gross recovery. The petition also discloses that counsel expects to receive additional compensation from Astghik Gevorgyan, not from the minor’s share, in the amount of $48,000.00.

The Court finds the proposed compromise fair, reasonable, and in the minor’s best interests. The Court further finds that the requested fee and costs to be paid from the minor’s recovery are reasonable, and that the proposed net distribution should be approved as requested.

The Petitioning Party is to give notice.
