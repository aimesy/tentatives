I.               BACKGROUND 

This is a motor vehicle action involving Plaintiffs Caterine Barrera Valero (consolidated action Case No. 25VECV05960), Sevak Khachatrian, and Davit Perikhanyan (jointly in lead action Case No. 25VECV03937) and Defendant Edder Israel Rojas Parra (“Defendant”).

Defendant moves for leave to submit tardy expert witness disclosure and to allow the depositions of experts through November 9, 2026.

II.            PROCEDURAL HISTORY

On July 15, 2025, Plaintiffs Sevak Khachatrian and Davit Perikhanyan (jointly “Plaintiffs”) filed a Complaint alleging (1) Negligence; (2) Negligence Per Se; and (3) Negligent Entrustment.

On August 18, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

On October 16, 2025, Plaintiff Caterine Barrera Valero (“Valero”) filed a Complaint alleging (1) Motor Vehicle tort and (2) Negligence.

On December 12, 2025, Defendant filed an Answer.

On August 7, 2026, the Court granted in part Defendant’s ex parte Application for an Order to Continue Trial and All Related Dates. The Court continued the trial date. Fact and expert discovery deadlines were not extended; they remained based on the prior trial date.

On August 13, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion.

On August 31, 2026, Plaintiffs filed an Opposition.

On September 9, 2026, Defendant filed a Reply.

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

A party who fails to submit expert witness information on the date specified in a demand for that exchange may seek leave to submit that information on a later date.¿(See Code Civ. Proc. § 2034.710(a).) [1] Absent “exceptional circumstances,” the motion for permission to file a tardy list must be made early enough to permit deposing the experts involved before the 15–day cut-off on expert witness depositions. (See id., §§ 2034.710(b), 2024.030.)¿ 

The court may permit tardy submission of expert witness lists and information if the following conditions are satisfied:¿ 

a)     The court has taken into account the extent to which the opposing party has relied on the absence of a list of expert witnesses.¿¿ 

b)    The court has determined that any party opposing the motion will not be prejudiced in maintaining that party's action or defense on the merits.¿¿ 

c)     The court has determined that the moving party did all of the following:¿ 

1.     Failed to submit the information as the result of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.¿ 

2.     Sought leave to submit the information promptly after learning of the mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.¿¿ 

3.     Promptly thereafter served a copy of the proposed expert witness information described in Section 2034.260 on all other parties who have appeared in the action.¿¿ 

d)    The order is conditioned on the moving party making the expert available immediately for a deposition under Article 3 (commencing with Section 2034.410), and on any other terms as may be just, including, but not limited to, leave to any party opposing the motion to designate additional expert witnesses or to elicit additional opinions from those previously designated, a continuance of the trial for a reasonable period of time, and the awarding of costs and litigation expenses to any party opposing the motion.¿¿ 

(See id., § 2034.720.)¿ 

IV.          MEET AND CONFER

Code of Civil Procedure § 2034.710(b)(2) requires the motion be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference. (See id., § 2034.710(c).)

Defendant’s counsel attests he emailed Valero’s counsel a meet and confer letter, but he did not receive a response. (See Declaration of Edward Alberola filed August 13, 2026 (“Alberola Decl.”), ¶ 9.) Defendant’s counsel attests, although he did not receive a response, during numerous communications with Plaintiff’s counsel, he opposed Defendant designating any experts in this matter, including objecting to Defendant’s August 11, 2026, designation and calling for them to be excluded. (See id.)

The Court finds Defendant’s counsel complied with his meet and confer obligation.

V.             ANALYSIS

Defendant moves for an order allowing him to submit tardy expert witness designations and allowing expert depositions through November 9, 2026. Defendant argues he has admitted liability and thus the only disputed issue is Plaintiff’s injuries, which will turn on expert testimony. (See Mot. at 4:5-11.) Defendant explains Valero designated experts and Plaintiffs have not. (See id. at 3:11-12.) Defendant argues Valero has not relied on the absence of a list of expert witnesses and will not be prejudiced because Defendant will agree to allow Plaintiff supplementing her list 20 days after Defendant’s proposed designation is allowed and will make his experts available for deposition up until 30 days before the December 9, 2026, trial date, so long as Valero makes his experts available for deposition. (See id., at 4:12-16.) Defendant’s counsel attests he failed to submit his expert list due to excusable neglect and inadvertence. Defendant’s counsel attests when Valero served her demand for exchange of expert witness designation on July 1, 2026, the handling attorneys had already left the firm or been taken off the case. (See Alberola Decl., ¶ 3.) The case was not reassigned until mid-July and Valero’s email was never forwarded to a calendar clerk or legal secretary for processing. (See id., ¶¶ 3, 5.) Defendant’s counsel attests on August 7, 2026, Valero forwarded the demand she had served on July 1st. (See id., ¶ 6.) Defendant’s counsel attests he served Defendant’s [Proposed] Designation of Expert Witnesses for Trial on all parties. (See id., ¶ 10, Ex. A.)

In opposition, Valero argues she relied on the absence of Defendant’s list of expert witnesses and would be prejudiced because Defendant would have the benefit of designating retained experts only after Plaintiff disclosed her experts and Plaintiff would have to reopen discovery, incur additional expert expense, and prepare for new retained defense opinions after the expert discovery deadlines. (See Opp. at 4:5-19.) Valero argues Defendant has not shown excusable neglect, but rather internal firm breakdown. (See id., at 4:24-25.) Valero argues Defendant did not timely seek leave as first he sought ex parte relief then served an expert list on August 11, 2026, attempting to designate retained experts for the first time. (See id., at 5:1-11.)

In reply, Defendant argues since the filing of this Motion, Valero has stipulated to make her experts available for deposition after this hearing and after the expert witness discovery cutoff so as to avoid any prejudice that might arise if her experts were deposed before the hearing on this motion and before Defendant’s experts could be deemed designated and deposed. (See Reply at 2:5-9; Alberola Decl., ¶ 2, Ex. A.) Defendant reasserts he has satisfied the statutory requirements and argues a genuine office calendaring error qualifies as excusable neglect; it was not a tactical decision. (See Reply at 2:18-23, citing Barboni v. Tuomi (2012) 210 Cal.App.4th 340; Reply Alberola Decl., ¶¶ 3.)

The Court will grant the Motion. As an initial matter, the discovery deadlines remain based on the initial September 9, 2026, trial date. (See August 7, 2026, Minute Order.) The Court notes the 15–day cut-off on expert witness depositions was August 25, 2026, and this Motion was filed on August 13, 2026. The Court finds exceptional circumstances exist here for bringing this Motion without sufficient time to permit expert depositions before the 15–day cut-off as¿Defendant’s counsel shows he did not become aware of a demand for expert designation until August 7, 2026, due to internal mishandling of this case.¿(See Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2034.710(b), 2024.030.)

The Court has considered the extent to which the opposing party has relied on the absence of a list of expert witnesses. To that end, Valero hasn’t shown she has taken actions in reliance on a lack of Defendant’s expert designation. Her argument instead focuses on suffering prejudice. However, the Court finds Valero will not be prejudiced in maintaining her action as the parties agreed to conduct expert depositions after the hearing of this Motion and Defendant will agree to Plaintiff amending her expert list after Defendant designates his experts. Both parties will incur costs in conducting expert discovery, as this is a basic feature of litigation and not actual prejudice. Thus, the Court finds Valero will not be prejudiced in maintaining her action against Defendant on the merits.  

The Court finds Defendant has shown he failed to submit his expert list due to excusable neglect and inadvertence as Valero’s expert demand was not received by an attorney active on the case or forwarded to the newly assigned attorney. Defendant promptly sought leave by filing this Motion six days after receiving Valero’s forwarded email containing her demand for exchange of expert witness information. Finally, Defendant’s counsel attested he served the proposed expert witness information on all parties. Nevertheless, the Court must deny the Motion because the supporting declaration fails to attach Exhibit A showing proof of service of the proposed expert witness information described in Section 2034.260 on all other parties who have appeared in the action as required by section 2034.720(c)(3). (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2034.720(c)(3).) The Court reached out to counsel prior to the issuance of this tentative ruling to address this clerical error but received no response to its request. Thus, the Court will inquire at the hearing for provision of this document.

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Defendant’s Motion for Leave to Submit Tardy Expert Witness Designation.

VI.          CONCLUSION

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Defendant’s Motion for Leave to Submit Tardy Expert Witness Designation.

          IT IS SO ORDERED.

ORDER GRANTING IN PART DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO COMPEL FURTHER RESPONSES TO FORM INTERROGATORIES (SET ONE)

 

I.               BACKGROUND 

Plaintiff Ester Machado (“Plaintiff”) filed this motor vehicle tort action against Defendant Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority (“Defendant”).

Defendant moves to compel further responses from Plaintiff to its Form Interrogatories (Set One).

II.            PROCEDURAL HISTORY

On November 17, 2025, Plaintiff filed a Complaint alleging (1) Motor Vehicle tort and (2) Negligence.

On April 23, 2026, Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleging the same causes of action.

On April 29, 2026, Defendant filed an Answer.

On August 12, 2026, Defendant filed the instant Motion.

On September 2, 2026, Plaintiff filed an Opposition.

On September 8, 2026, Defendant filed a Reply.

III.         LEGAL STANDARD

A.             Interrogatories  

A propounding party may move for an order compelling further responses to interrogatories if the responding party produced an evasive or incomplete answer or a meritless or overly general objection in response to an interrogatory. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.300(a).) A motion to compel further responses to interrogatories lies where the party to whom the interrogatories were directed gave responses deemed improper by the propounding party; e.g., objections, or evasive or incomplete answers. (See id. at § 2030.300.)¿The moving party must also include reasons why further answers should be ordered: legal or factual arguments why the answers given were incomplete or nonresponsive, or the objections invalid.¿(See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1345(c).)  

However, where a timely motion to has been filed, “the burden is on the responding party to justify any objection or failure to fully answer the interrogatories.”¿(Weil & Brown, Cal. Prac. Guide: Civil Pro. Before Trial (The Rutter Group 2017) ¶ 8:1179.)¿“The ruling usually is based on consideration of the following factors: [¶] the relationship of the information sought to the issues framed in the pleadings; [¶] the likelihood that disclosure will be of practical benefit to the party seeking discovery; [¶] the burden or expense likely to be encountered by the responding party in furnishing the information sought.”¿(Columbia Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Superior Court (Rolfe) (1968) 263 Cal.App.2d 12, 19.) 

///

IV.          TIMELINESS

A notice of motion to compel must be served within 45 days after verified responses, or any verified supplemental responses, were served, unless the parties agree in writing to extend the time. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.300(c).)

Defendant argues this Motion is timely as the statutory deadline was July 30, 2026, and on that date Plaintiff’s counsel granted an extension to August 13, 2026. (See Mot. at 5:20-6:2; Declaration of Russell Rubin filed August 12, 2026 (“Rubin Decl.”), ¶ 9, Ex. E.)

Plaintiff argues the Motion is untimely because it was filed after July 29, 2026, the statutory deadline for bringing this Motion based on responses served on June 12, 2026. (See Opp. at 5:12-14; Declaration of Leah Michele Kaufman filed September 2, 2026 (“Kaufman Decl.”), ¶ 3.) Plaintiff’s counsel attests she received a call from Defendant’s counsel on July 30, 2026, during which Plaintiff’s counsel informed him that the deadline had lapsed. (See Kaufman Decl., ¶ 10.) Plaintiff’s counsel attests she afterwards received on email from Defendant’s secretary on July 30, 2026, “confirming” the parties had reached an agreement regarding the extension deadline. (See id., Decl., ¶ 12.) Plaintiff’s counsel attests on August 7, 2026, she once again confirmed in letter the deadline lapsed before Defendant’s counsel contacted her and she also served amended responses to Nos. 6.4 and 10.1 to correct a typographical error. (See id., ¶ 13.)

Defendant argues the deadline was July 30, 2026. (See Reply the 1:13.) Defendant also argues the Motion is timely because the parties agreed on July 30, 2026, and Defendant would have until August 13, 2026, to file the instant Motion. (See id., at 1:6-7, 2:22-26.) Defendant also argues Plaintiff served amended responses to Nos. 6.4 and 10.1 on August 7, 2026, and thus the Motion is timely. (See id., at 1:17-19.)

The Court finds the statutory deadline to bring this Motion, except as to Nos. 6.4 and 10.1, was July 29, 2026. The Court does not find the parties agreed in writing to an August 13, 2026, deadline. Defendant’s office emailed Plaintiff’s counsel on July 30, 2026, purporting to confirm an extension deadline. (See Rubin Decl., ¶ 9, Ex. E.) However, Plaintiff stated in her August 7, 2026, letter that based on her understanding the deadline was July 29, 2026, she could no longer agree to an extension. However, if the deadline was on July 30, 2026, as Defendant’s counsel argued, she would agree to extend the deadline. (See id., Decl., ¶ 10, Ex. F.) Based on these communications, the Court doesn’t find the parties agreed to extend the deadline to bring this Motion, as the deadline was July 20, 2026. Thus, the instant Motion, which was filed on August 12, 2026, is untimely, except for Nos. 6.4 and 10.1.

As to these two requests, the 45-day clock was restarted from the date of service of amended responses on August 7, 2026. Thus, the Motion is timely as to these two requests, which will be addressed below.

V.             MEET AND CONFER

Before bringing a motion to compel further responses to any discovery request, the moving party must make efforts to meet and confer in good faith and submit a declaration showing a reasonable and good faith attempt, either in person, by telephone, or by videoconference, to informally resolve each issue presented by the motion. (See Code Civ. Proc. §§ 2016.040, 2031.310(b)(2), 2030.300(b), 2033.290(b).) Failure to make such attempt constitutes “misuse of discovery process”. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2023.010(i) [amended eff. 1/1/26].)  

“A reasonable and good-faith attempt at informal resolution…requires that counsel attempt to talk the matter over, compare their views, consult, and deliberate.” (Townsend v. Superior Court (1998) 61 Cal.App.4th 1431, 1439.) In evaluating whether a meet and confer was reasonable and in good faith, “[a]n evaluation of whether, from the perspective of a reasonable person in the position of the discovering party, additional effort appeared likely to bear fruit, should also be considered.” (Clement v. Alegre (2009) 177 Cal.App.4th 1277, 1293-94.)  

Defendant’s counsel attests he met and conferred by telephone on July 30, 2026, and exchanged letters with Plaintiff’s counsel on August 7, 10, and 11, 2026. (See Rubin Decl., ¶ 9-11.)

The Court finds Defendant did not satisfy its meet and confer obligation with respect to the amended responses served on August 7, 2026, because counsel did not confer in person, by telephone, or video conference as to these amended responses. Nevertheless, the Court will address the merits of these two amended responses and reminds counsel to meaningfully engage in meet and confer efforts in person, by telephone, or by video conference.

VI.          ANALYSIS

A.             No. 6.4

This request seeks information for any consultation, examination, or treatment provided by a health care provider for any injury attributed to the incident including (a) contact information, (b) nature of visit, (c) dates, and (d) charges. (See Sep. Stmt. No. 6.4.)

Plaintiff responds “yes” and for each subdivision cites Code of Civil Procedure § 2030.230. (See id.) Additionally, Plaintiff responds as follows: (a) “various health care providers at Kaiser Permanente are set forth in medical report/summary documents produced in response to Defendant’s Demands for Production and Identification of documents, Demand No. 2. Discovery is continuing”; (b) “various health care providers at Kaiser Permanente examined Plaintiff by prescribing X-ray imaging and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) for Plaintiff, in addition to physical examination of Plaintiff, as set forth in medical report/summary documents produced in response to Defendant’s Demands for Production and Identification of documents, Demand No. 2…”; (c) “10/23/2024, 3/2/2025; 8/8/2025; 8/31/2025 and as otherwise set forth in medical report/summary documents produced in response to Defendant’s Demands for Production and Identification of documents, Demand No. 2…”; and (d) “Responding Party does not have personal knowledge sufficient to respond fully to this interrogatory, but has made a reasonable and good faith effort to obtain the information by inquiry to Kaiser Permanente as set forth in medical billing documents produced in response to Defendant’s Demands for Production and Identification of documents, Demand No. 1.” (Id.)

Defendant argues the response is incomplete and Plaintiff fails to comply with § 2030.230 as the response fails to the specific documents, page numbers, and sections containing the responsive information. (See id.) Defendant also argues Plaintiff’s further response to (d) is warranted as Plaintiff has had more than one year to obtain the corresponding billing records or explain why this information is unavailable.

The Court finds further response to No. 6.4 is warranted. Code of Civil Procedure § 2030.230 permits a responding party to answer an interrogatory by referencing the statute and “specify the writings from which the answer may be derived or ascertained” “if the answer to an interrogatory would necessitate the preparation or the making of a compilation, abstract, audit, or summary of or from the documents of the party to whom the interrogatory is directed, and if the burden or expense of preparing or making it would be substantially the same for the party propounding the interrogatory as for the responding party.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.230.) Although Plaintiff may properly invoke § 2030.230 to the extent that the making of a compilation, abstract, audit, or summary of the document production would cost substantially the same for Plaintiff as it would Defendant, it is clear from the plain language of the statute that its application requires the responding party to “specify the writings from which the answer may be derived or ascertained.” Here, Plaintiff’s response generally refers to all documents produced in response to RFP Nos. 1-2. The Court finds Plaintiff insufficiently specifies the documents containing the information sought.

The Court finds the substantive portion of Plaintiff’s response to (d) is otherwise code complaint. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 2030.220(c).)

Thus, the Court GRANTS Defendant’s Motion FROGs No. 6.4.

B.             No. 10.1

This request seeks information concerning prior complaints or injuries involving the same injured body part in this incident including (a) description for the complaint/injury, (b) dates, and (c) contact information of the treating health care provider. (See Sep. Stmt. No. 10.1.)

Plaintiff responds “Yes. (a) Lower back and left knee pain (b) 2020 to over a year before the accident; was managing pain, at which point it became chronic and significantly worse. (c) [citing CCP § 2030.230], Responding Party responds as follows: treating providers set forth in medical report/summary documents produced in response to Defendant's Demands for Production and Identification of documents, Demand No. 2. Discovery is continuing.” (Id.)

Defendant argues the response to (c) is deficient for the same reasons as the above request.

For the same reasons above, the Court finds further response to No. 10.1 is warranted as Plaintiff fails to specify the specific documents and portions of the referenced documents containing the information sought. Thus, the Court GRANTS Defendant’s Motion FROGs No. 10.1.

C.      Sanctions

Given the untimeliness of the majority of the Motion, Defendant’s failure to properly meet and confer with respect to the amended responses, and the Court’s granting of the Motion as to the amended responses, the Court declines to award discovery sanctions to either party.
