HEARING: Demurrer with MTS

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

 

RULING

 

            The Court overrules the Demurrer and denies the Motion to Strike.

           

BACKGROUND

           

            On December 17, 2025, Plaintiff Doris Benett filed the Petition to Correct Arbitration Award.

 

            On May 29, 2026, Defendant filed the Demurrer and Motion to Strike.

 

            On June 3, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Opposition.

 

            On July 6, 2026, Defendant filed the Reply.

           

LEGAL STANDARD

 

            A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) “To survive a demurrer, the complaint need only allege facts sufficient to state a cause of action; each evidentiary fact that might eventually form part of the plaintiff’s proof need not be alleged.” (C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School Dist. (2012) 

53 Cal.4th 861, 872.) For the purpose of testing the sufficiency of the cause of action, the demurrer admits the truth of all material facts properly pleaded. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-967.) A demurrer “does not admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1967) 67 Cal.2d 695, 713.) 

 

A pleading is uncertain if it is ambiguous or unintelligible. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (f).) A demurrer for uncertainty may lie if the failure to label the parties and claims renders the complaint so confusing defendant cannot tell what he or she is supposed to respond to. (Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, fn. 2.) However, “[a] demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly’s of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.) 

 

DISCUSSION

 

Meet and Confer 

 

Before filing a demurrer, “the demurring party shall meet and confer in person or by telephone with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) 

 

            Here, Defendant has failed to provide a meet and confer declaration. However, as Plaintiff has filed a substantive Opposition, the Court finds that Plaintiff has not been prejudiced.

 

            Defendant demurs to the Petition.

 

Defendant argues Plaintiff's petition fails as a matter of law because it improperly relies on a fee arbitration award that is expressly rendered inadmissible and without preclusive effect under the Mandatory Fee Arbitration Act.

 

Defendant contends that Business and Professions Code section 6204, subdivision (e), expressly provides that, where a party elects to proceed to trial following arbitration, the arbitration award shall not be admissible or operate as collateral estoppel or res judicata in any subsequent action or proceeding. Defendant argues that courts interpreting the Mandatory Fee Arbitration Act consistently enforce this limitation, contending that subsequent proceedings are de novo and entirely independent of the rejected arbitration determination. Defendant further argues that because the arbitration award is legally inadmissible and without preclusive value, Plaintiff cannot rely upon it to establish any element of a cause of action or theory of liability, and that allegations founded upon legally ineffective matter are insufficient to sustain a valid pleading as a matter of law.

 

            “Except as provided in this section, the award and determinations of the arbitrators shall not be admissible nor operate as collateral estoppel or res judicata in any action or proceeding.” (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 6204, subd. (e).) Thus, the arbitration award would be inadmissible as the trial is de novo. (Giorgianni v. Crowley (2011) 197 Cal.App.4th 1462, 1473.) However, whether evidence is admissible is not a proper issue on demurrer, as a Demurrer looks only to the face of the pleadings, not to whether the evidence supporting the pleading is sufficient or admissible. (Tyree v. Epstein (1950) 99 Cal.App.2d 361,364 [“There is statutory and traditional procedure for testing the sufficiency of a complaint on demurrer. It does not encompass the consideration by the court of evidentiary matters”].) Thus, Defendant has not met his burden of showing that the Petition fails to state a claim. Furthermore, Defendant’s Motion to Strike relies on the same grounds.

 

            Therefore, the Court overrules the Demurrer and denies the Motion to Strike.

HEARING: Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admission

           

The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

 

RULING

 

            The Court grants the Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions.

 

BACKGROUND

 

            On October 1, 2026, Plaintiff David Schach filed the Complaint against Defendants Thomas Bennett and Paul Bohn, alleging causes of action for conversion and injunctive relief.

 

            On March 2, 2026, Defendants served the subject discovery on Plaintiff.

 

            On April 6, 2026, Plaintiff served responses.

 

            On June 9, 2026, Defendants filed the Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions.

 

            On June 30, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Opposition.

 

            On July 8, 2026, Defendants filed the Reply.

 

LEGAL STANDARD

 

The propounding party may file motions to compel further responses to requests for production if it believes the responses received are (1) evasive, (2) incomplete, or (3) if the objections raised are meritless or overly general. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (a

The burden is on the moving party to “set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the discovery sought by the demand.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.310, subd. (b)(1).)  This burden “is met simply by a fact-specific showing of relevance.” (TBG Ins. Servs. Corp. v. Superior Court (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 443, 448.) In response to a motion to compel further responses, the respondent bears the burden of justifying any objections. (Fairmont Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2000) 22 Cal.4th 245, 255.)

 

DISCUSSION

 

            Defendants request that the Court order Plaintiff to provide further responses to Defendant Bennett’s First Set of Requests for Admission, Nos. 4, 6, 7, 8, and 9, and to Defendant Bohn's First Set of Requests for Admission, Nos. 4, 5, and 6.

 

Meet and Confer

 

A motion to compel must be “accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040.” (Code Civ. Proc., 2031.310 subd. (a).) Section 2016.040 states that “a meet and confer declaration in support of a motion shall state facts showing a reasonable and good faith attempt at an informal resolution of each issue presented by the motion.” (Code Civ. Proc., 2016.040.)

 

On June 3, 2026, Defendants sent a meet-and-confer letter regarding the subject discovery, to which Plaintiff failed to respond. Therefore, the Court finds that Defendants have satisfied the meet and confer requirements.

 

Timeliness

 

“Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or any specific later date to which the requesting party and the responding party have agreed in writing, the requesting party waives any right to compel further response to the requests for admission.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2033.290 subd. (c).)

 

Plaintiff argues that the instant is untimely as no extension was granted. However, Defendants have provided the declaration of their counsel, who states that an extension was granted until June 9, 2026, through text message; the message has also been attached. (Nelson Decl. Exhibit A.) Thus, the Court finds that the instant motion is timely.

 

Merits

 

            Here, the Court has reviewed the subject discovery requests and finds them relevant. Furthermore, the Court finds that Plaintiff’s responses are not code compliant and that Plaintiff has failed to justify his objections.

 

            Therefore, the Court grants the Motion to Compel Further Responses to Requests for Admissions.

 

Sanctions

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.030, subdivision (a) provides, in pertinent part, that the court may impose a monetary sanction on a party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone as a result of that conduct. A misuse of the discovery process includes failing to respond or to submit to an authorized method of discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2023.010, subd. (d).)

 

Plaintiff requests that the Court impose monetary sanctions on Defendant in the amount of $652.70. However, as Defendant’s motion was successful, the Court finds no justification for imposing sanctions on Defendant.

HEARING: Demurrer with MTS

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

 

RULING

 

            The Court sustains the Demurrer with leave to amend.

 

            The Court denies the Motion to Strike as moot.

           

BACKGROUND

 

            On February 2, 2024, Plaintiff Alicja Dzikowski filed the Complaint against Defendants, City of Torrance, West Coast Terminals LLC, Orange Corrosion Services Inc., California Traffic Control Services, and Anthony Genova, alleging causes of action for dangerous condition of public property, negligence, negligence per se, and premises liability.

 

            On March 18, 2026, Cross-Complainant Orange Corrosion Services filed the Cross-Complainant (CC) against Cross-Defendant City of Torrance, alleging causes of action for equitable indemnity, contribution, and declaratory relief.

 

            On May 18, 2026, Cross-Defendant filed the Demurrer and Motion to Strike.

 

            On June 23, 2026, Cross-Complainant filed the Opposition.

 

            On July 8, 2026, Cross-Defendant filed the Reply.

           

LEGAL STANDARD

 

            A demurrer can be used only to challenge defects that appear on the face of the pleading under attack or from matters outside the pleading that are judicially noticeable. (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) “To survive a demurrer, the complaint need only allege facts sufficient to state a cause of action; each evidentiary fact that might eventually form part of the plaintiff’s proof need not be alleged.” (C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School Dist. (2012) 

53 Cal.4th 861, 872.) For the purpose of testing the sufficiency of the cause of action, the demurrer admits the truth of all material facts properly pleaded. (Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-967.) A demurrer “does not admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.” (Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1967) 67 Cal.2d 695, 713.) 

 

A pleading is uncertain if it is ambiguous or unintelligible. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.10, subd. (f).) A demurrer for uncertainty may lie if the failure to label the parties and claims renders the complaint so confusing defendant cannot tell what he or she is supposed to respond to. (Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp. (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, fn. 2.) However, “[a] demurrer for uncertainty is strictly construed, even where a complaint is in some respects uncertain, because ambiguities can be clarified under modern discovery procedures.” (Khoury v. Maly’s of California, Inc. (1993) 14 Cal.App.4th 612, 616.) 

 

DISCUSSION

 

Meet and Confer 

 

Before filing a demurrer, “the demurring party shall meet and confer in person or by telephone with the party who filed the pleading that is subject to demurrer for the purpose of determining whether an agreement can be reached that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) 

 

            On May 7, 2026, the parties met and conferred by telephone regarding the Demurrer. Thus, the Court finds that Cross-Defendant has satisfied the meet and confer requirements.

 

            Government Claims Act

 

Cross-Defendant argues the CC fails as a matter of law because public-entity liability is governed exclusively by the Government Claims Act, which abolishes all common-law tort liability against public entities and permits liability only where expressly mandated by statute.

 

Cross-Defendant states that where a public entity is sued for an accident occurring on its property due to the condition of that property, the proper statutory cause of action is for dangerous condition of public property, not negligence.

 

Cross-Defendant contends that the CC is therefore deficient on its face because it premises Cross-Defendant's liability for equitable indemnity and contribution on a negligence theory rather than on a statutory dangerous condition of public property claim. Cross-Defendant notes that Plaintiff herself sued Cross-Defendant on only a single cause of action for dangerous condition of public property, yet Cross-Complainant seeks indemnity based on Cross-Defendant's alleged negligence, which is not a cognizable basis for liability against a public entity.

 

In opposition, Cross-Complainant argues that the CC states cognizable claims for equitable indemnity, contribution, and declaratory relief because those claims are derivative of Plaintiff's own statutory dangerous condition of public property theory and do not seek to impose common-law negligence liability on Cross-Defendant.

 

Cross-Complainant contends that a defendant may seek partial equitable indemnity from concurrent tortfeasors based on comparative fault, and that the Supreme Court expressly extended those principles to governmental defendants.

 

Cross-Complainant also argues that Cross-Defendant's characterization of the CC as premised on negligence is incorrect, noting that Plaintiff herself sued Cross-Defendant on a statutory dangerous condition of public property theory, and that the CC is derivative of that same statutory theory rather than an attempt to impose independent common-law liability. Cross-Complainant further contends that Government Code section 815 does not bar derivative indemnity and contribution claims arising from statutory dangerous-condition liability, as such claims are a remedial allocation mechanism among defendants rather than a source of new primary liability beyond what the statute permits.

 

“Under the Government Claims Act, there is no common law tort liability for public entities in California; instead, such liability must be based on statute.” (R.L. v. Merced City School Dist. (2025) 114 Cal.App.5th 89, 104.) However, a cross-claim for equitable indemnity and contribution can be brought against a public entity when it is based on a claim for dangerous condition of public property. (See County of Ventura v. City of Camarillo (1978) 80 Cal.App.3d 1019, 1024-1025.)

 

Here, the Complaint alleges a cause of action against Cross-Complaint for negligence and a cause of action against Cross-Defendant for dangerous condition of public property. What the Court must determine is whether the Cross-Complaint seeks indemnity based on a theory of dangerous condition of public property or negligence. The CC reads “Plaintiff’s underlying Complaint alleges personal injuries arising from an incident involving a changeable message sign (“CMS”) and/or dangerous conditions of public property located at or near Crenshaw Boulevard, north of 234th Street, in Torrance, California.” (CC ¶ 10.) “Cross-Complainant is informed and believes, and thereon alleges, that cross-defendants and each of them, so negligently, carelessly, and recklessly conducted themselves so as to cause the incident complained of in plaintiff’s complaint. Said negligent conduct on the part of these cross-defendants, and each of them, was the sole proximate cause of, and/or contributed to, the loss, damage, or injury alleged in plaintiff’s complaint.” (CC ¶ 12.) “If Cross-Complainant is held liable and responsible to plaintiff herein for damages as alleged in plaintiff’s complaint, it will be solely due to the conduct of cross-defendants herein, and each of them.” (CC ¶ 13.) Thus, while the CC notes that a dangerous condition of public property claim was brought against Cross-Defendant, the CC's actual allegations against Cross-Defendant are that it acted negligently. Consequently, the Court finds that the indemnity and contribution claims are based on a theory of negligence and thus are improper. Furthermore, because the Court has struck the first two claims, there is no live controversy on which to base the declaratory relief claim.

 

Therefore, the Court sustains the Demurrer with leave to amend.

 

Motion to Strike

 

Cross-Defendant requests that the Court strike the requests for attorneys' fees from the CC. However, as all causes of action have been successfully demurred to, the Motion to Strike is moot.

 

            Therefore, the Court denies the Motion to Strike as moot.
