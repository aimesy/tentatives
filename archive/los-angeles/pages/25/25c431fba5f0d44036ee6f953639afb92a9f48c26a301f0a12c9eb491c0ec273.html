Case Number: 25NWCV00344    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: P

KUTKOWSKI v. MORENO, ET AL.

CASE NO.: 25NWCV00344 

HEARING: 07/28/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#9

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Defendants’ demurrer to the first amended complaint is SUSTAINED with 30 days LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

II.            Defendants’ motion to strike is MOOT.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Defendants Jason Moreno, Michelle G. Leblanc-Gutierrez aka Michelle Leblanc Trustee of the of the Comstock Ave. Trust, and Michelle Leblanc Trustee of the 20/20 Trust demur to the first amended complaint (FAC) and move to strike portions of the FAC seeking punitive damages.

 

Background

 

On January 28, 2025, Plaintiff Gabriel Kutkowski (Plaintiff) filed this action against Defendants Jason Moreno, Michelle G. Leblanc-Gutierrez aka Michelle Leblanc, Michelle Leblanc Trustee of the 20/20 Trust, Michelle Leblanc Trustee of the 20/20 Trust aka 20/20 Trust Dated January 23, 2022 (Trust), and Does 1 to 10, inclusive (collectively, Defendants).

 

On February 27, 2026, following the Court’s ruling sustaining Defendants’ demurrer to the complaint in part and sustaining with leave to amend in part, Plaintiff filed the operative FAC. The FAC alleges the following: On June 28, 2022, the Los Angeles County Superior Court entered a judgment for Plaintiff and against Moreno for $250,166.25 and post judgment interest has accrued on this judgment. (FAC, ¶ 8.) In November of 2022, Plaintiff recorded an abstract of judgment in Los Angeles County. (FAC, ¶ 9.) The FAC alleges Moreno has evaded service of a debtor exam and “[w]hile judgment was pending and unsatisfied, [Moreno] and [Leblanc] undertook efforts to hinder and delay the collection of [Plaintiff’s] judgment…through the conveyance of several real properties by [Moreno] to his spouse, [Leblanc].” (FAC, ¶¶ 10-11.)

 

The alleged fraudulent transfers involve the following properties, conveyed from Moreno to Leblanc on October 2, 2024 and currently owned by the Trust: (1) 6717 Comstock Ave, Whittier, CA 90601; APN: 8139-013-002 (6717 Comstock Ave.); (2) 13117 Philadelphia St, Whittier, CA 90601; APN: 8139-016-009 (13117 Philadelphia St.); (3) 10000 Paramount Blvd., Downey, CA 90240; APN: 6361-021-049 (10000 Paramount Blvd.); and (4) 6745 Washington Ave., Whittier, CA 90601; APN: 8139-016-006 (6745 Washington Ave.) (collectively, Subject Properties). (FAC, ¶ 19.)

 

The FAC asserts a single cause of action for fraudulent transfer.

 

Legal Standard

 

          Demurrer Standard

 

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action. (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.) In a demurrer proceeding, the defects must be apparent on the face of the pleading or via proper judicial notice. (Donabedian v. Mercury Ins. Co. (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 968, 994.) In assessing a demurrer, the court “treat[s] the demurrer as admitting all material facts properly pleaded, but not contentions, deductions or conclusions of law.” (Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) “The only issue involved in a demurrer hearing is whether the complaint, as it stands, unconnected with extraneous matters, states a cause of action.” (Hahn, supra, 147 Cal.App.4th at p. 747.) A complaint will be upheld against a demurrer if it pleads facts sufficient to place the defendant on notice of the issues sufficient to enable the defendant to prepare a defense. (Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 549-50.) 

 

“Generally it is an abuse of discretion to sustain a demurrer without leave to amend if there is any reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured by amendment.” (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) A “[p]laintiff must show in what manner he can amend his complaint and how that amendment will change the legal effect of his pleading.” (Ibid.) 

 

Motion to Strike Standard 

 

Motions to strike are used to reach defects or objections to pleadings that are not challengeable by demurrer (i.e., words, phrases, prayer for damages, etc.). (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 435-437.) A motion to strike lies only where the pleading has irrelevant, false, or improper matter, or has not been drawn or filed in conformity with laws. (Code Civ. Proc., § 436.) The grounds for moving to strike must appear on the face of the pleadings or by way of judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc., § 437.) 

 

Punitive damages may be recovered upon a proper showing of malice, fraud, or oppression. (Civ. Code, § 3294, subd. (a).) Conclusory allegations, devoid of any factual assertions, are insufficient to support a conclusion that parties acted with oppression, fraud or malice. (Smith v. Superior Court (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1042.) 

 

Meet and Confer

 

Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 requires that the demurring party meet and confer in person, by telephone, or by video conference at least five days before the responsive pleading is due. (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a).) The demurring party shall file and serve a declaration stating either: (a) “The means by which the demurring party met and conferred…and that the parties did not reach an agreement resolving the objections raised in the demurrer” or (b) “[t]hat the party who filed the pleading subject to demurrer failed to respond to the meet and confer request of the demurring party or otherwise failed to meet and confer in good faith.” (Code Civ. Proc., § 430.41, subd. (a)(3).)

 

The parties have adequately met and conferred. (Armenta Decl., ¶ 3.)

 

Request for Judicial Notice

 

Defendants request the Court take judicial notice of 41 documents including grant deeds, quitclaim deeds, deeds of trust, and interspousal transfer deeds related to the Subject Properties.

 

Defendants’ request for judicial notice is GRANTED pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subd. (h). (See Yvanova v. New Century Mortgage Corp. (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919, n. 1; Glaski v. Bank of America (2013) 218 Cal.App.4th 1079, 1090 [“Courts can take judicial notice of the existence, content and authenticity of public records and other specified documents, but do not take judicial notice of the truth of the factual matters asserted in those documents.”].)

 

Discussion

 

Defendants demur to the FAC on the grounds that it fails to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action.

 

“The UVTA, formerly known as the Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act [citation], ‘permits defrauded creditors to reach property in the hands of a transferee.’” (Lo v. Lee (2018) 24 Cal.App.5th 1065, 1071.)  “ ‘A fraudulent conveyance is a transfer by the debtor of property to a third person undertaken with the intent to prevent a creditor from reaching that interest to satisfy its claim.’” [Citation.] The transferee ‘holds only an apparent title [to the transferred property], a mere cloak under which is hidden the hideous skeleton of deceit, the real owner being the scheming and shifty judgment debtor ....’ [Citation.] The purpose of the voidable transactions statute is “ ‘to prevent debtors from placing property which legitimately should be available for the satisfaction of demands of creditors beyond their reach ....’ ” (Ibid.)

 

“Under the UVTA, ‘a transfer of assets made by a debtor is fraudulent as to a creditor, whether the creditor's claim arose before or after the transfer, if the debtor made the transfer (1) with an actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud any creditor, or (2) without receiving reasonably equivalent value in return, and either (a) was engaged in or about to engage in a business or transaction for which the debtor's assets were unreasonably small, or (b) intended to, or reasonably believed, or reasonably should have believed, that he or she would incur debts beyond his or her ability to pay as they became due.’” (Ibid., citing Civ. Code § 3439.04.)

 

 “A transfer in fraud of creditors may be attacked only by one who is injured thereby. Mere intent to delay or defraud is not sufficient; injury to the creditor must be shown affirmatively…It cannot be said that a creditor has been injured unless the transfer puts beyond [her] reach property [she] otherwise would be able to subject to the payment of [her] debt.” (Mehrtash v. Mehrtash (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 75, 80.) Fraud must be pleaded with specificity. (Small v. Fritz Companies, Inc. (2003) 30 Cal.4th 167, 184.)

 

The FAC alleges that Moreno and Leblanc undertook efforts to hinder and delay the collection of Plaintiff’s judgment through Moreno conveying the Subject Properties to Leblanc. (FAC, ¶ 11.) The FAC alleges “MORENO did not receive reasonably equivalent value in return from LEBLANC for the transfers. MORENO made these transfers knowing he had KUTKOWSKI’S judgment against him and knowingly made the transfers to frustrate KUTKOWSKI’S collection efforts.” (FAC, ¶ 16.) The FAC alleges that Moreno conveyed 6717 Comstock Ave., 13117 Philadelphia St., and 10000 Paramount Blvd. to Leblanc on October 2, 2024 and 6745 Washington Ave. to Leblanc on September 4, 2024, and Leblanc subsequently transferred the Subject Properties to the current owners. (FAC, ¶ 19.)

 

Defendants demur to the fraudulent transfer cause of action on the grounds that the FAC fails to cure the issues previously demurred to in the complaint. Specifically, Defendants argue that Moreno was never on the titles to the Subject Properties and the FAC fails to plead facts to sustain a claim for punitive or treble damages. Defendants generally cite to the judicially noticed exhibits showing purchases solely by Leblanc of the Subject Properties in support of this argument. Defendants additionally attempt to explain the reasoning behind certain interspousal transfers. The Court cannot regard these arguments as it involves extrinsic evidence explaining the reasoning behind the judicially noticed exhibits.

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that the FAC alleges each element for a fraudulent transfer claim. Plaintiff also argues that an inference that Moreno had an interest in the Subject Properties is supported by the fact quitclaim deed and interspousal transfer grant deeds were required. Plaintiff also points to an inference that funds used for the purchased may have been community property which renders the interests subject to transmutation.

 

In reply, Defendants argue that various transfers occurred prior to the judgment. However, not only do Defendants not address which transfers they refer to, Defendants also do not show that the transfer must occur after the judgment. (See Civ. Code, § 3439.04, subd. (b)(10) [consideration to determine actual intent includes “[w]hether the transfer occurred shortly before or shortly after a substantial debt was incurred.”].)

 

Here, taking the allegations as true as required on demurrer, the FAC generally alleges the elements of a cause of action for fraudulent transfer. However, this is insufficient since pleading fraud requires specificity and allegations are conclusory. (Small, supra, 30 Cal.4th at p. 184.) For example, Plaintiff merely alleges that it was “Defendants’ intent to hinder, delay or defraud the collection by KUTKOWSKI against MORENO.” (FAC, ¶ 19.) This is conclusory as it merely restates the language of the UVTA. (See Civ. Code, § 3439.04, subd. (a)(1) [“A transfer…is voidable…if the debtor made the transfer… [w]ith actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud any creditor.”].) While the timing and circumstances of the transfers may support a finding of a conspiracy of fraud, the allegations in the FAC are insufficient in light of Defendants’ arguments regarding the timing of the transfers and whether or not they predate the debt. (See Optronic Techs., Inc. v. Celestron Acquisition, LLC (2025) 108 Cal.App.5th 770, 786.) Since Defendants’ show that some transfers predate the debt, and the FAC specifically alleges the transfers occurred “to avoid the collection of his debt,” the Court cannot determine which transfers are alleged to be fraudulent or whether Plaintiff alleges that the intent to defraud predates the debt.

 

Moreover, the Court cannot consider Defendants’ arguments regarding the reasoning behind certain transfers as it is improper extrinsic evidence. (See Hahn, supra, 147 Cal.App.4th at p. 747.) However, based on Plaintiff’s reliance on new theories arising out of Defendants’ extrinsic evidence to support the allegations in the FAC, the Court finds that leave to amend is warranted to allow Plaintiff to include these allegations.

 

Accordingly, Defendants’ demurrer to the FAC is SUSTAINED with 30 days LEAVE TO AMEND.

 

Motion to Strike

 

Defendants move to strike portions of the FAC seeking punitive damages and treble damages. Pursuant to the Court’s ruling on the demurrer, Defendants’ motion to strike is MOOT.

Case Number: 26NWCV00360    Hearing Date: July 28, 2026    Dept: P

GUEVARA v. MORPRO INC., ET AL.

CASE NO.: 26NWCV00360 

HEARING: 07/28/2026 @ 9:30 AM

 

#11

TENTATIVE ORDER

 

I.             Specially Appearing Defendants Shipping U.S.A. Corp., Alvaro Sanchez, and Consuelo M. Quinones’s motion to quash service of summons and complaint is GRANTED.

 

II.            The complaint is DISMISSED as to Shipping U.S.A. Corp., Alvaro Sanchez, and Consuelo M. Quinones.

 

Moving party to give notice.

 

Specially Appearing Defendants Shipping U.S.A. Corp. (Shipping USA), Alvaro Sanchez, and Consuelo M. Quinones (Moving Defendants) specially appear to quash the service of summons for lack of personal jurisdiction and dismiss this action.

 

Background

 

On January 29, 2026, Plaintiff Kevin Illedge Guevara (Plaintiff) filed this breach of contract action against Defendants MorPro Inc. (MorPro); Yamil Morales (Yamil); Xitlalic Morales; Zonayad Akbar; Anthony Morales; Shipping U.S.A. Corp., Alvaro Sanchez; Conseulo M. Quinones; and Does 1 through 25. This action arises out of an agreement between Plaintiff and Yamil in which Plaintiff agreed to make an advance payment of $35,000 in exchange for Shipping USA to transport a specific 2017 Freightliner truck from Florida to Los Angeles County. (Complaint, ¶¶ 14-16.) On January 7, 2025, Plaintiff paid the full purchase price to the MorPro bank account and Yamil “provided Plaintiff with bill of sale and a copy of the certificate of title, which listed Shipping USA as the owner.” (Complaint, ¶ 16.) The complaint alleges that Defendants failed to perform and in June of 2025, Plaintiff learned that Shipping USA did not receive the full payment and that Yamil and MorPro owed a prior debt to Sanchez for an unrelated incident. (Complaint, ¶ 18.) The complaint alleges Sanchez would not release the truck until Yamil satisfied the debt and would not refund the money. (Complaint, ¶¶ 19-21.) Plaintiff later demanded the return of his money from Yamil who responded by brandishing a golf club and threatening him. (Complaint, ¶¶ 23-24.)

 

The complaint alleges six causes of action: (1) Breach of Contract (2) Promissory Estoppel (3) Unjust Enrichment (4) Fraudulent Misrepresentation (5) Unfair Competition (6) Breach of Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing.

 

Legal Standard

 

A defendant may move to quash service of summons on or before the last day to plead in response to the complaint or within such further time as the court may allow for good cause for lack of jurisdiction. (Code Civ. Proc., § 418.10, subd. (a)(1).) "A court of this state may exercise jurisdiction on any basis not inconsistent with the Constitution of this state or of the United States." (Code Civ. Proc., § 410.10.)

 

“Personal jurisdiction may be either general or specific. A nonresident defendant may be subject to the general jurisdiction of the forum if his or her contacts in the forum state are ‘substantial ... continuous and systematic.’ [Citations.] In such a case, ‘it is not necessary that the specific cause of action alleged be connected with the defendant's business relationship to the forum.’” (Vons Companies, Inc. v. Seabest Foods, Inc. (1996) 14 Cal.4th 434, 445-46 [Vons].) “If the nonresident defendant does not have substantial and systematic contacts in the forum sufficient to establish general jurisdiction, he or she still may be subject to the specific jurisdiction of the forum. (Id. at p. 446.) “A court may exercise specific jurisdiction over a nonresident defendant only if: (1) ‘the defendant has purposefully availed himself or herself of forum benefits’ [citation]; (2) ‘the “controversy is related to or ‘arises out of’ [the] defendant's contacts with the forum” ’ [citation]; and (3) ‘ “the assertion of personal jurisdiction would comport with ‘fair play and substantial justice’ ” ’ [citation].” (Jayone Foods, Inc. v. Aekyung Industrial Co. Ltd. (2019) 31 Cal.App.5th 543, 553 [Jayone Foods].)

 

“ ‘When a defendant moves to quash service of process’ [on jurisdictional grounds], ‘the plaintiff has the initial burden of demonstrating facts justifying the exercise of jurisdiction.’ [Citation.] ‘If the plaintiff meets this initial burden, then the defendant has the burden of demonstrating “that the exercise of jurisdiction would be unreasonable.” ’ [Citation.]” (Jayone Foods, supra, 31 Cal.App.5th at p. 553.) "The plaintiff must provide specific evidentiary facts, through affidavits and other authenticated documents, sufficient to allow the court to independently conclude whether jurisdiction is appropriate. [Citation.] The plaintiff cannot rely on allegations in an unverified complaint or vague and conclusory assertions of ultimate facts. [Citation.]” (Strasner v. Touchstone Wireless Repair & Logistics, LP (2016) 5 Cal.App.5th 215, 222.)

 

Discussion

 

Moving Defendants move to quash the service of summons and complaint to them on the grounds that the Court does not have personal jurisdiction over them.

 

Moving Defendants are Florida residents and a Florida corporation. (Sanchez Decl., ¶¶ 2-5; Quinones Decl., ¶¶ 3-4.) As Plaintiff concedes that there is no general jurisdiction, the Court proceeds to assess specific jurisdiction. (Opp., 8:1.)

 

“ ‘The purposeful availment inquiry ... focuses on the defendant's intentionality. [Citation.] This prong is only satisfied when the defendant purposefully and voluntarily directs his activities toward the forum so that he should expect, by virtue of the benefit he receives, to be subject to the court's jurisdiction based on’ his contacts with the forum.” (Pavlovich v. Superior Court (2002) 29 Cal.4th 262, 269.) Thus, the ‘“ ‘purposeful availment’ requirement ensures that a defendant will not be haled into a jurisdiction solely as a result of ‘random,’ ‘fortuitous,’ or ‘attenuated’ contacts [citations], or of the ‘unilateral activity of another party or a third person.’ [Citations.]’” (Ibid.)

 

Moving Defendants argue that they never purposefully availed themselves of California’s laws. Moving Defendants argue that the complaint does not identify any theory conferring jurisdiction. Moving Defendants argue that Yamil did not have authority to bind Shipping USA to any jurisdiction. “For a State to exercise jurisdiction consistent with due process, that relationship must arise out of contacts that the ‘defendant himself’ creates with the forum.” (Walden v. Fiore (2014) 571 U.S. 277, 284.) “ ‘[The] unilateral activity of another party or a third person is not an appropriate consideration when determining whether a defendant has sufficient contacts with a forum State to justify an assertion of jurisdiction.” (Ibid., citations omitted.) Further, the “ ‘minimum contacts’ analysis looks to the defendant's contacts with the forum State itself, not the defendant's contacts with persons who reside there.” (Id., at p. 285.)

 

In opposition, Plaintiff argues that Moving Defendants purposefully availed themselves of California by participating in the subject transaction to deliver a truck to a California resident.

 

Plaintiff argues that Shipping USA was the title owner and dealer in possession of the subject truck. Plaintiff also argues that the bill of sale and certificate of title show Shipping USA’s involvement and MorPro’s role in the transaction. (Opp., Exhs. 1, 2.) Plaintiff also points to several texts he exchanged with Moving Defendants. The Court notes that these texts are not admissible. The texts are in the Spanish language and are not accompanied with a certification of the translation. (See Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1110(g) [“[e]xhibits written in a foreign language must be accompanied by an English translation, certified under oath by a qualified interpreter.”].) Plaintiff also refers to a Release Letter by MorPro, represented by Yamil, and addressed to Sanchez. (Opp., Exh. 4.)

 

In reply, Moving Defendants argue that Plaintiff is improperly characterizing the purchase of a truck as a direct transaction with Plaintiff. Moving Defendants argue that there were two separate transactions: (1) Shipping USA selling the truck to MorPro in Florida, but MorPro never paid for the truck and (2) MorPro’s transaction with Plaintiff for the truck. Moving Defendants argue that since there were two transactions, Shipping USA never contracted with Plaintiff. Moving Defendants refer to Plaintiff’s declaration attesting that he never communicated with Moving Defendants until June of 2025, which was after Plaintiff had not received the truck. (Guevara Decl., ¶¶ 18-19.)

 

The Court agrees that neither the complaint nor any evidence shows that Moving Defendants directly transacted with Plaintiff. Plaintiff declares that “[Yamil] told me the vehicle was located in Florida with Shipping U.S.A. Corp., which he described to me as connected with or associated with his company. He told me that, after I paid, the vehicle would be delivered to me in Los Angeles County.” (Guevara Decl., ¶ 4.) The complaint alleges that Yamil provided the bill of sale and certificate of title to Plaintiff. (Complaint, ¶ 16.) Plaintiff attests that those documents confirmed to him Shipping USA was involved in the transaction and that the “the truck was being…sent in connection with MorPro Inc.” (Guevara Decl., ¶ 8.) Moreover, the Release Letter is between MorPro, via Yamil, and address to Sanchez. (Guevara Decl., ¶ 10.)

 

The Court agrees with Plaintiff’s evidence insofar as it shows that Moving Defendants were the owners of the truck and refused to release the truck based on a dispute with MorPro. (Sanchez Decl., ¶ 9.) However, these two facts do not demonstrate purposeful availment with the state of California. “The placement of a product into the stream of commerce, without more, is not an act of the defendant purposefully directed toward the forum State.” (Asahi Metal Industry Co., Ltd. v. Superior Court of California, Solano County (1987) 480 U.S. 102, 112.) “Additional conduct of the defendant may indicate an intent or purpose to serve the market in the forum State, for example, designing the product for the market in the forum State, advertising in the forum State, establishing channels for providing regular advice to customers in the forum State, or marketing the product through a distributor who has agreed to serve as the sales agent in the forum State.” (Ibid.)

 

Plaintiff does not show any conduct by Moving Defendants to indicate an intent or purpose to serve the market in California. Further, as discussed above, there is no evidence that Yamil acted as an agent on behalf of Shippers USA. Moving Defendants assert that “Yamil Morales is not an authorized agent of Shipping USA, nor has Shipping USA ever taken any action that could reasonably suggest to third parties that Mr. Morales was acting as its agent.” (Sanchez Decl., ¶ 10.) To the extent that Plaintiff argues that their interactions after the alleged purpose confer jurisdiction, Plaintiff specifies that he initiated contact with Moving Defendants. (Guevara Decl., ¶ 19.) This is not equivalent to Moving Defendants initiating contact with Plaintiff or the state of California.

 

Based on the foregoing, the Court finds that Plaintiff does not meet his initial burden of showing purposeful availment. As each prong must be met to confer specific jurisdiction upon a defendant, the Court does not reach the subsequent prongs. “If the plaintiff is unable to demonstrate sufficient minimum contacts with the forum to justify jurisdiction, a court is not required to engage in the process of weighing the defendant's inconvenience of litigating in the forum against the plaintiff's interests in suing locally and California's interest in assuming jurisdiction.” (Strasner, supra, 5 Cal.App.5th at p. 226.)

 

Accordingly, Moving Defendants’ motion to quash service of summons and complaint is GRANTED.

 

The complaint is DISMISSED as to Shipping U.S.A. Corp., Alvaro Sanchez, and Consuelo M. Quinones.
