DEPARTMENT B LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 24TRCV00159    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                      Receiver GS Strategies, Inc.

Responding Party:                  None

Motion for Order Approving (1) the Receiver’s Final Report and Accounting; (2) the Receiver’s Final Fees and Costs; (3) the Proposed Distribution of the Remaining Funds; (4) Exonerating the Receiver’s Undertaking; and (5) Discharging the Receiver

 

            The Court considered the moving papers and final report and accounting.  No opposition was filed.

BACKGROUND

On January 17, 2024, plaintiff and petitioner City of Rolling Hills, on behalf of the People of the State of California filed a complaint for nuisance per se against Milton Street Residential Acquisitions, Inc. and Christine Price Beedle and petition for appointment of a receiver.

On January 15, 2025, an order was entered appointing receiver GS Strategies, Inc.

On January 27, 2025, GS Strategies posted a receiver bond.

On February 4, 2025, GS Strategies filed an initial inventory report.

On August 12, 2025, the Court granted defense counsel’s motion to be relieved as counsel of defendant Beedle.

On September 24, 2025, the Court entered an order approving the Receiver’s proposed remediation plan and financing plan, issuance of a first-priority Receiver’s certificate of indebtedness, and the Receiver’s and City’s fees and costs.

On May 1, 2026, the Court entered an order approving the sale of the property, the Receiver’s and the City’s interim expenses, and the distribution of funds from the sale of the property.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1184 states:  “(a) Motion or stipulation - A receiver must present by noticed motion or stipulation of all parties:

(1) A final account and report;

(2) A request for the discharge; and

(3) A request for exoneration of the receiver's surety.

(b) No memorandum required - No memorandum needs to be submitted in support of the motion or stipulation served and filed under (a) unless the court so orders.

(c) Notice - Notice of the motion or of the stipulation must be given to every person or entity known to the receiver to have a substantial, unsatisfied claim that will be affected by the order or stipulation, whether or not the person or entity is a party to the action or has appeared in it.

(d) Claim for compensation for receiver or attorney - If any allowance of compensation for the receiver or for an attorney employed by the receiver is claimed in an account, it must state in detail what services have been performed by the receiver or the attorney and whether previous allowances have been made to the receiver or attorney and the amounts.”

Health and Safety Code §17980.7(c) states, in part:  “The receiver shall be discharged when the conditions cited in the notice of violation have been remedied in accordance with the court order or judgment and a complete accounting of all costs and repairs has been delivered to the court.  Upon removal of the condition, the owner, the mortgagee, or any lienor of record may apply for the discharge of all moneys not used by the receiver for removal of the condition and all other costs authorized by this section.

(10) After discharging the receiver, the court may retain jurisdiction for a time period not to exceed 18 consecutive months, which may be extended by order of the court to ensure continuing compliance, and require the owner and the enforcement agency responsible for enforcing Section 17980 to report to the court in accordance with a schedule determined by the court.

(11) The prevailing party in an action pursuant to this section shall be entitled to reasonable attorney’s fees and court costs as may be fixed by the court.”

DISCUSSION

            Receiver GS Strategies, Inc. requests an order pursuant to Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1184 (1) approving the Receiver’s final report and accounting; (2) approving the Receiver’s final fees and costs; (3) approving the distribution of the remaining receivership funds; (4) exonerating the Receiver’s undertaking; and (5) discharging the Receiver.

            The Court finds that the motion meets the requirements under Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1184.  After consideration of the final report and accounting, the Court is inclined to grant the motion at the hearing.  The Court finds that the conditions cited in the notice of violation have been remedied in accordance with the Court order and a complete accounting of all costs and repairs has been delivered to the Court.

ORDER

            The motion is GRANTED.

            The Receiver shall give notice.


Case Number: 25TRCV01856    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                       Plaintiff Bhavik V. Thakkar

Responding Party:                   Defendant American Honda Motor Co., Inc.

Motion to Compel Deposition of Defendant’s Person(s) Most Qualified

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

The motion is GRANTED.  See below.

BACKGROUND

On June 6, 2025, plaintiff Bhavik V. Thakkar filed a complaint against American Honda Motor Co., Inc. for violation of Song-Beverly Act as to a 2021 Honda Civic R. 

On May 22, 2026, pursuant to stipulation and order, the June 16, 2026 trial date was continued to June 28, 2027.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

CCP §2025.450(a) provides:  “If, after service of a deposition notice, a party to the action or an officer, director, managing agent, or employee of a party, . . , without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document . . . described in the deposition notice, the party giving the notice may move for an order compelling the deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production for inspection of any document . . . described in the deposition notice.”

CCP §2025.450(b) provides, “A motion under subdivision (a) shall comply with both of the following:

(1)        The motion shall set forth specific facts showing good cause justifying the production for inspection of any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing described in the deposition notice.

(2)        The motion shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040, or, when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, by a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”

DISCUSSION

Plaintiff requests that the Court compel defendant to produce a PMQ for deposition pertaining to matters for examination 1 through 11.

Plaintiff’s counsel states in his declaration that on May 15, 2026, plaintiff served a notice of deposition of defendant’s PMQ for June 10, 2026, detailing eleven matters for examination.  On June 8, defendant served objections and did not provide alternative dates.  On June 17, plaintiff served a notice of deposition, for June 30.  On June 24, defendant served objections and did not provide alternative dates.  On June 25, plaintiff served a notice of deposition for July 13.  On June 29, defendant served objections and did not provide alternative dates.  Plaintiff’s counsel states that he attempted to meet and confer.  On June 24, plaintiff’s counsel requested that defendant provide available dates.  Later that same day, defense counsel offered May 4, 2027, which is only one month before the continued trial date.  Plaintiff’s counsel requested an available date in 2026.  On June 29, plaintiff’s counsel followed up and explained that a deposition date no later than October 2026 was necessary to allow sufficient time to complete the remaining discovery and to adequately prepare for trial.  As of the motion filing date, defense counsel had not provided any earlier dates for deposition.

In opposition, defendant argues that it has never refused to produce a PMQ for deposition.  Defendant notes that as to the June 10 deposition, on June 8, defense counsel emailed plaintiff’s counsel that the PMQ will not appear on June 10 but that he would be in touch soon with a proposed date.  On June 9, defense counsel sent another email that the PMQ was in fact available for June 10, but plaintiff’s counsel never responded.  Defendant asserts that thereafter plaintiff would unilaterally notice deposition dates and that defendant properly objected and stated that counsel was “invited to contact defense counsel to coordinate rescheduling.”

The Court rules as follows:  The Court finds that defendant did not act reasonably in proposing an available date more than ten months in the future and a month prior to the trial date.  The motion is thus GRANTED.

Sanctions

Under CCP §2025.450(g)(1), “If a motion under subdivision (a) is granted, the court shall impose a monetary sanction under Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 2023.010) in favor of the party who noticed the deposition and against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”

Plaintiff requests $3060 in sanctions.  The Court notes that the notice of motion does not “identify every person, party, and attorney against whom the sanction is sought,” as required by CCP §2023.040.  Defendant requests $1350 in sanctions against plaintiff and his counsel.  Defendant likewise does not adequately notice the request for sanctions as required by CCP §2023.040.

ORDER

The motion is GRANTED. 

The requests for sanctions are DENIED.

Defendant is ordered to produce a PMQ for deposition within ___ days.

Plaintiff is ordered to give notice of ruling.


Case Number: 26TRCV00502    Hearing Date: September 23, 2026    Dept: B

Moving Parties:                       Defendant Carlton Square Homeowners Association

Responding Party:                   Plaintiff Cynthia Chaudhry

(1)   Motion to Compel Further Responses to Form Interrogatories, Set One

(2)   Motion to Compel Further Responses to Special Interrogatories, Set One.

 

            The Court considered the moving, opposition, and reply papers.

RULING

            See Order below.

BACKGROUND

On February 10, 2026, plaintiff Cynthia Chaudhry filed a complaint against Carlton Square Homeowners Association for negligence and premises liability based on a trip and fall on March 11, 2024.

On April 22, 2026, defendant filed a cross-complaint against Roes 1-20.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

45-Day Rule:  This motion must be served within 45 days after service of the response in question (extended if served by mail, overnight delivery, or fax; see CCP §1013); otherwise, the demanding party waives the right to compel any further response to the CCP §2031.010 demand.  CCP §§2031.310(c), 2016.050; see Sperber v. Robinson (1994) 26 Cal. App. 4th 736, 745.  The 45-day time limit is mandatory and jurisdictional.  Sexton v. Superior Court (1997) 58 Cal. App. 4th 1403, 1410.  The parties, however, can also agree in writing on a specific later date by which to file the motion to compel.  CCP §2031.310(c).

Meet-and-Confer Requirement:  The motion to compel further responses must be accompanied by a declaration showing “a reasonable and good faith attempt” to resolve the issues outside of court (so-called “meet and confer”).  CCP §§2016.040, 2031.310(b)(2).

Separate Statement:  Any motion involving the content of a discovery request or the responses to such a request shall be accompanied by a separate statement.  This includes a motion to compel further responses to demand for inspection of documents or tangible things.  CRC Rule 3.1020(a)(3). 

Interrogatories

CCP §2030.300 states:  “(a) On receipt of a response to interrogatories, the propounding party may move for an order compelling a further response if the propounding party deems that any of the following apply:   (1) An answer to a particular interrogatory is evasive or incomplete. . . . (3) An objection to an interrogatory is without merit or too general.  (b) A motion under subdivision (a) shall be accompanied by a meet and confer declaration under Section 2016.040. (c) Unless notice of this motion is given within 45 days of the service of the verified response, or any supplemental verified response, or on or before any specific later date to which the propounding party and the responding party have agreed in writing, the propounding party waives any right to compel a further response to the interrogatories. . . .”

DISCUSSION

            Defendant Carlton Square Homeowners Association requests an order compelling plaintiff Cynthia Chaudhry to respond further to defendant’s Form Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 2.11, 2.12, 2.13, 4.1, 6.3, and 12.1 and Special Interrogatories, Set One, Nos. 8 and 48.

            Defense counsel states in her declaration that on April 22, 2026, defendant served plaintiff with form and special interrogatories.  On June 1, plaintiff served verified responses.  On July 7, defense counsel sent a meet and confer email to plaintiff’s counsel that plaintiff’s responses were inadequate and requested further responses by July 13.  On July 16, defense counsel sent a follow-up email explaining why further responses were necessary.  As of the filing date of the motions, plaintiff had not served further responses.

            In an opposition, plaintiff’s counsel states in his declaration that supplemental answers were provided on August 7.

            The Court rules as follows:  After the motions were filed, plaintiff served supplemental responses.  As to the Form Interrogatories, plaintiff’s supplemental responses state, “We stand by the responses given” as to Nos. 4.1 and 6.3 and provide further information as to the other form interrogatories at issue.  The Court finds that the initial responses as to Nos. 4.1 and 6.3 were sufficient and code-compliant and the supplemental responses properly respond to the balance.  Thus, there is nothing to compel further.

As to the Special Interrogatories, plaintiff’s supplemental responses state, “We stand by the responses given.”  The Court finds that plaintiff’s responses are deficient.  As to No. 8, plaintiff fails to state with specificity and particularity how plaintiff calculated her claimed damages.  She provides only a lump sum number.  As to No. 48, she was asked to state the date that she last had her eyes examined by a health care professional before the incident.  She responded, “Never, I do wear glasses.”  The Court finds plaintiff’s response unclear.

            Sanctions

            Defendant requests $1410 ($225/hr. x 6 hrs., filing fee) as to each motion against plaintiff and her attorney of record.  The Court finds that sanctions are warranted in the amount of $570 ($225/hr. x 2 hrs., $120 filing fee) against plaintiff and her attorney of record, Kenneth C. Yeager.

ORDER

            The motion to compel further responses to form interrogatories, set one is DENIED.

            The motion to compel further responses to special interrogatories, set one is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to respond further to special interrogatories nos. 8 and 48, within twenty days.

            Plaintiff and her attorney of record Kenneth C. Yeager are ordered to pay sanctions to defendant in the amount of $570, in total for both motions within thirty days.

            Defendant is ordered to give notice of ruling.
