Case Number: 23AHCV00849    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING PLAINTIFF’S MOTION TO REOPEN ACTION AND RESTORE CASE TO CALENDER

I.         INTRODUCTION

Plaintiffs Weizhou Tong and Li Tian ("Plaintiffs") bring this action against Defendant Huan Da ("Defendant") asserting claims arising from Defendant's alleged misappropriation of funds that Plaintiffs provided in connection with posting bail for Plaintiff Tong. After Defendant failed to appear, Plaintiffs obtained entry of default and sought a default judgment. The Court continued the Order to Show Cause regarding default judgment on multiple occasions to permit Plaintiffs to submit a compliant default judgment package. When the deficiencies remained unresolved after several continuances, the Court dismissed the action without prejudice on September 30, 2025.

On March 30, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion to reopen the action and restore the case to the Court's calendar pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). At the May 6, 2026, hearing, the Court found counsel's declaration insufficient and continued the matter to permit Plaintiffs to file an amended declaration and memorandum addressing the requirements of section 473. On July 7, 2026, Plaintiffs filed the amended motion.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b) provides for either discretionary or mandatory relief from certain prior actions or proceedings in the trial court. (Luri v. Greenwald (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 1119, 1124.) 

“‘Under the discretionary relief provision, on a showing of “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect,” the court has discretion to allow relief from a “judgment, dismissal, order, or other proceeding taken against” a party or his or her attorney. Under the mandatory relief provision, on the other hand, upon a showing by attorney declaration of “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect,” the court shall vacate any “resulting default judgment or dismissal entered.”’ [Citation.] Applications seeking relief under the mandatory provision of section 473 must be ‘accompanied by an attorney’s sworn affidavit attesting to his or her mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect.’ The mandatory provision further adds that ‘whenever relief is granted based on an attorney’s affidavit of fault [the court shall] direct the attorney to pay reasonable compensatory legal fees and costs to opposing counsel or parties.’” (Ibid.; Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (b).) 

Application for this relief shall be accompanied by a copy of the answer or other pleading proposed to be filed therein, otherwise the application shall not be granted, and shall be made within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding six months, after the judgment, dismissal, order, or proceeding was taken.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 473, subd. (b).)

III.      ANALYSIS

As an initial matter, Plaintiffs timely sought relief within six months of the September 30, 2025, dismissal. Plaintiffs filed the original motion on March 30, 2026, and, at the May 6, 2026, hearing, the Court continued the matter to permit counsel to file an amended declaration and memorandum addressing the requirements of Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Thus, the Court finds the motion timely. 

Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 473, Plaintiffs seek to vacate the dismissal entered on September 30, 2025, and restore this action to the Court's calendar. Plaintiffs contend the dismissal resulted from counsel's mistake, inadvertence, and neglect caused by a serious medical condition. Counsel has submitted a sworn affidavit accepting full responsibility for the deficiencies in the default judgment package and asserting that his illness substantially impaired his ability to manage the litigation. (Motion, p. 10; Avery Decl., ¶ 9.)

Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b), authorizes both mandatory and discretionary relief. Mandatory relief applies when the application is timely, is accompanied by an attorney affidavit of fault, and the dismissal was in fact caused by the attorney's mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or neglect. Discretionary relief requires a showing of mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect.

Here, although counsel has now submitted an affidavit acknowledging fault, the Court is not persuaded that the September 30, 2025, dismissal was caused solely by the attorney error described in the declaration. Rather, the record reflects that the dismissal followed numerous continuances over several months, during which the Court repeatedly identified substantive deficiencies in Plaintiffs' default judgment package and afforded Plaintiffs multiple opportunities to submit compliant materials.

For example, at the May 14, 2025, OSC, the Court advised counsel that the default judgment package required correction and continued the matter to allow Plaintiffs an opportunity to do so. (May 14, 2025, Minute Order.) At the August 13, 2025, OSC, the Court again identified substantive deficiencies, specifically noting an inconsistency between Plaintiffs' alleged actual loss of $100,000 and the amount sought in the proposed judgment, as well as the absence of any declaration or evidence establishing that Plaintiff Tong suffered an out-of-pocket loss. The Court nevertheless granted Plaintiffs a final continuance to prepare and submit a corrected default judgment package. (Aug. 13, 2025, Minute Order.)

At the continued hearing on September 30, 2025, however, the Court observed that the matter had already been continued four times to allow Plaintiffs to submit a proper default judgment package free of deficiencies. After reviewing Plaintiffs' latest submission, the Court found that issues with the declarations and the amounts requested remained unresolved and dismissed the action without prejudice. (Sep. 30, 2025, Minute Order.)

While the Court is sympathetic to counsel's medical condition and recognizes the difficulties it may have presented, the record demonstrates that counsel was afforded multiple opportunities over many months to correct the deficiencies identified by the Court. Despite those opportunities and explicit guidance, counsel continued to submit deficient default judgment packages. Under these circumstances, the Court cannot conclude that the dismissal resulted solely from the attorney error described in counsel's declaration, but rather from the repeated failure to remedy the deficiencies despite multiple continuances and express warnings from the Court.

Further, the Court finds Plaintiffs have not established excusable neglect warranting discretionary relief under Code of Civil Procedure section 473, subdivision (b). Excusable neglect is tested by whether a reasonably prudent person under the same or similar circumstances might have made the same error. Here, the Court repeatedly identified the deficiencies in Plaintiffs' default judgment package, granted multiple continuances to permit correction, and expressly advised counsel of the issues requiring attention. Despite those opportunities, counsel continued to submit deficient materials. Under these circumstances, the Court cannot conclude that a reasonably prudent attorney would have repeatedly failed to cure the identified deficiencies after receiving explicit guidance from the Court.  Thus, discretionary relief is not warranted.

Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Plaintiffs motion to reopen this action.

IV.       ORDER

            The Court DENIES Plaintiff’s motion to reopen the case.

            Plaintiff is to give notice.

Case Number: 24NNCV00267    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING DEFENDANTS AND CROSS-COMPLAINANTS W&B AND HUI’S MOTION TO BIFURCATE

I.         INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff Milano Realty, LLC ("Plaintiff") brings this action against Defendants W&B Investment Group, LLC and Vincent Hung Shing Hui (collectively, "Defendants") arising out of a dispute concerning the sale of real property located at 12138 Valley Boulevard in El Monte, California. Plaintiff alleges Defendants breached the parties' purchase agreement by failing to convey title to one of the property's two parcels, resulting in delays in refinancing and additional interest, fees, and other damages. Defendants filed a cross-complaint asserting claims for, among other things, breach of contract, negligence, indemnification, and constructive fraud.

On April 27, 2026, Defendants filed the instant motion to bifurcate trial pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 598 Plaintiff filed an opposition on June 29, 2026, and Defendants filed a reply on July 7, 2026.

Request for judicial notice

Defendants request judicial notice of eleven exhibits (A-K) consisting of California Secretary of State records, California Department of Real Estate records, a fictious business name statement recorded with the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk and recorded corrective grant deed.

The request is GRANTED pursuant to Evidence Code sections 452, subdivisions (c) and (h), and 453.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

Civ. Proc. Code §598, permits a court to bifurcate a trial as to the issues of liability and damages when the ends of justice and the pursuit of judicial economy is best served. 

“The court may, when the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economy and efficiency of handling the litigation would be promoted thereby, on motion of a party, after notice and hearing, make an order, no later than the close of pretrial conference in cases in which such pretrial conference in cases in which such pretrial conference is to be held, or, in other cases, no later than 30 days before the trial date, that the trial of any issue or any part thereof shall precede the trial of any other issue or any party thereof in the case . . .” (CCP § 598.) Where trial of the issue of liability as to all causes of action precedes the trial of other issues or parts thereof and the decision of the court or jury is in favor of the allegedly liable party, judgment in favor of that party shall be entered and no trial of other issues in the action against that party will be had. (Ibid.) If the decision of the court or jury on the issue of liability is against the party allegedly liable, the trial of the other issues shall be had before the same or another jury as ordered by the court. (Ibid.)

III.      ANALYSIS

Defendants move pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 598 for an order bifurcating trial so that the following threshold issues are tried before liability, causation, and damages: (1) whether Plaintiff Milano Realty, LLC is the real party in interest; (2) whether the conduct, transactions, and alleged injuries attributed to "Milano Realty" are properly attributable to Plaintiff, as opposed to another entity or non-party; (3) whether Plaintiff possessed the legal capacity to sue during the periods relevant to its claims; and (4) the legal effect of Plaintiff's prior suspension on its ability to accrue and recover damages. The Court finds bifurcation is not warranted.

Bifurcation would not promote judicial economy because the proposed threshold issues substantially overlap with the merits

Code of Civil Procedure section 598 authorizes bifurcation where doing so would promote the convenience of witnesses, the ends of justice, or the economy and efficiency of handling the litigation. Here, Defendants have not demonstrated that bifurcation would achieve those purposes.

Defendants seek to try first whether Plaintiff is the real party in interest, whether the conduct and alleged injuries are attributable to Plaintiff rather than another entity, whether Plaintiff had the legal capacity to sue during relevant periods, and the legal effect of Plaintiff's suspension on its ability to recover damages.  (Motion, p. 2.) Although Defendants characterize these as threshold legal issues, resolution of each would require the Court to consider many of the same documents, witnesses, and factual issues that bear on Plaintiff's substantive claims.

For example, determining whether Milano Realty, LLC is the proper party necessarily requires examination of the purchase transaction, escrow documents, loan documents, communications between the parties, and the payments Plaintiff allegedly made—all evidence that will also be presented in connection with liability and damages. Likewise, determining the effect of Plaintiff's suspension on its claimed damages requires the Court to determine when those damages accrued and whether they were caused by Defendants' alleged conduct, issues that overlap substantially with Plaintiff's breach of contract and damages theories.

Based on this, bifurcation would not eliminate the need to present this evidence. Instead, it would require much of the same evidence to be presented in two separate phases of trial, increasing rather than reducing the expenditure of judicial and party resources. Under these circumstances, the Court finds that bifurcation would not promote the economy and efficiency contemplated by Code of Civil Procedure section 598.

The Court’s May 1, 2026 - summary judgment ruling weighs against bifurcation

The Court's prior summary judgment ruling further weighs against bifurcation. In ruling on Plaintiff's motion for summary judgment, the Court considered Defendants' arguments that Plaintiff lacked standing because another entity, CCL Financial Services, Inc., allegedly conducted business under the name "Milano Realty," and that Plaintiff's prior suspension affected its ability to recover. The Court concluded that Defendants' evidence did not establish that Plaintiff lacked standing, explaining that "[t]he existence of another entity claiming to use Milano Realty as a business alias does not defeat Plaintiff's standing to prosecute a suit in its own name." (May 1, 2026, Minute Order, pp. 4-5.) The Court also concluded that Peacock Hill Assn. v. Peacock Lagoon Constr. Co. (1972) 8 Cal.3d 369 did not support Defendants' suspension argument because it concerns a suspended corporation's ability to prosecute an action, and any suspension would warrant, at most, abatement until the suspension was cured. (Id. at pp. 5-6.)

Although the Court's prior ruling did not decide whether bifurcation is appropriate under Code of Civil Procedure section 598, it reflects that the issues Defendants seek to bifurcate are intertwined with the factual record and the merits of Plaintiff's claims, rather than discrete threshold issues capable of efficient resolution. Defendants have not shown that bifurcation would materially narrow the issues for trial or avoid the presentation of substantially overlapping evidence.

            Based on the foregoing, the Court DENIES Defendants’ motion to bifurcate trial.

IV.       ORDER

            The Court DENIES Defendants motion to bifurcate trial.

            Defendants are to give notice.

Case Number: 24NNCV06167    Hearing Date: July 14, 2026    Dept: P

[TENTATIVE] ORDER GRANTING PLAINTIFF YEELING KO’S MOTIONS TO COMPEL NON-PARTY A & W GROUP INVESTMENTS INC, AND GL LIGHTING SOLUTION INC TO COMPLY WITH DEPOSITION SUBPOENAS FOR PRODUCTION OF BUISNESS RECORDS and GRANTING MONETARY SANCTIONS

I.         INTRODUCTION

            This case concerns a dispute between the two shareholders and officers of Calpack International, Inc. Plaintiff Yeeling Ko (“Plaintiff”), Calpack’s majority shareholder and CEO, filed a First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) alleging that Defendant Gordon Iu (“Defendant”), the company’s minority shareholder, Secretary, and CFO, wrongfully excluded her from control over the business, financial records, and corporate assets. The FAC asserts causes of action for breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, conversion, fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, and accounting.

On April 23, 2025, following an ex parte hearing, the Court issued a Temporary Protective Order ("TPO") prohibiting Defendant from transferring any funds from Calpack's bank accounts. On July 14, 2025, the Court granted Plaintiff's motion for a preliminary injunction, continuing that prohibition pending final judgment.

On May 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed two motions to compel compliance with deposition subpoenas for production of business records directed to nonparties GL Lighting Solution Inc. (“GL”) and A & W Group Investments Inc. (“A & W”). No oppositions have been filed. Because the motions present identical legal and procedural issues, the Court addresses them in a single tentative ruling.

II.        LEGAL STANDARD

            Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1987.1, “the court, upon motion reasonably made [by a party] . . . may make an order . . . directing compliance with [a subpoena] upon those terms or conditions as the court shall declare . . .” (Code Civ. Proc. § 1987.1(a).) Likewise, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2025.480 subdivision (a), “[i]f a deponent fails to answer any question or to produce any document, electronically stored information, or tangible thing under the deponent’s control that is specified in the deposition notice or a deposition subpoena, the party seeking discovery may move the court for an order compelling that answer or production.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 2025.480(a).) If a nonparty deponent, properly served with a deposition subpoena, fails to attend a deposition, the court may impose¿sanctions. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 1987.2, 2020.240, 2025.440(b), 2025.480(j).) 

III.      ANALYSIS

            Plaintiff moves to compel nonparties GL and A & W to comply with the deposition subpoenas for production of business records. Plaintiff also seeks monetary sanctions against each non party.

Procedural requirements

On February 25, 2026, Plaintiff issued deposition subpoenas for production of business records to GL and A & W seeking documents relating to lease agreements and related payments. Both subpoenas were personally served on March 2, 2026, requiring production by March 30, 2026. GL and A & W failed to produce documents, serve written objections, request extensions, or otherwise respond. (Motions, pp. 4-5.) On April 7, 2026, Plaintiff's counsel sent final notices requesting compliance within five business days, but neither nonparty responded. (Id. at p. 5.) Plaintiff thereafter timely filed the instant motions to compel compliance. The Court finds that the motions are procedurally proper.

Merits

Here, neither GL nor A & W objected to the subpoenas, moved to quash, or otherwise responded. Although Plaintiff followed up after the production deadline and requested compliance, neither nonparty produced any responsive documents or communicated with Plaintiff. Plaintiff represents that the requested records relate to lease agreements and payments that are relevant to the claims at issue in this action.

Plaintiff's counsel further declares that Defendant's counsel objected to the subpoenas by email and represented that Defendant intended to file a motion to quash. (Xu Decls., ¶ 20.) However, no motion to quash was ever filed. On this record, the Court finds good cause to compel GL and A & W to comply with the subpoenas. (Code Civ. Proc., § 1987.1, subd. (a).)

Sanctions

Plaintiff seeks monetary sanctions against GL and A & W pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure sections 2023.010 and 2023.030 for their failure to comply with the subpoenas. Plaintiff requests $4,200.00 against GL and $5,600.00 against A & W, representing attorney's fees incurred in preparing the motions. (Motions, p. 6.) Counsel declares that he spent 4 hours preparing the motion against GL and 6 hours preparing the motion against A & W at an hourly rate of $350 and anticipates spending additional time preparing replies. (Xu Decls., ¶¶ 21, 24.)

The Court finds that monetary sanctions are warranted. Both nonparties failed to comply with properly served subpoenas or respond to Plaintiff's follow-up requests, necessitating these motions.

However, because no opposition was filed, the Court does not award anticipated reply time. The Court further finds the requested hours excessive given the straightforward nature of the motions and the substantial overlap between them. The motions are nearly identical and required minimal independent analysis.

The Court therefore GRANTS monetary sanctions against GL Lighting Solution Inc. in the amount of $700.00, representing 2 hours of reasonable attorney time at counsel's hourly rate. The Court also GRANTS monetary sanctions against A & W Group Investments Inc. in the amount of $700.00, likewise representing 2 hours of reasonable attorney time at counsel's hourly rate.

IV.       CONCLUSION

            The Court GRANTS Plaintiff's motions to compel compliance with the deposition subpoenas. GL Lighting Solution Inc. and A & W Group Investments Inc. shall produce all documents responsive to the subpoenas within 30 days of notice of this order. 

The Court further GRANTS monetary sanctions against GL Lighting Solution Inc. in the amount of $700.00 and against A & W Group Investments Inc. in the amount of $700.00, payable within 30 days of notice of this order.

            Plaintiff is to give notice.
