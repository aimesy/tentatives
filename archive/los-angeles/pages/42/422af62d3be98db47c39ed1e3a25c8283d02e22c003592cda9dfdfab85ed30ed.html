[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

 

Dept. 506

8:30 a.m.

August 25, 2026

 

On April 12, 2024, Plaintiffs Revel Management Group LLC, Laura Mellado Hallman, and Leslie Quezada filed this action against Defendants Pica Pica TX Snacks Inc. (“Pica Snacks”), Amanda Castillo, and Eusebio Salazar.  Plaintiffs later named Pica Pica TX Inc. (“Pica TX”) as Doe 1.

The Complaint alleges (1) breach of contract, (2) common count, (3) account stated, (4) open book account, and (5) quantum meruit/unjust enrichment.

On May 21, 2026, Plaintiffs filed a motion for summary judgment, or in the alternative, summary adjudication.

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Plaintiffs’ request for judicial notice is granted.

LEGAL STANDARD

A plaintiff moving for summary adjudication must satisfy the initial burden of proof by proving each element of a cause of action.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(1).)  That includes damages when damages are an element.  (Paramount Petroleum Corp. v. Superior Court (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 226, 241.)  Then the burden shifts to the defendant to show that a triable issue of material fact exists as to the cause of action or a defense.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 437c, subd. (p)(2).)  To establish a triable issue of material fact, the party opposing the motion must produce “substantial responsive evidence.”  (Sangster v. Paetkau (1998) 68 Cal.App.4th 151, 162-163.)

DISCUSSION

Plaintiffs entered into a series of written agreements with “Pica Pica Candy Inc.” between 2020 and 2023.  (Undisputed Material Facts “UMF” 3-4.)  “Pica Pica Candy Inc.” does not exist.  (UMF 5.)  Plaintiff relies on discovery admissions to establish that Pica Snacks was a party to the contracts and received benefits under the contracts.  (See UMF 43-44.)

 “Any matter admitted in response to a request for admission is conclusively established against the party making the admission in the pending action.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2033.410, subd. (a).)  “As a general rule an admission is conclusive in the action as to the party making it.  [Citations.]”  (Murillo v. Superior Court (2006) 143 Cal.App.4th 730, 736.)  But the trial court has the power “to determine the admissibility and relevance of evidence related to admitted facts. . . .  The court must have discretion to admit evidence to elucidate and explain an admission, because the admission of a fact does not always reflect the party’s reasonable understanding of that fact.”  (Fredericks v. Filbert Co. (1987) 189 Cal.App.3d 272, 278.)

 

After Defendants’ counsel was relieved, Amanda Castillo prepared and served discovery responses in which she mistakenly answered them for the wrong entities.  (Castillo Decl. ¶¶ 22-25.)  There is a triable issue of fact regarding Defendants’ liability under the contracts.

Plaintiffs also argue that Defendants are the alter ego of each other.  (See Motion at pp. 22-24.)  Even if Plaintiffs met their initial burden, Defendants provide evidence demonstrating distinct business operations.  (See Castillo Decl. ¶¶ 4-11 & Ex. B; Salazar Decl. ¶¶ 3-6.)

In sum, there are triable issues of material fact regarding each Defendant’s liability and obligations under the contracts.  Summary judgment is denied.

CONCLUSION

The motion for summary judgment is DENIED.

[TENTATIVE] ORDER DENYING MOTION FOR LEAVE TO AMEND; DENYING MOTION FOR ACCESS

On August 22, 2025, Plaintiff William Leo Jackson filed a first amended complaint (“FAC”) against Defendants Los Angeles County Sheriffs Men Central Jail, Robert Luna in his Capacity of Sheriff of Los Angeles County Sheriff’s Department and Facilities Only, and Sheriff Deputy Adam Kennedy.

On September 22, 2025, Defendants Los Angeles County Sheriffs Men Central Jail and Robert Luna in his Capacity of Sheriff of Los Angeles County Sheriff’s Department and Facilities Only filed a demurrer to the FAC.  The Court sustained the demurrer on April 16, 2026 and dismissed those Defendants.

On May 28, 2026, Plaintiff filed a “Motion for Leave to File Second Amended Complaint and Opposition to Defendants’ Demurrer” and a “Motion for Order to Access Court and Formal Notice to Prison of Plaintiff’s Liberty to Attend Court Hearings Unrestricted.”

MOTION TO AMEND

The Court may, in its discretion and after notice to the adverse party, allow an amendment to any pleading.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 473, subd. (a)(1).)  A motion to amend a pleading must include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments and must state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted or added, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the allegations are located.  (California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(a).)  The motion shall also be accompanied by a declaration attesting to the effect of the amendment, why the amendment is necessary and proper, when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered, and why the request for amendment was not made earlier.  (California Rules of Court, rule 3.1324(b).)

Plaintiff’s motion does not comply with these requirements.  Instead, it primarily opposes the September 22, 2025 demurrer that was sustained on April 16, 2026.  Plaintiff already opposed the demurrer with his Opposition signed and dated March 26, 2026.  Plaintiff also does not provide a copy of his proposed second amended complaint.

The motion to amend is DENIED.

MOTION TO ACCESS COURT

Plaintiff asserts that his failure to appear at hearings was due to the prison’s policies, and he “request[s] a rehearing of any and all hearings involuntarily missed.”

The Court’s prior orders were not based on Plaintiff’s failure to appear.  The Court decided the motions on their merits after consideration of the written filings.  Plaintiff may still attend future hearings telephonically.

The motion is DENIED.

On June 24, 2025, Plaintiff Ricardo Quezada filed this Song-Beverly action against Defendant General Motors, LLC.

On July 20, 2026, Defendant filed a motion to compel Plaintiff’s initial deposition under Code of Civil Procedure section 871.26, subdivision (c)(1).

Plaintiff objected to Defendant scheduling his deposition on January 9, 2026 and May 26, 2026, and he failed to appear for his May 26, 2026 deposition.  (Stark Decl. ¶¶ 5-6, 9-10.)  The statutory deadline to conduct the deposition of Plaintiff was March 13, 2026.  (Stark Decl. ¶ 11; see Code Civ. Proc., § 871.26, subd. (c)(1).)

Plaintiff contends that the motion is now moot because Plaintiff’s deposition is confirmed for August 31, 2026.  (Smith Decl. ¶ 13.)  Given Plaintiff’s prior failures to appear, his agreement to a future date does not moot the motion.

The motion to compel deposition is GRANTED.  Plaintiff is ordered to appear for his August 31, 2026 deposition.

Defendant’s request for mandatory sanctions is also GRANTED.  Plaintiff’s counsel is ORDERED to pay total sanctions of $1,500.00 to Defendant within 15 business days.  (See Code Civ. Proc, § 871.26, subd. (j)(2).)

[TENTATIVE] ORDER OVERRULING DEMURRER

 

Dept. 506

8:30 a.m.

August 25, 2026

 

On November 17, 2025, Plaintiff Keith Smith filed this action against Defendant Ingrid Quinn and others.  The Complaint alleges (1) quiet title, (2) partition and sale of real property, and (3) accounting.

On February 20, 2026, Defendant filed a demurrer.

REQUEST FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

Defendant’s request for judicial notice of filings in Case No BC439949 is granted.  “[E]ven though a factual finding in a prior judicial decision may not establish the truth of that fact for purposes of judicial notice, the finding itself may be a proper subject of judicial notice if it has a res judicata or collateral estoppel effect in a subsequent action.”  (Kilroy v. State of California (2004) 119 Cal.App.4th 140, 148; see also Evid. Code, § 452, subd. (d).)

DEMURRER

A demurrer for sufficiency tests whether the complaint states a cause of action.  (Hahn v. Mirda (2007) 147 Cal.App.4th 740, 747.)  When considering demurrers, courts read the allegations liberally and in context, accepting the alleged facts as true.  (Nolte v. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 1401, 1406.)  “Because a demurrer challenges defects on the face of the complaint, it can only refer to matters outside the pleading that are subject to judicial notice.”  (Arce ex rel. Arce v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 556.)

Defendant’s demurrer is based on the parties’ 2010 litigation in Case No. BC439949.  Defendant argues that this action is barred by the doctrine of merger, res judicata, collateral estoppel, and the compulsory cross-complaint statute.

In Case No. BC439949, Quinn sought to quiet title to the same property, with Quinn having a 70% interest and Smith having a 30% interest.  (RJN, Ex. 1, ¶ 22.)  Eventually, the parties entered into a settlement agreement requiring Quinn to pay Smith $60,000 in exchange for a deed quitclaiming all of Smith’s interest to Quinn.  (RJN, Ex. 2.)  The settlement was reduced to a judgment on June 27, 2011.  (RJN, Ex. 2.)  Quinn never paid Smith, Smith never quitclaimed the property, and the judgment was not renewed.  (Complaint ¶ 15.)

In this action, Smith “seeks a determination of his fee simple title in this action as of June 20, 2011, the date of the Judgment in the Quinn Action.”  (Complaint ¶ 20.)  Smith also seeks an accounting of rents and profits from tenants who resided at the property since June 20, 2011.  (Complaint ¶ 32.)

Although Defendant argues that ownership of the property was already adjudicated (Demurrer at pp. 5-7), the June 27, 2011 judgment is no longer enforceable.  Plaintiff also could not have filed a cross-complaint in Case No. BC439949 because Plaintiff seeks to adjudicate the parties’ rights after the date of the judgment.  (Demurrer at pp. 7-8.)  At the pleading stage, the Court cannot conclude that Plaintiff’s claims are barred.

The demurrer is overruled.

CONCLUSION

The demurrer is OVERRULED.  Defendant is ordered to file an answer within 10 days.
