Case Number: 21SMCV01950    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendant William S. Jensen’s Motion for Attorney’s Fees is continued toTBD. Defendant to submit a supplemental declaration within 10 days providing (1) the total number of hours spent on the matter and (2) a table detailing the number of hours spent by each timekeeper, their hourly rate and the total amount of fees attributable to each timekeeper.  

REASONING

Defendant Jensen moves for attorney’s fees pursuant to Civil Code section 1717, Code of Civil Procedure sections 1021, 1032 and the Vacant Land Purchase Agreement and Joint Escrow Instructions (“Purchase Agreement”).  The Court granted Defendant Jensen’s motion for summary judgment of Plaintiff’s complaint for breach of contract and negligence on May 12, 2026.  Defendant Jensen moves for fees pursuant to paragraph 31 of the Purchase Agreement.  Defendant Jensen requests fees in the amount of $58,340.34 against Thompson, which includes $57,591.25 in attorney’s fees and $749.09 in costs.  

It is axiomatic that a party is permitted to recover reasonable attorney fees, when authorized by contract, statute, or law, pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5, subdivision (a)(10)(A)-(C).  Plaintiff Thompson sued Defendant Jensen for breach of the Purchase Agreement.  The Purchase Agreement contains an attorney’s fees clause:

“In any action, proceeding, or arbitration between Buyer and Seller arising out of this Agreement, the prevailing Buyer or Seller shall be entitled to reasonable attorney fees and costs from the non-prevailing Buyer or Seller, except as provided in paragraph 28A.”  (Snyder Dec., ¶2, Ex. 1.)  

Defendant Jensen is the undisputed prevailing party in this action, because he prevailed on his motion for summary judgment of Plaintiff’s complaint on April 2, 2026.  (Snyder Dec., ¶5, Ex. 2.)  Judgment was entered on Thompson’s complaint on May 12, 2026.  (Id.)  Jensen is the prevailing party on Thompson’s action arising out of the Purchase Agreement and entitled to recovery of fees.

The fee setting inquiry in California ordinarily “begins with the ‘lodestar’ [method], i.e., the number of hours reasonably expended multiplied by the reasonable hourly rate.” (Graciano v. Robinson Ford Sales, Inc. (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 140, 154.) “[A] computation of time spent on a case and the reasonable value of that time is fundamental to a determination of an appropriate attorneys’ fee award.” (Margolin v. Reg’l Planning Comm’n (1982) 134 Cal.App.3d 999, 1004.)

Defendant has provided detailed billings sheets with the motion describing the work performed. (Snyder Dec., ¶¶13 and 18, Ex. 3.)  Defense counsel attests to an hourly rate of $475/hr for counsel’s services and an hourly rate of $150/hr for the services of legal assistants Oliver and Varley.  Defense counsel does not provide a total of the number of hours spent on the entire case.  Because the invoices include services performed by Varley and Oliver, the number of hours cannot be determined by simply dividing the total fees requested by the $475 hourly rate.  If such a formula were applied, the number of hours spent on the case would be 122 hours.  

Defendant is ordered to serve a supplemental declaration providing (1) the total number of hours spent on the matter and (2) a table detailing the number of hours spent by each timekeeper, their hourly rate and the total amount of fees attributable to each timekeeper.  Defendant is to submit the supplemental declaration within 10 days. The hearing is continued to TBD so that Defendant can provide this supplemental declaration.

Plaintiff must provide further briefing as to why the request for $768,333.56 in medical damages is reasonable. According to Plaintiff, the total medical bills amounted to $766,905.62. Plaintiff’s insurance paid $155,492.73 of the total bill, Plaintiff paid $858.36 out of pocket, and the hospital wrote off $584,745.14 as contractual adjustments. An outstanding balance of $25,809.39 remains.

First, explain the inconsistency between the request for $768,333.56 and the medical bills of $766,905.62.
Second, in Howell v. Hamilton Meats & Provisions, Inc., the Court held that “an injured plaintiff whose medical expenses are paid through private insurance may recover as economic damages no more than the amounts paid by the plaintiff or his or her insurer for the medical services received or still owing at the time of trial.” (Howell v. Hamilton Meats & Provisions, Inc. (2011) 52 Cal. 4th 541, 566.) The California Supreme Court concluded that “the negotiated rate differential—the discount medical providers offer the insurer—is not a benefit provided to the plaintiff in compensation for his or her injuries.”  (Ibid.) In light of the Supreme Court’s ruling, explain why Plaintiff is entitled to $768,333.56 in medical damages.

Case Number: 25SMCV04454    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Defendants’ Motion for an Order to Compel Responses to Request for Document Production, Answers to Interrogatories and for Sanctions is DENIED without prejudice.

Defendants to give notice.

REASONING

Defendants move for an order compelling Plaintiff’s response to Request for Document Production, Special Interrogatories, and Form Interrogatories for failure to respond to the discovery propounded on May 20, 2026.  Defendants also request monetary sanctions in the amount of $560.

The Court finds that Defendants have impermissibly combined motions seeking different forms of relief and authorized by different statutes.  Combining motions allows the moving party to avoid paying the requisite filing fees. Filing fees are jurisdictional and it is mandatory for court clerks to demand and receive them.  (See Duran v. St. Luke's Hospital (2003) 114 Cal. App. 4th 457, 460; Gov. Code § 70617(a).)  Defendants paid one filing fee for what should have been three separate motions.

The Court further notes that the Motion contains information inconsistent with the allegations in the complaint.  For example, the Motion states that the alleged collision occurred on October 25, 2008, rather than August 29, 2023, and that the complaint was filed on October 22, 2010, rather than August 27, 2025. The Court directs Defendants to review the moving papers for accuracy and consistency prior to filing.

For the foregoing reasons, the Motion for an Order to Compel Responses to Request for Document Production, Answers to Interrogatories and for Sanctions filed by Defendants Jasmine Everett Lewis and Austin Everett Lewis is DENIED without prejudice.

Case Number: 26SMCV00320    Hearing Date: September 18, 2026    Dept: N

TENTATIVE RULING

Plaintiff Michael Lewis’s Motion to Compel Initial Responses to First Set of Demands for Production of Documents is GRANTED. The Court orders Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections, within 10 days of service of the Court’s order.

The Court also grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,071.76, to be paid by Defendant within 30 days of service of the Court’s order.

Plaintiff to give notice.


REASONING

According to Plaintiff, on May 8, 2026, Defendant Global was served with Demand for Production of Documents (Set One).  Plaintiff states that he never received responses, objections, or requests for an extension.  Thus, Plaintiff filed the instant motion to compel Defendant’s responses to the discovery request.  Plaintiff also requests monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,071.76 against Defendant: $1,000 under Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.050, subdivision (a), $60 for motion filing fees, and $11.76 for electronic filing fees.

In opposition, Defendant states that it has fully responded to the Demand for Production of Documents.

In its reply, Plaintiff does not dispute that responses have been served.  However, Plaintiff requests that the Court order all objections to be waived and order Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections, and produce all responsive documents, within 10 days, given that the responses were not timely.  Plaintiff also requests sanctions in the amount of $1,000 pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 2023.050, subdivision (a), plus $71.76 in filing and electronic-filing fees, to be paid by Defendant within 30 days of the Court’s order.

A party must respond to requests for production of documents within 30 days after service. (Code Civ. Proc., §2031.260, subd. (a).) If a party to whom requests for production of documents is directed does not provide timely responses, the requesting party may move for an order compelling response to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., §2031.300, subd. (b).) The responding party also waives the right to make any objections, including ones based on privilege or work-product protection. (Code Civ. Proc., §2031.300, subd. (a).) There is no time limit for a motion to compel responses to production of documents other than the cut-off on hearing discovery motions 15 days before trial. (Code Civ. Proc., §§2024.020, subd. (a), 2031.300.) No meet and confer efforts are required before filing a motion to compel responses to the discovery. (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.300; Sinaiko Healthcare Consulting, Inc. v. Pacific Healthcare Consultants (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 390, 411.)

Code of Civil Procedure, section 2023.030, subdivision (a) provides, in pertinent part, that the court may impose a monetary sanction on a party engaging in the misuse of the discovery process to pay the reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred by anyone because of that conduct. Misuse of discovery includes “failing to respond or submit to an authorized method of discovery.” (Code Civ. Proc., §2023.010, subd. (d)). Furthermore, pursuant to section 2031.300, subdivision (c), the court shall impose monetary sanctions “against any party, person, or attorney who unsuccessfully makes or opposes a motion to compel a response to a demand for inspection, copying, testing, or sampling, unless it finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust.”  Finally, the court shall impose a $1,000 sanction if the court finds that “[t]he party, person, or attorney did not respond in good faith to a request for production of documents,” produced the requested documents within seven days before the hearing on the motion to compel production of documents, or failed to confer in a reasonable and good faith attempt to informally resolve the dispute.  (Code Civ. Proc., §2023.050, subd. (a).)

Having reviewed the moving papers, Opposition, and Reply, the Court makes the following findings. Although Defendant served responses to the discovery request, given that these responses were untimely, Defendant has waived all objections and has not sought relief from the waiver. The service of an untimely response does not divest the trial court of its authority to hear and grant a motion to compel responses. (Sinaiko, 148 Cal.App.4th at 405.) Accordingly, the Court orders Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections within 10 days of service of the Court’s order.

The Court also finds that monetary sanctions are mandatory because Defendant unsuccessfully opposed the motion and has not shown substantial justification or other circumstances making imposition of sanctions unjust.

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff Michael Lewis’s Motion to Compel Initial Responses to First Set of Demands for Production of Documents is GRANTED. The Court orders Defendant to serve verified responses, without objections, within 10 days of service of the Court’s order.

The Court also grants Plaintiff’s request for monetary sanctions in the amount of $1,071.76, to be paid by Defendant within 30 days of service of the Court’s order.
