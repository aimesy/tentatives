Basilia Villa Magana v. CJKANTRG PA, LLC

TENTATIVE RULING

 

The Court GRANTS final approval and awards/approves as follows:

 (1) $109,989 for attorney fees to Class Counsel, Blackstone Law, APC; (2) $17,967.61 for attorney costs to Class Counsel; (3) enhancement award of $5,000 to the class representative, Basilia Villa Magana; (4) $22,500 (75% of $30,000 PAGA penalty) to the LWDA; and (5) $4,750 for settlement administration costs to Apex Class Action, LLC.

 

Class Counsel is ordered to lodge a proposed Order consistent with this ruling and a separate judgment containing the class definition, release language, and a statement that no class members opted out of the settlement by __________________, 2026.

 

BACKGROUND

 

Plaintiff Basilia Villa Magana sues her former employer, Defendant CJKANTRG PA, LLC, for alleged wage and hour violations. Defendant is a staffing agency providing staff for produce processing plants, among other types of businesses. Plaintiff seeks to represent a class of Defendant’s current and former non-exempt employees.

            On September 14, 2023, Plaintiff provided written notice to the LWDA, pursuant to California Labor Code Section 2699.3, of the specified provisions of the California Labor Code alleged to have been violated by Defendant.

On October 23, 2023, Plaintiff filed a Class Action Complaint against Defendant alleging causes of action for violations of the California Labor Code for: (1) failure to pay minimum wages, (2) failure to pay overtime wages, (3) failure to provide compliant meal periods and premium payments in lieu thereof, (4) failure to provide compliant rest periods and premiums payments in lieu thereof, (5) failure to timely pay wages during employment, (6) failure to provide accurate wage statements, (7) failure to timely pay wages upon termination, (8) failure to reimburse necessary business expenses, and (9) for violations of California Business & Professions Code Section 17200, et seq. based on the aforementioned California Labor Code violations.

On November 20, 2023, Plaintiff filed a Complaint for Enforcement Action Under the Private Attorneys General Act, Cal. Labor Code §§ 2698 et seq. (“PAGA Complaint”) in the action entitled Basilla Villa Magana v. CJKANTRG PA, LLC, Case No. 23STCV28370, against Defendant (“PAGA Action”). The PAGA Complaint alleges a single cause of action for civil penalties under the Private Attorneys General Act of 2004 pursuant to California Labor Code Section 2698 et seq. (“PAGA”) based on the aforementioned California Labor Code violations.

On October 3, 2024, the Parties participated in a formal mediation conducted by Jonathan D. Andrews, Esq., which ultimately resulted in settlement. The terms of settlement were finalized in the long-form Joint Stipulation of Class Action and PAGA Settlement (“Settlement Agreement”), a copy of which was filed with the Court on June 5, 2025.

On December 10, 2025, after the parties filed further briefing and an Amended Settlement Agreement to address concerns raised by the Court, preliminary approval of the settlement was granted. All references below are to the agreement attached to the Supplemental Declaration of Annabel Blanchard ISO Prelim.

Notice was given to the Class Members as ordered. (See Declaration of Katie Tran (“Tran Decl.”).) Now before the Court is the Motion for Final Approval of the Settlement.

TERMS OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

            The essential terms are as follows:

·       The Gross Settlement Amount (“GSA”) is $330,000, non-reversionary. (¶9.p)

o   Escalator Clause: Defendant has represented that the Class Members worked a total of 3,731 Workweeks during the Class Period. If it is determined by the Settlement Administrator that the total number of Workweeks worked by the Class Members during the Class Period actually exceeds 3,731 by more than 10%, then the Gross Settlement Amount will be increased on a pro rata basis equal to the percentage increase in the number of Workweeks worked by the Class Members above the 10% buffer. For example, if the number of Workweeks increases by 11%, then the Gross Settlement Amount will increase by 1%. Defendant will have the discretion to end the Class Period when Workweeks hit the 10% threshold (i.e., 4,104 workweeks). If Defendant opts to increase the Gross Settlement Amount, the Parties agree that the portion of the Gross Settlement Amount allocated to attorneys’ fees will increase proportionally such that the total amount of attorneys’ fees remains thirty-five percent after the upward adjustment required by the provision is implemented. (¶16)

o   At final approval, the settlement administrator represents that the total number of workweeks worked by Participating Class Members during the Class Period is 3,034. (Tran Decl., ¶16.) Accordingly, the escalator provision was not triggered.  

·       The Net Settlement Amount (“Net”) estimated at preliminary approval ($155,261) is the GSA minus the following:

    • Up to $109,989 (33.33%) for attorneys’ fees (¶12);
    • Up to $25,000 for attorneys’ costs (Ibid.);
    • Up to $5,000 for an Enhancement Payment to the class representative (¶13);
    • Up to $4,750 for costs of settlement administration (¶15); and

·       There is no claim form requirement. (Notice, pg. 1)

·       Individual Settlement Payment Calculation: After Final Approval, the Settlement Administrator will divide the final Net Settlement Amount by the Workweeks of all Settlement Class Members to yield the “Final Workweek Value,” and multiply each Settlement Class Member’s individual Workweeks by the Final Workweek Value to each Settlement Class Member’s final Individual Settlement Share. (¶17.b)

o   PAGA Payments: The Settlement Administrator will divide the PAGA Employee Amount, i.e., 25% of the PAGA Amount, by the Pay Periods of all PAGA Employees to yield the “Pay Period Value,” and multiply each PAGA Employee’s individual Pay Periods by the Pay Period Value to yield each PAGA Employee’s Individual PAGA Payment. o   Tax Allocation: Each Individual Settlement Share will be allocated as follows: ten percent (10%) wages and ninety percent (90%) penalties, interest, and non-wage damages. Each Individual PAGA Payment will be allocated as one hundred percent (100%) penalties. (¶19)

In the event that a Class Notice is re-mailed to a Class Member, the Response Deadline for that Class Member shall be extended fifteen (15) calendar days from the original Response Deadline. (¶9.ii)

ANALYSIS OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT

 

A.     Does a presumption of fairness exist? 

The Court preliminarily found in its Order of December 10, 2025 that the presumption of fairness should be applied.  No facts have come to the Court’s attention that would alter that preliminary conclusion.  Accordingly, the settlement is entitled to a presumption of fairness as set forth in the preliminary approval order.

B.     Is the settlement fair, adequate, and reasonable?

The settlement was preliminarily found to be fair, adequate and reasonable.  Notice has now been given to the Class. 

  Reaction of the class members to the proposed settlement.

Number of Class Members: 87 (Tran Decl., ¶5.)   

Number of notice packets mailed: 87 (Id. at ¶7.)        

Number of undeliverable notices: 10 (Id. at ¶9.)

Number of opt-outs: 0 (Id. at ¶11.)

Number of objections: 0 (Id. at ¶12.)

Number of participating class members: 87 (Id. at ¶14.)

Average individual payment: $1,784.61 (Id. at ¶17.)

Highest individual payment: $13,663.38 (Ibid.)

The Court finds that the notice was given as directed and conforms to due process requirements.  Given the reactions of the Class Members to the proposed settlement and for the reasons set for in the Preliminary Approval order, the settlement is found to be fair, adequate, and reasonable.

 

C.     Attorney Fees and Costs

            Class Counsel requests an award of $109,989 in fees and $17,967.61 in costs. (Memo ISO Final at 20:16-23.) The Settlement Agreement provides for up to $109,989 (33.33%) for attorney fees and $25,000 in costs (¶12).

“Courts recognize two methods for calculating attorney fees in civil class actions: the lodestar/multiplier method and the percentage of recovery method.”  (Wershba v. Apple Computer, Inc. (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 224, 254.)  Here, class counsel request attorney fees using the percentage method, as crosschecked by lodestar. (Memo ISO Final at pp. 13-20.)

In common fund cases, the Court may employ a percentage of the benefit method, as cross-checked against the lodestar. (Laffitte v. Robert Half Int’l, Inc. (2016) 1 Cal.5th 480, 503.) The fee request represents one-third of the gross settlement amount, which is the average generally awarded in class actions.  (See In re Consumer Privacy Cases (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 545, 558, fn. 13 [“Empirical studies show that, regardless whether the percentage method or the lodestar method is used, fee awards in class actions average around one-third of the recovery.”].)

Counsel’s percentage-based fee request is lower than the unadjusted lodestar, and would represent application of a multiplier of approximately 0.57x to reach.

Here, the $109,989 fee request represents a reasonable percentage of the total funds paid by Defendant. Notice of the fee request was provided to class members in the notice packet, and no one objected. (Tran Decl., ¶12, Exhibit A.)

 

            As for costs, Class Counsel is requesting a cost amount of $17,967.61. This is less than the $25,000 cap provided for at preliminary approval, which was disclosed to Class Members in the Notice and not objected to. (Tran Decl., ¶12, Exhibit A.) Counsel represents that he incurred actual costs totaling $11,275.27, which include, but are not limited to: filing fees, service of documents, Case Anywhere, and mediation fees. (Blanchard Decl. ISO Final, ¶30, Exhibit 4.) The costs incurred appear to be reasonable in amount and reasonably necessary to this litigation.

 

Based on the above, the recommendation is to award $109,989 in fees and $17,967.61 in costs.

D.    Incentive Award

The class representative, Basilia Villa Magana, seeks an enhancement payment of $5,000 for her contributions to the action. (Memo ISO Final at 20:26-28.)   

In connection with the final fairness hearing, named Plaintiffs must submit declarations attesting to why they should be entitled to an enhancement award in the proposed amount.  The named Plaintiffs must explain why they “should be compensated for the expense or risk he has incurred in conferring a benefit on other members of the class.”  (Clark v. American Residential Services LLC (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 785, 806.)  Trial courts should not sanction enhancement awards of thousands of dollars with “nothing more than pro forma claims as to ‘countless’ hours expended, ‘potential stigma’ and ‘potential risk.’ Significantly more specificity, in the form of quantification of time and effort expended on the litigation, and in the form of reasoned explanation of financial or other risks incurred by the named plaintiffs, is required in order for the trial court to conclude that an enhancement was ‘necessary to induce [the named plaintiff] to participate in the suit . . . .’”  (Id. at 806-807, italics and ellipsis in original.)

Plaintiff represents that her contributions to this litigation include: conferring with her attorneys about the case, searching for and providing documents, answering questions, providing information regarding Defendant’s practices, and reviewing the settlement. She estimates spending 35-40 hours on the case. (Declaration of Basilia Villa Magana ISO Final, ¶¶4-9.)

Based on the above, as well as the benefits obtained on behalf of the class, the recommendation is to grant the enhancement payment in the amount of $5,000 to Plaintiff.

 

E.     Settlement Administration Costs

            The settlement administrator, Apex Class Action, is requesting $4,750 for the costs of settlement administration. (Tran Decl., ¶20.) This equals the estimated cost of $4,750 provided for in the settlement agreement (¶15) and disclosed to class members in the Notice, to which there were no objections. (Tran Decl., ¶12, Exhibit A.) Based on the above, the recommendation is to award costs in the requested amount of $4,750.
