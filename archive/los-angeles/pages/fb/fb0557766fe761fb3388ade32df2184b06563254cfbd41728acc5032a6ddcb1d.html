On July 22, 2026, Plaintiff filed his First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) against the following defendants: (1) 136 Beverly; (2) 155 Beverly Drive; (3) Colony Holdings; (4) Alexa Group; (5) Ardmore Homes; (6) Wilshire-Beverly No. 2; (7) Berendo Group; (8) Bristol Park; (9) 415 Camden; (10) Chelsea Corners; (11) HMH; (12) Julian M. LEGAL STANDARD

Trial courts may, in furtherance of justice and on any proper terms, allow a party to amend any pleading. (Code Civ. Proc., §473(a)(1); Branick v. Downey Savings & Loan Association (2006) 39 Cal.4th 235, 242.) Trial courts may also, in their discretion and after notice to the adverse party, allow, upon any terms as may be just, an amendment to any pleading or proceeding in other particulars; and may upon like terms allow an answer to be made after the time limited by this code. (Code Civ. Proc. §473(a); Branick, supra, 39 Cal.4th at 242.)  Leave to amend is generally liberally granted, provided there is no statute of limitations concern. (Kolani v. Gluska (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 402, 411.) Additionally, “judicial policy favors resolution of all disputed matters in the same lawsuit.”  (Kittredge Sports Co. v. Superior Court (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1045, 1047.) 

Trial courts may deny plaintiffs leave to amend if there is prejudice to the opposing party, such as delay in trial, loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation. (Branic, supra, at 242.)  

Under California Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324, a motion to amend a pleading before trial must (1) include a copy of the proposed amendment or amended pleading, which must be serially numbered to differentiate it from previous pleadings or amendments; (2) state what allegations in the previous pleading are proposed to be deleted, if any, and where, by page, paragraph and line number, the deleted allegations are located; and (3) state what allegations are proposed to be added to the previous pleading, if any, and where, by page, paragraph, and line number, the additional allegations are located. (Rules of Court, Rule 3.1324(a).) A separate supporting declaration specifying (1) the effect of the amendment; (2) why the amendment is necessary and proper; (3) when the facts giving rise to the amended allegations were discovered; and (4) the reason why the request for amendment was not made earlier must accompany the motion. (Id., Rule 3.1324(b).) 

 

DISCUSSION

 

Plaintiff seeks to further amend the complaint to re-insert two of the Hakim Defendants – Sam and Julia – who were dismissed with prejudice, as defendants. Plaintiff asserts that he would not be adding Hakim Defendants for the same action the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings addressed, but rather for Breach of Fiduciary Duty, Civil Theft, and Declaratory Relief.  Plaintiff contends that these claims were found upon review of Secretary of State Documents, including 2025 documents, when counsel was determining what claims and theories were available to Plaintiff. Plaintiff argues that leave should be granted because (1) unspecified “Defendants”[2] waited over two years after filing of the initial Complaint to file a Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings, disallowing Plaintiff the opportunity to refine his causes of actions; (2) the basis for such actions occurred in 2025; (3) the proposed amendments are based largely on the same facts as the SAC; (4) trial is scheduled for August 23, 2027, allowing ample time for discovery; and (5) unspecified “Defendants” would not be prejudiced by leave to amend.

 

Corporate Defendants argue that: (1) Plaintiff’s attempt to add back in Sam and Julia violates the Court’s July 17, 2025 Order granting the Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings without leave to amend as to the individual defendants; (2) final judgment has been entered as of July 30, 2025, dismissing Sam and Julia, and Plaintiff did not appeal; and (3) Plaintiff’s proposed amendment is intended to bypass the applicable statute of limitations.  

 

Corporate Defendants further argue that Plaintiff has cited no authority that would allow him avoid the bar of the dismissal and to bring Sam and Julia back into the action, and the Corporate Defendants would be severely prejudiced as the amendment would transform this case from an action for access to corporate records to an action for damages upon each Corporate Defendant, leading to a question of whether the case has been assigned to the proper division of the Los Angeles Superior Court. Corporate Defendants also contend that discovery would be transformed into a massive undertaking, and the timeline would be tight considering the five-year statute under Code of Civil Procedure section 583.310 will run on November 16, 2027.

 

In Reply, Plaintiff reiterates that the information underlying the new claims was found in 2025; Goddard v. Security Title Insurance & Guarantee Co. (1939) 14 Cal.2d 47 (“Goddard”) allows the Court to allow previously-unasserted tort claims despite a pleading stage ruling, and Corporate Defendants are not prejudiced as they did not identify any discovery they have not taken, would need to take, or would not have time to take. Plaintiff contends that the Court can bring this case to trial within 16 months by: (1) allowing discovery to proceed in parallel with any pleadings-stage motions defendants might file; (2) condition summary judgment motions on stipulations to extend the time to bring the case to trial; and bifurcate claims against Corporate Defendants, and Sam and Julia.

 

On July 30, 2025, Sam and Julia were dismissed with prejudice, and Hakim Defendants were considered the prevailing party on Plaintiff’s Complaint which contained one cause of action for accounting.

 

The gravamen of Plaintiff’s argument that he is not barred from adding Sam and Julia as defendants due to Goddard. (See Mtn. at 6:17-25; see also Reply 5:20-25.) However, Goddard does not address this situation. Rather, Goddard, supra, 14 Cal.2d 47, 51-53, specifically the section Plaintiff cited, addresses res judicata:

 

[] The major defense, and the only one which requires extended discussion, is that the judgment in the prior action in the federal court is res judicata and therefore a complete bar to the present action. To determine this point it may be desirable first to review some of the principles governing the effect of judgments of dismissal and judgments entered after the sustaining of demurrers without leave to amend.

 

"First, a final judgment, rendered upon the merits by a court having jurisdiction of the cause, is conclusive of the rights of the parties and those in privity with them, and is a complete bar to a new suit between them on the same cause of action. This is the general doctrine of res judicata. (See 2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.], sec. 627, p. 1321 et seq.; von Moschzisker, Res Judicata, 38 Yale L. J. 299;  Price v. Sixth Dist. Agricultural Assn., 201 Cal. 502 [258 Pac. 387].) Second, a judgment not rendered on the merits does not operate as a bar. ( Campanella v. Campanella, 204 Cal. 515 [269 Pac. 433]; 2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.], sec. 723, p. 1530; 34 C. J. 774, sec. 1193.) Third, a judgment based upon the sustaining of a special demurrer for technical or formal defects is clearly not on the merits and is not a bar to the filing of a new action. (2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.], sec. 745, p. 1569; 34 C. J. 797, sec. 1219.)  Fourth, judgments based upon sustaining a general demurrer have given rise to an apparent conflict of decision, and careful distinctions must be drawn between the cases. (See von Moschzisker, Res Judicata, 38 Yale L. J. 299, 318 et seq.; Clark, Code Pleading, p. 367.) A judgment given after the sustaining of a general demurrer on a ground of substance, for example, that an absolute defense is disclosed by the allegations of the complaint, may be deemed a judgment on the merits, and conclusive in a subsequent suit; and the same is true where the demurrer sets up the failure of the facts alleged to establish a cause of action, and the same facts are pleaded in the second action. (2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.] sec. 746, p. 1571; Erganian v. Brightman, 13 Cal. App. (2d) 696 [57 Pac. (2d) 971]; von Moschzisker, Res Judicata, 38 Yale L. J. 299, 318, and cases cited.)  But even a judgment on general demurrer may not be on the merits, for the defects set up may be technical or formal, and the plaintiff may in such case by a different pleading eliminate them or correct the omissions and allege facts constituting a good cause of action, in proper form. Where such a new and sufficient complaint is filed, the prior judgment on demurrer will not be a bar. ( Newhall v. Hatch, 134 Cal. 269 [66 Pac. 266, 55 L. R. A. 673]; Terry v. Hammonds, 47 Cal. 32; Dyment v. Board of Medical Examiners, 93 Cal. App. 65 [268 Pac. 1073]; 2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.], secs. 747, 748; von Moschzisker, Res Judicata, 38 Yale L. J. 299, 318.) This result has frequently been reached where the failure of the first complaint was in misconceiving the remedy, or framing the complaint on the wrong form of action. (2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.], secs. 734, 735; von Moschzisker, Res Judicata, 38 Yale L. J. 299, 318Fifth, an ordinary judgment of dismissal, for example, for want of prosecution, has the same effect as a nonsuit, that is, is considered not on the merits and not a bar to another suit on the same cause of action, unless the record shows that there was an actual determination on the merits. ( Everts v. Blaschko, 17 Cal. App. (2d) 188 [61 Pac. (2d) 776]; Matteson v. Klump, 100 Cal. App. 64 [279 Pac. 669]; Campanella v. Campanella, 204 Cal. 515 [269 Pac. 433]; 2 Freeman on Judgments, [5th ed.], sec. 753, p. 1582.)

 

Goddard does not address whether, once a defendant has been dismissed with prejudice from an action, the plaintiff may seek to bring that defendant back into the action.  And Plaintiff has offered not authority to support his argument supporting a right to do so.

Further, Plaintiff has waited approximately four years to assert these claims, which would drastically alter the nature of the case. Plaintiff, while claiming that his counsel found these claims in 2025, engaged counsel of record, Jeff A. Manon September 04, 2024, two years ago and records could have been reviewed earlier in the action. Plaintiff has also conceded that “the proposed amendments are based largely on the same set of facts as those of the operative Complaint[,]” indicating that Plaintiff could have made the claim at an earlier point during the litigation. (See Mtn. at 9:20-21.) This delay and the change in the nature of the action would prejudice Corporate Defendants who expected trial on August 23, 2027. Trial courts may deny the plaintiff’s leave to amend if there is prejudice to the opposing party, such as delay in trial, loss of critical evidence, or added costs of preparation. (Branic, supra, at 242; see also Hirsa v. Superior Court (1981) 118 Cal.App.3d 486, 490.)  

Accordingly, Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to Amend is denied.

 

Conclusion¿

 

Plaintiff Michael Hakim’s Motion for Leave to Amend to File Third Amended Complaint is denied.

TENTATIVE RULING

HEARING DATE:                 07/21/2026

CASE NUMBER:                   25STCV19132

CASE NAME:                        LEMUEL E HAMPTON, et al. v. FCA US LLC, et al.

MOVING PARTY:                 Defendant FCA US LLC

OPPOSING PARTY:             Plaintiffs Lemuel E Hampton and Linda Harrison

PROCEEDING:                      Demurrer with Motion to Strike

RULING SUMMARY:          FCA US LLC’s Demurrer is sustained.  FCA US LLC’s Motion to Strike is moot.  The action is dismissed as to FCA. 

                                                                                                                                               

Background

 

On June 30, 2025, Plaintiffs Lemuel E. Hampton (“Hampton”) and Linda Harrison (“Harrison” and collectively “Plaintiffs”) filed their Complaint against Defendants FCA US LLC (“FCA”), and Los Angeles Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram (“LA CDJR”), alleging causes of action for (1) Violation of Subdivision (d) of Civ. Code § 1793.2 against FCA; (2) Violation of Subdivision (b) of Civ. Code § 1793.2 against FCA; (3) Violation of Subdivision (a)(3) of Civ. Code § 1793.2 against FCA; (4) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability (Civ. Code §§ 1791.1, 1794, 1795.5) against FCA; (5) Negligent Repair against LA CDJR; and (6) Fraudulent Inducement Concealment against FCA.

 

Plaintiffs allege that on or about January 17, 2019 they entered into a warranty contract with FCA regarding a certified pre-owned 2018 Dodge Challenger with vehicle identification number 2C3CDZFJ6JH269370 (the “Subject Vehicle”), manufactured or distributed by FCA; the warranty contract included various warranties; defects and nonconformities to warranty manifested themselves within the applicable express warranty period, including, but not limited to, an engine defect; FCA had a duty to promptly offer to repurchase or replace the Subject Vehicle at the time it failed to conform the Subject Vehicle to the terms of the express warranty after a reasonable number of repair attempts; and FCA did not do so. Regarding LA CDJR, Plaintiffs allege negligent repair of the Subject Vehicle.

 

On September 25, 2025, FCA filed a Demurrer with Motion to Strike as to the Complaint.  The Demurrer was sustained without leave to amend in part as to the First, Second, and Third Causes of Action and sustained with leave to amend in part as to the Fourth and Sixth Causes of Action; and the Motion to Strike was deemed moot.

 

On March 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed his First Amended Complaint (“FAC”), alleging three causes of action for (1) Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability (Civ. Code §§ 1791.1, 1794, 1795.5) against FCA; (2) Negligent Repair against LA CDJR; (3) Fraudulent Inducement Concealment against FCA. The FAC also includes Plaintiffs’ repair history with the Subject Vehicle.

 

On April 15, 2026, FCA filed this Demurrer with Motion to Strike.  On May 19, 2026, LA CDJR filed its Answer.  On July 08, 2026, Plaintiff filed his Opposition.  On July 14, 2026, FCA filed its Reply.

 

MEET AND CONFER

Before filing a demurrer or motion to strike, the moving party is required to meet and confer with the party who filed the pleading demurred to, in person or telephonically, to determine whether an agreement can be reached through a filing of an amended pleading that would resolve the objections to be raised in the demurrer. (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 430.41, 435.5.)¿¿

This requirement has been met. (See Decl. of Steven D. Park to Demurrer and Motion to Strike.)

LEGAL STANDARD

 

A.    Demurrer

 

A demurrer is an objection to a pleading, the grounds for which are apparent from either the face of the complaint or a matter of which the court may take judicial notice. (Code Civ. Proc. § 430.30(a); see also Blank v. Kirwan (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)¿“To survive a demurrer, the complaint need only allege facts sufficient to state a cause of action; each evidentiary fact that might eventually form part of the plaintiff’s proof need not be alleged.”¿(C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School Dist. (2012) 53 Cal.4th 861, 872.)¿For the purpose of testing the sufficiency of the cause of action, the demurrer admits the truth of all material facts properly pleaded.¿

(Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 966-967.)¿A demurrer “does not admit contentions, deductions or conclusions of fact or law.”¿(Daar v. Yellow Cab Co. (1967) 67 Cal.2d 695, 713.)¿¿¿

 

B.    Motion to Strike

 

Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion to strike the whole or any part thereof. (Code Civ. Proc. § 435(b)(1); Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 3.1322(b).) The court may, upon a motion or at any time in its discretion and upon terms it deems proper: (1) strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading; or (2) strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of California, a court rule, or an order of the court. (Code Civ. Proc. § 436(a)-(b); Stafford v. Shultz (1954) 42 Cal.2d 767, 782 [“Matter in a pleading which is not essential to the claim is surplusage; probative facts are surplusage and may be stricken out or disregarded”].)¿¿¿¿¿

 

C.    Leave to Amend

 

“Where the defect raised by a motion to strike or by demurrer is reasonably capable of cure, leave to amend is routinely and liberally granted to give the plaintiff a chance to cure the defect in question.” (CLD Construction, Inc. v. City of San Ramon (2004) 120 Cal.App.4th 1141, 1146.) “A trial court does not abuse its discretion when it sustains a demurrer without¿leave to amend¿if either (a) the facts and the nature of the claims are clear and no liability exists, or (b) it is probable from the nature of the defects and previous unsuccessful attempts to plead that the plaintiff cannot state a claim.” (Cantu v. Resolution Trust Corp.¿(1992)¿4 Cal.App.4th 857, 889.)¿¿ The burden is on the complainant to show the Court that a pleading can be amended successfully. (Goodman v. Kennedy (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 348.)¿

 

DISCUSSION

 

A.    Demurrer

 

FCA demurs to the First Cause of Action (Breach of the Implied Warranty of Merchantability (Civ. Code §§ 1791.1, 1794, 1795.5) and the Third Cause of Action (Fraudulent Inducement Concealment).

 

1.     First Cause of Action (Beach of Implied Warranty of Merchantability) is Time-Barred.

 

FCA argues that Plaintiffs’ implied warranty claim expired after three months under Civ. Code section 1795.5(c) and, since Plaintiffs purchased the Subject Vehicle on January 17, 2019, the implied warranty at issue expired on April 17, 2019.

 

Plaintiffs argue that the defects for an implied warranty claim do not need to manifest within three months as the statute discusses that the duration of the implied warranty of merchantability shall be coextensive in duration with an express warranty which accompanies the consumer goods and, here, the Certified Pre-Owned (“CPO”) warranty provides powertrain coverage for seven years. Plaintiffs cite to Mexia v. Rinker Boat Co. (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1297 (“Mexia”) to support their argument.

 

In Reply, FCA argues that Mexia and similar cases are now foreclosed by Civil Code section 1795.5(c), and that courts hold that this cap on limitations for used goods is not subject to tolling, delayed discovery or equitable extensions. FCA reiterates that, under Civil Code section¿1795.5(c), the implied warranty expired no later than April 17, 2019.

 

*********Civil Code section 1795.5(c) provides:

 

The duration of the implied warranty of merchantability and where present the implied warranty of fitness with respect to used consumer goods sold in this state, where the sale is accompanied by an express warranty, shall be coextensive in duration with an express warranty which accompanies the consumer goods, provided the duration of the express warranty is reasonable, but in no event shall such implied warranties have a duration of less than 30 days nor more than three months following the sale of used consumer goods to a retail buyer. Where no duration for an express warranty is stated with respect to such goods, or parts thereof, the duration of the implied warranties shall be the maximum period prescribed above.

 

In Mexia, the appellate court held that the duration provision of Civil Code section 1791.1(c) did not limit when a defect must be discovered for purposes of the implied warranty, but instead, limited the period during which the implied warranty existed: “[T]he plain language of the statute, particularly in light of the consumer protection policies supporting the Song-Beverly Act, make clear that the statute merely creates a limited, prospective duration for the implied warranty of merchantability; it does not create a deadline for discovering latent defects or for giving notice to the seller.” (Id. at 1301.)“The duration provision provides, in essence, that the duration of the implied warranty of merchantability shall be the same as the duration of any reasonable express warranty that accompanies the product, but in no event shorter than 60 days or longer than one year. (Civ. Code § 1791.1(c).) There is nothing that suggests a requirement that the purchaser discover and report to the seller a latent defect within that time period.” (Mexia, supra, 174 Cal.App.4th at 1310.) “The implied warranty of merchantability may be breached by a latent defect undiscoverable at the time of sale. . . . Indeed, ‘[u]ndisclosed latent defects … are the very evil that the implied warranty of merchantability was designed to remedy.’ . . . In the case of a latent defect, a product is rendered unmerchantable, and the warranty of merchantability is breached, by the existence of the unseen defect, not by its subsequent discovery.” (Id. at 1304-1305.)

 

Mexia concerned an argument that conflated the duration of the implied warranty (one year), with the statute of limitations (four years). Mexia held that a latent defect that existed at the time of sale could constitute a breach of the implied warranty because the breach (of unmerchantability) occurred within one year, but if discovered outside of the year could still form the basis of a claim, if brought within the four-year limitation.

 

In this action, the issue is with a used CPO Subject Vehicle. This turns the focus to the section of Civil Code section 1795.5(c), which reads: “. . .but in no event shall such implied warranties have a duration of less than 30 days nor more than three months following the sale of used consumer goods to a retail buyer. . . .”  As FCA correctly points out, the duration of the implied warranty expired no later than April 17, 2019. The statute of limitations is four years. Thus, the question for this Court is: Can Plaintiffs bring a claim for breach of implied warranty when the implied warranty expired no later than April 17, 2019; the statute of limitations for a breach of implied warranty is four years, and Plaintiffs filed their Complaint on June 30, 2025?  Under both Mexia and Civil Code section1795.5(c), the answer is “no ,“as four years from April 17, 2019 is April 17, 2023. Even if Plaintiffs discovered the alleged latent defect that existed at the time of sale could constitute a breach of the implied warranty, they did not bring this claim until over two years past the four-year statute of limitations period.

 

This deficiency cannot be rectified by amendment due to the application of the statute of limitations.

 

2.     Third Cause of Action (Fraudulent Inducement Concealment) is Time-Barred.

 

FCA asserts that tolling doctrines cannot apply to Plaintiffs’ fraudulent concealment claim as courts hold that tolling doctrines require specific facts showing that a defendant induced Plaintiffs to delay suit or concealed facts that could not have been discovered earlier despite reasonable diligence. FCA argues that Plaintiffs’ assertions regarding the Subject Vehicle are conclusory and insufficient to toll the statute of limitations or plead a viable fraud theory.

 

Plaintiffs contend that FCA’s demurrer to the Third Cause of Action relying on the statute of limitations (1) is based on the premise that the cause of action accrued and ran on the date Plaintiff’s purchased the Subject Vehicle, and (2) fails because the fraudulent inducement concealment claim does not run from the date of sale.

 

In Reply, FCA argues that Plaintiffs’ fraud claim is time-barred because Plaintiff does not plead facts that show the time and manner of discovery, and circumstances demonstrating inability to have discovered the fraud earlier despite reasonable diligence. FCA highlights that Plaintiffs’ Complaint concedes that at least two repair attempts occurred by July of 2021, putting Plaintiffs on notice.

 

To invoke the delayed discovery rule, Plaintiffs must specifically allege “(1) the time and manner of discovery” and (2) “the inability to have made earlier discovery despite reasonable diligence.” (Saliter v. Pierce Bros. Mortuaries (1978) 81 Cal.App.3d 292, 297.) Moreover, the doctrine of fraudulent concealment applies to toll the Third Cause of Action only if there are facts showing that FCA prevented Plaintiff from discovering the existence of the alleged defect, the E-Torque Defect, at an earlier time. (Aryeh v. Canon Business Solutions, Inc.¿(2013) 55 Cal.4th 1185, 1192 [doctrine of fraudulent concealment tolls limitations where defendant through deceptive conduct caused claim to grow stale].)

 

Plaintiffs have alleged that the Subject Vehicle was brought in for repairs around: (1) June 7, 2021; (2) July 20, 2021; (3) June 9, 2022; (4) June 27, 2022; (5) August 29, 2022; (6) January 29, 2025. (See FAC ¶¶ 18-23.) While neither party provides discussion on how many repair attempts should be made to put a reasonable person on notice, the court in Dhital v. Nissan North America, Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 828 (“Dhital”) found three occasions for the same type of problem (i.e., transmission) to be reasonable. (Id. at 833; see also 2018 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 208129/ 2018 WL 6460196 at 5 [persuasive authority; “A reasonable person would be on inquiry notice when the same repair for the same problem is ineffective three times in a row.”) As alleged, the issues with the Subject Vehicle on or around June 7, 2021; July 20, 2021; and June 27, 2022 include issues where the Subject Vehicle made noises while idling, shifting, or accelerating which the Court views as the same problem. (See FAC at ¶¶ 18-19, 21.) The June 9, 2022 repair was substantially different as it involved radiator replacement. (See id. at 20.) Therefore, whether the discovery rule applies or not, Plaintiffs were put on notice of the Subject Vehicle’s defects on at least June 27, 2022 and the delayed discovery rule would not apply past that date.

 

The statute of limitations for fraud is three years. (See Code Civ. Proc. § 338(d).) Three years from when Plaintiffs were put on notice is June 27, 2025 [June 27, 2022 + 3 years]. Plaintiffs did not file their Complaint until June 30, 2025. Accordingly, Plaintiffs Third Cause of Action is barred by the three-year statute of limitations under Code of Civil Procedure section 338(d).

 

This deficiency cannot be rectified by amendment due to the application of the statute of limitations.

 

Accordingly, FCA’s Demurrer is sustained.

 

B.    Motion to Strike

 

As the Court has sustained the Demurrer to both causes of action directed to FCA, the FAC contains no cause of action against FCA.

 

The Motion to Strike is moot.

 

Conclusion

 

FCA US LLC’s Demurrer is sustained.  FCA US LLC’s Motion to Strike is moot.  The action is ordered dismissed as against FCA.
