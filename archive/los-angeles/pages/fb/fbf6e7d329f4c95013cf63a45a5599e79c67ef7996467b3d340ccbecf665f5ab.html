DEPARTMENT F43 LAW AND MOTION RULINGS


Case Number: 23CHCV00683    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-06-26

Case # 23CHCV00683, Thompson, et al. v. White House Recovery and Detox, LLC, et al.

Trial Date: 01-11-27

 

MOTION TO COMPEL DEPOSITION OF PERSON MOST KNOWLEDGEABLE

 

MOVING PARTIES:  Plaintiffs Cecilia Thompson and Brett Thompson, individually and as successors-in-interest to the Estate of Colin Thompson

RESPONDING PARTY:  Defendant Vanity Wellness Center, Inc.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order compelling defendant Vanity Wellness Center, Incorporated to attend and testify at a deposition and to impose $3,470 in monetary sanctions against defendant Vanity Wellness.

 

RULING:  Motion is moot.  The court awards Plaintiffs $1,985 in monetary sanctions.

 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On March 9, 2023, plaintiffs Cecilia Thompson and Brett Thompson (Plaintiffs) filed this wrongful death and survival action.  Plaintiff filed a First Amended Complaint on October 13, 2023, asserting claims against defendants White House Recovery and Detox, LLC, White House Recovery, LLC, Vanity Wellness Center, Incorporated (Vanity), and Kyle Perez.  Plaintiffs allege their deceased son, Colin Thompson, was in the care of defendant White House Recovery, LLC and other defendants when he overdosed and passed away.  Plaintiffs assert dependent adult abuse, negligence, wrongful death, and survival action.

 

Vanity filed and served an answer to the First Amended Complaint on January 5, 2024.  On January 8, 2024, Vanity filed a cross-complaint against its co-defendants seeking indemnity and declaratory relief.

 

On May 28, 2025, Plaintiffs electronically served a deposition notice on defendant Vanity to depose Vanity’s person most knowledgeable (PMK) on June 23, 2025 at 10:00 a.m.  (Declaration of Barret T. Alexander ¶ 6, Exh. C at p. 9.)  Vanity served objections on June 18, 2025.  (Alexander Decl. ¶ 7, Exh. D.)  The next day, Plaintiffs’ counsel agreed to re-notice the deposition if Vanity provided alternate dates by close of business that day.  (Id. ¶ 8, Exh. E at p. 1.)  Defense counsel did not respond before the noticed deposition, Vanity failed to appear at the deposition, and Plaintiffs took a non-appearance on the record.  (Id. ¶ 9, Exh. F.)  On June 23, 2025 at 1:07 p.m., the day of the deposition, defense counsel stated he was working on alternate deposition dates, would send them to plaintiffs’ counsel soon, and asked if the deposition could be conducted by Zoom.  (Id. ¶ 8, Exh. E at p. 1.)  Three days later, plaintiffs’ counsel emailed defense counsel asking for alternate dates.  (Id., Exh. G at p. 1.)

 

On August 15, 2025, Plaintiffs served a second PMK deposition notice on Vanity for August 29, 2025 at 10:00 a.m.  (Alexander Decl. ¶ 11, Exh. H.)  On August 25, 2025, defense counsel identified a PMK and stated he was available on September 16, 2025 at 10:30 a.m. by Zoom.  (Id., Exh. I at p. 1.)

 

On August 27, 2025, Plaintiffs served a third PMK deposition notice on Vanity for September 16, 2025 at 10:00 a.m.  (Alexander Decl., Exh. J.)  The day before the deposition, defense counsel sent an email, representing the need to reschedule the deposition, not having many answers, the inability to reach his client for several days, and having “no idea what’s going on.”  (Id., Exh. K at p. 1.)  Plaintiffs took a certificate of non-appearance at the September 16, 2025, deposition.  (Id. ¶ 16, Exh. 15.)

 

On September 23, 2025, Plaintiffs filed the instant motion to compel Vanity to attend and testify at a deposition and to pay $3,470 in monetary sanctions.  The hearing was originally scheduled for December 30, 2025.

 

The court record demonstrates Plaintiffs continued the instant motion hearing four times.[1]

 

Vanity filed an opposition on May 5, 2026.  In opposition, Vanity asserts the motion is moot because the first volume of the PMK deposition was completed on January 12, 2026.  The parties agreed to meet and confer about the second PMK deposition, but Plaintiffs unilaterally scheduled the second PMK deposition to April 22, 2026.  Vanity objected to the date and provided alternate dates:  May 28, 2026, or June 3, 4, and 5, 2026.  To date, Plaintiffs have not re-noticed the second PMK deposition.

 

No reply has been filed.

 

 

MEET AND CONFER

 

A motion to compel a deposition must include either a meet and confer declaration or, “when the deponent fails to attend the deposition and produce the documents, electronically stored information, or things described in the deposition notice, . . . a declaration stating that the petitioner has contacted the deponent to inquire about the nonappearance.”  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2025.450, subd. (b), 2016.040.)  In Department F43, meet and confer means in person or via phone.  A letter or email does not suffice.  (Department F43 Courtroom Information, p. 2.)

 

It appears from the moving papers that the parties did not directly meet and confer until after Plaintiffs filed the instant motion.  At most, counsel exchanged emails and documents.  (Alexander Decl. ¶¶ 6-15.)  If there was a direct meet and confer after Plaintiffs filed their motion, it appears the parties were able to agree (1) the PMK deposition would consists of two volumes; (2) volume one would and did occur on January 12, 2026; and (3) counsel would confer about scheduling volume two.  (Declaration of Arthur Villegas ¶ 2.)  However, the opposition does not state whether a direct meet and confer has occurred since the first deposition, and no reply was filed providing further information about Plaintiffs’ meet and confer efforts.

 

Therefore, the court finds that the “meet and confer” requirement was not met before the motion was filed.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Compelling the Deposition of a Person Most Knowledgeable Deposition

 

An oral deposition shall be scheduled for a date at least 10 days after service of the deposition notice.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.270, subd. (a).)  A properly served deposition notice is effective to require a party or party-affiliated deponent to attend and to testify, as well as to produce documents for inspection and copying.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.280, subd. (a).)

 

A party may depose an entity such as a corporation, partnership, or governmental agency by examining an agent or officer designated to testify on the entity’s behalf.  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.010.)  If the deposition notice is served on an entity, the notice must describe the matters on which questions will be asked must be described “with reasonable particularity.”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.230.)  The entity is under a duty to designate and produce the officers, directors, managing agents or employees “most qualified” to testify on its behalf.  (Ibid.)  “The person or persons designated by the entity must testify to the extent of any information known or reasonably available to the deponent [entity].”  (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.230.)  Further, when a request for documents is made, the witness or someone in authority “is expected to make an inquiry of everyone who might be holding responsive documents or everyone who knows where such documents might be held.”  (Maldonado v. Superior Court (2002) 94 Cal.App.4th 1390, 1396.)

 

“If, after service of a deposition notice, a party . . . without having served a valid objection under Section 2025.410, fails to appear for examination, or to proceed with it, or to produce for inspection any document . . . described in the deposition notice, the party giving notice may move for an order compelling deponent’s attendance and testimony, and the production . . . of any document . . . described in the deposition notice.”¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subd. (a).)¿ The motion must set forth both facts showing good cause justifying the demand for any documents and a meet and confer declaration.¿ (Code Civ. Proc., § 2025.450, subds. (b)(1)-(2).)

 

As stated above, the moving papers do not indicate the “meet and confer” requirement was met before this motion was filed.

 

Moreover, it appears counsel was able to complete volume one of the PMK deposition on January 12, 2026, and the parties agreed to meet and confer to schedule volume two.  Plaintiffs provide no insight into whether “meet and confer” attempts were made before Plaintiffs unilaterally noticed volume two for April 22, 2026.  (Villegas Decl. ¶ 2.)  Nor do Plaintiffs oppose Vanity’s claims that no such meet and confer occurred and that Vanity provided four alternative dates.  (Id. ¶ 3.)

 

Because the parties were able to schedule and complete the first volume of the PMK deposition before the hearing on this motion and the parties agreed to meet and confer further about scheduling the second volume, Plaintiffs’ motion is moot.

 

Although the motion is moot, the court still awards Plaintiffs sanctions because Plaintiffs were forced to file this motion in order for Vanity to submit to an agreed upon deposition date.  (See Masimo Corp. v. Vanderpool Law Firm, Inc. (2024) 101 Cal.App.5th 902, 909 fn. 9 [“[F]orcing a party to resort to the court to get discovery is . . . sanctionable behavior.”].)

 

 

            B.  Sanctions

 

Plaintiffs asks the court to impose $3,470 in monetary sanction against defendant Vanity.  (Alexander Decl. ¶ 22.)

 

The court may impose sanctions against a “party-affiliated” deponent who fails to appear or produce the request materials requested in the deposition notice.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2025.450, subds. (a), (g)(1), 2025.480, subd. (j).)  The court must impose monetary sanctions “against the deponent or the party with whom the deponent is affiliated, unless the court finds that the one subject to the sanction acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances make the imposition of the sanction unjust” or the prevailing party failed to make reasonable efforts to resolve the issues.  (Code Civ. Proc., §§ 2025.450, subd. (g)(1), 2023.030, subd. (a).)

 

Plaintiffs’ counsel charges $550 per hour.  (Alexander Decl. ¶ 22.)  The request includes:  (1) 4.0 hours preparing the motion—$2,200; (2) 1.0 reviewing an opposition—$550; (3) 1.0 hour replying—$550; and (4) a $60 filing fee.  The request totals $3,360 rather than $3,470.

 

The court finds the hourly rate is reasonable but the time requested is excessive because no reply was filed and Plaintiffs have failed to demonstrate the “meet and confer” requirement is met.

 

Therefore, the court awards Plaintiffs monetary sanctions in the reduced amount of $1,985:  (1) 2.5 hours preparing the motion—$1,375; (2) 1.0 reviewing an opposition—$550; and (3) a $60 filing fee.

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Motion is moot.

 

The court awards Plaintiffs $1,985 in monetary sanctions.  Defendant Vanity Wellness Center, Inc. is ordered to pay Plaintiffs’ counsel within thirty (30) days of this order.

 

Plaintiffs to give notice.


[1]           Plaintiffs continued the hearing one final time on May 6, 2026, the day after Vanity filed its opposition to the instant motion.


Case Number: 24CHCV01586    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-06-26

Case # 24CHCV01586, Moses v. Ross Dress for Less, et al.

Trial Date: 07-24-28

 

APPLICATION TO APPEAR PRO HAC VICE

 

MOVING PARTY:  Defendant/Cross-Complainant Brixmor Property Owner II, LLC

RESPONDING PARTY:  No responses were filed.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order approving attorney Daniel R. Sarther’s application to appear as counsel pro hac vice with attorney Jack. C. Hsu on behalf of defendant/cross-complainant Brixmor Property Owner II, LLC.

 

RULING:  Motion is continued for further arguments at the hearing on the application.

 

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On April 29, 2024, plaintiff Samantha Moses filed this personal injury action against several defendants, including Brixmor Property Owner II, LLC (Brixmor).  Plaintiff asserts a single premises liability claim and alleges on June 20, 2022, defendants negligently created a dangerous condition that caused her to suffer injuries.  Defendant Brixmor filed and served an answer on December 10, 2024, and filed and served a cross-complaint for breach of contract, contribution, indemnity, and declaratory relief on August 11, 2025.

 

On April 17, 2025, the court granted Brixmor’s application for attorney Panagiota N. Fortsas to appear as counsel pro hac vice with counsel of record Jack C. Hsu.

 

On June 1, 2026, defendant Brixmor filed another application for attorney Daniel R. Sarther to appear in this action pro hac vice.

 

No opposition or other responses were filed.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Appearing As Counsel Pro Hac Vice

 

Any attorney licensed to practice before the bar of any United States court or the highest court in any state, and who has been retained to appear in a particular cause pending in a California court, but who is not a licensee of the California State Bar may apply to appear pro hac vice in California by submitting a written application and mailing notice to all interested parties, as well as notice and a $50.00 application fee to the State Bar Association in San Francisco.¿ (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(a), (c), (e).)¿ An applicant may not reside or work in California, and may not perform regular or substantial business, professional, or other activities here. ¿(Id., rule 9.40(a).)

 

The application must state:

 

(1) The applicant’s residence and office address;

(2) The courts to which the applicant has been admitted to practice and the dates of admission;

(3) That the applicant is a licensee in good standing in those courts;

(4) That the applicant is not currently suspended or disbarred in any court;

(5) The title of each court and cause in which the applicant has filed an application to appear as counsel pro hac vice in this state in the preceding two years, the date of each application, and whether or not it was granted; and

(6) The name, address, and telephone number of the active licensee of the State Bar of California who is attorney of record.

 

(Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.40(d).)

 

Defendant Brixmor submits the verified application of attorney Daniel R. Sarther.  (Application of Daniel R. Sarther.)  Sarther is licensed to practice law in the State of Illinois and the State of Washington, is in good standing in both jurisdictions, and is not currently suspended or disbarred.  (Id. ¶¶ 2-4.)  Sarther is not a resident of the State California and is not regularly engaged in the practice of law in California.  (Id. ¶ 1.)

 

In the preceding two years, Sarther has applied to appear pro hac vice in seven matters and has been approved for five of those matters. (Sarther App. ¶ 5(a)-(e) [Los Angeles Superior Court, Orange County Superior Court, and San Joaquin Superior Court].)  These applications were approved on August 24, 2023, July 11, 2024, August 16, 2024, October 17, 2024, and January 20, 2026.

 

Sarther has two pending applications.  (Sarther App. ¶ 5(f)-(g) [Andrew Ilasa v. Brixmor, LLC, et al., Yolo County Superior Court, Case No. CV2026-0513 and Selena Quintana v. Vertex Builders, LLC, et al., Los Angeles Superior Court, Case No. 25PSCV03384].)  In the Yolo County matter, the court granted the application.  (6/09/26 Minute Order - CV2026-0513.)  In the Los Angeles County matter, the court expressed concern that attorney Sarther “has too many” applications in the last two years and requested a supplemental declaration on his specialized knowledge.  (6/22/26 Minute Order - 25PSCV03384.)  This court shares the same concern.

 

“Absent special circumstances, repeated appearances by any person under this rule is a cause for denial of an application.”  (California Rule of Court, rule 9.40(b).)

 

The court expresses some apprehension concerning attorney Sarther’s application due to the now six repeated appearances in California state courts in the past 2-3 years.  Both Sarther’s application and attorney Jack C. Hsu’s supporting declaration are silent concerning any special circumstances which justify the repeated appearances without being licensed to practice in California.

 

Brixmor’s counsel of record, Jack C. Hsu, is the California attorney whose contact information has been provided.  (Sarther Appl. ¶ 7;  Declaration of Jack C. Hsu.)  Sarther’s application, Hsu’s declaration, and the proposed order were served on opposing counsel and the State Bar of California.  (Hsu Decl. at pp. 10-12 [Proof of Service and Service List].)  And the application fee was paid on June 1, 2026.  (Application at p. 14.)

 

Although counsel have complied with the requirements of California Rule of Court, rule 9.40(c)-(e), the court seeks further clarification concerning attorney Sarther’s repeated appearances and any “special circumstances” justifying the repeat appearances.

 

Therefore, the court continues its ruling on the application to allow further oral arguments.  Attorney Sarther should be prepared to discuss any “special circumstances” which would justify the court allowing him to practice law for a seventh time in California in two years without a California law license.

 

The court will issue a ruling thereafter.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Attorney Daniel R. Sarther’s application to appear as counsel pro hac vice is continued for further oral arguments.

 

Defendant/Cross-Complainant Brixmor Property Owner II, LLC to give notice.


Case Number: 25CHCP00438    Hearing Date: July 6, 2026    Dept: F43

Dept. F43

Hearing Date: 07-06-26

Case # 25CHCP00438, Quality Loan Service Corp. v. All Claimants to Surplus Funds

Trial Date: N/A

 

MOTION FOR AN ORDER RELEASING SURPLUS FUNDS

 

MOVING PARTY:  David K. Gottlieb, in his capacity as the Chapter 7 Trustee of the Bankruptcy Estate of Lisa Hines

RESPONDING PARTY:  No opposition was filed.

 

RELIEF REQUESTED

Order releasing surplus funds in the amount of $315,897.82 to bankruptcy trustee Gottlieb.

 

RULING:  Motion is granted.

 

SUMMARY OF ACTION

 

On October 28, 2022, the LRD Done by the Light of Day Trust/The Dunn Clan of Lisa Robin Estate Trust (Dunn Clan Trust) was created.  The certificate of trust was recorded on January 23, 2025, identifying the trust as a living irrevocable trust and naming claimant Lisa Hines as a trustee.  (Petition, Exh. C.)

 

On March 30, 2006, Lisa Hines executed a Deed of Trust to secure a loan for real property located at 15606 Simonds St., Granada Hills, California 91344-4344 (the Property).  (Ex Parte RJN, Exh. B at p. 2 [stating the first Deed of Trust was recorded on April 6, 2006];  Ex Parte RJN, Exh. C: Deed of Trust [listing World Savings Bank as the lender, Lisa Hines as the borrower, and the recording date as April 6, 2006].)  Ms. Hines later transferred ownership of the Property to the Dunn Clan Trust by grant deed.  (Ex Parte RJN, Exh. A: Grant Deed [recorded on January 21, 2025].)

 

At some point, claimant Hines defaulted on loan payments and did not cure the default.  On April 29, 2025, the Property was sold at a non-judicial foreclosure sale for $680,000.  (4/30/26 Request for Judicial Notice, Exh. 1 at pp. 2, 25 [conveying ownership to DK Consolidated Investments, LLC].)  A Deed Upon Sale was executed on May 15, 2025.  (Ex Parte, Exh. B.)

 

On May 30, 2025, Ms. Hines filed a voluntary petition under Chapter 7 of Title 11 of the United States Code, initiating In re Lisa Hines, Bankruptcy Case No. 1:25-bk-10950-MB.  (4/30/26 Substitution of Bankruptcy Trustee, Exh. 1.)

 

On November 24, 2025, petitioner Quality Loan Service Corp. (Petitioner) filed a Petition and Declaration Regarding Unresolved Claims and Deposit of Undistributed Surplus Proceeds of Trustee’s Sale (Petition).  The gross amount of the surplus funds to be deposited was $315,897.82, not including Petitioner’s $5,419.66 in trustee sale costs and expenses and requested attorney fees and costs.  (Petition, at p. 3, ¶ 16(h).)  On December 11, 2025, the court (Hon. Gary Micon) issued an order to deposit the surplus funds.

 

On January 8, 2026, Petitioner filed a motion for the court to deposit the “surplus funds” pursuant to Civil Code section 2924j subdivisions (c) and (d), to discharge Petitioner from any further responsibility for disbursement of the funds, and to recover attorney fees and costs incurred in attempting to determine the proper claimants to the surplus funds.  The court granted the motion and awarded attorney fees and costs on April 14, 2026.

 

On April 30, 2026, David K. Gottlieb, as the Chapter 7 Trustee for the bankruptcy estate of Lisa Hines, substituted in as real party in interest for Ms. Hines.  (4/30/26 Substitution of Bankruptcy Trustee.)

 

Trustee Gottlieb now requests that the court release the surplus funds to him.  Trustee Gottlieb contends that the funds are the property of Ms. Hines’s bankruptcy estate because the Property was owned by the Dunn Clan Trust, the trust is a living revocable trust, and Ms. Hines is a trustee of the trust with authority to transfer assets in and out of the trust.  California law states that property which is part of such a trust is subject to claims against debtor by creditors.  No opposition has been filed.

 

On June 12, 2026, claimant Lisa Hines filed an ex parte application for an order prohibiting disbursement of the surplus funds pending adjudication of competing claims concerning ownership of the Property and entitlement to the surplus funds.  According to Ms. Hines, the bankruptcy court directed the parties asserting interests in property to seek relief in state court rather than bankruptcy court.  Based on this, Ms. Hines argues the Dunn Clan Trust rather than herself is the rightful owner of the surplus funds and the Deed issued after the foreclosure sale was issued in the wrong name.  Ms. Hines insists that the trust and its beneficiaries will be permanently deprived of resources necessary to litigate title defects, to recover the Property, to secure housing for displaced beneficiaries, and to obtain proper medical care.

 

Ms. Hines is self-represented.

 

 

REQUESTS FOR JUDICIAL NOTICE

 

Trustee Gottlieb asks the court to take judicial notice of the following, pursuant to Evidence Code section 452:

 

·         Exhibit 1:  Petitioner Quality Loan Service Corp.’s Petition and Declaration Regarding Unresolved Claims and Deposit of Undistributed Surplus Proceeds of Trustee’s Sale filed on November 24, 2025;

·         Exhibit 2:  Court order to Deposit Surplus Funds, entered on December 11, 2025;

·         Exhibit 3:  Voluntary bankruptcy petition filed by Lisa Hines under on May 30, 2025, pursuant to Chapter 7 of Title 11 of the United States Code (In re Lisa Hines, Case No. 1:25-bk-10950 MB).

 

The court grants Trustee Gottlieb’s request pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d).

 

 

Ms. Hines asks the court to take judicial notice of the following pursuant to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d) and section 453:

 

·         Exhibit A:  Grant Deed transferring ownership of the Property from Ms. Hines to the Dunn Clan Trust, recorded on January 21, 2025;

·         Exhibit B:  Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale executed by Quality Loan Service Corp. (as Trustee of the Deed of Trust) transferring ownership of the Property to DK Consolidated Investments, LLC, executed on May 15, 2025;

·         Exhibit C:  Deed of Trust securing a promissory note to unidentified property, listing World Savings Bank, FSB as the lender and Lisa Hines as the borrower, recorded on April 6, 2006;

·         Exhibit D: 

·         Exhibit E:  A table listing a summary of hearing dates, transcript citations, and quotes from hearings in bankruptcy court on June 17 and July 15 of 2025 before the Honorable Martin R. Barash;

·         Exhibit M:  Court records, transcripts, exhibits, an legal authorities from the bankruptcy court and case law.

 

The court grants the request in part for Exhibits A, B, and C, taking judicial notice of the existence and recordation of the exhibits according to Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (c).  (See Fontenot v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 256, 264-267, disapproved on other grounds by Yvanova v. New Century Mortgage Corp. (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919.)

 

The court denies the request in part for Exhibit D, which is nonexistent, and Exhibits E and M because they consist of Ms. Hines’s summary of court proceedings and legal authority rather than provide actual documents referenced in the exhibits.  A party’s summary of court proceedings or a court ruling are generally not the proper subject for judicial notice.

 

 

ANALYSIS

 

A.  Releasing Surplus Funds to a Bankruptcy Trustee

 

Civil Code section 2924j governs the handling of surplus funds from a trustee’s sale in nonjudicial foreclosure proceedings.

 

“The trustee, or the clerk of the court upon order to the clerk pursuant to subdivision (d) of Section 2924j, shall distribute the proceeds, or a portion of the proceeds, as the case may be, of the trustee's sale conducted pursuant to Section 2924h in the following order of priority: 

(1) To the costs and expenses of exercising the power of sale and of sale, including the payment of the trustee’s fees and attorney’s fees permitted pursuant to subdivision (b) of Section 2924d and subdivision (b) of this section.  (2) To the payment of the obligations secured by the deed of trust or mortgage which is the subject of the trustee’s sale.  (3) To satisfy the outstanding balance of obligations secured by any junior liens or encumbrances in the order of their priority.

(4) To the trustor or the trustor's successor in interest.  In the event the property is sold or transferred to another, to the vested owner of record at the time of the trustee's sale.”  (Civ. Code, § 2924k, subd. (a)(1)-(4).)

 

Based on the Petition and the Petitioner’s Exhibits, the vested owner of record of the Property at the time of the foreclosure sale was the Dunn Clan Trust.  On January 21, 2025, Ms. Hines recorded a grant deed transferring ownership of the Property from herself to the Dunn Clan Trust.  (Petition, Exh. B.)  Two days later, Ms. Hines recorded a Certificate of Trust, identifying the Dunn Clan Trust; classifying the trust as a “living revocable trust”; stating the trustees have the power to revoke the trust; naming Ms. Hines along with Lisa Amez Nefertiti Hines as trustees and beneficiaries of the trust; stating the trustees have authority to receive distributions made to the trust; and listing the Property (APN: 2662-006-030) as one of the trust’s legal properties.  (Petition, Exh. C.)

 

Therefore, any surplus funds from the Trustee’s Sale on April 29, 2025, less any junior liens, are the property of the Dunn Clan Trust.

 

Trustee Gottlieb claims that as of May 30, 2025, when Ms. Hines filed her bankruptcy petition, the surplus funds became the property of Ms. Hines’s bankruptcy estate.

 

Because Ms. Hines is a trustee-settlor of the Dunn Clan Trust, a living revocable trust, with the ability to revoke the trust the Trust’s property is subject to claims by Ms. Hines’s creditors.

 

California law treats a revocable living trust as merely a probate avoidance device and not a separate legal entity.  Property transferred to or held in a revocable living trust is the property of the settlor.  (Steinhart v. County of Los Angeles (2010) 47 Cal.4th 1298, 1319, citations omitted.)  “[A] settlor with the power to revoke the living trust effectively retains full ownership and control over any property transferred to the trust.”  (Arluk Medical Center Indus. Group, Inc. v. Dobler (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 1324, 1331-1332.)  “If the settlor retains the power to revoke the trust in whole or in part, the trust property is subject to the claims of creditors of the settlor to the extent of the power of revocation during the lifetime of the settlor.”  (Prob. Code, § 18200.)  “Under California law, a revocable inter vivos trust is recognized as simply ‘a probate avoidance device, but does not prevent creditors of the settlors—who are often also the trustees and the sole beneficiaries during their lifetimes—from reaching trust property.’”  (Zanelli v. McGrath (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 615, 633 [quoting Galdijie v. Darwish (2003) 113 Cal.App.4th 1331, 1349].)  “[I]n Galdjie, the court held an action for specific performance seeking to compel the settlors/trustors to convey title to property held in an inter vivos trust was not fatally defective for failing to name the settlors/trustees in their representative capacity as well as their individual capacity.  The court reasoned that the settlors in their individual capacity had full power to convey the property because a revocable inter vivos trust was merely a probate avoidance device, and does not prevent creditors of the settlors who were also the trustees and sole beneficiaries during their lifetime, from reaching trust property.”  (Id. at p. 633.)  “Property transferred to, or held in, a revocable inter vivos trust is nonetheless deemed the property of the settlor and is reachable by the creditors of the settlor.  These sections recognize that when property is held in this type of trust, the settlor and lifetime beneficiary has the equivalent of full ownership of the property.”  (Id. at pp. 633-634 [citing Prob. Code, §§ 18200, 18201].)

 

Because there is no distinction in California law between property owned by a revocable trust and property owned by the settlor of such a revocable trust during the settlor’s lifetime, property in which the Dunn Clan Trust has an ownership interest, including the Property and the proceeds from the foreclosure sale of the Property is property which Ms. Hines’s creditors may reach to satisfy Ms. Hines’s outstanding debt.

 

However, the filing of Ms. Hines’s voluntary bankruptcy petition cut off Ms. Hines’s and her creditors’ ability to take possession of the surplus funds.

 

The surplus funds from the foreclosure sale are property of the Chapter 7 Bankruptcy Estate of Ms. Hines.

 

“Under the Bankruptcy Code, “all legal or equitable interests of the debtor in property as of the commencement of [a bankruptcy] case” belong to the bankruptcy estate created at the debtor’s filing of a bankruptcy petition.”  (Bercy v. City of Phoenix (9th Cir. 2024) 103 F.4th 591, 594 [quoting 11 U.S.C. § 541(a)(1)]; M & M Foods, Inc. (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 554, 562.)  The trustee is the representative of the bankruptcy estate, the real party in interest, and the only party with standing to control the bankruptcy estate.  (Bostonian v. Liberty Savings Bank (1997) 52 Cal.App.4th 1075, 1081.)

 

In California, bankruptcy courts have consistently held that property held in a revocable trust is property of the bankruptcy estate when the debtor is the settlor.  “[W]hen the settlor of a trust retains the power to revoke a trust, the trust res remains the settlor’s property subject to his creditor’s claims.”  (In re Brooks-Hamilton (Bankr. N.D. Cal. 2006) 348 B.R. 512, 519 [citing Prob. Code, §§ 15800, 18200]; Empire Properties v. County of Los Angeles (1996) 44 Cal.App.4th 781, 786-787 [transferring real property to a revocable trust does not change ownership for property tax assessment purposes].)

 

The “[d]ebtor’s interest as a trustor and beneficiary under the Living Trust, among other things, all bec[omes] property of the bankruptcy estate pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 541(a) upon the filing of debtor petition.”  (In re Moffat (Bankr. C.D. Cal. 1989) 107 B.R. 255, 260.)  Because California law allows a creditor of a trustee-settlor of a revocable trust “to satisfy a claim against the settlor from property held by the trust . . . [s]ection 544(a)(1) [also gives] the bankruptcy estate an interest in the property.”  (In re Bogetti (Bankr. E.D. Cal. 2006) 349 B.R. 14, 18.)  This principle “invalidat[es] of a settlor to use a trust to shield property from his or her creditors[.]”  (In re Cutter (Bankr. 9th Cir. 2008) 398 B.R. 6, 20.)

 

Because the surplus proceeds from foreclosure sale are the property of the Dunn Clan Trust, the surplus proceeds are also property of Ms. Hines’s bankruptcy estate.  (See 11 U.S.C. § 541(a)(6).)

 

The court notes that the Dunn Clan Trust has two trustee-settlors: Ms. Hines and Lisa Amez-Nefertiti Hines.  (Petition, Exh. C at p. 3, ¶ 4.)  Unless otherwise stated in the instrument creating the trust, if a trust created by more than one settlor, each settlor may revoke the trust as to the portion of the trust contributed by that settlor.  (Prob. Code, § 15401, subd. (b)(1).)  The Certificate of the Dunn Clan Trust states “The individuals having the power to revoke the Trust are the Trustees, who retain full powers and authority, as amended.”  (Petition, Exh. C at p. 3, ¶ 5.)  This language suggests that both trustees individually have the power to revoke the trust on their own.

 

The court finds that Trustee Gottlieb has demonstrated that the remaining surplus funds are the property of the Dunn Clan Trust, the Dunn Clan Trust is a living revocable trust, and Ms. Hines is a trustee-settlor of the trust.  Thus, the surplus funds are the property of the Chapter 7 bankruptcy estate of Ms. Hines and bankruptcy trustee Daniel K. Gottlieb is entitled to take possession of the funds as part of the estate.

 

In her ex parte application, Ms. Hines claims there are competing claims concerning ownership of the Property and entitlement to the surplus proceeds.  According to Ms. Hines, the Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale identifies her rather than the Dunn Clan Trust.  Furthermore, the trust and its beneficiaries will be permanently deprived of resources necessary to litigate title defects or recover the property.  The court is perplexed by Ms. Hines’s assertion that the foreclosure sale was conducted in the wrong name.  (Declaration of Lisa Hines ¶ 4.)  A review of the Trustee’s Deed Upon Sale indicates that ownership was transferred to DK Consolidated Investments, LLC and that Ms. Hines was identified as the individual who signed the first Deed of Trust on the Property.  (Ex Parte, Exh. B at p. 2.)  Moreover, the documents presented with Ms. Hines’s ex parte application do not demonstrate any title defects.  Ms. Hines presents a copy of the first Deed of Trust on the Property but only provides two of the eighteen pages recorded for the first Deed of Trust.  (Ex Parte, Exh. C.)  Finally, Ms. Hines and her co-trustee are the only beneficiaries to the Dunn Clan Trust, and she identifies no other individuals with competing claims to the Property or surplus funds.

 

Therefore, the court grants the motion to release to David K. Gottlieb, in his capacity as the Chapter 7 Trustee of the Bankruptcy Estate of Lisa Hines, the surplus funds from the trustee’s sale of real property located at 15606 Simonds St. Granada Hills, CA 91344-4344.

 

 

CONCLUSION and ORDER

 

Motion to release the surplus funds is granted.

 

The court releases to David K. Gottlieb, in his capacity as the Chapter 7 Trustee of the Bankruptcy Estate of Lisa Hines, the surplus funds from the trustee’s sale of real property located at 15606 Simonds St. Granada Hills, CA 91344-4344.
