STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR PROCEEDINGS:

            Defendants Susan Ahn and Serrano & Sixth LLC (“Defendants”) prevailed at trial against Plaintiffs 3800 West Sixth Street, LLC and Leo Lee (“Plaintiffs”).  Defendants move to recover their attorneys’ fees in this case.  Plaintiffs oppose the motion.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendants’ motion for attorneys’ fees is DENIED.

Moving party to give notice. 

 

DISCUSSION:

The prevailing party must seek an award of attorneys’ fees via a noticed motion, and bears the burden of proof to justify the amount sought.  (See Code Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (c)(5)(A).) 

Contractual provision

Defendants contend they are entitled to recover attorneys’ fees under the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions.  In an action to enforce a contract that authorizes an award of attorneys’ fees, the party “prevailing on the contract” is entitled to recover attorneys’ fees.  (Civ. Code, § 1717.)  Here, in the operative first amended complaint, Plaintiffs did not sue Defendants for breach of contract.  Plaintiffs sued Defendants for misappropriation of trade secrets, unfair competition, and interference with prospective economic advantage.  Plaintiffs did not seek attorneys’ fees.  (See First Amended Complaint.)  As such, Plaintiffs did not sue Defendants on the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions, and Defendants did not prevail on a breach of contract claim.  Defendants are not entitled to recover attorneys’ fees under the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions.

In support of the argument that Defendants are entitled to recover fees under the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions, Defendants mischaracterize the holding of Lerner v. Ward (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th 155.  In that case, the Court of Appeal held that a party cannot recover attorneys’ fees based on a contract where the claims on which the party prevailed were tort claims, rather than contract claims.  (Lerner v. Ward (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th 155, 159.)  As such, Lerner v. Ward (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th 155 does not support Defendants’ argument that Defendants are entitled to recover fees.

 

Civil Code section 3426.4

Defendants seek attorneys’ fees and costs under Civil Code section 3426.4.  Civil Code section 3426.4 allows the Court to award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to the prevailing party where the other party has made a claim of misappropriation of trade secrets in bad faith.  (Civ. Code, § 3426.4.)  Under Civil Code section 3426.4, for a claim to have been made in bad faith, the claim must have been objectively specious, and must have been maintained in subjective bad faith.  (Gemini Aluminum Corp. v. California Custom Shapes, Inc. (2002 95 Cal.App.4th 1249, 1261-1262.)  As set forth above, Plaintiffs sued Defendants for misappropriation of trade secrets.  Defendants prevailed on that claim.  As such, Defendants are entitled to recover fees for defending the claim for misappropriation of trade secrets if the claim was frivolous and Plaintiffs acted in bad faith.  As Defendants point out, the Court granted summary adjudication of Plaintiffs’ claim for misappropriation of trade secrets.  (November 14, 2024 Minute Order.)  However, Plaintiffs’ claim for misappropriation of trade secrets is not frivolous simply because Defendants prevailed on the claim.  Further, Defendants do not address the issue of Plaintiffs’ bad faith.  The Court does not infer that Plaintiffs acted in bad faith because their claim for misappropriation of trade secrets failed.  By Defendants’ reasoning, any failed claim is frivolous and in bad faith.  Accordingly, Defendants fail to show they are entitled to recover fees under Civil Code section 3426.4.

Accordingly, Defendants’ motion for attorneys’ fees is DENIED. 

Moving party to give notice.

 

IT IS SO ORDERED.

STATEMENT OF MATERIAL FACTS AND/OR
PROCEEDINGS:

            Defendant
Steven C. Kim (“Defendant”) prevailed at trial against Plaintiffs 3800 West
Sixth Street, LLC and Leo Lee (“Plaintiffs”). 
Defendant moves to recover his attorneys’ fees in this case.  Plaintiffs oppose the motion.

 

TENTATIVE RULING:

Defendant’s
motion for attorneys’ fees is DENIED.

Moving
party to give notice. 

 

DISCUSSION:

The prevailing party must seek an
award of attorneys’ fees via a noticed motion, and bears the burden of proof to
justify the amount sought.  (See Code
Civ. Proc., § 1033.5, subd. (c)(5)(A).) 

Contractual
provision

Defendant
contends he is entitled to recover attorneys’ fees under the Agreement for
Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions.  Defendant is not a party to the
contract.  As a non-signatory to the Agreement
for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions, Defendant
is entitled to recover fees if Plaintiffs sued Defendant on the
Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions,
Defendant prevailed on the breach of contract claim, and Plaintiffs would have
had a right to recover fees had Plaintiffs prevailed.  (Reynolds Metals Co. v. Alperson
(1979) 25 Cal.3d 124, 128-129.)  Here, in
the operative first amended complaint, Plaintiffs did not sue Defendant for
breach of contract.  Plaintiffs sued
Defendant for misappropriation of trade secrets, breach of fiduciary duty,
unfair competition, and interference with prospective economic advantage.  Plaintiffs did not seek attorneys’ fees.  (See First Amended Complaint.)  As such, Plaintiffs did not sue Defendant on
the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow
Instructions, and Defendant did not prevail on a breach of contract claim.  Defendant is not entitled to recover
attorneys’ fees under the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and
Joint Escrow Instructions.

In
support of his argument that Defendant is entitled to recover fees under the
Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow Instructions,
Defendant mischaracterizes the holding of Lerner v. Ward (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th
155.  In that case, the Court of Appeal
held that a party cannot recover attorneys’ fees based on a contract where the
claims on which the party prevailed were tort claims, rather than contract
claims.  (Lerner v. Ward (1993) 13
Cal.App.4th 155, 159.)  As such, Lerner
v. Ward (1993) 13 Cal.App.4th 155 does not support Defendant’s argument
that Defendant is entitled to recover fees.

Likewise,
Defendant’s reliance on Kangarlou v. Progressive Title Co. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th
1174 is misplaced.  In that case, the
Court of Appeal held that a claim for breach of fiduciary duty may be a
contract claim or a tort claim and entitlement to attorneys’ fees on a claim
for breach of fiduciary duty depends on whether the claim is based on
contractual duties.  (Kangarlou v.
Progressive Title Co. (2005) 128 Cal.App.4th 1174, 1177.)  Plaintiffs’ claim for breach of fiduciary
duty against Defendant is based on Defendant’s fiduciary duty as an
attorney.  (See First Amended Complaint,
¶ 33,)  As such, it is a tort claim, not
a contract claim.

Defendant
also cites Wong v. Davidian (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d 264.  In that case, the Court of Appeal held that
attorneys’ fees are recoverable in an action for reformation of contract.  (Wong v. Davidian (1988) 206 Cal.App.3d
264, 271.)  No such claim is at issue
here.

Defendant
argues he is entitled to attorneys’ fees as a third-party beneficiary of the
Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property and Joint Escrow
Instructions.  That is immaterial, as Plaintiffs
did not sue Defendant on the Agreement for Purchase and Sale of Real Property
and Joint Escrow Instructions.  Furthermore,
Defendant makes no showing that he is a third-party beneficiary.  A
third-party beneficiary to a contract may enforce a contract if the third party
shows that the third party is likely to benefit from the contract, a motivating
purpose of the contracting parties was to provide the third
party with a benefit, and permitting the third party to enforce the contract is
consistent with the contract's objectives and the contracting parties'
reasonable expectations. (Goonewardene v. ADP, LLC (2019) 6 Cal.5th 817,
821.)  Defendant fails to address these
factors.  (See Motion for Attorneys’
Fees, pp. 11-12.) 

 

Civil
Code section 3426.4

Defendant
also seeks attorneys’ fees and costs under Civil Code section 3426.4.  Civil Code section 3426.4 allows the Court to
award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to the prevailing party where the
other party has made a claim of misappropriation of trade secrets in bad
faith.  (Civ. Code, § 3426.4.)  Under Civil Code section 3426.4, for a claim
to have been made in bad faith, the claim must have been objectively specious,
and must have been maintained in subjective bad faith.  (Gemini Aluminum Corp. v. California Custom
Shapes, Inc. (2002 95 Cal.App.4th 1249, 1261-1262.)  As set forth above, Plaintiffs sued Defendant
for misappropriation of trade secrets. 
Defendant prevailed on that claim. 
As such, Defendant is entitled to recover fees for defending the claim
for misappropriation of trade secrets if the claim was frivolous and Plaintiffs
acted in bad faith.  As Defendant points
out, the Court granted summary adjudication of Plaintiffs’ claim for
misappropriation of trade secrets.  (November
14, 2024 Minute Order.)  However, Plaintiffs’
claim for misappropriation of trade secrets is not frivolous simply because
Defendant prevailed on the claim. 
Further, the Court does not infer that Plaintiffs acted in bad faith
because their claim for misappropriation of trade secrets failed.  By Defendant’s reasoning, any failed claim is
frivolous and in bad faith.  Accordingly,
Defendant fails to show he is entitled to recover fees under Civil Code section
3426.4.

 

Code
of Civil Procedure section 2033.420

“Under
Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.420, a party who unreasonably
denies a request for admission may be required to pay the requesting party its
reasonable expenses (including reasonable attorney's fees) incurred in proving
the truth of the matter at trial.”  (In re Estate of Manuel (2010)
187 Cal.App.4th 400, 402.)  Defendant
argues that Plaintiffs unreasonably denied certain requests for admissions that
Defendant propounded on Plaintiffs. 
Defendant contends he proved the matters set forth in the requests for
admissions at trial.  Defendant does not,
however, address the issue of whether Plaintiffs’ denial of the requests for
admissions was unreasonable.  (See Motion
for Attorneys’ Fees, pp. 16-17.)  Accordingly,
Defendant fails to show he is entitled to recover fees under Code of Civil
Procedure section 2033.420.

Accordingly,
Defendant’s motion for attorneys’ fees is DENIED.

party to give notice.
