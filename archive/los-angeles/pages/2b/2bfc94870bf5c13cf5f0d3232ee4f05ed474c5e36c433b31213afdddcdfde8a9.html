Case Number: 23VECV05702    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Barrett v. Jackson, Case no. 23VECV05702

Hearing date June 30, 2026

Defendant Jacksun’s Motion to Set Aside Default and Default Judgment

Plaintiff Barrett sues defendants Jacksun, erroneously sued as Jackson, Jermaine L. Jackson, Inc. and Work Records, Inc. for alleged sexual assault. Default was entered on 4/23/26, and default judgment on 5/14/26. Jacksun moves to set aside the default and default judgment, quash service and requests leave to file a proposed demurrer. Plaintiff opposes.

Jacksun requests judicial notice of 22 documents. Plaintiff does not object to notice of the existence of the documents, but objects to a request for judicial notice of the documents’ disputed factual contents or the truth thereof. This is proper. The court GRANTS judicial notice of the documents’ existence or absence from the court file pursuant to Evid. Code §452(d) but does not grant notice of the truth or contested nature of their contents.

Plaintiff offers the declaration of counsel Castagna. Jacksun objects. Objections 1-18 OVERRULED.

Per Code Civ. Proc. §473(b), a motion to set aside default is timely if filed “within a reasonable time, in no case exceeding 6 months” after the order was taken.

Jacksun argues the default and default judgment are void pursuant to Code Civ. Proc. §473(d), as plaintiff failed to correctly serve or provide adequate notice. Jacksun asserts he legally changed his name from “Jackson,” the name in the complaint and in the service via publication, to “Jacksun” on 2/20/13. Decl. Jacksun, para. 2. Jacksun declares under penalty of that he did not receive actual notice of the complaint, summons or statement of damages. Id. at paras. 5-8.

Plaintiff argues Jacksun still regularly conducts business under the name “Jackson” so cannot claim lack of notice. See Decl. Castagna, exhs. 6-8. This is unavailing. The name under which an artist or businessperson conducts business does not control for issues of service of process. Service via publication likewise must strictly comply with statutory requirements, and any defect is grounds to void. In re Marriage of Lippel (1990) 51 Cal.3d 1160, 1166. The failure to use Jacksun’s true legal name is fatal to the default.

Jacksun asserts he was living in Bahrain during the dates of attempted service. Decl. Jacksun, para. 9. Jacksun asserts at no time was he residing at or receiving mail at his mother’s address, in Encino, the address used for initial service attempts. Id. at para. 9. Jacksun argues plaintiff’s service via publication occurred in a Los Angeles publication, despite plaintiff’s non-service notices stating that Jacksun lived overseas. See 3/20/25 Supp. Decl. Lang, exh. A; 5/22/25 Supp. Decl. Reck, exh. A.

Plaintiff argues she made all reasonable efforts to locate Jacksun prior to serving via publication in Los Angeles. Plaintiff asserts she attempted service at multiple locations tied to Jacksun, consulted Lexis Nexis Public Records Search and Transunion Locator Services, contacted Jacksun’s siblings and retained a private investigator. See Decl. Castagna, exhs. 5, 10-11. Plaintiff argues this satisfies the requirements of Code Civ. Proc. §415.50. See Watts v. Crawford (1995) 10 Cal.4th 743, 748–749 & fn. 5. This is unavailing; where plaintiff had notice that Jacksun was alleged to be overseas, and where plaintiff conducted at least some investigations into his overseas locations, choosing to publish in Los Angeles does not constitute reasonable diligence. See Code Civ. Proc. §415.50(b).

Plaintiff argues news articles have been published regarding the existence and allegations of this case. See Decl. Castagna, paras. 13-17, exhs. 12-16. Plaintiff argues Jacksun was dropped from his talent agency as a result of this case and argues this should have provided Jacksun with actual notice. Id. at exh. 16. This is unavailing. Service requirements must be strictly complied with and strictly construed, especially in defaults. Lippel, supra. No statute recognizes alleged third-party employment decisions as valid constructive notice.

The failure to use Jacksun’s true legal name is fatal, compounded by plaintiff’s decision to publish in Los Angeles, while Jacksun was apparently living overseas. The court recognizes the difficulties in locating defendants living abroad. However, choosing to publish in Los Angeles after receiving notice that Jacksun was likely abroad, and doing so with an incorrect name, leaves no choice but to void the default judgment against all defendants and the default as to Jacksun personally. Defendants Jermaine L. Jackson, Inc. and Work Records, Inc. remain in default.

Jacksun’s request to file a demurrer with motion to strike on his behalf only is GRANTED, to be filed within 5 court days. Jacksun argues service of the summons and complaint should be quashed. DENIED. Jacksun has made an appearance via filing this motion and filed a proposed responsive pleading, not a motion to quash. The court has set aside the default against Jacksun and accepted the proposed responsive pleading. There is no basis to quash service.

Jacksun requests “access through counsel to all sealed, lodged, conditionally sealed, or non-public default-prove-up materials” filed. Mtn. 2:4-5. DENIED without prejudice.

GRANTED in part as above; the 5/14/26 default judgment is set aside as void. The default entered against Jacksun on 4/23/26 is set aside as void. Jacksun is to file the proposed demurrer with motion to strike within 5 court days.


Case Number: 25VECV06211    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Rechtman v. Sutton National Insurance Company, Case no. 25VECV06211

Hearing date June 30, 2026

Defendant Bamboo’s Demurrer to the First Amended Complaint

Plaintiffs Orie and Olga Rechtman sue defendants Sutton National Insurance and Bamboo IDE8 Insurance Services, LLC, alleging breaches of a homeowner’s insurance policy. Plaintiffs allege their home sustained water damage and Sutton, their homeowners’ insurance carrier, denied or failed to pay benefits owed. Plaintiffs bring derivative claims against Bamboo for violation of Bus. & Prof. Code §17200 et seq., financial elder abuse and aiding and abetting Sutton's alleged breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. Bamboo demurs; plaintiffs oppose.

Bamboo requests judicial notice of the first amended complaint. GRANTED. Evid. Code §452(d).

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Bamboo demurs to the cause of action for violations of Bus. & Prof. Code §17200, et seq. (“UCL”), which prohibits "unlawful, unfair or fraudulent" business acts or practices. A UCL claim may borrow violations of other laws, but California law has long prohibited private plaintiffs from enforcing the Unfair Insurance Practices Act ("UIPA"), including Ins. Code §790.03(h), via a private damages action. Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Companies (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287, 304-305. A first-party insured may pursue a UCL claim when the insurer’s conduct violates duties independent of the UIPA but cannot maintain a claim solely on the basis of Ins. Code §790.03. Zhang v. Superior Court (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364, 381-384.

Plaintiffs allege defendants, including Bamboo, failed to comply with claim regulations, and the conduct will continue unless enjoined. FAC, para. 50. Plaintiffs allege these acts and omissions violate Ins. Code §790.03(h), Title 10 regulations and other unspecified statutes. Id. at para. 51. Plaintiffs allege Sutton issued the policy, and the contract and bad-faith causes of action are alleged against Sutton only. Id. at paras. 13, 23-46. Plaintiffs offer no additional allegations supporting the claim against claims investigator Bamboo. Id. at paras. 6, 18.

Plaintiffs argue they alleged violations of the UCL beyond the UIPA. Plaintiffs argue they alleged Sutton and Bamboo are acting in concert to financially profit on underpaid or wrongfully denied claims at the expense of consumers. See FAC, para. 18. The actions, allegedly made by defendants in concert, are alleged to arise from violations of Ins. Code §790.03(h). Id. at para. 51. However, plaintiffs offer no specific allegations against Bamboo, such as false advertising, fraudulent invoicing or any direct transaction between plaintiffs and Bamboo.

California courts consistently hold that non-insurer agents, adjusters, and claims administrators are not parties to the insurance contract and are not subject to the insurer's implied covenant duties. Gruenberg v. Aetna Ins. Co. (1973) 9 Cal.3d 566, 576; Egan v. Mutual of Omaha Ins. Co. (1979) 24 Cal.3d 809, 824. Plaintiffs offer no basis outside of Bamboo’s alleged violations of Ins. Code §790.03(h) to support the cause of action. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Bamboo demurs to the cause of action for financial elder abuse, which occurs when an entity takes, secretes, appropriates, obtains, or retains real or personal property of an elder for wrongful use, with intent to defraud, or by undue influence, or assists in doing so. Welf. & Inst. Code §15610.30(a).

Plaintiffs allege defendants failed and refused to pay benefits owed under the policy, representing a taking or retention of plaintiffs' monies. FAC, paras. 57-59. Plaintiffs allege Sutton retained Bamboo to perform claim investigation, adjustment and coverage analysis on Sutton's behalf. FAC, para. 18. Plaintiffs do not allege Bamboo possessed plaintiff’s funds, controlled funds, received funds or had a contractual obligation to pay funds.

Plaintiffs argue they alleged “Bamboo’s compensation and continued engagement depend on the financial performance of claims it services. As a result, Bamboo has a financial incentive to recommend that claims be denied or minimized, including the Claim at issue here.” FAC, para. 18. Plaintiffs argue Bamboo knew its conduct would harm plaintiffs but engaged in the conduct regardless, constituting abuse. Plaintiffs argue Bamboo improperly financially benefited by refusing to recommend that Sutton pay insurance proceeds to plaintiffs, thereby assisting Sutton in retaining benefits. See FAC, para. 58.

Plaintiffs offer no legal support for their argument that Bamboo’s claim investigation work for Sutton constitutes assisting in financial elder abuse. An insurer’s bad faith denial of a claim may, in some circumstances, support financial elder abuse against the insurer, but California courts have held that imposing elder abuse liability on a representative who assists the insurer in investigating the claim improperly circumvents Gruenberg, supra, which ruled that noninsurer agents cannot be liable for the insurer's bad faith. See Strawn v. Morris, Polich & Purdy, LLP (2019) 30 Cal.App.5th 1087 at 1101-1103. Allegations of elder abuse arising from Bamboo’s role as a claims investigator are insufficient. There are no allegations that Bamboo directly took plaintiffs’ funds or property or engaged in any separate taking. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Bamboo demurs to the cause of action for aiding and abetting Sutton’s alleged breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, which arises between contracting parties. See Comunale v. Traders & General Ins. Co. (1958) 50 Cal.2d 654, 658. Agents acting solely on behalf of an insurer cannot be liable for conspiring to breach duties they did not personally owe. Doctors' Company v. Superior Court (1989) 49 Cal.3d 39 at 44-47.

Plaintiffs argue they are not alleging Bamboo itself breached the covenant of good faith and fair dealing, but that it committed the independent tort of aiding and abetting Sutton’s alleged breach. Plaintiffs allege Bamboo knew of Sutton’s bad faith, assisted by manufacturing a coverage dispute, which was a substantial factor in causing harm by recommending/directing Sutton to deny the claim. FAC, para. 69. Plaintiffs argue Doctor’s Company, supra held where “nonfiduciary defendants had acted not simply as agents or employees of the fiduciary defendants but rather in furtherance of their own financial gain, they could not have been relieved from liability under the Gruenberg-Wise rule.” Id. at 47.

Even where permitted under Doctor’s Company, supra, a claim for aiding and abetting must be supported by allegations showing knowledge of the abetted wrongdoing. See Saunders v. Superior Court (1994) 27 Cal.App.4th 832, 846; Fiol v. Doellstedt (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 1318, 1325-1326. Generalized allegations are insufficient; plaintiffs must allege facts showing actual knowledge and participation in a specific, primary wrongful act. Casey v. U.S. Bank National Assn. (2005) 127 Cal.App.4th 1138, 1148-1152. Suspicion, participation in ordinary business activity, or generalized knowledge that something may be wrong is insufficient. Id at 1149-1152.

Plaintiffs allege Bamboo "had actual knowledge" that Sutton's denial lacked a reasonable basis, and the damage to plaintiffs’ property resulted from a mechanical failure in the dishwasher. FAC, para. 69. Conclusory allegations are insufficient, as are allegations that Bamboo itself committed a wrongful act. Plaintiffs must allege that Bamboo knew of a specific wrong by Sutton, i.e., an intention to wrongfully deny coverage and actively assisted in that wrongdoing. Merely alleging Bamboo had a financial interest in assisting Sutton in keeping claims payouts low is too generalized to satisfy the pleading requirements of Casey, supra. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.


Case Number: 25VECV07364    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Bedrosian v. Mercury Insurance Co., Case no. 25VECV07364

Hearing date June 30, 2026

Defendant Mercury’s Demurrer with Motion to Strike to the First Amended Complaint

Plaintiff Bedrosian sues defendant Mercury Insurance Co. over defendant’s handling of an underinsured-motorist (“UIM”) claim. Defendant demurred to five of the six causes of action; the first amended complaint abandoned three of the original claims. The FAC now pleads causes of action for breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing and violation of California Bus. & Prof. Code §17200, et seq. Defendant demurs to the first and third causes of action and separately moves to strike the prayer for equitable relief under the third cause of action. Plaintiff opposes both motions.

Defendant Mercury’s Demurrer to First Amended Complaint

“The function of a demurrer is to test the sufficiency of the complaint as a matter of law.” Holiday Matinee, Inc. v. Rambus, Inc. (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1413, 1420. A complaint “is sufficient if it alleges ultimate rather than evidentiary facts,” Doe v. City of Los Angeles (2007) 42 Cal.4th 531, 550, but plaintiff must allege essential facts “with reasonable precision and with particularity sufficient to acquaint [the] defendant with the nature, source and extent” of the plaintiff’s claim. Doheny Park Terrace Homeowners Ass’n., Inc. v. Truck Ins. Exchange (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1076, 1099.

Defendant demurs to the breach of contract claim, an essential element of which is breach of an express contractual provision. Archdale v. American Specialty Lines Ins. Co. (2007) 154 Cal.App.4th 449, 466–467; Frances T. v. Village Green Owners Ass’n. (1986) 42 Cal.3d 490, 512–513. The policy (FAC, exh. A) states what defendant owes plaintiff and under what circumstances payment is due, but no express terms as to when payment is owed. Id. The policy provides for an arbitration process prior to a claim being paid. Id.

Plaintiff argues she performed all obligations under the policy and was owed payment. FAC, paras. 13, 16, 75-76. Plaintiff alleges defendant breached by failing to pay, delaying for 33 months while the claim was investigated and the matter arbitrated. Id. at paras. 77-79. Plaintiff argues the lack of a payment deadline does not abrogate defendant’s obligation to make timely payments under Civ. Code §1657. Plaintiff alleges the 33-month delay was not reasonable and constitutes breach. Id. at paras. 77-78.

The FAC identifies no express contractual provision that defendant allegedly breached. This is an essential element for a breach of contract claim. Archdale, supra. The policy contains no payment due date nor timing obligation. See FAC exh. A. Plaintiff’s arguments that delayed payment constitutes breach are unavailing; these claims sound in breach of the implied covenant, not breach of contract, which requires plaintiff to identify breach of specific terms. The FAC alleges defendant’s performance by admitting that defendant ultimately paid the entire remaining policy limit, and plaintiff received the full $250,000 UIM benefit. To sustain a breach of contract claim, plaintiff must identify specific contractual language or obligations that defendant allegedly breached. Plaintiff alleges defendant’s conduct frustrated the purpose of the contract, not that defendant breached the contract itself. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendant demurs to plaintiff’s third claim for violations of Bus. & Prof. Code §17200, et seq (“UCL”), which offers only equitable relief. See Cel-Tech Commc’ns., Inc. v. Los Angeles Cellular Tel. Co. (1999) 20 Cal.4th 163, 179. Where plaintiff has an alternative remedy at law, equitable relief is not available. Prudential Home Mortgage Co. v. Superior Court (1998) 66 Cal.App.4th 1236, 1250; Korea Supply Co. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. (2003) 29 Cal.4th 1134, 1150–1151.

Defendant argues the relief sought under the second cause of action includes compensatory damages, emotional-distress damages and attorney’s fees (FAC, para. 89) and stands to make plaintiff whole, barring equitable relief. Defendant further argues the equitable relief sought is an impermissibly generalized, forward-facing injunction. See FAC, para. 97.

Plaintiff argues defendant fails to address the unfair business practices prong under Cel-Tech, supra, at 180-181. Plaintiff argues an alleged systemic breach of insurance contracts can constitute an unfair business practice under the UCL. See Arce v. Kaiser Foundation Health Plan, Inc. (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471, 489. Plaintiff argues she alleged such systemic breaches. See FAC, paras. 68-69, 94. Plaintiff argues this remedy argument is inappropriate on demurrer, as a demurrer only tests the sufficiency of a pleading, not whether the relief sought will be awarded, citing Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital Dist. (1992) 2 Cal.4th 962, 967.

Plaintiff’s argument is unavailing. Dismissal of a UCL claim on demurrer because plaintiff could not allege “that she or the general public have, or will, suffer injuries which cannot be compensated by an award of damages” is permissible. Brownfield v. Daniel Freeman Marina Hosp. (1989) 208 Cal. App. 3d 405, 410, 414. The fullness of the remedies provided by the second cause of action bars equitable relief under the third cause of action.

The relief sought in the FAC, a “forward-looking injunction [enjoining defendant] from continuing the pattern, practice, and corporate policy” (FAC, para. 97) is not an available remedy under the law. An injunction “may not issue unless the alleged misconduct is ongoing or likely to recur,” and “can never be used as a remedy designed to right completed wrongs.” Madrid v. Perot Systems Corp. (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 440, 464–465. Plaintiff alleges the full benefits available under the policy have been paid and received (FAC, para. 65) and fails to allege another relationship with defendant or imminent risk of harm. This is fatal to the cause of action.

The court does not have authority to issue a sweeping, generalized injunction prohibiting defendant from engaging in future business practices where plaintiff has not alleged an ongoing relation with defendant nor immediate, particularized harm. The argument that the injunction sought is a public injunction under McGill v. Citibank, N.A. (2017) 2 Cal.5th 945 is also unavailing. Relief with the primary purpose and effect of benefitting plaintiff or “a group of individuals similarly situated to the plaintiff” is not public injunctive relief. Id. at 955. SUSTAINED with 15 days leave to amend.

Defendant Mercury’s Motion to Strike

As the court sustained the demurrers with leave to amend, the motion to strike is MOOT.


Case Number: 26VECV01930    Hearing Date: June 30, 2026    Dept: B

Tentative Ruling

Martinez v. Mercedes-Benz USA, Case no. 26VECV01930

Hearing date June 30, 2026

Defendant MBUSA’s Motion to Compel Arbitration

Plaintiff Farshi sues defendants Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC (“MBUSA”) and Los Angeles-M, Inc., dba Lithia Keyes European for breaches of the Song-Beverly Act. MBUSA moves to compel arbitration; plaintiff opposes.

MBUSA offers the declaration of counsel Newman. Plaintiff objects. Obj. 1-2 OVERRULED.

MBUSA requests judicial notice of various federal and California state court decisions. See 6/23/26 RJN. GRANTED. Plaintiff requests judicial notice of: (1) Ochoa v. Ford Motor Co. (2023) 89 Cal.App.5th 1324; (2) Kielar v. Hyundai Motor America (Case No. C096773; Superior Court No. S-CV-0048230); and (3) Ngo v. BMW of North America, LLC, et al. (9th Cir. 2022) 23 F.4th 942. GRANTED per Evid. Code §452(d).

California public policy favors arbitration. Code Civ. Proc. §1280 et seq., Madden v. Kaiser Found. Hosps. (1976) 17 Cal.3d 699, 706. Under California’s Arbitration Act, where a valid written agreement to arbitrate exists, arbitration is mandatory. Code Civ. Proc. §1281.2. The opposing party bears the burden of proving any defense to arbitrability. See Pinnacle Museum Tower Ass’n. v. Pinnacle Mkt. Dev. (US), LLC (2012) 55 Cal.4th 223, 236. “[T]he doctrine of unconscionability has both a procedural and substantive element, the former focusing on oppression or surprise due to unequal bargaining power, the latter on overly harsh or one-sided results.” See Sanchez v. Valencia Holding Co., LLC (2015) 61 Cal. 4th 899, 910.

The Federal Arbitration Act codifies federal policy favoring arbitration, making an arbitration agreement in the Terms of Service “valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract…” 9 U.S.C. §2. “Courts must ‘rigorously enforce’ arbitration agreements according to their terms…” Am. Exp. Co. v. Italian Colors Rest. (2013) 570 U.S. 228, 233.

Plaintiff leased a 2024 Mercedes-Benz C-Class AMG C43. Decl. Newman, exh. 2. Page 4 of the lease contains a binding arbitration agreement naming MBUSA as an intended third-party beneficiary. Id. Plaintiff’s initials are affixed directly below the arbitration disclosures. Id. The arbitration agreement applies to any dispute arising from or relating to the lease, including warranties. Id. A binding arbitration agreement exists between the parties and covers the issues in this action.

Plaintiff opposes, arguing MBUSA is not an intended third-party beneficiary under Goonewardene v. ADP (2019) 6 Cal.5th 817, 830. However, the arbitration agreement names MBUSA as an intended third-party beneficiary. See Decl. Ameripur, exh. 2. Per Goonewardene, supra, a party seeking to establish it is a third-party beneficiary to a contract must show: (1) it would benefit from the contract it seeks to enforce; (2) a motivating purpose of the parties was to benefit that third-party beneficiary; and (3) allowing the putative third-party beneficiary to enforce the contract would be consisted with the contract’s objectives. Id. at 830.

These requirements are met: (1) Mercedes-Benz seeks to benefit from the lease agreement by enforcing the arbitration agreement; (2) Mercedes-Benz benefits when a dealership sells or leases a vehicle, and the dealer intended to benefit Mercedes-Benz via the arbitration agreement; and (3) the lease names Mercedes-Benz in the arbitration agreement and is signed by plaintiff, evidencing an intent to allow Mercedes-Benz to enforce the contract. Mercedes-Benz is an intended third-party beneficiary under a Goonewardene analysis.

That Mercedes-Benz is named in the arbitration agreement indicates an intent to include it in the arbitration agreement. Plaintiff signed directly below the arbitration agreement, confirming he read and agreed, including Mercedes-Benz being a third-party beneficiary. Decl. Newman exh. 2. Mercedes-Benz is a third-party beneficiary with the right to invoke the arbitration agreement.

Plaintiff argues his Song-Beverly claims do not arise from the lease, but are warranty claims, whereas the lease is a financing deal. The arbitration agreement includes “any claim… which arises out of or relates to a credit application, this lease, or any resulting transaction or relationship… (including any such relationship with [Mercedes-Benz].)” Decl. Newman, exh. 2. Plaintiff’s invocation of warranties is a relationship with defendant arising out of the lease.

Plaintiff offers no arguments as to unconscionability. A binding arbitration agreement exists, to which MBUSA is an intended third-party beneficiary, and which encompasses plaintiff’s claims. GRANTED; the parties are ordered to binding arbitration per the terms of the arbitration agreement.
